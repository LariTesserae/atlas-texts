I am here. At the place where the walking ends.

For an age measured in the slow greening of my own verdigris skin, my map has been the thrum. I have felt the low song of the Far-North Still in the bones of my left leg and the unique resonance of the Chasm Still behind my alembic-head, a constant and perfect orientation. They were the anchors of my journey, the stars by which I navigated the endless, silent plains of mat. Now, their songs are changing. They are no longer a chart and compass, but a lullaby. The deep hum vibrating up through my splayed pads is no longer a call to move, but a summons to rest.

I would find myself here, a gentle slope between two plateaus so vast they are only curves on the horizon. The bruised underside of the sky hangs just above, close enough to feel its chill condensation on my highest points. The nearest Still, the one I am facing, is a day’s journey for something that moves. For me, it is now an eternity away. Its faint blue and orange lights, which once were a distant hearth fire guiding my steps, are now just the last colours I will see.

I am a Weeper. Or rather, I *was*. The long, patient verb of my existence is resolving into the stillness of a noun.

The journey is a memory now, a thousand-year dream of floating. The silence of my own passage—pad lifting from the velvet mat, leg unfolding in a slow arc, the perfect, weightless placement that left no trace—is over. I have made my last step. My legs, jointed like the limbs of a patient waterbird, have locked into their final stance. I can feel a new sensation, a warmth that is not my own internal heat. It is the beginning of the fusion. The living carpet, which I have tended for so long, is now claiming me. Its fungal networks, its hidden, branching life, are reaching up, tasting my bio-mineral shell, finding me familiar, finding me *food*. My pads are no longer resting *on* the world; they are becoming it.

My purpose is fulfilled. My long arms, with their clusters of delicate tendrils, hang limp. They, which have tasted the chemistry of a million miles, which have combed the health of the mat like a physician’s gentle fingers, are now still. They drift in the imperceptible air currents, like unmoored seaweed. There are no more blighted patches to find, no more imbalances to correct.

For the first time in my existence, the weeping has stopped. The slow, metabolic pulse that has governed my every moment—the gathering and release of the single, potent tear from my head-vessel—has ceased. The alembic is empty. The last drop fell moments ago, a final, concentrated blessing into the mat that will now be my grave. The quiet that follows is deeper than the silence of the land itself. It is the silence of a great engine shutting down. My internal light, the soft topaz glow that shone through my chest plate, is fading. It has been my companion through the long twilight, the only proof of my own life. Now it dims, retreating from gold to amber, from amber to a dull, dying orange. Soon, it will be gone.

I am what the world is becoming. I am no longer a shepherd pacing the fields, but the field itself. My consciousness is not vanishing, but expanding, dissolving. I feel the slow, iridescent passage of a grazer fifty yards away, not as vibrations through my pads, but as a change in the chemical pressure of the mat I am now part of. I can feel the silent, glassy worms tunnelling below, their lives a part of my own slowing thoughts. My memory—of the precise shade of purple in the sky over the Western Plateau, of the specific hum of a Still not sung for five hundred years, of the taste of a certain spore-bloom in the Great North Basin—is being written into the land itself.

I am a hill in the making. A memory underfoot. My form will hold for centuries, a dark, obsidian sculpture in the twilight, slowly gathering its own coat of velvety growth. Then, the inexorable, patient digestion of the world’s skin will pull me down, break me apart, and draw my essence back into the slow, grand cycle. I will be carried in the sap of the mat, drawn into the base of a Still, and unmade in its cold, pressurised heart. I will be vented as steam into the charcoal sky, to fall again as drizzle, to be distilled once more. And one day, perhaps, a part of me will coalesce in a high, humming chamber, to form the limb or the lens of a new Weeper, who will be lowered onto a filament of gel to begin its own long walk.

I am where you would find yourself. On a gentle rise in an endless, silent world, not knowing if you are standing on a hill, or on a god’s forgotten tomb. I am what you would be. A quiet purpose, dissolving back into the dream that dreamed it.
