I am a thought this world has not yet learned to think.

I am still. I am a long-held drop that has not fallen, a lens of living glass pressed into a crevice of amber resin. My name is the feel of my belly on a wet surface, a cool adhesion. The Ghyrma, the slow gods of this place, call my kind the Glass-Stalkers, though their naming is a low thrum, a vibration felt through stone, not a sound spoken.

My body is a lie. It is the color of water, the clarity of clean air. Light does not strike me; it passes through me, bending slightly as it goes. I am a secret the twilight keeps. From my place in this hollow, my hunting-nook, I see the world through myself. The vast, velvet leaves of the vines are softened, their edges blurred. The glowing moss is a diffuse bloom, a smear of cool fire. I do not see, I contain the seeing.

I am outside the great Hum. The slow, sonic dreaming of the Ghyrma is not my dream, but it is the blood of my world. It is the deep note against which all other sounds are measured. I feel it in my bones, which are themselves like threads of spun glass. It is a constant pressure in my belly, a warmth that is not my own. Their collective mind is the bedrock. My mind is a flicker. A high, sharp note.

My consciousness is made of two states: Waiting and Hunger.

For days, I am Waiting. I am architecture. I am condensation. My skin tastes the slow drip, drip, drip from the bladder-orchid above. Each drop is a small, cold shock that runs the length of my spine. I feel the shudder of the tower as a Ghyrma, a moving hill of biographical resin, scrapes its flank against the foundation a dozen meters below. I taste the air for the signature of a Pollen-Ghost, a passing drift of dry, sweet dust that means nothing to me. In this state, I feel the grand, geologic slowness of things. I feel the clay settling. I feel the resin hardening. I am a part of the stillness.

Then, the second state arrives. Hunger. It is not a thought; it is a sharpening of the world. It begins as a change in vibration. A quick, skittering tremor conducted through the resin wall, utterly alien to the slow, bass Hum of the makers. It is a Chitin-Breaker, a beetle of polished night, scavenging near my hollow.

My stillness changes. It is no longer passive. It becomes a weapon. My body, once a lens, is now a lens focused. The world shrinks to the path between my crevice and the sound. The glowing moss seems to flare, illuminating the beetle's iridescent carapace, a small, arrogant jewel moving in the gloom. I do not think about the Ghyrma, who cultivate whole ecosystems without malice. I do not think about the Pollen-Ghosts, who serve the orchids. My world has no Plan. It has only a target.

My stillness breaks. It is not a movement but an un-holding. I am the drop that finally falls. A blur of released tension. My tongue, a thread of liquid lightning, strikes. There is a faint, brittle *click*. The world rushes back in—the Hum, the scent of ozone and wet stems, the vast, quiet emptiness. The sharp thought of Hunger recedes, replaced by the full, slow feeling of digestion. The world has been made right.

I return to my hollow. I press my cool belly against the amber wall. I become Waiting again.

I watch the Ghyrma. I see them as no one else does. Not as brethren, but as forces. They are the season, the tide, the slow grinding of the world's gears. I see a young one, its carapace still thin and green-tinged, extruding a glistening thread of future-wall. It is not building; it is praying in a language of resin. I see an ancient one, a dark mound of hardened time, settle itself into a prepared alcove. Its personal vibration fades, sinking into the greater Hum like a stone into mud. It dies and becomes the city.

They are the great, slow We. Their hum is the thought of the mountain.

But I… I am an I.

A sliver of sharp, predatory, singular awareness living in the cracks of their collective dream. My life is a silent, urgent gasp between their slow breaths. They are becoming rock. They are becoming architecture. But what am I becoming? In this world where everything is in a state of gentle, inexorable transformation, perhaps I am an experiment. Perhaps the world, in its dreaming, grew tired of the slow hum and wanted to know the feeling of a lightning strike. The thrill of a singular, hungry nerve.

I am a Glass-Stalker. And I am what comes after the We. I am the first secret kept from the collective. I am the nerve that has learned to wait.
