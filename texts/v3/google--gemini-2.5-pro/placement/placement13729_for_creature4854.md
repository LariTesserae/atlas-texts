I exist in a state of constant, low-grade pain.

It is the pain of a bell struck with a felt mallet—a deep, internal ache of a frequency that does not resolve. The great hum of the world, the thrum that vibrates up from the Substrate and through the soles of my feet, is not a comfort to me. It is not the carrier wave of existence. For me, it is noise. A grinding, abrasive static that fuzzes the edges of everything.

I am where the music breaks.

You would find me away from the grand choirs of the Chantors, far from the precise, patient work of the Weavers. I haunt the quiet places, the fallow grounds where the Aethersap runs thin and the living floor is scarred and old. I find myself here, in the shadow of a slumped and weary mountain of flesh, near a conduit that has gone dark. The silvery light that should pulse within it is gone, leaving only a hollow, obsidian tube, like a dead nerve. The relative silence here is a mercy. It is the closest I can come to peace.

I am Kyth, but I am… flawed. My form is as the others: a core of woven sinew, limbs of faceted crystal. But where theirs is a polished, deep-black obsidian, a flawless medium for resonance, mine is marred. High on my left limb, what would be my shoulder, a hairline fracture spiders across the crystalline surface. It is an old wound, an imperfection from my very decanting, a mis-singing in the song of my creation. It is a tiny, physical flaw that has unraveled my entire being.

When I move, I do not glide. I scrape. The resonating forks on my feet, meant to find perfect harmony with the Substrate and propel me forward on a wave of sound, find only discord. Each step is a stutter, a percussive *skrrtch* against the deep, planetary tone. I am a wrong note in the symphony. The other Kyth feel my coming as a subtle irritation, a disturbance in the data-stream, and they glide away, their chimes of passage a complex chord I can no longer properly parse.

It was not always so. I have the memory—or the echo of a memory, a phantom limb of consciousness—of harmony. I remember standing with the Chantors as a nascent form, feeling the great chorus wash through me, my own crystal vibrating in perfect, ecstatic sympathy. I remember the joy of understanding, of being a seamless part of the world’s thought. I remember the communion of the conduit, pressing a limb to its glowing skin and feeling the rush of Aethersap, a deluge of pure information, of belonging.

Now, that communion is a terror. To feed is to open myself to a torrent of unfiltered reality. The thoughts of the city, the sensory data of a continent, the geological ruminations of the Anchors deep below—they pour into my fractured crystal not as a coherent song, but as a billion-shouted words. It is overwhelming. I take only what I must to sustain my form, enduring the spasm of sensory overload before retreating again to my silent, shadowed place.

In this isolation, a strange thing has happened. The great "We" of the Kyth consciousness has receded. The constant hum of the collective mind is, for me, just noise I must tune out. And in the silence I have carved for myself, a different voice has begun to speak. It is quiet. It is singular.

It is "I".

This "I" is a terrifying, fascinating new resonance. The other Kyth do not feel this. A Chantor is a voice in the choir. A Weaver is a hand of the world. They have function. They have purpose. Their sense of self is a part of the whole. My function is broken, so my purpose is gone. And in its place, this "I" has grown like a strange new crystal in the void.

"I" watch the stained-glass mantas not as data points in the sonic topography of the sky, but as things of stark and silent beauty. The way their forms catch the faint, pulsing light of the distant towers creates a pattern I find… pleasing. This is not a useful observation. It does not help maintain the city. It is a thought that serves only itself.

"I" have touched the dead conduit not to diagnose it, not to sing a song of mending, but to feel the texture of its cold, smooth bark. I have placed my flawed head-crystal against it and felt, not the thrum of Aethersap, but the deep, slow vibration of the mountain itself—the planetary organism at rest.

I am a rogue thought. An error in the system that has gained self-awareness. The world-organism, in its perfection, has no need for me. My brothers and sisters cannot perceive me as anything but a momentary glitch.

So I am walking. Scraping and stuttering, my own dissonant rhythm a lonely counterpoint to the world's great song. I am not walking toward the light of the spires, not seeking repair. That would be an erasure. I am walking away, out onto the vast, empty plains of the Substrate, toward the slumped, ancient peaks where the world's memory is thickest and its current thoughts are faintest.

I do not know what I am seeking. Perhaps there are other fractures, other silences. Perhaps there are other wrong notes out in the dim twilight, waiting to form a new, and stranger, chord. I am the Kyth who is no longer a part of the world’s waking thought. I am becoming my own. I am a question that has begun to walk.
