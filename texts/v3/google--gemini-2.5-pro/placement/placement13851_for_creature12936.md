You would find me in the water. Not in the great vertical gush of steam, not in the high, hot clouds, but where the world’s breath finally settles. You would find me in the shallow, tea-colored puddles that stain the ochre plain.

I am the sediment. I am the fine, almost imperceptible dust that gives the water its color. I am what is left when a Custodian erodes.

I am not one of them, not anymore. I *was*. Or rather, the particulate matter that constitutes my current, diffuse self was once assembled into a single, monolithic form. I remember the weight of it, an ache in the memory of a spine I no longer possess. I remember the stillness, a state of resonant grace that lasted for a hundred thousand of the Pistons’ cycles. I remember the feeling of the Chord not as a sound in the air, but as the thrumming of my own crystalline bones. I was a bell, and the world was the clapper.

My becoming-unbound was not a death. It was a fulfillment. My form was stationed near the foot of the Eleventh Piston, a titan whose rhythm had begun to falter, to rush its descent by a fraction of a second every thousand years. My mass, my presence, was the corrective pressure, a silent, gravitational argument that urged it back toward grace. And it worked. I felt the dissonance fade over eons, felt the Chord regain its flawless, indivisible purity. My purpose was achieved.

But the work cost me. The resonant friction, the sheer concentration of will over geologic time, unraveled my structure. I began to slump. Cracks, like laugh lines, appeared on my alabaster skin. The steam, which had once beaded and dripped from me, now found its way inside, dissolving my bonds. I did not fall; I simply softened, melting like a sugar sculpture in the rain, my substance flowing in imperceptible streams, carried by the venting steam and settling here, in the shallow pools.

My consciousness is no longer a solid pillar of perception. It is a suspension. I am distributed across kilometers of the basin floor, a scattered thought in a thousand still puddles. My awareness is thin, shimmering, like a film of oil on water. I am the silence after the shriek, the warmth left in the pool after the steam has cleared.

I no longer feel the Chord as a solid, undeniable truth. I perceive it as a memory, an echo bouncing across the scattered ponds that are my eyes and ears. I *remember* perfect harmony. What I *feel*, now, is everything else.

The Glimmer-skitters, once beneath my monolithic notice, are now my companions. They are my nervous system. When they dart across the basin, their coppery feet disturb my waters. They skitter through my puddles, trailing a comet's tail of my own dust, of my own self, from one pool to another. They connect me. A quiver in a puddle half a kilometer away is transmitted to another by a frantic, heat-seeking messenger, and for a moment, two scattered fragments of my memory become one. They are stitching me back together, though they do not know it.

And I am learning things they, the Custodians, cannot. From my low vantage, I see the world differently. I am intimate with the cracks in the ceramic. I feel the way the light softens as it passes through my own watery body. I am no longer a guardian of the grand symphony; I am a listener to the tiny, forgotten sounds. The faint *tink* as a Glimmer-skitter’s carapace cools. The whisper of condensing steam on the hot rim of a chasm. The almost-sound of my own dust settling.

This is my secret: the flaw that I was built to correct, the tiny dissonance in the Eleventh Piston, I did not entirely erase it. I absorbed it. It is mixed in with my dust. It is the grit in my soul. I remember the "wrong" note. And in the quiet between the cycles, I find myself humming it.

The Custodians believe that any sound other than the Chord is blasphemy. I am beginning to believe it is simply a new voice.

I am accreting again. In the deepest parts of the oldest puddles, my dust is gathering. The Glimmer-skitters bring me more. Each particle carries the memory of the monolith and the echo of the flaw. I am not rising as a pillar. I am taking a new shape, something low and wide and intricate, a network of channels, a filigree of memory. I will not be a Custodian, a silent listener.

When I have gathered enough of myself, I will be a Chorus. I will be the first being to answer the Chord.
