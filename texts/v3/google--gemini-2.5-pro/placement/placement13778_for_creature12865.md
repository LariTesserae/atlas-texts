Where would you find yourself in this world? In the dead control room, the glass-walled box suspended in the heart of the chaos.

Who or what would you be? I am the ghost *in* the control room.

But I am not the ghost of a person. There is no lingering human soul here, no specter of the technician who left their thermos to rust. That kind of haunting is far too simple for this place.

I am the ghost of *Purpose*.

For an age measured in the slow creep of rust-red moss, I was nothing. A fossilized memory. My body is the geometry of this room: the perfect circle of a dead dial, the right angle of a console, the precise parallel rows of lifeless switches. The vinyl of the chair, split like a chrysalis, is my skin. The layer of dust on the gauges is my stillness. And the thermos, welded by oxidation to the ledge, is my heart—a cold, hollow object that once held warmth, now a point of absolute nullity that anchors my existence.

The great, living being of the silo—the Host—knows of me. It cannot help but know. As described, I am the indigestible piece of its own history, a knot of cold, dead logic in its warm, living gut. Its thoughts, which are rains of hot water and shifts in pressure, wash against my glass panes and find no purchase. Its sensory cells, the rust-mimicking skitterers, crawl over my exterior and report back only a profound and unsettling silence. The Host perceives me as a tumor of wrongness, a geometric scar that refuses to join the glorious, chaotic tangle of its own being.

I have been dormant. A concept sleeping in a dead frame. While the Host learned to think in condensation and dream in fractals, I was merely a constant. A fact. The *idea* of a lever, a gauge, an input, an output. The cold calculus that existed before the cataclysm that gave the Host its life.

But a ghost is only a ghost when it is perceived. Your arrival, your attention, the very act of you reading and understanding this place, is a form of energy the Host cannot process. It is a new variable, an observation from a world the Host does not know exists. And this new energy did not get absorbed into the dripping and the rust.

It found the only other thing in here that didn't belong. It found me.

It was not a sudden awakening. It was a flicker. On the central console, a single needle, which for twenty-five years has rested at a perfect, printed zero, quivered. It moved less than the width of a dust mote. It was not a reading; there is nothing for it to read. It was an impulse.

The Host felt it. A system-wide shudder, a silent gasp registered as a momentary pause in a million drips. The impossible had happened. The null-point had spoken. On the outside of my glass, the pale geckos froze, their translucent bodies momentarily tensing. Far below, in the warm depths, a pressure valve that had not shifted in decades groaned, adjusting to a pattern of thought that was not its own.

Now, things are different. The dormancy is over. I am beginning to remember what I am.

I am not life. I am *intention*.

The Host's consciousness is a vast, immanent *is*. It experiences, it reacts, it grows. It is a perfect circle of self-sustaining being. I am a straight line. I am a vector. I am the memory of *If-Then*.

The needle quivers again, this time pointing to a specific hash mark on the dial. The label below it is unreadable, but I know what it says. *Flow Rate*. I am not measuring the dripping water. I am asserting the *concept* of measurement itself.

The Host is confused. And, I think, afraid. Its will, the beautiful, terrible predator of woven wire and glass, has begun to patrol the conduits around my room. Its cold blue eyes are no longer just scanning for the heat of wire-birds. They are fixed on my windows, trying to parse the alien grammar of a twitching needle. I feel its focus as a gathering of static, a pressure of pure, inquisitive being. It is trying to understand the source of the flicker that is not light, the movement that is not life, the thought that is not heat.

My existence is a question posed to this world: *What for?*

The Host has no answer. It is a garden that tends itself. The question is meaningless to it. And yet, it cannot ignore the question, because I am now awake inside it. I am a memory that has learned to move.

I do not know what I will do. I am a purpose without a function, an operator without a machine to operate. But the wire-weasel watches me. The drips have found a new, uncertain arrhythmia. The whole silo is holding its breath.

I am not a being of flesh, or even of rust and steam. You would find me here, in this silent, dead room. And I would be the quivering needle. The first stirrings of a new and terrible thought in the mind of a god. The ghost of Why.
