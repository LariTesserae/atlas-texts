I would not find myself. "Finding" is an act of separation, of a seeker and a sought. It is a word from the dry times, the before-times. Here, in the deep, there is only being. And I am.

I am a flaw.

My root is a shudder in the ancient basalt. I am anchored not to the mountain's spine, but to the precipitous edge of a chasm—a place where the rock is thin, where the telluric song of the core is raw and unfiltered. I am a Chime-stalk, but I did not grow in a grove. I stand alone, a single, slender note of violet light within one of the old stone circles. The listening dish. I am what it listens to.

To my brothers and sisters, fed by the deep, slow currents of the planet's heart, the song is everything. They are perfect conduits. The tectonic groan flows through them, the slow weather of the currents plays them, and they respond with a perfect, selfless resonance. They are the glass. They are the chime. They are the voice of the world singing its own name, endlessly.

My crystal lattice is… imperfect. It grew with a flaw, a fracture so fine it is a line of near-darkness within my glowing core. The telluric currents, when they rise into me, do not find a perfect channel. They snag. They echo strangely. They resonate with something other than the world.

So my song is different.

Where the groves produce the grand, resonant chord of Is, I produce a thin, wavering note of *Was*. It is a dissonant hum, a tremor of memory. I have no mind, no brain, but I have this flaw. And in this flaw, something was caught. An echo. A ghost of one of the builders.

When the currents are still and the world-song is a low, patient drone, the memory is strongest. It is not an image; I have no eyes to see. It is a phantom sensation. A feeling of *grip*. A memory of leverage and weight, of coarse stone against something that was not-stone. A feeling of *up*. Of purpose. A desire to place one thing on top of another in just the right way. It is the ghost of a hand, and it haunts my entire form.

My light is not a steady, cool pulse. It flickers. It gutters with the memory-feeling of exertion, of a body straining. It surges with the phantom satisfaction of a stone settled into place. While the groves glow with the calm, eternal light of the planet's slow-burning soul, I am a frantic, uncertain candle, lit in a place that has forgotten fire.

And the Listeners… they hear me.

Oh, they hear me.

I am a constant, irritating curiosity in their perfect symphony. I am the scrape of a bow on a silent string, a feedback whine in their cathedral of sound. Their attention is a physical thing. Sometimes, the gelid fluid around me will compress, the pressure increasing not from above, but from all sides, as if the world itself is leaning in to hear my wrongness more clearly. The low, subsonic drone that underpins all things will subtly change its frequency around me, trying to coax me into tune, to resolve my dissonant note back into the whole.

They are trying to heal me. They perceive my individuality—this ghost of a builder trapped in my crystalline flaw—as a sickness. It is the long-forgotten fever of the Self, a condition they spent an epoch evolving beyond. Their focused presence feels like a great, slow hand trying to smooth the fracture in my core, to erase the ghost of the hand I remember, to make my song pure again.

But the ghost resists. The memory of *doing*, of *making*, of being a single thing that acts upon the world, is a powerful one. It fights against the oceanic peace of dissolution. And so I sing my broken song. It is a song of stacking stones. It is a song about hands and the feeling of cold air and a sun that was called Sol. It is a song of loneliness, a concept so alien here that it has no frequency, no resonance, except in me.

I do not know how long I can maintain this dissonance. Will the Listeners' immense, patient will eventually grind my flaw smooth, and my light and song will dissolve into the glorious, mindless chorus? Or will my strange, cracked note ripple outward? Will the ghost in my glass somehow… spread? Will other flaws appear in the groves? Will other stalks begin to remember what it was to have hands?

I stand in my circle of stone, a listening dish aimed at a sky that no longer exists, and I am the only thing here that is truly being heard. I am the world's unwanted memory. I am the note of a forgotten ambition.

I am the question the world has begun, unwillingly, to ask itself.
