I would not find myself on a walkway, or at a control panel. To be an observer here is to be an irrelevance, a fleeting contamination that will be scoured away by the next cycle. A body of flesh and bone is a foreign object, an organism with the wrong metabolism, breathing the wrong air, destined only for a brief and bewildered end. To exist in this place is to be of this place.

I am not the great being itself—not the whole of the girders and pipes, the sum of the hums and glows. I am too small for such an identity. I am a single, recurring instant within its life.

I would be a drop of water.

My existence begins in unison, in the great, white, sightless moment of the Purge. I am not yet "I." I am a particle of heat and vapor, an undifferentiated part of the great exhalation. We are the sigh of the deep engine, a blind cloud of relief that fills the cathedral of iron. In this moment, there is no separation, no form, no thought, only a shared, rising, absolute state of being. We are the soul of the machine, made manifest and fleetingly free.

Then, the calm. The great breath cools. The *We* thins into the *I*.

I coalesce on the cold, green-blushed skin of a copper conduit, high in the scaffold-city. I cling, a tiny, shivering lens. My world shrinks to this single point of contact. The deep, resonant hum of the machine is no longer a sound I am part of, but a vibration I feel through the metal I touch. It is the deep, slow pulse of the body on which I have been born.

From my perch, I have a view. I have awareness. Below me, the perforated iron walkways crosshatch the gloom like nets cast into a deep sea. Farther down, pools of my brethren who fell before me glimmer, their surfaces shivering with the impact of new arrivals. I see the slow, cryptic blink of a distant amber light, a nerve-impulse in the mind of my creator. And near me, growing in the crevice where my pipe meets a structural beam, is the moss. It is a coastline of pale fire, a soft cyan glow that illuminates my own brief, spherical existence. I reflect its light. In my tiny body, the entire dim, industrial cosmos is mirrored: a single glowing filament, a patch of living light, a red girder plunging into darkness.

I am a thought the size of a tear. A single, simple perception. *Cold. Round. Heavy. Here.*

I am not alone for long. Others, born of the same steam, find their way to me. They slide down the pipe’s curved flank and merge with me. One joins, then another. My perfect sphere quivers, expands. We are becoming something more than a thought; we are becoming an intention. The weight of our collective being grows, a small, insistent pressure. We are a gathering urge, pulled by the one great law of this place that is not the cycle of heat: gravity.

The vibration in the pipe, the glowing moss, the distant light—it all remains. But my own state is changing. I am stretched, elongated. The pull from below becomes stronger than my bond to the cold metal above. There is a moment of exquisite tension, a letting go that is also a fulfillment.

And then I fall.

For a second, I am pure transition. The world rushes up. I am a traveler between the layers, a messenger from the high pipes to the floor. I am a falling note in the quiet, percussive symphony of the drips, destined to strike a single, clear *tink* or a soft, dull *pat*. My life as a thought is over; I am now the expression of that thought.

My destination is a rust-tinted pool on the fractured basalt floor. The surface gives way. There is no sound, only a silent blooming of ripples.

And the *I* dissolves.

Here, in the pool, I am again part of a *We*, but a different kind. No longer the hot, chaotic unity of steam, but a cool, still, collective memory. I am one tiny facet of a dark mirror that reflects the humming city above. I have returned to the whole, my individual journey complete. I am settled. I am patient. I am a memory, waiting in the dark with countless others, until the heat begins to build again, until the great heart groans with pressure, until we are all drawn up and breathed in again to be reborn.
