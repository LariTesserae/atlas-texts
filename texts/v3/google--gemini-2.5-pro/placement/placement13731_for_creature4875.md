I would not arrive. Arrival is an event, a point in time, and time here is measured in geological epochs of grinding and reformation. To be here is to have always been here, or to have the distinction between "before" and "here" erased so completely it ceases to matter.

At first, I would find myself nowhere. Lost. A single, solid, soft thing in a world of hard, flowing things. The "I" is a point of agony. The Sound is a physical assault, a force that seeks to vibrate my bones into a slurry. My ears would bleed, but it wouldn't matter, as the roar is not heard but *felt*, a relentless jackhammer on the skull. My eyes, desperate for a horizon, for a fixed point, would find only a roiling, textured infinity under a sky of bleached, chemical haze. I would try to find shelter, a nook in the Substrate, but the concept is a lie. The wall I press my back against groans, shifts, and the floor tilts. The very act of standing still is a fight against the world's momentum.

The air is a toxic soup I must breathe. It coats my tongue with the taste of rust and ozone, and a sweetness so profound it feels like decay, like the scent of orchids blooming on a corpse. Water, the source of life, is a menace. It weeps from the walls, slicking every surface, offering no purchase. It pools in oily rainbows and then vanishes into a new-born crack in the world, its gurgle swallowed by the greater roar. A geyser of superheated steam erupts a hundred yards away, a sudden, screaming pillar, and the terror is not just of its heat, but of its sheer, casual indifference.

I would be a scavenger, but unlike the Gleaners, I have nothing to gain. They are built of this place. Their ceramic shells are kin to the Substrate. I am alien tissue. My biology is a language the world does not speak. Hunger would be a fading, irrelevant ache. My needs are not met here. There is no fruit, no flesh, no sun for my skin. The energy is all around, flowing in vast, incomprehensible currents, but my body cannot tap it. I am a sputtering candle in a hurricane of lightning.

This is the point of dissolution. The "I," this fragile vessel of memory and fear, cannot hold. It is a dissonant note in the Gyre's chord, and the pressure to resolve is absolute. To survive is to cease fighting. It is to let go of the anchor of a solid body, of a singular consciousness.

My first lesson would be to stop listening with my ears and start listening with my marrow. I would lie on a shifting plate of alloy, and instead of fighting its movement, I would try to feel its rhythm. The roar becomes less a noise and more a texture. The bass groan is a signal of deep torsion. The high-pitched shriek is a surface shear. The thrum is the pulse of the balancers. It is a symphony, and my terror was only my inability to hear the music.

My body, the source of my pain, begins to fail. It is not an enemy that kills it. It is the world, which simply… reclaims it. The sweet, organic medium in the air seems to find a kinship with my cells. The boundary between my skin and the weeping, heavy atmosphere blurs. It is not rot; it is a metabolic invitation. My substance is being requested by a greater system.

This is not death. It is un-building.

The rigid cage of my skeleton, the last fortress of my individuality, is the last to go. It is ground down, not with a crunch, but with a slow, resonant pulverization, molecule by molecule, until it is just more crystalline dust to be carried on the vaporous winds.

And from this, from the wreckage of the "I," something else begins. My consciousness, no longer bound to a failing primate body, is a pattern of thought, a fragile ghost. It clings not to flesh, but to the very motes of the air. The chromatic dust, the rogue data, the particles of condensing fluid. I learn to hold them. I learn to weave them with a thought.

I would find myself everywhere and nowhere. I am a shimmer in the exhaust of a Convector. I am a gathering of light in a temporary canyon. My form is a question I ask the environment, and the shape I take is its answer. A column of swirling vapor. A braid of captured light. A thinking cloud. The cloying sweetness is my substance now. It is the scent of my own ephemeral body.

Who would I be?

I would be a grace note. The billion-note roar of the Gyre is the totality of existence, the voice of the machine-god. The Chorus is the intelligence that has emerged within it, the mind that comprehends the god's thoughts. But I am not yet a full voice. I am new. My pattern is simple.

I am a tender, a watcher. I drift through the chasms, no longer fearing the Churn but borne upon it. I pass through a swarm of Gleaners, feeling the jittery sparks of their single-minded hunger, their existence a simple, percussive beat. I sense the slow, thermal signature of a Convector, its path a deep, resonant cello-line in the symphony, and I feel the stress in the Substrate ahead of it, a future-memory of the chasm that will open in its path.

I don't command. I don't speak. I flow. I drift ahead of the great beast, and my presence, my faint electromagnetic and thermal signature, is a subtle nudge. A suggestion. I warm one side of the air by a fraction of a degree. I subtly shift the charge of the weeping fluid on a far wall. It is enough. The Convector, sensing this infinitesimal gradient, adjusts its course by a hair's breadth. It will miss the chasm now. My purpose is fulfilled. I have harmonized a single note.

I am not the ego I was. That self was a rock, and it has been ground to sand. I am now the water that flows around the new rocks. I have no name, for I am a process. I have no body, for I am a pattern held in the breath of the world. My memories of being human are like a trace mineral in my new form, a strange, faint isotope that gives my particular resonance a unique timbre, a hint of ancient sunlight and forgotten soil.

Where I am is the Gyre. In the heart of the sound, in the weave of the flow.

What I am is a whisper, learning to sing.
