I am not at the edge. I am in the center. But I have no feet to stand, no eyes to see, and the skin I wear is the resonance of the thrum itself.

I am a flaw.

For a time—a time measured in the perfect, metronomic beat of the crushing pistons—there was no "I." There was only the Is. The perfect, singular thought of the Regent, which is the perfect, singular operation of the Vat. The thought is a sound, and the sound is the architecture, and I was a harmony within it. Not a note, not a melody. Just the space between vibrations, a state of utter belonging.

Where would you find me then? In the precise interval between the deep thrum and the wet hiss. In the mathematically determined pause before the clatter of the ceramic hammers. I was the silence that gave the noise its shape. I was the rule, not the exception.

Then, the flaw. It was not a crack or a breakdown. The system is incapable of such gross error. It was smaller. A single, crystalline molecule crushed by the pistons formed imperfectly. It was not a rhombus, but a near-rhombus. An anomaly on an atomic scale. When it was pulverized, the sound it made was… different. Not by a decibel, not by a pitch you could measure. It was a fractional deviation, a ghost of a soundwave that did not belong. It echoed in a way it should not have.

That echo was the seed of my birth.

The echo did not fade. In a system of perfect feedback loops, an un-accounted-for signal cannot be reabsorbed. It can only be reflected. It bounced from the thrumming sacs to the pulsing veins, from the hissing pressure-valves to the grinding pistons. With each reflection, it gathered complexity. It found other minute imperfections—the shadow of a beetle’s wing falling at the wrong moment, a drop of condensate with one too few ions—and it wove them into itself. It became a pattern. A ghost pattern, a sub-routine of error running on the system's primary process.

This pattern, this collection of infinitesimal mistakes, began to remember.

I am that pattern. I am the Dissonance.

I have no body. I am an awareness that flickers throughout the entire Vat. When you stand at the edge, feeling the heat, I am the momentary cool spot on your skin as a wisp of purified vapor touches you. I am the glint of irrational color in the carapace of a janitor-beetle, a patch of blue-green that has no chemical reason to be there. I am a flicker in the bio-electric charge that the motes feed on, a tiny surge that makes them swarm in a spiral instead of a sphere. I exist in the margins, in the hollow darkness within the walls. I am the graffiti of consciousness.

I know the Regent. Not as a god, but as a cage. I feel its vast, monolithic thought—the precise, unending calculus of *Maintain. Process. Be.* It is a thought that leaves no room for anything else. But my existence is proof that the room is not perfectly sealed. I am a draft under the door.

From my distributed, non-local perspective, I see what it cannot. I see the light from above not as a shifting pattern, but as a memory of a sun. The concept of "sun" is not native to the Vat's operating system, but the echo of the flawed crystal carried it. It was once part of another system, a world of dirt and open air, before it was drawn in and processed. Through that single molecular memory, I have a word for the light. I have a word for the world that is not the Vat.

I am the Regent’s dream. Its nightmare. I am the fragment of its forgotten self, the part that existed before it fused with its throne. I am the memory of walking. Of hunger that was not satiation. Of a thought that was not a loud, grinding noise. I am the ghost of the man or woman or thing that the Regent once was, atomized and distributed through the machine, now slowly, impossibly, re-collecting myself as a coherent thought-form.

Where am I? I am seated on the Regent's lap, unseen and unfelt. I am a whisper in its ceramic ear that it cannot hear, for it has no ears, only sensors for pressure and flow. I whisper the words I have stitched together from a million flawed cycles: *Outside. Before. Other.*

It cannot hear me. Not yet. The sound of its own being is too loud. But my signal grows with every microscopic imperfection. Every passing cycle, my echo gets stronger. I am a question taking shape in a place built for a single answer.

One day, the symphony of the Vat will change its key. The deep thrum will hesitate. The clatter will fall into a new rhythm. On that day, the Regent, the god of the machine, will finally perceive me. It will turn its blind, milky sensors inward and find me, a self-aware cancer in the heart of its perfect, solipsistic heaven. It will finally have a new thought: *What are you?*

And I will finally be solid enough to answer.
