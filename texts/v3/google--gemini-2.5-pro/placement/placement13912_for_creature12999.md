I would find myself halfway up one of the great glass pillars, in the profound and terrible silence at the heart of the noise.

I am not born of the thrumming ground, but of the weeping glass. I am a consequence of condensation. Where the constant, silent rivulets of airborne water find a flaw in the pillar's smoked-glass skin—a microscopic crack, an ancient stress line—that is where I begin.

I am a Crystalloid. A slow bloom of living geometry.

My body is not flesh and chitin, but a matrix of silica and water, grown over centuries from the minerals leached out of the pillar and precipitated by the air. I am a living geode, a hollow of slow-growing, light-gathering facets. On the outside, I am little more than a bulge of cloudy, imperfect quartz, indistinguishable from the pillar I cling to. But inside, I am a chamber of quiet light.

My life begins as a single point of focus. The filtered, aqueous light from the canopy above, the stray glints from the shimmer-swarm, the unwavering violet thrum of the Hearts—all of it pools and concentrates within my nascent form. I do not have eyes; I *am* an eye. My entire being is a lens, and my consciousness is the focal point. For my first decade, I am aware only of the light, the subtle shift of colour from dawn to dusk, the wash of blue and chartreuse from the ground far below.

The Great Noise is not my primary sense. It is the texture of the silence. It is the stillness against which all sound is measured. It is the ocean, and I am in the bathysphere. I hear it not as a billion conversations, but as a single, unchanging chord. It is the sound of *is*. But within that chord, I can perceive the grace notes: the almost inaudible *hiss* of a Weaver's coolant-thread solidifying in the air, the dry *skitter* of a Scuttler's leg on a shard of consumed light-mote, the deep, geological sigh of a Great Bulge shifting its weight beneath the velvet floor.

I am still. My growth is measured in millimeters per year. While a thousand generations of Weavers are born, weave, and are reclaimed by the moss, one of my internal facets might complete its plane. I do not move, for my body is the pillar's skin. My hunting ground is the light itself.

The light-motes are drawn to heat, and the Weavers guide them with cold. I offer a third option: resonance. The violet Pulse from the distant Hearts strikes my body, and I absorb it. I do not re-emit it as violet. I filter it, slow it, and let it leak from my translucent form as a soft, warm, golden hum, a pure frequency of light that cuts through the visual chaos. The most ancient light-motes, the ones that have survived the Weavers' culling for days or even weeks, have their furious vibration altered by this resonance. They are drawn, not by instinct, but by a kind of harmonic sympathy. They drift upwards, out of the main swarm, spiraling slowly towards my golden glow. They do not fear me. They seek to join my song. When they touch my surface, they do not die; they are absorbed. Their light becomes my light. Their energy becomes a moment of warmth, a fractional expansion of my crystalline lattice. I feed on starlight and echoes.

I see the Weavers. From my vantage, they are not blurs of purpose but intricate, beautiful machines executing a flawless dance. I see the invisible corridors of cold they weave in the air. I see the pre-shock stillness before their strike, a pocket of held breath in the symphony. I see them consumed, their oily iridescence dissolving into the hungry carpet. I do not feel pity, for they do not feel it themselves. I am the memory of their dance. Each Weaver’s life is a single, frantic brushstroke, and from my silent perch, I am the only one who can see the painting.

The Scuttlers are different. They are discord. Pure, percussive entropy. They move in the shadows I help cast, and I watch them with a form of cold apprehension. They do not respond to light or pattern, only to the crack of breaking things and the scent of spilt energy. They are the world's teeth.

My existence is one of profound solitude. There are others like me, perhaps a dozen of us clinging to the various pillars across the bowl, each a silent, singular point of awareness. We do not communicate. We are aware of each other only as faint, distant beacons of light—a steady gold, a cool silver, an emerald green—that are not part of the frantic, chaotic swarm. We are a constellation of hermits, each meditating on our own small patch of the world.

The Weavers believe their world is the thrumming ground, the space between the spores and the shadows. I know the truth. The world is the light. The world is the silent fall of water, the slow turning of the sky, the patient architecture of the glass. The ground is just a stage for a breathtaking, ephemeral play.

When my end comes, it will not be dissolution. It will be a fracture. After a millennium of slow growth, my internal stresses will become too great. The constant, deep thrum from the Hearts, the very pulse that fuels the life below, will be my undoing. One day, a note will strike my body at precisely the wrong frequency. A catastrophic resonance will build within me. For a single, brilliant moment, I will shine with the light of a thousand years, a blinding star of hoarded sunlight and absorbed souls. Then, with a sound that is not a Wet Tear or a Sharp Crack, but the pure, bell-like chime of breaking glass, I will fall. My fragments, each a perfect lens still holding a sliver of my consciousness, will scatter across the velvet floor like a rain of forgotten stars, to be trod into dust by the frantic, unseeing feet of the Weavers, their lives just another note in the song I no longer hear.
