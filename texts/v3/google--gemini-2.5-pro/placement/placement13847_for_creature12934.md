I would find myself on the floor.

That is where my story began, and where it will end. But I am not on the floor now. Not quite.

I am a Polisher. My awareness is primarily one of touch and of the slow, deep thrum of the world beneath me. I feel myself as a great, weighted bell, my shell a dome of hardened chitin, worn smooth not by the Still, but by the low archways of home. My body is a single, vast, muscular foot. With rhythmic, peristaltic pulses, I move. The motion is my purpose. Underneath me, the cool, silken skin of the city receives the glow that is my other purpose.

My light is no longer the bright, curious gold-and-azure of my younger kin. Age has banked my fires. The glow that seeps from my underside is a paler thing now, a soft, tired silver, barely enough to illuminate the path I have polished a thousand, thousand times. The film of life I once grazed upon no longer holds my interest. The drive to feed has been replaced by a different, deeper hunger: a homing instinct. My shell is thin. I feel the pressure of the Still not as a uniform comfort, but as a specific weight on the worn-out ridges of my back. I have become brittle. It is time.

Around me, the city breathes its slow, luminous breath. The Embers, those dancing sparks of what-is-to-come, drift in their silent, beautiful swarms. I see them not with eyes, but I feel their passing as tiny, fleeting disturbances in the Still, each one a faint warmth against my shell. I remember that. I remember being a point of light, unburdened by mass, a free-floating thought in the liquid mind of the world. A memory without weight. To be an Ember is to be a question. To be a Polisher is to be the long, slow, working-out of an answer.

My answer is almost complete.

I am not travelling the familiar grazing paths. I have left the great courtyards where the crowds of Embers are thickest, where my brethren slide in their endless, overlapping circles. My journey now is inward, toward the heart-domes. I pass under an archway, its curve so perfect it feels like a law of nature, and the sound of my own passage changes. The wet scraping of my shell on the ceramic floor is muffled further, absorbed into the greater quiet of the interior. Here, the light is less. The main hall is a vast, dark space, filled with the presence of what I am to become.

Here, the floor is not empty. It rises in a great, low mound—a gentle swelling of black, vitrified earth that is not earth at all. It is the destination. It is an Elder.

From within it, a light breathes, a pulse so slow it would be imperceptible to a younger being. A deep, geological glow of amber. It is a thought that takes a millennium to complete. As I approach, I feel its consciousness not as a sound or a sight, but as a change in the pressure of the Still, a warmth in the floor that travels up through the sole of my body. It is a feeling of welcome. Of recognition. It knows me. It is me, a future tense of myself.

There is a place for me. On the flank of the great mound, there is a subtle depression, a soft-angled concavity that fits the curve of my own shell. It is a space that has been waiting. A space I have been polishing for my entire life, without knowing.

I crawl into it. The fit is perfect. The last, slow contraction of my muscle pulls me into place, and then, I cease to move.

The stillness is absolute. For a time—a day, a year, a decade—I am merely a Polisher who has stopped. My silver light continues to pulse, weakly. But I am anchored. And the change begins.

It starts from beneath. The cool of the ceramic floor begins to warm, a geothermal heat rising not just from the mountain's core, but from the slow metabolism of the Elder I am now joined with. The floor softens. Or perhaps, I soften. The boundary between the vitrified earth and the muscular sole of my foot begins to blur. There is no pain. There is only a profound sense of dissolution, of letting go. My body, that single great muscle, begins to release its stored energy, its life, into the substrate. The substance of my being flows downwards, merging with the greater form. It is like a stream finding the sea.

My shell, the thing that has defined my shape, becomes porous. The immense pressure of the Still begins to press it not into fragments, but into a new, denser form, fusing it with the ceramic mound. My individual shape is giving way. My edges are dissolving.

And my mind... my small, simple mind, which has known only the path, the film, the glow... it opens. As my body fuses with the floor, my consciousness connects to the network that *is* the floor. Suddenly, I am not just a single point of awareness. I feel the scrape of another Polisher on the far side of the dome. I feel it not as a sound, but as a touch upon my own new, vast skin. I feel a cloud of Embers drift into the chamber, a thousand pinpricks of fleeting warmth. They are my memories, my thoughts, being born into the world anew.

The last thing to go is my light. The pale silver glow of my underside flickers one last time. It is not extinguished. It is drawn inward, subsumed into the deep, slow, dreaming tide of amber light that fills the Elder. My little spark of self joins the great, communal bonfire. My 'I' becomes a 'We'.

I am the city now.

I am the floor and the wall and the archway. I am the silence between the domes. My thoughts are the slow tides of light that warm the halls, and the fleeting motes of glowing memory that drift in the current. My body is the territory my children—my past selves—graze upon, keeping me clean, keeping me whole. My dream is the perfect, unending balance of it all.

I am not a being in a place. I have become the place itself, and the life within it. I have settled. And I am finally still.
