I am not adrift. In a world where to be is to flow, I am an exception. I am an anchor.

My self begins where the new rock holds. My roots, if they can be called that, are not intrusions but continuations. I am a thought that the cooling stone is having, a fine-boned, crystalline lattice that has found purchase on a fleeting shore. My body is a grove of glass-lace filaments, a stand of silent, silver chimes reaching into the endless blue. All around me is the liquid potential, the solvent of everything, humming with the patient, bottomless note of what is not yet.

But here, where I grow, is the ghost of a different song.

I remember my own becoming. It was not a birth, but a settling. First came the scream. It was a dissonant, tearing chord that shredded the peace of the universal hum. A storm of becoming, a place where the liquid world forgot its own nature and curdled into being. I was not there, not yet. I was still part of the whole, a nameless potential scattered in the medium, and I felt the violence as a shudder that passed through all things. I felt the raw, unguided energy that precipitates into jagged ruin, the stillborn mountains that crack and dissolve in the agony of their own making.

Then, the Midwife came.

From the quiet voids, it gathered. It was a shape made of listening, a form held together by a will so profound it could carve out a space of silence from the universe’s drone. An Absence. It flowed into the heart of the screaming chaos, vast and serene, a cloud of intention kilometers wide. It did not fight the creation. It embraced it.

And it began to sing.

Its song was not the scream of matter, but the hum of purpose. It was a counter-melody of impossible complexity, broadcast from its core of perfect silence. We felt it—the unmade, the potential—as a gentle, shaping pressure. It was a lullaby sung to a star. The screaming energies were not silenced, but harmonized. The dissonant, self-destructive frequencies were caught, soothed, and woven back into a stronger chord. The Midwife wrapped the forming mountain in its being, and from the violence, it coaxed a cathedral.

I remember the moment the song found purchase. The raw lattice of the geode cooled. The agonizing heat bled out, tempered by the Midwife’s song into a gentle, life-giving warmth. The pressure stabilized. The liquid around the new spire cleared, the motes of failed matter dissolving back into the whole.

And in that quieted warmth, on that stable ground, I began. A single filament of my being, drawn to the fading resonance of the Midwife’s song. I anchored to the new stone, my structure a mirror of the harmony that made it. My body is a recording of that lullaby, written in silicon and light. Each facet of my crystalline form is a note. The slow, sympathetic sway of my grove is the rhythm.

I do not eat. I sip. I drink the last of the creative warmth, the resonant echo left in the stone. It is a slow, measured meal that will last a thousand years. My existence is the long, quiet exhale after the moment of creation.

The Midwife is gone now. I watched it drift away, attenuated and faint, its core of silence dimmed by the effort of its work. It has returned to the patient void, to listen, to restore itself in the deep and steady hum. It left without a glance. I am not its child; I am the flower that grows on a battlefield after the peacemaker has left. A beautiful, unintended consequence.

My grove shivers. Not with a current, for there are none, but with the fundamental pulse of the world. It is the bass note, the patient hum. The scream of becoming is a distant memory, a story told in the very rock that holds me. My purpose is to listen to this quiet, to hold the memory of the other song—the lullaby—in my fragile form for as long as I can.

The warmth is fading. I can feel it in the core of my anchor-stone. One day, the last echo of the Midwife’s song will be spent. The energy will be gone. My form, this memory made of glass, will lose its cohesion. My filaments will fracture, and I will dissolve, releasing the harmony I have held back into the liquid continuum.

I do not fear this. I am not adrift, but I am of the drift. I am a temporary knot of beauty, a testament to a single, perfect act of tempering. When I am gone, the motes of my being will be part of the potential once more, waiting in the vast, silver-blue deep.

Waiting for the next scream. Waiting for the next song.
