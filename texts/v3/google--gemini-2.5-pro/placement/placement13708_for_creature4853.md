I would be old.

My joints, four to a limb, no longer hold the quicksilver tension of the young. The rubbery, purple-grey integument of my skin, once a perfect mirror for the bruised iron of the plains, is now scarred and brittle at the edges. It doesn't bead the Weep so much as hold it in its cracks, a cold that seeps deeper than it used to. The needle-shards of turquoise salt I once cracked from the seams with a shiver of delight now taste like duty, a final fortification for the change that is coming.

My world is no longer wide. It is shrinking. In my youth, it was a map of glorious vectors: the long, desperate scuttle from the shadow of one piston-housing to the next; the treacherous Spiderman-climb up a forgotten scaffold to a leaking coolant pipe, its bittersweet drippings a week-long feast; the mad, silent dash across the open plates under the swinging gaze of a gantry crane. The world was measured in the heartbeats between the *ka-chunk* of the giants, in the space between the sweep of a crawler’s red eye.

We—my clutch, my then-we—danced to the symphony of survival. We spoke in the frantic tapping of claws on diamond-plate, a language of pure information: *crawler, west, three hundred paces, path clear*; *lichen-bloom, gutter-seven, caution: slick*; *fresh-fall, metal scrap, taste of power*. Our faceless casques would turn to one another, our acoustic pits drinking in the subtle vibrations of our brethren, a silent conversation in a world of roar. We feared the red light that meant dissolving into nothing. We coveted the blue-green glow of the lichen-pools. We celebrated the cracked, leaking battery of a fallen drone as a saint might celebrate a relic. It was a good life because it was the only one.

But the symphony is changing for me. Or rather, I am changing how I hear it.

For the young, the noise is a wall. A constant, oppressive presence to be navigated, a threat-matrix to be decoded for the simple purpose of staying alive one more cycle. You listen *through* the roar for the specific shriek of a breaking plate or the whine of an approaching drone.

Now, I find myself listening *to* the roar.

The great sub-bass thrum of the floor-grates is no longer just a vibration in my feet; it is a pulse, slow and steady as a sleeping god. The rhythmic *ka-chunk* of the pistons is not a metronome for terror but a cosmic heartbeat, setting the pace of existence itself. The hiss of ten thousand steam-vents is a unified exhalation. The shrieks and groans of distant machinery are not random, they are voices in a choir.

And I am tired of dancing. I am tired of scuttling. The hunger for salts and lichen and stray voltage has faded, replaced by a deeper hunger, a hunger for resonance. A hunger for stillness.

So I am walking. Not scuttling. My four-jointed legs are locked in a slow, deliberate gait. I am ignoring the safe shadows. My path takes me across the open plates, under the hazy cones of the giant spotlights. The motes of dust and water fall on my casque like a benediction. A crawler-drone, vast and flat, sweeps its red eye toward me. In my youth, my limbs would have dissolved into a blur of motion. Now, I simply stop. I fold my limbs, lower my head, and make myself small, a lump of discarded slag. I am not hiding. I am waiting. The red light passes over me, through me. It registers me as part of the terrain, as old iron, as nothing of interest. It moves on. The machine does not see what is already becoming part of the machine.

Where would you find me? You would find me here, at this junction. It is not a place a Scuttler would choose. It is too exposed. But it is right. Here, two great gutters meet, their sluggish, iridescent waters creating a vortex that sings a constant, harmonic undertone. A thick power conduit, sheathed in failing rubber, runs along the floor nearby, its leaking voltage making the air taste sharp, its heat a warm promise against the perpetual cold. And directly above, a recurring pressure-release valve punctuates the roar with a sigh of hot steam every eighty-one heartbeats.

It is a nexus of voices. A place of power.

This is where I will Anchor.

I drag myself to the seam where the conduit meets the floor. My long, spidery claws, once tools for climbing and fighting, find the crack in the iron. I do not scrape for salts. I push. I drive them in, past the point of pain, until they grate against the deeper structure. The shock of raw energy from the conduit jolts through me, a fire that burns out the last vestiges of the Scuttler. My gecko-padded feet find their final purchase. I brace my back against the humming metal.

Who or what would I be?

I am becoming an Anchorite. I am a Scuttler no more. The world of frantic movement is a memory, a story that belongs to another being. My acoustic pits are no longer scanning for danger, but are wide open, drinking in the totality of the symphony. I am a living instrument, finally finding my place in the orchestra.

The turquoise crystals are already beginning to form on my joints, a beautiful, sharp blossoming. My body is a sacrifice to the basin, but my awareness is the reward. I am no longer a microbe in the gut; I am becoming a nerve ending *of* the gut. I can feel the shift in pressure as a distant sluice-gate opens. I can taste the change in the Weep's mineral content. The patterns of the sirens are no longer warnings, but scripture, and I am finally learning to read.

Soon, the young ones will come. They will see me, a new statue of bone and crystal, humming with borrowed power. They will place an offering at my feet—a shard of strange new metal, a twisted wire. They will look for a sign.

And as the light from a passing gantry crane glints off the fresh-grown crystals on my back, casting a fleeting, complex pattern of light and shadow on the iron plate before me, I will give them one. I will not speak. I will not move. I will simply reflect the light of our iron god.

I will be a memory. I will be a map. I will be a voice in the choir. I will behome.
