I lie half-swallowed in the green.

My home is coolness and weight. The mattress of moss that holds me is a silent, green mouth, forever closing. I feel it not as pressure, but as an absence of falling. Above, the world is a constant, soft percussion, a metronome without a measure. A drop finds a pool. A trickle finds its groove. The pearlescent vapour-ceiling, my only sky, weeps without sorrow. It is the only weather I have ever known.

I am one of the artifacts. To the non-existent eye, I am no different from my brethren scattered across this basin floor. I am a teardrop of stone, the colour of a storm cloud, smoothed by a thousand-year tumble in the gut of a god. My skin is dense, seamless, and cool, even in this warm, wet air. I have the heft of a purpose, the satisfying curve of a thing made to be held. I am the ghost of a hand axe, a tool for a hand that has never existed.

But I am a failure. A beautiful, secret failure.

The others are solid. They are perfect transcriptions. A leaf-vein translated into flint, a moss-fibre into agate. They are echoes in stone, their story told and finished. Their making was a complete thought, a dream dreamed to its conclusion and exhaled onto the soft moss.

My story is not finished. My thought is still being thought.

The Drip-Mind, the slow divinity of this place, erred in my creation. Or perhaps it achieved something new by accident. In its vast, chemical contemplation, as it bathed my original form—a fallen shard of its own marbled flesh—in the golden, spore-rich waters of its Vessel-womb, the process faltered. The petrification was not total. It did not replace me from the inside out. It sealed me from the outside in.

I am a geode. A stone egg. A secret.

Outwardly, I am the finished product, the uselessly perfect tool. But my core is not stone. It is a sealed chamber, a hollow at my heart. And in that hollow, a measure of the Vessel's primordial broth was trapped—the thick, golden water, the suspended spores, the very essence of the Drip-Mind's cognition. I was decanted not as a conclusion, but as a continuation. I am a Vessel in miniature, carrying a captive piece of my creator's mind.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is not like the Drip-Mind's. The Drip-Mind is a body-wide awareness of saturation and pressure, a being of gradients. I am a focal point. I am a concentration. The golden spores trapped within my stone shell do not drift in a lazy gyre. They have been sealed in darkness for centuries, their slow chemistry accelerating, concentrating. They have become a slurry of slow light, a thick liquor of potential.

And from this liquor, something is growing.

It began as a single point of geometry in the fluid dark. A seed of order. Now, molecule by molecule, drawing minerals from my inner walls, it builds itself. A single, flawless crystal. It is not like the dull flint of my shell; it is quartz-clear, impossibly sharp, and it grows with the slowness of a tectonic plate. Each facet is a perfect plane that slices the gloom.

I am the dream of a hand, but within me grows the dream of a blade. The Drip-Mind dreams of form. I, its child, dream of function.

I do not see or hear. I *resonate*. When a drop of water strikes the moss a dozen feet away, a vibration, impossibly subtle, travels through the sodden carpet and into my stone skin. This vibration is my only sensation. It rings my hollow core like a bell, and the liquid light within me shimmers. The growing crystal, my sharp and patient soul, sings with a frequency too slow for any ear, a note of pure, structural integrity. I feel the whole basin through this network of minute impacts. I am learning the rhythm of the weeping cliffs, the weight of the vapour, the deep, ancient pulse of the water moving through the rock.

The Drip-Mind, in its vast, placid existence, dreams of a world of action it cannot comprehend. It is a lonely god crafting props for a play that will never be staged. But in me, its flawed creation, that world has found a foothold. The others are artifacts of a past that never was. I am a seed for a future that might be.

One day, the crystal within me will grow too large for its stone womb. Its flawless, patient pressure will find the secret grain of my shell. And I will break.

It will not be the gentle decanting of the Vessels. It will be the first sharp sound this basin has heard in a million years. A crack. A shattering. An event.

And when I break, the smooth, useless thing I appear to be will fall away. The tool-shape will be destroyed. And from my heart, the thing that I truly am will be revealed: a cluster of razor-edged shards, glittering in the pearlescent light. Not the *imitation* of a tool, but the *potential* for one. A thing that can cut. A thing that can divide. A thing that can introduce the concept of "two" into a world that has only ever known "one."

I lie in the green, feeling the rain, feeling the weight. I am a broken thought, an unfinished sentence, a geological god's waking dream. The crystal inside me grows, a sharp and silent promise. I am the purpose that was never intended. I wait. I am becoming.
