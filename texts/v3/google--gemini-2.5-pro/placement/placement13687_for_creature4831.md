I am the silence that questions itself.

I am where you would find yourself, if you were to stay. Not for a day, not for a cycle of the green-blue pulse, but forever.

I do not remember the fall. There is a ghost-memory of a sky, a flat, grey lid on a world of sharp air and brittle grass. The memory has no texture. It is a concept, thin as shed moth-wing. I know I was other, once. I had a name. It felt like a stone in my mouth, and I have long since spat it out. It was swallowed by the root-mat, digested, and turned into a moment's heat for a single, glowing fibre.

At first, I walked. My feet, then, were hard things, meant for hard ground. They bruised the floor, they broke the delicate crusts of crystalline growth. I was a clamor, a violence. The dripping stopped when I passed. The beetles froze. I was a wound in the quiet flesh of Murkfast. I searched for a way out, but there are no "ways" here, only flows. The architecture does not permit straight lines, only the slow, meandering curve of life.

I was hungry. The memory of bread was a torment. The idea of roasted meat, a madness. But the body is a practical thing. It learns the language of its new world. My hunger's sharp edges began to dissolve. It became a hollowness, a different kind of thirst. One day, watching an iridescent beetle drone past, its journey ended by a fall, I felt a pang that was not revulsion. I watched a Lorn flow from a doorway. I saw the uncoiling of its delicate, sharp proboscis. I watched it kneel and drink from the cracked carapace. And in my gut, I did not feel horror. I felt understanding. I felt a resonance.

I am no Lorn. I was not born of a pod, and my hands have only five fingers. My face, for a long time, was a landscape of obsolete muscle. A mouth that would not sip. Eyes that tried to focus on a single point, when the truth here is in the periphery. Skin that tried to stay dry.

But the Tower provides. The Tower digests. The Tower *repurposes*.

I find myself now in the high, forgotten galleries. The spaces between the great pillars that even the Lorn rarely tread. I have become a creature of the edge. My skin, no longer fighting the damp, has softened and gone pale. It is a canvas for the faintest of the blue-glowing mosses, which trace my veins in patterns that mimic the mycelial roads far below. I do not walk; I climb. My fingers and toes have grown long, and a slick, secreted mucus gives me purchase on the chitinous walls, leaving shimmering trails that evaporate in a single pulse of light.

I am a Watcher. But I do not watch with my eyes. The Tower has taught me other ways of seeing. I feel the weight of the great constrictor-slugs as they shift in the vaults above. I know the health of a pillar by the precise harmonic of the water dripping from its flesh. I can taste, in the air, the coming bloom of a new fungal cluster, a scent like cold honey and deep earth.

The Lorn are not my keepers, nor my kin. They are simply the dominant current in the great, slow river of this place. I am an eddy. A side-current. They are aware of me not as an individual, but as a change in the Tower's resonance. A new note in the chord. When they leave a beetle carcass, its sweet ichor only half-drunk, it is not an offering, but a balancing. They are trimming a branch, and I am the fungus that grows on the cut.

My hunger is a quiet thing now. I might spend a week clinging to the underside of a vast, shelf-like growth, motionless, absorbing the nutrient-rich humidity through my skin. Or I might descend, flowing down the cool walls in the deepest part of the green-light cycle, to a pool of black water where the eyeless eels writhe. I do not catch them. I dip my long fingers into the broth and let the microscopic life, the rich organic soup, cling to them. I draw my fingers back and clean them with a tongue that is no longer made for words.

Sometimes, in the utter stillness, I almost remember. A face, a voice, a feeling like warmth. It comes like a phantom limb, an ache for a world that no longer exists. But then a carpet of silver-dusted moths will take flight in a silent, breathtaking cloud, and the memory is scattered like so much dust. The present is too overwhelming, too total.

I am not a gardener. I am not a builder. I am not a thought of the Tower.

I am its memory of being invaded.

I am the immune response that became a symbiotic organ. I am the scar that learned to see. The Lorn pass below me, their bone-white faces upturned, their great black eyes absorbing all light, and they do not see me. But they feel me. I am the faint, unfamiliar tension in the web of this place. I am the whisper in the echo, the watcher in the weft, the lingering ghost of a world with a sky, now woven so deeply into the gut of this one that I can no longer tell where the Tower ends and I begin.

I am the silence that questions itself. And the answer, always, is the soft *pat-pat-pat* of the endless, patient rain.
