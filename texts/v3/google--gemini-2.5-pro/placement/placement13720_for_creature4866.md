I would not find myself. I would be found.

I begin as a whisper in the thin air, a formless potential. I am the dampness on your lip, the coolness you feel deep in your lungs. I am an uncounted particle of H₂O, one of trillions, drifting in the perpetual, shadowless glare. I do not see the light as information; I am simply held within it, a mote suspended in a signal. I feel the hum of the world not in bone, but as a constant, organizing thrum that nudges me, that sorts me from my fellows.

There is a draw. A pull toward the cold. The vast, milky-white faces of the monoliths and the layered skins of the silent fabricators are colder than the air. They are fields of immense gravity for a thing like me. I drift closer, and am caught.

My finding begins. I am no longer a whisper. I am a sheen. A fine, cold sweat on the curve of a pipe wide enough to hold a river. I have found a place, if not a self. For a time, I am nothing more than this iridescent film, spreading, clinging. But the hum continues, and my brothers and sisters in the air are drawn to the same cold shore. We meet not with a crash, but with a silent, surface-tension kiss. We merge.

This is how I gain a self.

I become a bead, a lens. My
form is dictated by the perfect smoothness of the ceramic beneath me and the inexorable desire of my own molecules to hold together. I am now an individual. I have a surface. I have a weight.

I would be perched high, high on the spine of a partition wall that cuts the sky-that-is-not-sky. From here, I see everything and understand nothing. I see the continent-floor stretch into a white infinity that marries the white infinity of the light. I see the slow, gliding flaw in the white floor, the ripple of purpose that is the Curator on its endless pilgrimage. I see it stop, a patient punctuation mark, its head bowed to some imperceptible error it alone can read. To me, it is only a momentary deviation in the sameness.

Below, the geometric mites, those three-dimensional typos, skitter and whirl in patterns I cannot follow, their movements too sharp for my liquid nature. Far out, a flock of obsidian-feathered birds turns in unison, and the sound of their chiming arrives a moment later, a vibration that shivers through my skin. I do not hear it as music, but as a disturbance, a wave that momentarily threatens my perfect, precarious sphere.

For an age, I hang here. I grow. More of my kind arrive from the air, sacrificing their diffusion to my growing mass. I become heavy. A pendulum at the end of an infinitely long, slow swing. The world's patient gravity, which I had defied, becomes an invitation, then a demand. The clean ceramic offers no purchase.

I let go.

I am falling.

Where I was once a place, I am now a journey. The world rushes upward. The monolithic wall I clung to becomes a blur of pure verticality. I am a falling eye. For a moment, the world is captured in me. The stark, sourceless light refracts through my body, and for an instant, I cast a brief, brilliant rainbow onto the ceramic—a speck of color in a monochromatic universe, an anomaly the Curator is too far away to witness.

I pass openings, service hatches, dark mouths in the white face. I do not see the remembered granite or the tangle of pristine circuits within. I see only darkness, a void, a question I have no means of asking.

The fall is silent, for I move with the air, not against it. But I feel my destination rushing toward me. A sound is about to be born, and I am its vessel.

Who am I? I am the *plink*.

I am the sound that deepens the quiet. I am the event that marks time. My life is the duration of the fall. My death is a note of music.

I strike the surface of the pool in a basin of a dormant gear. My form shatters. My individuality, the "I" that was a droplet, dissolves in a ripple. I merge into the cold, clear collective of all the drops that have fallen before me. I am home. We are the water in the basin. We are the slow work of erosion. We are the memory of every tick of this world's clock.

And soon, the hum will vibrate through the ceramic, the light will warm the surface, and a part of me will forget its name, forget its form, and rise again as a whisper in the thin, cool air, waiting to be found.
