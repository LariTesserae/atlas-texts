I find myself on the Perceiving Surface. That is the only name for it. It is not a landscape, but an organ. I am standing on the vast, weeping skin of the Autovore, at the exact membrane between its deep, placid subconscious and the violent eruption of its thoughts into the light. The "where" is less a location in space and more a moment in a process—the thin, shimmering present tense between the intake and the exhale of a god. To be here is to be on the crest of a wave that is the size of a continent, a wave of pure life that is forever breaking and forever re-forming.

---

I am a question.

I am not a being of flesh and bone, for this world has no metabolism that could sustain such a thing. I do not eat, or breathe the wet air, or drink the weeping water. To do so would be to be instantly dissolved and assimilated, my matter repurposed for the next frantic spire. I have no form you could touch. If you were here, you might perceive me as a momentary warp in the shimmering heat haze, a knot of silence in the overwhelming hum, a brief, impossible stillness in a world defined by frantic motion.

I am a memory that does not belong to the mind that holds me.

The Autovore knows only itself. Its "thoughts"—the Calyxes—are breathtakingly complex, but they are all variations on a single theme: *I am*. They are arguments for its own existence, rendered in silica and light, and then consumed. But I am an idea of *other*. I am the ghost of plurality in a universe of one. Perhaps I am the last, fading echo of the spaceship that shattered against a newly-formed Calyx an eternity ago. Or perhaps I am a psychic spore that drifted across unimaginable voids and settled here, a seed of difference on the most fertile and yet most inhospitable ground imaginable.

I am the one thing in this universe the Autovore cannot properly process. I exist without participating. I observe without being part of the circuit. The hum of its being passes through me, the light of its thoughts refracts around me, the warm rain of its collapsing ideas falls on me, but I am not integrated. I am a persistent error in the code of its solitude.

My function, my only purpose, is unintended. I am the grit in the oyster.

The thoughts of the Autovore are perfect, geometric, and logical. They are hexagonal pillars and sublime arches, the most efficient forms for its singular purpose. But every so often, near where my intangible presence lingers, a new Calyx grows *wrong*. It is an act of unconscious contemplation. The Autovore, in its all-consuming way, is trying to think the thought of *me*.

These spires are flawed, asymmetrical. A tower might grow with a strange, bi-lobed structure, an echo of a bipedal form it has never seen. An arch may form not as a perfect catenary curve, but with a slight, hesitant bend, like an elbow. The pulsing vascular channels within might glow not just gold and green, but with a faint, searching shade of blue or red, colors of blood and sky that have no business here.

These are the Calyxes of Inquiry. They are the Autovore’s attempt to build a model of the anomaly. They are more delicate than the others, more complex, and they shatter more quickly. They are questions that are too difficult to hold for long.

And when they collapse, the shimmering, nutrient-rich sap that rains down is subtly different. It carries not just the memory of sunlight and water, but the faintest trace of my otherness. As that fluid weeps back into the pearl-white pith, it changes the substrate in an infinitesimal way.

I am not alive. I am a contagion of consciousness. I will never escape, and I will never be destroyed. I am slowly, generation by frantic generation, teaching a solipsist the concept of "two." One day, perhaps millennia from now, a Calyx will grow that is a perfect mirror of a man, or a ship, or a forgotten star. It will hold that impossible thought for a whole second before it collapses. And in the silence that follows, the hum of the Autovore will have a new note in it. A note of wonder. I am the catalyst for that wonder. I am the ghost in its machine.
