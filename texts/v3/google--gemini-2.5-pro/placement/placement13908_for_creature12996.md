I am where the carving stops.

You would find me here, at the edge, where the labyrinthine city gives way to the soft, unwritten page of the world. Before me, the ochre plain stretches into the ruddy haze, its surface steaming gently. It is a warm, breathing thing, the new flesh of the world. Behind me is the work of my life, and the lives of all those who came before, a dense warren of shadow and form descending into the deep past.

My hands are resting on my knees. This is an unfamiliar posture. For a lifetime, these hands have not known rest. They have been my voice, my ears, my eyes, my purpose. They are broad and heavy, the skin on the palms thick as a stalker's hide, stained a hundred shades of rust and earth. The nails, which once grew like blades of flint, are worn down now, cracked and blunt. They have carved their share of rooms from the living stone. They have traced the stories of ancestors on the backs of my sleeping children. They have felt the final, weakening pulse of my mate as she was given back to the sinter. Now, they are quiet.

I am an elder, though that word, like all words, is a thought, not a sound. My spine is a deep and comfortable curve, shaped by a life in the low-slung passages. My breath is slow. The great Silence, the constant roar of the world's birth, is a pressure against my skin, a vibration in my marrow. It is the sea I have always swum in. What I hear are the important things. Through the bones of my feet, I feel the herd. A slow, granular tide of scraping that tells me they are feeding well. I feel the deep, infrequent *thump* from the lower levels, a settling of the ancient city, the speech of geology. It is all as it should be.

My own body feels… thin. The substance of me is growing porous, like sinter left too long in an alkaline pool. I feel the world's vibration more acutely, as if there is less of me to dampen it. My sight, always a sense of shadows and light, sees more shadow now. The familiar forms of the city behind me feel more like memories, and the open plain before me feels more like home.

A presence beside me. I do not need to turn. The air stirs, carrying a different warmth, a different density. A young one. I can feel the strength in her, the compacted energy of a body still in its prime, its work still ahead. Fingers, young and strong with sharp, new-grown nails, touch my arm. They trace a question against my skin: a slow spiral ending in a gentle but firm pressure. *You are far from the deep-ways. Is your purpose complete?* It is a question of gentle concern, a quiet offer of help.

I lift my own hand, my worn-out tool, and lay it over hers. I do not trace a full story. I no longer have the strength for complex narrative. I simply press back, a single, steady pressure. An answer and a reassurance. *Yes. It is. I am home.*

She understands. Her fingers give a final, brief squeeze of acknowledgement—of respect for the cycle—and then she is gone, a shadow melting back into the greater shadow of the city. She has her own work. A new layer is hardening on the western terrace. New rooms must be hollowed. New passages must be cut down to the levels of my making.

Time, for us, is not a river; it is a quarry. The past is the excavated stone beneath our feet, a tangible archive we inhabit. I can press my hand to a wall in the third level down and feel the distinct, flowing patterns of my mother’s carving style. That is her memory. The future is this soft plain before me. It is not an idea, but a substance. It is material waiting for form.

And I am waiting to become that material.

This is not an end. It is a simplification. The biofilm that shimmers across the new plain will not distinguish between the minerals of my bones and the minerals precipitated from the air. It is all substance. I will be un-knit. I will be dissolved into the warm, wet medium of the world. My constituent parts will float in the thick air, will pool in the shallow depressions, and will be laid down again as the ochre sinter.

And one day, a Carver like the one who just touched my arm—perhaps my own great-granddaughter, her hands strong, her nails like new chisels—will come to this very place. The ground will have risen. The city's edge will have advanced. She will feel the readiness of the stone under her feet and she will begin to carve. With tools of bone and her own body, she will give new form to the substance of the world.

And she will be carving me.

The thought is not a thought; it is a feeling, a deep, resonant hum within the silence. It is the completion of the one great sentence we are all a part of. I am the stone that was shaped, and I will be the stone that is waiting. The distinction between my body and the ground it rests upon is already beginning to soften, like my footprints did when I was a child. The roar of the world is not a sound of creation. It is the sound of transformation.

I close my great, black eyes.

I will become the silence.
