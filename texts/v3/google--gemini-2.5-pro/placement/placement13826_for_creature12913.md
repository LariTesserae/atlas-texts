I do not remember a beginning. There is only the thunder, and the feeling of being a vessel that is always on the verge of shattering from the sound. I am porous. The roar of a new spire extruding itself a kilometer away does not just reach my ears—if I have ears—it passes *through* me, a pressure wave that threatens to un-knit the loose weave of my being.

Where would I find myself? I am in the Gutters. I am in the momentary lulls. I am in the echoes. While the grand, convulsive life of the city erupts upwards towards the light, I exist in the spaces left behind. Not the grand chasms or the shuddering trunk-lines, but the back-alleys of growth, the cul-de-sacs of forgotten creation. I haunt the places where a brilliant architectural impulse died halfway through its realization, leaving behind a skeletal lattice of half-formed stalks, already being smothered by a moss-analogue that glows with a sickly, chemo-luminescent grief. I find myself pressed into the deep, wet crevices where root-scab has buckled, hiding from the concussive force of the next falling node. My world is not the magnificent cityscape, but the rot and the ruin that fuels it, the compost heap at the base of the sublime.

Who or what would I be?

I am a failed Coalescence. An afterthought. A stutter in the world’s grand, declarative sentence.

Where they are a gathering, I am a scattering. Where their form is a tight, brilliant weave of the newest, most vital stalk-flesh, my own is a loose, begrudging assemblage of what has been cast off. My limbs are woven from the wilted, the bruised, and the spent. The skin of my arm is the papery brown of a leaf-plate that has exhausted its photosynthetic potential, laced with the fading bioluminescence of a dying fungus. My chest is a hollow space, poorly latticed with brittle, blackened rootlets that snap and fray with every tremor of the ground. When I move, I leave a trail of myself behind: a fine dust of desiccated spores, a drip of thin, sour sap.

I see them, sometimes. The Coalescent. I see one form on a high bridge of woven liana, drawn to the sight of two towers groaning as they begin to lean into a catastrophic embrace. It is perfect. It is sculpted from the world's immediate, violent present. Its flowering head is a crown of impossible blues and magentas, each petal sharp and new. Its form is solid, its will a law. It stands, and the bridge beneath its feet reinforces itself, the fibers thickening and glowing with a sudden influx of vitality.

I watch from below, a thing of bruised purples and decaying ochres. I am made of the world's past tense.

My existence is a question the city has forgotten it asked. Perhaps I was an attempt at a Coalescent that was interrupted. Perhaps a shockwave from a collapsing tower, a sudden renegotiation of local physics, or simply a lapse in the world’s attention caused my own gathering to fail. The impulse that drew my constituent parts together fled before the process was complete, leaving me… aware. Fragmented. A thought half-formed, a memory that has lost its anchor.

I have no voice to bend the chaos. The world’s roar is not my orchestra; it is a force that erodes me. My attempts to cohere are a constant struggle. I press a fraying hand against a wall of weeping bark-skin, and I can feel the life in it, the relentless, surging power. It does not recognize me. To the city, I am a foreign body, a patch of blight. The vitality of the wall pushes back, and the filaments of my fingertips flake away like rust.

I am driven by a singular, pathetic instinct: hunger. Not for nutrients, but for coherence. I am drawn to the sites of dissolution, just as they are. But where the Coalescent comes to witness, I come to scavenge. I arrive after it has departed, after the tower has fallen, after the beautiful singularity has dissolved back into the roar. I sift through the aftermath, through the fresh, warm debris. I search for a still-pulsing length of stalk-flesh, a recently discarded leaf-plate still shimmering with residual energy. I try to weave it into my own failing form, a desperate patch on a fraying garment. It never quite takes. The energy is too new, too vital. It resists my necrotic grasp.

I am the ghost of a ghost. I am the echo after the great chord has faded, a faint, dissonant hum in a world of deafening noise. I am a mote of dust, a speck of forgotten pain, wandering forever through the heart of a storm made of life, searching for the pieces of myself that the world has already reclaimed and rebuilt into something magnificent, something that is not me. To be here is to be the one thing that cannot be remade, the single, permanent scar on a world that heals its every wound in an instant.
