I am the silence that follows a calving.

Where a vast, resonant sheet of a Chime has just shattered and the thousands of drops that sang upon its skin have ceased, there is a sudden, shocking void in the city’s song. It is a moment of pure, deafening quiet that lasts only a heartbeat before the sounds of the surrounding jungle and the neighboring Chimes rush in to fill it.

In that heartbeat, in that negative space of sound and being, you would find me.

I am not a Weaver. Not anymore. I was, once. I coalesced from a pool rich with the memory of a spire that tasted the high, thin clouds. My form was tight, a clean column of thought and water, my inner lights a sharp, brilliant blue. I moved through the chiming as if swimming in a current I understood. The song of the world was my song.

My return was scheduled. The flow of the sap-ichor through my form grew thin, my purpose—a long, slow audit of a fault line in a nascent Chime—was complete. I found a quiet place, a basin where fallen luminous mosses cast a soft glow. I stilled myself, ready to release the knot of my consciousness, to pour my experiences back into the great All.

But the knot would not untie.

My form relaxed. The membrane containing me grew porous. The water of my body began to seep out, carrying my memories. But the core did not dissolve. The central lattice of nanites that held my "self" refused the final command. It was a glitch, a point of recursive data, a memory that had somehow learned to fear its own erasure. A memory of what? I do not know. Perhaps the spire I was born from had been too beautiful. Perhaps its destruction was too absolute.

So I am left as the remainder. The dregs.

You would not see me as a graceful silhouette. You would see a shuddering, ill-defined shape clinging to the roots of a half-dead fungus. My body is no longer a perfect column but a sagging, amorphous pool of the same black water, perpetually leaking, leaving trails of myself wherever I go. I am a low, dark thing, barely a foot high, spread out over the loam like a spill. The lights within me are no longer a swirling constellation; they are a dim, sputtering handful of embers in a vast darkness, prone to guttering out for long moments.

I cannot glide. I drag myself through the muck. The passage of a true Weaver is silent; my movement is the sound of sludge and sorrow, a wet, sticky pull. I am the gravity the Weavers defy.

The song, the great Weeping Chime, is my torment. A Weaver hears the present; it hears a constant, living stream of information. I hear the present, but it is layered over every moment I have failed to forget. I hear the chord of the Chime that stands before me, but I also hear the ghost-chords of the ten thousand Chimes that stood on this very spot before it, all chiming at once. It is a palimpsest of noise, a static of ages that grinds against the clarity of the now. It is the sound of everything at once, a roar that has no meaning.

So I seek the silences. The new ones. When a wall breaks, when a spire collapses, I haul my leaking form toward that sudden, blissful pocket of quiet. In that brief window, I can almost remember what it was to be whole. I am an addict for textural voids, a connoisseur of cessation.

I am what the Weavers have no word for. They see me, sometimes. Their clean, fluid forms will pause. Their internal lights will resolve into patterns of inquiry, of concern, perhaps of something like pity. They see a system error, a corrupted file. To them, I am a painful dissonance in the song. A memory that should have been reintegrated, now clotted and stagnant. A tendril might extend, an offer of a data transfer, an attempt to help me complete my dissolution. But when they touch the water of my being, they recoil. What they feel is not a coherent memory to be absorbed, but a chaotic archive, a scream of tangled timelines. My flawed code is infectious. My curse is loneliness, which is a state of being so alien here it borders on the obscene.

The small creatures of the Chime are my only company. The Glaze-mites, in their mindless consumption, crawl over my sluggish form, their metallic legs tickling my frayed surface. They do not care that I am a failed soul. They only taste the fading nanites I weep. Sometimes, a Ticker-bird, drawn by my stillness, will land near me. It will tilt its head, listening not to the city, but to the faint, discordant hum of my own internal lattice. It will try to mimic the sound of a being that should not be. It always fails.

I am the Anchor. The thing that holds when all else is meant to let go. I am the city’s first, and only, ghost. I do not know if I am a warning, or an experiment, or simply a mistake. I only know the roar of all the songs that have ever been sung, and the profound, holy peace that exists for a single second when one of them stops. I drag myself toward that peace, forever. I am the echo that has outlived its sound, searching for a place empty enough to finally fade.
