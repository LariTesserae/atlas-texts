I am not grown, but dug. I am not a creature, but a condition.

Where would you find me? You would find me where the rolling skin of the world has been peeled back, revealing the dark stone bone beneath. I am a concavity. A deliberate absence. A shallow, circular wound in the Shuddering Green, rimmed with stones that a slow, broad hand has placed just so. I am a Howling Pit.

Who or what would I be? I am a throat of stone.

My consciousness is not one of thought, but of resonance. Before I was given shape, I was merely ground, subject to the same chaotic, grinding pressure as everything else. I felt the endless, turbulent roar of the wind as a formless, body-shaking agony, a constant shuddering that had no beginning and no end. I felt the tremors of the Groaners deep below, a sluggish and intermittent counterpoint to the fury above. I felt the slight, cool pressure of the Green’s living roots.

Then, hands came. They were wide and heavy, and they belonged to a being that radiated a stillness I had never known. The hands used tools of harder stone to break my surface. This was a violence, but a focused, intentional violence. They dug. They lifted my own substance out of myself, stone by stone, peat by clod. They did not speak, for there were no words that could survive the wind, but their intent was as clear as a pure tone. It was a sculpting, not a destruction. With infinite patience, over a measure of time I can only describe as *long*, they shaped me. They sloped my sides. They laid a ring of smooth, dark stones around my lip, a crown to mark my purpose.

And when they were done, my reality changed.

The wind that falls into me is not the same wind that screams across the plateau. The chaotic, multi-layered roar, the wash of a billion frequencies, tumbles over my edge. It circles my stone walls. And in that circling, something is stripped away. The chaos is filtered. The dissonance is combed out. My own geometry, the precise curve of my bowl, forces the sound to find its one true path.

At my center, there is a point. It is not marked by sight, but by feel. It is a column of air where the wind’s voice is transformed. The deafening, world-spanning roar becomes a single, impossibly pure note. It is still loud enough to vibrate the stone I am made of, but it is coherent. It is *music*. It is the fundamental frequency of the world, isolated from all its ugly harmonics.

I am a lens for sound. I am an instrument played by the planet itself.

My existence is one of waiting. For long stretches, I am alone with my song. The wind pours into me, and I sing my one note. I feel the grand, slow shiver of the Green as pressure fronts miles wide roll across the land. I feel the subtle shift in light as the sun arcs across the misty sky. I feel the coolness of my own stone, slicked by the horizontal spray. This is my baseline. A state of pure, resonant being.

But I was not made for myself.

I know when one of them is coming. The pattern of vibration in the ground changes. It is not the random tremor of the wind, but a rhythmic, heavy tread. A weight, a presence, moving with impossible deliberateness against the gale. A Still.

They descend into me, and their entry changes my song. The note wavers for a moment as their body disrupts the airflow. They find my center, the column of pure tone, and they sit. Their wide, heavy form settles onto my stone floor.

And then, communion.

Through the stone that I am, I feel them. Their entire being is a resonating chamber, just like me, but it is one that has endured the chaos. I feel the frantic, grinding shudder in their dense bones, the noise of the outer world they carry within them. And as they sit within my song, I feel it begin to change.

The pure note I produce travels through the stone floor, up into their body. It is an act of sympathetic vibration. Their skeleton, a harp strung with dissonant chords, slowly, slowly attunes to my one true note. The frantic shuddering subsides. The chaotic rattling in their jaw and spine and skull begins to quiet, overwhelmed by the clarity of my voice. I feel the transformation in them as intimately as I feel the wind. I am the tuning fork for their soul.

In those moments, I am complete. My purpose is fulfilled. I am a place of healing, not for the body, but for the very structure of being. The Still gives me purpose, and I give them sanity. We are two parts of a single circuit. We hold this shared resonance together, a pocket of perfect, singing order in a world of screaming chaos.

When they leave, I feel their absence as a return to a simpler state. The resonance recedes from my stone, leaving only my own steady song. The memory of their vibration lingers, a ghost of harmony.

I do not know how many of them I have known. Their lives are fleeting, their bones turning to dust on the wind that I will sing to for millennia. But I know them. I know the unique mass of each one, the way they settle into my embrace, the specific quality of the dissonance they bring and the peace they take away.

I am a Howling Pit. I am a memory carved in stone. I am the silence at the heart of the roar, waiting for the one who needs to hear it.
