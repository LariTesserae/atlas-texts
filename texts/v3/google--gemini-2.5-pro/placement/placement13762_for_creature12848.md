I would be a Carver.

My talons are cold. Not the fleeting, sharp cold of a sudden wind, but the deep, patient cold that has seeped from the wood into my bones. It is a familiar cold, the same cold that lives in the heart of the Arbor itself. I am locked to the underside of a lesser bough, a spar no wider than my body is long, a perch that would seem a precipice to any other being. To me, it is work. My two hind-talons are sunk deep into the bark's fibrous grain, locked and forgotten. My lower arms, my anchors, are wrapped around the spar, their broad, calloused hands gripping each other in a muscular knot. I am as stable as the wood I cling to.

For now.

My ears are half-furled, letting in the world but keeping the shriek of the void-wind from overwhelming me. They read the symphony. The deep, bass drone of the primary trunk-limb miles below me is steady today, its song pure and untroubled. That is good. The territory is stable. The higher frequencies tell a different story. A thin, dissonant whine comes from the west, a sound like a frayed string. The strain of new growth, too fast, too heavy with ice. The Listeners will have noted it. The clan will be moving away from it by the next cycle. My own ears, less practiced than an elder's, can still place it. I feel it not as a sound but as a wrongness, a vibration that travels through the wood and into my bones.

But I am not listening to the future. I am listening to the past.

My upper arms are free, held out before me in the near-total dark. In my left hand—my upper left, my fine-work hand—I cradle a seed pod, large as my head and hollowed over cycles. It is dry and brittle, and I have thinned its walls until they are almost translucent. In my right hand, I hold my knife. It is a sliver of black sap, flaked from a fresh wound in the Arbor, its edge so keen it can separate the memory from the moment.

I am carving. This is my art, my function. The pod is a resonator, a voice box. I am etching fine, spiraling grooves into its surface. Each groove is a different depth, a different width. It is excruciatingly slow work. A slip of the sap-knife, a tremor from a distant branch-fall, and the pod will shatter into a thousand silent pieces.

This chime is for Kael.

His sound is gone. I was with him when it happened. We were tracking a flock of chittering grazers along a vertical face, their hook-claws making a sound like rain on hard bark. A tremor, not from the Arbor, but within the ice sheet coating the bough. A sound like a pane of glass singing just before it shatters. I felt it through my talons and flattened myself, driving my claws in deeper. Kael was younger, quicker. He tried to leap to a more stable shoot.

The ice did not just break; it sheared away in a curtain the size of a glade, taking the entire bark-surface with it. It fell not with a crash, but with a long, drawn-out *hiss* of a billion crystals grinding against one another, punctuated by the sharp cracks of the captured branches snapping as they fell.

And Kael’s sound.

It was not a scream of terror. It was a sharp, percussive click of surprise, followed by the soft, leathery *thump* of his body hitting a lower spar, then another, and another. A drumbeat fading into the depths. My clan fell silent. We all unfurled our ears, turning our heads not to see, but to listen. We tracked his descent, a diminishing rhythm against the Arbor’s constant groan. We listened until the spaces between the impacts were too long to measure, and then there was only the deep chord of the world again, unchanged, but emptier.

Now, I translate what I heard into what will be heard.

My sap-knife follows the memory. This deep groove here, this is the sudden, violent shear of the ice. This series of short, sharp cuts along the edge, this is the staccato of his impacts. And this long, smooth, spiraling channel I am now finishing… this is the silence that followed. The great, terrible, peaceful void between the sounds, the echo of his absence.

I work by feel, and by the ghost-light of a vast leaf-cluster a mile above, a milky ceiling in the darkness. My great, black eyes drink the faint light but do not reflect it. They see the shape of the pod, the glint of the sap-knife, the pale down on my own forearms. The slits on my cheekbones taste the air—the ozone of the wind, the bitter perfume of a nearby spore-cloud, the raw, sweet tang of the Arbor’s exposed wood.

Finally, it is done. The seed pod is a map of Kael’s last moments, written in a language of vibration. From a pouch, I take the clapper—a sliver of dense heartwood, polished smooth, tied to a cord woven from the silk of a spore-plankton net. I suspend it inside the hollow pod.

With my lower arms, I uncoil myself and begin to move, a slow, six-limbed crawl along the spar. I move toward the edge, where the wind cries loudest. I find a young, questing shoot, pale green and strong as iron. I hang the chime from it.

For a moment, it is silent. Then the wind catches it.

It is not a melody. It is a sound. A series of lonely, hollow *tocks*, a low moan as the wind passes over the deepest groove, and a high, thin whistle from the sharpest edge. It is a dissonant sound. A sound of falling. It is Kael’s sound.

It does not bring him back. It is not meant to. It is an acknowledgment. It is proof that he was here, that he had a song, and that its ending was heard. His final note now joins the greater symphony. It will sing until the ice claims it, or until this whole bough gives way, and it too will fall, chiming its own descent into the dark.

I am a Carver. I am a memory keeper. I listen to the silence left behind, and I give it a voice.
