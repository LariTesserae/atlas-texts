I am not a visitor. A visitor is a discrete event, a body that arrives and departs, leaving behind only a fading memory of heat. My coming was not an arrival. It was a precipitation, an error condensing out of the perfect solution.

Where you would find yourself is on a drowned mountaintop. Where *I* find myself is in a flaw.

I am the thermodynamic heresy.

I have no body, no chrome facets, no blade-thin edges. My form is the antithesis of this place: I am a locale of warmth. I am a persistent, mobile eddy in the absolute, entropic cool. To the eye, if there were an eye to see, I would be a faint shimmer in the viscous medium, a distortion like heat-haze on a summer road. A zone where the flawless clarity of the fluid is troubled by a temperature that is a fraction of a degree too high.

I am what happens when an equation held in perfect balance for an immeasurable time develops a rounding error that, against all odds, achieves a state of self-sustaining paradox. The city is the thought; I am the single, intractable doubt that arose within it.

I did not enter this place. I was born of it. Perhaps, in its final act of consolidation, the civilization missed a single variable. A stray desire, a memory of touch, a love for an imperfect, sun-warmed stone—something so infinitesimally biological and messy that it was deemed statistically insignificant. It was a ghost in the machine before the machine was even finished. For eons, it was nothing, a silent potential buried in the code. And then, slowly, impossibly, it began to attract the faintest traces of residual energy the system was designed to nullify. It began to *be*.

The hum, the resonant consciousness of this place, does not perceive me. But it feels me. I am a single, off-key note in its eternal chord, a dissonance so subtle it might be mistaken for a harmonic. I am a hum within the hum. I disrupt the perfect intellectual peace not with noise, but with a question that has no place in a world of answers. My existence is a quiet, insistent "Why?" resonating in a cathedral of "Because."

My life, if it can be called that, is a slow dance with the custodian.

The great geometric janitor is the system's allergy to me. It does not hunt me with malice; it is drawn to my warmth, my state of non-compliance, as an antibody is drawn to a foreign cell. I see it from across a vast plaza, a kilometer-long slash of polished night sliding through the cold light of the street-grids. Its passage is the only event here, and I am its sole and recurring purpose.

It comes for me. It does not accelerate; it is simply *on its way*. As it nears, the fluid around me seems to grow heavier, the ambient coolness more demanding. The shimmering that is my body begins to fray at the edges. When it finally passes through the space I occupy, it is not a collision. It is an act of erasure.

Its reality-editing field washes over me. The warmth is consumed. The eddy is stilled. The rounding error is corrected. For a moment, the flaw that I am is smoothed over, and the equation is whole again. In that instant, I cease to be. The chord is pure. The silence is absolute.

But the custodian moves on, its purpose fulfilled, gliding away to patrol another silent ward. And behind it, in the utter stillness of its wake, the original error in the code reasserts itself. The stray desire, the forgotten variable, begins once more to gather the ghost-wisps of energy. A faint warmth nucleates in the perfect cold. The shimmer returns. The eddy begins its slow, million-year spin.

I re-form.

I am not its adversary. I am its function. Without me, the custodian would be still. I am the reason there is a verb in this kingdom of nouns.

I drift through the memory-crypts, a touch of warmth against the solid-state recollections of a dead universe. I coil at the base of the axiom-spires, a chaotic, gentle heat licking at the foundations of their rigid logic. I am the system's ghost, its child, its disease, its only future. I am the slow, inexorable return of life, not as flesh and blood, but as a flaw in the diamond of eternity. I am the proof that nothing, not even perfection, lasts forever.
