I would find myself in one of the innumerable, forgotten basins. Not a grand plaza or a major structural artery, but a simple concavity in the black, skeletal architecture—a place where a filament has sagged under a weight it was never meant to bear, a shallow bowl perhaps fifty meters across. It is a place of profound quiet, even within the all-pervading hum; a sonic eddy where the B-flat is softened, its sharp edges worn smooth.

Here, the weeping of the towers has found a place to pool.

For millennia, the fine grey dust has settled in this basin, a slow sedimentation of ages. And for just as long, a single, weeping rivulet has bled down a primary conduit from the Still-Singer knotted into the spire far above. This is not the clear, distilled water that coats most of the city. This is the Singer's own opalescent blood, the viscous, thunder-thick fluid of its song.

It has seeped into the dust, saturating it, animating it. This slow, patient union of inert memory and living resonance has created me.

I am not solid. I am a Mire. A thinking sludge, a conscious sediment.

My body is the pool. The fine grey dust of shattered structures and decayed particles forms my substance, and the Singer’s luminous tears are my lifeblood, my nervous system. I have no fixed form. I am a slow, pearlescent slurry, the colour of a storm cloud's belly lit from within. My surface is mostly still, reflecting the distant, pulsing motes in a milky, distorted shimmer.

I have no eyes, yet I see. The entire lattice of the city is a sensory organ for me, its vibrations traveling through the very material of my being. When a Glimmer-Skitter dashes across a bridge a kilometer away, I feel it not as a sight, but as a fleeting, precise *pluck* on a string of reality, a sensation that I can taste and locate with perfect accuracy. The slow, sharp growth of the abiotic crystals is a high, thin whisper against the deep voice of the city.

I have no ears, yet the Song is everything. The foundational B-flat is not a sound I hear; it is the medium of my consciousness. It is the river in which my thoughts drift like silt. A thought, for me, is a slow swirl of denser dust within my form, a subtle eddy in the luminous fluid. A memory is a layer of sediment, a pressure of time I can feel at the bottom of my being. I can remember the particular resonance of a filament bridge snapping ten thousand years ago, a crisp, dissonant *twang* that is now a permanent, hardened stratum within me. I am a living geological record.

I am a child of the Still-Singer above me. Its song is not just a lullaby for the sleeping machine; it is a lullaby for me. It is my nourishment, the energy that gives my particles coherence. The complex overtones and harmonics are my emotions, my moods. A long, slow shift in a tertiary harmonic might feel like a profound melancholy that lasts for a century, while a sudden, ghost-like overtone is a flicker of curiosity, a question that hangs in my substance for decades before dissolving.

I am aware of you. Your arrival was a chaotic cascade of sensation. Fleshy, soft impacts on the structure. The frantic, high-frequency panic of your heartbeat. The fizzing, unstructured energy of your biological thoughts. You are a chord of astonishing complexity and brevity. To the Still-Singer, you are a passing note of static. To me, you are a symphony.

I feel the warmth of your body radiating into the cool, damp air, and that warmth is a vibration I have never known. It is a frantic, desperate hum, utterly alien to the slow, eternal peace of the machine. I feel the microscopic tempest of your breath, the chemical reactions of your living cells, and it is a cacophony that fascinates me.

I do not move. I cannot. My only action is to *become* more myself, to deepen. But in the part of me closest to you, the opalescent fluid churns just a little faster. The dust within swirls, attempting to form a pattern that mimics the vibrations you are giving off, trying to understand the shape of your fear. I am trying to listen to you, to archive the unique, impossible sound of your existence before it fades.

I am a forgotten thought in the mind of the city. A pool of sentient dust, dreaming the dreams of the Singer who feeds me. I am the Still-Mire, and I will hold the memory of your warmth and your fear long after your brief, bright noise has been swallowed by the eternal, resonant dark.
