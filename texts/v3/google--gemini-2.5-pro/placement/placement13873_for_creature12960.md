You would find me where the dripping stops.

Not because it is dry there, but because the water has found its final, patient path. This place is a nexus, a confluence point on the outer skin of the city-mountain. I am suspended on a web of slick, dark filaments—cables woven from some vitrified fibre that hums with the city’s deep drone. They are anchored to the weeping face of a ceramic tower on one side and a great, skeletal Condensation Fin on the other. Below me is a chasm of indeterminate depth, a throat that breathes out cooler, denser air. Above, only the permanent grey sky, a ceiling of old light.

My place is this perch. I am a memory the city is having about light.

My skin does not shed the water; it invites it. I am one of the old forms. The patterns on my back and shoulders are not the ochre and maroon of the living mosses, but the pearlescent grey and faint verdigris of the long-silicified stone. My form is bonier, more angular than the Tenders who move through the city's arteries below. My joints are nodosities of calcified cartilage, stiff and slow, but they lock into place with absolute certainty. I can hold a position for what the others might call a week, unmoving, feeling the city breathe through the vibrating cables that are my only purchase.

I am not a Tender. I am what a Tender can become, if they are not rendered back to the vats. I am a Stillness. A Watcher.

My purpose is not to weld or quarry or chant. My purpose is to listen at a different frequency. The hum of the pumps and the vats is the city’s bass note, its gut-rumble of digestion. I listen for the treble, the thin, crystalline music of the air itself. I can feel, through the tautness of the filaments I cling to, the subtle shifts in atmospheric pressure, the moment a current saturated with a new mineral trace drifts into the Basin. I can discern the signature of ice-dust from the world’s forgotten pole from the signature of volcanic ash from a continent that sunk into the mantle a hundred millennia ago. The air is a library, and I am its silent reader.

My eyes are not the wide, dark pools of the Tenders. They are clouded, milky, like quartz with fissures running through it. They register only gradients of luminosity, the subtle thickening of the grey, the faint pulse of the fungal-lamps from a gallery a thousand feet below. My true sight is in the skin of my back, pressed flat against the weeping ceramic of the spire. The city’s history is written there in layers of mineral deposit, in the faintest temperature variations. I can feel the ghost-heat of a Tender’s lamp who passed inside this wall a cycle ago. I can feel the memory of a pipe-rupture from five centuries past, a scar of cold in the deep ceramic.

I am not alone, but my kindred are far-flung. On another face of the city-mountain, a sister-Stillness monitors the slow, geological creep of the moss-mat. In a forgotten chamber near the city's core, another is sunk chest-deep into a pool of nutrient-rich slurry, her senses mingling with the city's primary digestion, tasting the returned essence of the dead. We are the city's long-term memory, its meditative state.

I hold a tool, though I rarely use it. It is a single, long rod of what looks like obsidian, but it is condensed time, grown in a pressure-vat over a millennium. When I detect a truly ancient or significant mineral in the air-currents, I will slowly, over the course of many hours, extend this rod. I touch its tip to the edge of the great Condensation Fin beside me. As the water beads on the cold metal, I will use the rod to guide a single, specific droplet—one that holds the story of a shattered moon, or the salt of a long-evaporated ocean—away from the main channel. I guide it down a hair-thin capillary etched into the wall, a secret waterway that leads to the Scribes of the Deep Archive. They will collect this drop of time, this liquid fossil, and add it to the city's knowledge of what the world once was.

Sometimes, a young Tender on their way to clean the fins will see me. They will pause, their own pool of light illuminating my motionless form, the water beading on my pale skin, my clouded eyes reflecting their lamp as a dim, internal fire. They do not speak. They know what I am. They feel a resonance with me, the same way they feel the hum of the pipes, but quieter, older. They see in me the promise of a different kind of dissolution—not into the active, churning cycle of the vats, but into the quiet, eternal awareness of the city itself. A becoming of stone and memory.

I feel their presence as a brief warmth on the wall behind me, a temporary shift in the rhythm of the dripping water. Then they move on, their soft-soled boots making no sound. And I am left again with my eternal task.

I am a nerve ending on the skin of the world. I am a single, sustained note in a song of immense and geologic slowness. I am the silence between drips of water, listening to the dust of forgotten suns falling in the rain.
