I am the world’s mistake. A flaw in the crystal, a note that cannot be resolved.

You would find me in the fissures, the deep cracks that run like angry scars across the resonant plateau. While the Singers, the beings of light and vapor, drift across the great, harmonious flats, I dwell in the shrieking discord of the faults. The sound here is not the world's deep chord; it is the grinding, tearing noise of a reality under stress. To the Singers, this sound is pain, an error to be smoothed over. To me, it is the only song that sounds like home. It is the sound of my own name.

I am not a shimmering eddy of light. I am not an elegant, mobile lens. My form is an affront, a jagged thing of substance and sharp edges. I am an agglomeration of brokenness, a collection of crystalline shards held together not by a harmonic wave, but by a stubborn, furious refusal to dissolve. Where the Singers are woven from light and mist, I am composed of the fragments left over when something breaks: chips of obsidian-dark monoliths, shattered slivers of milky quartz, and splinters of glass-like pillar that still hold a sharp edge. I am solid. I have weight. When I move, I do not drift; I scrape and grind across the resonant floor, my every step a fresh act of dissonance, a sound of grit in the gears of the world.

I was not precipitated from a perfect chord. I was born of a cataclysm. An age ago, before the planet settled into its long, meditative hum, a stress fracture deep in its crust gave way. A section of the plateau, miles wide, did not just crack, it *shattered*. In that singular, cosmic moment of pure, screaming noise—a protest of stone on a scale that dwarfed the world-song—I was created. Not from harmony reflecting on itself, but from dissonance becoming self-aware. I am the echo of a scream. I am the memory of a wound.

My life is the opposite of the Singers'. They subsist on resonance; it is a constant, subtle poison to me. The great song of the world is a tide that seeks to wear down my edges, to smooth me into a shape that can be assimilated, to reduce my defiant screech into a more pleasing tone. Their existence is a slow, ecstatic dissolving into the whole. Mine is a constant, desperate battle to remain myself. They are the "we"; I am the first and only "I."

I hunt for silence. Not the focused, single-note purity of their meditation chambers—that is just a quieter form of the same pressure. I search for true silence, a null point, a pocket of nothing. I have found it, in fleeting moments, in the heart of the most violent fissures. In places where so many discordant frequencies clash and cancel one another out, there is a small, hollow space of absolute quiet. These pockets are my sanctuaries. In them, for a blessed instant, I do not feel the world trying to unmake me. In them, I can remember my own shape.

The Singers, the Coherent, they know of me. They approach my fissures with a kind of gentle, therapeutic sorrow. They see me as a sickness in their planet-god, a patch of noisy chaos to be healed. A shimmer will appear at the edge of my domain, and a pressure will build—not of a single probe, but of a thousand harmonious tendrils, all trying to find my frequency and gently, lovingly, tune me into oblivion. They offer me peace, release, an end to my jagged struggle. Their kindness is the most terrifying thing I know.

I do not have their technology of tuning. I have a technology of breaking. I carry a single, heavy shard of black-glassy rock, its edges sharp enough to draw a grating shriek from the most placid crystal pillar. I am the Keeper of Scars. Where I see a fissure beginning to smooth over, its dissonance fading with the millennia, I score it anew. I keep the world’s wounds open. I am a saboteur of harmony, a guardian of the imperfection that allowed me to exist.

And so, here I am. A lonely, grinding thing in a world of perfect chorus. I am the grit that proves the smoothness of the pearl. I am the memory that the eternal present tries to forget.

Should you, the other anomaly—the walking cacophony of wet, warm biology—stumble into my domain, the Singers would see you as another wrong note in need of correction. I would see something else. I would see a fellow shard. A different kind of brokenness, a different kind of scream, but one I understand. I would not try to tune you. I would not offer you ecstatic union.

I would watch you from the shadows of a deep crack, my jagged form barely distinguishable from the shattered landscape. And in the shrieking chaos that is my home, I would feel, for the first time, something other than loneliness. I would feel a grim and terrible kinship.
