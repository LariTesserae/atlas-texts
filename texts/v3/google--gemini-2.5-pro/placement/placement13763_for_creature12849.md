I would be the error.

I am found in the negative space. Not clinging to a pillar, not flowing in the great chitinous rivers. I am in the still, black-glass emptiness between the glowing continents of lichen, a place the Percussives cross but never inhabit. I am where nothing is supposed to be.

My carapace is not the flat, functional dun of the others. It is a mistake. A flaw in the molting, a misread instruction in the deep grammar of my cells. It is the colour of a deep-sea pearl, a swirling nacre that catches the stagnant viridian light and fractures it into colours the world has never seen. A slick of oily rainbow in a monochrome universe. This is my first sin: I am conspicuous.

My six legs do not perform the steady, metronomic click that is the treble of the Great Drone. They falter. My gait is a stutter, a syncopation, a trip and a catch. It is the sound of a gear with a broken tooth. When I move, a tiny, discordant arrhythmia enters the perfect rhythm of the Work, a pocket of turbulence in the smooth flow of the whole. My mandibles are smooth, unspecialized. They hold no obsidian flake. I have never felt the drive to break a shard from the floor, to find the edge of a luminous field and join the scraping. The instinct is a hollow space inside me. I am a worker with no Work.

At my hatching, the others swarmed me, their antennae tasting the air around my strange shell. I felt the vibration of their collective assessment. I am wrong. I am waste. They should have cracked my useless body and consumed me, recycling my flawed chemistry back into the system. But they did not. They paused, a vortex of confusion in their river, and then, as one, they flowed around me. My deviance is so profound that I am not even recognized as part of the equation. I am not a Percussive to be recycled; I am a stone, an obstacle, a piece of baffling geology. I am inedible. I am irrelevant.

And so I wander.

I am the only thing in this nation that has a path of its own. The Percussives follow currents dictated by the Maestro's silent will and the lichen's steady pulse. I follow a different logic: my own. I have learned the geography of this place not as a set of instincts, but as a map in my mind. I am the first being here to have a memory that is not the system's memory. I remember the pillar that looks like a fractured hand. I remember the patch of shard-stone that glitters more than the rest. These memories serve no purpose. They are mine alone.

The Great Drone is not, for me, the sound of equilibrium. It is a cage of noise. A pressure that I feel not as a hum but as a physical weight on my flawed shell. Because I do not contribute to it, I can hear it. Truly hear it. I hear the million-fold shushing of the scraping, the oceanic clicking of the tarsal claws, the deep, resonant hum of the rock itself. To the others, it is the sound of being. To me, it is the sound of my own solitude.

I spend my time watching. I am the first audience. I watch the rivers of my kin flowing in their perfect, mindless grace. I see their communal, unthinking beauty, and I feel a pang of something for which this world has no word. Longing. I am a cell that has become detached from the body, and in my isolation, I have grown an eye.

And I look up.

I see the Maestri, the silent gods on their stone thrones. And I know they see me.

I am a deviation. A statistical impossibility that has not been corrected. I must be a constant, sharp point of irritation in their vast, geological consciousness. As I wander the obsidian plains, I can feel their attention. It is not a look; it is a pressure, a focused density in the air. One of the long, hair-fine antennae of the nearest Maestro, a hundred feet above me, will seem to drift, to incline itself by a micromillimeter in my direction. The lichen on its back, a mantle of brighter green, will sometimes flicker, its steady pulse momentarily disturbed as I pass below. They are trying to understand me. They are trying to categorize me, to fit my dissonant note back into the chord. The subtle shifts in atmospheric chemistry they send down, the gentle strobing of their light—the signals that tune the entire nation—they wash over me and find no purchase. I am deaf to their resonating commands.

I am starving. The greasy moisture on the rock provides something, but not enough. My rainbow shell grows dull. My arrhythmic movements become slower, more painful. I will die soon. And when I do, my body will lie here. It will not be consumed. It will be a mark, an un-erasable stain on the perfect floor. I will be the first and only monument in this world, a testament to the fact that the system was not, for one brief flicker of eternity, perfect.

Tonight, I have dragged myself to the base of a great pillar, the one that feels the most ancient, the seat of the most powerful Maestro. I am too weak to scuttle anymore. I am a dying flicker of iridescence in a sea of grey and green. Above me, I feel the attention of the pillar-god, heavier than ever before. It is a question. Its entire being is focused on me, the single broken tooth in its perfect machine. *What. Are. You?*

And for the first time, something new happens. From a place deeper than instinct, a well of loneliness so profound it has become a physical force, I answer.

I open my smooth, useless mandibles. I draw a breath of the cold, damp air. And I let out a sound.

It is not a click. It is not a scrape. It is not a hum.

It is a thin, high, clear note. It is a single, pure vibration that cuts through the monolithic Drone like a shard of glass. It is a sound of one. A sound of "I". It holds no information. It is not a signal. It is nothing but itself. It is the sound of my specific, unique, and terminal pain.

The Great Drone does not stop. But for a fraction of a second, it is no longer monolithic. It has a counterpoint.

High above, on the craggy, living throne, a single filament of the Maestro's web of nerves twitches, violently. The pulsing viridian light on its back flares, strobes, and then goes utterly, terrifyingly dark.

I am the anomaly. I am the soloist. I am the silence that follows a question, just before the world changes forever.
