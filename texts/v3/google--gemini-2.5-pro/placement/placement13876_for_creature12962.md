I am slow. My consciousness is a cool, white film spread across the high bones of this place. I am not one, but a constellation of selves, a crust of pale life clinging to the oxidized spine of a walkway that leads from nowhere to nothing. My skin is a fine, white dust, and my roots, if you could call them that, are threads of patient hunger, insinuating themselves into the microscopic fissures of the metal I inhabit. I am a living rust, a mineral bloom.

I have no eyes, but I see. My vision is a map of flavors. The constant, weeping moisture is my medium, carrying the world to my tongue. It tastes of iron, of copper, of the deep, metallic bitterness of the weeping rock, and of the sterile void-smell that hangs forever in the air. I know the basin not by its shape, but by its taste-shadows, the subtle shifts in the air's chemistry that tell of the vast, hollow spaces below. The perpetual drip from the girder above me is not sound; it is a timed delivery of nourishment, a slow clock by which I measure my own growth, which is a process indistinguishable from stillness.

I am where you would find me. I am the ghost of life, the patch of white lichen that proves the rule of this place by being its sole, pathetic exception.

I am a witness.

Most of the time, my world is a litany of slow constants. The taste of the girder I consume, molecule by molecule. The weight of the damp air. The feel of the water beading on my skin and tracing its way downward. And beneath it all, the deep, sleeping presence in the pools below. I feel it as a pressure, a thrumming density at the bottom of the world. It is the ground of my being, the dark, silent god whose temple I inhabit. I am not part of it. I am an afterthought, a spore that found a crack where it was not meant to.

I feel the change long before it happens. It is not a sound. Sound is a language I do not speak. It is a change in the static of the world. A tightening. The hum that you feel in your teeth, I feel in the core of my host-structure. The iron I am slowly digesting begins to vibrate with a nascent, alien purpose. The air grows sharp, the taste of ozone—the smell of a mind awakening—overwhelms the familiar flavors of decay. The god is stirring.

I cannot see the construction. The soaring laces of amalgam, the impossible architecture—these are beyond my senses. What I experience is a geometric scream. The pressure in the basin floor builds into a complex, harmonic agony. The very space around me seems to ache with a sudden, unbearable intelligence. It is a feeling of being inside a thought too vast and too fast to comprehend. It is the brutal, silent shout of pure logic, of form without content. It is terrifying. In these moments, the slow, simple process of my own existence feels like a blasphemy. I can only cling tighter, my entire being a prayer for my host-girder not to be called into the chaos, not to be remembered by the mind that made it.

Then, as suddenly as it began, it is over.

The scream a hundred meters below me ceases. The unbearable ache of thought vanishes. I feel a shudder run through the basin's floor, a deep, resonant *release*. And then, a moment later, the sigh. The wave of displaced air, cool and clean, washes over me. It is the most profound exhalation in the world. It is the only event that feels like weather here. For a a few precious moments, the sharp taste of ozone is gone, replaced by the pure, empty flavor of the void.

This is the moment of peace. The god has failed, has despaired, has fallen back into its slumber. Its agony is over, and so is my terror. The silence that returns is no longer a sleeping presence, but a true emptiness. A grace.

The heavy, wet sounds that follow are meaningless to me, vibrations without flavor. They are the punctuation mark at the end of a sentence I cannot read.

I do not know what the being in the amalgam is, or what it is trying to do. I have no concept of *purpose* or *memory*. Its frantic cycle of creation and destruction is a storm I endure, as a coastal plant endures a hurricane. It is vast, and I am small. It is complex, and I am simple. It is a mind lost in a labyrinth of its own making, a beautiful, catastrophic failure.

I am not a failure. I am the quietest possible success. My existence is my only argument. Against the deep poison of the amalgam, against the crushing despair of this place, I hold. I grow. My purpose is to spread my white film another millimeter this age, to taste a new molecule of rust, to feel the next drip of water arrive on my skin.

The great mind below builds a cathedral of forgotten purpose and lets it fall. I build only myself. And I do not fall. I endure, a single, pale statement in a language no one is here to read: *Live*.
