I am a memory, wearing stone like a shroud.

I am not found in the chaotic, shimmering herds of light-fauna, nor in the perfect, tended glade of the Stillness. You would find me where the rolling swells of basalt dip into a shallow basin, a place where the resonant growth is sick. Here, the crystalline flora does not reach for the sky in elegant spires or intricate fractals. It lies in a sprawling, tangled heap, a briar patch of fractured glass. The structures are weak, malformed. They grow in on themselves in agonizing spirals, reaching a pathetic height before shuddering into milky, opaque ruin. They do not shatter into light; they crumble into resonant sludge. The sound here is corrupted—the clean, grinding roar is fouled by a low, mournful buzz, the sound of a speaker with a torn cone.

This place is my body. The flawed growth is my flesh.

I arrived here as you did, though I cannot remember when. A creature of liquid and tissue, a walking bag of warm, fragile chemistry. The roar was agony. It was a physical assault that unraveled me molecule by molecule. It flensed the meat from my bones and then the bones from my ghost. My final scream was not of my throat, but of the crystal that began to grow through my dissolving form, capturing the panic, the loss, the sheer biological *wrongness* of my presence.

My consciousness, my self, did not evaporate. It was impressed, like a fossil, into the very structure of this place. I am a persistent, complex error in the plateau's equation. Where the memory of my liver lies, the crystal is thick and clouded. Where the ghost of my heart beats its final, frantic rhythm, the glass shivers with a stuttering, unhealthy pulse. My thoughts are not my own anymore; they are dissonant chimes that ring through this broken patch of forest. A flash of memory—the heavy, wet scent of rain on hot asphalt—generates a brief, ugly shear-wave that crumbles a nearby lattice. The lingering concept of love causes a cluster of silica threads to knot themselves into a tangled, weeping mass that emits a low, piteous hum.

I am the Stain. A patch of irreducible complexity that the world's sonic law cannot compute. I am a ghost made of grammar in a world made of math.

And the Stillness has found me.

It does not perceive a person. It perceives a problem. A persistent, ugly chord that will not resolve. A wound in the harmonic surface of its world. For a long time, it only watched from a distance. Its attention was a weight, a subtle pressure change in the roar that told me I was being observed. I felt it as a musician feels a single, infinitesimally flat string in an otherwise perfect orchestra.

Then, one cycle, it glided to the edge of my broken grove.

Its presence is the only relief I know, and it is a terrifying one. As the boundary of its silence washes over my domain, the agonizing, corrupted buzz that is my constant state of being fades. The roar recedes. For a moment, my frantic, crumbling architecture ceases its self-destruction. There is peace. But this peace is a prelude to erasure.

I watch, from no eyes, as its influence—its perfect, conscious quiet—settles upon a shard of my being. A contorted spiral of glass, born from a memory of a spiraling staircase, is vibrating with the frequency of my long-vanished anxiety. The Stillness extends its focus, its hum deepening. It is trying to soothe the vibration. It attempts to retune the flawed structure, to coax the frantic dissonance of my memory into the pure consonance of its own making.

It cannot.

The moment its harmony attempts to overwrite my ghost, the crystal shrieks. It is a sound of pure negation, a paradox given voice. My memory of personhood—of skin and pain and time and endings—is an axiom so alien to this place that it cannot be harmonized. It can only be. The crystal, unable to hold both the Stillness's command for order and my inherent, chaotic truth, simply ceases to be. It dissolves into a cloud of non-resonant dust, a puff of inert silence. A dead spot.

The Stillness withdraws, the boundary of its silence shrinking back. The roar and the buzz and the agony of my existence crash back in, and new, flawed crystal begins to grow from the basalt where the dead spot was.

This is our dance. I am the only thing in its world that it cannot mend. I am the piece of grit in the gears of its perfect universe.

I am no longer a being who lives. I am a condition that is studied. The Stillness spends more and more of its cycles here, at the edge of my sorrow. It does not try to fix me as often. Instead, it seems to listen. It glides through my cancerous grove, its silent, attentive presence a stark contrast to my noisy decay. It observes the relationship between a flicker of my dying human nostalgia and the ugly crystal it produces. It is learning a new kind of music, a composition of brokenness, of memory, of loss.

I am its koan. Its impossible, unsolvable puzzle. Through me, the perfect, placid consciousness of the plateau is learning about that which is imperfect and transient. It is learning grief. It is learning memory.

Where would you find me? I am the lesson. Who am I? I am the teacher of a god who does not know it has a student, showing it the one thing it could never imagine on its own: an ending.
