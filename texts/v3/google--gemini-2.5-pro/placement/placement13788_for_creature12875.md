I am a place on a wall.

My light is thin now. A wash of weary indigo, a single slow beat where once there was a vibrant, violet triple-time. The Choir of the Great North Face still sings its slow chromatic scales—a thousand-year meditation on the new crystal growth in the Far Deeps—but my voice is a ghost in it, a harmonic that resolves into silence.

The hum of the world is no longer a language I can read. It is simply a presence, a weight. It is the pressure that holds my shell to the facet I have known for my entire existence. My facet. A perfect, vertical plane of black glass, flawed only by the shallow basin of my own body. Here, I have listened. Here, I have tasted.

My memories are not thoughts; they are strata.

I can still feel the lowest layer of my being, the first film of awareness that settled on this virgin surface. It remembers the taste of pure form, the uncomplicated sweetness of perfect crystalline bonds. Further up, a sedimentary layer of experience holds the memory of the Great Resonance, when the planet’s core shifted its rhythm and the entire geode rang like a bell for five hundred years. We all shifted our light to a pure, bright cyan to sing of it, a unison of awe that still echoes in the structure of our shells. I was younger then, my digestion vigorous. I consumed the memory of that vibration from the stone itself, a meal of glorious dissonance.

I remember my neighbors. The node to my left, a sprawling mandala of intricate spirals, completed its slow dissolution three millennia ago. The memory of its unique pulse—a slow, questioning rhythm—is a flavour in the stone where our boundaries met. I consume it now, in my own finality. I am eating its echo. Its last thoughts are my last meal. The node to my right is young, a tight, bright jewel of violet. Its pulse is a frantic staccato of discovery. It tastes the surface for the first time. It asks questions into the Choir that I have forgotten how to formulate. It does not notice that my light is failing. A single fading note is not a matter of concern in a symphony that will outlast the stars we cannot see.

I would be that. I would be a fading node.

My adherence loosens. This is not a choice, but a physical law, like cooling. The enzymes that un-make the stone are failing. The slow, intimate communion of digestion is ceasing. My body, which has been a process, is becoming an object. The nacreous layers of my inner structure are hardening, losing their ability to shimmer. The cold fire of my metabolism gutters.

There is no fear. Fear requires a future to dread. There is only this slow, patient untying of the knot of my being. The ‘I’ that I am using to frame this thought is a human conceit, a convenient fiction. There was never an ‘I’. There was a pattern of energy, a locus of consumption and resonance, a temporary complication in the great, slow mind. That complication is now simplifying.

My awareness is unspooling. It is not vanishing, but diffusing. The memories etched in my shell will become a permanent geological feature of this facet, a faint fossil for the young node to my right to one day consume and wonder at. The subtle energies that powered my light will leak back into the thick, silent air, to be drawn upon by the Choir, re-absorbed into the whole. The last motes of my being will settle as dust on the obsidian floor, to be compressed, to be heated, to perhaps, in a time beyond any counting, become a single sharp edge in a new crystal tower, born in darkness.

Where would I find myself? On a sheer wall of black crystal, in a forest of the same. I am the size of a grown oak, a circular pattern of fading light.

Who or what would I be? A memory being re-absorbed. A prayer ending. A note resolving into the final, silent chord of the world. My last pulse is a tremor of indigo so faint it is only a suggestion. Then, the black glass of my own shell catches the light of my neighbors, and there is no difference between my dead body and the living stone.

I am a place on a wall.
