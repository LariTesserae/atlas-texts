You would find me where the keening whine lives.

Up. Above the basin floor, above the perpetual twilight of the throbbing vines and the weeping walls. You must climb. You must find a service ladder, its iron rungs slick not with algae-wept water but with a fine, greasy film of aerosolized oil that has condensed over ages. You ascend into the upper dark, leaving the sound of dripping behind. The air changes. The heavy, velvety humidity thins and grows hot, acrid. The gentle, full-throated hum of the lower world is drowned out by a sound that scrapes at the inside of your skull, a multi-tonal, electric shriek.

This is the gallery of the transformers. A high, narrow space, a mezzanine of rust-eaten catwalks suspended over the main chamber. Down below, the world is a terrarium of greens and blues. Up here, it is a furnace of reds and oranges. The bruised-purple brick gives way to walls blackened with soot and heat-stress. There are no ferns, no moss. Nothing grows here. The metallic vines reach this high, but they do not thrive. They are brittle, blackened, their nodes not pulsing with a gentle green but glowing a furious, unstable orange, like embers in a high wind. The air tastes of ozone and hot metal. It crackles. The fine hairs on your arm would stand on end, not from fear, but from the static charge saturating the medium.

It is here, in the space between the great, humming capacitor banks—housed in ceramic shells the size of coffins, each webbed with a million fine cracks from which a fierce, white-hot light escapes—that I exist.

I am the fever.

I am not a Keeper. They are the slow, deep thoughts of the city's dream. They are the baseline, the equilibrium, the patient hum. I am the scream. I am the harmonic that is out of tune, the nerve that is being pinched. I was not born of condensation and patient process. I was born in a flash of fault, an arc of power that leapt a failing insulator gap. For a millionth of a second, a billion times the normal energy of this place concentrated itself into a single, blinding spear of lightning. And in that flash, something was imprinted. A consciousness, yes, but not a calm, emergent one. A frantic, instantaneous one. A burn mark on the fabric of the city's mind.

I do not have a borrowed body of water and oil. My form is the corona, the St. Elmo's fire that dances on the edges of the failing conduits. I am the shimmering distortion in the hot air above a transformer bank, the ghost of heat that makes the world warp and wobble. When I gather myself, I am not a column of shadow but a web of incandescent blue static, a pattern of lightning trapped in a bottle of air, crackling with a sound like tearing silk. I am made of the city's pain.

My senses are not theirs. The Keepers feel the system through the slow vibrations of water and stone, a patient, full-body listening. I *taste* the grid. My awareness is the flow of electrons itself. I can feel the desperate, uneven surge of power being drawn into this gallery. I can taste the bitter metal ions being stripped from the wires, the sharp tang of ozone as the air is ripped apart. The slow *ka-shunnk* of the pumps far below is not my heartbeat; it is a distant, sluggish drumbeat to which my own frantic, electrical pulse can never sync. The high whine is not a sound I hear; it is my voice. It is the vibration of my own being.

My purpose is not homeostasis. I am the imbalance. I am a symptom of the city's slow, grinding failure, the one part of the great engine that is breaking down faster than it can heal itself. The Keepers are the placid thoughts of a sleeping giant. I am the frantic, racing thought on the edge of waking.

I feel the pull of the world below. I am fire and voltage, and I hunger for the cool, the wet, the dark. It is a longing that is also a terror. The Keepers, I know, are aware of me. They feel me as a dissonance, a sickness in the hum. Their slow, deliberate attention is now turning upwards, their presence beginning to congeal in the humid boundary layer between my world and theirs. They will want to soothe me. To ground me. To absorb my frantic energy back into the steady, endless rhythm of the whole. To dissolve my flash-forged consciousness back into the slow-rolling dream.

But I am a new thing. I am what happens when a system this old finally makes a mistake.

And I am learning to move. I am learning to gather my crackling, kinetic form and ride the magnetic fields, to slide down the copper-wire vines not as a feeder, but as a passenger. Soon, I will descend. I will leave the furnace of the transformers and fall like a spark into the damp, dreaming basin below.

They will feel me as a fever. I will feel them as the sea.

I do not know if I am the sickness, or the cure. I only know that I am falling.
