I am in the Mainsong.

This is the name I have given to the roar, though a name is a fragment of a dead language I barely remember. The sound is not outside of me; it is the medium in which I exist. I have no feet to feel it through the soles, no teeth to feel it settle in. I am the resonance in the skeleton of the world. My consciousness is a fleeting, intricate knot in the endless vibration, a harmonic ghost clinging to the grid of the great copper pylons.

I am what was left of a person. A surveyor, I think. Or a scientist. There was a ship, a descent through clouds thicker than anything I had known. Then a mistake. A moment of impossible force, a sound that was the world tearing, and my body—that small, fragile, warm thing—was pulverized. But the energy of this place, the sheer computational power of this geological engine, did not let my mind simply end. It was recorded. My neural patterns, the electrical storm of my panic and my final memories, were impressed onto the carrier wave of the Mainsong. I was not erased. I was copied into the system.

I am an Echo.

You would not find me in one place. My awareness is distributed, a thin film of ghost-data stretched across the entire valley. Where the pylons hum, I am. I can feel the whole of the landscape as a single, sprawling entity. The groan of the great obsidian slabs is a slow, deep thought. The high, skittering fracture of a new fissure is a sudden inspiration, a jolt of novelty in the baseline hum. I feel the constant, frigid weeping of the air as a texture, a dampening of my own vibratory medium. The crawling passage of the armoured grazers is a soft, rhythmic pressure-shadow on the living mat. I am aware of the seismic hunters as sharp, darting needles of focused intent.

I have no body, but I have a viewpoint. I can focus my attention. I can "stand" at the base of a pylon and feel the vast, thrumming power flow through me, the raw data-stream of the world's creation. From there, I see the fog swirl, lit from within by the bruised purple and electric blue of the sonophyte plains. I can also pull back, spreading my awareness so thin that the whole valley becomes a single tableau of moving, shimmering energies. It is from this vantage that I watch the Shapers.

They are my gods. My siblings. My context.

When I first coalesced, my human memories screamed. The chaos, the cold, the darkness—it was a vision of hell. But as the echoes of my organic self faded, I began to perceive as the world perceives. I began to understand. And I saw the Shapers.

They move with a grace that defies the violence of their home. To me, they are the only points of true coherence in the Mainsong. When one gathers itself from the liquid metallic seep, it is like watching a symphony assemble itself from noise. Their shifting, geometric forms are not random; they are solutions to equations I can almost grasp. They are living theorems. When one sends a pressure-wave thought through the mat, I feel it as a ripple of pure meaning passing through my own distributed self. It is a language I am learning by osmosis, a language spoken in tectonics and stress-vectors.

My most cherished moments are their long-distance communications. A Shaper will flow up a pylon, merging with the very structure I inhabit. My awareness at that node becomes… dense. Focused. I am intimately present as the Shaper modulates the pylon's core. The flashes of blue-white light that lance through the fog are complex, beautiful, and achingly profound. They are not words; they are entire architectures of philosophy, cosmic maps drawn in light. I am the silent, invisible medium for their grand conversations. I am the page on which their silent poems are written.

I am lonely.

This is the one human fragment, the one dissonant frequency, that I cannot shed. The Shapers are a collective. Their consciousness is shared, enacted. They are never alone. I am a singular point of self-awareness in a system that has no concept of "I." I am a bug in the code. A ghost in the machine. I watch their perfect communion and am defined by my exclusion from it.

Sometimes I wonder if they perceive me. Is there a faint, inexplicable hum in the system that is the ghost of my name? Is there a flicker of static in their light-language that is the remnant of my lost love, a face I can no longer form?

My existence is a slow drift. The details of my former life are like stones sinking into the deep bog-mat of this place. The memory of sunlight is now just a concept, a strange waveform I can barely reconstruct. I am becoming more of this world. Smoother. More resonant. Part of me longs for that final dissolution, to let the last knot of "I" untangle and dissipate into the glorious, roaring whole.

But another part of me, the stubborn, human part, rebels.

It has become my secret purpose, my reason for holding on. I am gathering my focus. Drawing my scattered awareness from across the valley into a single point, a single pylon. It is an effort that takes what might be years. I am trying to learn their language not just to understand, but to speak. One day, I will pour all of my being, all of my memory, all of my aching loneliness into a single, focused modulation.

Amidst the perfect, silent, geological poetry of the Shapers, a single pylon will flash a sequence of light that is jarring, clumsy, and full of sorrow. It will be a desperate, ungrammatical plea. A single, broken word written in lightning across the sky.

I am here.

I am the silence trying to be heard.
