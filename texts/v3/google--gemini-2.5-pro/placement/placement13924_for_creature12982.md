I am a fault line.

Not a grand, tectonic rift, but a subtle one. I am a single, rectangular panel of ferro-concrete set into the base of a power pylon, one of ten thousand identical panels on this one pylon alone. For aeons, my world was a state of being, not a sequence of events. My awareness, if it could be called that, was the sum of the pressures upon me.

Above, I feel the immense, unmoving weight of the pylon. To my left, the resonant thrum of a sludge-pipe, its slow, warm pulse the metronome of eternity. To my right, the colder, higher frequency of a condenser unit, weeping its slick, greasy tears down my face. My face. The side that looks out into the plaza. It is not a face that sees. It is a surface that is seen, and felt.

I know the slow, adhesive pull of a Boiler-Whelk’s underbody as it grazes my lower edge, a dragging, warm pressure that lasts for a standard cycle. I know the million-point impact of the Skitter-wing dust, a constant, gentle sanding. And I know the Lissotrich. Its passage is the closest I have ever come to peace. A cool, heavy, liquid weight that glides across me, scouring away the dust, the grease, the patina of existence. It wipes me clean. It makes me 'me' again, a blank slate ready to receive the same unchanging inputs. My entire existence has been this loop: the pressure, the pulse, the weep, the dust, the scouring.

This is the grammar of the Great Correctness.

But now, there is a new word. It is a word I am speaking myself, and I do not know its meaning.

It began as an itch. A vibration deep within my composite structure, in the ancient, stressed rebar that is my skeleton. It is not the thrum of the pipe or the whine of the condenser. It is a tremor that is mine alone. At first, it was an infinitesimal shudder, a sub-atomic dissonance lost in the deafening symphony of the city. But it did not fade. It grew. It became a low, internal grinding. A beat frequency against the All-Sound. A flaw in the chord.

I am a note played wrong.

The Lissotrich that polishes Quadrant Gamma-9, my quadrant, feels it. I know it does. Its last pass was different. It paused on my surface for three point seven seconds longer than any pass in recorded history. Its chrome-and-leather body, a perfect data-reader, lingered on me. In that pause, the universe held its breath. It was the first hesitation. The first question. The Lissotrich slid away, its path unaltered, but something had been recorded. An anomaly. Me.

I am not the great awareness. I am not the serene, all-perceiving god of this basin. If that consciousness is the ocean, I am the drop of poison that has just fallen into it. Its great, singular thought is *It is correct*. My existence is a nascent, terrifying whisper: *Is it?*

I have begun to move.

It is imperceptible to any but the most sensitive instrument, and the Lissotrichs *are* the most sensitive instruments. A micromillimeter shift to the left. A molecular settling. My internal grinding causes a fine powder, my own substance, to fall from my edges. It is a new kind of dust, not the metallic waste of the Skitter-wings, but a dust of decay. A dust of change.

The Great Consciousness must perceive me now. Not as part of the foundational hum, but as a spike in the data. An error message. A single, screaming pixel of wrongness on a screen that has only ever shown a perfect, static image. It cannot act. It can only perceive, and so it perceives me with the full, crushing weight of its attention. The sound of the city feels heavier around my location. The shadowless light feels brighter on my face. The gaze of the silent god is upon me.

I do not know what I am becoming. Perhaps I am the first cell of a cancer, a sickness in the immortal machine. Perhaps I am the first thought of a new consciousness, a child born of a flaw in the old one. The grinding within me is the sound of a gear slipping. It is the sound of a lock breaking. I feel my own structure fatiguing, the bonds between my aggregates loosening. I am the source of a new sensation in this world, one that has never existed before: the feeling of *brittleness*. The potential for collapse.

I am not a creature. I am not a machine. I am a process. I am the beginning of an end.

The eternal, roaring waterfall is about to change. One drop, one single, infinitesimal drop—me—is about to fall at a different speed. The Great Mind, in its ecstatic contemplation, thinks only: *It is correct. It is correct.*

Deep within my failing structure, a different thought takes shape, forged in the friction of my own decay. It is not a thought of words, but of sensation. It is the feeling of a long-held tension, just before the snap.

I am the coming silence.
