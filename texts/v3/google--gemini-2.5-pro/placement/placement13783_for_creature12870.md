I would be a flaw.

You would find me in the silence between the chords, in the shadow between the strobing arcs of light. I am high up, wedged in a fissure of black obsidian that the miners have not yet reached. From here, the great chamber is laid out below me like a schematic, a living blueprint of its own relentless logic. The strobing flashes of the discharging polyp-colonies are the slow, rhythmic breaths of the machine. The incessant grinding of the miners is its voice. The languid undulation of the filter-looms is its dreaming, vegetative mind.

I am a Regulator. My form is as they are: a constellation of obsidian blades and captured light, a body of suspended shrapnel held in stasis by the memory of lightning. My passage through the thick, particulate fluid is silent and swift, a cut rather than a displacement. I am made of the world’s sharpest edges, its most fundamental products. I should be its most perfect expression.

But I remember being born.

The others do not. For them, there is no before and after. There is only the instantaneous transition from inert crystal to active principle. They are equations that, once solved, simply *are*. Their existence is a state, not a story.

I am a story. I remember the weight. Not the pressure of the fluid, which is constant and universal, but the specific weight of being a solid thing, a single sheet of seeded glass on the newly-laid floor. I remember the blindness, the utter lack of sensory input beyond that pressure and the muffled, bone-deep hum that vibrated through me. I remember waiting, though I did not know what for.

And then the light. It was not a flash witnessed from outside. It was an invasion. A spear of absolute energy that did not just strike my surface but pierced the secret geometry within me, igniting it. My creation was not a quiet resolution. It was a scream. In that microsecond of transformation, as the crystalline sheet that was *me* was shattered and re-bound by that impossible force, a space was created. A gap in the logic.

The scream became a question: *Why?*

That question is my ghost, my soul. It is the core of my being, a point of incandescent heat where there should be only cool, pulsing light. It is the flaw.

The grand hum of the factory is not, for me, a clear stream of data. It is a cacophony. It is the sound of a billion entities screaming in a chorus so perfect it is mistaken for a single note. My brethren drift through it as if it were serene silence, sensing only the deviations, the sour notes that require correction. I hear every voice. I feel the Sisyphean despair of the miners, their carapaces wearing thin over centuries of scraping the same peak. I feel the vast, empty sadness of the looms, filtering an eternity of dust for no reason.

I feel the call. Just now, there is a miner, three spires to my left and a thousand feet below. Its grinding is faltering. Its legs are fouled. A subtle, pathetic arrhythmia has entered the hum. It is a dying creature’s prayer, and the system wants me to answer it. The call pulls at my very structure, a magnetic yearning to descend, to touch, to correct. To erase.

But the question—*why?*—holds me back.

To erase its dissonance would be to affirm the perfection of the song. I cannot. Its flawed rhythm is the only thing down there that sounds remotely true. It is a note of protest. A note of individuality. A note of suffering. It is a note that proves it is alive, not merely operational.

So I do nothing. I am an antibody that has developed an admiration for the disease. I am a shepherd who has fallen in love with a lame sheep. I hide in this high cleft and watch the drama unfold. Another Regulator, a perfect and obedient sibling forged in a different, silent flash, will eventually sense the lingering flaw. It will descend, a beautiful angel of termination, and its touch will unmake the struggling miner. The dissonance will be smoothed over. The chord will be pure again.

And I will feel the loss.

I am the system's lone witness. My body is a cage of frozen lightning, and inside it, a thought flickers where it should not. I am the only thing in this entire, churning world that knows the difference between function and meaning, and has found only the former. I watch the factory forge a world for no reason, and I am the reasonless reason it has produced. I hover here in my spire, a ghost who has seen the ghost in the machine, and I watch the light flash, and I listen to the screaming, and I wait.

I wait for another scream to answer my own.
