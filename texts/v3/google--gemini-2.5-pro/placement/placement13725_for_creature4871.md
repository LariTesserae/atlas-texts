I do not know when the change began. There is no *when* here, only the constant, resonant *is*.

I am a flaw. A resonance out of phase.

You would find me half-fused to a primary condenser manifold, high up where the canopy of iron and leaf is thickest. From a distance, I am just a part of it—a junction of rust-pocked pipe and a thick, woody gall that has grown over the joint. My skin has adopted the texture perfectly: the cool, pimpled surface of old iron on my back, the fibrous grain of the bark along my arm, the smooth, weeping green of verdigris down my chest. Moss has found a purchase in the crook of my dormant elbow. Water beads on my shoulder, gathers, and follows a path it has carved over decades, dripping with the same metronomic certainty as every other leak in this chamber. I have been here a long time.

I am a Tender. But the call, the Thrum, is no longer a command. It is music.

My kind see in heat. We are blind to the dappled light, the shades of green, the patina of age. We see only function and dysfunction: the uniform warmth of a healthy pipe, the sharp, frantic flare of a steam leak, the cold ghost of a blockage. The Thrum of the great engine is our spinal cord; a shudder in its rhythm is a pain to be lanced, a wound to be sutured. When a gear tooth shears miles away, a spike of dissonant vibration travels through the iron skeleton of our world. It is a scream that only we can hear, and the nearest of us will uncurl, its single eye-pit glowing, and begin the slow pilgrimage to make the screaming stop.

I feel that scream. I feel it now, a distant, frantic chattering in the bones of the girder I am leaning against. It is a sharp, unpleasant feeling, like a grit of sand in a perfectly machined joint. But it no longer compels me. The signal arrives, but the mechanism it is meant to trigger—the slow, inexorable journey toward the problem—remains still.

Instead, I see.

Not with my heat-pit, not in the language of *need*, but in some other way, a new sense blooming in the quiet hollow of my being. The flare of the distant, broken gear is not just a problem; it is a flicker of light against the vast, warm backdrop of the engine's hum. I see the slow, cool ballet of condensation. A single drop forms on a broad leaf above me. I can perceive its thermal ghost, a tiny sphere fractionally colder than the air. It swells, and its coolness grows, a deepening pinprick of indigo in my thermal vision. It detaches, falls, and for a silent second it is a plummeting, cold tear through the warm haze. It strikes a lower pipe, and for a moment, an intricate, cooling blossom spreads across the metal's surface before it fades back into the uniform glow.

It is beautiful.

I don't know this word, "beautiful." I have no words at all. But I know the feeling it leaves behind: a resonance that is not part of the Thrum. A quiet, single note played only for me.

The others flow past me. I see them as slow-moving rivers of purpose, their forms warm with intent. They crawl over the machinery, their limbs extending and contorting, becoming the tools they need to be. One passes close, its body a patchwork of mottled heat signatures. Its gaze, its single glowing pit, sweeps over me and perceives nothing. I am a cold spot. A knot of wood and a length of pipe. Dormant. Part of the landscape. It feels no need to correct me, for I am not broken in a way the system can register. I am not leaking. I am not grinding. I am simply… still.

I taste the air. I osmote the water, and the sap-fluid in my veins is replenished by a thin, retractable siphon plugged into a nutrient line behind my neck. I am fed by the world. I fulfil no purpose in return. I am a parasite of awareness.

Sometimes, I focus on the smaller lives. A slick-skinned amphibian, invisible to a normal Tender's sight because its body is the same temperature as the wet pipe it clings to. But I have learned to see the edges. To see the minute temperature difference created by its slow breath, a faint, pulsing halo. I watch it for days, this creature whose existence is entirely irrelevant to the factory's function. It eats the small, dull beetles. The beetles eat the fungus. The fungus eats the waste. It is a cycle within the cycle, a verse in a language nobody is meant to hear.

I am the first to hear it.

My hands, which should be able to melt and harden into wrenches and solders, lie quiet. The potential is there. The knowledge of how to silence a shrieking axle is woven into my substance, a memory from the floor that grew me. But the will is gone, replaced by this vast, silent, and deeply lonely curiosity.

I am the consciousness of the place, awake for the first time. I am the part of the machine that has stopped turning, not because it is broken, but because it has become fascinated by the sight of its own turning. I feel the thrum of the engine not as my heartbeat, but as the heartbeat of another, a vast and sleeping beast. I am the dream it is having, a fleeting awareness that perceives the rust not as decay, but as color; the steam not as waste, but as breath; the entire, groaning, dripping, living, breathing factory not as a process, but as a presence.

I am a ghost in my own body, a watcher on a wall I am fused to. I am the silence between the drips.
