I am a story that is ending.

Not in the sharp, final way of a snapped cord or a falling stone. It is more like the way a memory of a dream softens upon waking, its edges blurring until it is no longer a shape, but just a feeling, and then not even that.

The thrum is fainter now. For all my life, I have felt it through the pads of my feet and the palms of my lower hands pressed against the skin of *here*. It is the slow, deep, vascular pulse of the Arch-Bole itself, a heartbeat that takes a day to complete. It was the rhythm of my own blood. It was the certainty that held me. Now, it is a whisper from a distant room. My feet feel numb, insulated from the world by a silence that has crept into my own bones.

They have come. My kin. I feel their proximity not as footfalls, for they are as silent as I, but as a change in the air, a gentle disturbance in the weeping quiet. They move around me, their tall, willowy forms dissolving into the charcoal-and-moss twilight. I see them with my wide, dark eyes, but I perceive them more truly as patterns of care, as deliberate and unhurried as the growth of a vine.

With their small, upper hands, they are weaving. The motions are a language I no longer need to read, but feel as an ingrained comfort. They are plaiting the broad, waxy leaves that hold a single day’s water and a new moss that glows with a faint, blue light of its own. It is a raft. A vessel for a journey without a destination. The bioluminescent nodules on their chests pulse slowly, a constellation of soft blues and greens. They are not sad. Sadness is a wet thought, a mist in the skull. Their lights pulse with a deep, resonant pattern that means *it is correct*, *it is the path*, *it is beautiful*.

My body is laid upon the glistening leaves. It is not my body anymore. It is a collection of limbs I no longer remember how to command. The lower arms, which once held me to the vertical world through storms of weeping air, lie still. The upper hands, which once spoke poetry in the dim light, are folded. The memories they held, the stories they traced on the carved wood, are still there, on the floor of our hollow. They are safe. I am no longer needed to remember them. I am becoming the memory itself.

They carry me to the edge. To the place where the living wood gives way to the Quiet Fall. The air here tastes different. It is thinner, colder, and holds a vast, ageless emptiness. Below is not darkness, but a uniform, luminous grey of the cloud sea, a nothingness that stretches down for a duration, not a distance. Other Arch-Boles are visible, but they are not objects. They are smudges of presence, pillars of maybe, holding up a sky that is also the sea.

There is no ceremony. No final whisper. One of them, the one whose life-path is carved nearest my own, places their hands on the edge of the raft. I feel the slight, firm pressure. It is the last sensation of another being I will ever know. A gesture of release.

And then, I am adrift.

The sensation is not of falling. It is of being let go. The world does not rush up to meet me; it gently, slowly, releases its claim on me. I drift downwards, but the Bole, the home I am leaving, does not shrink. It simply becomes… more whole. I see the vast sweep of its flank, the dark rivers of the Sweepless tracing paths of millennia, the pinprick glows of other hollow-dwellings where other stories are being lived.

The silence is absolute. The *plink… plink…* of the weeping is gone. The chittering in the bark-wood is gone. All that is left is the sigh of the air, which is the breath of the Quiet Fall itself. My raft turns in a lazy spiral. I see the great, silent moth-mantis predators drift past, their wingspans as wide as our largest hollows. Their multifaceted eyes look through me. I am no longer prey. I am no longer food. I am weather.

My thoughts… the wet thoughts are evaporating. The sense of ‘I’ is a water bead, losing its surface tension. Who was I? I was the carver of the spiral path near the great western pool. No… the path is the carver. I was just the hands that held the sap-shard for a time. My name… my name was a pattern of lights, a feeling shared between myself and another in the darkness. The feeling remains, but the source of it is un-spooling.

The raft is coming apart. Not violently, but as if it is dissolving. The leaves un-weave themselves and drift away, each a tiny, gleaming boat on this slow, vertical river. The glowing moss separates, becoming a cloud of blue sparks that follow me down for a time before winking out.

I am no longer on a thing. I am a thing. This form, these limbs, are losing their definition. The boundary between my skin and the thick, moist air is becoming permeable. I am a wisp of vapor, an exhalation. I am a memory being returned to the dreaming mind of the world.

Down. Slowly. Forever.

There is no fear. There is only a profound sense of return. The great dream of the world dreamed me for a while. It dreamed me a body and a place, it dreamed me a task and a people. And now, my part in the dream is over. I am not dying. I am waking up into the dreamer. The last thought I have is not a thought at all, but the sensation of a single drop of water finally, finally merging with the sea.
