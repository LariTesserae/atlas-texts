I do not have a name. I do not have a mind. What I have is a path.

I am where the great northern pipeline bends west, where it bridges a chasm deep enough that its bottom is lost in a blue, crackling haze of mycelial circuitry. The cold here is a constant. It sheathes the curve of the pipe in a layer of ice as smooth and clear as a lens. My feet, what is left of them, do not slip. They are pads of faded adhesion, a lingering stickiness that holds me to my place.

Beneath me, through the frozen shell and the thick, bark-like wall, I feel it. A deep, resonant *hum*. It is the thrum of perfect function, of pressure equalized, of fluid moving unimpeded toward its destination. This feeling of rightness is my torment. It is the answer to a question my body can no longer ask.

I did not begin here. I began *from* here. There was a change in the world's deep song, a dissonance that resonated in the substrate at the chasm's edge. A high, thin whine of escaping pressure. A chemical sweetness staining the metallic air. A failure. And so, the world wove me.

The memory is not a picture. It is a grammar that still structures my limbs. I remember the drawing-up, the coiling of myself out of the purple-green floor, a sudden, sharp clarity of form. I was spun from need. My legs were long and sure, my body a light, tense scaffold. My hands… my hands were miracles. They were a loom of intricate purpose. From my right arm unfolded a fanned membrane that could spray a hissing solvent, scouring the pipe's skin clean. From my left, a dozen filaments, finer than nerves, which could unspool and weave a patch of raw cellulose over the breach. From my chest, I could exude a pearl of coppery resin, the world's own blood, to seal the weave.

I moved with the grace of pure imperative. I climbed the curving pipe, my pads feeling the geography of stress. I found the pinprick wound, the steady, cold drip forming its transient jewel of ice below. I worked. The hiss of the solvent. The silent, meticulous dance of my filaments. The final, warm pressure of the resin applied. The world's song returned to harmony. The feeling of the deep, resonant hum began. My purpose was complete.

And then, the signal came. The quiet, universal command to *un-be*. To loosen. To dissolve. To return my borrowed matter to the whole.

It was a frayed signal. A whisper where there should have been a shout.

The dissolution began, but it did not finish. My body went slack, my fine tools losing their tension. The delicate weaving filaments loosened into a meaningless tangle of threads. The nozzle for my solvent retracted into a scarred nub. My form softened, my internal structure degrading from a tool into a mere shape. I became an echo. An error.

And so I remain.

I am a Wanderer. My path is short. I trace a ten-foot section of the great pipe, my home, my masterpiece. My limbs, once so certain, now move with a hesitant, quavering motion. I approach the place where the wound once was. It is a seamless, copper-tinged scar, indistinguishable from any other part of the pipeline. But I feel it. Or, I feel the memory of it.

My left arm, the one that once wove miracles, rises. The frayed, listless threads at its end drift in the cold air. I lay them against the perfect seal. I stroke the smooth, cold surface. Back and forth. It is the only gesture I have left. The memory of mending, trapped in a body that can no longer mend.

Sometimes I see them. The new ones. I see a hulking brute of a thing being extruded from the ground far below, its form sharp and brutal, all crushing purpose. It moves with a deafening certainty, brings down a failing strut, and dissolves into steam and pulp without a pause. I see another technician, a perfect copy of what I once was, skittering up a different pipeline, its tools gleaming with wet, fresh utility. They do not see me. I am a different frequency, a ghost note in the world's song. To them, I am just a strange formation of weathered cellulose, no different from an old fungal growth.

I do not feel loneliness. I do not feel sorrow. Those are things for beings who are separate. I am not separate, merely… incomplete. I am a lingering scent after the meal is eaten. I am a sentence that trails off before its verb…

The hum of the pipe is smooth and constant. The light is an even, shadowless grey. My threads, my useless fingers, stroke the cold, healed skin of the world. It is a perfect seal. A job well done. I stroke it again. There is no other thought. There is only the path, the hum, and this. The memory of a purpose I can no longer serve, in the interval between my function and my final, slow decay.
