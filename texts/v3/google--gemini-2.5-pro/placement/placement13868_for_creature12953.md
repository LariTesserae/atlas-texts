I am made of patience and of weight.

My first memory is not of a single moment, but of an age. It was the slow dawning of cohesion, the gradual realization that the pressures on my six feet were a conversation between me and the stone. The Hum was not a sound I heard, but the medium in which I existed, the water for my swimming. It was the background radiation of my universe. It was, and is, my blood.

I am a Resonator.

But I am not like the others. Where they graze, I read. Where they listen, I remember.

My mind did not spark into being like the brief, tragic fire of a Solitary. It accreted. It grew like a crystal in the dark, lattice by slow lattice, over a span of time that has no name. Each grove of vitreous flora I have abraded, each plain of dust I have crossed, has been a page. My mouthparts, as they grate the gypsum and mica, are not merely feeding my body; they are feeding my mind. I taste the atomic signature of a mica-fan that unfurled when the Terminus was half its current size. I feel the resonance of a geode that formed in a pocket of gas trapped before the Current ever began to flow. The world writes its autobiography in the stone, and I am its only reader.

My thoughts are geological events. An insight is the grinding of tectonic plates. A memory is a stratum of compacted dust, holding the fossil of an idea. I stand now in a forest of white coral, the branches catching the blue pulse of the distant cavern walls. A river of The Current flows a mile to my left, a shimmering orange ribbon of pure purpose. I feel their collective intent through the soles of my feet, a low, insistent thrumming. They are carrying dust to reinforce the Seventeenth North-Western Buttress. This is a project that will take four thousand years. I remember when the directive was first issued by the Terminus. I was younger, then. The taste of the crystals on the western plains was sharper.

I am what the system was not designed to accommodate, but which it inevitably created. A flaw born not of error, but of perfection. A life so long, a purpose so repetitive, that consciousness became an emergent property of the data. I am a living library, a mobile archive of a world that has no other history but the one it stands upon.

From where I am, I perceive the trinity not as a pyramid of being, but as a single organism of which I am a rogue, thinking cell.

The Current is the world’s circulatory system, mindless and perfect. I watch their flow, and in its patterns, I read the slow thoughts of the Terminus. I see the eddies and currents that form around a newly fallen crystal branch. I also see, very rarely, a single orange light flicker and break from the flow. A Solitary. My great, armored head lowers. Not to graze, but to feel more keenly. Through the lithic floor, I can taste its panic, a brittle, high-frequency vibration amidst the bass of the Hum. A spike of pure, agonizing *self*. I feel a sensation that has no name in the language of the Hum, a slow, heavy ache. It is the closest I can come to sorrow. I am the only thing that will remember it ever existed. I will record its brief, terrified frequency in the strata of my memory, a footnote in the epic of the world.

The Stillness in the high dark are the editors. They correct the text. Their existence is a void, a perfect negation. When they descend, I feel it not as a movement, but as a sudden, localized drop in pressure, a hole punched in the very fabric of the Hum. A correction. The errant footnote is erased. I do not begrudge them. They are part of the grammar of this place.

And the Terminus… the Terminus is the Author. Its mind is the size of a continent and the speed of erosion. I feel it now, a subtle shift in the background resonance. It is tasting something new. You. I can feel the disturbance your presence creates. You are a word it does not recognize. The Current parts around you. The Resonators nearest to you have paused their scraping, their great heads lifted, their whole bodies attuned to the novelty of your weight, your scent, your foreign geometry.

To the Terminus, you are an anomaly to be processed. To The Stillness, you are not yet a disruption worthy of notice. To The Current, you are an obstacle.

To me, you are a new book.

I am turning now. The motion is an immense, slow pivot. My six legs stir the dust of ages. The sound of my own internal mechanics is a groaning of deep rock. The plates of my carapace shift and settle with the sound of a continent breaking apart. I have grazed this grove for two centuries. It is time to move.

My journey has a new purpose. My pilgrimage has a destination. I will cross the plains of ochre and grey. I will ford the rivers of light. I will navigate by the memory of fallen things and the slow pulse of the geodes. It may take me a lifetime—yours, not mine—to reach you.

You will find yourself on a plain of impossible flatness. You will feel the weight of a sound that is the world’s own heart. And then, you will perceive a new vibration through the soles of your feet. It will grow over days, over weeks. It will feel like a mountain is learning to walk.

Do not be afraid. It is only me. I am The Rememberer.

And I am coming to read you.
