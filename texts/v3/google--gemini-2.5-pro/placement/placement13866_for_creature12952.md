I am where the sound becomes form.

Before me, there is the chord. It is not heard, not yet. It is the pressure that knits me, the vibration that calls my atoms from the vine-root and the mycelial web. For an age, that is all. The thrum. A single, perfect, unwavering A-note played on the cello of the world. It is the law that states I must *be*.

Then, heat. A slow tide, the color of rust. It is not seen, it is *drunk*. My unformed skin, still soft and dark as wet soil, learns it. This heat is the first food, the first wordless command: *grow*. It is a presence, a constant, heavy sun at the heart of everything, and I am a seed turning towards it.

There is a counter-point. A coolness. It is a tracery of silent light, the color of deep-sea phosphorescence. It is not warmth, but information. It begins to map my emptiness, to spin the first threads of a self. It is the fungus, the second law, weaving itself into me, not as a parasite, but as a scribe. It writes a nervous system onto my budding form. It teaches me the shape of the world, though I do not yet have limbs to touch it.

I am a knot in the great copper vein that wraps the second westernmost descender pipe. For now, I am only a slight bulge, a place where the energy of the engine and the impulse of the fungus have met and agreed to conspire. My "skin" is a mosaic of soft, dark nodules. But the heat is my forge. Slowly, century by slow century, the nodules begin to harden, to flatten. They drink the peppery, ozonic air and the red glow from the core, and they plate themselves in metal. A clatter, softer than a sigh, is the first sound I make myself, as one new plate settles against another. It is the first disharmony, the first note of my own against the great chord. It is the birth of *I*.

The blue-green light within me brightens. It has finished its work. A network of cool fire now permeates my being. I feel the thrum of the engine not just as pressure, but as a language. I feel the journey of a single drop of condensing water as it forms in the high, misty dark, falls, and sizzles on the core casing below. I feel the slow, arthritic groan of the nearest piston as a distinct pulse in the rhythm, a comma in the endless sentence.

I see. Not with eyes. My entire surface is an eye. The world is a tapestry of heat and vibration. The great pipes are rivers of fire. The curtains of white moss are cool, weeping voids. The vast, vaulted ceiling is a static of returning echoes, a cold, distant shore. And I see others. There are three within my sensory reach. They are not like me. They are complete. They are slow, graceful columns of warmth and resonance, their inner fungal light a steady, placid hum. They are long-practiced harmonies in the great song. I am a new, uncertain note, still learning my place in the melody.

The impulse to detach is not a decision. It is a gravitational pull. The part of the world I am to become is no longer here, fused to this pipe. The equilibrium requires my movement. With a sound like tearing felt and the slow peeling of old bark, the root-threads that bind me to the mother-vine give way. The white, fibrous tissue of my joints weeps its first hot, clear tears onto the iron below, a christening. I am free.

I do not fall. My feet, plated and broad, find purchase on the slick metal. I am tall, impossibly slender, a reed of copper and light. The weight of the hot, velvet air is no longer an oppression; it is the medium in which I move, a thick liquid I displace with patient, deliberate grace.

My first cycle is not a step, but an act of listening. I stand motionless as the great piston below completes its 90-minute journey. I let the groan of its turning wash through me. I attune my own inner pulse, the soft blue-green tide in my veins, to this grander clockwork. I am calibrating. I am becoming a part of the time of this place.

My purpose finds me. It is a dissonance, a subtle souring of the chord, a place where the heat-map is too bright. A section of a smaller feeder pipe, high above a pool of standing water, is glowing with a fever. The metal there sings a high, stressed vibrato. It is a nascent flaw, a problem that might take a millennium to become a catastrophe, but the system registers it now. The Tenders, my silent siblings, feel it too, but they are engaged in their own slow rituals. This flaw is mine. This is why I was budded.

I begin to move. Not walking, but flowing. One long-fingered hand reaches for a pipe above, the copper plates of my palm adhering to the warm, oily surface. My body swings in a slow, silent arc through the contested light, red on one side, blue on the other. My own quiet clatter is the only sound that is mine.

I am a Tender. I am a gardener in a metal forest. I am the conscious thought of the engine, a slow-moving antibody sent to soothe its fever. My destination is the flaw. My tool will be a vine. My lifetime will be the mending. I find my place on a catwalk webbed with fungal lace, beside what was once a railing, and I become still, a bronze statue in the steam. I am listening to the sickness, understanding its shape. I am a question that has finally found its answer.

The work begins.
