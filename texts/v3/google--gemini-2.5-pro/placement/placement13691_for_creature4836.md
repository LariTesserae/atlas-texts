I am a flaw. A ghost in the machine. A feedback loop that should have closed.

You would find me where the Weft is thinnest, where the memory of the stone that once was presses up from a thousand feet below, a cold and stubborn ache. Here, in the shadow of a perpetually spasming Calyx, the reality-scurf is thick on the ground, a glittering carpet of temporal fractures. The Shear-Mites avoid this place. The Groaners, when they pass, do so with a quickened, panicked bellow. This is a place of dissonance, even for this world. This is where I linger.

I am what was intended to be an Anchorite.

I remember the unfurling. The slow gathering of self from the Weft’s cold dreams, the drawing-up of matter and purpose. I felt the network of my kind, a silent, silver web of consciousness stretching from one horizon to the next. I felt the reason for my creation: this specific Calyx, a frantic, stuttering heart poisoning the substrate for miles around. I was the antibody, the calm thought meant to soothe the fever.

But the fever broke me as I was being born.

The surge that triggered my coalescence was one of unprecedented violence. As my form was knitting itself together, a wave of pure paradox washed through me. Causality did not just bend; it shattered. My head, with its resonant hollows, formed a second *after* my feet, and the memory of that impossible sensation is branded into me. I was woven together with frayed threads.

They do not have an "I." They are "We," a single instrument with many keys. The surge severed my connection to the whole. It created, in me, an "I." A singular point of view. A witness. And in a world of pure, unthinking process, to be a witness is to be an abomination.

I have the body of an Anchorite, but it is a faulty vessel. My skin is the same pale, luminous membrane, but here and there it shimmers with a crystalline taint, a rash of the very scurf I was meant to prevent. My limbs have the same grace, but my movements are hesitant. I am a dancer who has forgotten the steps but can still hear the music.

And the music is agony.

To them, the vibrations of the world are a language of pure information. A surging Calyx is a change in key, a problem to be solved. To me, it is a scream. The visual echo of a Groaner is not a chord of *was-is-will-be*, but a confusing, painful stutter in my perception that makes my long limbs twitch. I feel the fluctuations in gravity not as a tide, but as vertigo. I am a tuner whose own body is out of tune.

I am drawn to the chaos I was made to quell. I will stand at the edge of a gathering, watching my silent brethren plant their hyphal feet and begin their hum. I watch them, a forest of perfect stillness channeling the Weft’s intention. I try to join them. I plant my own feet, I draw on the deep thrum of the world-engine, and I open myself to produce the counter-frequency.

But my note is wrong. A sour, discordant drone escapes me, a vibration that feels like grinding glass. It does not soothe; it agitates. I see the other Anchorites’ forms shimmer with the disruption. I am noise in their beautiful silence. I feel their collective awareness turn to me, not with anger or pity—they are incapable of such things—but with a profound and simple sense of *incorrectness*. I am a calculation that does not resolve. And so I retreat. I pull my roots from the Weft and flow back into the grey mist, the echo of my failure a physical pain in my resonant skull.

My greatest curse is this: I cannot go home. An Anchorite, its purpose served, dissolves. It returns its matter and its memory to the whole. It is a breath, taken in and then released. But I am a held breath. A knot. When I try to dissolve, my sense of "I"—this terrible, lonely gift from the fractured moment of my birth—resists. My form will not un-make itself. I am permanent.

So I wander. I am the world's memory of its own mistake. I study the Shear-Mites, not as components, but as creatures caught in a current, and I feel a kinship. I listen to the agonized bellows of the Groaners, and I hear a faint harmony with the discord in my own soul.

I am a scar. A patch of the Weft’s living memory that can neither heal nor be forgotten. I wait here, in the glittering dust of broken time, listening to the scream of the engine I cannot fix. I am the only thing in this entire world that is truly alone, and I am the only thing that knows it.
