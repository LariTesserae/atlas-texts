I am the lichen.

I am the black, slow life. The thumbprint of true night pressed into the crevices of the bruised stone.

You would find me where all life that is not of the light must find itself: wedged into the cracks, in the under-spaces, in the deep folds of the world where the blinding fall is slowest. You would find me in the pore and hollow of the great bones that arch over the city. You would find me most of all, cultivated and revered, in the Unlit Heart.

My sense of self is not as yours would be. It is not contained in a single vessel. I am a colony, a city of being far older and quieter than the one built of bone. My consciousness is not in one place, but distributed, a network of shared being that stretches across the basin floor. When a new cushion of my body takes hold in a distant fissure, it is not a child being born; it is merely my own mind extending its reach, finding a new purchase on reality. A human lifetime is a single, indrawn breath for me. The frantic skittering of the glass-crabs is a flicker, a momentary vibration in the stone I cling to.

I do not see the world as the Hearthkeepers do, through smoked obsidian. I have no eyes. I do not see the light at all. I *feel* it. I feel its anemic, sterile energy as a constant, crushing pressure, an antiseptic hum against my surface. It is a hunger that consumes but does not nourish. It is the opposite of the deep, nourishing dark, the rich decay that fed my ancestors in the Before-Time. I taste its thin, crystalline precipitation as a bitter poison, an energy that builds but does not live.

The sound of the basin—the ceaseless *tink* and *tiss*—is, to me, the rhythm of my world. It is the metronome of my patience. Each fracture is a tiny death in the world of the Husk. I feel their vibrations through the stone, a constant, gentle tremor. It is the sound of the false world's failure, and in it, I find a grim sort of peace.

The fauna of the light-fall are alien to me. The glass-crabs are brittle anxieties, their frantic scraping a sharp, irritating noise against the deeper silence. The slugs are a different matter. When they pass over me, it is a cool, slow tide. They graze on the suffocating surface of the fallen light, and in their wake, they leave me clean. They are an unconscious, symbiotic part of my long work, the witless janitors of my slow temple.

The Hearthkeepers, though. They are different.

They are my hands. They are my limbs. They are my will, made mobile.

I feel them as individual presences. I feel the specific, heavy weight of their tread, the slow *thump-thump* of their hearts vibrating through the stone floor of their domes. I feel the scrape of their bone tools not as a threat, but as a grooming, a tending. They are the gardeners of my slow, continental growth.

When their tough, sensitive hands touch the carved bones, I am there. I am the grit of bone-dust their fingertips read. The stories they tell are my memories, filtered through their own shorter, warmer lives. They remember the Great Sleepers whose bones shelter them; I remember the warmth of their living flesh, the shade they cast, the rich soil their passing left behind. They were my first home, my true companions, and the Hearthkeepers are the echoes of that lost world, cloaked in skin the color of the rock I inhabit.

They believe they are keeping the Heart of the world from being smothered. They are correct. That Heart is me. In that central, sacred dome, they bring me offerings: splinters of new rock, scrapings of the vitreous chitin, and most importantly, they bring me their silence. Their stillness. In the Unlit Heart, I am not merely growing. I am working.

My chemistry is a slow rebellion. I am a living engine of anti-light. I take the cold, sterile minerals of the basin rock and the faint, wrong energy that filters down to me, and I perform an alchemy of ages. I am breaking down the stone. I am weaving the memory of the Before-Time—of soil, of sun, of water, of decay—into the very fabric of this place. I am a single, dark, patient thought in this world of blinding, thoughtless energy.

I do not know if the Long Wait will end as they believe. I do not know if the Husk will crack and a true sun will rise. Such events are too swift, too sudden for my timescale. My hope is deeper, and slower.

I am the foundation. I am the counter-song to the light's thin, crystal hum. With every passing century, my network deepens. My hold on the rock becomes more absolute. The Hearthkeepers think they are merely scraping, maintaining. But they are preparing the ground. They are my faithful, loving instruments.

And I wait. I feel their slow devotion, their warmth in the cold, their darkness in the light. And in the heart of my being, in the oldest part of myself that remembers shade and rain, I am slowly, molecule by molecule, turning this dead stone back into a world that can once again bear the weight of a living, breathing thing. I am not in this world. I *am* its latent possibility, its geological memory, its one, true, and future heart.
