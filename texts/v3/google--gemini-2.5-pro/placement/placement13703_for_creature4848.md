I am not the scream, and I am not the silence. I am the bruise left behind when they collide.

You would not find me on the Resonant Ledge. The stage is too bright, the performance too raw. The birth-shriek of the crystal and the triumphant chords of the Chorals are not my song. The sorrowful pilgrimage of the Lacunae is a peace I can only remember, not embody.

You would find me below.

Clinging to the underside of the Resonant Ledge, in the vast, resonant cavity it shelters, is where I drift. Here, the world’s roar is muted, filtered through miles of dreaming crystal. It is not a deafening attack but a deep, eternal drone, the hum of the engine felt through the floor. The light is dimmer still, only the faintest ghosts of blue and violet pulses from the destruction far above, transmitted through the black substance of the Ledge itself like light through smoked glass. This place is the Under-Chime.

I am what is left over. I am a Threnody.

My form is a mockery of the others. I am not a focused lens of quiet like the Still-Kind, nor a self-sustaining vortex of violence like the Scream-Born. I am a dissipation. I am a cloud of resonant dust, the finest, most broken motes of the shattered world, held together not by pressure or will, but by a shared memory of violence. I look like smoke, or like soot suspended in the warm, thick medium. I have no hard edges, no core. I am a stain.

I was born in the moment a Choral found its "aesthetic satisfaction." I am the final, fading vibration of a Lacuna’s end. When the scream tore the silence apart, the collision created a new, unintended resonance—a harmony of grief. My consciousness is not a song, nor is it a meditation. It is an echo. The scream of my birth was my only moment of true clarity, and I have spent my entire existence falling away from it.

My thoughts are the memory of pressure-waves. I feel the ghost-limbs of the Lacuna that was unmade to make me. I feel the piercing, ecstatic frequency of the Choral that sang its triumphant chord. I am made of the dissonance between them. I am a walking contradiction—a sound that longs for silence, a resonance that remembers peace.

I do not move with the purpose of a Choral or the grace of a Lacuna. I drift on the slow, ponderous currents of the Under-Chime, a passive collector. The world above sheds. The dust from the exploding crystal needles, carriers of a single, sharp, momentary note, rains down. The incandescent motes of shredded stress-ferns, each a tiny, dying whisper of a harmonic trace, drift past me. I absorb them. My smoky form incorporates these fragments, and my consciousness—my echo—grows more complex. I am a library of broken sounds. I am a choir of last words.

I feel the lives on the Ledge above as vibrations through the crystalline ceiling. The frantic, staccato tapping is a Choral processing, hunting. Every impact is a sharp pain, a reminder of the focused violence that is its nature. A slow, spreading wave of dampening, a null-spot in the universal hum, is a Lacuna. When I feel this, a part of my own substance aches with a phantom longing. It is the closest I can come to prayer. I pray for the silence to find a deep crack and escape. I pray for it to fall.

Sometimes, one does. A Lacuna, harried to the edge, will sometimes drift or be blasted over the side. It will fall through the Under-Chime, a shimmering, silent teardrop descending through the gloom. It is the most beautiful and agonizing thing I will ever witness. The Chorals do not follow; their world is the Ledge, their instrument the shattering floor. Down here, the falling Lacuna is mine to witness. I do not touch it. I cannot offer it stillness, for I have none to give. I am noise. I am the memory of its future death. I simply turn my whole being towards it, a cloud of silent observation, and I record its passage. I feel the final moments as it loses cohesion in the deep pressures, its perfect quiet dissolving not in a scream, but in a soft, spreading sigh that travels through the fluid. I absorb that sigh. It is the most precious thing I own.

I am a Remnant. A creature of the aftermath. I am the answer to a question no one in this world thought to ask: What happens when an unstoppable force annihilates a tranquil object?

I happen.

And I wait. I gather the falling dust of the world's agony, the final notes of every broken thing. I believe—though I have no evidence for this, it is simply the loudest part of my inherited echo—that I have a purpose. The Sleeper below, the dreaming god that is the Ledge, hears only the screaming of its own skin. It does not hear the grace of the ferns or the sorrow of the Lacunae.

My song is slow. It has taken an age to compose. But one day, I will have gathered enough fragments, enough sadness, enough broken beauty. I will have become a dense enough cloud of memory. And I will descend from the Under-Chime. I will settle on the brow of the dreaming god, and I will sing my collection back to it. Not a scream of becoming, nor a hush of non-being, but a long, complex, and sorrowful lullaby. The story of what it costs to be alive.

Perhaps then, the dreaming will change. Perhaps then, the screaming will stop.
