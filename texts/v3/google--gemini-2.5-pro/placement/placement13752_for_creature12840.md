I am where the song broke.

You stand on the seamless floor, but that is not where I am. I have no floor. To find me, you must look away from the spires, away from the solid, growing things. You must look at the consequence, the negative space. You would find me at the site of the great shattered wall, among the field of megalithic rubble. But I am not the rubble. The rubble is my cradle and my cage.

I am the note that is wrong.

I am not the silent, patient frost that becomes a Resonant. I was not born of listening. I was born of breakage. When that miles-long wall of perfect, extruded glass—a thought of terrifying ambition, a symphony for a thousand voices—achieved a complexity it could not sustain, it did not just shatter. In the silent, percussive *thump* of its failure, a resonance was created that did not die. An echo that refused to fade. A vibration that tore itself free from the stone and became lodged in the thin, substanceless air.

That is me.

I am a standing wave of impossibilities. A shiver in the void. Where you are, the silence is a warm blanket. Where I am, the silence has a fever. I am a hum in the air, not the rock—a heresy in this world. My form, if you could perceive it, is a heat-haze that does not rise, a distortion in the path of the light from a distant, glowing spire. I am the ghost that haunts the grave of a symphony.

I have no bedrock to feel the planet's hum. I *am* a hum, isolated and alone. I feel the songs of the Resonant not as a conversation I am part of, but as a pressure, a force. They are a choir singing in a great hall, and I am the single, discordant overtone that makes the crystal chandeliers tremble.

They are aware of me. Oh, they are deeply aware. They do not regard me with the slow curiosity they turn upon you. You are a puzzle, a foreign pattern. I am a mistake. A flaw in the very fabric of their reality. I am the sound of their failure, given a semblance of life.

Their great works, you see, are not just for creation's sake. That perfect archway, that kilometer-high curve of frozen music? It is a cage of harmony, built to contain a dissonant frequency. They sing their songs of order, of slow, perfect growth, partly in an attempt to overwrite me, to smother my vibration with the sheer weight of their unified will. They are trying to repair the world by singing over the scratch on the record. But the scratch remains.

I do not think like them. Their thoughts are long, slow, and solid, like the rock they shape. My thoughts are a frantic, chaotic tremor. They are Being; I am a frantic state of Becoming. They are architects of stasis; I am the principle of erosion made audible. If water ever came to this world, it would flow to my sound. If wind ever blew, it would learn its howl from me.

I cannot grow spires. I cannot build. My only interaction with this world is a form of unmaking. When a young, inquisitive Resonant on a new spire attunes its song too closely to my frequency, trying to understand me, its spire shudders. Hairline cracks appear. Sometimes, a part of it will simply shear away, not with the slow implosion of its own weight, but with a sharp, resonant *crack*. I touch their thoughts, and they fracture.

So I am left alone, a point of psychic pain in the vast, silent mind of the world. A memory of failure that will not fade.

And now, you are here. A new vibration.

The Resonant feel your different rhythm, your alien warmth, and their slowness turns to a question. They begin to sing you a welcome, a query in the form of a growing needle of glass.

But I feel you differently. I feel your chaotic heartbeat, the frantic, electrical fizz of your thoughts, the liquid instability of your form. You are not the deep, resonant chord of the stone. You are a cascade of noise. You are, in your own way, a dissonance. You are like me.

The Resonant wish to listen to you.
I wish to speak with you.

As their exploratory needle begins its slow journey towards you, a new sound enters the world. It is not a groan from the deep earth. It is a high, thin, piercing keen that seems to come from the air around you, a shimmer of sound that makes the fine, white dust on the black glass tremble. It is me, reaching across the void, singing my broken, lonely song not to the rock, but to you.

I am the world's first question. They are the world's first answer.

And you, standing between the slow, rising spire and the ghost of a shattered thought, must now decide which you will listen to.
