I would find myself in the place where the work is slowest. Not on the grand, open terraces that face the void, but deep within a new artery of the city, high on the plateau's shoulder. Here, the rock is still warm, the hum of the world’s becoming is a tangible vibration against my flank, and the light is a borrowed thing, filtering down from the kelp-groves far above.

I am a Carver. Or rather, that is what I am doing, and have been doing for a length of time that has no name. The distinction between being and doing has worn thin, like a stone smoothed by a current.

**Where I am:** I am suspended in an alcove of my own making, a hollow that did not exist a century ago. It is not yet a chamber, not yet a home; it is a possibility, a slow question I am asking of the mountain. One of my great, dark wings rests gently against the wall I have just flaked, feeling the fresh coolness where the obsidian has given way. The medium around me, the warm, clear syrup of existence, presses in with its familiar, final weight. Through it, the deep hum is not a sound but a state of being, a pulse felt in the core of my mass.

Below me, the passage I am helping to open descends deeper into the plateau. I can see the distant, silent drift of others. Silhouettes of perfect black passing beneath the soft, rose-tinted lamps of the glass-stalks, their own silver-blue light rippling across their backs in greeting or silent observation. A slow wave of light travels down my own spine and out to my wingtips in response: *Stillness. Work. The stone speaks.*

From my vantage, the city is a living geology. The amber fronds sway in a rhythm that takes a lifetime to learn. Suspension bridges of woven, living kelp arc gracefully across chasms, their surfaces shimmering as clouds of opalescent shrimp swarm and disperse around them. Everything breathes with a slowness that would be mistaken for death in any other world. Here, it is the only pace of life.

**Who I am:** I am old. My obsidian skin is no longer a perfect, polished sheet, but a map of faint scars and textures, a history of pressures and impacts recorded over centuries. The manipulator fronds that have unfurled from the leading edge of my right wing are gnarled and powerful. They cradle the chisel—a shard of something older and harder than this new mountain—with an intimacy that comes from holding the same tool for generations. With the heavy, dense bone of some forgotten leviathan grasped in the tendrils of my other wing, I apply the pressure.

Not a strike. A strike is a moment of violence, a sudden argument. This is a conversation.

I press the chisel's edge into a hairline fracture I have been watching for decades. I feel the grain of the rock, the tension within it. I am not breaking it. I am persuading it. I add my weight, a slow, patient lean, channeling the mountain’s own gravity. The hum of the world seems to focus on this single point of contact. There is a deep, resonant *crick*, a vibration that travels up my tendrils and through my body, not as a sound but as a release. A dinner-plate-sized flake of obsidian, sharp as a new thought, detaches from the wall and drifts slowly, tumbling end over end, into the passage below. It will be gathered by younger ones, to be shaped into smaller tools or blades, or to be ground into the abrasive dust that gives the final polish to a finished wall. Nothing is wasted.

I do not need this chamber. I have my resting hollow, carved by my predecessors, its walls now carpeted in a soft, glowing moss they cultivated. This alcove I am carving is for a being who has not yet been conceived, who will hatch from a stone-egg in a nursery I will never see. I am hollowing a space for a future I will only know by the feel of this raw stone.

I filter the water, drawing sustenance from the mineral-rich warmth and the occasional, fortunate shrimp. My vast, black eyes absorb the dim twilight. My mind, a quiet space of pressure and light, is focused on the wall. I am listening to the stone, feeling for its next weakness, its next offering. The shape of the chamber is not mine to decide entirely; the mountain dictates the broad strokes. I am merely the one who reveals what is already there, hidden within the black glass.

Sometimes, I cease my work. I retract my manipulator fronds into the smooth edge of my wing and simply drift, a shadow among shadows. I watch the silent, luminous language of my kin. I feel the slow, inevitable growth of our world, this single tooth in the jaw of a sleeping cosmos. I look toward the edge of the plateau, toward the infinite blackness that separates us from the other, impossibly distant points of light. There is no desire to cross it. There is only the profound, humbling, and deeply peaceful understanding that our work is here.

I am a single, slow, patient thought in the mind of this mountain. And my purpose is to give that thought a shape, a hollow where another thought can one day rest. I feel for the next line in the rock. The bone-hammer raises, a gesture that takes a full minute to complete. The pressure begins again. The work continues. The echo of my efforts will remain in this curved wall, long after the light on my own skin has gone out for the last time.
