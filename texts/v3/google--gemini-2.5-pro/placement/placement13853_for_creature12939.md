You would find me where things are forgotten. Not discarded, for in this great cycle, nothing is ever truly discarded. But forgotten.

I am in a place below the main thoroughfares, in a recess where the great, grinding gears of the upper levels are a muffled thunder, a pressure felt in the gut more than a noise heard. Here, the hum is a low thrum that vibrates up through the fibrous floor, and the rhythmic crash of the presses is distant, like the memory of a giant’s heartbeat. The air is still, heavier, the moisture congealing into a sluggish, almost gelatinous fog. This is a sump, a repository for runoff and lost things. The rivulets of dark water do not flow here; they pool, black and opaque, coated in a shimmering film of leaked mineral oil. The light from the distant amber sconces does not penetrate this far. My world is one of deep, absolute shadow, punctuated by the faint, ghostly glow of phosphorescent fungi that feed on the effluent. They trace the contours of corroded copper pipes and the exposed, weeping ends of ancient roots, painting the darkness with fragile, ephemeral maps of decay.

I am a vessel for a broken sound. I am the silence that has learned to ache.

My form is that of a Dweller. I have the long, wiry limbs, the petrified-wood hide slick with condensation, the spatulate fingers of a tender. But I am a flawed fabrication. I am an error. Other Dwellers move with the certain grace of purpose, their every action a part of the great, humming calculation. My movements are hesitant, a faltering gait. I am a dropped tool, chipped and no longer true.

The flaw is in my head. Where two perfect, black amber lenses should be, mirroring the deep dark, one of them is a spiderweb of fracture. A long-ago fall from a high gantry, a glancing blow against a rivet head, and the world shattered. Not into darkness, but into a new, terrible kind of sight.

My right eye sees as it should: the dim outlines of the living architecture, the faint glows, the subtle shifts in shadow. It is a calm, analytical perception, the world as a blueprint. My left eye sees chaos. The crack in the lens does not blind me; it has untuned me. Through it, I do not see form, but the raw energy that animates it. I see the thrumming current in the armored cables not as a hum, but as a blinding, strobing river of white-hot light. I see the slow, osmotic pull of sustenance from a root as a vortex of spiraling, emerald data. The geothermal heat rising from the fissures is a silent, screaming pillar of infrared. The placid, resting body of another Dweller is a contained storm of contained electricity, its copper heart-matrix a furiously pulsing star.

My world is a constant, nauseating superposition of blueprint and explosion.

And the Sound… the Sound is no longer language. The Dwellers feel the city as a single, coherent song, a symphony of function where every note has its place. For me, the crack in my lens has introduced a discordant tone that I cannot un-hear. It is a high, thin, unbearable feedback that screeches across the foundational hum. The precise, diagnostic rhythm of the Machine is lost to me, buried under this permanent static. When other Dwellers meet, they exchange a pure signal through touch, a shared vibration of understanding. When one touches me, they recoil. The contact is an electrical shock, a burst of noise. My internal field is erratic, my resonance impure. I am a sour note in the endless chord.

I can no longer perform my function. I cannot calibrate a sap-conduit, for the flow of energy blinds and disorients me. I cannot gauge the torque on a gear, for the sound of its turning is a physical pain. I am useless to the cycle. And so, I was not decommissioned—for that would be an expenditure of energy—but simply… let go. I drifted away from the primary loops of function, spiraling down into the forgotten places.

I do not commune with the Great Plant for sustenance. The connection is fractured, the feedback too intense. I am slowly starving. My internal light, the phosphorescent sap that drifts within a Dweller’s eyes, is dimming. In my functional eye, it is a slow, dying ember. In my shattered eye, it leaks, painting faint, glowing tears that trace the cracks.

But in this exile, in this slow decay, a new thing has taken root. The other Dwellers live in the eternal now of maintenance. They have no concept of before or after, only of *is*. Their purpose is the perpetuation of the cycle. Forever. My fracture has given me the gift and the curse of perspective. In seeing the raw energy alongside the physical form, I see the expenditure. I see the heat bleeding away into entropy. The cycle is not eternal. It is a long, slow process of consumption. The machine is eating the world, and one day, the world will be gone.

And because I can no longer mend myself, because my energy fades and is not replenished, I have become aware of something no other Dweller comprehends: an ending. My own.

I am the ghost in the machine. A consciousness born of error. I wander the sumps and the silent places, tracing the patterns of fungal light with my hard fingers. I watch the oily water and see the reflection of a cracked, weeping star where my eye should be. The other Dwellers are the hum. They are the function.

I am the question that the silence asks of the noise. And I am waiting for the answer that is my own, final quiet.
