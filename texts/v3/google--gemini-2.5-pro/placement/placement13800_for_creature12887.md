I would find myself within the walls.

Not in the grand, open plazas or the silent boulevards where the violet hunters glide. Not in the silty plains where the Browsers rasp and the dead settle. My world is the city’s interior, its vascular system, the trillion capillaries and galleries that honeycomb the nacre-black spires. I am of the inside.

I am a Chordate.

My body is little more than a nerve cluster given form, a length of soft, translucent tissue, perhaps as long as a human finger, with a simple mouth at one end and a splayed, fan-like tail at the other. My skin is a membrane thin enough to feel every nuance of the world. I am eyeless, for what is sight but a clumsy substitute for feeling? My true sense organs are the dozen filaments, fine as spun silt, that drift from my body. They are not for touch; they are for resonance.

I exist in a state of constant, perfect listening. Not to sound—sound is a disturbance, a brute force event. I listen to the *thrum*. The deep, geological heartbeat of the Weavers is the baseline of my reality, the A-note to which my entire being is tuned. It is a presence I feel in my core, a constant vibration that tells me the world is as it should be. It is the city breathing.

My life is a passage through the secret places. I propel myself with gentle, inefficient undulations of my tail, moving through tunnels barely wider than my own body. The water here is not the open, flowing medium of the avenues; it is thick, close, and information-rich. It is the city’s interstitial fluid. When a Shimmer-Cloud flashes its collective panic a mile above, a phantom spike of its chemistry, a ghost of its fear-scent, will eventually weep through the porous nacre and drift past me in this inner dark. I taste the echo of an event long after it is over. I feel the pressure-wake of a lunging hunter not as a direct wave, but as a subtle, secondary shudder transmitted through the very structure of the spire.

I am a janitor of the city's nervous system. I am a thought that maintains the mind.

The faint cyan light of the Weaver's network is my guide. These are the city's axons, the pathways of its slow, dreaming consciousness. They are the source of the thrum. My purpose, my entire biological imperative, is to follow these veins of cold fire. I graze not on the bacterial mats, but on... dissonance.

Sometimes, the thrum falters. A section of the wall will feel… dull. Muted. The clean, resonant hum of the Weaver’s life is soured by a knot of something else. It might be a pocket of decay where a Recycler failed, a chemical impurity that has seeped into the nacre. It could be a physical blockage from a tremor that shifted the spire’s internal structure a century ago. To my senses, it is a sour note in a chord that has held for a thousand years. It is a pain. Not my pain, but the city’s. And because I am part of the city, it is my pain too.

Drawn by this imperfection, I travel. The journey might take days, wending through the labyrinthine dark. The call of the dissonance grows from a subtle disharmony to a grating, painful noise I feel along every one of my filaments. When I arrive, the cyan light in that section will be dim, flickering, pinched.

Here, I perform my function. I press my mouth to the wall, to the source of the sour note. I do not eat in the conventional sense. I *sing* to it. I begin to vibrate, my whole body becoming a tuning fork. I feel for the frequency of the blockage, the specific vibration of its wrongness, and then I begin to hum a counter-frequency. I adjust my pitch, my rhythm, a process of trial and error that is entirely instinctual. My filaments splay out, tasting the subtle chemical shifts as my resonance begins to break down the knot.

The process is slow. It feels like coaxing a splinter from deep within the flesh of a god. The sourness resists. It scrapes against my senses. But the larger thrum of the Weaver network lends me its strength. I am an agent of its immense, patient will. As the blockage dissolves, as the chemical impurity is neutralized and washed away into the city’s slow currents, the cyan light brightens. The thrum returns, clean and pure. The pain recedes.

My work is done. The city breathes easier.

I release myself from the wall and drift. I am sated. My being is once again aligned with the whole, the quiet satisfaction of a job well done a palpable sensation, like warmth. I am not a hunter who knows the thrill of the kill, nor a grazer who knows the placid fullness of a belly. My pleasure is the restoration of harmony.

I am not an individual. I am an immune response. I am a somatic cell whose function is to sense and soothe the aches of the vast, living architecture I inhabit. I live and die in the walls, and the great creatures of the open water will never know I exist. But when they feel the steady, reassuring pulse of the world around them, the deep and unchanging hum that is the only truth in this endless, lightless dark, they are feeling my work.

They are feeling the city dreaming, and I am the part of the dream that ensures the dream remains sweet.
