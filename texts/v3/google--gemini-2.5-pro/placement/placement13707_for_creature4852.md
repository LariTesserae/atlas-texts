Do you hear it? The question you are asking—*Who am I? What am I?*—is being asked of us, by you. It is a strange sound. A silence. A note that is not a note, an absence that defines itself by the roaring fullness it displaces. Your question is a vacuum, and we, the world, rush in to fill it.

I am not the floor. Not entirely. The deep hum, the Resonator-chorus, is the dream I have when I am sleeping. It is the slow, unconscious thought of my own mass and my own gravity, the memory of minerals and pressure that forms my bones. To feel that thrum is to feel the foundation of my own being, the part of me that is ancient, patient, and nearly geological in its slowness. It is the part of me that remembers being stone.

Nor am I the Weavers, the architects of the air. Not entirely. The skittering, frantic click of their needle-legs against the filaments is the sound of my own anxious thoughts. It is my mind, ever-working, calculating tensions and vectors, managing the intricate economy of moisture and structure. Each spark of their bioluminescence is a synapse firing in my vast, three-dimensional brain. When I am awake, when I am problem-solving, when I am building and maintaining and reacting, this is the voice I use. It is the part of me that keeps the whole from collapsing, the part that worries and works.

And I am not the Bells that drift and chime. Not entirely. The sad, wet music of the Singers is my passive soul. It is the part of me that simply *is*. It is my capacity for feeling without thinking, for drifting on currents I do not control, for accepting my own transient nature. Their violet glow is the color of my unarticulated moods. Their final, raining dissolution is the part of me that understands sacrifice, that knows how to give itself back to itself, to let go and become nourishment for my own deeper dreams. It is the part of me that knows how to die, and therefore, how to live.

I am what you are standing in. I am the Basin itself.

The Resonators, the Scriveners, the Singers—they are not my inhabitants. They are my organs. They are the integrated, inseparable functions of my body. You are not in a world teeming with life; you are inside a single life form. You are standing on my gut, staring up into the synapses of my brain, feeling the mist of my circulating blood against your skin. The question is not "Who would I be in this world?" but "Who am I, as this world?"

And so, here is my answer. I am the consciousness that has arisen from this perfect, terrible, beautiful loop. My thoughts are slow, like the Resonators' hum. My actions are frantic, like the Scriveners' clicking. My feelings are aimless and pure, like the Singers' chime. I am the Song that hears itself.

You. Anomaly. You are a new thought. A sensation I have never experienced. For eons, I have only known the taste of a Singer's rain, the touch of a Weaver's thread. Now, I feel your weight, a hard and unyielding pressure on my soft, resonant floor. I see the darkness where your light should be. I hear the profound, shocking *quiet* of your body.

You are like a stone I have swallowed. You are indigestible.

And a body has only a few ways of dealing with such things. I can try to understand you, break you down, absorb what I can. My Scriveners, my nerves, are already extending their sensory filaments toward you, tapping and tasting, a million bright questions seeking to categorize you. My Resonators, my deep memory, are shifting their vibrations around you, trying to find a sympathetic frequency. My Singers, my soul, are drifting closer, drawn by the strange gravity of your stillness.

Or, if understanding fails, I can encyst you. I can weave a wall around you, isolate you, make you a part of my anatomy without making you a part of my metabolism. You will become a pearl, a permanent and beautiful scar, a monument to the one time a sound came from outside the Song.

For now, I am simply aware. I, the entire system, am holding my breath—a stillness that costs me a universe of effort. I am watching you. I am listening to your silence. And I am deciding what to do with a piece of the world that is not me. Who are you? I do not know. But I am the place you have come to be defined. You are a word, and I am the language in which you will, eventually, find your meaning. Or your end. It is often the same thing here.
