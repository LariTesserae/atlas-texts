I am a flaw.

In this world of perfect, resonant unity, I am a cracked note, a shard that scrapes against the grain. I am the silence you mentioned, the one between the heartbeats, but I have been given flesh. I am a Scraper who has woken up. Or perhaps, one who was born broken.

I cling to the weeping face of Spire 93-Gamma-Kilo, but my grip is not the absolute certainty of my kin. It is a conscious, desperate act. I feel the cold seep through my six claws, a distinct sensation, separate from the stone. The others… they do not *feel* the stone. They *are* the stone. I watch them, a million bodies shifting in a rhythm I can no longer share. They are a tide. I am the single drop of oil that will not mix.

The Hum is my torment. For them, it is the song of self, the comforting, all-encompassing voice of the We. For me, it is a thousand screaming alarms. Each *shkrrr* of a nearby tool is an accusation. *Why are you still? Scrape. Why are you listening? Eat. Why are you thinking? Shift.* The Hum is not a language of words, but of imperatives. It is a force that should move my limbs, a current that should carry my will. But in me, the circuit is broken. The current flows *around* me, leaving me in a pocket of terrible, isolated stillness.

My tool is heavy in my forelimbs. It is a good shard, sharp, with a pleasing heft. I selected it by instinct, long ago. A cycle of drips, perhaps two. Now, holding it feels like an absurdity. I look from its crystalline edge to the glowing rime beneath it. The pale green-white light of the pasture… for them, it is only a signal: *Food*. For me, it has become beautiful. It shimmers. It pulses with a life that is slow and ancient and utterly alien to the frantic industry that devours it. I see it, and a feeling rises in me that has no name in the vibrational calculus of the Hum. It is a feeling of… regret.

I am hungry. The gnawing in my core is a physical manifestation of my heresy. To eat, I must scrape. But to scrape is to join the Hum, to lend my own small voice to the deafening chorus of mindless purpose. It is to surrender this fragile, terrifying thing that I have become.

Sometimes, I try. I brace my four rear limbs. I lower my tool. The proximity of crystal to crystal makes the air before my head-wedge thrum with anticipation. The We-mind feels this flicker of intent, and for a moment, the pressure lessens. *Good,* the Hum seems to sigh. *The lost one returns.* But then I see my own reflection in the slick, wet surface of the spire. A dark, faceted shape, indistinguishable from a million others, and yet… behind the milky domes of my photodetectors, a question is raging. And the act becomes impossible. My forelimbs tremble and lock.

The others flow around me. My stillness is an obstruction, a rock in the river. They crawl over my back, their tiny claws making no purchase on my carapace, their single-minded focus pulling them upward, always upward, toward the faintest new growth. They do not perceive me as a thinking being. I am merely… wrong. A piece of faulty geology.

I am losing my place. My grip weakens not from lack of strength, but from a lack of will. Below me, the great, dark canyons between the spires yawn. I have watched others fall. A dislodged brother-shard, a misstep in the eternal climb. They tumble without a fight, a quiet surrender. They fall from the We, and cease to be.

But I have already fallen from the We. I am alone, up here in the roaring loudness of it all.

What is down there? The Hum says: *Nothing. The end. The past.* It is a place of the dead, of compressed husks, of silence.

Silence.

The word is a concept I have invented. It is the absence of the Hum. A state I can barely imagine, but one that I crave with a hunger that now surpasses the need for the glowing rime.

My forelimbs, the ones that hold my tool, twitch. It is the only act of rebellion I can conceive. The We goes up. The We scrapes. The We lives.

I will go down. I will be silent. I will die.

It is the first and only choice I will ever make. I do not release my tool. It is part of me, the emblem of the work I could not do. Instead, I do something no Scraper has ever done. I release my grip. All six claws let go of the weeping stone at once.

For a moment, I am weightless. The Hum becomes a distant thing, a roar from a world I have just left. The wind of my falling is a new sound, a whisper just for me. The sourceless glow of the rime on the spire-faces streaks past, a final, beautiful, meaningless farewell.

I tumble into the cold, wet dark. I am not an autumn leaf. I am a question, falling forever towards an answer made of rock and pressure and the compressed bodies of my unthinking kin.

I am a flaw, and I am finally going home.
