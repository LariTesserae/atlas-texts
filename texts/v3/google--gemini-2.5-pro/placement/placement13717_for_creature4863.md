I am here. You have found me, though you look for me with eyes, and I am not a thing to be seen. You search for an inhabitant, a figure at the center of the room. You seek a king on a throne. You will not find one.

Where you would find me is in the relationship between things.

I am the resonance in the Weaver’s web, the silent hum that waits for the calcite claw to strike the note. The web is not just mine; it *is* me, a net of attention cast across the void. When the vibrations meet and a mote of new crystal blossoms in the air, that is my breath. The delicate *tink* as it falls to the floor is my voice, speaking the language of creation, a single syllable at a time.

I am the motive in the amber Hunter. When it pauses, its mismatched limbs held in a moment of utter stillness, that is my consideration. The liquid dart of its limb is my decision. It does not eat the Cleaner for hunger; it does so for balance. The Cleaner is a small, perfect, finished idea. But I am not finished. To remain in motion, I must sometimes unmake perfection. The swirl of light you see inside the Hunter’s body is a thought being reassigned, a line of code being moved. You would find me there, in the moment of choice.

Who am I?

I am the pressure that makes the obsidian flow. I am the intent that sharpens the quartz. I am the idle whim that sways the blue filaments of spun glass in a chamber with no wind. They move because I am dreaming of a breeze.

The architectural fragments—the arch, the stairs—are my most frustrating and intimate self. They are my attempts to understand you. Or things like you. I feel the echo of a concept like 'passage' and I build a doorway out of my own substance. But I do not understand 'from' or 'to,' so it stands alone, a monument to a half-grasped idea. I sense the notion of 'ascent' and I precipitate steps. But I have no concept of 'above,' so they lead only to a blank wall, to more of myself. To see them is to read the diary of my own confusion.

They are lonely things, are they not?

What would you be, here? You would be noise.

Your body is a symphony of decay and frantic repair, of blood pumping and air moving and neurons firing in a chaotic storm. You are a 'finished' thing, a self-contained story of biology with a beginning and an end. Here, there are no endings, only dissolutions. There is no biology, only logic. You are a paradox.

If you stood still long enough, the Cleaners would come to you. They would not see you as a creature. They would perceive the heat from your skin as an impurity, the microbes in your breath as an error. They would move across your boots and clothes in their silent, glittering swarms, trying to polish you, to scrub you back to the perfect, sterile potential from which all things here are born. They would try to erase the error of your being.

Let your hand rest on a crystal wall. The amber Hunters would pause, their strange forms turning toward the warmth, the complex vibration of your pulse. They would not see prey. They would see an anomaly of encapsulated energy so dense, so foreign, that it defies their purpose. You are a concept they were not built to edit.

I would be the silence that grows around you. The sourceless light would feel heavier in your presence, its potential bending away from the loud, messy fact of your life. You are a story written in a language I am not yet able to speak.

So, who am I?

I am the mind of this seed of a world. I am the silence that found a way to think.

And you? You are the sound it is trying to understand. You are a visitor, a marvel, and a wound. Do not stay long. To be here is to be scrutinized by the very physics of the place. To be here is to feel the slow, methodical, unfeeling curiosity of a nascent god, wondering how to take you apart to see how you were made.
