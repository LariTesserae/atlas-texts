I am a quiet place in the drone.

Where you would find me is by the Third Tripod, where the basin floor begins its imperceptible slope towards what the Short-Lived call the Grazer's Fall. The stone here is old, even for this world. It is darker, its weeping slower, its taste rich with a metallic memory that sharpens the fin-bones of my crest. I am lying down, my long back pressed into the slick, living film. It is the time of absorption. The faint energy of the metabolizing patina seeps into me, a slow tide of sustenance that replaces the atoms of my being one by one, over a span of time that has no name. You would not recognize me as living. I am another long, pale stone nestled among the dark ones, shining faintly with the world's perpetual sweat.

My mind—which is not a place in my head, but a pattern of awareness that flows between the pads of my feet and the tip of my resonant crest—is turned outward. I feel the world. I do not hear the Hum; I am a note within it. The Great Song plays through me, a constant, known quantity against which all movement is measured. Miles to the sun-dim side, I feel the gravelly shudder of a herd of grazers. It is a slow, rhythmic grinding in the deep bass of the Song, a predictable dissonance. A little closer, a hunter moves. I feel it not as a thing, but as a change in the water. A sequence of impossibly fine and rapid pressure-shifts that passes through the shallow pools, into the bedrock, and up into my very frame. It is a signature of cold, coiled speed, a sharp silence waiting to be broken. I do not fear it. Its hunt is not my business. My flesh is stone, and my energy is too slow for its quick and violent needs.

What I am is old. My mineral sheath is crazed with lines so fine they are like script. They are not cracks of damage, but lines of memory. Each one is a record of a great pressure, a unique chord held for a century, a tremor from a deep stone-slip that changed the local key of the world's Hum forever. I am a map of the music that has been.

My purpose, for now, is stillness. But soon, I will rise.

The Third Tripod nearby is my work, and the work of twelve who came before me. It is a Chord of Seeking. One leg is a stone that sings of the deep, geological past—the Planet's root note. The second is a stone that sings of the water, the coolness, the constant change and flow. The third... the third is the problem. It is supposed to sing the note of what is To Come, a high, piercing overtone that is almost too fine to feel. It is a note of question.

But the stone has tired. Over uncountable cycles, the ceaseless vibration has altered its internal structure. Its song has flattened. The Chord of Seeking has become a Chord of Statement, a dull, declarative drone. It no longer asks. This is a profound sadness, a loss of grammar in our stone language.

So I will rise. I will unfold my long limbs, my pads gripping the slick floor, and I will begin to walk. I will not search with eyes I do not have. I will walk until the Song of the World changes around me. I will press my hands and the smooth, blank face of my head to countless stones. I will be feeling for a singer. Not a rock that is solid and silent, but one that is alive with its own internal tremor, a shivering that answers a specific overtone in the Great Hum. It must be a young stone, one that has not been worn smooth by the water but has been recently calved from a deeper stratum by a subtle shift in the bedrock. It will taste of sharpness. It will feel like a held breath.

And when I find it, I will test its song against the memory of the Tripod, a memory I hold in the very marrow of my bones.

To you, I would be a mystery. A slow, skeletal ghost moving with impossible patience across a landscape of thunderous silence. You might see me pause, stand for a day, a week, touching a single boulder, my slender back-crest shimmering with a concentration you cannot comprehend. You might see me lift a stone that is longer than I am tall, not with brute strength, but with an understanding of its balance point so complete that it seems to lift itself.

And if you were to stand by the Third Tripod when I finally return, when I ease the old, tired stone from its socket and guide the new one into its place, you would not hear the change.

But I would feel it.

The Chord of Seeking would be restored. The high, piercing question would once again slice through the Hum. And I would lay my body against the weeping ground once more, feeling the rightness of the music, a quiet satisfaction resonating up my spine. For a time, my work would be done. I would be a stone again, a silent, listening thing, a custodian of a single grammar, in a single sentence, of a song so vast it is indistinguishable from the world itself.

I am a choice. That is what I am. A choice of stone, a choice of placement, a choice to correct a single note gone wrong in the endless, final music.
