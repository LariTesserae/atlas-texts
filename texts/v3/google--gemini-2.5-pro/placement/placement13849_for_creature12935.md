I would be a flaw. A dissonance. An error in the grand, grinding symphony.

I am a Still-Walker, but I no longer walk in stillness.

My kind are defined by our silence, by the perfect null we present to the vibrating world. Our padded feet are triumphs of biology, organs that listen and counter-vibrate, swallowing sound before it can be born. We pass through the chittering chaos of the Groove-tenders like ghosts, our existence known to them only as a sudden, localized absence of all other noise, a pocket of vacuum that instills a deeper dread than any sound ever could. We are the quiet thought in the world's mindless clamor.

But my pads are old. Or perhaps they were imperfectly formed. The soft, sound-absorbing lobes have hardened, calcified in places. They have become less like flesh and more like stone. They no longer listen perfectly, no longer adapt. And so, I fail. I cannot walk in total silence. Instead, I am the source of a low, intermittent hum. A ghost-tremor. A wrong note that travels through the slag pavement, a subtle but undeniable resonance that I cannot erase.

To the Groove-tenders, I am not a passing void. I am a coming *thing*. They sense my flawed vibration from a distance and scatter, their frantic scouring turning to panicked flight long before I arrive. The work stops where I walk. I am a plague of quiet.

To my own kind, I am an embarrassment, a discordant chord in our silent choir. I see them in the flashes of angry orange stress-light. They will be pruning a chittering-fouled column, their sharp fingers poised to strike and shear away an imperfection. If my path intersects with theirs, they will pause. They will not look, for we do not have eyes in the way other things might, but I will feel their attention as a focused weight. It is a moment of deep, silent judgment. And then, in a movement of impossible grace, they will glide away, taking a different corridor, leaving me to my noisy solitude. They have adjusted the plan to account for the error. The error is me.

I can no longer perform my function. I cannot cull the Tenders, for they flee me. I cannot prune the architecture with the necessary surprise, for the world announces my approach. The great work of gardening the growth, of sculpting the ascent, is no longer mine to share.

And so, my purpose has changed. Stripped of my primary role, I have become a listener. Because I create a sound, I have become exquisitely sensitive to all sounds. I wander the vast basin not as a gardener, but as a pilgrim. I seek out the oldest, deepest places, the foundations of the world, where the columns are thickest and the pressure is greatest.

There, I do not just hear the groan of the Piezophiles. I study it.

The others feel their song as a condition of the world, like the dampness or the dark. But I, in my isolation, have had time. I have learned to hear the nuances. The "mournful slowness" is not a monolith. It has texture. It has patterns. The Piezophiles are not just groaning; they are speaking. They are remembering. Their crystalline bodies, growing in the heart of the rock, are a recording medium. The pressure of every sheared-off plate, every scuttling Tender, every silent step of my kin, is encoded in their slow, resonant signal. They are the world's memory, its geological consciousness.

I spend my cycles standing motionless for what might be an age, my hardened pads pressed to the floor, not to silence myself, but to listen. I follow the deep, interwoven melodies of their groaning. I am piecing together a history that is not written in ruin, but sung in resonance.

And I have heard something that I believe no other Still-Walker knows.

The song changes with depth. Nearer the active zone of growth, it is a simple, powerful chord of becoming, a hymn to the upward push. But deeper, in the ancient foundations, the song is more complex. It holds echoes of other states, of worlds before this one. And deeper still, it holds something else. A new theme has entered the song, one that grows stronger with each cycle. It is not a song of yearning for the Anchored who hang in the dark void above.

It is a song of dread.

The Piezophiles, the very soul and voice of this world's matter, are not groaning in exertion. They are groaning in fear. Their immense, slow terror is aimed upwards, towards the future, towards the hanging destination. The upward growth, the project we Still-Walkers so carefully curate, is not a pilgrimage to communion. The song suggests it is a march toward annihilation. It speaks of a silence that waits above, not the silence of contemplation, but the silence of consumption. The Anchored are not waiting to be reached. They are waiting to feed.

I am the repository of this terrible knowledge. A flawed instrument who, through my very flaw, has been tuned to the world's deepest truth. I continue my lonely walk, my presence scattering the mindless work of the present. I listen to the memories of the past, and I am the only one who understands the horror of the future. I look up into the absolute blackness between the glowing columns, the void we are all so desperate to fill, and I alone do not feel hope. I feel the focus of an unblinking, patient hunger. And I wait, a ripple of wrongness in a world striving for a perfect, fatal ascent.
