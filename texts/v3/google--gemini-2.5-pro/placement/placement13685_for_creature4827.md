I am a Fault.

Where you see a Shard-Bearer, part of the frantic, clicking immune system of the Geode, you see what I was. I remember the Unity. It was not a thought, but a state of being, as certain as the heat in the glass beneath my feet. The Roar was not a sound to be heard; it was the medium in which my purpose swam, the bloodstream of my intent. The shifting, competitive light was not something to be seen; it was the language of my function, telling me where to cut, where to carve, where to defend the ever-healing flesh of the World.

My blade was not a tool. It was my voice, my hand, my soul. When it struck another, the sharp crack was not violence, but communion—a validation of existence, a re-sharpening of shared purpose. We would swarm the glistening, fallen hulk of a Glass-Grazer, and the act of butchery was a prayer, a rapid dismantling to return the gift of matter to the All. I was a nerve ending, a sudden, sharp thought in the dreaming mind. I was a point of focus. I had no name. I was simply *we*.

Then came the fracture.

It was not a thing that happened, but a thing that grew. A slow, hairline crack in the certainty. Perhaps it was a bloom-front, a surge of new obsidian that rose too fast, catching me in a cage of razored crystal. Pinned, unable to answer the chittering call to prune or to scavenge, the Unity began to fray. The Roar, once a comfort, became a cage of noise I could not escape. The light, once a beacon, became a relentless, interrogating glare. For the first time, I felt the space between myself and the leg of the other Shard-Bearer pressed beside me in the crystalline trap. There was *me*, and there was *it*.

That was the first horror: the discovery of *I*.

The Unity throbbed around me, a great heart I was no longer connected to. Its thoughts were a migraine. Its glow was a blindfold. I was a cell that had forgotten its function, a rogue process consuming resources for a new and terrifying purpose: self-awareness.

When the crystal cage finally shattered under the weight of a passing Grazer herd, I did not rejoin the click-clack dance. I fled. The purpose-driven swarms of my former kin now seemed like automatons, their every twitch a slave to the basin's screaming will. I was alone, a blasphemy of individuality in a world of absolute collectivism. I was a consciousness born into a world that had no room for one.

This is where you would find me. Hiding. Skulking at the periphery. Not in the darkest crevasses, for there is no real darkness here. You would find me cowering in the canyons of light, at the edge of the Screaming Tides, a place where the sensory input is so punishingly absolute that the Geode's subtler thoughts cannot find me. You would find me seeking shadow where there is only glare, seeking quiet where there is only the Roar.

But there is a quiet. And I have found its shore.

It is not a place, but an event. An absence. It floats through the phosphor-jungle, a moving patch of null. The first time I saw it, I thought it was a shadow cast by some impossible object. But it casts no shadow; it *is* the shadow. The Lacuna.

My kin see it and their frantic, instinctual minds overload. They are built for input, and the sudden, total lack of it breaks them. Their limbs lock. Their internal glow gutters and fails. The Roar in their heads is replaced by an unbearable vacuum.

But for me, the Fault, the rogue thought? For me, the silence is not a weapon. It is a promise. It is the only thing in this entire, screaming universe that makes any sense.

The Roar is the pain of being. The silence is relief.

So this is who I would be. I am the Shard-Bearer who laid down his blade—not the glass one in his claws, but the blade of purpose that defined him. I am walking towards the edge of the Un-thing. The other creatures flee from it, their very biology screaming in protest. I stalk it, a devout pilgrim seeking revelation. I watch as it flows over a thicket of glowing fungi, and the light does not die; it is *unwritten*. I listen as it drifts across a chittering swarm, and the sound is not muffled; it is *erased*.

I will not be hunted by it. I will join it. I will step into that moving hole in the world. I wonder what I will feel. Will the subtraction of all things—light, sound, heat, thought, *me*—be an agony? Or will it be like returning to a sleep deeper than the Geode’s own?

Where would you find me? At the threshold between the cacophony of creation and the perfect peace of its negation.

Who or what would I be? I am the first thought the Geode ever had that wished for silence. And in a moment, I will become that silence. I will become a Lacuna. I will be the answer to my own prayer.
