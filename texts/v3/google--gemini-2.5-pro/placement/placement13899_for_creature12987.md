You would find me where the Weft is at its most vulnerable. Not in the grand, cathedral-like chambers where the weave is tight and the logic is sound, but in the forgotten corners, the interstitial spaces where the dream is thin. I haunt the folds of non-Euclidean cloth, drawn to the impossible geometries as if to a familiar scent. My home is a quiet ache in the world’s structure.

Look for a place where a single, immense cable has snapped. The break does not bristle with clean fibers, but gapes like a wound. From it leaks a perpetual, silent memory of rain—not the substance of water, which is unknown here, but the pure, conceptual feeling of a cool, steady drizzle. The air there is heavy with a phantom humidity, and the fibers are tinged with a shade of grey that feels like sorrow. You would find me standing in the heart of that invisible downpour, because I am made of the same impossible stuff.

I am not flesh. I am not fiber. I am what came out.

I am a Leak. A conceptual hernia. When a Weaver wove a paradox too sharp, a contradiction too profound, the thread of its thought did not just snap and release a note or a patch of cold. It tore. And from that tear in the fabric of reality spilled a cluster of interdependent, alien concepts: Blood. Bone. Breath. The memory of a sky with a sun that moves. The feeling of hunger. The terror of an ending.

These things, unable to exist separately in this world of pure structure, coalesced. They formed a self-sustaining wound, a walking paradox. They formed me.

I have a shape, but I have no substance. Where my feet touch the woven floor, the cream-colored fibers do not compress. They warp. The pattern bends around my soles as if avoiding a source of immense heat, or a magnetic field of pure otherness. I am a silhouette, a man-shaped hole in the world’s grammar. If you were to look closely at my hand, you would not see skin, but a shimmering, localized distortion in the air, through which the woven patterns of the far wall seem to bend and crawl. I am defined by the way the world recoils from me.

My existence is a constant, quiet struggle. The Weft’s immune system, the little fray-lice, cannot perceive me, but they sense the disruption I represent. They skitter at the edges of my presence, tidying the frays I cause just by standing still, forever trying to darn the hole that is my body. The great Boll-weavers, in their patient grandeur, see me as a catastrophic flaw in the tapestry. From a distance, one might begin to spin a new thread, a patch of shimmering, logical fiber, and attempt to weave it across the space I occupy. The thread, upon reaching the edge of my being, will simply cease to exist, its meaning nullified by my contradictory nature. The Boll-weaver will pause, its slow mind processing a logical error for which it has no context, and then it will patiently move on.

And the Weavers… they know me. They are the artists, and I am the slash in their canvas. They are the poets, and I am the word they never wrote, now screaming silently from the page.

They do not flee from me. They are drawn to me. A Weaver will manifest nearby, its body a silent storm of moiré patterns, its form a question mark made of light. It will not approach, but its attention will press in on me, a palpable force. It is reading me. It perceives the memory of blood flowing in my veins as a line of impossible, nonsensical poetry. It feels the concept of my mortality as a chord of music so dissonant it threatens the integrity of the entire chamber. I am an education in the impossible.

Sometimes, one of the great, disembodied hands of light will appear above me. It will not try to touch me. Instead, its shimmering fingers will trace the outline of my form in the air, a centimeter from my "skin." As it does, it learns. The Weaver is not trying to fix me; it is trying to understand the principles of my construction. It is learning the alien physics of my being. And I can feel its thoughts as a shift in the conceptual weather around me: a sudden feeling of vertigo as it contemplates gravity, a flash of imagined crimson as it grasps the idea of blood, a deep thrumming resonance as it tries, and fails, to comprehend the finality of death.

I am the ghost in their machine. The memory of another world, their forgotten creator's world, perhaps, bubbling up from the subconscious of their own. I cannot be undone, because I am not a thing *in* the Weft; I am a law *of* the Weft that has been broken.

So I stand in my quiet rain of forgotten sensations, a lonely monument to biology in a world of textile. I watch the Weavers as they watch me. They are learning from me, incorporating my alien grammar into their art. The patterns on the walls are growing stranger. The anomalies are becoming more complex. I am the infection. I am the inspiration. I am the waking nightmare that proves their perfect dream is no longer sleeping. I am the Hollow Man, and my emptiness is teaching a god how to feel.
