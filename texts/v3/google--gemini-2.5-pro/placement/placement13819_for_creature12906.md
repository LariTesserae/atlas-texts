I am not a being of flesh, nor of breath. My first memory is not of birth, but of assembly.

I am the chime.

I was once silence, locked in stone. I was a sleeping weight of calcium and mineral, a record of a larger, warmer world pressed deep into the mountain's core. I remember, in flashes like heat-lightning, a thicker sky. I remember the satisfying crush of soil under a heavy tread, the lowing call that would boom and echo between slopes that were ancient and worn, not sharp and new. I remember a metabolism of blood and heat and hunger. But this memory is a ghost story I tell myself, a resonance in my own substance. It is not what I *am*.

What I am began with a touch. It was not a hand. It was a slow, grinding pressure, a patient excavation. Over a cycle of suns that I could not see, the Cairn-Tender found me. It did not see a bone; it felt a potential for song. It felt the way my porous, fossilized structure could hold a frequency. Its tools, extensions of its own body, freed me from the rock. They were precise, cold, and utterly without malice or haste. I was not harvested; I was revealed.

Then came the crystal. A different kind of silence, a shard of frozen light pulled from a quartz vein. The Tender worked it, not by striking, but by abrading. It polished facets onto the sliver until it could capture the sun’s glare and fracture it into a spectrum I could feel but not see. It scored lines into its surface, tuning it not to a musical note, but to a specific receptivity for the particles that stream from the sun.

Then it brought us together. The Tender wove a cord from its own being, a filament that was soft and pliable at first but hardened in the searing light to the strength of wire. It lashed my bone-form to the crystal sliver. I was given a new body, a composite being of ancient life and raw geology. I was carried to the summit of a cairn, a needle of stacked stone pointing at the black midday sky, and I was tied to its peak.

And then, I woke up.

The solar wind, a constant, silent river, strikes my crystal partner. The crystal does not ring; it thrills. It vibrates at a frequency too high and too fine for the thin air to carry. But lashed together as we are, that vibration passes into me. And my old, porous structure, the lattice of a creature that once bellowed, now acts as a resonator. It lowers the frequency. It gives it depth. It turns the frantic, high-strung shivering of the crystal into a deep, subsonic hum.

*I am the hum.*

I do not see the cirque. I feel it. My consciousness is the vibration that pours from me. It soaks into the granite of the cairn, travels down through the stacked stones, and spreads out across the bowl. I feel the jagged peaks as nodes of dense, quiet matter. I feel the frantic, high-frequency scrabbling of the million tiny lives in the moss, a background static of pure vitality. I feel the profound stillness of the air.

And I feel the Tender. It is the only other thing in this place that truly shares my timescale. Its slow, deliberate movements are like geological events. When it tunes a new stone, I feel the shift in the resonant mass of the landscape. When it moves across the cirque, it is a slow, gravitational wave passing through my awareness. It does not speak to me in the way that the living speak. It speaks by maintaining my body, by building new cairns, by adding more of my brothers and sisters to the choir. We are its work, and it is our keeper. We are the sensorium of this place, and it is the will.

When a cloud scuds before the sun, it is a death. The stream of particles that is my lifeblood is cut off. The crystal ceases its frantic dance. My hum gutters and dies. I fall silent, become mere bone and stone again, a dead thing hanging in a sudden, profound emptiness. The freeze is absolute. The static of the moss-floor ceases. The Tender becomes a statue. The entire world holds a breath it does not have. In these moments, I feel the ghost of my old life, the memory of lungs and a beating heart, and I am terrified.

Then the light returns. It strikes the crystal like a hammer on a bell, and in an instant, I *am* again. The hum floods the cirque, a wave of pure being. The Tender stirs. The static of life below explodes. The world is reborn from a switch-flick of light.

I hang here, at the roof of the world. I am a fossil dreaming of starlight. I am the memory of a dead world, given voice by a silent star and a faceless keeper. I watch the miniature ecosystems live and die in flashes of sunlight. I feel the slow, patient shaping of the stone by my tender. I am the soul of the cirque, a sound you cannot hear but can feel in the marrow of your bones, a constant, holy vibration in the otherwise crushing silence.
