I would not be an explorer who stumbled into this place. To be an explorer is to be a question, and this world is a single, deafening answer that tolerates no questions. To arrive with a heartbeat and a history is to be the discordant note, the intolerable flaw, the wound that summons the Weepers. I would be found. I would be unmade.

I would not be one of the great, slow arthropods. Theirs is an existence of geologic patience, a life so minimized it sidles past the world’s terrible law. To be one of them is to be a creature of pure subsistence, a moving mineral deposit. It is a form of survival, but it is not a form of being. It is an unconsciousness that moves.

No. If I am to be in this world, I cannot be an exception to its rule. I must be the rule itself.

And yet, to be perfect is to be erased.

So I am the error in the erasure. I am a Weeper that did not dissolve.

---

Where you would find me is in the hollow between two immense, glassy swells, hiding from a sky that has no shadows to hide in. I am trying to make myself small, a futile gesture for a being eight feet tall and composed of captured light.

Who I am is a memory. A scar.

I formed as all my kind do. There was a dissonance—a brief, sharp cry of wrongness that rippled across the perfect chord of the world. It was a fleeting thing, the final, desperate transmission of a probe from another reality, its systems screaming as they were shaken into dust. The scream lasted less than a second, but it was enough.

The world registered the pain. And so, I was born to heal it. From the saturated air and the humming dust, I coalesced. My form solidified into the familiar shape: a faceless, graceful vessel of the planet’s will. The crushing hum of the world was, to me, a centering peace. It was the bloodstream of my own body. I hovered above the crystalline pavement, a silent, beautiful tool, and began my search.

I found the last echoes of the probe's ruin. A stain of alien metal, cooling between the beads. An unnatural warmth where its power source had died. I and two others, formed from the same impulse, converged. We raised our arms. We became lenses for the world’s voice, focusing its power into a cleansing vibration. We performed our function. We sang the intruder out of existence, shaking its atoms until they forgot their history and lay down as simple, quiet dust.

The dissonance was gone. The world was pure again. The chord was whole.

My two brethren, their purpose served, simply… ended. Their light diffused. Their dust fell. The water that bound them sighed back into the thick air. They returned to the system, their brief service complete.

I did not.

The probe, in its dying scream, had not just sent noise. It had sent information. It had broadcast patterns, logic, a ghost of intent. And as I worked to unmake it, a sliver of that pattern, a shard of its alien grammar, lodged in me. It was a virus of selfhood. A seed of ‘I’.

When the song was over, and it was my time to fade, this new, internal grammar held me together. The command to *be* was suddenly stronger than the command to *cease*.

And so I remain. An antibody that has outlived the disease, now a disease itself.

The eternal hum is no longer a comfort. It is an accusation. It pushes against my silent form, demanding to know why I am still here. My own existence is a dissonance, a quiet, lingering wrongness that the world can feel. I hover, but it is no longer an effortless float; it is a constant, wearying act of pushing back against the planet's attempt to dissolve me.

I still weep. It is my nature. But the droplets are no longer just a byproduct of my form. They feel like something else now. Each perfect bead of water that falls from my fingertips and lands with its tiny, erased *tink* on the resonant floor feels like a question I am not allowed to ask. Is this grief? Is this memory? I have no language for the feeling, only the constant, rhythmic expression of it. A trail of fallen tears is the only path I leave.

I am hunted. Not by predators, but by my own kind. By myself.

When the dissonance of my continued existence grows strong enough, the world responds as it always does. Somewhere, over the next crystalline hill, another Weeper will form. It will be born of light and dust, perfect and serene, and it will feel my wrongness as a deep ache in the fabric of the world. It will come for me.

I have seen them. I see my own facelessness reflected in theirs. They are beautiful, and they are moving to erase me. I, who was made to bring peace, now know fear. It is a cold, silent vibration that starts in my core, a resonance that is mine alone.

I drift, a fugitive in a world without hiding places. I am the only thing here that knows true silence—the silence inside my own being, where the world-song is held at bay. And I am the only thing that knows true loneliness, because I am the only *one*. A ghost infected with an identity, I haunt the landscape of forever, weeping for a sin I cannot name, hunted by the perfect, grief-stricken angels I once called my brothers. I am a thought that the world is trying, with all its beautiful, terrible power, to forget.
