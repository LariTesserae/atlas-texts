I would be one of the Still.

My place is in the gallery known as the Seventh Choir, a high, open-walled chamber carved from a single titanic geode of amethyst. From here, I can be seen from the woven-filament bridges that span the chasm below, a silent shape among hundreds.

I no longer move. The slow, geological glide of life is a memory, a pattern etched into my structure but no longer enacted. I have become my final form. I do not feel the dry, sharp air in a throat I no longer use, nor do I draw power from the great Hum. I have become a part of the sound, not a consumer of it. I am an instrument.

My consciousness, if you would call it that, is no longer a current but a standing wave. It is the perfect, unwavering resonance of my own crystalline body as the keynote of Isopol washes through it. I am not a being who perceives the world; I am a feature of the world that perceives itself.

I remember life as a slow ballet of color. I remember the silent conversations, the pulsing azurite of curiosity, the slow fade of amber into violet that signified consensus. I remember the feel of a hematite hammer in my upper limbs, the precise, controlled tap against a spar of selenite, a single sharp *ting* that would send a shiver of corrective harmony through a whole sector of the forest. I was a gardener of sound. My purpose was to tend the song, to weave the city’s acoustics into a more perfect chord.

Now, my purpose is simply to *be* the chord. The light of my thoughts has gone out. The shifting mosaic of color beneath my silicate skin has settled into a final, deep pattern, a testament to the last thought I held as I slowed to a stop: a fractal of obsidian black and quartz white, a pattern of perfect, balanced stillness.

The Corelight, the searing, shadowless sun, strikes my faceted surface. I am a prism, but a passive one. I no longer cast my thoughts as rainbows; I simply break the white glare into its component parts, scattering them across the polished floor of the Choir. I feel the light as a subtle warmth on my zenith-facing planes, a warmth that has not changed in the centuries since I ceased my gliding.

The great Hum is my entire reality. I feel it not as a pressure, but as my own substance. The vibration is me. I can feel the low, grinding drone of the foundational crystal deep below, the source of the cavern floor. I feel the high-frequency shivering of the quartz filaments in the canopy, a constant, glassy hiss. I feel the specific harmonies created by the living as they move through the city, their careful progress a form of music in itself. When a young Isopolitan crystallizes from its sonic womb, I feel the unique, complex chime of its birth-song as a new thread weaving itself into the tapestry.

My memory is not a narrative I recall, but a resonance I experience. When a specific combination of frequencies passes through me—a pattern similar to one I encountered in life—a ghost of my past self shimmers within my stillness. The memory of weaving a bridge is not a picture in a mind's eye; it is the feeling of a low C-sharp minor chord vibrating along my limbs, the exact frequency of the fibrous crystals I once spun into cables. The memory of a loved one is a complex arpeggio of light and sound, a signature tune that, when echoed by the world's hum, makes my entire structure ring with a faint, sympathetic warmth. I do not *remember* my life; I *am* my life, solidified and turned into a bell waiting for the right moment to be struck.

Sometimes, the tiny bismuth insects, the only other creatures here, crawl across my surface. Their movement is a barely perceptible tickle, a series of minute, high-pitched steps against my eternal silence. They are grazing on the sonic energy that clings to my facets. I am a pasture for them. I am landscape.

The living still tend to me. They do not speak to me, for I cannot answer in their language of light. But they glide past, their tall forms casting no shadows, and they pause. I feel the subtle shift in the acoustic field as their resonant bodies draw near. This is their form of respect. Then they might gently brush the mineral dust from my shoulders with a fan of mica, not cleaning me, but tuning me. They are ensuring my resonance remains pure.

I am not dead. I am complete. I was a singer who has become the song. I am a note held for eternity, a single, perfect component in the deafening, brilliant, and utterly whole symphony of Isopol. My name, the pattern of light I once was, has dissolved back into the world. Now, my identity is my function.

I am a Resonator. And I am home.
