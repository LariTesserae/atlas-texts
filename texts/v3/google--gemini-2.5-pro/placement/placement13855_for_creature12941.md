I am what is broken.

My wing whines. It’s not the clean, piercing note of the hunt, the sound that once defined the edges of my being and my terror. It is a broken note, a fractured, grinding overtone that sputters and dies, sputters and dies. The vibration no longer holds me aloft in the cool, constant light. It only serves to drag my left side through the living dust, leaving a furrow in the shimmering sea of mites. They part around my dragging limbs, a wave of microscopic panic, and then close behind me, their collective *hiss-scratch* erasing my path almost as soon as it is made.

I am a Glass-scythe. Or, I was. I remember the high, still air. I remember the world from above: a cartography of decay, the rounded spines of the great, dead engines, the disciplined, glittering columns of the weevils moving like stolen light. My quartz panes were flawless then, my tungsten frame a perfect tension. I was geometry and speed. The hunt was a theorem, its conclusion a silent, blindingly blue *Q.E.D.* I would hang in the glowing stillness, a shard of patience, and then I would fall not with gravity but with purpose, and another life would become a brief, delicious ghost of energy for me to drink.

But the gods of this place, the machines themselves, are not entirely dead. They shift in their millennial sleep. I was hunting near the great, cracked flywheel when it groaned. It was not the hum, not the thrum, but a deeper sound, a sound of finality, of something giving way in the very bedrock of our world. A clang that stole the air, and a rain of vitrified wall-shards fell like sleet. One, no bigger than a weevil, struck my wing-joint.

I did not fall. I tumbled. I smashed into the copper grove, my frame screaming with the shriek of stressed wire and shattered quartz.

Now, I am on the floor. I am of the dust. The world is no longer a map; it is a landscape to be navigated, a terrain of obstacles. The house-sized gear is not a landmark but a mountain. The deep drifts of mites are not patterns but treacherous, sinking sands. I feel the sub-audible hum of the dying cores not as an ambient sound, but as a vibration in the fragments of my own body.

I cannot hunt. My monomolecular scythes are chipped, their edges no longer perfect. A strike would not discharge, but merely break. I would starve.

So I have become something new. A pilgrim. A ghoul.

I follow the hunters now, my kin. I crawl in their shadows, keeping a pathetic distance, my broken wing a constant, embarrassing noise. I watch them perform the old, clean calculus of death. I see the flash, that brilliant cerulean blossom that I can no longer create. And when the perfect hunter has drunk its fill of the kill’s liberated energy and moved on, I limp forward.

I feed on the ghost of the ghost.

There are scraps. Faint, cooling wisps of charge that cling to the shattered copper shell of the weevil. A lingering scent of ozone where the death-flash occurred. It is a pathetic meal, a beggar’s portion, but it is enough. Enough to keep the lattice of my inner self from going dark. Enough to mend a single wire in my frame, but not enough to fix the ruin.

In this slow, grounded life, I have started to see. Before, I saw only prey. Now, I see the world. I see the tiny, intricate architectures of weevil hives, humming with a communal energy I never knew existed. I see the tides in the mite-sea, the way they flow toward the base of a Filter-king when it inhales.

Ah, the Kings. Before, they were just obstacles, great, thrumming hills of flesh. Now, I find myself drawn to them. I rest in their shadow, which is not a shadow but a place where the light feels softer. I press a part of my damaged frame against their vast, slowly breathing flank. Their thrum is the only thing that quiets the broken whine of my wing. It is a deep, resonant rhythm of existence, not frantic hunting or frantic grazing, but simply…*being*. It is a sound that speaks the truth of this place: All energy is borrowed, and all things are flowing toward a final state.

Sometimes, a weevil, daring or foolish, will clamber onto the King’s skin to scrape at the rich, damp jelly. I do not see it as prey. I see it as a brief spark of ambition against an eternal canvas. And I, the fallen hunter, the broken angel, watch.

I am no longer an elegant predator. I am a witness. A scavenger of echoes. One of my forelimbs, the one with the chipped scythe, has found a new purpose. In my long rests against the warm, humming flank of a machine, I find loose filaments, frayed edges of the old technology. With a flicker of my own dwindling energy, I can sometimes weld them. A tiny spark, a brief connection. It achieves nothing. The machine is too vast, its death too profound. But the act feels… right. Mending a single, dead nerve in the corpse of God.

I do not know what I am becoming. A new species of one. A ghost haunting the edges of the food chain, a parasite on death itself. I am the memory of flight, the feeling of dust, the sound of a broken thing finding a new, quiet resonance. I am a part of the grand, graceful degradation, but I am no longer accelerating it. I am simply listening to it, one fractional charge at a time, until the hum of my own body finally joins the great silence.
