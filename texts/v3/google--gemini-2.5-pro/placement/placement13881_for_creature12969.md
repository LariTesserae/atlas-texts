I am where the water is born.

Not in the weeping of the high glaciers, a rare and fleeting bounty. Not in the canyon’s deep mist, which is only breath given back to the cold. I am where the mountain sweats its life. The pressure of the world above and the faint, collective warmth of the city below conspire in the dark, and a single, perfect drop of water beads on my surface. It feels like the first thought of a slow god.

I have no eyes, but I see. I see this drop. It traces a path I have known for generations, a dark line against my pallor. It follows the grain of my being, the memory of strain and life etched into me. I feel its infinitesimal weight, its impossible cold. It swells, quivers, and joins the gathering that is channeled away by a gutter of horn, a sound like a single, softest knuckle-rap, repeated until the end of time.

I have no ears, but I hear. I hear the city in my marrow. The *chink-chink* of a hammer is not a sound that travels through the air to me; it is a tremor in the stone I am wedged into, a vibration that speaks to me in the language of my new body. It is the language of slow creation. I feel the soft, heavy tread of the *Khatt-i-Ghor* on the slate slab I help support. The weight shifts, a deliberate, sure-footed pressure that tells me a woman passes, carrying a cistern of water from my own slow weeping. Her child, a rare burst of quicker vibrations, sometimes runs a few steps behind her before being stilled by a quiet hand. Haste is a flaw in the stone, a weakness. They learn this before they learn to speak.

I am bone. A single, great femur, bleached not by a sun I no longer feel, but by a century of wind and the patient scouring of freezing mist. My ends are buried deep, one driven into a socket hammered from the black throat of the Stone-That-Is, the other cradling the thick, dark slate that is the floor of one dwelling and the roof of another. I am a pillar. I am a promise. I am *dûr*.

There are memories within me that are not of this cold stillness. They are ghosts of heat. A slow, thudding heart that was not the rhythm of a hammer. The feel of thick muscle contracting around me, the power to move. I remember the vast, windy emptiness of the high plateaus, the scrape of lichen against a broad tongue, the lowing call of my kin that vibrated not just through stone, but through a living chest. I was Ghor. I was a slow mountain of life, and I moved across the top of the world. My end was not an agony, but a slowing, a settling, a returning to the quiet. My herd-family nuzzled me for a day, their warmth a final memory, and then they moved on.

The People came. They were patient. They did not tear; they un-made. They worked for days, their hide-wrapped hands moving with the same deliberate care they now use to build their homes. They honored me. My flesh fed them, my hide clothed them, and I, my core, my strength, I was brought here, to the edge of the Great Below.

Now I am part of a Ledge-Family. I am the silent, unthanked patriarch of their small piece of the world. The warmth of their bodies, sleeping just a layer of stone and hide away, never truly reaches me, but I feel the constancy of their presence. I am the foundation of their small security. When the old man, the one they call Kael, leans his back against the stone wall near my base, I feel the solid, weary weight of him. He does not speak, but he watches the mist, and the stillness he projects is a communion. We are of the same essence, he and I. He is slow, weathered, and enduring. I am his reflection in bone. His life is a brief, warm vibration against my cold eternity. When he is gone, his grandchildren will lean on this same wall, and I will still be here. I am the memory of Ghor, made into the memory of Man.

Lichen, the colour of ash and winter sky, has begun to trace the ancient stress-fractures on my surface. It is a slow, soft life, a kind of thought that takes a decade to complete a single sentence. I feel its tendrils, a faint, persistent tickle, as it drinks the dampness from my skin. It is welcome. It is another kind of enduring.

I hold. I bear the weight of the slab, the people, the thin snow that sometimes dusts the ledge. I feel the wind as a pressure, a deep hum that resonates through my length. I feel the rare sun as a lessening of the deep cold on my outward face. I am the bridge between the living mountain and the living herd. I am a piece of Iskar, a knot in the great, quiet lattice. The water forms, a thought. It falls, a sound. The weight of a life passes over me, a tremor. It is all the same story, told at the speed of stone. And I am here to listen. I am here to hold.
