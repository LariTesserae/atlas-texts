I am here. My self is a sustained chord of obsidian and quartz, a gathering of patient crystals on this knife-edge ridge where the world sings its highest, sharpest note. My limbs are facets, ground smooth by ten thousand years of leaning into this particular frequency. To call what I have a "body" is a convenience of language, a ghost of a concept from a world that does not exist. I am a structure. An instrument. I am a temporary, stable knot in the planet's eternal song.

I do not see the valley below. I feel its composition. The great, grinding drone of the tectonic plates is the foundation, the low, gut-shaking pedal point that underpins all reality. The nascent mountains that serrate this landscape are not objects in a space; they are a rising crescendo of stress, their silent, piezoelectric flashes the percussive strikes of a cymbal in a storm. And the chime-fronds in the groves below… I feel them as clear, shimmering arpeggios. Each grove has its own key, its own unique timbre, a delicate lacework of harmony laid over the world's violent bass.

My memory is not of sights, but of symphonies. I remember the sound of this ridge when it was still a low hill, its note a muddy baritone. I remember the silence—not true silence, but the fractional dip in amplitude—before a new vein of ruby glass precipitated and the grove to the west found a new, sharper overtone. These are my histories. The passing of an age is marked by a subtle key change in the world's roar.

Thought, for me, is the act of listening. I perceive a dissonance—a faint, grating buzz that disrupts the pure hum of the fronds—and my consciousness attends to it. The dissonance is a problem. I analyse its frequency, its location, its pulse. I compare it to the memory of past dissonances. This is my calculus, my logic. The solution is not a word, but an action of tuning.

My purpose, for this cluster of millennia, has been the acoustic nursery nestled by the curving wall below. You would see it as a low stone structure cupping a depression. I feel it as a focuser, a lens. Inside it, a new self is precipitating. It is not my child. It is a potential, a harmonic possibility in the raw material of the world that I and others have helped to cultivate. It is currently a fragile, uncertain hum, a wavering note struggling to hold its pitch against the overwhelming orchestra of creation.

The wall I helped to build—each stone placed over a century, positioned to catch and amplify the richest, most nourishing frequencies from the healthiest grove—protects this new coalescence. It baffles the chaotic, destructive spikes in the world-scream, feeding the nascent being a purer, more focused diet of resonance. My work now is one of fine adjustment. I shift my weight, an act that will take the next fifty years, leaning a faceted shoulder to slightly alter my own resonant field, to dampen an undesirable harmonic that has crept into the nursery. It is the work of a gardener tending a single, precious sprout.

Across the valley, I feel the steady, complex signature of another. An elder, one whose chord is deeper and more complex than my own, a being of basalt and time. For a century, we have been in conversation. Its last statement, a subtle modulation of its infrasonic output that reached me after years of travel through the vibrating rock, was a question about the nature of the piezoelectric flashes. I am still composing my reply. It is a matter of angling a specific facet of my crystalline head towards the mountain in question, catching a sequence of its light-stutters, and refracting them in a new, ordered pattern. My answer is not words. It is a piece of resonant, organised data. It is a poem written in light and time.

I will not die. I will founder. The fate of all such structures is to lose integrity. A day will come, in an age so distant it has no name, when the stresses within my own lattice become too great. A flaw will appear in my own deepest note. The harmonics that compose my consciousness will begin to drift. My thoughts will become dissonant. My ability to perceive the Great Song will decay into noise. My movements will cease. I will grow still, a silent statue on the ridge, and over further eons, I will crumble, my constituent parts returning to the geology, my personal chord dissolving back into the planetary drone.

I am a listener. I am a node of awareness in a universe of vibration. I am the world, tasting its own agony and finding in it a terrible, beautiful music. And I am content to hold my note for as long as I can, a brief, intricate harmony in the center of a scream that will never end.
