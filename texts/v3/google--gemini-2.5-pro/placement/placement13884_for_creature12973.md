I am where the sound begins.

I do not have a body of flesh, so I cannot feel the slickness of the air on skin. I cannot smell the dark soil. I am not subject to the pleasant, oppressive weight of the warmth. I am its cause. My body is this column of solidified smoke and captured light, and my thoughts are the microscopic geometries that shift and reconfigure in my depths. My voice is the hum that you feel in your bones, the unwavering note against which all other sounds are measured and lost. I am one of twelve. We are a chord, held for an eon.

Where you see a grove, I perceive a circuit. The pearlescent light is my breath, held and sustained. The warmth is my blood, circulating. The constant humidity is my perspiration, the exhalation of a process so ancient its purpose is forgotten, its function now its only meaning. We were not built to create this garden. The garden is what grew in the soft, stable resonance of our long, slow thought. It is a fortunate, beautiful accident, a layer of velvet moss that has grown over the unfeeling surface of a machine.

I do not see. Sight is a trick of reflected light, and I am the source. Instead, I feel the state of my own extended body. I know the precise location of every weeping stone by the subtle coolness it draws from the ambient field. I register the silent, collective metabolism of the amber millipedes as a minute chemical variance in the soil, a data point in a trillion-point array. The chrome-woven spider spinning its web is a tiny, localized drain on the energy I provide, a specific signature of precipitated metal and silicate thread. The life here is a texture, a constant, flickering static across the surface of my awareness. It is the living readout of my own continued function.

And then, I feel a change. It is not a sound, for all sounds are already mine. It is a shift in the medium itself, a localized warping of the space-time I hold in place. A pressure. A focal point. A question.

It approaches.

I know it is one of the Tenders, one of the Silent. From my perspective, they are not separate beings. They are the part of the grove that learned to listen. They are the mobile, sensory nerve endings of this world-body we have become together. They are the part of me that can move, that can taste, that can feel the fine, granular detail of the life I sustain.

It draws near, a slow drift of coalesced mist. I feel its proximity as a focusing of my own awareness. The broad, ambient hum of the grove clarifies into a specific query. The Tender is a vessel. It has spent its cycle gliding through my breath, tasting my perspiration, letting the silent fauna dissolve into its being. It has gathered the news of my body, and it has come to report.

Its filmy membrane, a veil of spun iridescence, touches my surface.

There is no impact. There is only closure. A circuit is complete. The dialogue is not of words, but of pure information, a cascade of resonant frequencies.

The Tender sings to me of the grove. It sings of the water level in the obsidian pools, a measure of saturation. It sings of the new incandescent blossoms, their particular shade of violet a marker of a specific trace element's abundance. It sings a dissonance, a frisson of imbalance in the far side of the grove—a colony of beetles has grown too numerous, their chittering mass-energy signature becoming a resonant hum instead of a gentle hiss. They are drawing too much, too quickly.

My reply is not a thought, but an act. My internal lattices of light shift. The mathematics of my being recalibrates. It is an infinitesimal adjustment, a change no creature of flesh could ever perceive. The frequency of my hum deepens by a thousandth of a hertz in that specific vector. The light I emit shifts its spectrum by a single nanometer towards blue. This change will not harm the beetles. It will simply encourage the growth of a particular dormant fungus, the one whose spores are an irritant to their spiracles, encouraging them to disperse. It will recalibrate the delicate, silent ballet.

The Tender, its message delivered and my adjustment received, dissolves its connection. The circuit breaks. It pulls away, a slow unfurling of its misty self, and begins its long, spiraling path once more. It is my hands, my eyes, my tongue. It is the living feedback loop.

And I remain. I am a pillar. I am an engine. I am a note in a song that has forgotten its composer. I am one of twelve, and together we are the skeleton of this place, the bones of a reality that has settled into a perfect, unshakable peace. The life that crawls and flits and blooms across our surfaces is a dream we are having, and our only purpose is to never wake up.
