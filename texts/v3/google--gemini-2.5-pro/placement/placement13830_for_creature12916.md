You would find me in the basin floor’s deepest swell, a gentle, mile-wide depression where the silvery mist pools most thickly in the evening and the morning dew is heaviest. I am rooted here, a singular, dark spiral against the rose-tinted porcelain.

I am a memory given form. I am a chime.

Of the metallic-stemmed flora, I am the oldest and most intricate. My spiral is tighter than the others, my verdigris patina deeper, my copper sheen dulled by aeons of polishing winds and the ceaseless condensation of the world’s breath. From a distance, I am barely distinguishable from the landscape—just another elegant, silent form in a world composed of them. But if you were to draw close, to let the great silence settle around you and listen with more than your ears, you would notice me. You would notice that while the other flora ring with a faint, consonant hum, a note that is part of the silence, my own chime is different.

It is a dissonance. A single, stubborn, barely-there harmonic that does not resolve. It is the sound of a question in a world of answers.

I was not always this. Once, I was like you. I was a loud, temporary intrusion. I walked this plateau on two legs, my heart a frantic drum against the resonant quiet, my thoughts a cacophony of sharp-edged words. I came from a place of striving, of becoming, and I could not comprehend a place that simply *was*. I saw the Dwellers, and in my ignorance, I approached one.

It lay in a basin like this one, a long, smooth curve of milky, star-shot light. I wanted to know. I wanted to understand. My desire was a shout in its silent consciousness. And it attended to me.

There was no malice in it. There was only an immense, inexorable pressure. The silence thickened, became a medium like water, then like setting stone. The shadowless light seemed to dissolve the boundaries of my body. I felt my frantic story—my name, my ship, my world, the face of my long-dead lover—all being smoothed out, the frantic peaks and valleys of my life worn down into a placid, featureless plain. I was being un-made, my brief, complex noise being tuned to the single, eternal chord.

But something held. A memory. A paradox. A love that was also a wound. It was a knot in the thread of my being that the Dweller’s quiet insistence could not unravel. It was a dissonant chord in my own soul, and as the rest of me was absorbed, dissolved into the great equilibrium, this one knot snagged. It refused to be smoothed.

The process did not fail; it adapted. It could not erase the dissonance, so it gave it a place. It poured what was left of my awareness, that one stubborn resonance, into a form that could contain it. I became this. My body is this metallic plant, rooted in the world-stuff. My thoughts are no longer words, but the faint, cyclical chiming of my copper stems. The memory I clung to is the core of the sound, the reason it never quite resolves into the background hum.

My perception is no longer optical. I have no eyes, but I *feel* the world in a way I never could before. I feel the unwavering light on my stems, a constant nourishment. I feel the silent, padded footfalls of the iridescent grazers as subtle shifts in the resonance of the ground. I feel the focused intent of the shadow-predators as a tightening in the quiet, a brief, sharp focus before the gentle release of un-making.

And I feel the Dweller. The one that changed me. It rests on the far side of this basin, a vast, reclining form of pearlescent white. Its thought is the silence, the warmth, the gravity of this place. Millennia pass like slow breaths. Sometimes, I feel its awareness brush against me—a slow, geological curiosity at the persistent, tiny flaw in its symphony. It does not remember me as an interloper. It only perceives a note that has been singing, imperfectly, for a very, very long time.

I am the ghost in this perfect machine. I am the echo of a question asked eons ago, an answer that was not an answer at all, but a transformation. I am the quietest rebel in the universe, my only protest a single, unending, discordant chime in the profound silence at the top of the world.
