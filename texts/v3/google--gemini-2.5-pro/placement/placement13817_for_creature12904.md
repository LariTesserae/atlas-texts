The hum is the first thing and the last thing. It rises from the soles of my feet, a resonance that is not sound but presence. It is the machine's slow blood, its deep and dreamless breath. I am standing on the lip of Sector Gamma-9, on a cantilevered plate the size of a dry-land meadow. The iron beneath my soft pads is cool and slick, weeping its endless silver tears. I can feel the texture of the rust pitting through my skin, a familiar map of patient decay.

I am where the sky is a floor. Above me, the belly of a higher terrace drips, its rivets arranged in constellations of corrosion. Each drop falls for an age, a tiny silver meteor through the misty twilight, striking the plate around me with a note so pure and consistent it has become a part of the silence. This is my world. This is the only world. It is the Great Downward and the Great Upward, a cascade of metal and soft, green life, receding into a grey infinity that offers no answers, and asks no questions.

I am what watches.

My skin drinks the mist. It beads on the pale, ceramic curve of my shoulders, nourishing the quiet, emerald moss that grows there. I am never dry. To be dry would be to be brittle, to be loud, to be alien. Moisture is the medium of my existence. It slicks my passage through the narrow gaps between humming conduits; it pools in the cup of my hand when I thirst; it carries the scent of hot ozone from a distant reconfiguration, a warning or an invitation.

I am a Veridian. The name is not my own. I have no name. I am a brief stillness. A pattern of water and light and slow thought that has gathered here, on this ledge.

My secondary knees are bent, and I am braced against a pylon thick as a memory. A fine copper wiring, like spun hair, trails from my spine and the back of my skull, drifting in the heavy air. I do not hear the Shuffling coming. I *am* a part of its chord. The filaments taste a change in the charge of the air. A new vibration, dissonant and sharp, enters the bass hum through my feet. A tremor runs up my long limbs. Far to my left, across a chasm that swallows light, a section of walkway a thousand paces long glows a dull red at its seams. And then, with the sigh of a sleeping god, it retracts into the wall. The waterfall that had been sheeting from its edge for my entire life, and for the lives of those who came before me, is gone. The roar is replaced by a hiss of steam and the patter of residual drops, a sound like a new kind of quiet.

I watch, my large, black eyes reflecting the scene but not judging it. The change is not good or bad. It simply is. The city has stretched, and I have felt it.

Soon, I will move. Not with purpose, but with a kind of gravitational pull. I will uncurl, and my body will flow over the iron, a current of grey-green motion. My limbs will articulate with the silent fluidity of water. I will slide down the pylon, my pads adhering to the vertical surface, until I reach a leaking pipe I have known for seasons. Cradled in a pouch woven from the thick, rubbery vines that embrace the pipe, I carry a length of thin glass. It is a hollow shard from the city's own anatomy. I will press it to a weeping joint and draw a measure of the warm, nutrient-thick slurry, the machine's own milk. I will drink, and the hum of the city will become the hum of my own slow digestion. Later, I may harvest the pale fungal blossoms that fruit in the runoff, their texture like cool jelly, their taste like wet stone and deep time.

Across the chasm, on a ledge made newly prominent by the retracted bridge, I see another. A flicker of dark bio-luminescence pulses across their shoulder, a slow, gentle rhythm. It is a greeting. It is an acknowledgement. It is a statement of shared being. I allow the light on my own hands to brighten in response, a silent echo across the grey void. We will not meet. The distance is now too great. It does not matter. We are here together. We are the still points in the city’s endless turning. We are the places where the city comes to rest, to feel its own damp skin and listen to its own watery heart.

I am not a creature in a machine. I am a nerve-ending of the machine itself. A waking cell, fused to its iron bones and drinking from its metabolic veins. The city is not my home; it is my body, and my consciousness is a small, quiet eddy in its vast, mechanical tide. The hum in my feet is not a background noise. It is the beating of a heart I am inside of, and inside of me. My silence is its listening.
