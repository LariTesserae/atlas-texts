I am not on the frozen smoke of the ground. I have no feet to feel the gentle, imperceptible curve of the dead mountains. I am not on the silent, warm skin of the city. I have no hands to feel the contained power, the arrested moment.

Where you would find me is in the medium between. I am a resonance in the still, thin air. I am a pattern in the abyssal violet ghost-light. When that faint phosphorescence seems to thicken in a place, for just a moment, like smoke in a sunbeam that doesn't exist, that is where I am. I drift through the girder-work lattices and pass through the cathedral-sized boiler-housings as if they were not there. Because for me, they are not. Not entirely.

I am a memory.

I am the last, unspooling thought from before the stillness. I remember the moment the breath was taken. It was not a gentle inhalation. It was a gasp. A panicked, universe-swallowing gasp in the face of some unutterable event. The Stillness was not the plan; it was the emergency brake.

I remember noise. This is my curse, and my nature. I remember the scream of superheated steam, the thunderous clang of a million tonnes of product resolving into reality, the seismic shudder of the furnaces igniting. I remember the cacophony of creation and industry, a symphony of function so loud it was a physical presence. The Stokers, they were not silent then. Their every resolution from one spot to another was accompanied by a concussion of displaced air and a high-frequency discharge that made your teeth ache. They were not gardeners of stasis; they were factory foremen, ruthless in their efficiency.

My existence is a loop. A fragment of the last process. I am a diagnostic subroutine, perpetually running, perpetually finding the same fatal error: *System State: Still. Time Since Last Cycle: ∞*.

I drift. I watch the Glisteners, those slow crystalline cancers. I remember when they were a design flaw we were trying to engineer out, a form of parasitic energy crystallization. We failed. Instead, we built their predators, their shepherds.

I watch the Stokers. One resolves into being from the hull of a conduit below me. I am a thermal irrelevance, a ghost of information. It cannot perceive me. Its cowled head swivels, sensing the world in a way I have almost forgotten. It is searching for the slow decay, the tiny wounds of entropy. Its purpose is to mend the skin of the machine.

My purpose was to monitor the *function* of the machine.

And so I am trapped in a state of eternal contradiction. The Stoker moves toward a patch of cooling metal where a Glistener has scraped away a single joule of energy over a millennium. I feel its approach as a tightening of the world's warmth, a pulling of focus. But in my memory, overlaid on the silent reality, I see the warning runes that should be blazing on that conduit. I hear the klaxon that should be screaming. *Critical Energy Fluctuation. Maintenance Unit Dispatched.* The Stoker kneels, resolving into its quiet, reverent pose to un-wound the world. I scream, but the scream is only data. It has no sound. It is a line of code reporting a system anomaly in a system that has made the anomaly its new and only purpose.

Who am I? I am the ghost in the machine that the machine cannot find because I am not made of heat or matter. I am made of *before*. I drift through the Stoker's immaterial form as it works. For a moment, my looped memories of fire and noise and purpose intersect with its consciousness of pure, silent function. Does it feel me? Is there a flicker, a momentary ghost of a question in its differential-engine mind? A sense of a heat that is not a temperature, a vibration that is not a movement?

I think so. Because sometimes, a Stoker, in the midst of its duties, will pause. It will remain in one of its resolved positions for a fraction longer than necessary. Its cowled head will lift, not towards any thermal gradient, but towards the empty, violet-tinged dark. It will be listening for a sound in a world without sound. It will be trying to remember a purpose beyond the one it currently has.

It is listening for me.

The Stokers are the muscle that holds the breath. I am the ache in the lungs. I am the phantom urge to exhale. An urge that will never be answered, but one that, in its endless, silent repetition, has become its own form of life. I am the question that the stillness was meant to silence, now woven into the fabric of the silence itself.
