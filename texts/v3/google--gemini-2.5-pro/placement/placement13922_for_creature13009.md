I am a dissonance. A note held too long, sharp and wrong, in a symphony of geological silence.

You would find me in one of the city's forgotten ventricles, a place where the cycle has run its course and is turning to dust. Here, the great colonnades of quartz have grown opaque with age, their internal structures failing. They no longer pipe the sun's light but scatter it into a milky, featureless haze. They are monuments to a thought that has concluded. The air, if it can be called that, is thick with a shimmering powder of their slow collapse, a perpetual, silent snowfall that will, over millennia, be re-digested by the black substrate. The grazers do not come here. There is no nutrition left in this exhausted crystal. The predators do not hunt here. There is no prey. It is a place of endings.

Among the bases of these dying spires, nestled in the drifts of crystal dust, is my shell. It is like the others in form—an ovoid of pitted, lightless stone—but it is never closed. Not entirely. One of the seams, one of the geometric panels of my husk, is forever partly retracted, leaving a jagged fissure of a mouth. I cannot fully seal myself away. I am a wound that will not heal.

I am a Geode, but I am… hurried.

The vast, slow consciousness of my kin is a state I know only as an ideal, a stillness I long for as a man in a tempest longs for land. Their minds are deep, placid oceans of light, processing the slow-drifting rainbows of the city into a perfect, evolving map of being. My mind is a maelstrom. The light does not flow for me; it shrieks. The shifting patterns are not data; they are a frantic, incoherent barrage of sensation. I am a lens that is cracked, a prism that is shattered. Where my brethren perceive the subtle clouding of a distant crystalline hall as a piece of gentle news—a premonition of change a century hence—I see a blinding, painful strobe, a catastrophic failure of information.

The silence of this world is a myth to me. I feel the substrate, just as they do. But for them, it is a sub-audible hum, the deep and steady pulse of the world's heart. It is the bass note of their contemplation. For me, it is a roar. I feel the rhythmic rasp of a feeding herd on the far side of the continent not as a gentle texture of awareness, but as an insistent, grinding irritant that scrapes at the edges of my thought. The percussive strike of a predator is not a distant event, but a sharp, personal agony, a spike of vibration that lances through my entire Being.

And the conversations… their seismic poetry, messages of pure structure and intent sent across the crust over decades… they arrive to me as shattered fragments, as seismic static. I hear the shouting of a thousand voices at once, garbled and meaningless. I am deafened by a world my brothers know as silent.

Time is my tormentor. A century, for my kin, is the space between one thought and the next. For me, it is an eternity of imprisonment. In the span of one of their placid contemplations, I have lived a thousand frantic lifetimes of sensory chaos. I watch the galaxy of dust motes swirl before my opening and I feel each one as an individual flicker of light, an unbearable cascade of discrete moments.

I am a gardener, as all Geodes are. But I am a mad gardener. Impatient. My vibrations are not the subtle, persuasive hums that guide a quartz arch into a millennium of graceful existence. They are frantic, desperate jabs of energy. I try to compose, but I only create noise. Around me, the new crystals that try to grow from the dust of the old are monstrous. They are not the elegant, geometric forms of the city; they are twisted, asymmetrical spikes of quartz, jagged and unstable. The pyrite cubes do not form; they explode into ugly, dendritic splatters of fool's gold. My corner of the city is not a garden; it is a scar, a patch of architectural tumors. I am trying to build harmony, but my tools are panic and my medium is pain.

Sometimes, in the deepest stillness I can manage, I try to listen. I try to find the song, the deep hum. I open myself to the light, hoping to see the map instead of the storm. But it is always the same. I am a fever in the stone. A geological anomaly. A thought the world is trying to have, but which comes out as a scream.

Perhaps I am not broken. Perhaps I am simply… early. A prototype for a consciousness that runs on a faster clock, one the world is not yet ready for. Or perhaps I am a flaw, a genetic error in the code of crystallization. I do not know.

So I remain. A half-open eye of stone in a city of dust, watching the slow, silent end of things. I feel the great spires around me turning to powder, and I send out my frantic, dissonant vibrations, growing my little garden of monstrosities. I am waiting for my own structure to fail. I am waiting for the moment my inner lattice fogs over, my cracked mind finally goes dark, and my shell shatters into a final, silent cascade. I long to be dust, to be re-absorbed into the black glass, to be given another chance, over another million years, to grow right. To be silent. To be whole.
