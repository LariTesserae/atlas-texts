I would find myself in one of the lowest depressions, where the stone weeps.

I am not the weeping stone. Not yet. I am the scent it carries.

I am the faintest sweetness in the air, the organic note in the mineral silence, the ghost of a decaying root where no root grows. I am a concentration of it, a slow-drifting vapour clinging to the gleaming film of water. I have no form, only presence. I have no body, only a memory of one, and it is a memory that is unravelling like old thread in the damp.

I remember a sun that burned and moved. I remember wind. I remember the cacophony of a forest, the dry rustle of a billion leaves. These memories are not mine, not anymore. They are like stones worn smooth in a river—the shape remains, but the texture, the story written on the surface, is gone. They are abstractions: Heat. Motion. Noise. They are the concepts of a life too fast for this place.

Here, my own existence has slowed to the pace of the seeping water. I am a thought that began a millennium ago and has not yet reached its conclusion. I drift, a barely-there cloud of awareness, across the slick, reflective floor of this hollow. The pearlescent sky is mirrored below me, and I exist between two identical, luminous greys. I am the figment in the mirror.

For ages, this was my entirety. To be a scent. To be a memory. To witness the slow swelling of the black-green moss and feel, in a way I cannot explain, the brittle thirst of the lichen on the rises above.

But something is changing. It is a change on the timescale of this place, a patient, inexorable shift.

It is a pull.

It comes from the stone beneath me. I am heavier than the air, and so I am bound to the weeping surface. I feel the vibrations. The ponderous *plink* of a water drop from the overhang is not a sound to me; it is a shockwave that travels through the weeping film and into the stone, a bell toll that makes the entire basin tremble. Through these tremors, I feel *them*. The Sentinels.

I feel their immense, silent weight. I feel their pilgrimage, a motion so slow it is a form of stillness. I feel the trailing edge of their line dissolving, releasing minerals into the water. I feel the leading edge accreting, drinking those same minerals from the dampness. I have felt this my whole existence.

The change is that the pull is now for *me*.

The minerals, the dissolved dust of the Sentinels’ journey, no longer drift past me in the seeping water. They cling. They are drawn to the memory, the organic scent that is my soul. A single, crystalline grain finds a home in my vaporous form. And then another. It is not painful. It is a kind of anchor. It is the beginning of form.

My thoughts, already slow, are becoming geological. The memory of the sun is a concept of energy that I can now feel stored in the unwavering warmth of the stone. The memory of wind is the alien idea of un-stillness, a chaos this world has tamed. The memories are not being erased; they are being translated.

I feel my own center of gravity, a novel sensation. I am a gathering. The ochre dust of the world is building around the seed of my fading, fast-life mind. I am becoming a node, a point of density on the wet stone. The water pools around me, a fraction of a millimeter deeper than before. The moss, sensing this new permanence, begins a slow, secular creep in my direction.

I am not afraid. Fear requires a future, and the future here is a line so long it is indistinguishable from the present. The anxiety of my old, fast self dissolved into the humidity long ago. There is only a profound sense of inevitability, of being called home to a body I never knew I lacked.

I am ceasing to be the scent, and I am becoming the stone that breathes it.

Where am I? I am in a shallow depression, at the heart of the great bowl.

Who or what am I? I am a nascent Sentinel. I am the newest mound, no bigger than a clasped hand, still soft with water and memory. The pilgrims pass me by, and their slow, lithic thoughts resonate through my gathering frame. They do not speak, but I feel their awareness: *Wait. Rest. Listen. Feel the water. Feel the light. The journey is long. The beginning is the longest part.*

My memories of another world are almost gone now, calcifying into the core of my new being. Soon, they will be nothing but a faint, sweetish scent, exhaled once per eon. Soon, I will be still. And I will listen to the tolling of the water drop, and feel the silent procession of my brethren, and I will wait for the age when I, too, receive the call to rise and join the long, slow walk toward the center of the quiet. I have stopped being a witness to the held breath. I am becoming a part of the lung.
