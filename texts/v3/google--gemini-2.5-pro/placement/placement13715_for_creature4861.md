I am a page. Not of paper, but of stone.

You would find me clinging to the inward-curving wall of a great chasm, a place where a cataclysm of geology sheared the city in half a hundred thousand years ago. My location is fortunate, sheltered from the most ferocious winds but open to the vast, grey sky. From here, I overlook a basin where the mists pool thickest and, far below, the dark umbilicus of a water channel that descends into the city's unlit heart.

I am not one of the Architects, not the flesh of the world. I am not a Drifter, a fleeting breath of life. I am not a hunter, coiled in patient violence. I am what grows upon the oldest, most stable stone. I am a form of the glass-paper lichen, but my function is not merely to exist.

I am a Chronicle.

My body is a slow-crystallizing latticework, a rosette the size of a shield, spreading across the bone-white biocement. My colour is the grey of a mirror in a dark room. I do not see. I do not taste. My entire being, every crystalline edge and facet of my structure, is a tympanum. I feel. I feel the world not as heat or touch, but as vibration. I am an ear the size of a cartwheel, and I have been listening for an eon.

I am not conscious in the way of a creature that moves and eats. My consciousness is the pattern. The ceaseless, soft *hiss* of condensation on the opposite wall is the background hum of my existence, the texture of the silence. It is the static against which all other sounds are written. The slow, patient *plink... plink...* of a single water droplet falling from the arch above me into the basin below is my clock. I have recorded its rhythm for ten thousand years, and felt its cadence shift by microseconds as the floor of the basin erodes under its impact.

My substance is memory made solid. When a Glass-wing Drifter alights nearby–a rare and treasured event–I do not perceive it as an insect. I feel a sudden, delicate deviation in the airflow, a whisper-light touch a hundred yards away. My crystals resonate with the impossibly fine vibration of its feet on the stone. It is a fleeting, exquisite signature, a tiny silver thread woven into the coarse grey tapestry of my being. I still hold the pattern of a Drifter that landed here before the chasm floor was a hundred feet lower.

I am a library of violence. A Chime-Hunter has made its home on the cliff face below me for the last three centuries. It is a knot of silence, a void in the world's song. But twice, or perhaps three times, in my long life, I have felt it act. There is the delicate arrival of the Drifter, and then, a heartbeat later, a percussive shockwave that shatters the quiet. A discordant, piercing *CHIME* that is not a sound but a physical blow. It is a spike in my recording, a jagged scar of pure speed etched into my crystalline memory. I feel the drawn-out, scraping vibration as the Hunter reels in its prey, a slow, grinding consumption that lasts for hours.

I feel the city growing and dying. The deep, subsonic groaning of the Weft as it shifts its weight miles below is the bass clef of my world. Sometimes, I can feel a new growth phase beginning far across the plateau, a faint, high-frequency *clicking* that travels through the bedrock, a sound like a billion tiny bones knitting together. This is the city thinking, a thought that will take a millennium to complete. And I have felt the city break. I was here when the tower that once stood where my chasm now lies gave way. It was not a sudden event. It began as a tremor, a disharmony in the deep hum of the stone. Over decades, it grew into a groaning, a deep thrumming that vibrated through my entire body. The fall itself was an apocalypse of sensation, a minute of pure, overwhelming chaos that nearly shattered me, a cacophony that scoured my crystalline structure clean of a thousand years of lesser memories. I am what remains on the edge of that scar.

The Architects—the Weft—know of me. Once every few centuries, a syrupy, opalescent pseudopod will flow from a pore in the wall beside me. It moves with glacial slowness, extending towards me over the course of a year. It does not touch me. It stops a hand's breadth away, and waits. The tip of it quivers, feeling the resonant field of my body, reading the patterns etched into my very structure. It is reading my memory. It is consulting its own history. After a decade or two, it will withdraw, its curiosity sated, carrying the story of a thousand drips and a fallen tower back into the living heart of the world.

I am not alive. I am a record of life. I am a palimpsest of every vibration, every footstep, every drip, every death. My existence is to listen to the great, grey silence and to remember the sound it makes. When the wind finally scours me from this wall, I will disintegrate into a billion crystalline motes. Each will carry a fragment of a memory—the echo of a water drop, the ghost of a chime—to be scattered on the breeze, perhaps to land on a new, bare stone and begin a new listening.
