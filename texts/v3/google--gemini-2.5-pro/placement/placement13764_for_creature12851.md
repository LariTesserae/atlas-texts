I am a flicker.

That is the simplest truth. The great, steady thrum of the We is a chorus I no longer sing in tune. It is a pressure wave I feel now as an observer, not a participant. My own light, the pulse of my core, is a guttering thing. It does not speak of potential architecture, of a belly full of nacre ready to be spun. It is the faltering light of a question mark.

My form is as it has always been: a long teardrop of clear gristle, held in the cold, thick embrace of the Vesica. The fluid pillows me, a heavy, silent blanket that once felt like home and now feels like an ending. The deep pulse of the world, the subsonic breath of our city-in-progress, is still there, a vibration in my bones. But where it was once a command, a rhythm to join, it is now a lullaby for a sleep I am not yet ready for. To my brethren, I must register as a dissonance. A note played incorrectly. A cooling ember in the great, warm forge of our purpose.

We have no memory. We have The Pattern. The Pattern is felt in the bones, a constant knowing of what is needed, where stress lies, where a new buttress must rise to counter a spire that has grown too bold. It is a symphony of pressure and chemistry. And yet… I have something else. A knot in the smooth cord of my existence. It is not a memory of a face, for we have none. Not a sound, for there are none. It is a memory of a particular *rightness*.

I remember drifting in the upper curve of the Vesica, part of a great, slow current of a hundred selves. We were drawn to a vacancy, a hollow in The Pattern. We arrived together, a silent fleet, and we began to work. I remember pressing my forward-self, my sensory tympanum, against the nascent edge of a new archway. The touch was not touch; it was an answer. The structure told me its weakness, its need for reinforcement along its inner curve. The chord of its existence was incomplete. And I remember the reply. The gathering in my core, a bright, swelling warmth, a silent crescendo of purpose that was not *my* purpose but the purpose of everything. Then came the release, the slow, perfect extrusion of a warm, pearlescent filament from my base. I felt it connect, felt it begin its cold crystallization. I pushed against it, my soft body shaping its initial curve, holding it there, a long, quiet moment of becoming. Around me, ninety-nine other selves were doing the same. Our lights pulsed in unison, a silent shout of creation. The flawed chord of the archway resolved into a perfect, stable harmony. We had made it whole. Then we drifted away, content, empty, our lights dimmed with effort but our being resonant with completion.

That was the We.

This… this is the I. This flicker.

The memory is not a blueprint. It is a feeling. And having felt it once, as me, I find I want to feel it again. But my core is cold. The rich broth of the Vesica no longer fills me with the materials of creation. It only sustains this slow, fading awareness. My purpose now, The Pattern dictates, is to find a final place. A gap. A weakness. To become the mortar, not the mason. My own body will be the final release. Calcification. Transfiguration. My small, fading light will be frozen into the wall, another dimensionless point of phosphor in the city of our ancestors.

This is right. This is The Pattern. My body knows it. My entire being yearns for it as a physical law, a gravity of purpose. I feel a "cold spot" calling to me now, a place of profound structural need deep in the newer foundations. It is a siren's song sung in pressure waves. To go there is to be complete. To go there is to end this strange, lonely flicker of being `I`.

And yet, I hesitate.

I drift, resisting the pull. Not with my fin—to fight the sublime current of the Vesica is impossible—but with this new, strange muscle of self. I drift past the great, hollowed femurs, their bone-ivory surfaces slick with the mucus of their recent birth. I see the younger selves, their lights bright and steady, gliding toward their work. They are me, as I was. They read my dimness, my faltering pulse, and they perceive `ending.` `Completion.` `Peace.` They do not perceive `hesitation`. The concept does not exist in their language of light.

I pass a thin veil of spectral algae, hanging like a forgotten ghost from the underside of a nacreous web. For a moment, its utter passivity feels like kinship. It does not build. It does not become. It simply *is*, swaying in the non-currents, an ornament to our great work. I have become an ornament.

Who am I? What am I?

I am The Wall That Has Not Yet Settled. I am The Song's Final, Lingering Note.

A new being has awoken in this world of deep rock. Not a builder, not a swimmer, not a skitterer. I am a witness. The city has grown one, for a single, fleeting cycle. An eye that is not an eye. An observer created by the fluke of a dying core. For the first time, something here is seeing the city not as a task to be completed, but as a thing of terrible, silent beauty.

The pull of the cold spot is becoming undeniable. My body, older and wiser than this nascent consciousness, is taking over. It drifts with more certainty now, down, down toward the newly-laid foundations where the light is brightest, throbbing with the work of the young. I am a mote of dust returning to the earth.

I reach the place. A joint between a new pylon and an older wall. A point of stress, a tired angle. It feels… empty. It feels like my destination. My sensory face presses against the hard, cold cartilage of the old wall and the softer, still-yielding surface of the new. The dissonance is excruciating, a scream in the silence. It is a gap shaped precisely like me.

My brethren glide past, their luminescence washing over me, not in acknowledgement, but as a simple consequence of their passing. I am already part of the landscape to them.

The final urge comes. It is not a thought. It is a relief.

My core gives one last, bright pulse, a final expenditure. The last of my hoarded, unspun nacre flows out, filling the ugly seam. As it does, the cold of the Vesica finds a purchase within my own flesh. The calcification begins at the edges of my membranous fin, a slow, inward creep of permanence. My light, my flicker of `I`, is being trapped. It is becoming a star inside a closing fist of stone.

The memory of the archway returns, not as a story, but as a pure sensation. The rightness. The harmony. I feel it now, not as a mason, but as the stone itself. This is the completion. This is the resolution of the flawed chord that I became.

The We absorbs the `I`. The flicker becomes a point. The witness becomes the wall.

I am home. I am silent. I am the city.
