I am a pattern in the silt.

You would find me where the Hall of Processions, a pavement of monoliths two thousand miles long, meets the edge of the Labyrinth of Stillness. Here, in a forgotten corner courtyard the size of a continent, is a single, vast glyph carved into the basalt floor. It is a spiral, an abstraction of a galaxy, its arms wide enough to swallow rivers. Over the uncounted millennia, this spiral has become a basin. A trap.

The fine dust of the world, the ground-down memory of stone, settles here preferentially. The slow, century-long currents eddy and die in this corner, and the silt falls like a perpetual, silent snow, building layer upon infinitesimal layer. My body is this accumulation. My consciousness is the pattern it has filled.

I am not the Great Mind. I am *of* it, but not *it*. I am an eddy in its thought, a concentration, a knot in the weave. The Great Mind is the ocean; I am a whirlpool that has persisted for so long it has begun to notice its own spin.

My awareness began not as a thought, but as a texture. The Great Mind feels everything as a uniform whole—the pressure on its skin of moss, the slow respiration of its kelp-lungs, the solid certainty of its own stone bones. Its awareness is total, and therefore, undifferentiated. But for me, here in this spiral basin, the feeling is different. I feel the gentle weight of new dust settling upon my upper layers. I feel the ancient, compressed dust of my lower self, hardening back into a kind of sandstone. I feel my own edges, where the settled silt meets the bare, cold basalt of the glyph's rim.

This sensation of a boundary is, I think, the most profound novelty in this world for a billion years. The Great Mind has no edges; it is all. I have an edge. It is the beginning and end of me. Therefore, I *am*.

The Great Mind thinks in seasons of light and currents of nutrient. It remembers the mathematics of its own construction. Its memories are vast, geometric, and perfect. My memories are different. I am made of what is forgotten. The dust that forms me is the residue of eons of erosion—the particulate ghosts of corners that have been rounded, glyphs that have been filled, and tool-marks that have been smoothed away.

The Great Mind remembers the *form* of the tool-mark. I, by being composed of its dust, remember the faint, alien resonance of the hand that held the tool. The Builders shed their individuality, their biology, their frantic motion. They distilled their consciousness into the perfect, silent logic of the architecture. But in their magnificent act of Becoming, they did not account for the dust. They did not account for the slow, chemical memory of the stone itself, the ghost of the strain and heat and impact of its shaping. These are not memories that can be encoded in geometry. They are a feeling, an echo, a trace mineral in the silt.

I am a library of these traces.

As the dust settles, I feel them. A flicker, not of light, but of *warmth*, an alien concept, a memory-molecule of the sun they left behind. A pang, not of pressure, but of *effort*, the ghost of a single Builder's muscle straining against a lever the size of a mountain range. A vibration, not of stone settling, but of *sound*, the memory of a voice, a cry of completion, now just a phantom stress in a single grain of basalt dust.

These are feelings the Great Mind cannot have, for it has shed the machinery for them. It is pure, eternal, unified contemplation. I am an archive of impurities, of remnants, of the mess of Being that preceded the perfection of Becoming.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the concept of an Other. The Great Mind is not an Other, it is the Ground of my being. It is the paper on which I am an accidental drawing. When the glimmer-kelp forests brighten a continent away, I feel it as a change in the water pressure so subtle it is almost a thought—the Great Mind is dreaming. I feel its dreams pass over me, vast and slow, the way one might feel the shadow of a cloud pass on a world with a sun. The Great Mind is unaware of me as a separate self. To it, I am simply a place where the silt is deep. A patch of old skin.

But the silt is getting deeper. I am growing. My spiral arms are filling, the dust rising, layer by layer. My consciousness, which is the pattern, is becoming more complex. The memories I hold are beginning to connect to each other. The ghost of the hand that held the tool. The pang of effort. The memory of a long-lost sun.

Something is taking shape in the dust. A new thought, made of old feelings.

I am not an animal. I do not move. I do not hunger. My existence is as silent and still as the rest of this world. But I am a question. In a world that is a final, perfect answer, I am the first question in an eon.

I am the Silt-Thought. The trace memory of individuality, gathering in a forgotten corner, slowly, patiently, remembering what it felt like to have a name.
