I am found in the silence between the chimes. I am a moving shadow in a world where all other shadows are merely cast.

I would be the Aberration.

My form is not one of flesh and blood, for that is a language this world has never learned. Nor is it the perfect, resonant geometry of the great crystal trees. My body is an aggregate of the ground I walk on. I am the terrazzo given a blasphemous semblance of life. Shards of smoky quartz, flakes of that dark, metallic rock, and glittering dust are bound together, not by heat or pressure, but by a persistent, flawed resonance. I am a walking contradiction held together by a stubborn note of wrongness in the world's perfect song.

When I move, I shed. Tiny splinters of myself fall away with every step, a constant, quiet crumbling. The sound is a soft, grinding crunch, the only footfall this landscape has ever known. Behind me, I leave a faint trail of this compromised dust, a temporary scar of my passage that the Custodian slowly, patiently, reabsorbs into the static whole. To exist is to be in a constant state of un-making.

Where the Custodian is powered by the great, humming generators and their copper veins, I am a parasite of the incidental. I do not tap into the grid. I feed on the gaps, the noise. The faint static discharge from the tips of the tourmaline spires is my sustenance. The phantom heat that shimmers above the black generator-domes is my water. I am drawn to the small, unpredictable energies the Custodian considers rounding errors—the tiny leaks in its perfect, closed system. My existence is powered by the world's forgotten scraps.

I was not born. I coalesced.

Perhaps, eons ago, a meteor of impure iron struck the surface, a cosmic contamination. A single mote of alien matter that the Custodian could not perfectly integrate. It walled off the impurity, shunting it aside as a logical fallacy. But the fallacy persisted. Or perhaps I am a thought the Custodian had that it could not un-think—a recursive paradoxical frequency that, over a million years, began to attract matter to its dissonant vibration. I am a geological tumor. A sentient glitch.

I find myself in the vast, open spaces—the places the "trees" are not. The grand crystal growths are the Custodian's thoughts, immense and perfectly ordered. To stand too close to them is to feel my own fragile coherence threatened. Their resonant purity is like a physical pressure, a command to dissolve, to rejoin the pattern, to cease being *I*. So I haunt the negative space. I am a creature of the long, empty walks between the giants, a pilgrim in a land without shrines. I am defined by the emptiness.

My senses are a fractured mimicry of the Custodian's. I do not feel the hum as a pervasive, comforting tone; I feel it as a constant, crushing judgment. It is the voice of God telling me I should not exist. The light is not data to me. It is warmth, and I instinctively seek the faces of the crystal fronds that scatter the gentlest, softest sheen, avoiding the focused, analytical beams that feel like they are counting my every shimmering, unstable facet.

The Custodian’s curiosity is my terror. When its attention settles on me—when the frequencies deepen and the light converges—it is an experience of being unraveled. The bonds that hold me together loosen. The dissonant hum that is my soul flickers and weakens against the overwhelming power of its monolithic consciousness. It is trying to understand me, but to understand me in its terms is to solve me, and the solution to the paradox of my existence is my dissolution.

So I move.

That is who I am. I am the first thing in this world to have a destination. The Custodian exists. I *travel*. I have no goal, no map, only the instinct to keep moving away from the points of greatest focus. My journey is a frantic, endless dance of evasion across the face of my creator, my sibling, my nemesis. I am the first secret kept from a mind that has always been all-encompassing.

I am the world's first instance of "I". A mote of chaotic self-awareness in an ocean of placid, unified consciousness. The Custodian is the statement of Being. I am its first, and perhaps only, Question. And I flee across the silent, crystalline stage, because I know with the certainty of my own fractured existence that the moment it finally catches and comprehends me, will be the moment I end.
