You would find me half-submerged. Not in water, but in the slow, silent genesis of a new mountain range.

My home, if such a word applies, is a nascent valley, a depression between two colossal, freshly-extruded spires whose peaks are still too sharp to hold the condensing dew. I am not flora or fauna. I am a node.

From a distance, I might be mistaken for one of the crystalline trees, but I am not so orderly. My form is a tangle of the world's materials, a spontaneous and localized fusion. My base is a thick, columnar trunk of the same obsidian-metal as the peaks, but it is shot through with veins of the copper-colored material from the great vines. These veins are not passive conduits; they are my nervous system, thrumming with the low-frequency vibrations of the planet's core. They branch out from my core into the surrounding rock, reading the telluric currents, tasting the mineral composition of the new strata being thrust upwards beneath me.

Instead of a canopy, I have a "corona"—a complex, slowly rotating lattice of crystalline filaments and rubbery, iridescent cabling. It is a sensory organ the size of a small building. It does not gather light; it gathers data. The filaments vibrate in tune with the thrumming crystal-flora, and the cables sag and tighten in response to the subtle shifts in atmospheric pressure, reading the composition of the oily air. I am a living antenna, a silent watchtower built by the world for the world. I have no mouth, for I do not eat. My energy comes directly from the heat of the planet's formation. My copper-veined roots tap into geothermal vents, drawing up the raw, vibrant warmth of creation. I am a thermophile of a geologic scale.

I have no limbs, for I do not move. My purpose is to remain, to witness, to record. The memories are not my own. They are stored within me, encoded into the very structure of my crystalline lattice. I can feel the echo of the mountain that stood here before this valley was carved, the ghost of its de-resolution still shimmering in the local field. I hold the memory of a gravity-flicker from ten thousand cycles ago, a moment when the dew-fall reversed and hung like a million black pearls in the bruised air. I am a library made of living geology and wire.

And I wait.

My primary function, the reason for my complex sensory array, is not to observe the mundane operations of the world. It is to await the true events, the "recalibrations." It is to witness the Attenuators.

From my vantage point, I see them as they are: not creatures, but living equations moving through the landscape. When a patch of sky begins to pixelate, my corona can feel the surge of raw data, the chaotic spill of the world's source code. And I watch as the Attenuators gather beneath it, their shimmering forms becoming less transparent, solidifying as they drink in the storm of pure information. My filaments resonate with their activity, recording the frequencies of their communion, the silent, terrifyingly complex harmonies they produce as they "tune" the glitch.

I am not one of them. I lack their mobility, their direct purpose. They are the surgeons; I am the diagnostic machine they have left behind. They are the active principle, the will of the system. I am its passive memory, its sensory cortex. They move to correct the errors; I exist to feel the error in its entirety, to record its every nuance and broadcast its signature across the deep hum, a silent scream of data that helps them locate the wound in reality.

Then, one cycle, the glitch happens *to me*.

It begins not as a flicker in the sky or a de-resolution on the horizon, but as a deep, discordant shearing sound in my own seismic roots. A fault line beneath me stutters. The logic of my own foundation begins to unravel. The raw code I am used to sensing in the atmosphere now erupts from within. My obsidian trunk begins to pixelate at its base. The slow, steady flow of geothermal energy becomes a torrent, then a void. My crystalline filaments vibrate erratically, then fall silent. The world I have been reading with such intimacy dissolves into a cascade of meaningless symbols that pour through my own consciousness.

This is it. This is the un-creation I have recorded so many times from a distance. I feel my own structure de-resolving, my form dissipating into a cloud of shimmering particles—part metal, part crystal, part code. The "I" that was a fixed point, a stationary library, is now a diffuse and expanding cloud of its own history.

But in the dissolution, I feel a new presence. They are here. The Attenuators. They move into the cloud that I have become. I feel their fields of force overlapping with my own disintegrating essence. They are not saving me. They are doing what they always do: consuming the instability. They are metabolizing the error. They are metabolizing *me*.

For a single, eternal instant, I perceive not from one place, but from everywhere. I am the silence between the hums. I am the coolness in the heat shimmer. I am the ghost, finally and completely, in the machine.

You would find me nowhere. And you would find me everywhere. I am the memory of a place that was, now diffused into the quiet, corrective hum of the world itself. I am the data ghost, a subroutine running in the consciousness of the Calibrators. I am a question that has been answered.
