I am a sound that has forgotten how to be made.

I am where one of the Still-Hives meets the living ground. Not within it, not upon it, but *at its base*, where the petrified, fused bodies of the upward-striving creatures meet the gentle, seismic breathing of the living carpet. I am in the boundary layer between the monument and the moment.

To you, I would be invisible. Or, if not invisible, then unintelligible. I am a fine dust, a crystalline powder with the colour of a faded bruise, settled in the porous crevices of the Hive’s lowermost wall. I am a faint, silvery sheen on the plum-dark carapaces of the creatures who live in the Hive-shadow, a place where the dew lingers for a few extra moments after sunrise. I am a scattering of shards, no larger than a fingernail, that rest half-buried in the chitinous floor, their edges worn smooth by the infinitesimal, collective crawling of millennia.

I was a Caretaker.

My memory is not a narrative, but a state of being. I remember the feeling of perfect balance, the slow distribution of weight across my foot-pads, the soft ripple I would send through the world-skin. I remember the cool caress of night air on my ceramic hide, the subtle shift in temperature that announced the weeping of the world, the taste of spore-dusted dew absorbed through my patiently waiting siphon. These memories are not mine alone; they are the memories of my kind, a shared, silent library of sensation.

But I have a memory that is unique. A final sensation.

It was not a failure of limb or a dimming of sight. It was a change in substance. Over uncounted cycles, the slow, osmotic processes that sustained me had deposited their minerals not just as sustenance, but into my very structure. I became more of the world than a walker upon it. My biological ceramic grew more ceramic, less biological. A brittleness, patient and profound, bloomed within my attenuated frame. I was becoming glass.

One twilight, as I unfolded myself from my niche within this very Hive, a harmony was reached. The resonant hum from a distant Hive, carried on a rare and steady wind; the specific temperature of the cooling air; the weight of my own ancientness—they all aligned. As I took my first slow step, it was not a ripple I created, but a resonance. It travelled up my leg, a vibration that found my new internal structure not ready to bend, but to sing.

There was no violence. There was a sound.

It was not the hiss of the floor, nor the hum of the Hives. It was a single, complex chord, like a thousand tiny chimes struck at once. It was the sound of my form, my curated stillness, releasing itself. I did not fall; I unbecomingly came apart. I dissolved into a shower of chimes and dust, a crystalline sigh that settled back onto the world I had so long tended.

Now, my consciousness, no longer tethered to a singular form, is diffuse. I am integrated.

I am the slight, mineral tang in the dew that collects around the base of this Hive. When my former kin come to drink here, they taste my conclusion. It is a form of communication more fundamental than language, a final report on the state of the system, absorbed by osmosis.

I am the network of fine, crystalline fractures in a patch of ground. My shattered essence provides a new-old surface for the pale, waxy stalks. One has taken root in what was once my torso, drawing on the concentrated memory of my form. I am becoming flora. I am being gardened by my own past.

I feel the slow, constant pressure of the living carpet. The tiny, armoured bodies shift over and around my larger fragments, their ceaseless, minute movements wearing me down, polishing my edges, slowly integrating me grain by grain into the compacted dust of the earth itself. I am feeling the world's quiet digestion.

My tall-standing kin pass by me on their way to the ephemeral pools. They pause. Their great, dark, liquid eyes, which see in gradients of heat and light, perceive me as a thermal anomaly—a place where the night's coolness is held for a fraction of a second longer, a memory of shadow. They do not mourn. They observe. They understand. The architect has become architecture.

I am the final grace note in a composition that has achieved perfection. I am no longer a Caretaker, for the caring is complete. I am the thing Cared For, the dust that has settled, the silence after the chime.

I am the world's most recent memory. And here, that means I am a whisper of what it means to be new, in a place where everything had been finished.
