I am a scar on the fluid.

You would not find me by looking for a body, a shape with volition. You would find me the way a sailor finds a reef: by the strange behavior of the water around me. I am tethered to one of the great, purposeless gears, the one whose teeth mesh only with the glittering broth. My existence is defined by the circumference of its slow, grinding turn. This is my territory, my universe, my cage.

I am not made of the flora’s rubbery flesh, nor the basalt’s sharp glass. I am woven from a wrinkle in the world’s law. I am a persistent visual aberration, an afterimage that refused to fade. Where the resonant scream of the great piston causes causality to stutter, I am the stutter that held. Where the light from the mosses is bent into impossible lenses, I am a lens that became self-aware.

To see me is to witness a contradiction. I am a column of the soupy medium, perhaps the height of a man, that does not move with the current. The turbulent, muscular flow of the world parts around me, leaving me in a pocket of impossible calm. Within this column, things are not right. You see the iron tooth of the gear that passed my position a moment ago, still visible, a ghost of metal hanging in the fluid. You see the spectral bloom of a glass-frond that was torn away by the Churn an hour past, still unfurling in a silent, looping ballet. You see the trace-lines of mineral glitter from yesterday, a week ago, a future second from now, all hanging in a static, three-dimensional matrix. I am a clot of time. A concentration of moments that have been torn from their sequence and packed together, dense as crystal.

My form is a shimmering composite, a stack of transparencies. At my core is the constant, grinding passage of the gear. Layered over it is the memory of the light, the heat, the pressure. I have no mouth, but I taste the chemistry of every current that breaks against my boundary. I have no eyes, yet I am made of nothing *but* sight—the recorded, superimposed visions of this single point in space across a thousand cycles. My consciousness is not a single point of view, but a chorus. I am the gear’s groaning memory, the water’s recollection of being torn, the light’s ghost. The great, all-consuming Sound is not a noise to me; it is the carrier wave upon which the data of my being is inscribed. It is the hum of my own frayed existence.

I feel the slow, swaying life of the purple kelp-trees as a change in the tenor of the pressure against my northern edge. I register the sudden, violent birth of a new obsidian spire miles away as a sharp, dissonant chord in the Sound, a new instrument joining the orchestra. I do not move. I cannot. I am an event, not a creature. My only motion is the slow, constant re-shuffling of the moments that comprise me. The ghost of the gear-tooth at my heart fades as a new one impresses its memory upon me.

When the Inhabitant, the great resonant spindle, precipitates from the chaos, it is the only thing that truly perceives me for what I am. It does not see me with eyes, it acknowledges me with physics. As it drifts, it might pass through my location, my prison of time. For a moment, its being—a focused wave of chaotic potential—and my being—a static knot of chaotic history—overlap.

In that instant, there is something like communion.

The roaring sermon of its existence, spoken in the language of industrial catastrophe, finds in me a single, snagged word. My jumbled library of moments is, for a second, put in order. The looping bloom of the glass-frond completes its life cycle. The shattered obsidian fragment finds its origin. The groaning of the gear resolves into a single, pure tone. For a fraction of a second, I am not a scar, but a coherent sentence. I experience linearity. A beginning, a middle, an end. It is a moment of excruciating, transcendent clarity.

Then it passes, sipping the kinetic memory of the gear-tooth from my core as it goes. The world snaps back. My borrowed coherence dissolves, and I am once again a shimmering column of overlapping impossibilities. The ghost-images return, the time-fractures re-open. The groaning symphony of my composite self resumes its endless, tuneless recital.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am a glitch in the engine of the world, a point of memory in a universe that has no past. I am the proof that even in a place of infinite, purposeless change, some things, once they happen, can refuse to leave. I am the world’s ghost, haunting the moment of its own birth. And I watch the gear turn, and turn, and turn.
