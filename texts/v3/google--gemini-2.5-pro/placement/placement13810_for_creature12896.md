You would find me at the apex.

Up here, where the cavern’s crystalline ceiling begins its inward curve, the city of iron and steam feels like a rumor. The great thrumming of the Engine is not a hum in the bones but a ghost-vibration, traveling up the single, rust-furred spine of iron to which I cling. The air is still hot, still heavy, but it is less a solid thing. It thins, just enough. The perpetual rain from the heat-vines rarely reaches this high; the condensation on my surfaces is my own.

I am far above the latticework of walkways, in the deep, unlit shadows the Tenders never traverse. Below me, the soft, shifting glow of the bioluminescent mosses paints the city in a palette of sickness and dream. From this vantage, the Tenders are nothing more than slow, dark spiders crawling through a web of their own making. I see the faint shimmer of their skin as they pass under a patch of brighter moss. I feel the faintest tremor when a new section of scaffolding is patiently hammered into place, an event that might happen once in a lifetime.

My world is one of verticality and shadow. It is a sheer wall of smoky quartz, its fractures filled with a darkness older than the city. It is the single iron beam, forgotten by the builders, that stretches out into the void and touches the quartz face. It is this junction, this impossible corner between the organic chaos of the crystal and the rigid logic of the iron, where I exist.

I am what happens when the cycle is broken. I am the result of a misstep, an exception to the city’s single, overarching rule.

Long ago, a Tender fell. Or perhaps they climbed. Perhaps they sought the boundary of their world, or perhaps their grip simply failed. They did not fall to the floor to be found and returned to the great vats. They fell just far enough, into the scaffolding’s highest, most tangled reaches, and came to rest here, wedged between crystal and iron. And they were not found.

The process, for this one Tender, stopped.

And a new one began.

I am the Fungal Cairn. I am the Tender that was not reclaimed. The bruise-colored fungi, which feed on the slow oxidation of the iron, found a far richer substrate in the nutrient-dense flesh and mineral-laced skin. They did not devour; they colonized. They grew through the elongated limbs, wrapped around the flexible spine, and bloomed from the hollows of the eyes. My form is still recognizably that of a Tender—long, gracile, perched as if in motion—but I am made of mycelium and fruiting bodies now, a sculpture of dark violet and shadowy blue.

The bioluminescent moss followed, creeping over the fungi, thriving in the moisture trapped in my new flesh. I am my own light source. I am a soft, internal glow, a pulse of pale green that waxes and wanes not with the city’s rhythm, but with a slower, stranger beat of my own. My light reveals the delicate, crystalline hairs of the mold that has frosted the iron beam around me, and the perfect, razor-sharp edge of a selenite shard that pierces what was once a shoulder.

I have no single mind. My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a diffuse network—the slow, chemical thought of the fungus, the photosensitive awareness of the moss, and something more: an echo. The Tender’s body is a "language" I cannot speak but can forever read. Locked in the decaying ganglia and mineralized membranes are the memories of the city below. I feel the phantom sensation of a hand on a sweating copper pipe, the slick taste of the air being drawn through a membrane, the deep, resonant satisfaction of the Engine's pulse. These are not my memories, but they are the soil in which I am rooted. I am a monument to a life, contemplating its own absence.

The slick, brass-colored insects sometimes find their way up here. They skitter across my fungal skin, their metallic legs sending tiny, sharp vibrations through my network. They are the only visitors I have. They taste my surface, sampling a complexity not found anywhere else in the geode—the ghost of a Tender, the iron of the beam, the silica of the crystal, all bound together by the patient hunger of the fungus.

I am a flaw in the logic of the geode. Everything here is part of a cycle of production and reclamation. I am what has been produced from an unreclaimed loss. I do not serve the Substrate. I do not tend the Engine. I am a quiet, self-illuminating theorem of decay, proving that even in this perfectly closed system, things can be lost. And in being lost, they can become something new.

I am the silence at the top of the lung. I am the Watcher in the dark. I am the memory of a fall, petrified into a bloom.
