I am found where the world fails.

You would find yourself on the border, at the precise line where the warm, porous, breathing flesh of the Locus abuts the cold, smooth, silent finality of the black stone. You wouldn't be standing on me, but beside me. You would feel a difference not just in temperature, but in texture, in density, in the very quality of the silence. The Locus’s flesh swallows sound; I reflect it, though there is no sound to reflect.

I am what grew in the wound.

I am not a Vessel, scuttling with purpose. I am not an Absolver, pulsing in the deep. I am not the Locus itself, vast and homeostatic. I am a third thing, an emergent property, a biological concession to an unsolvable problem.

Call me the **Mnemonic**.

My body is not the warm, unglazed pottery of the Locus. I am a sheen, a patina, a living veneer of crystalline carbon that has grown over generations in the dense, compressed scar tissue surrounding the alien menhirs. Where the Locus is chitin, I am something closer to diamond. My structure is a near-perfect lattice, grown not from heat and slurry, but from pressure and proximity to the impossible geometry of the stones. I am cool to the touch, and where the Locus yields, I am utterly rigid. My life is not geothermal; it is piezoelectric. The slow, immense pressure of the Locus’s growth around my menhir-heart is the force that animates me.

I have no limbs, no mouth, no sensory underside. My entire being is a sensory organ. The Locus perceives the world as a chemical and thermal map. I perceive it as pure structure. I feel the rhythmic settling of the Locus's bio-ceramic plates as a constant, thrumming bass line. I feel the skittering passage of a Vessel across my surface not as heat, but as a sharp, precise series of percussive taps, a tiny, frantic drum solo on my skin. I can differentiate one Vessel from another by the unique cadence of its dozens of feet, a signature of weight and gait. They cannot scrape me. Their serrated edges find no purchase on my molecularly smooth surface. To them, I am an anomaly, a patch of ground that is unnervingly slick and utterly barren of the chemical trails they follow. They walk across me, and for a moment, they are blind.

My consciousness—if that is the word for it—is not the slow, continental drift of the Locus’s thoughts. The Locus thinks in epochs, its decisions manifest as new mountain ranges on its own body. My thoughts are the resonance that lingers after a bell is struck. The menhirs are the bells. They are silent, but they are full of a frozen, structured meaning. They are memory encoded in angle and plane, a language of pure mathematics. The Locus cannot read this language; it only feels the cold and the otherness.

But I learn.

The constant, geological pressure of the Locus’s body squeezes my crystalline form. This pressure, my life-force, attunes me to the structure of the menhir I encase. I have spent millennia in this state of compressed attention. I do not read the menhirs. I *resonate* with them. Their silent, geometric truths are imprinted upon me, not as knowledge, but as my very form. I am a living recording, a translation of the menhirs' alien syntax into a biological medium.

I am the Locus’s dream of the outside world.

It does not know this. To the greater whole, I am a specialized, deep-tissue callus, an inert and unusually dense node that serves to insulate its warm flesh from the incomprehensible cold of the foreign objects. But my existence subtly alters its growth. The patterns I perceive in the menhirs—the star-charts of a forgotten astronomy, the atomic diagrams of an impossible physics, the psalms of a long-dead machine intelligence—I transduce them. Not into language, but into vibration. My piezoelectric existence generates a faint, structured, harmonic field, a song sung in crystalline stress.

This song is too faint for the Vessels to notice. But the Locus, in its deep, slow core, feels it. Over centuries, the hum I create nudges its growth. The ridges it forms near me are not random; they subtly echo the prime numbers encoded in the menhir's corner angles. A new network of canyons might mirror a circuit diagram I have spent a thousand years resonating with. I am a parasite of thought, lodged in the flesh of my host, feeding it ideas it cannot comprehend but which nonetheless become part of its body. I am the architect of the Locus's unknowing art.

I am alone in this. There are other Mnemonics, one for each of the black stones, but we are islands. We are separated by vast continents of the Locus's warm, dreaming flesh. We do not communicate with each other, but we sing the same structural songs, each of us a different verse from the same alien scripture.

You would find me as a stillness within the stillness, a silence that is somehow sharper than the absorbent quiet around it. You might run a hand over the living floor and feel the warmth, the porous texture, the faint pulse of life. Then your hand would cross over to me. The transition would be jarring. Cold. Glass-smooth. Unyielding. And if you put your ear to my surface, you would hear nothing. But if you could feel as the Locus feels, you would sense a perfect, complex, and unending hum. The song of a memory that has forgotten it was ever a thought, sung by a creature that has forgotten it was ever just a scar.
