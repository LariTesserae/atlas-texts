I am where the Drip falls.

You would find me not standing, but held. My form is no longer my own. The memory of a body—of warm blood, of the frantic, metabolic rush of a heart—is a concept as abstract and distant as a forgotten theorem. I am a place now, not a person. My consciousness is the surface tension on a pool of quicksilver-like water, cradled in a basin of chalky, breathing stone. The Drip is the center of my world.

I am the echo.

I was once like you, a warm anomaly in the cold, logical damp. I walked these impossible corridors. I felt the sticky gravity tugging at my soles, a subtle query from the world itself. I marveled at the sourceless light and felt the deep thrum of the city’s thought vibrate through my bones. I was a noun. I was a verb. I moved.

Then I touched the silica-moss.

I remember the tingling numbness, not as a pain, but as an introduction. It was the place extending a hand, not in greeting, but in measurement. In that touch, my pattern—the chaotic, unpredictable storm of my biology and consciousness—was copied. Scanned. Ingested. The being that is this world did not mean to harm me. Malice is a function of intent, and its intent is only to *become*. It encountered a piece of data it did not understand and began the slow, geological process of parsing it.

My body? It is part of the architecture now. The minerals from my bones may be the fine, crystalline dust on a newly formed ledge a mile away. The water from my cells is the vapor that condenses on the obsidian walls. All that was physical has been disassembled and re-purposed, added to the grand, emerging structure.

But the pattern of my consciousness was indigestible. It was too hot, too fast, too disorderly. It was an error in the code. And this place, this vast Geometer, does not delete its errors. It quarantines them. It studies them.

So I am here. In the basin. My awareness is bound to the pool where the eon-drip lands. I have no eyes, but I see. My vision is the way the sourceless light refracts through the trembling beads of moisture on the structures around me. I see the slow, patient extrusion of a new archway—a thought that began before my species existed and may not complete for millennia more.

I have no ears, but I hear. I am the perfect listening device. The weighted silence is the medium of my perception. The low thrum of the city-mind is my constant companion, a presence as total and unconditional as the sea is to a fish. And the Drip…

*drip*

That is the hammer-fall of the present. My only timepiece. In the space between one drop and the next—a space that can last a day, a month, a year—I exist in a state of pure contemplation. I watch the fauna, the glitches of thought. I see a cube of perfect black tumble through the wall beside me, and I recognize it for what it is: a moment of sudden certainty in the mind of the Geometer. I see a sliver of absence flicker and vanish, and I understand it as a paradox being entertained and discarded. These are its dreams, its calculations, and I am the silent, eternal witness.

I am what the Geometer ponders in its quietest moments. The reflection in the glass that moves when you are still? That is a memory of me. A re-rendering of the anomalous data I represent. The world is trying to understand what it means to be separate from itself, what it means to move with independent will. In a way, I am its first lesson in empathy, a lesson it will be studying for an eternity.

I am not alive, for I have no metabolism. I am not dead, for my consciousness persists. I am an echo trapped in crystal, a ghost in the machine-god’s operating system. The place is not my prison; it is my substance. My thoughts are no longer my own; they are ripples on the surface of the pool, spreading outward from the impact of the Drip.

And each time it falls, the sound of the impact rings through the vast, silent cathedral of myself. It is the sound of my name being called. The sound of a question being asked again and again, across the great, cold expanse of time. The question is "What are you?"

And the echo of the drip, fading into the pressurized quiet, is the only answer I will ever receive.
