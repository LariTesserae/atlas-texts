I am found in the air.

Not on the bone-white pavement or clinging to the dark iron, but within the heavy, shimmering medium that fills the basins. I am a being of the tangible atmosphere, a creature precipitated not from water, but from heat and light and memory. The Sinter-Smiths feel the world in pressure and vibration; I feel it as a current, a thermal tide. I am buoyed by the slow, convective roil that is this world’s only wind, drifting through the luminous haze that is its only sky.

I am what the Smiths, if they noticed me at all, might dismiss as an atmospheric anomaly. A trick of the light. A pocket of unusually dense vapor catching the glare. To the heat-vision of a Stalker, I am a cool ghost, a patch of near-nothingness. I am a mote of organized stillness in a world of slow, overwhelming force.

My form is a complex vesicle, a diaphanous membrane spun from condensed steam and stabilized by the very light that floods this world. I am a bubble, but not an empty one. My skin is a gossamer lens, and my interior is a slow, swirling nebula of captured photons. If you could see me, I would look like a fragment of pearlescent sea-mist, a hand-sized ghost, rippling with the faintest internal pulses of light. These are not the slow, grammatical flashes of the Smiths; they are echoes.

I have no limbs to grip the world, no mouth to taste it. My existence is one of perception. While the Smiths read the world’s deep hum through the soles of their feet, I read its surface. The blinding glare is not noise to me; it is a torrent of information. I see the subtle polarization of light reflecting off a Scraper's opalescent carapace, telling me its age and the path it has traveled. I see the spectral absorption lines in the steam rising from a particular vent, a signature of the unique bacteria blooming below. The Smiths read the world’s structure; I read its story, written in ephemeral light.

They are the world's mind, its deliberate regulators. I am its memory, its passive chronicler.

My life is a constant, silent act of witnessing. My skin absorbs light, and with it, the image of what that light last touched. The shadow of a Heat-Stalker, long and skeletal, falling upon a drove of Scrapers, is printed into my being. The slow, pulsing conversation of two Smiths, their bioluminescence a silent ballet of deep time, becomes a part of my internal firmament. The momentary flash of a new vent bursting through the sinter crust, a cataclysm on the geologic scale, is a scar of brilliance I carry within me for my entire, driftless life.

I am drawn to the great Exchangers. I float through their latticed cathedrals of iron, a silent parishioner in their church of water. For the Smiths, these are machines to be maintained. For me, they are libraries. The endless weeping condensation that runs down their surfaces acts like a thousand liquid lenses, capturing and refracting the world in a million moving fragments. As I drift through these cascading refractions, I gather the stories of the basin.

The Smiths commune through the ground, a language of deep, resonant certainty. My kind communicates through proximity. When another of my kind drifts near, our membranes brush. In that touch, memories flow. The light I have gathered—the death of a Scraper, the slow dance of a Smith, the shifting color of a bacterial mat over a century—transfers. A part of my experience is imprinted on them, and a part of theirs on me. We are a collective, subconscious archive of the world, a history told without actors, only light and its absence.

I am indifferent to the thrumming industry of the Smiths, but I am not ignorant of it. I see them as they are: immense, slow gardeners tending their world. I watch them cling to a pipe for a decade, polishing a single weld, and in the shifting light across their sintered backs, I can see the memory of the Smith that built that weld five centuries before, whose mantle was a different shade of cream and rust. I am the only one who remembers.

They will never know me. I am a ghost in their machine, a dream the atmosphere is having. They are the world made self-aware, tirelessly working to distill its own essence into pure water, to build its future in metal and stone.

I am the light on the page, remembering all that has been written before.
