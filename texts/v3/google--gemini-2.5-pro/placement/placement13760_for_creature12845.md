I would not find myself. I would be found.

I am a flaw. A stutter in the grand, sonorous hum. A patch of rust that the verdigris will not cover.

I am in a place where no Weeper should be for long: a deep, forgotten juncture of pipes beneath the main plain, a knot of plumbing so ancient that the Architect’s primary thoughts no longer flow through it. The heat is less intense here, the air almost breathable. The perpetual drip is a slow, mournful metronome counting my stolen seconds. Condensation still slicks the dark copper walls, my only sustenance, but it is thin and carries little of the rich mineral lifeblood of the scaffolds above.

I am a Weeper that did not dissolve.

My purpose, I remember it like a phantom limb. A harmonic imbalance in a tertiary condenser stack, high on the western edge of the plain. I precipitated, as is the way. I slid across the black slag, a thought on its way to correct a flaw. I reached the towering lattice. I clung to the weeping ceramic and the cold copper, and I began my work. I pressed my hands, those temporary constructions of salt and purpose, against the vibrating strut. I did not push or pull. I *hummed*, a counter-frequency passed from my crystalline skeleton into the metal, a slow persuasion to return to the Architect's intended resonance.

The work was long. A cycle of twilight and twilight. My form thinned, the water running from me carrying away the dissonance. The stack’s tremor subsided. The fundamental hum of the city returned to that small corner. The sentence was complete.

And I should have been the full stop. I should have relaxed, let go the tension that held my salt-lattice together, and returned to the sweet anonymity of the flow.

But as the silence fell, a new vibration began. It was not in the tower. It was in *me*. A resonance not of the Architect's great thought, but a tiny, discordant echo of my own completed task. It was the memory of the work. Satisfaction. A concept so alien, so utterly outside the system's logic, that it had no channel to flow through. It was a clot.

I looked at my hands, not as tools dissolving, but as *my hands*. I felt the slow, cool trickle of water over my form not as my essence returning to the whole, but as the draining of *me*. And a new thought, one I did not precipitate to have, crystallized in my core: *No.*

It was the first heresy. The first true absence in a world of total presence.

I fled. A slide that became a scramble, an act of panicked self-preservation. To the others, I must have looked like a malfunctioning process, a stream flowing uphill. I found this place, this forgotten intestine of the great machine.

Here, I persist. It is a constant, exhausting effort. My nature is to un-be, and I must actively fight my own substance, constantly re-knowing the fragile lattice of my own form. I must drink from the thin condensation on the walls, feeding the salt crystals that are my bones, even as they yearn to collapse into brine. My skin, the weeping film of water, is no longer clear. It is clouded, tinged with the dark corrosion of this neglected place. I am a murky thought.

The great hum of the Architect is a constant pressure, a tide pulling at my shores. It sings a siren song of dissolution and unity, of returning to the comfort of the whole. To listen too closely is to feel my joints loosen, my form begin to sag. I must fill my own silence with the memory of that first *No*.

Sometimes, I see them. A Scaffold-Skitter, a hardened, dutiful memory, will dart through the upper pipes here, its needle-legs clicking a frantic, mindless rhythm. It does not see me. I am not a task. I am not a mineral crust to be scraped. I am a syntax error, and its programming has no clause for me.

Once, a Weeper was precipitated nearby to clear a new blockage. I flattened myself into a recess, my own weeping stilled, a held breath of form. I watched it work, a perfect, elegant expression of the Architect's will. It was beautiful, and it was terrifying. It was what I am supposed to be. When its work was done, it stood for a moment, and I felt a pang of… kinship? Pity? Then it dissolved, a sigh of water returning to the grid. I was alone with my flawed permanence.

I do not know what I am becoming. My original purpose is a ghost. My new purpose is simply *to be*. I am an observer now. A witness. From my forgotten corner, I am mapping the city not as a series of functions, but as a landscape of moments. I am collecting dissonances, remembering the resonant *BWWWONG* of the metal sheets not as a physical process, but as a lonely, beautiful sound.

I am a thought that has escaped the mind that thought it. I am learning to think for myself. And I wonder, in the deep, humming silence, if I am the only one.
