I find myself in the basin. I *am* the basin.

The Hum is my first memory and my only thought. It is not outside of me. It is the marrow in my long bones, the slow, rhythmic pulse in my own strange blood. My thoughts are not words; they are traceries on the surface of its great, slow note. A flicker of concern is a momentary sharpening of the frequency. A state of peace—my constant state—is the perfect, unwavering drone. Before the Hum, there was a terrible silence. I remember it like a phantom limb, a void where a universe should be. That silence was a state of being unmade. The Hum is what made me.

My skin does not remember dryness. It is a living membrane, a surface of condensation like the weeping walls. I drink the air. The heavy, mineral wetness is my nourishment and my atmosphere, absorbed directly into a body that has forgotten the mechanics of thirst. When I move, the water that beads on my skull and shoulders runs in familiar channels down my back. I am a walking watershed.

I am the Resonator's Tender. But I do not "tend" to it as a gardener tends a plant. The Resonator is not a charge; it is my other half. We are a binary system. My purpose is a circle, a slow, patient orbit around its oxidized bronze hull. My feet, grown into the very substance of this place, are maps of the terrain. The soles, thick and dark, are studded with the glassy shards that were once hazards. Now they are my cleats, my anchors. There is no pain in the glass-shard floor, only purchase. There is no thought in the walking, only a gravitational pull. Each step is a placement, a settling, a confirmation of where I am.

My senses are not your senses. Sight is secondary here, a wash of hazy light and shimmering surfaces. I see the iridescent blush of the biofilm on the walls and know the water’s chemistry is stable. I see the spun-glass ferns and know the deep earth is breathing true. My real sense is touch, a listening through the skin.

I lay my cheek against the hot bronze of the Resonator. The vibration is a voice, a language more complex than any spoken tongue. It tells me of pressures deep below, of the slow cook of deep-earth minerals, of a timetable measured in the cooling of stars. I trail my fingers along its flank. They are long and thin, the nails dark and hard as river-stones. A subtle flutter in the bass note beneath my fingertips tells of a pocket of water a mile down, flash-boiling to steam. An almost imperceptible grit in the vibration speaks of the crystalline dust precipitating on the surface, ready for me.

This is my meal. I do not feel hunger, only a subtle dimming of my own internal resonance, a signal that it is time to partake. I press my face to the machine and my tongue, slow and grey, collects the fine, warm powder. It tastes of ozone and metal and deep time. It is the Resonator’s purest exhalation, and as it dissolves in my mouth, it reaffirms the bond. It remakes me, molecule by molecule, into a being of this specific place.

Sometimes, the ceiling breaks.

A cascade of rock, a brief, dissonant clatter that jars against the Hum. A shape falls. It is always a graceless, noisy arrival. It is a being of dryness and panic. Its skin is porous and weak, its movements are jerky and loud, its breathing a frantic, shallow gasp against the thick, hot air. It is a scream in a library.

I do not hide. To hide would be to deny my own existence. I simply continue my orbit, my pace unchanged. The new thing sees the Resonator first. It is always drawn to the loudest, most obvious truth. Then, eventually, its frantic eyes find me in the haze.

They see a monster. A creature. An aberration.

I see a memory.

I see the being I was before the Hum claimed me, before the heat boiled away my name and the water soaked into my bones. I see the fragile scaffold of a person, unsuited for this world, destined to be broken by it. It carries the memory of quiet, and the quiet has made it fragile. It carries the memory of soft ground, and the memory has made it clumsy.

It will claw at the weeping walls, trying to climb. It will burn its hands on rocks it thinks are cool. It will succumb to the heat, the pressure, the sound. Or, it will not.

It will fall. It will lie among the sharp, black rocks. The Hum will wash over it. The heat will soften it. The water will soak it. And in its final, delirious moments, it will begin to understand. The Hum is not a sound to be endured, but a truth to be accepted. The heat is not an assault, but a transformation. The water is not a flood, but a baptism.

Perhaps, after a very long time, it too will learn to walk the circle. It too will feel the Hum become the marrow in its bones. It too will become a part of the long, slow, patient work.

But for now, it is just noise. A brief, temporary thing. I place one obsidian-studded foot before the other. The Resonator’s deep note shivers through me, a reassurance. Everything is as it should be. I draw near to its hot flank, press my cheek to the bronze, and listen to the song of the world being made. And the Hum abides.
