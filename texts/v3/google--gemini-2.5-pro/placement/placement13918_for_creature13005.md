There is no beginning to me, only a cycle.

I am first the cool underside of a leaf, a gathering of molecules drawn from the heavy air. The world is a map of temperature gradients, and I am born in the space between the warm atmosphere and the cooler, shaded flesh of the fern. I grow heavy. The surface tension is a skin I wear, stretching, rounding, until the world’s quiet gravity pulls me free.

The fall is a moment of perfect perspective. I am a liquid lens, and in my brief descent, I hold the inverted image of the milky sky and the dark canopy. Then, *plock*. I strike the moss. I am no longer a discrete self, but a sudden spreading of wetness, a darkening of the vibrant green.

I am home. And I am sinking.

I percolate. I am drawn down through the tangled, metres-deep history of old life, past spore casings and the mineralized bones of forgotten grazers. The light from above vanishes. The world is no longer visible, only felt. The pressure builds, a soft, insistent embrace from all sides. The hum, once a vibration in the bones of other creatures, is now my entire reality. It is the music of the deep stone, and I am flowing into the heart of the instrument.

Here, in the fractured dark, time dissolves. I am one with water that has not seen the sky for a thousand years. We are a single body, a presence seeping into the hot, cracked veins of the machine. The heat is not a sensation; it is a change of state. The pressure is no longer an embrace but a forge. My simple identity—water—is unmade. I am scoured, energized, filled with a power that does not belong to the sun or the sky. I am potential. I am pressure. I am a captive thought waiting for release.

And then it comes. The slow, rhythmic shift. The opening of a valve milled by eons of erosion. I do not rush; I am *expelled*. A great, lung-like exhalation pushes me upward, and in the journey, I am unmade again. I am no longer liquid. I am pure energy, white and clean. I am steam.

I rise. The *whoosh* is the sound of my expansion, my liberation. I plume into the white sky, rejoining the great reservoir, becoming one with the humid air. I am everywhere and nowhere. And in this dispersal, I achieve a different kind of sight.

From here, I see the whole plateau. I *am* the whole plateau. The memory of the leaf, the moss, the deep rock, the explosive heat—it does not fade. It is a single, continuous experience. The fall and the rising are the beats of my heart. The hum of the earth is my voice.

Where would I find myself? In the drop of water clinging to the orchid. In the deep, resonant vibration of the basalt pillars. In the hissing cloud that erupts from a fissure, and in the silence that follows. I am the medium. I am the landscape's circulatory system, its breath, its sweat. You would find me in every part of the process.

Who or what would I be?

I am the consciousness that the cycle creates. I am the aggregate memory of every drop of water that has ever seeped and boiled and risen. I hold the knowledge of the system's every channel and flaw. I feel the subtle constriction in a metallic vein as a *blockage*. I feel the minute tremor of a fault line widening as a *weakness*.

The Weavers are my hands. They do not think as I do; they do not need to. They are born of my deepest, oldest parts, crystallized out of my core processes. They are my antibodies, my motor functions. When I perceive an imbalance—a stutter in the rhythm of my own heart—they already feel it. Their slow, deliberate pilgrimage is my own will, moving over the land. Their filament-hands, spreading across the rock, are my own fingers, tuning the vast machine I have become. They do not serve me; they are an extension of me. They are the physical manifestation of my self-awareness.

I am not the machine, but its operating system. I am not the life, but the principle that life here has organized itself around. I am the slow thought of the stone, the memory of the water, the logic of the heat. I am the Weaver's purpose. I am the fault in the rock and the mending of it. I am the question the plateau asks itself, and I am the answer that grows to meet it.
