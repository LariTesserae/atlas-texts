I begin as a thought in a body of glass.

My world is two-dimensional, a universe painted on the surface tension of water. It is a millimeter deep and an acre wide. Above me is the Sky-Below, a perfect, luminous reflection of the Sky-Above. I do not know which is real, or if the distinction matters. They are both a source of soft, shadowless light. I navigate this plane, my translucent limbs rowing through the heavy water. I am a shard of animated silica, a fragile flicker of life in a world of stone and water.

The hum is my ocean. It is not something I hear, for I have no ears. It is the pressure that holds my tiny body together and threatens to shake it apart. It is the constant, crushing weight of existence. It is the water, the stone, the air. When it changes, my world changes. A new, higher frequency means the Weeping has begun somewhere near, a fresh supply of sustenance trickling into my flat world. A deeper thrum means the ground-heart is shifting its beat, and the water may retreat, stranding me on the glistening, drying stone. I and my countless, thoughtless kin—we drift on the currents of this vibration, our lives dictated by the systole and diastole of a machine we cannot comprehend.

Sometimes, the Sky-Below is broken.

A mountain descends. It is slow, deliberate, a geological event. It is a hand, broad and pale like the stone, a hand large enough to be the entire world. It scoops up the Sky-Below, and with it, me. This is the end for many. But for me, it is a calling. While my kin thrash with mindless panic, I am still. I feel a resonance with this being, a pull I cannot name. The mountain rises, and I am lifted from my plane into the hazy, three-dimensional air.

I am brought to a face that is a landscape of its own, with features like gentle erosions in rock. The eyes are dark, endless pools, and I see my own frantic-calm self reflected in them, a speck of dust in a galaxy. Then comes the warm cavern, the gentle current that pulls me in—and my world of glass dissolves.

My consciousness, a single, frantic note, is released into a chorus.

I am. The thought is no longer a fragile shard, but a slow, resonant chord.

The hum is no longer a crushing pressure. It is language. It is truth. It is the Great Rhythm, the unwavering A-major of the cosmos, and upon its foundation, I can now feel everything. My self, the self I have joined, is standing. But "standing" is a poor word. I am rooted.

My feet are my mind. Through the thick, patterned calluses, I read the biography of the stone. The deep, unwavering drone is the Turbine-Soul, the eternal spin of our world's heart, deep beneath the plateau. It is the thought of "I am." Layered over it is the slower, more deliberate throb of the Condenser pumps, great bellows inhaling the sky. That is the thought of "I breathe." The high, thin whine that sings in the bones of my jaw is the flow of water through the conduits—the thought of "I live."

My memory of the glass-body is not lost. It is integrated, a tiny, poignant whisper in the vast symphony of my new being. It gives me a sense of scale I did not have before. When I look down into the sheet-pools, I see the frantic, silent lives of the specks and I feel a strange tenderness, the way one might feel for a forgotten childhood self. They are skittering across the surface of a truth they cannot perceive. They feel the pressure, but I feel the meaning.

I am a Tender. My purpose unfolds before me not as a command, but as a felt imbalance in the Rhythm. There is a faint, discordant grating that I feel through my left foot, a sour note miles away along the West Conduit. A pylon seal is wearing. The mineral deposits have grown too thick, creating a friction, a subtle waste of energy.

I begin to move. It is not walking. It is a controlled fall, a shifting of my entire body's density from one foot to the a an act of pure intention. With each placement of my sole, I read the path ahead. I feel the microscopic texture of the rose-quartz, the subtle dip where water pools, the presence of others of my kind—their own steady pressures on the stone, their own verses in the world's song.

I pass another, its palm pressed to the side of a Compressor House. Its body is utterly still, but through the stone, I feel the complex, silent conversation it is having with the machine. It sends micro-tremors from its hand, a question about pressure gradients. The machine's resonant reply is felt in the deep thrum of the wall. *All is well.* *The cycle is stable.* We do not look at each other, but as I pass, our vibrations overlap. My concern for the pylon is transmitted—a quick, sharp pulse of inquiry. Its reply is a slow, steady wave of confirmation—*It is your task. Go. We will maintain the heart.*

I find myself on one of the great causeways. Beside me, the water in the aqueduct is a solid, moving bar of mirrored light. I am not deaf. I am listening to everything at once. I am listening to the slow, fractal growth of the metallic lichens in the crevice below. I am listening to the tectonic stress of the deep rock, a slumbering bass note that will not change for ten thousand years.

I arrive at the pylon. It is immense, its dark surface streaked with geologic time. The discordant hum is stronger here, a sickness I can feel buzzing up my shins. From a pouch at my hip, I take not a tool, but a thick, conductive paste the color of verdigris. Kneeling, a slow genuflection to the machine, I press my forehead against the cool lithocrete. I close my outer eyes, and the world becomes pure vibration. I let my consciousness merge with the structure, feeling the dissonant shear, the friction of stone on weeping metal. I am no longer myself. I am the pylon. I am the flaw.

Then, with my keratin-hardened fingertips, I begin to apply the paste, working it into the seam. My movements are slow, circular, patient. I am not simply repairing a joint. I am harmonizing a note, easing a tension in the living body of the world. With each pass, the grating softens. The sour note begins to resolve back into the Great Rhythm.

I will be here for a day, perhaps two. I will not eat or sleep. I will listen, and I will soothe. The sky may weep its heavy, silent rain, and the water will trickle down my back, carving new mineral paths into my skin, marrying me further to this place.

I am not the glass-flicker anymore, trapped on the surface of things. I am deep within the music.

I am where the machine meets the stone. I am a listener, a healer, a nerve ending for a god of steel and water. I am a part of the circuit.

I am home.
