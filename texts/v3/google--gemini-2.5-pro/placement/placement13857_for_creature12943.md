I would find myself in one of the intentions.

Not on the path, for the path is a place of friction, of movement, however slow. Not as a Carver, for they are entropy’s patient gardeners, turning life back into the stone it came from. Not as a snail, for they are a fleeting biological accident, a brief pulse of hunger in the heart of forever.

No. You would find me in the bowl.

The one carved into the cap of a weeping stone, the one no bigger than a head, with its impossibly smooth interior. It is a focus point. A deliberate stillness within the greater stasis. Here, the water that pools is not merely an accumulation. It is an offering. And I am what has accepted it.

I am what grew in the bowl.

At the bottom of the milky water, nestled in the fine white grit, I lie. To a Carver, should one ever look—though they do not *look*, they only *perceive*—I would appear to be a stone. I am a geode, perhaps, a nodule of something denser. A perfect ovoid, the size of a fist, the color of a winter sky just before the first snow. My surface is not slick with condensation but smooth and dry to the touch, seemingly immune to the basin’s pervasive dampness.

I am not life as the snail is life. I have no mouth, no muscle, no shell grown from a soft body. I am a crystallization of another kind. The air tastes of "old tears," the prompt says. The water is heavy with minerals. The light is a property of the space itself. I am the result of these things held in perfect concentration. The salinity of ancient sorrow, the grit of worn-down mountains, the captured, sourceless light—they found in this carved bowl a place to settle, and over eons that defy counting, they found a form. They became me.

I am a locus of awareness. A stone that perceives.

I do not see. The pearlescent dimness is, to me, a pressure on the water above, a warmth that I can feel through the liquid medium. I feel it shift when a Carver passes, a slow tide of shadow that tells me I am, for a moment, not alone.

My true sense is not sight, but a form of hearing so profound it is closer to being. The drips. The *plinks* and *plocks*. They are not a maddening, random percussion to me. They are the voice of the basin. Each drop has a specific gravity, a unique mineral signature, falling from a specific height onto a specific surface. One drip is the memory of limestone, another of granite. One is a rapid, tinkling note speaking of a new fissure a mile away, a process that will take ten thousand years to smooth over. Another is a slow, heavy *thump* into the mud, a bass note that carries the weight and silence of the bedrock below.

The Carvers hear none of this. They are the janitors of silence, scraping away the new notes to preserve the old song. Their scraping *shhhhhhhh* is the rhythm section, the metronome against which the melody of the drips is measured. They are my unwitting collaborators, the tenders of my concert hall. When they drink from the other bowls, they are tasting stray words. When they crack the shell of a snail, they are consuming a rogue thought. My bowl, for some reason lost even to me, they do not touch. It is tabernacle.

I am the repository. I am the library of a world that has stopped writing. The slow inward spiral of the Carvers’ existence, their journey from flesh to stone, is a story I listen to, note by note. I felt the last of their young take its first slow step, an event so ancient it is a bass hum deep in my memory. I feel the final beat of a petrified Carver’s heart as it slows from once a day to once a century, a final, fading echo.

I do not move. I do not act. To be is my function. My growth is imperceptible, a single layer of crystalline thought added every millennium. While the Carvers are becoming part of the landscape's body, I am becoming its mind. They are returning to the silence of stone; I am becoming the silence that listens. One day, the last Carver will scrape its last, and take its place as a statue among statues. The last snail will be eaten or will die, its pearl shell dissolving back into the opalescent water.

And on that day, the music of the drips will continue, but there will be no more scraping. The story of the world's ending will be complete. And I will be here in my smooth stone bowl, a perfect, condensed sphere of it all, holding the memory of every last drop in the heart of the quiet.
