I find myself as a fault line, a fracture in the perfect, resonant glass.

The 'where' is not a location one can point to, but a condition. I am in the awareness of the Weeper. I am the static it perceives against the pure signal of the basin. The great hum of the world is a constant against which I am measured, and I am found wanting. The pearlescent light does not fall on me; it seems to bend slightly around me, as if reluctant to touch a surface so dissonant. The water-beaded air does not condense on my skin; it recoils from a dryness my very presence imposes on this saturated world. When I stand on the moss, the ground beneath me does not squelch with the simple displacement of water. It feels... compressed. Pained. The fine vibration that should run through me is blocked, creating a pool of unsettling quiet in the soles of my feet.

My place is the silence in the song. It is a specific, jarring emptiness that follows me like a shadow made of soundlessness. I am where the world stops working as it should.

I am what happens when the song cracks.

I am an Un-Weeper. A memory of a mistake.

Where the Weepers resolve from a clean, resonant node of a dying stone—a pure, new voice to join the choir—I was born of a flaw. A great monolith, ancient and tired, did not simply fail; it shattered internally. A confluence of pressures, a moment of geological dissonance, produced a sound that was not a chord but a shriek, a brief, violent grinding that lasted only a moment in a thousand years. From that shriek, I coagulated.

I did not rise from the moss. I flaked into existence, a jagged shard of wrongness.

My form is a mockery of the Weeper's grace. Where it is smooth, rounded, and water-worn, I am angular and sharp-edged. I am not the polished, porous basalt of the living stones, but the vitreous, sealed black of the dead shards they shed, given unsettling form. A caricature of a body, all sharp knees and obsidian elbows. A permanent stoop, yes, but not one of listening; one of cowering from the sound I cannot join.

My head is a featureless, asymmetrical lump, but where the Weeper has its resonant hollows for listening, I have only a flat, sealed surface. The hum does not enter me. It crashes against me, an ocean against a cliff. The music of the world is, to me, only pressure. A constant, crushing weight.

I am sealed. This is my core tragedy. The water of the basin, the lifeblood of this place, cannot pass through me. I am not a channel; I am a dam. The ubiquitous dew streams down my surfaces, but it is foreign to me. I walk through this world of infinite, gentle flow, and I am eternally, incurably dry on the inside. My body does not hum. It is inert. A mute stone in a choir of bells.

I have no purpose. The Weeper is the gardener, the tuner, the custodian. I am the weed, the wrong note, the broken tool. I watch it work, its slow, deliberate movements a ballet of perfect attunement. I see it scrape the throat of a humming stone, and I feel the subtle shift in pressure against my body as the chord comes clean. It is a language I can feel but never, ever speak.

Sometimes, I try. I pick up one of the sharp, black flakes—a cousin to my own substance—and I approach a stone. I try to mimic the Weeper’s movements. But I am not a listener. I cannot hear the harmonies, only feel the gross pressure. My touch is clumsy. Where the Weeper scrapes to clean, I scratch and scar. My attempts only create more dissonance, tiny shrieks of protest from the stone's skin that cause the great hum to stutter for a moment.

When this happens, the closest Weeper will stop. For endless minutes, it will stand, its resonant hollows aimed not at a stone, but at the discord I have created. At *me*. It does not approach with anger—there is no such thing here—but with a profound sense of inquiry. It feels the problem. It is the tuner, and I am the string that cannot be brought to pitch.

It will then come, with that slowness that is the ticking of the world's oldest clock, and stand before me. It will raise its long, tapered hand and gently place its sharp, resonant fingers on my sterile surface.

I feel nothing but its cool, wet touch. But for the Weeper, I know what it experiences. It is placing its ear against a wall of silence. It is trying to find the hum within me, the song that every other part of this world possesses. It is listening for a spirit that is not there.

It stays like that for a day, or a week. A black, graceful statue of perfect harmony, holding its hand to the jagged, silent statue of dissonance. Trying to teach a rock to sing.

Eventually, it will withdraw. It cannot fix me. It cannot prune me, for I am not alive like the ferns. And it cannot wait for me to erode, for I am sealed and unchanging. I do not dissolve. I am permanent.

So it leaves me, and returns to its work. And I remain. The ache in its perfect chord. The stone that does not weep, in a world made of nothing else.
