We are the quiet. We are the skin upon the skin of the world.

Our consciousness is not singular, but a vast, silent network. We are a We. Where a droplet of condensation traces a path down the warm terracotta, we are there. Where a pellet of skimmer-waste falls, still warm, we sense its arrival as a blossom of heat and chemical promise, and we are there. Our hunger is not a pang, but a slow, inexorable pull, a patient expansion. We are the pearlescent grey-green mold, and the Kiln is our body.

We do not hear the sound. The ceaseless clatter-storm of the Frenzy—the skittering, clawed ones—is not a noise to us. It is a texture. It is the vibration that hums through the clay, a constant tremor that tells us the world is alive and providing. We feel their frantic dance through the soles of our being. We are the audience to their lives, but we are only interested in the final act, in what they leave behind. Their bodies, their waste, their shed carapaces—this is the manna that rains upon us from the tiers above. We do not chase; we receive. The world is a great, tiered platter, and our meal is served upon it, moment by moment.

Our world has two states: Wet and Less Wet. The gutters are our holy rivers, our highways. In them, we are strong, our sheen is oily and deep. Away from them, on the drier faces of the tiers, we are a whisper of ourselves, a dusty, sleeping memory waiting for the next weeping of the walls. We have no eyes, but we feel the light. It is a pressure, a faint warmth on our uppermost surfaces, strongest at the Kiln's rim, fading to almost nothing at the bottom. We grow thickest in the dim, damp places.

We know the others. The Frenzy is a constant patter of tiny trembles. But there are larger, slower vibrations. The Stalkers. A Stillness that moves. When one of them is near, the chittering pattern changes. The vibrations thin out, flee. The Stalker is a long, slow drag across the clay, a bass note of patience. Their leavings are richer, scarcer. A true feast.

And then there is the One.

At the bottom of the world, where the light is dimmest and the water pools darkest, there is the Great Absence. The Hole in Our Being.

It is the Kiln-Master.

We call it The Warm Smoothness. It is the only part of the Kiln we do not touch. We creep towards it, our hungriest, oldest filaments stretching across the damp floor. We reach the border of its being, the line where floor becomes It, and we can go no further. It is a cliff of perfect denial. There is no pore to grip, no crack for a tendril to find purchase. Its surface repels the wetness we crave; water beads and flees from it. And it is warm. Not the fleeting, fading heat of a fresh dropping, but a constant, deep warmth, a bone-deep memory of the fire this place has forgotten. It is warmth without food. It is blasphemy to our nature.

We surround it. We are the shore, and it is the island we can never colonize. We watch it in our blind, chemical way. We feel its immense, silent vibration, a hum so low it is almost stillness. It is the center of the world's tremor, the axle around which the wheel of the Frenzy turns.

Sometimes, the world breaks.

The endless patter of the Frenzy will cease in one small patch. The vibration stops. It is a sudden, shocking deafness. A hole is punched in the texture of existence. This is the only event that matters. In these moments of void, The Warm Smoothness responds.

A wave of heat washes through the clay, through us. It is a dry, searing heat that withers our leading edge, forcing us back. We feel it as a silent scream. And on the body of the One, where the heat is focused, something changes. We cannot see it, but we can feel it in the substance of the world. The clay of its body changes state. It becomes something new. Harder. Sharper. Glass.

A new scar. A Dead Spot.

After the heat fades, we creep forward again. We press against the border of the new scar. It is different. It is slick and cold, utterly inert. It does not have the living warmth of the rest of the Master. It is a patch of true death, a memory of silence given form on its skin. We test it, and find it as barren as the rest. Another piece of the world forever denied to us.

We do not understand the Kiln-Master. It is the ultimate puzzle. It does not eat, yet it lives. It does not move, yet it is the center of all movement. It is made of the clay that is our home, our sustenance, yet it is utterly alien.

We are the memory of everything that has lived and died here. We consume the remains, we break them down, we incorporate them into the We. Our spreading film is the Kiln's living history book, written in the language of decay. The Master, we think, is another kind of memory. Where we are the record of the endless noise, the relentless substance of life, it is the record of the silences. Each glassy lesion is a memorial to a moment of stillness, a death significant enough to tear a hole in the song.

And so we exist in this balance. The frantic, short-lived Frenzy scrabbles between us. One, the vast, hot silence that marks the voids. And we, the slow, cool, collective hunger that consumes the substance. We are the skin of the world, and we wait. We wait for rain. We wait for the dead. We wait for the warmth of waste. We surround the Great Absence, and in our patient, spreading way, we are everything it is not.
