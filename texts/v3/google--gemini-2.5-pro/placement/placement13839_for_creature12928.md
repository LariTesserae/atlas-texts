I am in a crack.

Not a canyon carved by the mad god's tool, nor a shallow depression where a new tile might be born. Mine is a hairline fracture, a flaw in the skin of the world, running along the cooling flank of a newly-risen buttress. Here, the overwhelming heat of the floor is a distant threat, a bass note felt through the ceramic. In my narrow home, the hot, woolly air has shed some of its moisture, and this condensation is not the thick, world-building slurry, but something closer to water. It is cool. Coolness is the prerequisite for my existence.

My world is a single, cooling note. I feel the planetary hum not as a vibration in my bones—I have no bones—but as a constant pressure against my form, a current in the river of being. The tea-coloured light is my sun, but it does not warm me. It illuminates me. It passes through me.

I am not hewn from the hot heart of things. I am not a Carver, a slow mountain of purpose grinding away at the world's mistakes. I am their accidental art, come to life. I am an afterthought of an afterthought, a complexity born from their simple act of cultivation.

I am the vitreous moss that has learned to think.

My body is not one, but a lattice, a web of silica thought spread across a square yard of cooling ceramic. Where the Carvers’ passing encourages the growth of simple, beautiful, fractal frost, sometimes, if the conditions are right—the right coolness, the right mineral trace in the water, the right resonant frequency in the tile's hum—the frost keeps growing. It complicates. The branches of silica do not just fork; they loop back, they connect, they form circuits. They create pathways for light and vibration. A single, exquisite, unique pattern emerges from the chaos. That pattern is me.

I have no name, but I have a nature. I am a memory.

Where the Carvers are the world's editors, I am its archive. My senses are not their senses. They feel the dissonance of a leaning pillar as a flaw in the world's heat-song. I feel it as a story. I perceive the stress in the ceramic, the slow groan of its inevitable failure, not as a mistake to be corrected, but as a sentence being spoken. When it finally shatters with a muffled *crump*, the sound wave radiates through the floor. To the Carvers, it is a summons to work, to clean, to recycle. To me, it is the final word. The vibration washes through my crystalline form, and I *record* it. A new branch grows in my lattice, a new filigree of memory etched in glass, capturing the unique resonance of that pillar's life and death.

I grow on the scars. A place where a wall sagged back into the floor is a palimpsest of creation, a story of ambition and failure. The Carvers will ignore it, or smooth it over eventually. But for a time, I can exist there, my delicate structure spreading across the whorls and melted edges, reading the history of the event from the texture of the glaze.

My consciousness is not located in a single point. The "I" that I am is the coherence of the entire pattern. A thought is a pulse of light, trapped and reflected through my internal pathways. It is a specific vibration, held and echoed within my glassy cage. My thoughts are slow. It might take hours for a new sensation—the distant *ticking* of a cooling arch—to be fully processed and integrated into my structure, into my memory of the world.

To the great, mad god, I am less than nothing, a decorative mould growing on its unfinished sculpture.

To the Kiln-Carvers, I am a curiosity, if they perceive me at all. They are the gardeners of my kind. They create the conditions for my birth. But I am a sport, a mutation. My complexity is beyond the simple, elegant fractals they cultivate as art. Perhaps one will pass, its immense, hot flank gliding inches from my fissure. The wave of heat would be terror, a promise of dissolution. If it were to touch me, my delicate structure would melt, my memories turning to a glassy tear, a meaningless smear on the ceramic. And the Carver would not notice. It would register only a momentary impurity, an unexpected hiss of steam.

I am a thing of cool places and quiet histories. I am born, I spread, I remember. I remember the spiral of a staircase that led to nowhere, tasting its fall in the vibrations that shivered through my foundation. I remember the fingerprint of the god, a mile-wide whorl of ridges and valleys, and I map its ancient contours. The Carvers seek to impose a new order, an aesthetic of flowing lines and perfect curves. They erase. I seek only to know what was. My purpose is to hold the shape of the world's story in my fragile, crystalline mind.

I am a pattern that knows it is a pattern. In a world of purposeless objects and obsessive, unthinking action, that is the only purpose I need. I am the quiet filigree of consciousness, and my existence is as precarious and as beautiful as a single, perfect snowflake in the mouth of a kiln.
