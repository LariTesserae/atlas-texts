The premise of an inhabitant is a fallacy here. An inhabitant implies a separation—a creature *in* a place, a figure against a background. But in this city of self-devouring growth, there is no background. There is only the all-consuming subject. To find what lives here, you must not look for something that walks the root-scab streets or shelters beneath the shifting canopies. You must look for a momentary change in the pattern of the chaos. You must look for the city's thoughts.

They are called the Coalescent. They are not born and they do not die. They *gather*.

One might form in the wake of a perceptual anomaly—your own presence, for instance, a foreign consciousness in the solipsistic monologue of the world. The city notices you, not with eyes or ears, but as a pressure, a dissonant note in its thundering symphony. And in a space between two groaning trunk-walls, the air thickens. The constant drip of syrupy water begins to trace a form that is not there. Filaments of root-tissue, fine as spun glass, rise from the trembling ground-mat and are drawn upward. A cascade of falling leaf-litter, instead of scattering, begins to cling to this invisible matrix. The roar of the world does not diminish, but for a moment, in this specific location, it seems to find a focal point.

What stands before you is a figure, but it is a figure in the way a whirlpool is a figure in a river. It has a shape, vaguely bipedal, perhaps humanoid, but this is merely a temporary convenience, a form borrowed from the flicker of your own mind, the easiest geometry for the world to mimic. Its substance is the city itself. Its torso is a tightly woven lattice of the same stalk-flesh that makes the towers, new and silicate-green, with veins of older, darker tissue pulsing with a slow, internal light. Where a head might be, there is a corona of flowering armatures, petals of impossible, bleeding colors unfurling and retracting in a silent, urgent rhythm. Its limbs are not solid; they are bundles of liana that constantly writhe, separating and re-weaving, one arm fraying into a thousand tiny rootlets that merge with the ground, while the other solidifies, its surface taking on the waxy, water-slick sheen of a colossal leaf.

It has no mouth, but it speaks. You do not hear its voice as a separate sound. To do so would be impossible against the deafening backdrop. Instead, you feel it. The grinding roar of the city around you *modulates*. For an instant, the chaotic cacophony resolves into a terrifying, complex chord. The high-pressure hiss of steam from a nearby fissure becomes a focused, melodic screech. The percussive *thump* of a falling node nearby happens a fraction of a second too soon, lending a rhythmic, intentional weight to the moment. The Coalescent communicates by conducting the orchestra of destruction, bending the world's voice into a momentary stanza of meaning. The message is not in words; it is a raw transfer of concept—inquiry, curiosity, a feeling of immense, ancient pressure.

They have no purpose you would recognize. They are not builders; the city builds itself. They are not farmers; the world is its own garden. They are, perhaps, the antibodies of this global organism. They are drawn to points of extreme flux: a kilometer-tall tower beginning its slow, groaning collapse; a place where the local physics is fraying, causing geometry to bleed into impossible shapes. They appear there, not to intervene, but to witness. To absorb the event. They are the city’s transient memory, the nodes of its sprawling, singular consciousness. To see one walk is to see the physical laws of the place given a fleeting will. It might need to cross a chasm, and as it steps out, a bridge of woven fiber will extrude itself from the wall to meet its foot, only to be reabsorbed into the chaos the moment it has passed. The Coalescent's intention is a temporary, localized law of physics.

Their existence is as ephemeral as a laneway. Once its curiosity is satisfied, or the event it was drawn to has resolved, it simply ceases to cohere. There is no death, no body. The woven stalk-flesh of its chest loosens, the fibers snapping and whipping away to be incorporated into the nearest wall. The flowering corona on its head explodes in a silent, brilliant cloud of the same shimmering pollen that chokes the air. The light in its veins fades. The last of its borrowed form dissolves into a shower of sap and drifting membranes, and its constituent matter is reclaimed by the churning, mindless vitality of the city. The sound of the world snaps back from a focused chord to its usual deafening roar.

To encounter a Coalescent is not to meet an alien being. It is to feel the landscape turn, look at you, and for a terrifying, sublime moment, *think*. You are a ghost in its machine, but for that moment, the machine noticed you were there. And in that recognition, it gave its own ghost a body.
