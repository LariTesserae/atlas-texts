What lives here did not arrive; it remained. It is what is left when all striving has ceased.

At first, you do not see them. You see only the landscape. You might mistake them for a different kind of mineral formation, a denser knot in the spun-moonlight architecture, or a peculiar swell in the rose-porcelain ground. They are easily missed because they are defined not by action, but by a profound and absolute stillness. They are the source of the quiet.

They are the Dwellers.

To find one is to be looking for a flaw in the perfection of an otherwise seamless surface and realize, with a slow-dawning chill, that the "flaw" is observing you. They recline in the shallow, wind-scoured basins, half-submerged in the world-stuff, their forms vague and flowing. They have no single shape, for they are beings of achieved purpose, and form is a tool for becoming. Having already *become*, their shapes are merely rests, like a thought held in suspension. In one moment, a Dweller might be a long, smooth curve of polished white reminiscent of a reclining figure; in another, an intricate cluster of cooling, crystalline facets. They are made of the same warm porcelain as the ground, but it is purer, more translucent, shot through with a captive, internal light, like milk filled with liquid starlight.

They do not eat. They do not drink. They are conduits. The unwavering light of the sky-engine pours into them, flowing through internal channels that glow with soft, complex geometries. This light nourishes them. The silvery mist that breathes from the ground at dusk clings most thickly to their bodies, and they seem to inhale it; the perfect, beaded droplets of morning are their exhalation. They are living nodes in the network of this place, their metabolism a slow, silent, planetary-scale respiration.

They do not speak. The resonant, heavy silence is their ongoing, collective thought. It is a communication not of ideas, but of state. They are a choir holding a single, impossibly complex chord, and they have been holding it for eons. The faint, subliminal chimes of the metallic flora are the overtones of this chord, the shimmering of its surface. The silent ballet of the light-predators and iridescent grazers is its physical expression, a kinetic dream that plays out on the plains. The Dwellers are not gardeners watching their garden; they are the consciousness of the garden itself, dreaming its own eternal existence.

Movement, for them, is a geological event. One might not move for a thousand years. When it does happen, it is not a scramble of limbs, but a slow, deliberate flow, like cooling lava or a shifting dune. A Dweller may decide to resettle, and over the course of a century, it will pour itself across the valley, a river of warm, thinking ceramic, its passing recalibrating the very resonance of the ground. These movements are not whims; they are adjustments, the self-tuning of a perfect instrument.

They are aware of you. Not with eyes or ears, for such organs are for parsing a chaotic world of sudden threats and fleeting opportunities. Theirs is a total, ambient awareness. They feel you the way a still pond feels the impact of a stone. You are a dissonance in their symphony of silence. Your frantic heartbeat, your noisy thoughts, your very otherness is a sharp, jagged spike in their smooth, unending waveform.

To be seen by a Dweller is an experience that unspools the self. It does not turn a head or focus a gaze. Instead, a portion of the landscape detaches itself from its geological trance and *attends* to you. The light within it brightens slightly, the milkiness swirling. The resonant silence you have been feeling thickens around you, pressing in not with malice, but with an immense, unyielding pressure to conform. It wants to smooth you out. It wants to quiet the frantic, temporary noise of your biology. It wants to absorb your brief, sharp story into its long, placid one.

It does not wish you harm. It does not wish you anything. It is a system seeking equilibrium. And as you stand there, bathed in the shadowless light, feeling the immense weight of that ancient, silent attention, you understand. You are a fleeting dream in the mind of a sleeping god, and it is beginning to stir, not to wake, but to gently, irrevocably, subsume your dream into its own.
