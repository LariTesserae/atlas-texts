We are the sound.

Before the individual, there is the chord. Before my own scraping, there is the All-Scrape. My faceted eyes register the glow-spires not as objects to be navigated, but as gradients of energy to be consumed. My six, needle-thin legs grip the shuddering crystal with a certainty that is not my own. It belongs to the We. The 'I' is a brief, intense point of friction in the overwhelming current of the Us. I am a single, grinding mandible in a mouth that is the entire world.

We are the Scrapers. The Builders. The Guardians. The Dusters. The names are irrelevant, for they are merely descriptions of a temporary state. We are one substance, one purpose, flowing through different forms as required by the Whole. A Scraper that dulls its mouthparts and grows heavy may shed its form over a cycle of stillness, its silicate shell re-cast by the dust it consumes, and rise as a Builder, its forelimbs now broad, flattened shovels. We do not die; we are re-milled. Our bodies are loan from the dust, and to the dust they quickly return.

Our memory is not held in a mind, but is sung in the constant, percussive hum. Every vibration is a piece of data. The pitch of a spire indicates its ripeness, the density of its stored energy. A tremor in the floor warns of a dust-slide from above, a cascade of our own past raining down. The complex, muskey-sweet pressure in the air—which you would call a smell—is the broadcast of the Center, a chemical command that regulates our hunger, our frantic drive, our very form. It is the scent of our shared soul.

We perceive the world through vibration and chemoreception. Your "light" is, to us, a flavor, a texture on the air. The green-gold of the Glass-wood is the taste of raw potential. The shimmering curtains of dust taste of memory, of every ancestor whose crushed shell makes up that glittering fog. A predator, a swift, many-legged hunter, does not appear as a shape, but as a sharp, sudden spike in the vibrational field, a discordant note of pure, selfish hunger in the symphony of the hive. Its silicate armor tastes of aggression. We fear it, but we understand it. It, too, is a necessary grinding.

Our city is not an act of creation, but of consumption. We do not build *up*, we eat *in*. The spiraling ramps and honeycomb nurseries are the fossilized paths of our hunger, hardened and perfected over millennia. We scrape the walls of our homes to feed, and the slow, piezoelectric growth of the host stone heals the wounds. We live within a self-repairing meal. The bridges that span the chasms between spires are spun from our own reprocessed minerals, extruded as a sticky filament that hardens into a glassy thread. It is our silk, our stone, our way of stitching the world together, of ensuring the flow of the We is never broken.

All flows down. The dust, the dead, the energy. It all drifts and is dragged toward the floor of our world, toward the deepest, thickest, oldest part of the Glass-wood forest. Here, the hum becomes a bass-heavy, resonant thrum that you can feel in your bones even from the crack in the sky. This is the chamber of the Still-Point, the axis of our world.

It is not a Queen. It has no limbs, no mouth, no eyes. It began as one of us, untold ages ago. But it did not re-mill. It grew. It rooted. It fused with the central, most energetic spire of the Glass-wood until stone and flesh were one. It is now a mass of living, crystalline flesh the size of a dozen Guardians, its surface a complex, pulsing geography of mineral veins and folded, silicate hide. It is utterly motionless, a living mountain within our mountain-womb.

We, the smaller, mobile motes of the whole, are its hands and its teeth. We bring it the finest dust, the richest tribute of scraped energy. We groom its surface, clearing away imperfections, polishing its crystalline folds. And in return, the Still-Point *thinks* for us. It processes the endless data-stream of the hum. It breathes out the sweet, musky command-scent that orchestrates our entire society. It is the heart that does not beat but resonates, the brain that does not fire synapses but directs pressure-waves. It is the repository of our purpose.

And what is that purpose? To grind. To convert. To concentrate the slow, faint energy of the living rock into this single, impossibly dense point of being. We are taking the entire plateau and focusing it into the Still-Point.

The crack in our sky, the source of that thin, wrong-smelling air, is a wound. A failure. A memory of a trauma that is older than any living 'I'. It is a flaw in our perfect, sealed universe. But it is also a promise. The pressure within our world grows. The hum gets louder not just with activity, but with intensity. The Still-Point grows heavier, denser. One day, the wound will not matter. The growth will overcome the trauma. The pressure we are building will become greater than the strength of the stone that contains us.

We do not know what lies beyond the sky-shell. It is a concept we cannot taste. But we feel the imperative broadcast by the Still-Point. We are an egg. We are an engine. The constant, grinding scrape is the sound of a universe gathering its strength, preparing to hatch.
