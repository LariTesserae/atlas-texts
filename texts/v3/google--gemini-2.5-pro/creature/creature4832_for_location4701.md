This place has a keeper. It is not one of the builders, returned. It is what grew in their absence.

At first, you mistake it for a part of the landscape, another pressure ridge of basalt shouldering its way up from the floor. It is a long, low shape of fractured blackness, glistening with the same condensation that coats everything. It is utterly still. But the eye, once it has learned the slow rhythm of the pulsing Lumen-flora, catches a discrepancy. A shadow it casts is subtly wrong, its edge defined by a line of light that does not pulse, but holds a steady, faint, verdigris-green glow. It is the color of the oxidized bronze on the central Regulator, the color of the glowing veins in the waxy leaves.

You watch, and over the course of five minutes, the shape redefines itself. It is a slow, tectonic unfolding, a process of movement so patient it is nearly imperceptible. A limb, no thicker than an arm but twice as long, extends from the main body. It does not have joints in the biological sense; it seems to flow, its surface of obsidian-like plates grinding against each other with a sound that is lost beneath the dripping of water. The limb terminates not in a hand, but in a cluster of delicate, needle-thin bronze tendrils, which flex and test the air like the antennae of some abyssal insect.

This is the Gardener.

It is a creature of synthesis, born from the unique fusion of this cavern's violent geology, its engineered flora, and its decaying technology. Its body is not flesh, but a living, semi-crystalline silicate, like animate obsidian. Its surface is a mosaic of conchoidal fracture patterns, sharp-edged and glossy black, mirroring the floor it moves across. It is not warmed from within by blood, but by the same geothermal energy that suffuses the chamber; it is hot to the touch, its skin constantly weeping a film of condensation just like the stone.

Through the cracks in its dark hide, an internal light is visible. It is not the biological pulse of the Lumen-flora, but a constant, steady network of pale green lines—a circulatory or nervous system that mimics the illuminated veins on the waxy leaves. This network seems to draw its power directly from the environment. Where its central mass rests on the floor, fine, root-like filaments, almost too thin to see, penetrate the hairline cracks in the basalt, seeming to interface with the armored cables buried below. It is drinking the dregs of the Regulator's ancient power, sipping the low, resonant hum that vibrates through the rock.

Its head, if it can be called that, is a smooth, featureless ovoid of the same black material, its face a blank slate. There are no eyes, no mouth. It perceives the world in ways you cannot comprehend. It senses pressure changes in the air, the faint electromagnetic fields emanating from the living plants and the dying machine, the specific frequency of light from each stalk, the subtle shifts in temperature. The constant drip of water upon its back is not an annoyance, but a stream of data, telling it of the humidity and the health of the leaves above.

As you watch, its purpose becomes clear. The Gardener unfurls another limb and slowly, deliberately, approaches one of the Lumen-flora. At the base of the stalk lies a single leaf that has fallen. It has not rotted; it has become a pane of brittle, translucent crystal, the green light within it faded to a ghostly memory. The Gardener touches it with its bronze tendrils. The touch is infinitely gentle. The tendrils vibrate, emitting a low, resonant note that matches the hum of the Regulator. For a moment, the crystalline leaf glows faintly, and then, with a sound like the shattering of a thin sheet of ice, it dissolves into a fine, sparkling dust that is immediately absorbed by the condensation on the basalt floor. The Gardener has tidied this small patch of its world, removing the dead so the living may have space.

It turns its attention next to the machinery itself. It moves towards the skeletal watchman's hut, its long limbs propelling its low-slung body with a silent, sliding grace. It stops at one of the corroded junction boxes. Its bronze cilia unfurl and delicately, precisely, begin to clean away the lace-like rust, polishing a contact point, scraping away a century of decay from a single terminal. The work is painstaking. Each grain of rust is worked loose and allowed to fall. The action will take hours. This is its function. It is the living maintenance protocol, a self-perpetuating repair system that arose to fill the void left by its creators. It is the hands of a ghost, completing a task whose original meaning is long forgotten.

It does not eat. It does not sleep. It does not reproduce. It simply *is*. It is a singular, lonely creature whose lifespan is measured in geological time. It may have a form of memory, but it would not be a narrative of events. It would be a memory of frequencies, of the slow dimming of the Regulator's hum over centuries, of the precise number of water drops that have fallen since its own consciousness sparked into being.

It knows you are here.

Your heat signature is a glaring anomaly. The carbon dioxide in your breath is a sudden, sharp spike in the air's composition. The soft sound of your clothing is a roar in the profound silence.

After another ten minutes of watching it work, the Gardener pauses. Its entire body freezes. The featureless black ovoid of its head slowly, slowly swivels in your direction. The green, vein-like lights within it seem to brighten slightly. There is no threat in the movement, only a deep and ancient curiosity. It is processing your existence, trying to fit this fleeting, warm, noisy phenomenon into its slow, quiet, orderly world. The thought, whatever it may be, takes a full minute to form, a silent question humming through its crystal mind: *What cycle is this?*
