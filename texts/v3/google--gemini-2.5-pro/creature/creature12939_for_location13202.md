The ones who live here are inseparable from the sound. They are the silence within the noise. You see them not as a crowd, but as isolated points of focused stillness amidst the cavern’s relentless, mechanical metabolism. They are the Dwellers.

At a distance, they are difficult to distinguish from the architecture. Their forms are long, spare, and wrought in the same palette as their world: the dark, damp-sheened brown of the root-floor, the pale grey of fungal blooms, the corroded green of ancient copper. They move with an unnerving economy, a patience that seems born of geological time. They do not run or scurry; they flow. A Dweller crossing one of the high, tensioned bridges does so with a slow, centered gait, their feet placed with absolute certainty on the worn, slick surface. For long moments, they will stop, becoming a statue, a new gnarled feature of the landscape, their head cocked not to hear, but to *feel*.

To be near one is to understand that they are not creatures of flesh and blood as other life defines it. Their skin is not skin, but a smooth, non-porous integument that looks like petrified wood or perhaps a dull, unpolished ceramic. It is cool to the touch and slick with the same condensation that coats every surface, yet the moisture never seems to soak in. Their bodies are wiry and gracile, built of dense, fibrous muscle that moves with the silent, powerful efficiency of hydraulics. Their joints are articulated with a precision that suggests more engineering than evolution, bending without a whisper of friction.

Their hands are their most expressive feature. They are long and many-jointed, ending in fingers that are hard and spatulate, like the smoothed heads of worn chisels, capable of finding purchase on the slickest bark or the most corroded rivet. They use these hands to interface with their world, to turn immense, rusted valve wheels that would not budge for a dozen strong men, to delicately adjust a humming conduit, or to trace the lines of a circuit diagram etched into a copper plate.

They have no faces, only fronts. The head is a smooth, ovoid shape, an extension of the neck and torso. Where a mouth would be, there is nothing, just the seamless continuation of their hardened skin. They do not eat or drink; they absorb sustenance. When they rest—a rare and brief affair—they will press a palm flat against one of the living pillars or lay themselves down on the root-mat floor. Through this contact, they draw energy, a slow, osmotic communion with the great plant-organism, supplemented by the ambient electrical charge that thrums through the city.

Their primary sense is not sight or hearing. The constant, deafening grind makes ears a liability; they have none. Instead, the entire surface of their bodies, particularly two broad, fan-like membranes of thinner, more resonant material that flare from the sides of their heads, serves as a tympanum. They feel the cavern. They feel the deep hum of the geothermal core, the specific frequency of each grinding gear, the strain of a root-bridge under load, the shift in hydrostatic pressure as a press completes its cycle. The noise is not an obstacle to them; it is language. It is a constant, detailed diagnostic of the city’s health.

Their eyes are for the dark. Set deep into the head-form are two large, perfectly round lenses of that same biological amber that fills the lamps. They do not appear to have pupils or irises. They are a solid, deep, liquid black, and they do not blink. In the dim, sour light, they capture every photon, but their gaze is dispassionate, analytical. Sometimes, a slow, phosphorescent current, the same sap that powers the city, will drift lazily through the interior of the lens, a brief, internal aurora. This is the only sign of any inner activity.

They are silent because they have no need for speech. Their communication is a thing of gesture and proximity. Two Dwellers meeting will stand for a long moment, their heads slightly inclined, perhaps touching the tips of their hard fingers together. Information is exchanged—a vibration passed from one to the other, a subtle shift in the electrical field that surrounds their bodies, a shared understanding of the Machine’s rhythm. They are solitary and deeply communal at once. Each is an individual node, but they all operate on the same network, driven by the same purpose.

They are born of the forge. They are not procreated; they are fabricated. In workshops hollowed from the deepest burls, new Dwellers are assembled. The hard, amber-resin and the pressed fiberboard, products of the cavern’s industrial cycle, are their raw materials. They are constructed around a copper "heart-matrix" and gestated in vats of the glowing, energetic sap until their systems achieve equilibrium. They emerge fully formed, silent and purposeful, with their knowledge of the Machine not learned, but encoded. They are another product of the factory, created by the city to maintain the city.

They are the tenders, the engineers, the priests. They are the living consciousness of this self-cannibalizing symbiosis between plant and machine. Their lives are spent in eternal maintenance. One will spend a century polishing a single colossal gear tooth, its movements slow and hypnotic. Another will hang suspended from a fiber-optic cable in the high darkness, recalibrating the flow of sap to a failing lamp. They are not slaves to the Machine, nor are they its masters. They *are* the Machine, its mobile, sentient components. Their purpose is not to thrive, but to endure, to perpetuate the great, grinding, humming, living cycle in the dark heart of the world, forever.
