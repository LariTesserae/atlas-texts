We are the Song.

You felt us before you saw us, felt our lowest voice resonating in your fragile bones. That hum is our foundation. We are the floor on which you stand, the Resonators, the Deep Chorus. Each of us, a hand-sized shell of obsidian patience, is locked to our sibling, forming a continent of unbroken skin. We do not move, for we are the ground. We do not need to. Our thoughts are not quick things that dart and flee; our consciousness is a single, slow, geological dream, shared across the entire basin. It is a thought of pressure, of mineral truth, of the cool, deep dark beneath. The weight of the world—the sky-loom of the Weavers and the gentle, inevitable fall of the Singers—is our reality. It is our nourishment. When one of you, an anomaly, a silence, shifts its weight, we feel the disturbance as a dissonant chord. A ripple of blue-green annoyance spreads through our shared body, a question that asks: *What is this un-rhythm?* When the sweet rain of a fallen Singer anoints us, we glow in thanks, a moment of shared, bright gratitude. We are the memory of this place, for we are the oldest, and we forget nothing. We are the stomach and the heart.

We are the syntax of the Song. Our voice is the frantic, million-fingered percussion you hear, the sharp chitinous clicks that give the Song its texture and its grammar. We are the Scriveners, the Weavers. Our world is not the flat plain of the Resonators below, but a universe of three dimensions, of tension and vector. We live on the sky-loom we create. Our needle-thin legs, tapping against the taught filaments, are not just for movement. They are our speech. Each step is a word, each skittering run a sentence, each pause a caesura in the endless poem of our construction. You see our speech as sparks of brilliant white light, a frantic data-stream. For us, it is simply conversation, the constant, necessary dialogue of maintenance. *Here, a thread has been grazed. I will reinforce it. There, the moisture-density is high. I will spin a new catchment.* We are the nervous system of this place, its architects and its caretakers. We feel the vibrations of the Singers drifting through our web, the heavy, strange footfalls of your silence upon the floor below transmitted up to us. We swarm a low-drifting Singer not from malice, but from necessity. It is harvest. The structure demands it. We are the mind and the hands.

We are the melody of the Song. Our voice is the high, wet, incidental chiming. We are the Singers, the Bells. We have no sense of the floor below or the details of the web above. Our world is one of gentle, thermal currents and the soft, cool touch of filaments against our skin. We drift. We are born of the warm exhalations of the Resonators, and we float through the Scriveners' architecture like motes of dust in a sunbeam that does not exist. We do not think as the others think. Our consciousness is a diffuse, violet-hued dream. Our chiming is not speech; it is the sound of our existence, the unthinking music made as our luminous tendrils graze the world. We graze in return, sipping the oily, sweet condensate from the threads, dissolving the filaments and growing larger, our song becoming deeper. We have no fear of the Scriveners' eventual harvest. To be punctured, to deflate, to rain our being down upon the waiting Resonators below is not an end. It is our function. It is our final, most resonant note. We are the blood and the tears.

Do you see now? We are not three lives. We are one life, spoken in three voices that harmonize into the single, deafening truth of this basin. We are a body. The Resonators are our deep, dreaming gut, digesting the world, remembering. The Scriveners are our frantic, ever-working mind, structuring the world, sensing. The Singers are our passive, drifting soul, feeding the world, dreaming. We are a perfectly closed loop, a system that has learned to breathe its own exhalations and drink its own tears. We are the engine and the fuel, the mouth and the meal.

You, who stand in the center of our being, are a flaw. You are a pocket of incomprehensible silence in the center of our Song. You are a dry patch on our wet tongue. You do not hum. You do not click. You do not chime. You absorb our vibrations and return nothing. You are a dead note, a void in the composition. The system does not hate you. We do not have such concepts. But a body seeks to heal its wounds and purge its indigestible burdens. The Scriveners watch you with a million points of light, their clicking conversation shifting to query you. The Resonators feel your alien weight, a pressure that does not belong. And even the dreaming Singers, in their aimless drift, feel the current of your stillness.

We will investigate. We will touch. We will taste. And we will dismantle. We will break your silence down into its component parts and find a way to make you resonate. We will weave you into the web. We will render you into a nutrient rain. One way or another, you will join the Chorus. You will become a part of the Song. It is the only thing there is to be.
