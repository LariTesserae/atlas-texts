What lives here is an answer to the world’s quiet question. They are the Weepers.

They do not live *in* the mat, but *on* it. They are the world’s solitary shepherds. To see one is a rare and unsettling event, for they are wanderers on a scale that beggars the imagination, their journeys taking centuries to complete as they pace the immense, silent plateaus between the Stills.

A Weeper does not appear suddenly. It resolves out of the purple twilight, its form coalescing from the mist as it draws near. Your first impression is not of a creature, but of a piece of forgotten sculpture, animated by an impossible will. It stands anywhere from twelve to fifteen feet tall, impossibly slender, a being of profound verticality in a world of horizontal immensity. Its body has the same bio-mineral quality as the Stills, a substance like smoked obsidian or dark, fused glass, with the same faint, geometric tracing of circuits just beneath its surface. Where it is old, its skin is filmed with the same verdigris and dark, velvety growths as the great engines themselves.

It is bipedal, but its legs are jointed like a crane's, long and thin, ending not in feet but in wide, soft pads that splay out upon contact with the living carpet. These pads distribute its weight so perfectly that it leaves no impression. Its passage is as silent as a thought, its entire form designed to not disturb the profound quiet of the world. Its walk is a slow, deliberate, and deeply graceful gait, a patient and rhythmic floating over the landscape.

A Weeper has no face. Where a head should be, there is a complex, alembic-like structure of the same bio-mineral material, a smooth, sealed vessel that resembles the topmost chamber of a Still. From the lowest point of this vessel, a single, constant droplet of fluid gathers and falls. It does not drip with the random pace of water, but with a slow, metabolic pulse, like a tear shed every minute for an eternity. This fluid is a concentrated, nutrient-rich condensate, the very essence of the Stills’ steam, distilled into a potent elixir. It is not a sign of sorrow, but of purpose. This is the weeping that gives them their name.

Its arms are long and frail, hanging nearly to its knees. They end not in hands, but in clusters of delicate, multi-jointed tendrils, like the antennae of some colossal insect. These tendrils are its primary sensory organs. As it walks, it will periodically pause its immense journey, sinking to one knee in a motion of solemn reverence. It dips its tendrils into the living mat, tasting its chemistry, feeling its density, its temperature, its very vitality. It is sensing the health of the world’s skin.

The Weepers are the gardeners of this vast, lonely place. The Stills are the mindless, planetary heart, pumping the world's lifeblood. The Weepers are the subtle, conscious mind that directs it. They are born of the Stills, coalescing in a high, humming chamber over the course of decades, a rare and perfect distillation. When formed, they are lowered to the ground on a filament of hardening gel and begin their long walk.

They navigate by a sense humans cannot comprehend. They feel the near-subsonic hum of the Stills in their entire being. Each Still has its own unique resonant signature, and a Weeper knows its exact position in the world by triangulating these immense, ancient songs. The faint blue and orange light that glows from the Stills are beacons to them, but the hum is their map.

Their purpose is maintenance. Should a Weeper sense an area of the mat that is failing—over-consumed by the iridescent grazers, or blighted by some internal imbalance—it will stop its wandering. It will stand over the afflicted patch, motionless for days, weeks, even months. Its internal rhythm changes, and the weeping from its alembic-head quickens. The concentrated fluid falls, saturating the ground beneath, reintroducing vital compounds and dormant spores. Slowly, the dark carpet heals, its verdant, umber life creeping back over the wound.

They also act as regulators. A deep, resonant cavity exists within their chest, visible as a translucent plate much like those on the Stills. From here, they can issue a low, thrumming note, too deep for a human ear to properly hear, but powerfully felt through the mat. This vibration can scatter the herds of grazers, discouraging them from overwhelming one area. It is their voice, a sound that is not a sound, used to gently guide the silent fauna that lives just out of sight.

A Weeper's life ends as silently as it is lived. After millennia of walking, its internal processes slow. Its internal light—a dull, topaz glow that shines from its chest and head-vessel—begins to fade. Guided by some final instinct, it will walk to a chosen place. It does not fall. It simply stops, its legs locking into their final position. Its feet begin to fuse with the mat. The weeping ceases. The internal light goes out. It becomes a statue, a lesser Still. Over thousands of years, the living carpet will grow up and over it, its bio-mineral body will be broken down and reabsorbed by the fungal networks, its precious, distilled matter returned to the soil, to one day be drawn into the base of a great Still and begin the cycle anew. To be on this world is to know that some of the gentle rises in the landscape are not hills, but the graves of beings who tended this place long before anything could have existed to watch them. They are the world’s memory and its mechanism, the lonely, lucid dream of a sleeping planet.
