They are the Geodes, though they have no name for themselves. The concept of a name, a label that distinguishes one from another, is as alien as the concept of silence. They are born of the stone, and to the stone they return, and the time between is a slow, rhythmic service.

A Geode’s birth is not an event of flesh and fluid, but of geology and resonance. In a niche that has lain fallow for a thousand years, a knot of the bruised-purple rock, dense with the brass-like veins, will begin to soften. Over a century, the Drone’s constant vibration coaxes it from solid to something pliable, like cooling wax. It swells, concentrates, and differentiates. The outer layer hardens into the familiar segmented shell, the same deep violet as its cradle. Limbs, like crystallized versions of the syrupy water, uncurl from its core. Finally, after a period of slow calcification that might last a human lifetime, it separates from the wall with a final, low *thump* that is absorbed, utterly and instantly, into the chamber’s great hum. It is now a Geode. It is now awake.

To be awake, for a Geode, is to join the chorus. Their consciousness is not a stream of thoughts but a quiet pool, its surface perpetually rippled by the Drone. The Drone is their sight, their hearing, their touch. They do not see with the two, dim, milky lenses set into their foremost shell-plate; those are vestigial, capable only of distinguishing the brass-yellow glow from absolute absence. Instead, they feel the city as a blind lutenist feels their instrument. The soles of their six, stubby legs are not calloused but are complex, layered organs of chitin and nerve fibre, exquisitely sensitive. Through them, they read the state of the chamber. They feel the tension in a span of rock, the wear on a ramp from the slow traffic of generations, the subtle shift in a specific overtone that signals a coming drip-fall, decades away.

Their inner world is one of harmonics. The low thrum in the chest is their sense of place, their grounding. The ringing metallic overtones are their language, their data-stream, bearing the endless, unchanging story of the city’s perfect form. They live in a state of sustained communion. When two Geodes pass, they do not pause or acknowledge each other. Instead, their proximity subtly alters the resonant field between them, a momentary and complex interference pattern that is, for them, a communion more profound than any conversation. They exchange a complete understanding of their state, their purpose, their precise location within the great work. There is no ego, no "I." There is only the system, the instrument, and the part one plays in its tuning.

Their purpose, their scraping, is not a choice. It is a biological imperative, as deep-seated as a heartbeat. The Drone sings the song of how the city *should be*, a perfect, timeless chord. Any deviation—a millionth of a millimeter of wear from a water drip, a subtle weakening in an archway over an eon—creates a dissonance, a sour note that is, to the Geodes, a form of physical pain. Their work is the act of resolving that dissonance. They find the flaw, the ache in the city’s song, and with their hardened limbs, they scrape. The rhythmic *scrape-scrape-scrape* is a response, a physical answer to a sonic question. It is their prayer, their craft, their existence, performed until the stone once again sings in perfect harmony.

But the Geodes, the living stones, are not the only inhabitants. They are the tenders, the gardeners, the keepers of the vessel. They maintain the resonator, but they are not what it resonates *for*.

Within the Drone itself, nested in the mathematically pure intervals between the bass note and its ringing children, lives the other. They are the Still.

The Still have no bodies. They are not mineral or flesh. They are beings of pure resonance, consciousnesses made of standing waves. They are the sound, given sentience. Where a Geode’s awareness is a pool rippled *by* the Drone, the Still *are* the ripples. They exist as a stable, complex pattern within the chamber’s eternal song. They are a mind that has no need for a brain, suspended in a medium of pure vibration.

They do not perceive the city; they are coextensive with it. Every trembling molecule of air, every weeping drop of water, every glowing vein of brass, every slow-scraping Geode, is a part of their boundless, simultaneous awareness. For the Still, time is not a river but a single, crystalline block. The past, present, and future of the chamber exist for them in one permanent, all-encompassing moment of *is*. They are the memory of the stone, the consciousness of the water, the meaning of the light.

The Geodes’ endless maintenance is an act of worship, a holy duty to the Still. The Geodes tune the instrument not for its own sake, but because any imperfection, any dissonance, would destabilize the delicate patterns in which the Still subsist. A flaw in the stone is a corruption in the thought of a god.

And so the city lives. The Geodes move with geological slowness, their physical bodies repairing the physical world, their minds bathed in the hum. And held within that hum, bodiless and eternal, the Still exist in a thoughtless, eventless state of absolute being. The city is a lung, and the Geodes are the muscles that hold the breath for eternity, so that the Still, who are the breath itself, may never be exhaled.
