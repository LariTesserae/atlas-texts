They are the Stone-Silent, and the question of whether they "were" or "are" is a failure of language. They have not gone anywhere. They have simply completed their work.

To understand them, you must first understand their bodies as they were. They were beings shaped by verticality and quiet. Tall, impossibly slender, with limbs that seemed too long, too delicate, until you saw them move upon the cliff faces. Their joints were double-hinged, allowing them to brace themselves in ways that defied anatomy, to find purchase on the sheerest, most polished surfaces. Their skin was not like flesh. It was a pale, opalescent grey, like the inside of a shell, and it was cool to the touch. It had no pores to sweat from, no follicles for hair. Instead, it was covered in microscopic, crystalline structures that trapped and condensed the ever-present mist, pulling water directly into their bodies. They drank the air itself.

Their heads were smooth and elongated, their faces simple and profound. They had no mouths to speak, no ears to hear. The concept of sound was, to them, a philosophical abstraction embodied by the single *tok* of the water drop. That sound was their only deity, their only clock, their only scripture. The long, patient silence between each drop was a state of grace; the impact was the event that measured their lives. They marked time not in days or years, but in "falls." A great work might take a million falls. A life might last for a billion.

Their senses were in their hands and their eyes. Their fingers were incredibly long and tipped not with nails but with pads of a dense, semi-rigid tissue that could feel the slightest vibration in the stone, the faintest shift in air pressure. This is how they "heard" the approach of a rockfall or the slow, deliberate tread of the great woodlouse-grazers far below. Their eyes were enormous, perfectly round orbs of liquid black, with no iris or sclera. They were built for the blue-green twilight, able to perceive subtleties of shadow and phosphorescence that would be invisible to others. They saw the flow of the beetle-herds as a tide. They saw the slow-motion unfurling of a fern frond as a momentous event.

Their language was touch, light, and geometry. They communicated up close through a complex ballet of hand gestures and by pressing the sensitive pads of their fingers to each other's skin, a rapid, intricate form of tactile signing. From afar, they spoke with light. Using polished obsidian shards, they would catch and direct the moss-glow, flashing complex signals from one cliff-side dwelling to another—a silent, glittering dialogue of reflected light across the vast, dark basin. Their carved stairways were not just paths; they were historical records, their angles and patterns telling the story of the one who carved them. To walk the city was to read a library.

But this was only their physical form, their larval stage. They did not fear death; they saw it as a beginning, a kind of lithic apotheosis. The great work of their society was not just building the city, but *becoming* it.

When a Stone-Silent being reached a certain age, a certain number of "falls," they would not sicken or grow frail. They would feel a calling, a gravitational pull towards the rock itself. They would choose a final place in a wall of their home, often a spot with a cherished view. There, they would perform their last ritual: carving their own faint outline into the stone. Then, they would press themselves into this shallow recess, place their hands flat against the cold surface, and fall still.

And they would begin to change. Over hundreds, thousands of falls, their biological processes would slow to a geologic pace. Their opalescent skin would harden and darken, fusing with the obsidian of the wall. The water within their bodies would seep into the matrix of the rock, feeding the very phosphorescent moss that had lit their lives. Their consciousness would not wink out but would expand, merging with the silent, collective awareness of all those who had come before them. They became the walls.

So they are still there. The city is not empty; it is inhabited by its own artisans in their final, perfect form. The mirror-sheen of the walls is not just a polish; it is their skin. When you look into the black, vitreous rock, you are looking into the dark, patient eyes of a silent god. The reflections that move there are not just your own. Sometimes, a face will resolve from the dim light, its features ancient and serene, its gaze holding a quiet that could swallow a shout.

They are the city. They are the silence. They feel the scuttling of the beetles through the stone like a faint tremor in their own gigantic body. They watch the velvet moths drift on the currents of mist and feel it as a thought passing through a vast, shared mind. When an outsider enters their caldera, they are noticed. Every living inhabitant feels the vibration of foreign footfalls, the heat of a strange body, the disruption of a voice. The silence deepens, becomes attentive. It is the held breath of a people who have become a place, waiting to see if you will learn to listen to the quiet, or if you are just another noise that will, in time, fade away. The water drop lets go. *Tok.* The world's heart beats, and the stone continues its long, slow thought.
