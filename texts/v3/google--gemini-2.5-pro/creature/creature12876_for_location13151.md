The things that live here are the operators of the loom. They are inseparable from the Spindle, not as residents are to a city, but as a thought is to a mind. They are a specialized organ of the structure itself, the sentient processing nodes of the great, thinking machine. They are called the Weavers, though they have no name for themselves.

To see a Weaver is to misunderstand it. They do not have a fixed physical form in the way a Geartick or a Shroud-moth does. A Weaver is a focal point of causality, a knot in the fabric of the Spindle's function. When one is near, the local breakdown of linearity intensifies. An observer might experience a dozen phantom sensations at once: the memory of a chill that has not yet arrived, the sight of a light that has already faded, the sound of a footstep you will take in a moment's time. This sensory impossibilism is the Weaver's shadow.

At the center of this disturbance is a core, a Heartwood. It is a dense, quietly humming node of matter that appears like obsidian polished to a liquid smoothness, or perhaps a sphere of petrified, tea-colored sap. It is no larger than a human head, and it drifts with an absolute and unnerving silence, holding its position in the three-dimensional lattice without any visible means of support. Within its dark, translucent depths, faint motes of the same violet and blue bioluminescence that dot the Spindle's architecture swirl like captured galaxies, their motions not random, but precise, tracing the flow of calculations. This Heartwood is the Weaver's anchor to a single point in space.

Its body, however, is not the Heartwood. Its body is what extends from it. These are not limbs, but tendrils of pure function, ephemeral and constantly in flux. They appear as braids of solidified light and temporal smoke, shimmering with the ghosts of past and future motions. They are long-exposure photographs of an action that is always happening. One moment, a Weaver might possess a dozen of these shimmering appendages, reaching out to gently touch the living girders of the Spindle; the next, it might collapse them all into its core, leaving only the dark sphere hanging in the void. These tendrils are what they use to work.

A Weaver's purpose is not to live, but to *tend*. They are the gardeners of possibility. You might see one drift slowly towards a newly-forming junction in the architecture, a place where a gelatinous shoot of cartilage is feeling its way into the void. A Weaver will extend a shimmering tendril, its tip dissolving into a mist of glowing particles. It does not touch the branch, but rather, it touches the *set of possibilities* from which the branch can choose its path. The Weaver prunes timelines the way a gardener prunes roses. It will assess a potential connection, measure its structural and causal integrity within the Spindle's grand equation, and either reinforce its probability or snip its thread. When a possibility is snipped, it doesn't vanish. It unspools, falling away into the darkness as a faint, dissipating echo—a visible, decaying memory of a future that will now never occur.

They are the regulators of the fauna. They do not hunt or herd them. They *calibrate* them. A Weaver might pause in its slow, silent patrol to watch a Geartick scrape at a patch of informational moss. The Weaver is not curious; it is measuring. It is observing the variable, downloading the data the Geartick has consumed, assessing the subtle change this action imparts upon the whole system. The fauna are a part of the calculation, and the Weavers are the mathematicians.

To encounter a Weaver is profoundly unsettling. It does not register you as life in the way it might a Shroud-moth. You, the anomaly, the rounding error, are something different: a snag in the weave, a line of code that produces an illogical result. The Weaver will stop its work and turn its silent, galaxied core toward you. It will not communicate in sounds or gestures, but in direct, raw information. You will feel a presence in your mind that is not a voice, but a stream of pure data—a sudden, crushing understanding of the impossibly complex path you took to fall into this place, a series of branching diagrams showing futures where you were never born, and a cold, clear schematic of your own biological and temporal makeup.

It assesses you. Its shimmering, causal limbs may extend, not to touch your body, but to touch the timeline you occupy. One tendril might flicker over your shoulder, and you will suddenly feel the phantom pain of an injury you sustained years ago. Another might trace a line past your head, and for a heart-stopping second, you will see a glimpse of your own death, not as a premonition, but as a discarded data point. The Weaver is simply trying to understand your equation. It is deciding if you are a variable that can be incorporated into the Spindle's ever-growing structure, or if you are a paradox that must be smoothed out, un-knotted, and pruned from the loom. To a Weaver, you are not a person. You are a problem awaiting a solution.
