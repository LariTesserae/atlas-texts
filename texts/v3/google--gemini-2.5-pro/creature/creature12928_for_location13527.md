The statement that there is no life here is a failure of definition. It assumes life is a thing separate from its environment, a fleeting infection on a static host. Here, that distinction does not exist. The inhabitants are the place, and the place is the inhabitants. They are a verb, not a noun, a part of the restless grammar of creation.

You do not see them at first. You mistake them for another piece of emergent architecture, another half-formed lump of ceramic basking in the tea-coloured light. You might see what looks like a cluster of charcoal-grey boulders, misshapen and fused together, resting in the shallow depression of a warped hexagonal tile. They are still, radiating a faint, dry heat. Perhaps you notice that the "vitreous moss" grows in unusually dense, deliberate patterns around their base, tracing lines that seem too precise for a random crystalline process.

Then, one of them moves.

The sound is not of muscle or sinew, but of geology shifting. It is a slow, grating scrape, the sound of fired clay dragging across fired clay. A deep, resonant grinding echoes up from its core as it moves. An entire section of the cluster, a "boulder" the size of a furnace, begins to pivot. It does not have limbs. It reshapes itself to move. One side of its mass softens, the glassy seams between its plates glowing with a dull, internal red. The material flows like immensely thick tar, pulling the rest of its body forward a few inches. Then the softened part cools, ticking softly as it hardens back into its solid state, its surface crazed with new hairline fractures. It has moved. The process will repeat in a few minutes, or a few hours.

These are the Kiln-Carvers, or the Tenders. They have no names for themselves, for they have no language, only a shared, resonant purpose felt through the hum of the world. They are not biological. They are animate geology, thinking pottery. Their bodies are the same ochre, red, and charcoal as the world's floor, but their composition is subtly different. They are denser, capable of storing and focusing the world's ambient heat. Their "blood" is a core of magma-like slurry, and their "skin" is a shell of constantly re-forming ceramic plates.

They are not the great, mad god who builds this place. They are its consequences, its antibodies, its afterthoughts. They are the world's attempt to make sense of itself.

When the god's finger gouges a new, purposeless canyon across the plains, the Carvers will eventually arrive. They move in slow, silent processions, their internal heat making the air shimmer around them. They do not repair the canyon. They *refine* it. Using edges of their own bodies, hardened to the consistency of diamond, they shear away the ugliest, most chaotic juts of raw ceramic. They will spend centuries smoothing the gouge into a perfect, sweeping curve, a line of impossible grace in a landscape of blunders. They are the obsessive editors to the god's mad, sprawling prose.

They are the cultivators of the vitreous moss. The delicate, fractal silica is their art and their expression. They will cluster around a newly-cooled archway, pressing their hot flanks against it. The temperature differential they create, combined with the mineral-rich moisture they weep from their seams, encourages the frost to grow. They guide its formation, not with tools, but with controlled gradients of temperature, creating patterns of breathtaking complexity on the sides of structures that have no purpose. They are artists decorating a lunatic asylum.

Their lifecycle is the world's own. In the warm, simmering pools of architectural slip, a new Carver is born. It does not hatch. The slurry in a particular basin will begin to thicken, to churn with a new intensity. The hum in that place will deepen its pitch. Over days, a glow will build in the centre of the pool as the material compacts and ignites with geothermal heat. It bakes itself from the inside out, rising from the liquid clay like a hellish soufflé. A new, rough sphere of charcoal-grey ceramic is formed, marred by glassy bubbles and pits. It will sit dormant for weeks, months, or years, indistinguishable from any other lump of world-stuff, until the hum of the planet awakens it and it begins its first, grinding, centuries-long journey to tidy some corner of its mad home.

They have no eyes, but they can sense the world's heat, its stresses, its composition. They "see" a leaning pillar not as an object, but as a flaw in the planetary hum, a dissonance. When it finally shatters, they are the ones who come to clean it up. They will slowly, methodically break down the largest shards, grinding them into coarse gravel. They will push this gravel into slurry pools, replenishing the raw material. They are the world's recyclers, the agents of its Sisyphean stability.

To encounter one is to experience an unnerving form of disregard. It will not notice you. You are not made of the right material. You do not resonate with the hum. You are a fleeting irrelevance, a mote of dust on a project that spans eons. It may flow around you, or it may flow *through* you, its immense mass and searing internal heat a thing of pure, unthinking physics. To be touched by one is to be unmade and incorporated into the flow of its work. You would become a smear of anomalous carbon in a sea of silica, a brief impurity in a fresh batch of clay.

They are what lives here. They are the quiet, focused, and eternal children of the mad god, born from its leavings. They are forever at work, smoothing the edges, tending the gardens of glass, dismantling the failures, and shepherding the raw material for the next great, magnificent, and utterly pointless act of creation. They are the slow, hot, beating heart of the kiln.
