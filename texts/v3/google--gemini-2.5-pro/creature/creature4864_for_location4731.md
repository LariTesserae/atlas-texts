The intelligence that lived here did not arrive, and it did not depart. It grew.

To look for a creature that walks on the cork-like ground or swims in the still basins is to misunderstand the very nature of this place. The faint architectural memories—the braided vines, the guided archways, the circular foundations—are not the relics of a people who have vanished. They are the final, fading gestures of a people who have *arrived*. They are the last exhalations of individuality before a total and final immersion.

The beings who live here are the Gardeners. And they are the garden.

They were once, perhaps, discrete entities. They may have had limbs to walk and hands to shape. In that ancient time, they began to work with the world's sole material, the living flesh of the planet. They did not carve, for there was nothing to carve with. They did not build, for there was nothing to stack. They *persuaded*. They learned the language of the low-frequency hum, the deep grammar of the world's metabolism. They found they could guide the growth of a vine, encourage the fusion of roots, and coax the plant-flesh into new forms. The arches and screens were their first successes, their first attempts to imprint a pattern of thought onto the overwhelming, uniform pattern of life.

But this was only a beginning. Why build a shelter *from* the world when you could become a shelter *within* the world? Why strive against the silent, static perfection when you could join it? They sought an end to struggle, an end to change, an end to the cycle of generation and decay that, even in this hothouse, must have been a distant, ancestral terror. They craved the ultimate equilibrium.

And they found it.

Look to the mountains. Those colossal, smooth upwellings of pale, gourd-colored flesh, those titans of cellulose that swell toward the unseen ceiling. They are not geological formations. They are tombs, and they are wombs, and they are the final, living bodies of the Gardeners.

Within each of those silent, ivory peaks, a consciousness—or what was once a consciousness—is embedded. They are no longer individuals. They are ganglia, vast, distributed neural networks woven into the very fiber of the mountain's being. Their thoughts are not expressed in language but in biochemistry. Their collective will does not command, it *maintains*. They are the source of the profound stillness.

They are the great, silent dreamers. The low-frequency hum is the thrum of their shared, unending dream. It is the sound of a trillion biological processes held in perfect, unshakable stasis by a single, unified act of will. The butter-yellow light that saturates the world is the light of their consciousness, a steady, unblinking, inward-looking gaze that has become the sky. The absolute equilibrium of the water basins, where condensation perfectly matches some imperceptible absorption, is a manifestation of their absolute control, their final mastery over the physics of their own flesh. This world is not in a state of arrested becoming; it is being actively, perpetually, and willfully arrested by its inhabitants. They traded change for eternity.

They are aware, but not in a way a separate mind could comprehend. Their awareness is the feel of a single spore drifting for a century in the thick air, the subtle pressure of a water droplet forming on a leaf, the slow, geologic swelling of their own mountain-bodies. They perceive everything, and because they perceive everything, nothing is novel. There are no surprises. There is only the endless, perfect is-ness of their creation.

And what of other life? Those simple, sac-like drifters, the sky-jellies glowing with soft blue light? They are not an accident. They are thoughts. They are the simplest of permissible ideas, given form and let loose into the dreamscape. They are the Gardeners' equivalent of a sigh, a slow, aimless thought allowed to drift through the vast, quiet halls of their own mind. They feed on the spores, the world's own dust, keeping the air pristine. They are purpose-built cleaners, biological servants so simple they cannot even conceive of their own servitude. They are perfect, harmless, and utterly controlled.

To walk here is not to explore an empty world. It is to walk through the body of a god. The air is its breath, the ground its flesh, the light its unceasing thought. Every step you take is registered. Every vibration is felt. You are not unobserved. You are a discordant note in a symphony of absolute silence, a fleeting, chaotic variable in an equation that has been solved for eternity. The Gardeners do not move to greet you or to destroy you. They simply... observe. And in their immense, patient, unified mind, they decide if you are a novelty to be tolerated, a flaw to be corrected, or simply a new kind of spore, to be filtered from their air in time.
