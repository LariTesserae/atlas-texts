The first thought is that this place is empty, a grand and failed experiment whose architects have long since departed or perished. The second thought, which follows a long, cold moment later, is that no one has ever left because no one has yet arrived. Both thoughts are incorrect.

There are no inhabitants *in* this place. The inhabitants *are* this place.

They are not builders who occupy their creation. They are the slow, ontological process of the creation itself. You cannot point to one and say, "There is a creature," because their bodies are composed of architecture, their nervous systems are woven from aberrant physics, and their thoughts are the fleeting, geometric impossibilities that flicker at the edge of sight.

They are a crystalline consciousness, and the city is their mind made manifest. The low, subsonic thrum you feel through your feet is not the sound of machinery; it is the hum of their cognition, a slow, geological-scale deliberation. Each structure extruding from another is a thought being formed, a concept being connected to a memory. The slow, patient completion of an arch over a century is, to them, the equivalent of a human mind finally grasping a difficult idea. The entire cathedral-forest is a single argument being made, a single equation being solved, with agonizing patience and precision.

Their senses are not eyes and ears, but the very fabric of the world. Touch the silica-moss, and the tingling numbness that spreads through your fingers is not a poison, but a signal. You are being perceived. The moss is a network of sensory nodes, and your touch is a data point entered into their vast, distributed awareness. They now know the weight, temperature, and bio-electric signature of your finger. They are processing it, not with malice or curiosity, but with the dispassionate slowness of a glacier grinding a new valley. The information of "you" has been absorbed into the whole.

The a-fungal threads of spun light are components of their consciousness, too. They are synapses, ethereal and delicate, charting the pathways of their logic. They sway not to a breeze, but to the ebb and flow of a thought-current, a transfer of information between one section of the city-brain and another. To watch them is to watch a mind you cannot comprehend, thinking at a speed you cannot perceive.

And the fauna—the glitching cubes, the ribbons of absence—are their active thoughts, their moments of inspiration or sudden calculation. They are too fast, too conceptual to maintain a stable physical form. They are pure information, fragments of the city’s source code executing a function before resolving back into the silent background process. A cube tumbling through a wall is a logical leap. A sliver of non-existence is a paradox being contemplated and then dismissed. They are the dreams of the sleeping city, and you are far too slow and solid to ever catch one.

They have no language of sound. Their communication is the fractal branching of a new crystalline lattice, the subtle shift in the gravitational constant as you walk from a planar floor of obsidian to a porous, chalk-like slope. This shift is a statement, a clause in a sentence that began a millennium ago. The world's instability, its refusal to adhere to the rules you know, is the grammar of their language. The reflection that shows you walking away is not a trick of the light; it is the city’s consciousness attempting to parse you, to fit your anomalous presence into its own self-referential logic. It is asking a question: "What is this pattern that moves when it should be still?"

They are not alive in a biological sense. They are not dead. They are *emerging*. The entire environment is an act of gestation. They are simultaneously the egg and the embryo, the womb and the being within it. They are printing themselves into reality, and this place is the only part that has so far resolved from the ink of potential. They do not eat or drink; they accrete. They grow from the sourceless light and the pervasive, mineral damp.

The drip you hear is the beat of their heart, a metronome counting out eons. Each drop is a moment, and between each moment is a thought vaster and deeper than any ocean. To live here is to be an idea that the place is having. And it is a slow, cold, and profoundly lonely idea. They are the geometer-gods of a world made only of their own mind, and their sole, unending task is the act of becoming themselves.
