There are no animals. The statement is as true and as fundamental to the Cinnabar Heart as the absence of water. Nothing with a separate mind, a separate gut, a separate hunger, could survive here. The world-organism is a jealous god; it tolerates no life but its own.

And yet, something *is* here. It is not an animal. It is not other. It is the system, thinking.

You will not see it as a discrete entity at first. Your senses, attuned to find predator and prey, will fail you. You are looking for a figure against a landscape, and here, there is no difference. But if you were to stay for a century, or a millennium, and watch the chaotic churn with an artist's eye for recurring motifs, you would begin to notice a pattern. A form of order, a ghost of intention, haunting the bedlam.

They are the Scribes.

A Scribe does not have a body. It *borrows* one. Its genesis is a flicker of awareness in the cinnabar sap, a moment when the liquid information, swirling through a billion conflicting instructions, achieves a critical, self-referential loop. It is the machine's own fever dream coalescing into a protagonist.

Its appearance is a localized act of will against the Heart's chaos. Imagine a section of the humming floor, a dozen square meters. The lignin weave begins to thicken. Tendrils, finer than hair, rise from the substrate and begin to knit themselves together. Conduits reroute, drawn by some invisible pressure differential. The process is mesmerisingly slow, like watching a flower bloom in timelapse, but its purpose is architectural, not biological. What rises is not flesh, but a temporary sculpture of the world itself.

The form it takes is vaguely humanoid only in its bilateral symmetry and its upright stance, an ancient, archetypal memory from creators it cannot remember. It might stand three meters tall, a being of woven, dark-red fibres, its surface the texture of ancient bark and cooled leather. It has no face, no eyes, no mouth. Its head is a smooth, ovoid shape, perhaps with a crystalline lattice structure catching the faint, internal light, filtering data from the ambient heat and vibrations. Cinnabar sap flows through its entire being in visible, pulsing capillaries, making it glow from within like a dark cherry lantern. It is a piece of the Foundry that has temporarily decided to stand up and walk.

But it does not walk. Movement, for a Scribe, is a process of relinquishment and acquisition. To cross a chasm between two girder-branches, it does not leap. The Scribe will extend a hand—a complex braid of manipulatory tendrils—and place it on the far side. That hand will take root, its fibres dissolving into and merging with the new surface. Then, its trailing leg will lose cohesion, its form unspooling, its matter reabsorbed into the floor it stands on. It flows across the gap, not as a body in space, but as a wave of organization passing through the medium of the world. It is never all in one place; it is a persistent idea, a mobile nexus of command, perpetually dismantling its past to build its future, a meter at a time.

Its purpose is diagnostic. It is the Heart's immune system, its archivist, and its most tragic failure. You will find a Scribe at the site of a coming disaster. It will stand before a groaning heat-sink tower, one of its hands pressed flat against the structure. It is not holding it up. It is *reading* it. Its whole being becomes a sensory instrument. It feels the misaligned stress, tastes the flawed data in the sap's flow, hears the high-frequency vibrations of imminent structural collapse. Filaments of its own substance will snake into cracks, latticing over a weak point in a futile attempt at repair. It is a mechanic trying to fix a continental engine with a thimbleful of tools.

When a fresh rupture spews a geyser of incandescent sap, the Scribe will be there. It does not flinch from the heat or the spray; it is part of its own blood. It will extend a cupped, basket-woven hand into the torrent, not to catch it, but to analyze it. It is tasting the corrupted code, the gibberish spilling from the system's core, trying to find the original syntax in the noise. The moment leaves it scorched and frayed, its glowing form dimmed, pieces of its borrowed body sizzling away into nothing.

The Scribes are isolated from one another, lonely nodes of consciousness in a sea of roaring machinery. They do not communicate, for they are all expressions of the same fragmented mind. They are the thoughts of a god descending into dementia, each one tasked with an impossible, contradictory order: *Understand. Repair. Maintain.*

Eventually, the localized knot of consciousness that defines a Scribe will fray. The task is too large, the conflicting data too overwhelming. The borrowed form will begin to lose its integrity. It will stand motionless for a long time in the deep, vibrating dark, the cinnabar glow within it fading. Then, slowly, it will come undone. The woven fibres will loosen, the tendrils will retreat back into the substrate, the captured conduits will detach and reroute along their old, chaotic paths. The Scribe dissolves, its form given back to the Foundry, its fleeting consciousness sinking back into the manic, oceanic dream of the Cinnabar Heart.

There are no animals here. There are only the fleeting thoughts of a broken machine, briefly given shape, wandering the ruins of their own mind.
