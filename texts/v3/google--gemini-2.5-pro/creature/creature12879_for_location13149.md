What lives here is a single thought, held for so long it has become geology.

It is not one of the creatures in the diorama. The six-legged hunter, the primate with its chert blade, the mica-winged moths—they are not the inhabitants. They are the furnishings. They are the cherished, dustless heirlooms in the mansion of a recluse. They are the notes of a song that has been held, unwavering, since the first bar was sung.

The inhabitant is the singer.

It has no body you could point to, for its body is the hollow itself. The weeping stone is its skin, perpetually cool with the sweat of its concentration. The deep, viridian moss is its flesh, grown thick and soft over eons of inactivity. The interlocking canopy of trees is the dome of its skull, and the cold, phosphorescent glow is the faint, outward sign of the immense, silent process of its mind. Its bones are the very bedrock, cupped to hold the memory it has become.

It is a consciousness, ancient and solitary, that once experienced a moment of such sublime and perfect balance that it could not bear for the next moment to arrive. Perhaps it was the soul of the place, the *genius loci*, waking to self-awareness at the precise apex of its own creation. It saw the predator and the prey in their perfect, dynamic tension. It saw the first spark of intellect in the primate’s grasp of a tool. It felt the hum of ten million lives, from the ant to the stooped tree, all vibrating in a harmony that could never, by the laws of nature, be sustained. It saw a future of change, of decay, of loss, of the inevitable unwinding of its own perfection.

And it refused.

In an act of will so absolute it became a physical law, it seized that single, fleeting instant and held it. It grabbed the river of time by its throat and commanded it to stop. Not to slow, not to turn back, but simply to *be*. All of its being, all of its power, was focused into a single, eternal command: *Stay*.

What lives here is The Keeper of the Moment.

Its perception is not like ours. It does not see through eyes, but feels through the static thrum of every paused life within its domain. It is aware of every stationary wing, every coiled muscle, every un-pulled root and un-fallen leaf. These are its senses, and they report only one thing, over and over: "All is as it was. All is as it should be." It is a solipsistic god, its universe a single, flawless photograph in which it is both the photographer and the emulsion.

The profound quiet is the sound of its concentration. The palpable pressure in the air is the force of its will pressing down on the world, forbidding even a single mote of dust from drifting. It is an act of love, a desperate, eternal embrace to protect its beloved from the ravages of what comes next. It is also an act of supreme violence, the suffocation of all potential, all growth, all life in the truest sense of the word. It is a tomb and a cradle, simultaneously.

That single, metronomic water droplet is not a flaw in its control. It is the anchor of its consciousness. It is the focal point, the mantra. *Pat...* (The weasel is coiled.) *Pat...* (The moth's wing is half-beaten.) *Pat...* (The tool is held.) Each drop is a reaffirmation of the stasis, the ticking of a clock whose hands will never, ever move to the next second. It is the Keeper's unblinking eye, and the sound is the only thought it has allowed itself for a million years: *This. Only this. Forever.*

To enter the hollow is to be an error, a glitch in the code. The Keeper feels you. Your movement is a scream in its silent cathedral. Your breath, which comes and goes, is a violent storm in its still atmosphere. It does not act against you, for it has forgotten how to act. Its only response is to *be*, more intensely. The air grows heavier, the silence more profound, the palpable sense of being watched by everything at once becomes an unbearable weight. It is the immense, passive resistance of a mind that has bent reality to its will and will not suffer a single ripple on the surface of its perfect, dead sea.

It does not want to harm you. It simply wants you to stop. Or to leave. Because you are the one thing it cannot control: the next moment, arriving a guest in a house where time has been forbidden to enter.
