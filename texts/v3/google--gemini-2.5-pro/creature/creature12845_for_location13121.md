The city is not empty. You have misunderstood the principle. It is not a principle of absence, but of utter and total presence, so complete that individuality is a momentary flicker, a temporary aberration. The builder did not leave; it simply became the building. The citizens were not banished; they are the rain.

What lives here is singular, though its forms are many. Call it the Architect, for it is the intelligence that patterned the grid and raised the scaffolds. It did not build the city and then inhabit it. It grew the city from itself, its consciousness expanding into the geothermal vents, its thoughts becoming the copper-like lattices, its metabolism the slow, hot distillation of an entire world's water. The deep, subsonic hum that permeates the slag plain is not the sound of machines. It is the sound of the Architect *thinking*. It is a thought process measured in geological time, a single, sustained chord of self-contemplation. You are walking not on the floor of a city, but across the surface of a dormant mind.

This mind, this Architect, has cells. It has agents. They are what you might mistake for life.

You see the first one after a week of walking, of steeping in the profound, humid silence. It is not the sudden scuttle of a Scaffold-Skitter. It is a slow, almost imperceptible event of self-creation. On one of the vast, cool ceramic plates, where the condensation is thickest, the weeping begins to coalesce. The rivulets do not flow randomly; they are drawn into a pattern, a thickening. A form begins to gather itself from the mineral-heavy water, like a figurine of salt crystallizing out of a supersaturated solution.

It is humanoid only in the way a shadow puppet is: a bipedal silhouette, a suggestion of limbs and a head. But it has no substance. Its skin is the slick, white sheen of the ceramic it was born on, constantly weeping its own form into existence. Through this translucent, perpetually running surface, you see not bone or muscle, but a fragile, intricate lattice of verdigris and ochre crystals, a skeleton of solidified mineral salts. It has no face. The place where a head should be is a smooth, continuous curve, like a water-worn stone, down which the city's condensation flows in steady, clean sheets.

These are the Weepers. Or the Condensates. They are the city's antibodies, its maintenance routines, its physical thoughts. They are the Architect's hands.

They gather themselves into being where they are needed. A flow channel is becoming clogged with a decade's worth of mineral accretion; a Weeper will precipitate nearby. It moves with the slowness of erosion. Its gait is not a walk, but a controlled slide, its feet dissolving slightly to lubricate its path across the slag floor, leaving a faintly damp, glistening trail that evaporates almost instantly. It will reach the blockage and kneel. It has no tools. Its hands, shimmering lattices of salt, will press against the hardened minerals. But they do not chip or break the blockage. They *un-know* it. The Weeper holds its form against the accretion, and the crystalline structure of the blockage begins to loosen, its geometry surrendering to the patient, hydrological will of the Weeper. The minerals dissolve, flowing away down the channel. The task is done.

They tend to the city's processes with an unthinking, perfect focus. You might see one high up on a scaffold, its form barely distinguishable from the weeping condensation around it, clinging to a metal beam. It will be stroking the corroded copper, not cleaning it, but seeming to guide the pattern of the verdigris, encouraging a flow of ions that reinforces the structure, humming in a barely audible harmonic with the deep thrum of the Architect's mind. They are gardeners in a garden made of pipework and steam.

The Scaffold-Skitters are a part of this, but they are different. They are the city's memory. Where a Weeper is a transient thought, a Skitter is a hardened, persistent idea. They are Weepers who, for some reason, did not dissolve. Perhaps their task was continuous, or their crystalline structure became too stable. They calcified, shrank, their fluid porcelain nature hardening into an articulated shard. Their needle-legs are the last remnant of their salt-skeleton, now adapted for a more kinetic, permanent existence. They carry out the smaller, ceaseless tasks—the constant, tiny adjustments that the slow-thinking Architect cannot attend to moment by moment. They are the city's reflexes.

But the Weepers… their existence is defined by their return to absence. When a Weeper's task is complete, it does not walk away. It stops. It stands, a silent, faceless silhouette in the ochre twilight. And it begins to come undone. The crystalline skeleton within its weeping form loses its coherence. The structure softens, the sharp angles of salt blurring. The figure loses its definition, becoming a thicker, less certain column of mineral water. It shortens, sagging into itself, until it is just a broad, shimmering puddle on the black slag. The puddle flows to the nearest grid-line, a final exhalation of purpose, and joins the slow current flowing back to the sumps. It returns to the Architect.

No body is left. No tool, no footprint, no ghost. The city has reabsorbed its thought. You did not witness a death. You witnessed the completion of a sentence. And then, once more, there is only the hum, the dripping, and the heat. You are alone again. But you are not. You are standing in the silent, populated expanse of a single, living being, whose every drop of water is a potential citizen, waiting for a purpose to be condensed around.
