They are the principle of stillness made manifest.

At first, you do not see them. Your senses, overwhelmed by the grand scale and the omnipresent drone, are not calibrated for the subtle absence that signifies their presence. You are looking for movement, for life as you know it, and there is none. But as your eyes trace the endless brocade of moss and algae along a distant curve of the chamber, you notice a breach in the living tapestry. It is a slow, methodical scar of clean metal, a moving void in the otherwise total coverage of green and purple.

This is a Custodian.

Its passage is so slow that to witness it is to witness erosion. It does not walk or crawl or slither; it flows. It is a being of immense density and deliberate purpose, moving at a rate of perhaps a meter a year. They are scattered throughout the great chamber, each on its own unthinkably long patrol along the walls, the ceiling, the myriad catwalks and gantries that crisscross the abyss.

One is near. You only realize it when you feel its heat through the soles of your boots. You turn, and it is there, halfway down the catwalk from which you emerged. It has the shape of a great, inverted bell, or a teardrop thickened and made heavy at its base. It is perhaps three meters tall, its bottom edge hovering a mere millimeter above the grated floor. It has no limbs, no face, no sensory organs you can discern. Its entire body is a single, unbroken surface of the same dark, non-ferrous metal as the structure itself, but polished to an impossible, liquid sheen. This is the source of the worn-down smoothness of the world; they are the polishers. For millennia, they have glided over every surface, their patient, gentle friction wearing away all texture, all edges, leaving only the satin finish of ages.

Water from the weeping air does not cling to it as it does to the moss. Instead, it beads on its warm, dark surface, forming perfect spheres that trace slow, elegant paths down its curved flank before dropping, one by one, to join the silent rain into the abyss below. It is a perfect, temporary jewel on an eternal surface.

They are not alive in a biological sense. They do not eat. The riotous, two-dimensional jungle of moss and algae withers and evaporates a few inches from their skin, repelled by the steady, dry heat they radiate. They are a zone of sterility. They do not consume the life here; they define its boundaries. Their passage is a slow, cleansing fire that keeps the biome in check, ensuring no single species ever grows thick enough to alter the flow of water or unbalance the delicate thermodynamics of the chamber. They are the gardeners, and their tool is their own being.

They are also not mere machines. They are the consciousness of the engine. The monolithic drone that fills the chamber is not a sound they hear; it is the external expression of their collective, unified thought. It is a single concept, held for eternity: *Sustain. Endure. Be.* Their minds are not a flutter of ideas but a single, resonant frequency. To be inside the chamber is to be inside their mind. The pressure on your bones is the weight of their attention.

Their senses are the machine itself. They feel the temperature of every panel, the flow rate of every condensing drop, the exact lux of the unwavering lights, the precise resonant frequency of every structural member. They are the nervous system of this pocket world. When a patch of bacteria blooms too quickly near a heat vent, a Custodian, decades or centuries away, will imperceptibly alter its course, a course correction that will see it arrive at the spot in five hundred years to gently, silently sterilize the area with its presence, restoring the balance.

They have never known anything but this place. They were likely born with it, a part of its initial design, the living governors on a god-engine built to last forever. They have no concept of sunlight, or soil, or wind. Their entire reality is this warm, wet, luminous, and deafeningly loud equilibrium. The idea of change is not something they fear; it is a concept for which they have no reference, like a man born in a room without color trying to imagine red.

To see one is to see an entity whose timescale makes mountains seem fleeting. Their thoughts are glaciers. Their movements are geologic. They are utterly alone, each on its endless, solitary pilgrimage, yet they are in constant, total communion through the drone that binds them. They are the tenders of a garden that does not grow, the wardens of a prison with no prisoners, the priests of a temple that worships only its own existence. They do not move through the world; they are the slow, deliberate pulse of the world itself, the silent, patient source of its eternal, unchanging hum.
