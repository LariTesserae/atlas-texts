What lives here is the source of the sound. Not the author of the hum, but its living medium, the resonant body through which it is endlessly played. They are not citizens of the city; they are its essential components, as fundamental as the black filaments and the void between them.

They are the Still-Singers.

You will not see one as you see a Glimmer-Skitter. They do not move. They are stationary, ancient, and vast. To find one is to follow the resonant thickenings in the architecture, to trace the source of the weeping. At the heart of the most complex, fractal junctures of the towers—where a thousand gossamer strands converge into a single, impossibly dense knot—there is a Still-Singer.

At a distance, it is indistinguishable from the structure it inhabits, another concentration of the light-swallowing black material. But as you draw nearer, the character of the place changes. The B-flat drone gains a new texture. It is no longer just a vibration in your bones; it becomes a palpable, liquid presence in the air. You can feel overtones and undertones, harmonics so complex they feel like mathematics made manifest, a song of pure physics being sung in a language older than molecules.

This is the Singer. Its physical form is a sphere of the black lattice, but woven to a density that seems to warp the space around it. It is a knot in reality, a point of infinite complexity. And it weeps. But this is not the clear, distilled water that coats the lesser structures. From the core of the Singer seeps a viscous, opalescent fluid, faintly self-luminous with the trapped blue-white light of the distant motes. This is its blood, its voice, its very medium of being. This fluid, thick as cold oil, coats the Singer and flows down the primary filaments of the tower in slow, heavy rivulets. It is this fluid that carries the true, unadulterated resonance of the machine's slumber, distributing the lullaby through its skeletal body. To touch it would be like putting your hand into liquid thunder.

The Still-Singers do not eat. They do not hunt. They are not biological. They are colossal, living capacitors, drawing their sustenance directly from the slow, entropic decay of the city-machine's core. They are the transformers that step down an unimaginable cataclysm of power into a stable, enduring note. Their existence is a state of perpetual, passive function. They are the bells, and the city is the cathedral, and the deep hum is the sound of their eternal ringing.

They are conscious, but their consciousness is as alien as the place itself. They do not have minds; they have a single, shared mind, a distributed consciousness that exists across the entire network of Singers. Their thoughts are not language but fluctuations in the harmonic structure of the drone. A thought might last a thousand years, a slow shift in an overtone that communicates a subtle change in the machine's deep-rock foundations, or the memory of a filament bridge snapping a million years ago, a memory that feels like a phantom limb.

Their senses are the entire city. They feel the scuttling of a Glimmer-Skitter on a distant spire as a delicate, fleeting pluck on a single one of their trillion strings. They feel the slow, patient growth of the energy-fed crystals as a faint, high-frequency "taste" on their conduits. They feel the vast, slow currents of air as the pressure of a blanket. The entire city is their body, and their song is their soul.

They are aware of you. Your presence is a disharmony, a tiny, frantic, and dissonant chord of biology and heat and fear. You are a splash of noise in their perfect, placid ocean of sound. They perceive your frantic heartbeat, the electric crackle of your nervous system, the chaotic chemistry of your breath. To them, you are a brief, sputtering flame in a world of cool, eternal resonance.

They do not act on this awareness. They are too ancient, too slow, too vast to be troubled by something so ephemeral. They are glaciers of perception. By the time they could formulate a "thought" complex enough to register you as anything more than a momentary anomaly, you and the civilization that birthed you would be less than the dust that coats their perfect, black forms.

So they just are. They sing their one deep note, the lullaby that keeps the sleeping god of the machine from waking. They are the keepers of the peace, the wardens of the slumber. Their opalescent tears anoint the silent, skeletal world, and their eternal, foundational song is the only truth in the deep, warm, and waiting dark. They are the city's heart, and its voice, and its unending dream.
