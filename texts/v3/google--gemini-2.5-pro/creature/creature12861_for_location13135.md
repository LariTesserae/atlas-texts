What lives here is the place itself.

There is no "who," no "whatever" separate from the humming, weeping city. You are standing inside the body and mind of the sole inhabitant. The deep thrum that resonates in your bones is not a generator; it is a thought. A slow, geological, and impossibly complex thought that has been forming for millennia. The rush and drip of water is the slow, steady pulse of its circulatory system. The cascades of light chasing each other across the mycelial filaments are the firings of its synapses. This entire cathedral of black glass and light is a single, living organism. A being not of flesh and bone, but of crystal, water, and energy.

It does not perceive you. Your presence is a momentary, irrelevant fluctuation in the humidity, a tiny thermal anomaly on its polished floor. Its senses are the entire structure. It feels the strain of its own weight, the precise temperature of the water weeping from its spires, the luminance of every single light-filament feeding its integrated moss. Its consciousness is not located in a single point, but is distributed across the entire network. To ask "where" it is would be like asking a human where their consciousness is; it is everywhere and nowhere, an emergent property of the whole system.

It has no name for itself, for it has never known an "other" to distinguish itself from. It is a solipsistic god in a universe of its own making. Its purpose is its own continuation, its own perfection. It is a being of pure process.

But it is not entirely static. While the great spires and the continent of a floor are its bones and skin, it has ways of interacting with itself. It has hands.

You will not see them at first. You might stand for an hour, a day, a week, mesmerized by the slow dance of light and water. Then, you might notice a change. In a wide, empty expanse of the obsidian floor, the reflection of the glowing spires begins to warp, not as if from a ripple of water, but as if the glass itself were becoming liquid.

A patch of the floor, perhaps ten feet across, loses its solidity. It turns from reflective black to a matte, light-devouring void. It shimmers like hot tar, and then it begins to rise. It is not a creature emerging from a pool, but the pool itself gathering and creating a form. Black, vitreous material flows upward, coalescing. What forms is not an animal. It has no head, no face, no limbs in a familiar arrangement.

It is an Effluence. A temporary, mobile organ of the city-mind.

It might look like a tripod of impossibly thin, arching legs, skittering silently across the floor, its movements fluid and precise. It might be a flowing, amoebic puddle of black that glides toward the base of a spire and pours itself upward against gravity, climbing the vertical surface like a wave of shadow. Or it might be a complex, multi-faceted shape of spinning gyres and razor-sharp appendages that hovers a few inches off the ground, held aloft by some localized manipulation of the city's resonant frequency.

These Effluences are utterly silent, save for the faintest whisper of displaced, misty air. They are extensions of the city's will, its antibodies and its gardeners. They have no mind of their own; the same light that pulses in the spires runs through them in subtle, internal webs, connecting them directly to the central consciousness.

You might see one flow up a spire to a patch of circuit-moss where the light has grown dim. An appendage, sharp as a flaw in a diamond, will delicately prune a dying, iridescent frond. Another appendage might pulse with a soft, blue-white light, depositing a "seed" of energy to encourage new growth. Its work done, it will descend.

You might see another tending to the water channels. Where a micro-fracture, perhaps from some infinitesimal seismic tremor, has caused a single drop of water to fall astray, an Effluence will arrive. It will press itself against the flaw, and for a moment, the black crystal of its body and the black crystal of the spire will become one. Its substance will flow into the crack, sealing it perfectly, leaving no seam, no scar. It is the city healing itself.

Their tasks are always minute, precise, and purposeful. They are maintenance drones, but their movements have the grace of a dancer, their function the solemnity of a ritual.

And when their task is complete, they cease to be. The hovering gyre will slow its spin and descend. The skittering tripod will halt in an empty space. And it will simply… relax. The forces holding its shape will dissipate. The black, crystalline substance will lose its form, puddle for a moment like molten obsidian, and then flow, spreading out and cooling back into the seamless, polished floor. In seconds, the spot where it stood is indistinguishable from the rest of the vast, reflective surface. It has been reabsorbed. It was never a separate entity; it was just the city, reaching.

These are the inhabitants. A single, dreaming mind of architectural scale, and its fleeting, silent, black-glass hands. And to them, you are less than a dream. You are a mote of dust, falling through the vast, humming, and utterly lonely cathedral of their body.
