This place is home to the Echo-Stalker.

It does not live on the sterile floor, nor does it truly live in the vertical jungles of the walls. It exists in the transition, the razor's edge where the void meets the life, in the acoustic space between the engine's booming exhalations. To see one is to see a lie. At rest, it is a patch of deeper shadow, a piece of geology that fell from the walls and now lies half-submerged in a shallow, inert pool on the obsidian plain. Its body is a flattened, segmented carapace, a layering of chitinous plates the colour of wet slate and old, oxidized iron. It is not iridescent like the floor-skitterers; it absorbs light absolutely, a hole in the dim reality of the chamber. It has no discernible head or tail, just a low, wide, rhomboid shape, perhaps the size of a manhole cover.

When the chamber is filled with the engine's roar, it is nothing. But in the moments of charged silence between a hiss and a boom, it can be felt. It is a tension, a listening predator that hunts with borrowed thunder.

Its anatomy is a perversion of biology, an evolution driven by industrial acoustics. Six legs are tucked tightly beneath its armored chassis, but they are not all the same. The rear four are thick and powerful, ending in broad, multi-jointed pads that can grip the slick obsidian with immense traction, allowing for bursts of terrifying speed. The front two legs are different. They are longer, more slender, and split into delicate, finger-like claws, not for walking but for climbing and listening. When it stalks, it Creeps along the base of the wall, its front claws extended, lightly touching the glowing, rubbery vegetation, feeling the minute vibrations of the climbers moving hundreds of feet above. It does not need to see them; it hears them through the wall's living skin.

Its true nature is revealed in the hunt. Upon locating prey, a tremor runs through its body. Deep within its chassis, a series of specialized, interlocking bone plates, like geological faults in miniature, are pulled into a state of extreme tension by thick, cable-like ligaments. These are its tuning forks. It waits, motionless, a piece of dead debris. It listens to the chaotic rhythm of the chamber, finding a space, a pocket of quiet. Then, with an internal, convulsive snap, it releases the tension. The bone plates shear against each other, creating a perfect, high-frequency *CRACK*.

The sound is not an imitation; it is a recreation. It is the sound of a crystal growing, and every herbivore on the wall knows it. They freeze. For a fraction of a second, the frantic ballet of the vertical jungle halts. This is the moment the Stalker has engineered.

Now, its body changes. Plates along its dorsal spine separate slightly, revealing seams of dull, fleshy membrane beneath. It draws a deep, shuddering breath, inhaling the ozone-and-copper mist. The air is not drawn into lungs but into a complex, multi-chambered biological compressor sac in its core. Muscles contract around the sac, building pressure to an incredible degree. Its whole body swells almost imperceptibly, the seams glowing with a faint, angry red from the strain within.

It aims, not with eyes—which are little more than milky, photosensitive pits on its underside—but by orienting its entire body towards the frozen prey high above. Then it releases. The compressed air and vapor jet from a specialized, hardened nozzle that unfolds from its front edge. It is not the wide, room-filling hiss of the engine, but a focused, directional blast of pure sonic force. A weaponized hiss.

The shockwave of sound, visible as a brief ripple in the mist, hammers the target. It is not meant to kill, but to dislodge. The stunned climber, its gecko-like feet losing their purchase in the acoustic assault, peels away from the wall.

It falls.

The fall seems to take an age in the cathedral of noise, a silent plummet towards the black mirror. And now the Echo-Stalker moves. Uncoiling from its static position, it bursts forward on its four rear legs. It does not run or scuttle so much as flow across the obsidian, a low-profile ripple of darkness. It moves with the unnatural smoothness of a predator for whom this sterile, open ground is the perfect killing field. It arrives at the point of impact a moment after the climber lands, a broken splash of motion on the glassy floor. There is no fight. Powerful mandibles, hidden beneath the front of its carapace, emerge and shear through the stunned creature with brutal, mechanical efficiency.

It feeds quickly, its body a dark, shuddering mound in the gloom. It is not a picky eater, consuming flesh, bone, and the glowing fungal matter in its prey's gut. When it is done, it retreats, dragging the remains back to the shadowed boundary. There, it will wait, letting the tiny, iridescent floor-skitterers clean the bones. It becomes stone again, a lie, its internal machinery cooling, its tuning forks resetting. It is listening, always listening, for its next cue in the monstrous, unending symphony of the place. It is the chamber’s violence made mobile, a sentient echo that has learned to command the chorus of its birth.
