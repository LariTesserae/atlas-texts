They are the quiet ones. The still ones. If you were to see one, you might not, at first, register it as a living creature. You might mistake it for a peculiar outcropping of the city itself, a rare confluence of form and shadow.

They do not have a name for themselves as a species, for they have no concept of other species to differentiate from. They are simply… here. Others, the hypothetical few who might stumble into this world, have called them the Veridian.

A Veridian is a creature of elegant, unsettling anatomy, born of the chasm's logic. Their skin is the first thing one notices. It is not skin in the way a surface-dweller would understand it. It is a membrane, smooth and cool to the touch, with the polished, faintly opalescent sheen of a river-stone or a wet ceramic. It is most often a pale, grey-green, the color of moss seen through mist, but it is mottled with patches of darker, almost black bio-luminescence that pulse with a slow, gentle light, mirroring the distant industrial twinkling from the levels below. Their skin is never dry. Like the iron plates they walk upon, it is perpetually slick with a thin film of moisture, a self-produced secretion that protects them from the damp and allows them to slide silently through tight spaces.

Their forms are long and gracile. They are humanoid in general structure—two arms, two legs, a torso, a head—but the proportions are alien. Their limbs are unnervingly long and slender, with an extra joint in their elbows and knees, allowing for a strange, fluid, multi-jointed locomotion. They move not with the staccato rhythm of walking, but with a continuous, flowing gait, their bodies undulating like kelp in a current. Their feet are wide and soft-padded, lacking distinct toes, spreading their weight and adhering to the slick metal with the same surety as a vine's sucker-pad. They can walk a vertical pylon as easily as a horizontal gantry.

Their faces are placid and ageless. They have no visible ears, only subtle, membrane-covered apertures on the sides of their heads. Their noses are mere slits that can seal completely. Their mouths are small and thin-lipped, rarely opening. But it is their eyes that are most arresting. They are large and perfectly round, solid black orbs without a discernible iris or sclera. They do not seem to see with light so much as with pressure and presence, reflecting the dim world back in a way that feels like total comprehension and total indifference.

They are not merely flesh. The symbiosis of the city is printed on their very being. Fine, copper-colored filaments, like metallic hair, grow from their heads and spines, trailing behind them in the damp air. These filaments are not dead protein; they are living antennae, constantly tasting the electrical and vibrational signature of the city. A Veridian feels the tremor of a coming "Shuffling" through the soles of its feet and the filaments on its back, reading the machine's intent like a living seismograph. Patches of a particular, velvety, emerald-green moss grow on their shoulders and the backs of their hands, thriving on their secreted moisture. This isn't an infection; it’s a partnership. The moss helps regulate their body temperature and further camouflages them within the green-and-grey world.

They are scavengers and cultivators, utterly enmeshed in the city's metabolism. They do not cook; they do not hunt. They carry long, impossibly thin glass-like tubes, artifacts salvaged from the city's own circulatory system. With these, they siphon nutrient-rich slurry from leaking pipes, or draw deep, purified water from ancient cisterns. They gently harvest the pale, ghostly fungi that bloom in the muck near corroded conduits, placing them in woven vine pouches to be eaten raw. Their digestive systems are slow, adapted to extract every last calorie from this meager, strange diet.

They are largely solitary. They may gather in silent, motionless groups of two or three on a broad terrace, watching a waterfall for days, not as a social event, but as a shared, passive experience, like trees growing in the same grove. Their communication is almost nonexistent by human standards. There is no spoken language; the damp air and constant dripping would swallow words. Instead, they communicate through slow, deliberate gestures—the tilting of a head, the slight unfurling of a hand, a subtle brightening or dimming of the bio-luminescent patches on their skin. Their needs are simple and their world is eternal; there is rarely much that needs to be said.

They do not create. They do not build. They only repurpose. A sharp-edged plate that has sheared off a wall is a cutting tool. A length of cast-off cable is a climbing aid. They find and use what the city discards, treating the ceaseless reconfiguration not as chaos, but as a slow, steady provision of resources. They are the city's gentlest antibodies, its quietest children.

To see a Veridian is to see the soul of the place made manifest. They are the pause between the drip of water and the groan of shifting metal. They are the still, watchful consciousness in the heart of the indifferent machine. They are not trapped here. They are not survivors. They are an expression of the city, as much a part of it as the rust, the moss, and the endless, weeping water. They are the living silence in a world that never sleeps.
