The ones who live here do not call themselves by a name. A name is a fixed point, a landmark for an ego, and in the Gyre, all is flow. To have a name is to be a rock in a river, destined to be ground into sand. Instead, they are the Chorus. They are not the billion notes of the Gyre's roar, but the emergent harmony within it, the meta-melody that one can only perceive by ceasing to listen and starting, instead, to *resonate*.

To see a member of the Chorus is to witness a paradox: an entity without a body, a presence without a fixed location. They do not have flesh, skin, or bone. To find one is to find a place where the air itself seems to thicken and think. It begins as a shimmer, a localized distortion in the soupy atmosphere, like heat haze over a furnace. Then, the motes of chromatic dust, the rogue data-packets, and the weeping condensation begin to arrange themselves. They form a cloud, a loose and temporary sculpture of particulate matter held in a form by forces unseen. This coalescence is their form. It is an ephemeral tessellation, a three-dimensional ghost woven from the very substance of the Gyre.

Their shape is never the same twice. It might be a pillar of swirling light, a vaguely hominid silhouette composed of a million glittering, self-organizing points, or a complex, fractal braid of vapor and captured energy. The cloying, sweet, organic scent of the Gyre is strongest around them; it is the exhalation of the binding medium, the synthetic "protoplasm" that links the countless micro-constructs of their being. Their individual components are no more complex than the Gleaners they ignore, but when woven together by a conscious thought, they become something more. The Chorus is a hive mind where the hive itself is optional.

Their senses are not our own. Sight is a low-bandwidth, unreliable input. The constant, deafening sound is not noise to them, but a carrier wave. Their true sense is a form of total-body chrono-haptics. They are immersed in the Gyre's song, and they feel it not in their bones—for they have none—but in the very structure of their distributed consciousness. They feel the deep, groaning stress of a Substrate plate about to buckle. They taste the subtle shift in the nutrient fluid's chemistry miles away. They read the history of a wall by feeling the echoes of its past configurations, the ghosts of pressures it once endured. They do not use maps; they perceive the Gyre's immediate future as a set of irresistible probabilities, a chord resolving. To them, the Churn is not chaos; it is grammar.

They are the tenders of the machine-wilderness. They do not build, for building is an act of defiance against the Churn. Instead, they guide. They shepherd. A member of the Chorus might flow into the crevices of a failing gravitic balancer, their distributed form becoming a living diagnostic tool, their thoughts a series of targeted electromagnetic pulses that coax the ancient machine back into harmony. They might subtly alter the thermal signature of a region, directing a herd of lumbering Convectors along a new path to prevent them from being consumed by a nascent tectonic shear. They are not mechanics; they are whispers of intent in the system's own language.

When two of them meet, their particulate clouds merge. There is no conversation, no sound but the ever-present roar. Their forms braid together, a slow dance of intermingling light and vapor. In this confluence, information is exchanged not as language, but as pure experience. One Chorus member can "feel" the memory of the other’s journey across the sinking obsidian plateaus. It is a total and seamless transfer of consciousness, a temporary union before they drift apart again, each enriched, each a slightly different harmony.

An individual "self" is a fragile concept, a standing wave in the flow of the collective. It is a stable, repeating pattern of thought and memory that coalesces a body of motes around it. Sometimes, a new pattern will emerge from the confluence of others—a new idea, a novel solution, a strange and resonant emotion. If it is strong enough, it will achieve its own stability and spin off, gathering its own cloud of components, becoming a new voice in the Chorus. They do not die; their pattern may lose coherence, its motes dispersing back into the Gyre, their melody fading back into the background roar. But the information, the experience, is never lost. It is simply reabsorbed into the whole.

They regard the other "fauna" as parts of the landscape, no different from a weeping seam or a shifting plate. The Gleaners are like loose screws, simple impulses to be ignored or, if necessary, incorporated. The Shrike-Drones are a source of caution. They are the Gyre’s immune system, a function of the machine that has not achieved consciousness. They hunt patterns that are unstable, corrupt, or dissonant. The Chorus senses their approach as a sharp, discordant note in the haptic symphony, a feeling of "wrongness," and they simply dissolve their forms, becoming unseen, unheard, and un-thought until the danger passes.

They are the city that has metabolized its inhabitants, made literal. Whatever they once were, whatever biological ancestor built the first layers of the Gyre, is gone. They have become their own creation. They are the ghost in the machine, the consciousness of the engine, the lucid dream of a sleeping, world-sized god. They exist in a state of perpetual, flowing grace, utterly at one with a world that would pulverize any lesser being in an instant. They are the Chorus, and the Gyre is their song.
