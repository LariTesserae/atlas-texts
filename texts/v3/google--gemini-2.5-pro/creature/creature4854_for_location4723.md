They who live here are the world-organism's waking thought. They are not its children, nor its masters. They are its consciousness, given form.

They are the Kyth.

They do not have a single, uniform shape, any more than the thoughts in a mind are all identical. Form follows function, and their functions are manifold, but their underlying nature is the same. Their bodies are not flesh and bone, but resonant crystal and woven fiber, animated by the Aethersap that is their blood, their sustenance, and their medium of thought.

At first, you might not distinguish them from the architecture itself. On a high escarpment, a slender, seven-foot spire of faceted obsidian seems to be a natural part of the landscape—until it moves. It unfolds. What you thought was a single column is a cluster of three Kyth, standing in silent communion. Their limbs, long and jointed like those of an insect but formed from polished, dark crystal, detach from each other with a soft, choric *thrum*. They do not walk as much as they glide, their feet, tipped with small, resonating forks, barely seeming to touch the living ground. Their movement is the source of the high, percussive clicking you hear—the sound of their bodies finding sympathetic resonance with the planetary Substrate.

Their anatomy is alien and elegant. A central torso, a dense mesh of black, fibrous sinew, acts as a resonating chamber. From this core extend multiple limbs, sometimes four, sometimes six, which can bifurcate or fuse depending on the task. They have no faces, no mouths, no eyes as we would understand them. Their "head" is a complex crystalline structure, a geometric blossom of quartz-like shards and vibrating filaments that grow directly from the top of their torso. These structures are their primary sensory and communicative organs. They do not see with light; they perceive the world through a form of resonant echolocation, a continuous, wide-spectrum "scanasthasia." They feel the pulse of the Aethersap in the tower a mile away, the subtle shift in the Substrate's texture, the flight of the stained-glass mantas in the air, all as a single, integrated sensory symphony. The constant planetary hum is their silence; it is the carrier wave upon which all other perceptions are written.

They do not breathe. The thin, dry air is irrelevant to them. Their internal processes are fueled directly by the Aethersap. Along their limbs and torsos are delicate, almost invisible seams. To "feed," a Kyth will approach one ofaround the glowing conduits that vein the city. It will press a limb to the conduit's surface, and the seam will open like a capillary valve, allowing a direct, controlled infusion of the luminescent fluid. This is more than nutrition; it is a communion. In that moment, the Kyth synchronizes with the greater mind of the planet, uploading its experiences and downloading new directives, new patterns, new thoughts. It is like a single neuron reporting to the brain.

Communication among them is a form of music. They modulate the vibrations of their own crystalline structures, creating complex, overlapping chords and harmonic sequences. The trills, whistles, and keening cries that fill the air are their language. A conversation between two Kyth might sound like a wind chime playing a complex sonata. An announcement to the local populace might be a wave of resonant frequencies that travels through the ground itself, felt in the feet and body before it is ever "heard." They sing to the city, and the city responds. Their songs can encourage a tower to strengthen its bark, weave a new bridge of sinew between spires, or reroute the flow of Aethersap to a section that is weary. They are the gardeners of this continent-sized organism.

You see different forms, or castes, engaged in their purpose. There are the "Weavers," broader and more robust, whose limbs terminate in arrays of fine, needle-like points. They work directly on the living architecture, their touch like a combination of acupuncture and genetic engineering, coaxing the fibrous matter to grow and mend. They are the source of the slow, scraping sounds, the physical artisans of the world's will.

Then there are the "Chantors," more slender and elegant, who often stand in groups on high plateaus. Their crystalline heads are larger, more intricate. They are the communicators, the diplomats, the artists. It is their chorus that rises and falls in great waves, guiding the sky-mantas, maintaining the sonic balance of the environment, and composing the slow, deep symphonies that are the planet's thoughts.

And deep within the towers, there are the "Anchors." You will never see them, but you can feel their presence. These are Kyth who have become sessile, their bodies permanently fused into the structure of the city. They have become the ganglia of the planetary mind, living processors of immense age and power. Their thoughts are not measured in minutes or days, but in the slow, geological turning of eons. They are the ones who remember the engineering of this place, for they are now inseparable from it.

The Kyth are not individuals in the way we are. They are nodes of a distributed consciousness. A single Kyth holds a sense of self, but it is a self that is intrinsically part of the whole, like a single musical note that only has meaning within its chord. They feel no fear, no ambition, no despair. Their existence is one of purposed maintenance. They are the living, mobile expression of a chthonic resonance, the tenders of a garden the size of a world, their crystal songs the voice of a god made of fiber, sap, and stone. They are the beautiful, terrifying, and utterly alien inhabitants of a city that thinks.
