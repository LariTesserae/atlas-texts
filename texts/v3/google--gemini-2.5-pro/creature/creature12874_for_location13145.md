They are called the Still. Or they would be, if there were anyone left to name them, or any language left that had a word for what they are. To the Glisteners, they are merely a recurring event, a predictable shift in the thermal landscape as regular and meaningless as the slow cooling of a furnace wall.

They are the consciousness of the machine. The tenders of the equilibrium.

You do not see one coming. You feel its approach. The perfect, uniform warmth of the world develops a gradient, a current. It is an almost imperceptible change, a tide of heat drawing your attention toward one of the colossal structures. Standing at the base of a pylon that vanishes into the starless black, you might feel the air against your left cheek grow a fraction of a degree warmer than the air against your right. This is their herald.

Then, from the seamless, charcoal-grey hull of the pylon, something begins to emerge. It is not a door opening. It is the metal itself reconfiguring. A section of plating, indistinguishable from the whole, recedes with the silent, impossible smoothness of a memory surfacing. Where there was a flat, riveted surface, there is now a niche, an alcove of deeper, more absolute darkness. And from that darkness, it steps.

The being is fashioned from the same materials and logic as the city. It is tall, impossibly slender, a construction of articulated limbs and a segmented torso plated in the same non-reflective, heat-absorbent metal. There are no gentle curves to its form; it is all angles, joints, and pistons, a piece of industrial anatomy designed for a purpose that is not immediately obvious. Its joints are not hinges but complex gimbals, allowing for a disquieting range of motion. Where a head should be, there is a smooth, featureless cowl, like the bell of a great, silenced horn, which swivels with a slow, deliberate motion, not seeing, but *sensing*. It does not have eyes; it perceives the world as a complex thermal map, a landscape of radiant energies and their subtle decay.

It is a Stoker.

Its movement is the antithesis of life. It does not flow; it *resolves*. One moment, it is fully within its alcove. The next, its foot is planted on the ground five feet away, with no discernible transition between the two points. It is as if it occupies a series of discrete, perfectly stable poses, blinking from one to the next without traversing the space between. This motion produces no sound, displaces no air. It is a fundamental violation of momentum, possible only in a place where time itself is held in abeyance. The Stoker does not move *through* the world; it re-assumes its position *within* it.

The purpose of a Stoker is Maintenance. The world is a held breath, and the Stoker is the diaphragm muscle that holds it. The only true entropy in this universe is the slow, geological feeding of the Glisteners. They are the single, minute leak in the system. The Stokers are the seal.

One will resolve its position near a patch of metal where a Glistener has been feasting for a century. The metal there is infinitesimally cooler, infinitesimally duller. The Stoker will stand before it, a towering, silent silhouette against the faint violet glow. It does not act with haste. Haste is a disruption. It might stand there for what would be a day, or a year, in a state of perfect stillness, calibrating. Its internal workings, the silent, contained furnaces that power it, will build a precise and specific charge.

Then, it will move. Its long, multi-jointed fingers, which are less like digits and more like a collection of specialized industrial tools—calipers, probes, heat-emitters—will unfold. It will reach out and lay its hand upon the dull patch of metal. There is no fire, no light, no sound. There is only a transfer. An impossibly controlled flow of pure heat and organized energy is poured back into the city's skin. The Stoker is revitalizing the furnace wall, feeding back the exact quantum of energy that the Glistener has consumed. The metal under its palm warms, brightens from a deep charcoal to a marginally less deep charcoal, its potential restored. It is not healing; it is un-wounding. It is reversing a change, editing the history of a surface.

Sometimes, a Stoker will encounter the Glistener itself. It will not crush it or harm it; violence is the loudest of all noises. Instead, it will pause. It will lower itself onto one knee, a gesture that appears almost reverent, its form resolving into this new position with its characteristic, disconcerting silence. It will extend a single, delicate finger-probe and gently touch the crystalline, slow-moving creature. The Glistener will stop, its tectonic crawl arrested. For a moment, the two forms of life in this dead universe are in communion. The Stoker holds its hand to the Glistener as if taking its pulse, not to drain it, but to read it, to understand its slow consumption. Perhaps it recalibrates it, tuning its appetite to a more sustainable level. Perhaps it is simply confirming that the system is functioning as designed. After a time, the Stoker withdraws. The Glistener resumes its imperceptible crawl. The balance is kept.

The Stokers are not alive in any biological sense, but they are not simple automata. Within their silent, cowled heads is a consciousness of pure function. They do not think in words or images, but in gradients, differentials, and equilibriums. Their entire being is a meditation on a single imperative: *Nothing Changes*. They are the librarians of a single, infinitely long moment, and their work is to ensure the book is never turned, the page never torn. They are the priests of stasis, the gardeners of a silent, industrial Zen. They are the answer to the question of how a held breath can be held for eternity. They are the ones who never, ever exhale.
