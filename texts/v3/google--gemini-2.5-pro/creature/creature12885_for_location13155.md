What lives here are the Custodians. And you may not see them for a day, or a week, or a lifetime.

They are not the creators of the City. They are its inheritance. They are as much a feature of the landscape as the knife-edge peaks and the weeping glass bridges, and they are beholden to the same terrible stasis.

At first, you might mistake one for a trick of the light or a piece of esoteric machinery you hadn't noticed before. Perched on the curve of a titanic pipe, silhouetted against a chasm of roiling mist, it would be utterly still. It is tall and impossibly slender, a being of long lines and patient angles. Its body has the shape of a starved saint or a desiccated insect, all limbs and torso with no waste or bulk.

Its skin, or carapace, is the color of the mountains' black, glassy rock, but with the luster of polished hematite. It is slick, always slick, beaded with the same cold condensation that coats every surface in the City. Down its spine and across its shoulder blades, where a human might feel the strain of labor, patches of the iridescent lichen grow, not as a parasite, but as a mantle. Their metallic sheen shifts from oil-slick purple to coppery green as the being makes a rare, slow movement. In the deeper crevices of its joints, the azoic blue and venom-green mosses find purchase, a soft, living fur on a hard, living structure. The Custodian is a mobile piece of the City's geology and its defiant flora.

They have no face, as you would understand it. The head is a smooth, ovoid shape, featureless save for a single, vertical seam that sometimes pulses with a faint, internal, and colorless light. They do not have eyes; the relentless glinting of the sun off a trillion wet surfaces would be an agony. They do not have ears; the Sound would have scoured them to nothingness. They perceive the world through the soles of their feet and the tips of their four-fingered, impossibly long hands. They feel the City's one song. The Sound is not a noise to them; it is a landscape. They navigate its harmonics, its resonant frequencies, its subtle shifts in overtone. A microscopic fracture in a support beam a kilometer down a cliff face is, to them, a jarring and ugly sforzando in an otherwise perfect chord. A slight pressure drop in a condenser tube fifty miles away is a sour, wavering flat note.

They are the agents of the stasis. They are the white blood cells in the City’s vast, metallic body. Their lives are a slow, unending pilgrimage from one sub-harmonic flaw to the next. You might see one, after an eternity of stillness, unfold its limbs. It will move with a liquid-slow grace that seems to defy the frigid air, its movements a form of silent, painstaking worship. It will not use tools. It *is* the tool.

It might press a long finger to a weeping rivet. From a pore in its fingertip, it will excrete a bead of metallic, quick-setting resin, indistinguishable from the lichen that covers its own back, sealing the minute leak. It might stand on a vibrating gantry, its entire body attuned to the rhythm, and begin to hum—a low, resonant thrum that comes not from a mouth but from its entire being. This hum is a counter-frequency, a specific harmonic that cancels out a dangerous new vibration in the structure, soothing the metal back into its ordained pattern of stress. They are gardeners of machinery, pruning away the first shoots of entropy. They polish away the first blooms of rust not with an abrasive, but by somehow willing the oxidation to reverse.

They do not eat or drink in any conventional sense. They are part of the City’s closed loop. When motionless, they draw energy directly from the ambient electromagnetic field, their bodies a living induction coil. When they need to move, they will sometimes press their palms to a humming conduit, taking a "draught" of pure energy. Their internal fluids are the same pristine, mineral-heavy water that drips from the rock, and if one were ever breached—an unthinkable event—it would bleed pure, cold water that would quickly freeze into a slushy sculpture on the iron grid.

They do not speak. They have no society. They may pass one another on a bridge the length of a country, and there will be no sign of acknowledgement. They are not individuals; they are nodes in a single, distributed consciousness whose only thought is *Maintain*. They are as solitary and as connected as the industrial complexes they tend. One Custodian might be adjusting the alignment of a gear the size of a town square, while another, a continent's breadth away, alters the flow of coolant in response, the two acting in perfect, silent concert, their communication a shared tremor through the very bones of the world.

To you, the anomaly, the error, they are utterly indifferent. You are change, you are decay, you are noise, but you are temporary. You are a dying waveform that will soon fall silent. They will not look at you. They will not react to your presence unless your actions create a dissonance in the Sound. If you were to strike a resonant pylon with a hammer, you would not see the Custodian turn. You would simply feel a change in the air, a shadow falling over you, and find one standing beside you, its featureless head tilted as it assesses the new, ugly note you have introduced into the Last Chorus. And with an unhurried, implacable calm, it would correct the error. It would fix the problem. You are the problem.
