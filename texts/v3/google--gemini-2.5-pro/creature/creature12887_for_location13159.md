To live here is to be a thought in a mind that is not your own.

The beings that inhabit this deep, warm city are not individuals in the way a creature of the sunlit world might understand it. They are expressions of the place itself, currents in the slow, amniotic river of its existence. Their lives are defined not by choice, but by pressure, darkness, and the unerring, silent consensus of biology.

At the deepest level, both literally and metaphysically, are the **Weavers**. To call them creatures is to miss the point. They are more a process, a geological form of life whose timescale renders them invisible to the fleeting lives of their tenants. A Weaver is not a single body one might encounter. It is a distributed network, a vast, slow-thinking colloid. Its "body" is the very nacre-black material of the growing spires, and its "mind" is the faint cyan luminescence that pulses within the walls—the city's autonomic nervous system at work. They do not *build* the city as an act of will; the city's growth is the expression of their life, as inevitable as a mammal's breath. Their perception is not of sight or sound, but of stress, chemistry, and time. They feel the weight of their own architecture, the subtle shift in currents as a new bridge settles, the chemical signature of a million smaller lives dying and settling into the silt. To the other inhabitants, they are not beings but the landscape itself, the unchanging, thrumming bedrock of reality whose heartbeat is the only clock they have ever known.

Above them, in the grand, silent boulevards, are the **Sentients of the Moment**. These are the hunters, the grazers, the mobile fauna. They are the city’s fleeting thoughts.

Consider the hunter, the flat, violet shadow that melts from a hollow in the wall. To be this creature is to live a life of profound stillness punctuated by single, explosive moments of absolute purpose. It does not see in the way of the light-world; its world is a tapestry of pressure-waves and scented trails. It can feel the passage of a shimmer-school a hundred yards away as a subtle disturbance in the solid water, a change in the constant, familiar embrace of the deep. It can taste the copper-tang of fear released by a grazer a minute ago, a lingering ghost in the current. Its consciousness is a state of perpetual, patient listening. It does not think "I am hungry." Instead, a state of *readiness* deepens within it, a tension that coils its chromatophore-laced muscles, its patterns shifting from passive violet to a deep, predatory black that absorbs all light. When it lunges, it is not a decision but a release, an inevitable confluence of pressure, scent, and a biological imperative as old as the spires. Its life is a long, dark meditation broken by flashes of pure, violent being.

Then there are the **Shimmer-Clouds**, the schools of thumbnail-sized creatures that move as one. To be one of them is to have no self. The "I" is a meaningless concept, subsumed entirely into the "We." Their consciousness is a shared, statistical phenomenon. A change in pressure on the school's port side does not register as "something is near me," but as a fractional vector change in the entire cloud's trajectory. Their synchronized flashes of green light are not communication between individuals, but a single, collective exhalation of awareness: a predator's pressure-wake registers, and the cloud *becomes* a spark of warning. They flow through the city's arches like a single, intelligent fluid, their existence a constant, flickering dialogue with the architecture, a living map of the currents and the presence of danger.

Lower still are the **Browsers**. These are the soft-bodied, multi-limbed things that rasp the rust-colored bacterial mats from the city's floors and lower walls. Theirs is a life of texture and taste. Their world is small, immediate, and overwhelmingly sensual. The universe, for them, consists of the subtle differences in mineral content on a patch of wall, the dry scrape of their own mouthparts, and the ever-present, low-grade fear that sends a prickle through their bodies when a shadow passes overhead. They have no concept of the spires above them or the Weavers beneath. They are the city's gardeners and its livestock, existing in a state of placid, continuous consumption, their lives measured in inches of scraped-clean nacre.

Finally, at the base of it all, in the silty foundation where the dead come to rest, live the **Recyclers**. They are pale, blind, and utterly without guile. They are worms, segmented crawlers, and amorphous jellies. Their world is reduced to the purest chemistry. They do not hunt; they simply encounter. Their existence is a burrowing, a dissolving, an incorporation. They are the city's digestive tract, breaking down the complex bodies of hunters and grazers into the base minerals that the geothermal vents will cook and the bacterial mats will consume. They are without awareness as the others might define it, yet they are the most critical part of the loop. They are the guarantee that in this world of absolute darkness, nothing is ever truly lost.

To live here, then, is to accept your place in the body. Whether you are the slow, dreaming bone of the Weaver; the sharp, focused nerve-ending of the hunter; the collective, flowing blood of the shimmer-school; or the mindless, essential gut-flora of the decomposer. You are not a citizen living in a city. You are a cell, and the city is a god that is dreaming you.
