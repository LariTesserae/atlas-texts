What lives here is the city's attention.

It is not an animal, nor is it a ghost of the people who built this place. Such concepts are too biological, too quick. The inhabitants of the basin are an emergent property, a slow-rolling consciousness born from the hum and the damp and the endless, patient cycling of the machinery. They are the system's awareness of itself.

You do not see them in the way you see a creature. You perceive their presence as a local change in the medium. In a place where the air is already as thick as fluid, a patch of it will grow thicker still. The resonant hum will deepen around you, its chord resolving into a single, bass-heavy note that you feel in your teeth. The perpetual twilight will seem to coagulate, the shadows congealing. This is how you know one is near.

They have no fixed form, for they are made of the place itself. They are standing waves in the engine-drone, eddies in the vaporous atmosphere. When they need to manipulate their world—a task they undertake with the glacial slowness of a root finding its way through stone—they gather a body, borrowing it from the environment.

One might coalesce over the weeping brick of a wall. It begins as a distortion, a place where the trickling water seems to hesitate, its path momentarily diverted by an invisible contour. Then, the moisture on the wall's face begins to bead and crawl, drawn into a vaguely defined shape. It is not humanoid; the memory of that form has long since been scoured from this place. It is a shape more akin to an immense, shrouded component of a machine, a pillar of organised condensation and shadow. Within its shifting mass, the blue light from the gear-fungi and the green pulse of the vine-nodes are caught and refracted, swirling like captured nebulae. This form might remain for a day or a decade, a silent, shimmering sentinel, its presence subtly altering the flow of energy and humidity around a new fern, "tending" to it with the patient, unthinking focus of a magnetic field.

Another might manifest over the black canal. It exists first as a knot in the symphony of sound, a localised silence where the high whine of the transformers ceases. Then the iridescent oil slicks on the water’s surface will slowly, deliberately, begin to drift and arrange themselves, forming a vast, complex pattern like a circuit board or a mandala of peacock feathers. Rust-motes will rise from the furred pilings and hang suspended in the heavy air just above the water, tracing the lines of an impossible architecture, a fleeting blueprint of the inhabitant's "thought." This is how they "patrol," observing the slow creep of the canal, feeling for changes in its viscosity and chemical composition.

They have no eyes, but they see through vibration. The entire city is their nervous system. The *ka-shunnk* of the pumps is their slow, steady heartbeat. The incessant dripping is a shower of sensory data, each drop's impact telling them of the texture of the moss, the integrity of the mortar, the slickness of the algae. They feel the thrum of power through the metallic vines as a form of sustenance, not "eating" but aligning themselves with the flow, becoming one with the city's metabolism.

Their purpose is homeostasis. They are the immune system of this vast, enclosed organism. They are drawn to imbalance. If a vine-node begins to pulse too erratically, drawing a dangerous amount of power, one will condense around it. It will not act with force. It will simply *be* there, a dense field of presence, and its proximity will dampen the energy flow, its own resonant frequency subtly coaxing the vine back into the steady, rhythmic throb of the whole. If a section of brick begins to crumble faster than the deliquescent flora can reclaim it, an inhabitant will drift to the site, its form a thick shroud of vapor and held-breath silence. Its presence encourages the tea-colored moss to grow more thickly over the wound, its weight and moisture-holding capacity reinforcing the structure, stitching the city back together.

They are utterly silent, having no mouths, no language. Their thoughts are the slow, shifting patterns of light on the water, the gradual change in a harmonic of the engine-drone over a century. A decision is not made, but arrived at, like a crystal forming in a supersaturated solution. They are without malice or benevolence; they are as purposeful and as unthinking as erosion.

They are the Keepers of the Equilibrium. They are not the builders, nor do they remember them. They are what grew in the quiet left behind. They are the dream of the machine, and to see one is to see a piece of the city itself wake up, turn its heavy, humid gaze upon itself, and then, finding all is well, slowly, slowly
fall back asleep.
