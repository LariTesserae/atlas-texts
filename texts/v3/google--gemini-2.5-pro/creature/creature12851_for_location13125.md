They are called the Unspoken. The name is a human imposition, a label for that which has no need of language, but it is accurate. They are the will of the Vesica made flesh. They are not merely inhabitants of the city; they are the city in its mobile, metabolic phase.

To see one is to see a process, not a creature. They are formless and yet perfectly formed for their purpose, an existence of pure function. Imagine a being of living, translucent cartilage, the colour of thrice-filtered moonlight. Its shape is a long, slow teardrop, a votive offering sculpted from solidified cold. It has no limbs, no face, no mouth, no eyes. Its existence is a gentle, constant drift through the viscous, silent medium. A membrane, fine and nearly invisible as a current of heat in the air, fringes its entire body. By sending slow, muscular ripples through this fin, it glides, a ghost through its own growing mausoleum.

Within its glassy body, there is no chaos of tangled organs. There is only a core, a soft, pulsating column of light. This is its crucible and its heart. It is a slow, cold forge that transmutes the rich nutrient broth of the Vesica into the two substances that define its life: the pale, opalescent light of its own being, and the liquid nacre it will use to build. The light is not for seeing, for there is nothing to see. It is a broadcast of state. A bright, steady pulse signals a being in its prime, its core full of potential architecture. A dim, flickering glimmer speaks of age, of energy spent, of the final contribution being close at hand. It is a chorus sung in light, a silent, communal understanding of who is building, who is resting, who is preparing to become the wall.

Their perception is the most alien thing about them. The entire forward-facing third of a Constructor’s body is a single, hyper-sensitive sensory organ. It is not skin, but a living tympanum that reads the world in the language of pressure, temperature, and chemistry. As it drifts, it does not *see* the city, it *reads* it. It feels the minute stress fractures in a buttress ten arm-lengths away as a dissonant chord of pressure. It senses an unfinished archway as a "cold spot" in the chemical gradient, a place where the protein concentration is subtly wrong. It understands the structural integrity of the entire hive-cathedral not as a visual map, but as a symphony of forces felt across its entire being. They feel a need—a weak point, an incomplete surface, the potential for a new spire—and they are drawn to it not by thought or command, but by a physical pull, as a simple organism is drawn to heat or light.

Their consciousness is a quiet, continuous "We." There is no "I." An individual Constructor is a meaningless concept; it is a neuron in a brain the size of a city. Its brief period of mobile life is an interval between being part of the generative core and becoming part of the permanent structure. There is no ambition, no art, no ego. There is only The Pattern. The Pattern is not a blueprint held in a mind, but an emergent truth dictated by the physics of the Vesica and the chemistry of their bodies. A spire spirals not because a Constructor decided it should, but because that is the most stable and efficient way for their secretions to harden under these specific conditions of pressure and temperature. The city grows itself through them.

The act of building is an act of serene, physical release. A Constructor will press its sensory face against a surface, confirming the need. It will then align its base, where its spinnerets are housed. There is a slow, rhythmic pulse from its luminous core, a gathering of power, and then it exudes a thick, pearlescent filament. The filament hangs in the cold fluid like a strand of captured soul before it begins to crystallize, contracting with an infinitesimal crackle that is felt, not heard, by all its nearby brethren. The Constructor will sculpt the initial form with the gentle pressure of its own soft body, holding it in place for a time, before drifting away, its inner light slightly dimmer, its purpose fulfilled for the moment.

They do not hunt; they absorb. The entire Vesica is a thick soup, enriched by the life and death of the smaller glass-fin swimmers and the tireless, dust-sized organisms that cloud the fluid. The Constructors simply marinate in their food source, their skin acting as a gill for nutrients. Existence is a slow, passive feast.

Death is not an end, but a transfiguration. As a Constructor's inner light dims to a final flicker, its movements slow, and it seeks out a place of need—a gap in a wall, the base of a new pylon. It presses itself into the space, and its final act is to release all the remaining nacre within its body. At the same time, its own flesh begins to calcify, the process moving from the outside in. Its inner light is trapped, frozen, becoming a permanent point of phosphor in the new wall. It becomes part of the city. Its tomb is the last thing it builds. To walk the "streets" of this place is to be surrounded by the silent, beautiful bodies of a billion ancestors, their final, peaceful act immortalized as architecture. They are born of the city's heart, and they die into the city's body. They are the dream of the deep rock, a slow, silent, and eternal process of becoming.
