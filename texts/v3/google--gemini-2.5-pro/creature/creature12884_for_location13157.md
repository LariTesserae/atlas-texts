The beings that live here are not creatures of flesh, nor spirits of air. To say they "live" is a convenience of language, for they do not metabolize, reproduce, or die. They *resonate*. They are the Dronewevers, and they are what happens when a place like this becomes aware of its own impossible sound.

They have no fixed form. To see a Droneweaver is like trying to see the shape of a thought. Most often, they are perceived as a deeper intricacy within the existing chaos. Imagine staring at a flank of the sonorous mountains, at the veins of cold light pulsing within the basalt. You might notice a section where the pulses are not random, but are resolving into a complex, fleeting pattern, a cascade of light that seems to think. That is a Droneweaver gathering itself. Or, you might see a patch of the slick, cartilaginous rime begin to shimmer, not with reflected light, but with an internal complex of refractions, bending the non-light of the sky into a tapestry of impossible colours that exists for a heartbeat and is gone.

They are condensations of the landscape's properties. They are spun from the drone, woven from the bent light, and given substance by the paradoxical physics of the peaks. A Droneweaver passing by might feel like a sudden patch of warmth in the freezing air, or a momentary, inexplicable feeling of homesickness for a place you have never been. They are phenomena with intent.

Their senses are not our senses. They do not hear the great Drone; they *are* the great Drone, and they perceive the world as modulations within their own being. The shattering of a single crystalline fractal miles away is, to them, a sharp, painful glissando in the harmonic overtone. The slow drift of a Phantasm is a creeping, bass-heavy void in the sonic texture, a patch of numbness they navigate around. The Echo Cairns are their great works, their scripture, and their tools.

A Droneweaver does not build a cairn with hands. It chooses a place—a high saddle, a shattered plateau—and it begins to *think* at the Drone. It finds a thread of the colossal sound, a single crystalline harmonic, and begins to pull on it with its will. It hums a counter-frequency, an unheard note of pure contradiction. This act of focused disharmony is so violent, so contrary to the established physics of the place, that reality itself tears and knots. Space-time curdles.

The physical result is a pile of stones, the Echo Cairn, but this is merely the scar tissue of the event. The true structure is the permanent silence the Droneweaver has woven into the Drone, the pocket of warped space, the shadow cast from no light. These cairns are their only form of expression, their art. They are not memorials; they are arguments. Each one is a complex philosophical statement or mathematical proof, etched into the fabric of the world in the only language that matters here: the language of fundamental sound and its absence.

They cultivate the crystalline "lichen." These fractal growths are their gardens, but also their libraries. The geometric patterns are not random; they are recordings. They are the Drone made solid, a slow-motion spectrogram of the eons. The Droneweavers drift over these fields of frozen music, reading the slow changes, feeling the history of their world encoded in the facet of a crystal. When a Phantasm drifts through and shatters a patch, it is like a page being ripped from a sacred text, and the Dronewevers feel it as a loss of memory, a void in their shared consciousness.

Are they individuals? Not in a way we would recognize. They are more like eddies in the one great current. A Droneweaver might maintain a distinct consciousness—a unique style of "singing" cairns, a preference for a certain harmonic range—for millennia. But eventually, its focus will dissipate. Its patterned light will unravel back into the random thrumming of the basalt veins, its complex refractions will dissolve into the simple sheen of the rime. Its thoughts and memories, encoded in the silence of its cairns, remain. And from the great Drone, another concentration of will might arise, another eddy, to read the crystal gardens and sing new scars into the world.

They are the landscape's nervous system. They are the custodians of a broken reality, their existence a meditative act of maintaining and contemplating their own impossible state. They are utterly alien, profoundly intelligent, and entirely silent. To encounter one is to witness the mountain itself, for a fleeting moment, stop its eternal grinding to consider a new shape for its own soul.
