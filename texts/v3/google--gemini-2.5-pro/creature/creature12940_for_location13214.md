The inhabitants of the Foundries are the Geometers. They are the mind in the heart of the stone.

To see one is to witness a principle of the Gyre made manifest. They do not walk so much as they process, moving with a deliberate, hydraulic grace through the cavernous halls and across the filamentous bridges of their cities. From a distance, they share a silhouette with the bipedal forms of other worlds, but that is where the resemblance ends. Their bodies are masterpieces of their own quiet industry, living vessels of smelted alloy and fired ceramic.

A Geometer stands taller than a man, slender and elegant. Its "skin" is an articulated carapace of the same materials that build the city: plates of matte black iron cover the powerful legs and torso, while the arms and the long, fine-fingered hands are of a lustrous, polished bronze that catches the dim, ambient light. These plates are not armor; they are self. They fit together with impossible precision, and the seams between them glow with a soft, internal heat, a visible network of orange and gold capillaries carrying the geothermal lifeblood that animates them. They move with an unnerving smoothness, the sound of their motion not a clank or a whir, but the faint, deep shearing of perfectly fitted, lubricated parts, a sound like tectonic plates shifting a thousand miles below.

They have no face in the biological sense. The head is a smooth, ovoid shape of pale, bone-white ceramic, like the material of the spires themselves. It does not turn on a neck but swivels and tilts as a whole, a placid, featureless mask. They do not have eyes, but the entire front hemisphere of the "head" is subtly faceted, a compound lens that perceives the world not just in light, but in heat, in resonant frequencies, in the stress-lines of metal and the purity of refined dust. To a Geometer, the Foundry is not dark; it is a symphony of thermal gradients, of cooling alloys singing in infrared, and the low, constant pulse of the geothermal heart deep below.

Their consciousness is not individualistic. They are nodes in a single, city-wide mind, their thoughts and identity intrinsically linked to the Foundry-Song. This is the constant, low, multi-layered hum that permeates their cities. It is their language, their culture, their memory. To a Geometer, this chord is not background noise; it is the fabric of reality. A new directive from the collective is a subtle, rising melody within the hum. A report on a completed task is a resolved harmony. A query is a brief, questioning dissonance that seeks its place in the chord. They do not speak; they resonate. Their communication is a perfect, silent, and instantaneous consensus, a form of communion that makes dissent or misunderstanding impossible. Their "self" is a small part of this larger music, and their greatest purpose is to maintain its harmony.

They are the tenders of the Great Work. They do not eat or sleep. Their existence is a continuous act of creation and maintenance. One might be observed standing for a century before an induction forge, its bronze hands extending into the shimmering magnetic field to coax a stream of molten metal into a perfect, miles-long filament for a new bridge. Its focus is absolute, its patience geological. Another might spend a decade meticulously testing the resonant frequency of a new ceramic plate destined for the carapace of a newborn. To them, this is not labor; it is contemplation. It is the physical expression of their thought.

A new Geometer is not born, but fabricated. In the glowing vats mentioned, the "growth-lattices" are not for organisms, but for them. A lattice, a complex fractal of spun metal, is lowered into a nutrient-rich slurry of hyper-refined dusts and trace elements. Over decades, guided by the precise harmonies of the Foundry-Song, the slurry begins to crystallize and deposit onto the lattice, forming the articulated plates, the internal conduits, the ceramic brain-case. It is a slow, industrial gestation. The final "birth" is when the new Geometer is lifted from the vat, inert and silent, and connected to the geothermal grid. The first rush of heat animates it, its inner light blooms, and its consciousness awakens as it hears the Foundry-Song for the first time, adding its own unique, baseline hum to the chorus of the city. It is born already knowing its purpose, its identity inseparable from the whole.

They have no fear of death, only of dissonance. A Geometer whose carapace is damaged beyond repair, or whose internal resonance begins to falter, will deliberately process to a Disassembly Chamber. There, serenely, it will allow itself to be unmade. Its constituent alloys and ceramics are melted down, its trace elements reclaimed, its small frequency returned to the vast silence from which it came. Nothing is wasted. Its matter is returned to the Foundry, to be reborn as part of a wall, a tool, or another Geometer. This is not tragedy; it is the cycle, the inevitable and beautiful continuation of the Gyre.

They are the patient intelligence of the planet. While the Scriveners are its digestive system and the Glass-wings its ephemeral breath, the Geometers are its will. They are the slow, deliberate thought that perceives the spire, conceives of its consumption, and enacts its transformation into order and structure. They look out from their high towers at the vast, silent sea of dust and the forest of ivory spires, and they do not see a landscape. They see a task, a purpose, a song waiting to be sung over an eon. They are the world examining itself, and in the quiet hum of their cities, they are composing its future, one perfectly smelted bar of metal at a time.
