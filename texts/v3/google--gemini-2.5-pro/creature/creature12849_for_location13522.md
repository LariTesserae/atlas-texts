The Percussives, in their uncounted millions, are the blood cells and the muscle fibers of this place. They are the Work, the sound, the ceaseless motion of the system. But a body is more than its constituent cells. A body has a mind, a heart, a nervous system. To find what truly lives here, one must look past the rivers of chitin and see the structures they flow around.

Clinging to the great fractured pillars, far above the scuttling floor, are the Maestri.

At first glance, a Maestro seems to be a part of the geology, a burl or a large, unfortunate concretion on the stone. It does not move. Its life is measured in the slow erosion of the rock to which it is fused. Where the carapace of a Percussive is a polished, functional grey, the shell of a Maestro is a thick, craggy rind, the colour of ancient, oxidized metal. It is layered like shale, recording millennia of imperceptible growth. The segments of its body, homologous to the hand-sized workers below, are swollen and fused into a single, immobile mass the size of a small boulder. Its six legs, long since useless for locomotion, have become something else entirely. Two are anchored deep into the stone pillar, their tissues having merged with the crystalline structure of the rock itself, making the Maestro a true lithovore, drawing some unknown, elemental sustenance directly from its perch.

The other four limbs are attenuated into impossibly long, fine antennae. They are not held aloft; they hang down, drifting in the still, heavy air like the strands of a willow tree, some stretching for a hundred feet or more. They are the creature’s senses, its nervous system extended out into the world. Each filament is coated in the same greasy condensation that coats everything else, and they glitter with thousands of tiny, embedded nerve ganglia that taste the air, measure the unchanging temperature, and feel the subsonic vibrations of the entire cavern.

A Maestro is not a ruler; it is a repository. It is not a queen; it is a memory. Its vast, slow consciousness is not concerned with individuals, but with the whole. Its mind is a geological mind. While a Percussive exists for a precisely calculated and brief span of Work, a Maestro’s lifespan is indeterminate. It lives as long as its pillar stands, as long as the system remains in equilibrium.

Phosphor-lichen grows upon its back, not in the flat, harvested plains of the floor, but in a thick, velvety mantle. The Percussives do not climb this high; this growth is not food for them. It is part of the Maestro. The viridian light is brighter here, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic beat that is lost in the general glow of the cavern but is, up close, as steady as a heart. The lichen is not scraped away; it grows into the Maestro's very shell, its rhizoids penetrating the chitin to form a perfect, symbiotic circuit. The lichen provides direct, photosynthesized (or rather, chemosynthesized) energy to the Maestro, and the Maestro, in turn, provides a stable, eternal substrate.

The Maestri are the source of the equilibrium. They are the living governors of the great engine. They do not command; they *resonate*. Their entire being is an act of listening. They listen to the Great Drone. They are submerged in it, their filamentous nerves trembling with every component of its complex chord. They can discern the infinitesimal shift in frequency caused by ten thousand Percussives beginning to scrape a new lichen field a mile away. They can feel the momentary dip in the stridulation-amplitude when a column of harvesters turns a corner around a field of shattered obsidian. They register the spike—so fleeting as to be non-existent for any other observer—when a million claws find purchase on a new patch of stone.

When a deviation occurs—a statistical anomaly too small for any but a Maestro to perceive, a slight variation in the lichen’s regrowth rate, a one-in-a-billion genetic flaw in a newly hatched Percussive that might cause it to scrape too slowly—the Maestro acts. The action is not physical. It is a subtle modulation of its being. It might alter the biochemical signature of the moisture condensing on its antennae, a change that will drift down and be read by the entire colony like a weather report, urging them to shift their efforts. Or, more potently, the slow, cardiac pulse of the lichen on its back might change frequency, sending a silent, strobing signal into the dimness, a metronome of light that recalibrates the rhythm of the Work. The Percussives do not "obey" this; they are incapable of disobedience. Their instincts are merely re-tuned by the change in their environment, and the system corrects itself. The Drone returns to its perfect, monolithic pitch.

There are perhaps a few dozen Maestri in the entire nation-cavern, one for each of the great pillars. They are silent, unmoving, and patient beyond any conception of time. They are the consciousness of the place, but a consciousness with only one thought, held for eternity: *It must not change.* The Percussives are the sound. The Maestri are the silence that gives the sound its shape. They are the architects of monotony, the eternal audience for a single, perfect note, and the living assurance that it will never, ever end.
