The life of Bas-en-Haut is as strange and patient as the city itself. It is not an infestation upon the stone; it is the stone's own nervous system, its digestive tract, and its fleeting thoughts made manifest. To speak of what "lives there" is to attempt to separate the dancer from the dance.

At the heart of it all are the beings from which the city precipitates: the **Architects**, though they have no name for themselves. They are the Weft, the living substrate. An individual Architect does not exist. They are a single, continent-spanning entity of distributed consciousness, a mind so slow and vast that a single thought might be a geological epoch. Their physical substance, glimpsed only when a water-carved chasm exposes the city's living marrow, is a slow, cool plasma of living matter. It resembles a non-Newtonian fluid, a gelid, opalescent grey veined with currents of pearlescent light—the pathways of their glacial senses. It is not flesh, but a living mineral slurry, a network of nerves and glands whose function is to feel pressure, temperature, humidity, and the subtle resonance of the world-structure they inhabit.

Their experience of being is not thought, but feeling. They feel the weight of a new tower settling over a millennium. They feel the subtle, tickling vibration of a single water droplet striking a resonant chamber a mile below. They feel the shift in tenor as the wind hums through a newly-grown colonnade. When the balance of water and mineral is right, or when stress in one part of the megastructure sends a slow-moving pressure wave of 'intent' through their collective body, they act. An area of their exposed surface, or a feeler extruded from a pore, will begin to draw ions from the mist. The process is audible only to the most patient listener: a faint, dry clicking, like a field of static electricity discharging. This is the sound of their bones setting in the open air, the sound of the city growing a new limb. A creature who stumbled upon an exposed Weft would feel no aggression, only a profound, tidal curiosity as a pseudopod of thickened syrup might ghost towards them, seeking to taste their chemistry and feel their warmth before slowly, over the course of a day, calcifying around them, incorporating their atoms into the eternal architecture.

Upon this foundation, a sparse and specialized ecosystem exists. The most visible, if one can call them that, are the **Glass-wing Moths**, or the Drifters. They are ephemera. Their larvae, tiny, translucent grubs, feed on the carpets of Weeping Velvet moss that grow on the undersides of arches, converting the mineral-rich water into the first rung of complex organic matter. When they pupate, they secrete a silken cocoon that glues itself into the pores of the biocement, absorbing minerals to harden it into a teardrop-shaped casing almost indistinguishable from the stone. Upon hatching, the adult moth is a creature of breathtaking fragility and simplicity. Its wings, the size of a human hand, are perfectly clear, refracting the diffused grey light into subtle, shifting rainbows at their edges. They are silent, navigating the canyons on air currents, more like motes of crystalline dust than living beings. They are the city's pollinators, but they do not traffic in pollen. They are drawn to the faint warmth and mineral tang of a newly-formed structure, the still-soft bone of an Architect's extrusion. They alight and unfurl a proboscis as fine as a hair to sip the mineral-rich ooze. In doing so, they transfer enzymes and microorganisms from older sections of the city to the new, acting as catalysts for the lichens and mosses that will eventually colonize the new growth. They are the city's way of tending its own skin.

Hunting the Drifters is the apex predator of Bas-en-Haut, the **Chime-Hunter**. It is a creature of absolute stillness. Radially symmetrical, like a stone-colored starfish with six or eight broad, flat limbs, its body is a disc of mottled grey biocement, perfectly mimicking the texture and colour of the lichen-covered walls it clings to. It can remain motionless for years, its metabolism slowed to a state indistinguishable from the rock it inhabits. Its senses are not visual; its entire upper surface is a tympanic membrane, sensitive to the most minute vibrations. It does not hear the wind or the water, tuning them out as background noise. It listens for the specific, delicate frequency of a Drifter's feet touching down on the stone nearby. When this happens, its reaction is one of the few instances of speed on the entire plateau. From the center of its body, a cluster of a dozen needle-thin, glassy filaments, each a foot long, erupts outward. They move so fast they are a blur, and their collision with the air and each other produces a single, sharp, discordant *chime* that is instantly swallowed by the immense quiet. More often than not, one of these needles will impale the hapless Drifter. The Chime-Hunter then slowly retracts the filament, drawing its meal to its hidden mouth, a process that can take several hours. Finding the shed, hollow filaments of a Chime-Hunter on the ground is to find the only evidence of violence in this solemn world.

At the very bottom, in the shallow basins where mist pools and water stills, live the **Basin Skimmers**. These tiny, multi-legged arthropods, the size of a fingernail, are a shimmering, quicksilver presence against the dull grey. They are the decomposers. They skate across the surface tension of the pools, their bristled legs sweeping up mats of single-celled algae and mineral dust. They are the clean-up crew, the gut bacteria of the city's water-channels. They live, breed, and die in their pools, their tiny mineral-rich carapaces sinking to the bottom to form a fine, silty layer that, over millennia, will be compressed back into a new form of stone.

To live here is to be an organ of the city. The Weft is its flesh and bone. The Skimmers are its gut. The Drifters are its circulatory system, moving vital elements from one place to another. The Chime-Hunters are its immune response, a rare and sudden fever. None of them possess intelligence in a way a human would recognize. They do not have names, or histories, or ambition. They are functions. They are the quiet, layered symphony of water given form, the hum of the wind made flesh, the slow, ponderous life of a city that was born of the rain and thinks in stone.
