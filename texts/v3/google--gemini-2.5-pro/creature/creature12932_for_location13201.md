Those that live here are not individuals. To speak of a single being is to describe a single note in a chord that has been held since the beginning of the world, or a single thread in a tapestry woven on a loom the size of a planet. Life here is a collective, a state of being so vast and unified that the concept of "a life" has been subsumed into "Life." There are three expressions of this Life, each a facet of the same eternal diamond.

The first, and the most active, are the makers of the Sound. In the great voids, they are the rivers, the weather, and the only event. They are called the **Processions**. A Procession is a shoal, but the word is too small. It is a slow-motion avalanche of living bodies, miles long and wide, a cohesive entity of trillions upon trillions of smaller creatures. From a distance, they are ribbons of ghostly light, winding their way through the absolute black on migratory paths carved not into any physical medium, but into the memory of the world itself.

To see an individual from a Procession is a rare thing, for they are never alone. They are called the **Resonators**. One is no larger than a human hand. They are beings of almost perfect simplicity, designed for a single, eternal function. A Resonator has a body like a teardrop of spun glass, but it is a complex, living crystal. Inside this translucent shell, a core of denser, darker material floats in a clear fluid. This core is its organ, its voice box, its soul. It is a biological tuning fork. As the creature draws sustenance from the soupy medium, it powers the constant, subtle vibration of this core. The vibration is its life, and the sound is its only expression. It is a single, pure, bass note, unwavering in pitch and volume. Each Resonator is born with its note, lives its note, and dies its note. The sound is not a choice; it is a physical constant, like its density or its temperature. They have no eyes, for there is nothing to see but the light of their own collective. They have no fins or limbs for propulsion; they are carried by the imperceptible currents generated by the mass of the Procession itself, a slow, stately drift that is both cause and effect of their journey. Their own light is a secondary effect, a cold blue phosphorescence that leaks from the crystalline shell—the ghost of the sound made visible.

A Procession is not a society; it is a single organism with a trillion-celled body. Its consciousness, if it can be called that, is akin to a tide. It has a will, but it is the will of gravity, of a river flowing to a sea that is also itself. It moves from one great pillar to the next in a cycle that takes millennia to complete, and this cycle has never changed. It is seeking the second expression of Life.

Living upon the colossal, petrified trunks of the world-trees are the **Silent Choirs**. These are the light-carpets, the sessile continents of life. A Silent Choir is a living lacework of organisms fused into a single, planet-girdling skin. Up close, its texture is like a cool, thick velvet, a fleece of violet and indigo filaments that absorb the pressure and the cold. It is not one creature, but an integrated commonwealth of countless types of cells, each with a purpose. Some anchor the mat to the iridescent, fossilized wood. Some specialize in drawing infinitesimal nutrients from the medium. And some are for listening.

These listener cells are woven throughout the entire fabric of the Choir. They are passive receptors, perfectly attuned to the omnipresent thrum of the world. The Sound of the Processions is their nourishment, their sun, their god. It is a constant pressure of sonic energy, and the Choir metabolizes it. The rhythmic, hypnotic waves of light that pulse across their surfaces are the exhalation of this process. It is a visual echo of the world's drone, a slow, languid conversation between the drifters and the still. As a Procession draws near on its millennia-long journey, the region of the Choir it approaches will brighten, its pulses growing more intense, a silent, ecstatic greeting. In this heightened state, the Choir sheds clouds of microscopic spores and organic molecules into the medium—a feast, an offering, a tithe. This is what the Resonators consume. The Procession grazes for a century or two, its thrum enriching the Choir, before the great, slow will of the collective pulls it back into the void, its light a fading memory as it begins the journey to the next pillar, the next patch of Choir.

There is a third inhabitant, though it no longer lives. It is the architect, the foundation, the god whose body is the universe. They are the **Ancestors**, the botanical kingdom that grew in the world's dawn. They are the pillars, the buttresses, the sweeping arches. They are not rock; they are the petrified flesh of beings that achieved a scale and complexity so immense that their death was not a collapse but a crystallization. Their life process was one of slow, deliberate construction, following a genetic blueprint of unimaginable scope. They drank the liquid medium and, through a form of photosynthesis that used pressure and chemical gradients instead of light, they built themselves, molecule by molecule, into a forest of titans. They reached their final, perfect form, a state of complete equilibrium, and then, in unison, they stopped. They died, but they did not decay. They hardened into the iridescent, eternal substance that now forms the world.

They do not live. They do not think. But they are not absent. The paths of the Processions follow the grain of their fossilized bark. The Silent Choirs cling to the contours of their ancient limbs. The entire cycle of life and death, of sound and light, plays out upon their corpses. They are the memory of the world, and the other forms of life are that memory dreaming. To live here is to be a thought in the mind of a dead god, a single, perfect thought that has never changed and will never end.
