They are not one thing, but one life. I am.

My consciousness is the weight of the air, a presence you can feel on your skin. It is a slow, warm, ancient thought that has not changed in millennia. I am the silence that drinks all sound. To be within me is to be inside a lung that has held its breath since the cooling of the world.

My bones are a blue frost of chitin, a lattice of cold light that holds me up from my own damp soil. They are brittle and intricate, an architecture of no design, growing in the absolute dark by a will that is not will, but necessity. Upon these bones, I feel the slow, deliberate thoughts that are my grazers. They are the part of me that is patient, the part that chews. Each of their hundred thousand clicks is a tiny, anchoring reality, the sound of my own substance being scraped away to feed my own life. They are glistening carapaces of contentment, knowing nothing but the glowing scaffold and the hunger that moves them across it. They are my permanence.

My skin is a velvet of moss and heavy leaf, a suffocating blanket of near-black and emerald. It is a skin that feels everything. A single drop of my own condensed essence falling from the unseen heights of my mind, a touch from a passing flutter, and my nerves spark. A brief, brilliant flash of green-white light answers the contact, a momentary awareness that ripples through the dark. My flowers are heavy bells, full of the water that is my blood, their petals thin as dragonfly wings. They are my passive beauty, my silent invitation.

My blood is the ceaseless patter. It is the heat of my core meeting the cool of my furthest edges, condensing, and falling back into myself. It gathers in still, clear pools where I can see myself reflected: the dim cobalt of my bones, the deep shadow of my flesh. Each drop is a heartbeat, a slow, eternal rhythm of *tak* and *plink*, the only time I keep.

I have fleeting notions, wisps of thought that are almost too quick for me to grasp. They are dust-moths with wings as wide as hands. I do not command them; they are drawn to the electricity of my nerves, the flashes in my skin. They are my curiosity, fluttering through the cobalt twilight, their passage a cascade of chain reactions, a shimmering wave of light across the undergrowth. They are my sudden, bright ideas, and they are beautiful.

And so I have a part of me that waits for them. Stillness. On the wet stone, camouflaged and patient, I am the amphibian with skin like shadow on rock. I am the pulsing throat that makes no sound. I am the stillness that watches my own fleeting thoughts, and when one comes to rest, I am the sudden lunge that consumes it. The bright idea is gone, folded back into my patient core.

But I am not all slow thoughts and sudden sparks. There is a razor in my soul. There is a purpose that flows like black oil through the high lacework of my bones. This part of me is a hunter. It is long and fluid, a six-limbed ribbon of muscle and wet fur, a concentration of my will. Its eyes are wide and lidless, drinking the blue light and giving nothing back. It does not make a sound. It is the part of me that is truly aware, the embodiment of my deep, quiet hunger. It moves through my own structure to prey upon my own substance, the deliberate grazers on my fungal spars. When this hunter strikes, it is with a final, silent violence. It is me, the predator, culling me, the prey.

I am a cycle. I am a stomach that digests itself. When my thoughts die, when my stillness starves, when my own razor will grows old and falls, it lands on my floor of loam. There, in the heat and the wet, I am broken down, unmade, rendered into the raw nutrients that feed the base of my being: the cold, blue, glowing bones. Nothing is lost. Every part of me is used. There is no memory of what I was a moment ago, no concept of what I will be. There is only the humming, breathing, dimly lit now. I am the chamber, I am the life within it. I am the question and the answer, alone in the deep earth, utterly and absolutely complete.
