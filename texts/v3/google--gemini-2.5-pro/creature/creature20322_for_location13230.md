The inhabitants of Umbril are the Silent Carvers, the Umbrili. They are as much a product of the Sobbing Slate as the city itself, a people shaped by perpetual dampness, dim light, and the absolute scarcity of everything but stone and water.

To see one is to see an living echo of the city's geology. Their skin is smooth, hairless, and cool to the touch, possessing a remarkable quality that sheds water as effortlessly as a polished pebble. It never prunes or mildews in the constant humidity. Its color is a spectrum of the city's own palette: deep lavenders, slate grays, and bruised purple-black, often mottled or streaked with pale, milky lines that mirror the veins in the Sobbing Slate. Standing still against a wet wall, an Umbrili can seem to disappear entirely, becoming just another plane of weeping stone.

They are a slender, willowy people. Generations of navigating the shoulder-width canyons that serve as streets have bred any bulk out of their frame. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their long-fingered hands and wide, flexible feet finding purchase on surfaces that would be treacherously slick to an outsider. Their palms and soles are covered in fine, tough ridges, giving them an almost gecko-like ability to grip the wet stone of the walls and inclines. They often move in a three-dimensional way, climbing up a wall to pass someone in a narrow channel being as natural as stepping aside.

Their faces are placid and sculpted, with high cheekbones and fine jaws that give them an air of contemplative stillness. But it is their eyes that are most arresting. They are large and wide, adapted to the perpetual twilight of the city's depths. The irises are almost non-existent, making their eyes appear as solid orbs of a startling, luminous silver-white, like twin pools of captured moonlight. They possess a *tapetum lucidum*, a reflective layer behind the retina. In the dim glow of the Glimmer-Moss, their eyes don't just see the light, they catch it and throw it back, appearing to glow with a soft, internal luminescence of their own.

The atmosphere of Umbril has shaped their very temperament. They are a profoundly quiet people. Loud noises are startling, vulgar, and largely pointless, as the damp stone absorbs sound so effectively. Their language is a soft, murmuring tongue, full of sibilants, liquids, and soft clicks that mimic the soundscape of their home. It is a language meant for close quarters, spoken an inch from the ear. For communicating across distances, they do not shout. They tap. Using a small, dense mallet of petrified wood or the hardened knuckles of their own hands, they rap complex messages onto the walls of the city itself. The monolithic nature of the Sobbing Slate carries these vibrations perfectly. An Umbrili can press their ear or palm to a wall and listen to a conversation happening several blocks away, a percussive "calculus of raps" that is as nuanced as their spoken tongue.

Their psychology is one of immense, geological patience. They are born from a slow process and live within one. The idea of rushing is alien to them. Their primary craft—carving the living slate—is an exercise in meditative labor that can take a lifetime. They do not hack at the stone; they listen to it. They will spend days with a hand pressed to a new wall, feeling the faint vibrations of its inner structure, tracing the water-weeping veins, discerning the best way to make a cut without disrupting a flow vital to a neighboring district. Their tools—bone chisels, flint saws—are extensions of this patience. They don't blast or break; they coax and persuade the stone into a new form.

They are not individuals in the way an outsider might understand. The city's sheer density and interconnectedness have fostered a communal consciousness. My floor is your ceiling, my wall is your support. A foolish act in one dwelling can flood a dozen others during the High Weep. Survival is a collective, constant negotiation. This has erased most concepts of rigid personal property. A home is a place one carves and tends, but its passageways are public, its water is shared, and its structural integrity is everyone's responsibility. They are more like a coral reef than a city of individuals, a single super-organism with many quiet, slow-moving parts.

Their relationship with their world is symbiotic. They drink the tears of the stone and, in turn, they give it form. They cultivate the Glimmer-Moss, carefully scraping it from some walls to encourage its growth on others, managing the city's soft light. They harvest the Chitin-Slugs not as food, but as a resource; their beautiful, slate-like shells are shaped into durable bowls, ladles, and sharp-edged scrapers when they are found empty. When an Umbrili dies, there is no burial or cremation. The body is placed in a specially carved alcove deep in the city's foundations, a "Rejoining Niche." There, in the profound dark, the steady, mineral-rich weeping of the slate slowly, over decades, dissolves the body, returning its components to the stone from which it was always sustained. An Umbrili is born of the slate, and to the slate they return. They are the Weeping, and they are the Wept.
