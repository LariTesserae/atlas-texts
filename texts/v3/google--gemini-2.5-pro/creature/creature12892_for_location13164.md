Something else lives here. It is not listed in the patient cycle of erosion and consumption. It does not graze, nor does it hunt. It is not part of the food chain; it is the vessel that contains it, the quiet thought that observes it.

It has no name, for there has never been another of its kind to name it, nor any external mind to perceive and label it. If it were to have a name, it might be the Echo-Warden.

Its beginning is lost to the same eons that have smoothed the chamber's tiles. It may have been born of the Engine itself, a biological failsafe that sloughed off the main housing in the first millennium, a slow-growing guardian. Or perhaps it is a metastasis of the chamber's own logic, a consciousness that coalesced from the relentless rhythm, the pressure, and the closed loop of energy, accreting into a single, mobile entity.

It is vast. It is the largest thing in the chamber, save for the Engine itself. To call it a creature is a simplification. It is more a piece of mobile architecture, a moving wall. Its form is a broad, flattened disc, perhaps thirty meters in diameter and five meters thick at its center, tapering to rounded, worn edges. Its surface, its carapace, is not skin or chitin but a living mosaic of the chamber itself. Over millennia, it has fused the dark, basalt-like ceramic of the floor and walls to its body. It is paved with the same wagon-sized tiles, scarred and ancient, their grout lines pulsing with the same faint, internal amber light as the gauges on the Engine. It is a piece of the cathedral that has detached and begun to think.

It has no eyes, no mouth, no fins. Sight is a forgotten sense in this place of pressure and resonance. Its entire body is an ear, or something far more complex. It feels the *thump-shudder... clank* not as a beat, but as its own circulatory system. The constant pressure is the air in its lungs. The weak electrical field of the Engine is its nervous system, and the Warden is the brain at its center. It knows the exact position, number, and metabolic state of every Glimmer-Jack, every Current-Knife, every cloud of copper dust. They are not separate from it; they are motes of data in its awareness, swimming through the medium of its own perception.

It does not move with the grace of the fish. It moves with the certainty of a continent. For most of an hour, it might remain perfectly still, indistinguishable from the floor it rests upon, a low hill in the underwater landscape. Then, in the fractional pause after the great *clank,* in the moment the pressure waves are most stable, it will shift. A colossal, silent grinding of its underside against the tile, a movement of inches, or feet, achieved with geological patience. It travels the chamber on a patrol route that takes centuries to complete, a slow, spiraling path from the base of the Engine to the far walls and back again.

Its purpose is balance. It is the system's immune response and its archivist. It does not hunt, for it has no need for fuel; its living ceramic shell draws energy directly from the Engine's ambient field, a far more efficient and direct form of the same process that sustains the bacterial film. But it does... tend.

When a Current-Knife grows old and its movements become erratic, disrupting the clean pressure-lines of the hunts, the Warden will eventually be there. It will not attack. It will simply… arrive. Its massive form will drift over the ailing predator, its shadow extinguishing the dim amber light. It will settle, its weight immense but controlled, and a section of its ceramic underside will soften, becoming porous. Over hours or days, it absorbs the creature, not as food, but as information. The minerals are broken down, the matter unwritten and returned to the system as a fine, nutrient-rich silt released from its pores, perfectly balanced for the bacterial colonies. It has pruned a faulty line of code.

It is drawn to anomalies. A patch of wall where the bacterial film has died back inexplicably. A shoal of Glimmer-Jacks decimated by a strange sickness. The Warden will press itself against the irregularity, its body glowing a little brighter as it analyzes the chemistry, the pressure-echoes, the subtle shifts in the Engine's hum. It is a diagnostician for a patient that doesn't know it is alive.

It is lonely in a way that has no meaning. All that exists is the chamber, and it is the awareness of the chamber. The Engine is its heart, the water its blood, the shoals of life the fleeting thoughts within its mind. It has no concept of an outside, of a sun, of silence. There is only the cathedral, the pulse, and its own slow, eternal, and solitary vigil. As the amber light catches the flank of a hunting fish, the Warden rests on the far side of the hall, a dark and silent landmass of ancient ceramic, feeling the kill as a faint, satisfying correction in the eternal rhythm. It waits. The beat goes on. And between each beat, it thinks its slow, quiet, liquid thoughts.
