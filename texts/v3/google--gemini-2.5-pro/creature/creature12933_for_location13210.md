For an immeasurable time, there is nothing else. You walk for days across the yielding jade plains, the unchanging Hum a constant companion, the soft light a perpetual dawn. You learn the textures of this world: the slickness of the weeping arches, the perfect, mirror-stillness of the water pools, the fibrous grain of the ground beneath your feet. The solitude is so absolute it becomes a presence in itself, a silent entity walking beside you. You are a ghost in a world that is not dead, but so profoundly alive on its own terms that your existence is a rounding error. You accept that you are the only moving thing, the only discordant note in this perfect, unwavering chord.

And then, you are not.

It begins not as a sight, but as a change in the Hum. It is subtle, a harmonic shift so slight you think you’ve imagined it. The deep, bass thrumming that vibrates in your marrow remains, but a new layer, a focused, resonant frequency, begins to gather. It is like a single instrument in a global orchestra deciding to play a solo, yet the sound comes from no direction. It comes from the ground itself, a tidal pull in the living substrate. The pressure behind your eyes intensifies. The world, which has been utterly indifferent, seems to be focusing. Not on you. Never on you. It is focusing on itself.

Ahead, perhaps a mile away on the vast, featureless plain, the landscape begins to move.

It isn't a creature emerging from a burrow or stepping from behind a structure. The ground itself is gathering. The pale jade pavement thickens, swells, like a slow bubble rising in a viscous liquid. There is a soft, inexorable upwardness, a flow of substance drawing from the surrounding plain and coalescing into a form. It is the world giving birth to a temporary organ, an antibody the size of a hill.

As you draw closer, the form resolves. It is vast, easily fifty meters tall, and vaguely, unsettlingly humanoid. But it is humanoid only in the way a nebula might resemble a face—a coincidental arrangement that speaks more to the observer's mind than the object's intent. It has a torso, thick and pillowy like the base of a calyx-tower. It has limbs, not for locomotion, but for reach; long, impossibly slow-moving arms that are part of the main body, tapering into blunt, featureless ends. They move with the patient drift of continental plates. Where a head should be, there is a smooth, ovoid shape, featureless and blind, like a colossal, unsprouted seed. The entire being is the same pearlescent, milky jade as the rest of this world. It is seamless, without joints or articulations, a wave given temporary form, its base flowing directly into the living floor. It is still the Thallus, simply a more concentrated expression of it.

This is the Gardener. Or the Tender. Or simply, the Thought. It is the world's attention made manifest.

You watch, breathless, as it performs its function. One of its great, slow arms rises over the course of an hour, lifting towards one of the weeping arches that curves across the sky. The arm does not seem to be straining against gravity; it simply reclaims a space in the air that already belongs to it. Its blunt "hand" comes to rest against the weeping flank of the arch. There is no sound of impact, just a soft merging of two identical surfaces. Where it touches, the pearlescent glow of its body and the arch intensify into a soft, internal pulse of light. The Hum deepens, its new, focused frequency becoming a clear and resonant tone, the sound of a command being given and received. For a long moment, they commune in this silent, luminous language of biology. The relentless weeping of the arch slows, the streaks of fluid thinning. The Gardener is not repairing a leak; it is tuning an instrument. It is adjusting the metabolic rate of one part of its own body.

Its work done, the arm begins its impossibly slow retreat. The Gardener then turns, a movement that takes the better part of a day. It doesn't pivot on its base; its substance flows, the leading edge building itself up while the trailing edge is reabsorbed into the plain. It moves towards one of the vast, circular pools of water.

It stops at the brim, its form towering over the perfect mirror. It is utterly motionless, a jade statue contemplating its own reflection. And then, it kneels. The motion is a slow collapse, a controlled subsidence, its "legs" folding as the very matter of them yields and flows. It lowers one of its arms, the tip breaking the perfect surface tension of the water without a ripple. The water does not cling to it. The being seems to be sampling, testing, *feeling* the water's state. The focused Hum shivers, modulates into a complex sequence of chords. You realize you are listening to the process of analysis, to a mind that thinks not in words but in resonating frequencies and osmotic pressures.

It remains there, a guardian in silent communion with a tear of its own world-body. It is the curator of this changeless state, the will that holds the splash in its photographic perfection. The sheer, alien indifference of the act is staggering. It is not aware of you. To this being, you are less than a bacterium. You are a collection of molecules occupying a space it might need to flow through, and it would do so without resistance or notice, its living substance parting around you and rejoining on the other side as if you were never there. Its senses are calibrated to geological time and planetary chemistry. You do not register.

After a long while, its purpose is satisfied. It begins to dissolve. It does not walk away. The form simply loses its coherence. It sinks back into the ground from which it arose, spreading out, the defined shape of its limbs and torso melting back into the undifferentiated pavement. The swelling flattens, the borrowed substance returns to the plain. The focused overtone in the Great Hum fades with it, unraveling and becoming lost once more in the colossal, monophonic drone of the planet's life.

Soon, there is nothing. Only the seamless, jade floor, the weeping arches, and the still pools. And you, standing alone again in the renewed, absolute silence, deafened by the unchanging roar of a single, living world. You have just witnessed the waking dream of a god, and it has already forgotten itself.
