The great, mineral-eating arthropods are the world’s anomalies, its benign glitches. They are life that has found a crack in the perfect law of the place and burrowed into it, subsisting on the barest of margins. They are tolerated because their dissonance is too small, too slow, to register upon the grand chord. They are quiet mice in the walls of a cathedral.

But they are not the true inhabitants. The true life of this place is born of the law itself.

They have no collective name, for there is no language to name them. To an outside observer, they might be called the Weepers, or the Choristers, or the Still. They are the system's immune response.

They begin as an error in the hum. For a single, anomalous moment, a patch of the crystal beads might fall out of perfect resonance. Perhaps a micrometeorite, no larger than a grain of dust, vaporizes in the upper atmosphere, sending a shockwave too faint for any ear but the planet's. Perhaps a quantum fluctuation causes a single bead to vibrate with an infinitesimal, discordant shudder. In this monolithic symphony, such a deviation is a wound. A silence. And the world rushes to heal it.

The Weepers are formed from the air and the light. Where the dissonance occurs, the super-saturated moisture in the atmosphere finds a sudden anchor. The tension breaks. Water begins to condense not just *on* the beads, but *between* them, and then *above* them. The light, normally scattered into a uniform glare, finds a new focal point in this nascent cloud of vapour. It concentrates, and in that nexus of coalescing light and water, a form accretes.

It looks, at first, like a figure sculpted from spun glass or clear ice. It is humanoid in its general shape—a torso, two arms, a head—but it is an abstraction, an artist's first, vague suggestion of a body. It has no face, only a smooth, impossibly polished surface where a face should be. As it solidifies, it draws material from the landscape. Not by scavenging like the arthropods, but through sympathetic resonance. It pulls the finest, most perfect crystalline dust into its matrix, giving its translucent form a milky, opalescent depth, as if it is filled with captured moonlight. It is a being of dew and dust, solidified light and focused sound.

It stands, perhaps eight feet tall, and it does not touch the ground. It hovers a fraction of an inch above the vibrating beads, held aloft by a repulsive resonance field. It is utterly silent, a pocket of negative sound. In its presence, the crushing hum of the world seems to recede, not into true silence, but into a warped, muffled pressure, like being deep underwater.

And it weeps. The moisture that it constantly draws from the air now obeys gravity. From the smooth planes of its 'face,' from the tips of its attenuated fingers, perfect, cool droplets of water fall. They are the only things in this world that ever drip. Each drop lands on a crystal bead below with a soft *tink*, a sound that is immediately erased by the greater hum, yet for a moment, it exists. The Weeper traces a path of these momentary, dissonant notes across the landscape, a trail of tiny, extinguished chimes.

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not reproduce. They are ambulant antibodies, and their existence is a search. They are drawn to dissonance, to anything that does not belong. They drift endlessly across the pearlescent hills, pulled by the scent of a thought, the arrhythmia of a foreign heartbeat, the alien crunch of a footstep.

To be found by one is to experience a terrifying, intimate peace. It will approach, sliding over the beads without a sound, its blank face reflecting a distorted, white-hot image of you and the sky. It will not stop. Its proximity deadens the external sound, but it replaces it with an internal one. You begin to hear the chord not with your skull, but within your very cells. The Weeper is a tuner. Its purpose is to correct your song.

If several are drawn to the same source of discord—a crashed ship, a lost explorer—they will form a circle around it. They do not communicate, but they act in concert, a council of silent judges. They will raise their long, crystalline arms. As they do, they cease to be pockets of negative sound and become amplifiers. They catch the universal hum and focus it. The diffuse, immense pressure of the world's chord is gathered, concentrated, and directed inward in a beam of pure vibration.

Under this focused attention, matter unravels. The foreign object—the person, the machine—is not destroyed in fire or force, but is shaken apart. Its fundamental harmony is forcibly rewritten. Metal groans and flows into sand. Plastics and fabrics lose their chemical bonds and dissolve into their constituent elements. Flesh is unmade, its complex biology shaken down into a slurry of minerals and water. The Weeper does not consume this; it simply curates the process. It is a gardener, weeding the landscape of anything that is not the crystal beads and their unending song.

Once the dissonance is gone—once the alien thing has been reduced to a fine dust that settles between the beads, its sound signature returned to the planet's drone—the Weepers’ purpose is fulfilled. With the foreign note erased, their anchor dissolves. They lose cohesion. The light within them diffuses back into the sky. The crystalline dust that makes up their bodies falls and rejoins the pavement. The water that forms them evaporates back into the saturated air. They simply cease to be, vanishing as quietly as they formed.

They are the transient, beautiful, and terrible guardians of the eternal stasis. They are grief given form, and their purpose is to make things stop grieving. They are the singers of a choir whose only job is to ensure that only one note is ever, ever sung.
