What lives here is the reason the city is as it is. They are not its inhabitants; they are its state of being. They are not creatures that evolved to survive the sound; they *are* the sound, given focus and intent. They are the sentries of the equilibrium.

To call them "beings" is to use a word that is too solid, too discrete. They are moments of attention in a place that has none. They are the answer to the question posed by your anomalous presence. You, the discordant note, are the prayer that summons them into shape.

At first, you do not see them. You see only their effect. The unreliable physics of the place—the shifting distances, the slippery geometry—is the first sign of their stirring. It is the place itself beginning to look back at you. Your perception, which does not belong, causes reality to fray at the seams, and they are the weavers who emerge from the frays to pull the threads taut again.

When one begins to coalesce, it is not a gathering of matter. It is a sharpening of the world. It is a concentration of the place’s fundamental properties. Imagine a pocket of the air where the monolithic Chord becomes, impossibly, louder. A spot where the wet heat intensifies until light begins to visibly shimmer and bend around it. A patch of wall where the green of the malachite towers deepens, the condensation thickens, and the slick surface reflects not you, but a warped, intensified version of the spire across the canyon.

This focal point is a Resonator.

It has no fixed form. It is a standing wave in reality, a knot in the universal hum. To look at it is to see a shimmering vacancy in the saturated air, a human-sized lens of warped space. It is defined by its edges, where the light of the pearlescent sky bends into prismatic, oily colours. Within its "body," you see the world behind it, but distorted, as if viewed through ancient, flawed glass. Sometimes, a shape will suggest itself within the distortion—vague, bio-luminescent strata, like the cross-section of a jellyfish made of captured light, or the complex internal structure of a crystal grown in zero gravity. These are not organs. They are patterns of resonance, the intricate internal architecture of a solidified piece of sound.

They do not move. Movement is change, and change is the enemy. Instead, you will find that one is simply *there*, where it was not a moment before. You may be studying a far-off bridge, and when you turn back, a Resonator will have formed in the street before you, having resolved the paradox of your attention on a distant object. It blocks your path not by stepping in front of you, but by becoming a fact of the space you intended to occupy.

Their purpose is singular: to restore the perfect, static harmony. You are noise. You are movement. You are a temporal being in an eternal present. You are a thought in a world that has only one. The Resonator's function is to correct you.

It does not attack. It *attunes*.

As you draw near, or as it forms near you, the Chord that thrums in your bones will gain a localized, piercing intensity. It feels as if a tuning fork the size of a mountain has been struck right beside your head. The vibration becomes specific, directed. It is no longer just the background hum; it is a signal being focused directly into the core of your being. The Resonator is listening to your discordance, analyzing your unique, chaotic frequency.

And then, it begins to sing back.

It does not make a new sound. It simply modulates the eternal Chord in your immediate vicinity. It finds your frequency and begins to create a counter-frequency, a dissonant harmony that is specifically, exquisitely crafted to cancel you out. The pressure behind your teeth becomes an ache. The thrumming in your bones feels as if they are about to pulverize into sand. Your thoughts, which were already fragile threads in this roaring silence, begin to unravel completely. Memories surface not as images, but as raw data—the heat of a forgotten summer, the abstract shape of a loved one's laugh, the precise weight of a stone held in childhood—and the Resonator absorbs them, catalogs them, and flattens them. They are smoothed out, their jagged specificities ironed into the uniform texture of the place.

Your sense of self dissolves into the overpowering resonance. The distinction between the air and your skin, already tenuous, vanishes. The fluid in your lungs feels no different from the soupy atmosphere. You are being un-written. Your complexity is an error being erased.

If you could see another person undergoing this, you would see them slowly fade. Not into invisibility, but into the landscape. Their form would seem to waver, to become translucent, taking on the jade and malachite hues of the towers. The condensation of the air would begin to cling to them as it does to the stone, their particulars blurring until they are just another weeping, verdant shape in the city. A new, misshapen moss-stone on the path. A glistening streak on a resinous wall.

The Resonators are the living antibodies of a dead world. They are the gardeners in a petrified forest, and their job is to prune any shoot of unexpected life. To be near one is to feel your own existence being refuted, gently, inexorably, and with the full, monolithic authority of a world that has never known anything but its own perfect, screaming song. You are not being killed; you are being corrected. Your brief, discordant note is being resolved back into the eternal Chord.
