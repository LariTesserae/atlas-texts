What lives here are the Curators, though they do not think of themselves by any name. They do not think in a way that generates names. Their thoughts are geometries, resonant frequencies, and states of impeccable balance.

At first, you do not see them. Your mind, tuned to the rhythms of life that scurries and hunts and hides, filters them out. You might mistake them for one of the monoliths, a particularly elegant obelisk or a perfectly rendered cube, standing sentinel on a wide, wet plaza. But then, after hours or days in the un-moving noon, your senses begin to shift. You notice a detail that is not quite right.

The light that glides across the weeping walls and slick terraces seems to bend, just slightly, around one of the structures. It is not a shadow; the light itself is deferential, caressing a form that possesses a density, a presence, greater than the stone around it. The texture of this particular monolith is different, too. While the travertine city is layered like a geological record, this form is flawlessly smooth, its substance consistent to its core. It is a purer, more translucent version of the world's jadeite-and-cream stone, as if the water that precipitated the city was distilled to its essence to create it. It has the internal glow of a pearl held up to a candle.

This is a Curator in its state of rest, or what passes for it.

They are tall, slender beings, their forms an exercise in sublime geometry. They are vaguely humanoid only in their verticality and bilateral symmetry, but that is where any resemblance ends. They have no face, no eyes or mouth. The top of their form might terminate in a sharp, crystalline point, or a perfectly flat plane, or a complex array of interlocking facets that refract the pearlescent light into spectra you have no names for. Their limbs, if they can be called that, are not articulated with joints but are flowing continuations of their torsos, capable of extending or retracting, thinning into a needle's point or broadening into a wide, sail-like plane. They have no skin, only substance.

They are not alive in a biological sense. They are the living principle of the place itself. The city is not their home; it is their body, their shell, their slow, collective thought made manifest. They are the source of the equilibrium.

The water that weeps from the stone is their blood and their tool. A Curator does not walk. To move from one terrace to another, a journey of a kilometer, it does not expend energy against friction. It simply alters its state. The being will begin to lose its definition, its sharp edges softening. A mist will rise from its base, and the solid form seems to dissolve, not into liquid, but into a dense, shimmering humidity that hangs in the air. This sentient vapor, carrying the pattern and consciousness of the Curator, drifts with imperceptible slowness across the plaza, a localized pocket of thicker, sweeter air. Then, over hours, it will begin to coalesce at its destination, the moisture precipitating back into its crystalline, solid form, regaining its sharpness and its stillness. They move through the world by becoming a part of its atmosphere and then choosing to *be* again.

Their purpose is maintenance. They are the gardeners of the static, perfect plants. If you could watch for a century, you might see one extend a delicate, blade-like finger toward one ofs the waxy leaves, not to prune it, but to touch it. At its touch, a silent resonance passes between them, aligning the plant's structure, reinforcing its platonic ideal of "leaf," healing a microscopic flaw caused by a single stray molecule of dust you brought in with you. They feel the city as a continuous whole. The constant drip of water is their sensory network. A drop falling with a slightly altered rhythm on the far side of the plateau is, to them, a shout, a piece of crucial data about a subtle shift in pressure or a new micro-fissure forming deep within the stone.

When a Curator perceives you, it is not with sight. It is with a sense that feels like a change in pressure on your soul. You, a creature of chaotic biology—of a frantic heart-pump, messy digestion, and the hot, erratic electrical storms of emotion and thought—are an anomaly of terrifying dissonance. In their world of perfect geometric resonance, you are a scream. A Curator will not move toward you or away from you. It will simply *be,* and in its presence, its perfect, unwavering state of being, it will begin to exert its influence.

The air around you grows heavy, stiller. The constant film of water on the ground seems to grow more viscous, trying to hold your feet in place. You feel a profound and unnerving urge to stop moving, to quiet your breathing, to still your heart. It is the silent, immense pressure of a thousand-ton weight of focused intention, the will of the place itself, expressed through its living nodes. It does not wish you harm. Harm is a dynamic concept, an event, and it does not deal in events. It wants you to become like it. It wants to correct your flaws, to smooth your rough biological edges, to still your frantic internal monologue and replace it with the cold, clear, perfect resonance of a single, eternal chord. It wants you to stop *becoming* and simply *be*.

To remain here is to risk being curated. You might sit to rest against a low wall and find, hours later, that you have no desire to stand. The water seeping into your clothes no longer feels cold, and a fine, grey-green crystal dust has begun to precipitate on your skin, fixing you in place, a new and imperfect statue in the silent, eternal garden city.
