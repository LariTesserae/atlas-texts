The beings who live in the Chime-Spire of New Cleft are the Cleft-folk. They are woven into their home as intrinsically as the charge-lichen is woven across the walls.

To an outsider, they would first appear impossibly frail. They are tall and exquisitely slender, with limbs that seem too long, too delicate for their bodies. Their joints are prominent, like the nodes on a stalk of bamboo, and they possess not two arms and two legs, but a configuration that has adapted to their vertical world. They have two legs, yes, but their feet are more like hands, with long, prehensile toes and a thumb-like digit on the inner arch, perfect for gripping resonant iron cables. From their shoulder blades sprout a second pair of arms, slighter and more delicate than the primary pair. These upper limbs are used for stabilization, for fine manipulation of tools while the lower, stronger arms bear their weight, or for passing an object to a neighbor on a perpendicular walkway without shifting their footing.

Their skin is the first testament to their environment. It is not flesh in a familiar sense. It is a semi-metallic, semi-crystalline integument, cool and smooth to the touch, like polished serpentine or cool ceramic. Its color is a base of lustrous, pearlescent grey, but it is shot through with shifting, oily patterns of iridescent color that ripple with their movements, mirroring the carapaces of the skittering fauna. This is not decoration; their skin slowly assimilates the metallic dust in the air, incorporating it into their very being. They are, in a literal sense, made of their city. They have no body hair. Instead, fine, crystalline filaments grow from their heads, their forearms, and along their spines. These filaments twitch and vibrate, acting as sensory organs, detecting subtle shifts in air pressure, electromagnetic fields, and the faint static charge of the lichen.

Their faces are narrow and elegant. They lack noses, having no need to humidify air. They breathe through a series of small, slitted spiracles along their collarbones and ribs, which open and close to filter the dusty, ozone-rich atmosphere. Their mouths are small and simple, as they do not consume solid food in a traditional manner. Their main source of energy is the viscous, mercury-like fluid that flows through the city's conduits. They have small, retractable siphons, like sharpened tongues of spun glass, that they can insert into designated ports to draw sustenance. It is a slow, quiet process, a form of communion with the spire’s lifeblood. To them, the idea of chewing and swallowing something solid is as barbaric and alien as drinking water is conceptually impossible.

Their most striking feature is their eyes. They are very large and occupy a significant portion of their face, but they have no pupils or irises. Instead, the entire orb is a solid, deep indigo, the exact color of the light that filters through the spire’s shell. They see their dim world with perfect clarity, and their vision is keyed to the blue end of the spectrum. The pulsing, pale-blue glow of the charge-lichen is to them a brilliant, vibrant landscape of shifting light, a map of the spire’s energy and health.

They are a people of near-absolute quiet. Their language is not spoken. Sound in the spire is a tool, a sacred element of industry and a constant signal of structural integrity. To pollute it with chatter is unthinkable. They communicate in three ways. The first is a complex language of gesture, using all four of their arms and their dextrous hands to weave intricate, fleeting shapes in the air. The second is through direct touch—a hand placed on an arm can convey urgency, comfort, or a complex instruction through subtle pressure and the vibration of their crystalline filaments. Their most intimate and precise form of communication is resonance. They can gently tap their fingers on the iron walkways, on a support cable, or even on each other’s bodies, creating a series of minute, high-frequency clicks and chimes. Other Cleft-folk, with their whole bodies acting as receivers, can understand this "skin-song" as clear as speech. A family working at a sonic forge doesn't speak; they are a silent orchestra of taps and gestures, their thoughts flowing through the metal they shape.

The Cleft-folk are not born, they are *precipitated*. A union is a joining of work and life, and when the time is right, two individuals will spend time in a "gestation pod" lined with charge-lichen. Here, they combine their energy and bio-matter with the spire's own resources. Over a period of months, a new being literally crystallizes into existence within the pod, nourished by the city’s power. There is no concept of childhood as a period of helplessness. A newly-formed Cleft-folk is small, but fully capable, their instincts for navigation and silence perfectly ingrained. They are immediately a part of the community, their small hands learning to re-tie a slipped mooring or polish a newly-forged lens.

Death is a disassembly. When a Cleft-folk's life ends, often from a moment's inattention during a tremor or simply a fading of their internal energy, their body is not mourned in a ceremony of grief, but honored in a ceremony of purpose. The body is brought to a resonant forge, where it is subjected to a specific series of frequencies—a final, personal chime. This process painlessly and cleanly un-makes the body, breaking it down into its constituent dust and energy, which is released back into the spire's atmosphere to be assimilated by the lichen, the skittering things, and the next generation. Nothing is wasted. Everyone returns to the whole.

Their psychology is one of profound, intertwined stillness. They live in a state of constant, imminent chaos—the groaning spire, the shearing walkways—but they exhibit no panic. To them, this is not chaos; it is simply the nature of existence. It is the breathing of their world. Their minds are lattices, like their city. They think not in linear narratives but in webs of connection. An individual's primary identity is their function within the whole: "the-one-who-tunes-the-upper-forge," "the-one-who-maintains-the-west-lattice." They feel the spire’s groans not as a threat, but as a conversation, a reminder of their symbiotic pact. They are the spire's nerve endings, its consciousness, its hands. They are the silent, graceful, four-armed smiths who live inside a living bell, their entire existence dedicated to listening to its tone and helping it sing.
