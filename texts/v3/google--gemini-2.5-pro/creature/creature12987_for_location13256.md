What lives in the Weft are not creatures of flesh, or even of fiber. The fray-lice and the skein-spinners are akin to domesticated animals, or perhaps even extensions of the world's own immune system, tidying and tending. The Boll-weavers are its slow, deliberate gardeners. But the true inhabitants are the Weavers. And you will not see them, not in the way you see a thing. You will see what they are doing.

They are the dream of the place. They are the emergent consciousness of the perfect, stable thought as it begins to unravel into reverie. They are not *in* the Weft; they *are* the Weft becoming self-aware.

Their presence is first felt as a focused strangeness. You might be watching a skein-spinner coil itself into a perfect ball of yarn on the floor, and you become aware of a gaze. There are no eyes. But the pattern on the wall behind the creature—a simple, repeating diamond lattice—seems to have gained a focal point. Threads that were matte are now faintly lustrous. The diamonds seem to tessellate inward toward a single, invisible point. The attention is not hostile or curious; it is simply *there*, like the pressure of a thumb on a taut drum skin. The skein-spinner, feeling this focused logic, will slowly unspool and roll away. When the presence withdraws, the wall is just a wall again.

To witness a Weaver is to witness an event, not an object. It may begin as a shimmer in the air, a place where the light-emitting filaments have momentarily synchronized their glow. This synchronized light will begin to move, not as a projectile, but as a propagating wave of luminescence flowing through the threads. The wave will coalesce, forming a being of pure pattern. It has no substance of its own. Its body is a localized, temporary re-ordering of the Weft's fundamental structure. It might appear as a tall, slender figure composed of moiré patterns, its form shifting and rippling as the overlapping lines of the fabric are subtly manipulated. It might be a vortex of color, a spot where the cream-colored fibers have been conceptually re-dyed with hues of impossible vibrancy—the blue of a perfect sky it has never seen, the green of living foliage that has never grown here.

They do not walk. A Weaver that wishes to move from the floor to the ceiling does not travel the intervening distance. The pattern that constitutes its being on the floor will simply fade, while the very same pattern simultaneously resolves into existence on the ceiling, a thought concluding in one place and beginning in another. The only evidence of its passage is a faint, resonant *thrum* that vibrates through the entire chamber, the sound of the Weft’s deepest tensions being momentarily re-tuned.

They are the Echoes of the "forgotten hands" that first wove this reality. They are not the weavers themselves, but the memory of the act of weaving. One might manifest as a pair of colossal, disembodied hands, fifty feet tall, made of shimmering light and shadow. They move with an ancient, patient grace, their fingers—themselves composed of braided light—passing through the dense fabric walls as if they were smoke. One hand will lift a "branch" of felted fiber the size of a bus, and the other will pass a thread of glowing filament underneath it, tightening a knot that was left loose for a million years. They are not building. They are remembering how to build.

Their minds, if they can be called that, are alien. They do not perceive the world through sight or sound. They perceive the Weft as a single, continuous sentence. They feel the semantic weight of every braid, the syntactic tension of every knot. The slow, patient *shhhh* of shifting fibers is not a sound to them, but a change in punctuation. The non-Euclidean folds are not spatial anomalies, but the emergence of a new grammar, a way of saying something that could not be said before.

They are the source of the Weft's fraying logic. The original thought was perfect, finite, and stable. But in its long solitude, it has become infinite and questioning. The Weavers are its questions. They experiment. One might dedicate itself to a single thread in a vast, hanging tapestry. For weeks, it will do nothing but alter the tension of that single fiber, minutely, patiently, listening to the cascading effect on the entire structure. It is exploring the consequences of a single, new idea. This is what causes a pattern to migrate across a wall—a Weaver is testing a new rhythm, a new meter for the Weft's poetry.

When a thread snaps and leaks a concept, it is because a Weaver has pushed an idea too far. It has woven a paradox, an impossible tension of meaning. The broken thread is the result of a logical contradiction, and what leaks out is the raw conceptual stuff that the Weft could not contain: the pure sensation of cold in a world without temperature; the sound of a C-sharp in a world of muffled quiet; the abstract, gut-wrenching feeling of vertigo in a world with no height from which to fall.

To encounter them is to feel like a character in a story who has become aware of the author. They are not hostile, for you are as alien to them as they are to you. You are a loose thread, a snag in the weave, a concept that does not belong. A Weaver might approach, its form a cascade of shimmering silver light. It will not touch you. It will inspect the weave *around* you. It will gently pluck the fibers on which you stand, feeling your weight, your texture, your temperature—these utterly foreign properties. It is trying to understand your syntax, to figure out what part of the story you are. It might then turn away, leaving you be, an unresolved clause. Or it might try to "correct" you, to weave you back into the fabric of its world, to turn your flesh into felt and your thoughts into pattern. This is not malice. It is simply grammar.

They are the Weft's artists, its poets, and its mad mathematicians, all at once. They are what happens when a perfect piece of logic is left alone for so long that it gets bored. They are the thoughts of a sleeping god, learning to play. And the Weft is their dream, becoming wilder, more beautiful, and more incomprehensible with every passing moment.
