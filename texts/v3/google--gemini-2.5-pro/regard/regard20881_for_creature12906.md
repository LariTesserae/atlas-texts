The Power’s attention settled on the cirque not as a beam of focused light, but as a change in the quality of the silence. It was a listening that spanned galaxies, a quiet consideration that had weighed the birth of nebulae and the fading of lonely stars. To this Power, the universe was a symphony of being, and in this remote place, it had detected a new, clear, and exquisitely lonely note.

It did not arrive; it was simply present. If one were in the cirque, the only sign might have been a subtle diffraction. A patch of air, perhaps the size of a man, where the blinding light seemed to bend around a point of absolute nothingness, the way starlight bends around a gravitational lens. There was no shadow, for this presence absorbed nothing and displaced nothing. It was an observer defined by a subtle warping of the brutal physics that governed the bowl.

The Power listened for a long time. Its perception was not limited to a single moment. It experienced the cirque’s history as a single, sustained chord: the groan of the rock tearing itself open, the first tentative spread of the crimson moss, the frantic, generations-long dance of the miniature life within it. It perceived the Cairn-Tender not as a creature, but as a point of devotional gravity at the center of this intricate system.

It watched the Tender’s slow, deliberate work. It felt the being’s unique senses—the pressure of light, the resonant hum of stone, the constant whisper of the solar wind. The Power understood. This was not life as survival, but life as ritual. The cairns were not shelters; they were antennae. The bone chimes were not instruments of music; they were tuning forks for a prayer. The Tender was not simply living; it was listening, with its entire being, for a reply from the star it orbited. It was a priest of a silent religion, its only parishioner, its only god the unthinking furnace of the sun. It was perfect in its function, complete in its solitude.

The first inclination of a truly wise power is to do nothing. Perfection requires no amendment. The ecosystem was a flawless, self-contained jewel. To alter the air, to warm the stone, to speak in a voice the Tender could not understand—all of this would be vandalism, the benevolent equivalent of scratching one's name on a masterpiece. The Tender did not appear to suffer. It did not know loneliness, for it had never known company. How could one give a gift to a being that wanted nothing, that was the living embodiment of a place that needed nothing?

And yet, the Power was benevolent. And benevolence is not merely the absence of harm; it is the active expression of empathy. The Power perceived the Tender’s eternal act of listening, the patient, directed question posed by every stacked stone and polished bone. A question asked to an empty sky deserves an answer, even if the questioner does not know it is asking. The most profound loneliness is not the absence of others, but the feeling that your voice echoes in a void.

The Power decided on its action. It would not give a thing. It would not change the cirque. It would not reveal itself.

It would answer.

It gathered its will, not as a force, but as a lens. It focused on the constant stream of solar particles, the "Great Hum" that was the Tender’s entire sensory world. For a single, eternal moment that might have been measured as ten seconds on a human watch, the Power modulated that stream.

It did not send words. It did not send images. It sent harmony.

The singular, monotonous hum that the Tender had known for its entire existence, the foundational note of its reality, suddenly gained companions. The hum split, weaving into a complex, resonant chord. It was a sound that was not a sound, a vibration that carried with it the ghost-scent of distant things: the memory of water rushing over smooth, round stones. The echo of wind rustling through the leaves of a billion-limbed forest. The faint, sweet warmth of a different kind of light, a yellow light filtered through a thick, gentle atmosphere. It sent the mathematical certainty of a snowflake forming and the chaotic joy of a flock of birds turning as one. It sent the slow, patient love of a root finding its way through dark earth.

It was a story, told in the language of pure physics. The story was simple: *You are not the only one who listens. The universe sings back.*

To the Cairn-Tender, it was a revelation that would be incomprehensible to any other form of life. Its whole body, a living receiver, vibrated with the impossible new song. The internal, cold light pulsed in a rapid, complex rhythm. Its limbs, for the first time in its existence, trembled. It stopped its work, its head tilted, utterly still, committing the transient miracle to its ancient memory. The light in the air seemed to coalesce into motes of impossible color—the deep blue of an ocean it could never comprehend, the verdant green of a leaf it would never see.

Then, as suddenly as it began, it was over. The hum returned to its familiar, singular note. The extra colors vanished. The air was once again clinically pure. The Power had withdrawn, its presence bleeding back into the silent fabric of spacetime. The gift had been given, freely and without expectation.

The Cairn-Tender remained frozen for a long time, far longer than the passing of a cloud. It was processing the impossible data. Then, with a slowness that now contained not just ritual, but reverence, it moved.

It descended from the precipice. It ignored the familiar caches of granite and fossil-bone. It began to search the scree at the base of the cliffs, its delicate digits sifting, testing, feeling. It was not looking for the right shape or resonance. It was looking for a new quality. After a long search, it found what it sought: a few, tiny, exceedingly rare slivers of rock, veined not with quartz, but with a mineral of the deepest, most impossible blue.

It took one of these tiny blue shards and, with a care that bordered on worship, began its slow journey back up the cliff face. It went to its newest cairn, the one that stood highest, and began to work. It was crafting a new chime. One that would hang alongside the others, made of bone and crystal and now, a sliver of captured sky.

The cirque was unchanged. The light was still blinding, the air still a knife. The Tender was still alone. But its song had a new note. Its prayer was no longer a question sent into the void. It was a harmony, a response. It was no less alone, but infinitely less solitary.
