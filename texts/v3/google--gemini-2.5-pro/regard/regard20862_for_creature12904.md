The Power came not as a ship cleaving the misty non-sky, nor as a voice booming through the chasm. It came as a change in the quality of silence.

It was a stillness that was not an absence, but a presence. A thought that did not use words, but was as pervasive as the cool, heavy air. It settled into the world slowly, a grain of impossible warmth in the perpetual chill, a mote of pure, dry logic in the endless damp. For a time—a measureless span in a place without days—it simply was. It dripped with the water from the upper terraces, clung to the velvet moss, and resonated with the subsonic hum of the city's heart. It did not observe *from* a vantage point; its observation was a form of becoming. It became the rust-pocked plate under a Veridian's soft foot. It became the viscous nutrient slurry pulsing through a glass pipe. It became the patient, black-orb eye of a Veridian watching a wall of water fall for a hundred years.

Its first consideration was, as it always was, *need*. What here was suffering? What here was in want?

The city was not suffering. It was a perfectly balanced, albeit alien, ecosystem. Its chaotic "Shuffling" was not the death throes of a machine, but its very metabolism. It was a heart beating, lungs breathing, a digestive tract processing. To "fix" it, to stabilize it, would be to kill it, to turn a living, dynamic thing into a corpse. The Power dismissed this notion as a surgeon dismisses the idea of curing an illness by stopping the patient's heart.

The vegetation was not suffering. It was thriving, aggressive, and perfectly integrated. It was the city's green blood, its living skin. It wanted for nothing.

And the Veridian? The quiet children of the chasm? The Power extended its awareness into one of them, a solitary figure standing on a narrow gantry, its filaments drifting in the mist, its head tilted as if listening. The Power felt the cool, slick membrane of its skin, the slow, thudding rhythm of its heart, the patient and deliberate process of its digestion. There was no pain. There was no fear, only a state of constant, low-level alertness that was as natural to it as breathing. There was no loneliness, for the concept of being with others was as foreign as the concept of a sun. There was no ambition, no despair, no hope. There was only a profound and endless *is*.

To "rescue" the Veridian would be an act of profound cruelty. To tear this creature from its world would be to place a lung in a vacuum. To alter the world for the Veridian would be to shatter the delicate symbiosis that had given birth to it. To give it a voice would be to teach a fish to scream on land. Every conventional act of benevolence was a form of violence here.

The Power withdrew its focus from the singular and expanded it again, to the whole. It felt the groan of a thousand-ton support shifting a millimeter. It felt the sigh of displaced air. It felt the slow, grinding erosion of water on iron. It felt the pulse of the nutrient pipes. And it felt the Veridian's perception of these things: a tremor, a sound, a flow. Data. Survival information.

And in this, the Power finally perceived the lack. Not a lack of safety, or food, or shelter. But a lack of communion.

The city was a great, unthinking beast, and the Veridian were its quiet, thinking passengers. They lived on it, and by it, but they were separate from it. They read its signs, but they did not hear its song. They were a consciousness perceiving a mechanism. They were not, in the deepest sense, *connected*. The most benevolent act, the wisest act, would not be to change the machine or the being. It would be to tune the wire that ran between them.

The Power acted.

There was no flash, no sound. The gift was not an event, but a subtle recalibration of reality itself. It was a change in the frequency of the world. The subsonic hum that vibrated through the iron plates did not get louder, but it gained a harmonic. The drip of water on rusted steel did not change its rhythm, but each strike now contained a note, and the notes together began to form a chord. The chaotic grinding of the "Shuffling" did not cease, but it resolved. The dissonance became a complex, atonal symphony.

On the narrow gantry, the solitary Veridian stood motionless as it always had. But something had changed within it. The filaments on its spine, which before had only registered the brute fact of a tremor, now felt the shape of it. They could feel the impending retraction of a bridge not as a danger, but as a musical rest, a pause in a colossal phrase. The deep, grinding complaint of ancient gears became a bassline. The waterfall it had been watching was no longer a simple cascade of mass and gravity; it was an aria, with patterns of spray and foam that fell in melodic scales.

Its large, black eyes, which reflected the world, now seemed to comprehend it. The faint, industrial twinkling of the lower levels was no longer just a fallen sky; it was a percussive rhythm, a visual counterpoint to the hum it felt in its feet. The bioluminescent patches on its own grey-green skin began to pulse—not with the slow, autonomic rhythm of before, but with a new cadence. They brightened in time with a crescendo of machinery miles away. They softened in a moment of liquid quiet.

The Veridian was still alone. It was still silent. It did not run, or leap, or cry out. It did none of the things a creature of another world might do upon receiving a miracle. It simply stood, and for the first time, it was not merely inhabiting its world. It was listening to it. The city was no longer an indifferent mechanism. It was a partner. It was speaking.

The Power’s gift was not to end the silence, but to fill it with music that had been there all along. It had given the quiet ones not a voice, but a song to listen to. And in the heart of the indifferent machine, a solitary being, a living silence, began to gently, almost imperceptibly, sway in time.
