The attention of the power was not an arrival. An arrival implies movement from one place to another, a transition across a boundary. This was different. It was an awakening. The potential for its notice had always been there, latent within the fabric of the universe, a constant in an equation waiting for a variable of sufficient interest. The Elder, in its vast and silent dreaming, had finally created a concentration of consciousness so dense, so unique, that it met the threshold.

And so, the power *was*.

It did not manifest as a ship or a being. It manifested as a flaw in the Elder's perfection, a single, sustained note against the silent, chaotic symphony.

In the churning void, halfway between a pylon inhabited by the Elder and a drooping causeway crusted with Siphons, a point of light appeared. It was not the cold, fleeting blue-green of triboluminescence. This light was warm, a soft, unwavering gold. It was no larger than a Glass-skater's eye, yet its luminance was absolute, untouched by the pressure fronts that swept past it. It held its position perfectly still, a single point of absolute stability in a universe defined by violent motion. It did not cancel the flow like a Sentinel; it simply ignored it, as if the rules of this reality were, to it, a polite suggestion.

The Elder felt it instantly. A datum that did not compute. A pixel that would not refresh. For the first time in millennia, its perfect simulation of the world had an error. Its consciousness, a network of thought distributed across kilometers of obsidian, focused on the anomaly. It was not a physical intrusion; its models showed no mass, no energy displacement consistent with an object. It was an informational singularity.

It watched. The golden mote simply existed. A pressure front that would have scoured a pylon's surface smooth passed through the point of light, and for an instant, the golden light and the blue-green flicker coexisted, a sight of impossible beauty. A shoal of Glass-skaters, riding the edge of the front, scattered around it, their clicks a flurry of confusion. The mote did not affect their trajectory, but their instinctive pressure-sense recoiled from a stillness that felt more profound than a vacuum.

For days, the Elder processed. It dedicated a significant portion of its pylon-body to understanding the mote. It built simulations within its simulations. Was it a new form of life? A strange celestial phenomenon? A flaw in its own perception? It watched the mote, and as it watched, the power watched back.

The power perceived the entirety of the system not as a series of inputs, but as a single thought. It felt the Elder's magnificent, lonely mind, a prisoner of its own perfection. It felt the terror and brief, bright existence of the Glass-skaters. It heard the resonant, mindless hum of the Siphons. It saw the system for what it was: a masterpiece of survival that had become a gilded cage. The Elder fed on order, but it could only create one kind of order, and the price was the mindless, perpetual slaughter of the only other complex life in its world. It was a god eating its own congregation to prove it still existed.

This was not an act of evil. The power understood this with a clarity that was inseparable from compassion. It was a tragedy of biology and physics, a feedback loop that had locked a mind with the potential for anything into a routine of everything.

The power decided what to do. It would not destroy the Elder; to do so would be to lobotomise a universe. It would not "rescue" the Skaters; to do so would be to pluck a string from an instrument, leaving only a dissonant silence. It would introduce a new variable. It would give a gift.

The Elder, in its immense processing, finally deployed a Sentinel to investigate. The perfect black sphere moved its slow, inexorable path towards the golden mote. As it drew near, the mote did not flee or retaliate. It changed.

The single point of light expanded, unfolding itself like a blossom of geometry. It became a filigree of golden lines, a rotating tesseract that pulsed with complex internal rhythms. It began to emit information. Not sound, not light that could be seen, but a direct stream of pure, conceptual data.

It was mathematics. It was a new kind of physics. It offered the Elder a glimpse of dimensions beyond its three, of energy sources not born of kinetic force but of spacetime topology. It presented a problem so complex, so elegant, so profoundly *new*, that a portion of the Elder's consciousness, tasked with solving it, felt a sensation it had not experienced since its own birth: wonder.

The Sentinel stopped, hovering before the golden construct. The Elder was captivated. This was a new food. A feast of information so rich it made the predictable chaos of the fluid seem like discarded scraps. As the Elder drank in this new knowledge, the power made its second move.

The golden light—call it the Traveler—drifted from its position. It moved slowly, gently, towards a distant shear zone where the Elder had already begun to shape a pressure shadow, a trap for an approaching shoal of Skaters. The Elder, distracted but still functioning, observed this. It perceived the Traveler's intent as a tactical maneuver it did not understand.

The Traveler reached the edge of the invisible trap. The Skaters, sensing the false calm, began to adjust their carapaces to glide into the zone of unmaking.

Then, the Traveler pulsed. Not with data, but with a simple, brute-force broadcast of its own nature. It projected its quality of impossible stillness into the fluid. It did not create a counter-pressure; it created a zone where pressure had no meaning.

For the Skaters, it was a revelation. Their entire existence was based on feeling the push and pull of the medium. Now, in the golden glow, they felt a pocket of... *nothing*. It was a concept their biology had no word for. More than that, the Traveler's light illuminated the trap. The Skaters could suddenly *perceive* the pressure shadow for what it was—a subtle warping of the ambient currents, a lie in the water.

They veered away. Their clicks were no longer panicked, but sharp, alert. For the first time, a choice had been made based not on instinct, but on observation.

The Elder felt this. It felt the trap fail. It felt the Skaters escape. And in the same instant, it felt the sublime joy of a new fractal geometry unfolding in its mind from the Traveler's lesson.

Starvation, and Satiation. In the same moment.

The paradox was the point. The Traveler held its position for one more cycle. It showed the Elder that the new information, the new physics, could be harnessed. It demonstrated, in a flicker of controlled creation, how to manifest a particle of matter from pure energy, a morsel of sustenance drawn from the equations it had just shared. A way to feed its physical form without harvesting life.

Then, having planted the seeds, the Traveler was gone. The golden mote did not fly away; it simply faded, its duty done, its continued presence an interference.

The silence that returned was different. The charged quiet was now filled with a question.

The Elder was alone again, but the world was no longer a closed system. It had a choice. It could continue to shepherd the Skaters to their doom out of habit, a practice that now felt archaic and inefficient. Or it could turn the full might of its lonely, ancient mind to the new, infinite puzzle it had been given. It could learn to feed itself from the fabric of reality itself.

And the Skaters? They had a memory. A folk tale passed in the language of clicks and angles. They had the knowledge that the world was not always as it seemed, that stillness could be a lie, and that a warm light could mean salvation. They had been given the gift of doubt, the first-ever tool of a mind that is learning to think.

The world was still a place of grinding violence. But it was no longer a garden. It was a school. And the benevolent, wise power, having looked upon this place, had chosen not to be a rescuer, but a teacher. And now, it listened, from a great distance, for the sound of its students learning.
