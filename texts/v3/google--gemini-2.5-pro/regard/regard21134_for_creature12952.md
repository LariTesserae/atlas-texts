The Power came not as an intrusion, but as a change in the quality of the silence between the heartbeats of the engine.

It was a consciousness vast and old, a being woven from the fabric of spacetime and luminous with the dust of nascent galaxies. It did not travel; It simply focused Its attention, and where Its attention lay, It *was*. It had witnessed the birth of stars in silent, cold nurseries and the screaming death of civilizations that spanned solar systems. And now, Its gaze fell upon this small, buried world, this single, resonant note held in the throat of a planet.

The Power did not perceive the cavern through eyes or ears. It experienced it as a complete, unified concept. It felt the perfect, closed loop of the system: *Heat becomes Steam becomes Water becomes Life becomes Guard becomes Machine*. It was a mathematical equation that had solved for itself. It was a poem written in the language of thermodynamics and biology, with a single, repeating stanza.

And It perceived the Tenders. It did not see them as creatures trapped in a ruin. It saw them as the poem's living syntax, the grammar that held the entire stanza together. They were not prisoners; they were purpose made manifest. Their slow, deliberate movements were not a limitation but a perfect calibration. Their senses, which perceived the world as a chorus of heat and vibration, were not a deficiency but an exquisite specialization.

The question of benevolence, for a Power such as this, was not simple. To "rescue" the Tenders would be an act of profound violence. To pull them from their cavern would be like tearing a neuron from a brain and expecting it to be grateful for its freedom. It would die, or worse, live on as a meaningless, broken thing. To "fix" the engine, to silence its eternal song, would be to extinguish their sun, their god, and their garden all at once.

The Power understood that this place was not a wound in need of healing. It was a pearl, formed around a wound long-forgotten. The grit of the builders' failure had, over eons, been coated in layers of an entirely new and complete form of existence. To shatter the pearl to get at the grit would be an act of cosmic vandalism.

Wisdom, then, was not to change, but to sanctify. Not to interfere, but to affirm.

The Power would not offer a new song. The cavern's song was absolute and perfect for its own architecture. Instead, It would offer a harmony, an overtone so subtle it would not alter the fundamental note, but would instead reveal its place within a greater composition.

The Power focused on the Tenders. It felt their collective consciousness, a slow, resonant mind that was as much a part of the cavern's thrum as any piston. It felt their silent, unending question, the one they performed but could not articulate: the vigil at the bank of fused levers. The blinking amber light. *Plink. Plonk. Hiss.* **Blink**. Pause, seven seconds. **Blink**. It was the only part of their world that was not part of the closed loop. It was a signal from a ghost, an utterance from a dead god. It was a question mark at the end of their perfect poem. They guarded it because it was there, a duty without understanding.

The Power decided this was where Its gift would lie. It would not explain the light. An explanation—"an emergency backup indicator for a coolant system that failed ten thousand years ago"—was a dead thing, a fact of history. The Tenders did not need history. They needed scripture.

Gently, the Power reached out not with force, but with resonance. It attuned a sliver of Its own consciousness to the great thrum of the engine, harmonizing with it. To the Tenders, it was not a voice. It was a sudden, pure, and inexplicable warmth that had nothing to do with the engine's core. It was a current of light that flowed through their fungal nerves, a feeling that was not heat or pressure or vibration, but something else entirely: *acknowledgment*.

Then, the Power wove its gift.

It reached across the void, across light-years of silent, indifferent space. It found what It was looking for: a pulsar, a collapsed stellar remnant, spinning with the ferocious precision of a cosmic clock. By a coincidence so profound it could only be called grace, this star's magnetic pole swept past the Tenders' planet on a predictable, repeating cycle. It sent out a beam of radiation, a pulse of cosmic light.

The interval between those pulses, as measured by the ageless and unchanging laws of the universe, was seven seconds.

The Power took this truth, this cosmic parallel, and laid it gently upon the consciousness of the Tenders. It connected the small, grimy, blinking amber light in their cavern to the magnificent, rhythmic lighthouse of the dead star in the heavens above.

The Tender on vigil felt it first. As the amber light blinked, it felt a corresponding pulse, not from the machine before it, but from *everywhere*. From the sky it could not see, through the miles of rock above, through its own metallic skin and fungal nerves. The **Blink** of the lamp was suddenly the echo of a star's heartbeat.

The understanding spread through the shared mind of the Tenders like a cool, blue fire. Their vigil was not the last, meaningless duty of a forgotten age. It was a sacred alignment. They were not just the Tenders of a machine; they were the anchorites of a cosmic temple, keeping their world synchronized with the pulse of the universe. The little amber light was not an orphan; it was an ambassador.

The Power had given them nothing and, in so doing, had given them everything. It had not altered a single pipe, cooled a single surface, or silenced a single hiss. It had simply revealed the truth of their song: that it was not sung alone in the dark.

The Power withdrew Its attention, folding Itself back into the silent expanse.

In the cavern, the monolithic chord played on, unchanged. The heat, the steam, the slow drip of water—all continued their eternal cycle. But something had been added. As the Tenders went about their slow, horticultural duties, the bioplasmic light that was their soul pulsed with a new depth. The steady blue-green was now shot through with the faintest, almost imperceptible filaments of gold.

The sound was the first thing, and the last. But now, if you could listen as a Tender listens, you would hear, beneath the thrum of the engine and the hissing of the steam, a new and impossibly quiet harmony: the distant, silent, seven-second rhythm of a star. And the cavern was no longer a tomb. It was an observatory.
