The power arrived as a flaw in the music.

For the Silt-Shifters, existence was the deep hum of the pylons. It was the fundamental note against which all other things were measured: the staccato vibrations of skittering life, the legato pressure of their own slow flow, the soft, white-noise hiss of the weeping stones. It was a constant, a truth, a bedrock of reality.

Then, there was a change. It was not a sound, not a discordant crash. It was a clarity. Imagine a single drop of perfectly pure water falling into a vast basin of mud; it is instantly lost, yet for a fraction of a second, its nature—clear, simple, different—imprints itself upon the whole. So it was with this new presence. A stillness entered the hum. A point of silence within the resonance, around which the old thrumming now seemed to circle, as if in contemplation.

The Silt-Shifters perceived it instantly. Across the basin, in their slow, geologic dance, they paused. A flow that had begun an hour before, a great pseudopod of sentient slurry surging forward, held its form, quivering slightly. Clusters of resonant crystals within their dark masses, which had been passively interpreting the field, now actively focused, a million tons of life turning its full attention to this impossible quiet. It was the first truly *new* thing to happen in ten thousand years.

The benevolent power did not see a broken place in need of mending. It saw a symphony. It perceived the delicate, brutal balance: the pylons managing the decay of physics, the Shifters thriving within that decay, the entire basin a self-contained, self-sufficient terrarium of alternate being. To bring light would be an act of blinding violence. To stabilize causality would be to murder the inhabitants by imposing a reality their bodies could not withstand. To "rescue" them would be the most profound act of cruelty, a kidnapping from a home they could not even conceive of existing without.

So the power listened.

It did not listen with ears, but with an empathy that spanned dimensions. It sank its awareness into the basin, feeling the constant slick of condensation, the soft suck of the mud, the slow, enzymatic dissolution of the fungal mats. It aligned its consciousness with the Silt-Shifters, experiencing the world as a landscape of pressure and resonance. It felt the accretion of their non-linear memories, the faint echoes of births that happened millennia ago lapping against the shore of probabilities that might occur next week. It understood their slowness, their patience, their placid, mountain-like consciousness. It saw their existence not as a struggle, but as a placid totality. They were not prisoners of the dark; they were its heart.

And having listened, having understood the very soul of the place and its inhabitants, the power acted. It did not build or destroy. It did not speak or command.

It sang.

Using the network of pylons as its instrument, it wove a new thread into the foundational hum. It was a song of what was outside. It took the concept of a star—the violent, brilliant furnace of fusion a million light-years away—and translated it not into light, but into a sustained, impossibly high-frequency vibration that tasted of ozone and a heat that was not warmth. It took the concept of a blue-green world orbiting that star, a place of wind and liquid water and crashing waves, and encoded it as a complex, rolling cadence of gravitational waves, a soft, rhythmic pressure that felt like being gently rocked in a cradle of spacetime. It described a supernova, not as a flash, but as a single, devastating crescendo that warped the perceived distance between the pylons for a breathtaking moment before resolving back into harmony, leaving a lingering taste like rust and sorrow.

It did not show the Silt-Shifters the universe. It allowed them to *feel* it, in their own language.

Across the great black basin, the Shifters did not move, but something within them stirred. Their slow, accreted consciousness, which had only ever known the interior of the basin, was suddenly touched by an externality. It was not a threat. It was not a call to leave. It was a piece of art. It was the discovery of color for a being that had only known texture.

The concepts, alien and incomprehensible, settled into their quantum-state minds not as facts or information, but as aesthetics. The translated light of a nebula became a feeling of immense, slow grace. The spin of a pulsar became a frantic, joyful rhythm. The vast, cold emptiness between galaxies became a profound bass note that resonated with their own deep quiet.

They were still Silt-Shifters. They were still vast, slow, mountain-like beings flowing across a plain of black mud, grazing on fungal mats in a world without light. Their physical existence was unchanged. But now, their inner world—the symphony they perceived—was infinitely richer. Their non-linear thoughts now contained echoes of alien starlight and the memory of a wave crashing on a shore they would never see.

The song lasted for what might have been a day, or a decade. Time was pliable here. Then, as gently as it began, it faded. The pure, silent clarity withdrew from the foundational hum, leaving it as it had been before—a steady, bone-deep resonance.

But it was not the same. Woven into the hum now, forever, was a faint and complex overtone. A ghost of the song. An echo of the gift.

The benevolent power had looked upon the preserve of impossibility and its children. It had not judged them incomplete or broken. It had seen them as they were, and had done the only wise and benevolent thing there was to do.

It had told them they were not alone, and that the universe, in its infinite variety, was beautiful. And then, it left them in peace, in their perfect, absolute, and now subtly richer, darkness.
