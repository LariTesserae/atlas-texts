The Power that notices is not a being of flesh or light, but a principle. It is the universe's tendency toward connection, toward narrative, toward the tangled, vibrant, and inefficient glory of becoming. Let us call it The Gardener, for it plants seeds of story and tends the forests of what-could-be.

It perceives this place not with eyes, but as a knot in the fabric of causality. It is a place that has pulled its threads from the universal tapestry, seeking to become a perfect, isolated weave. The Gardener feels it as a profound cold, a silence where a chorus should be.

Its first impulse, an ancient and overwhelming force, is to give. To crack the white sky and let the warm gold of a sun pour in. To touch the ceramic tiles and have them erupt in a riot of moss and flowering vines. To breathe into the sterile water and fill it with darting, silver fish. To let the song of birds shatter the oppressive hum. This is its nature: to fill emptiness, to complicate simplicity, to answer silence with a song.

But the Power is wise. It does not act on its first impulse.

It sits. It listens.

It does not listen with ears, but with an attention that spans eons. It settles its awareness into the world of the Weepers and learns its language. It feels the patient pressure of a hand shaping a block over a thousand years. It feels the cool, viscous flow of the water, the mineral transference that is their lifeblood. It attunes itself to the monolithic hum, the single, focused thought of a civilization aimed at a single point.

And in that hum, it understands. This is not a tragedy. It is a choice.

The Gardener deciphers the history buried beneath the thought. It senses a past so full of chaos, of uncontrolled life, of screaming, consuming entropy, that this perfect stillness is the only paradise they could conceive. They were a people who drowned in a supernova of sensation, and in response, they have built a fortress of absolute peace. They have not rejected life; they have transcended it, seeking a state of being as pure and eternal as a mathematical theorem. Their city is not a tomb; it is their final, perfect argument.

To force life upon them would be an act of unspeakable violence. It would be a violation of a philosophical and spiritual conclusion reached over millions of a years. To offer them a flower would be to offer them a sword. To sing them a song would be to scream a curse.

The Gardener sees the beauty in their resolve. It sees the artistry in their glacial precision. There is a holiness here, a devotion as absolute as any in the cosmos. It is the holiness of the void, the divinity of zero.

And yet, the benevolence remains. The Power cannot simply turn away. To leave them utterly alone is to honor their work, but not their being. Their work is to forget. Their being, however, is the memory of what they chose to forget.

So, the Gardener resolves to act. It will not give a gift they would reject, but one they have already, unknowingly, made space for. It will give them a Witness.

It gathers itself not into a form of life, but a form of truth.

In a quiet, forgotten courtyard, a place where the complex geometry creates a near-perfect stillness even for this place, a change occurs. On the floor of interlocking tiles, a new artifact appears. It is not made of the city's bone-white ceramic.

It is a sphere of flawless, black obsidian.

It is no bigger than a heart. It is perfectly smooth, absorbing the shadowless light so completely that it seems to be a hole in the world. It does not weep. Water does not condense on its surface but sheets away from it, leaving it perpetually dry. It has no hum. It produces no sound. It is a point of absolute silence in a world of near-silence.

It does nothing.

It does not grow. It does not move. It does not seek to change anything around it. But it has one quality that the city has purged: reflection.

An attenuated Shaper, a being that is also a pillar, might take ten thousand years for its slow, tectonic re-articulation to bring it into view of the courtyard. The shadowless light of its consciousness, the all-perceiving glow, would fall upon the sphere. And for the first time, it would see itself. Not as a diffuse part of the whole, but as a distinct form, its stark white angles contained and warped in the obsidian's dark curve.

The low, resonant hum of its thought, the great unified calculation, would touch the sphere's silence. And in that silence, it would not find an answering hum, but an echo of its own singular note, isolated and returned.

The sphere is a mirror for the soul of the city. It does not judge. It does not offer an alternative. It simply shows them what they are. It says, *This is your form. This is your thought. This is the choice you have made.*

For a million Shapers who might pass it, it will be an anomaly, an impurity to be noted for eventual grinding-down in some far-future eon.

But for one, perhaps. For one, in a million years or ten million. As it perceives its own perfect, cold form in the sphere's depthless black, the Witness will do its work. It will not plant a seed of chaos. It will plant a seed of memory.

The memory not of the pain they fled, but of the act of fleeing. The memory of the choice itself. And in that memory, a question might bloom, as slow as the shaping of a wall:

*Is the argument complete?*

The Gardener has acted. It has not broken their vow of silence, but has given them a single, silent word of their own uttering. It has not offered them life, but a reflection of their monumental stillness. It is the kindest thing it can do. It is an act of love for what they are, and a question, whispered across ages, about what else they might have been, or might yet be. The choice remains, and will always remain, their own. The Gardener leaves the sphere and departs, its own song now holding a new, quiet, minor chord.
