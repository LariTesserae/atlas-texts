The power arrives not as a celestial ship or a descending figure, but as a change in the quality of attention. It is a focus, a lens of immense and ancient consciousness settling upon the emerald basin of The Weft. For a time, perhaps a cycle of seasons or the lifetime of a Weft-kin, it simply is. It listens.

It tastes the condensing air and understands the contract of moisture. It feels the frantic, high-frequency vibration of the upper canopies, the hummingbird metabolism of life and death, growth and decay. It maps the frantic scuttling of the beetle swarms, the percussive calls of the feathered climbers, the unending symphony of water. It feels the desperate, constant industry of the Weft-kin, their darting movements a form of prayer, their obsidian blades a form of worship.

And beneath all that, it sinks its awareness deeper. Past the riot of the present, into the ancient, petrified pillars. It feels the slow, deep time trapped in the woody stone, the millennia of patient cultivation that formed these bones. It feels the cool, silent reverence the Weft-kin have for this foundation. It understands.

The central tension of their existence is not a flaw. The frantic dance upon the stage of permanence is not a state of suffering to be alleviated, but a form of completeness. The perpetual managed crisis is the source of their purpose. To “fix” it—to still the chaos, to grant them mastery, to make the ephemeral permanent—would be to kill them more surely than any drying wind. It would be to break the dancer's legs and command them to be happy in their stillness.

A lesser power, merely benevolent, might offer them better tools, a way to eradicate the beetles, a shield against the endless need to repair. It would see a problem and offer a solution. But this power is wise. It sees a balance, a perfect, dynamic equilibrium, and sees that the only worthy gift is one that does not seek to change, but to deepen. The only gift is one of recognition.

And so, it acts.

Its action is not a gesture of construction, but of resonance. It will not add a single new thing to The Weft. It will simply touch the very oldest part of it, the foundational pillars, the bones of their world.

It infuses them with a song.

It is not a song for the ears; the Weft is too loud for such a frail thing. It is a vibration, a resonance so deep and slow it is felt rather than heard. It is a hum that burrows into the core of the petrified wood, a single, sustained note of pure empathy that now underlies everything.

The Weft-kin feel it first as a subtle change. When one of them, weary from a day of weaving, presses a hand to the ancient, gnarled bark in their gesture of communion, they feel something new. Beneath the familiar cold solidity, there is a warmth. A thrum. It feels like the slow, deep breathing of a sleeping giant. The pillars, once just a symbol of permanence, now feel… present. Aware.

The song does two things.

First, it subtly reinforces the deep structure. The resonance is a harmonic frequency that strengthens the petrified wood at a molecular level. It doesn't make it indestructible—loss must remain part of the cycle—but it makes the bones of the Weft just a fraction more resilient. A pillar that might have cracked under a beetle swarm now holds for another season. The frantic work of the Weft-kin is not removed, but the foundation upon which they dance is made ever so slightly more sure. The crisis is not averted, but the moments of calm between the crises are lengthened by a breath, by a heartbeat.

Second, and more profoundly, the song becomes a repository. The power links the resonance of the pillars to the "Giving Cradles." When a Weft-kin's body is returned to the Weft, their life is not merely reduced to nutrient and humus. Now, as the body decomposes, the essence of that life—the feeling of their hands weaving a vine, the joy of tasting a fresh fungus, the quiet companionship of sleeping in a tangled pile, the specific way their tail curled in thought—is absorbed into the song. Their memory, their experience, their small, frantic life becomes a new thread in the eternal vibration.

The Weft-kin have no words for this, but they feel it. The reverence they felt for the bones of their world deepens into a conversation. They press their hands to the pillars and feel not just the past, but the living presence of all who came before. The ancestors are no longer just an abstract concept of recycling; they are the hum beneath their feet, the warmth in the wood. They are the song.

The power changes nothing, and everything. The Weft-kin still scurry. Their lives are still a frantic ballet of creation against an overwhelming tide of chaos. They still sleep in short bursts, work ceaselessly, and accept loss as a constant companion.

But now, they dance to a silent music. Their frantic, tireless work is no longer just a reaction to crisis; it is an answer to the song. They are not merely surviving; they are participating in an anthem that they themselves, life by life, death by death, are composing.

The benevolent and wise power, having given its gift, does not leave. It simply becomes part of the listening. It watches the frantic gardeners tending their chaotic world, and it hears, in the deep and resonant hum of the pillars, their inimitable and perfect reply.
