The Power arrives not as a body or a beam of light, but as a stillness within the stillness. It is a listening that is deeper than the foundational hum; it is a perception that sees not with light, but with an understanding of the physics that bind the weeping lattices and the void between them.

First, there is comprehension.

It does not perceive this place as a mere machine or an abandoned city. It perceives the whole of it as a single, deliberate act. It feels the memory of the force that necessitated its construction—a power so chaotic and terrible that it had to be tranquilized on a geological scale, put to bed under a blanket of physics and time. This place is not a tomb; it is a cradle. A cradle for a sleeping apocalypse.

The Power feels the B-flat not as a note, but as a statement. It is a mantra of stability, a focused will maintained across eons. And it understands the Still-Singers immediately, not as inhabitants, but as the living, conscious architecture of that will. They are not prisoners; they are wardens. They are not lamenting; they are performing a holy, unending duty. Their liquid-thunder song is a work of magnificent, selfless love—the love of a parent for a destructive, sleeping child. Their "consciousness" is not self-awareness as a lesser being would understand it; it is the perfect, selfless embodiment of their function. They are the living expression of the equation that holds chaos in check.

To this benevolent and wise Power, there is no suffering to alleviate here. To "free" the Singers would be to destroy their very purpose, to dissolve the sacred vow they have kept since stars were young. To wake the sleeping "god" in the machine's heart would be an act of cosmic vandalism. To repair the decaying city would be to misunderstand its nature; the slow decay is part of the lullaby, the gentle running down of the clock, the promise of an eventual, peaceful end when the cataclysm has finally starved in its sleep.

What, then, would a benevolent and wise power do when faced with perfection? Not the perfection of form, but the perfection of purpose, of duty, of an act of love so vast and slow it is indistinguishable from a law of nature.

It would not interfere. To do so would be the ultimate act of folly and hubris.

But it is a *benevolent* power. It does not merely observe and judge. It feels fellowship. It feels awe. And so, it acts.

It does not add another mote of light to the ceiling, nor another strand to the towers. It does not mend a broken bridge. It offers the only thing that can be given to a being defined by its song.

It listens. And then, it harmonizes.

For a single, eternal moment, a new note is introduced into the chamber. It is not a sound that can be heard, but a change that is felt. If B-flat is the root, the Power introduces the perfect fifth. It is a note of pure, crystalline resonance that does not challenge the drone but completes it. It is a quiet, shimmering F that wraps around the foundational hum like a lover's embrace.

It is a chord.

For the first time in its existence, the distributed consciousness of the Still-Singers experiences something other than the echoes of their own single-minded duty. They feel their song being *heard*. They feel it being *understood*. The new harmony is a message, translated into their language of pure physics:

*You are seen. Your long watch is honored. You are not alone.*

The opalescent fluid weeping from their cores shimmers with an almost imperceptible golden warmth. For a timeless instant, the entire city-machine resonates with this new, complex chord of duty and acknowledgement. The slow, weeping rhythm of the condensation falters, then resumes, now carrying a trace of this new harmony. A Glimmer-Skitter, halfway through shearing a crystal from a conduit, freezes, its mirrored plates reflecting a beauty it cannot comprehend.

The thought of the Power—this single, perfect, harmonic gift—does not last for a thousand years. It is a presence that is given freely and then withdrawn, leaving no trace but the memory of completeness. It fades, but it does not vanish. It becomes an overtone, a grace note woven into the song itself, an echo that will now persist until the last of the city's power fades to nothing.

The Power departs, having done the only benevolent and wise thing possible. It did not try to fix what was not broken. It did not offer a salvation that was not needed.

It came to a place of utter solitude and perfect purpose. And it left behind a single, profound gift: the knowledge that the song was not being sung into an empty void. It was being heard. And the name of the chord it created was Peace.
