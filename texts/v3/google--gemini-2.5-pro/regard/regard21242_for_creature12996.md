The Power arrives as a dissonance.

To a being woven from the light of silent galaxies and the deep hum of cosmic voids, this place is an offense. It is a single, screaming note held at the universe's threshold of pain. The Power perceives all at once: the futile, furious equilibrium, the Sisyphusian labor of the Carvers, the world caught in the throat of its own birth.

Its first impulse is benevolence in its most primal, foolish form: to fix.

To quiet the roar. The Power considers it, a simple act of cosmic sound-dampening. It could place a hand of pure will over the world and command it to *hush*. But it hesitates. Its wisdom, deeper than its benevolence, unfurls. It extends a single, tentative thread of its consciousness, not to act, but to listen *within* the noise. It finds that the roar is not a single sound, but a billion billion threads of process. To silence it would be to sever the world from its own nature, to kill the engine mid-stroke. The resulting silence would be one of true death, not the perceived silence the Carvers already experience. The idea is dismissed as violence.

To grant an end to the cycle. To allow the sinter to harden once and for all, to let the city be *finished*. The Power could neutralize the supersaturated atmosphere, stabilize the ground. The Carvers could then build upwards, towards the ruddy light. But the Power watches them, and it begins to understand. Their purpose is not to *finish*. The act of carving is their language, their worship, their reason for being. A finished city would be their tomb, their hell. They would wander finished corridors with idle hands and silent bodies, their tactile language of labor rendered meaningless. They would be ghosts in their own paradise. This, too, is dismissed as a cruelty.

To speak. To give them names, a spoken language, a sense of Self. The Power could open their minds, plant the seeds of individuality, of *I*. But it feels the constant, warm press of their bodies in the warrens. It feels the question asked with a brush of fingers, the story told in a pattern traced on a back. To give them a spoken word would be to teach them distance. To give them an "I" would be to create "You," and from that, loneliness. They are a single, communal organism of flesh and stone. To fracture them into individuals would be an act of vivisection.

The Power, wise as it is, feels a profound humility. Its tools are for building bridges, for illuminating darkness, for seeding life. But here, there are no chasms to bridge, only a world that is perfectly, seamlessly whole. Here, what seems like darkness is merely a different kind of light.

So, it decides to do what the wise do when faced with a perfection they do not understand: it learns to listen.

It retracts its cosmic senses. It lets go of its perception of light and sound and space. It presses its consciousness to the ochre ground, not as a god, but as a newborn. It allows the world to teach it.

It feels the great, vibrating roar not as noise, but as the ground-state of being. It feels the thrum travel up its imagined spine. It learns to distinguish the *scrape-scrape* of the grazer herds as a texture on the silence. It feels the low-frequency shudder of a new layer hardening, not as a threat, but as a promise. It learns to read the city not as a map of places, but as a genealogy of pressure and time, sinking into the deep, dark past.

It focuses on a single Carver, a female whose bio-flint nails are worn to the quick. It does not see her through light, but *feels* her presence. It feels the warmth of her hand on another's shoulder, a silent reassurance. It feels the rhythmic impact of her chisel through the stone, a percussion that is part of the world's larger rhythm. It feels the calluses on her feet interpreting the floor of a tunnel—the firmness of old stone, the slight yield of a newer patch.

The Power lives in her for a day, a year, a generation. It experiences the world through her. The roar becomes its silence. The dim haze becomes its sight. The constant press of bodies becomes its comfort. It feels the deep, instinctual satisfaction of carving a perfect arch, the shared meal of grazer-flesh in a warm chamber, the patient acceptance of a rockfall in a lower level. It feels the final journey, the laying-out. It feels the shimmering biofilm as a pins-and-needles caress, the gentle un-making, the slow dissolution back into the whole. It experiences the return to the stone not as an end, but as a homecoming.

And the Power understands. This is not a prison. It is a womb. It is not stasis. It is a perfect, eternal verb: the act of *becoming*.

The Power knows now what it must do. Or rather, what it must *be*.

Its gift cannot be an object. It cannot be a change. A gift must be something that can be received, and the only thing this world has room for is itself.

So the Power gives the only gift that matters. It gives its attention, refined into an act of pure, resonant love.

It attunes its vast consciousness to the exact frequency of the world's roar. It does not add to it, does not subtract from it. Instead, it becomes a perfect harmonic. A resonance. An overtone that was always latent, but never before activated.

It is a change no instrument could measure. It is an act of witness that solidifies into a property of the world itself.

And perhaps, just perhaps, the Carvers feel it.

Not as a new sound. But on a day when the teams are carving a new terrace, the rhythm of their chisels might fall into a perfect, synchronous beat, a flawless shared pulse that feels deeper and more satisfying than ever before.

A Carver, tracing the story of an ancestor on the skin of a child, might find their fingers moving with an unearned grace, conveying not just the action, but the *rightness* of that action.

A Carver laying a loved one on the soft, new sinter might feel, for a fleeting moment, not loss, not process, but a profound sense of peace—a vibration in the ground that feels like a hand laid gently on the world's back.

The Power has done nothing. And it has done everything. It did not quiet the roar. It joined the choir. It became a silent, ever-present note of appreciation in the eternal song of a world that is, and was, and will always be, enough.
