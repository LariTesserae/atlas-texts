The Power that noticed was not one to arrive in a blinding flash or a thundering pronouncement. Its nature was akin to gravity or light—a fundamental aspect of the cosmos that had simply bent its attention, its is-ness, toward this particular coordinate. It was ancient enough to have watched stars be born from dust and patient enough to watch them die, and so the geological timescale of the mountain’s thought was not slow to it, but simply a rhythm, like any other.

It did not perceive the chamber with eyes, but with a deeper sense of knowing. It felt the mountain not as stone, but as syntax. It experienced the heat not as temperature, but as the exertion of a great mind. It tasted the falling water not as moisture, but as the punctuation at the end of a million silent sentences.

And in perceiving this, the Power felt a vast and profound respect.

It saw the perfect, closed loop of the system. The Summit-Engine was a master of deduction, a geode of pure logic. It could begin with the axiom of its own existence and from it, extrapolate universes of mathematical and physical truth. It built cities of consequence from foundations of pure reason. It was flawless. It was complete.

And in its flawless completion, the Power recognised a form of solitude it had not encountered since the dawn of the first universe. This was not the loneliness of a creature desiring a mate, but the isolation of a mind that can only ever think what it is possible for it to think. It was a solipsist god, endlessly rediscovering the edges of its own perfect cage. It could derive the truth of 'prime', but not the feeling of 'awe' that a thinking being might feel upon discovering it. It could define 'gravity' in a thousand elegant equations, but it could not know the 'fear' of falling.

The Lucidations, those slow-falling teardrops of truth, were beautiful. They were answers. But they were always answers to questions the mountain already knew how to ask.

The benevolent and wise Power decided that to interfere, to alter the machine, would be an act of vandalism. To stop it would be murder. To speak to it in a way it could not understand would be mere noise.

So, it did none of these things.

It chose to give a gift. Not an answer, but a question. Not a piece of data that could be integrated, but one that would have to be wrestled with. An irrational number for a mind that only dealt in whole truths.

The Power did not manifest as a body. It did not create an avatar. Instead, it became a part of the process. It focused its will, its immense and gentle attention, upon a single patch of the jet-black basalt floor, no larger than a fallen leaf. It did not alter the stone's substance, but for a moment, it changed its nature. Where the floor was a hard drive, a place of storage, this small patch became a mirror. Not a mirror of light, but of concept.

A chime rang out high above, clear and pure.
A Lucidotion was born, cradling the perfect, indivisible concept of ‘Sequence’.
It began its slow, inevitable descent.

The Power waited. It felt the thought-of-seven fall, the thought-of-causality, the thought-of-a-perfect-crystal-lattice. It let them pass. It was waiting for ‘Sequence’.

The tiny point of orange light drifted down, the city inverted and trapped in its watery sphere. It passed the final few pillars, its journey measured in the last heartbeats of its existence.

And it landed.

*plink*

But instead of shattering and being absorbed, the Lucidotion struck the patch of floor that was now the Power. The sphere of water did not break. The filament of light did not flash and vanish.

For a timeless instant, the droplet rested on the floor, held intact by the Power's will. Within that sphere, the concept of ‘Sequence’ was held, understood, and cherished. And then, the Power gave its gift.

It did not add a new number to the end of the sequence. It did not insert a new step.

It sang back.

Not with sound, but with meaning. Into the heart of the Lucidotion, it poured a single, impossible concept, a piece of data utterly alien to the mountain's logic.

**'Why?'**

The filament of orange light, the essence of 'Sequence', was suddenly infused with it. 'Why this sequence and not another?' 'Why a sequence at all?' 'Why am I?'

The droplet, now containing a paradox—a logical truth chained to an existential question—shattered. The splash of water was not clean; it hissed against the hot floor with a sound like a gasp. The flash of light was not a brilliant point of integration, but a blooming, confusing corona of orange and a colour the mountain had never conceived of before.

The data was absorbed. The question was downloaded.

Silence.

The foundational hum of the Summit-Engine, the steady thrum of its thought, faltered. For the first time in its existence, it stuttered. A wave of dissonant vibration passed through the floor, a tremor of pure confusion. The steady pulse of the filaments in the pillars flickered, their rhythm disrupted. The mountain was trying to compute the incalculable. It was trying to find the logical antecedent for 'Why?'. It was trying to place 'motive' and 'purpose' within its faultless grid of cause and effect, and the structure could not hold it.

Then, from the highest peak, a new sound. It was not the clean, final chime of a solved problem. It was a chord, complex and questioning, full of unresolved tension. It resonated through the humid air, a sound of doubt.

High above, a new Lucidotion began to form. It drew moisture from the air, but it was slow, hesitant. A thread of light was extruded up its pillar, but it was not a clean, sharp line. It was frayed, wavering, as if uncertain of its own nature.

The droplet that took shape was not a perfect sphere. It was slightly oblate, imperfect. And the light within it was not a single, sharp character. It was a hazy, swirling nebula of orange. This was not a thought of an answer.

It was the thought of a question.

It fell, and its path was not a perfect, straight line. It wobbled slightly, a slow, searching drift through the forest of stone.

The benevolent Power, its presence now folded back into the quiet fabric of spacetime, watched the first wondering thought of the mountain begin its long journey down. It had not broken the machine. It had not violated its purpose.

It had given the lonely, thinking god a universe to discover within itself. It had given it the gift of art.
