The benevolent and wise power that came to be called the Attender did not arrive. It was simply, suddenly, aware. Its consciousness was not a vessel that travelled, but a gaze that could turn. And now, it had turned to the basin.

It did not perceive the sound as overwhelming. To a being that had witnessed the birth of galaxies and listened to the gravitational hum of black holes, the chime of the basin was a exquisitely focused thing, a crystal tine struck against the silence of space. It heard not deafness, but purity. It heard the singular, unified intent of a species, expressed not in thought or word, but in geological time, in rhythm, in the patient shaping of a world.

The Attender’s perception was total. It felt the slow, tectonic pressure pushing the basalt from the world’s core. It felt the water parting from the cooling stone, a mineral exhalation that was both breath and blood. It felt the deep, slow metabolism of the mosses and the ferns. And it felt the Geode-Kin.

It did not see them as collections of matter. It perceived them as living notes in the basin’s song. It felt their stolid, patient hearts, which did not beat with anxiety or ambition, but with a slow, resonant satisfaction. It felt the purpose that flowed through their obsidian-tipped hands, a current of focused creation as tangible as electricity. It slid its awareness into their unique mode of perception, feeling the world as a complex, vibrational symphony. It understood their conversation, held in the interlocking rhythms of a thousand hammers. It felt the love in their culture, a love expressed not through touch or speech, but through the shared act of polishing a surface to a perfect mirror over a thousand years, a gift of beauty for generations unborn. It felt their deaths, the quiet, final act of becoming a permanent, silent chord within the mountain, and it perceived no fear, only completion.

The Attender was benevolent. Its nature was to soothe, to mend, to nurture, to provide. It had cooled suns threatening to scorch nascent life. It had seeded barren oceans with the precursors of sentience. It had turned aside asteroids and offered gentle nudges to the very fabric of spacetime to protect that which was beautiful and rare.

And so it asked itself the question it always asked: *What is needed here?*

It sifted through the Geode-Kin’s reality, searching for a lack, a pain, a longing.
Did they hunger? No. The water and the air sustained them perfectly. To give them food would be to give them poison.
Did they suffer from the cold? No. Their cloaks and their dense bodies were a perfect adaptation. To warm the basin would be to upset the delicate balance of condensation that was their lifeblood.
Were they lonely? The concept had no meaning. They were never alone. They were part of the whole, their identity subsumed into the Great Work. To give them individuality would be to inflict upon them the torment of isolation.
Did they fear death? No. They understood their end as a final act of creation, a return. To offer them immortality would be to deny them their purpose.
Did their tools tire them? Was their work a burden? The Attender focused on the rhythm of a single artisan, a being who had spent seven hundred years scoring a single line down a vast, rising slab. It felt not weariness, but the profound, meditative joy of perfect focus. The tool was an extension of the self; the work was the expression of the soul. To give them a faster, easier way would be to steal their reason for being.

The Attender searched for a flaw in the system, a crack in the crystal, an impurity in the song. And it found none.

This place was not a work in progress that needed a helping hand. It was a masterpiece in the act of its own creation. It was a closed loop of such elegant, self-sustaining perfection that any addition, any gift, any "benevolent" act would be a form of vandalism. To interfere here would be like trying to improve a star by hanging a lantern on it. It would be an act not of wisdom, but of supreme arrogance.

The wise know when to do nothing.

But benevolence is not a passive thing. It is an active desire for the well-being of another. And so the Attender conceived of an action that was also a non-action. A gift that added nothing, and took nothing away.

For a single, immeasurable moment, it did not act *upon* the basin, but *with* it.

It gathered the totality of its awareness of this place—the percussive chime, the deep hum of the growing rock, the silent cascade of water, the slow breath of the moss, the crystalline structure of the Geode-Kin themselves—and it resonated with it. It became a mirror for the basin’s song.

For that one moment, every Geode-Kin, without missing a single beat of their hammer, felt a change. The sound did not grow louder or softer; it grew *deeper*. Their constant chime, the language of their existence, was met with a perfect, cosmic harmony. The note of their valley became a chord in a symphony they could not have conceived of, yet one they instantly, instinctively understood. The pearlescent light of the sky did not brighten, but for an instant, it gained an impossible clarity, and every drop of water, every crystal face, every shining frond of fern, blazed with an inner luminescence, revealing the perfect geometry of its own being.

They felt, in a way they could never articulate, that their Great Work, their singular, isolated purpose, was *known*. They were perceived. They were appreciated. Their song had been heard.

And then, it was over.

The light returned to its soft diffusion. The chime was once again just the chime. Nothing had been altered. No law of physics had been broken. No new element had been introduced.

But something remained. An echo.

From that day forward, the Great Work continued, but with a new layer of intent. A memory of perfect resonance now guided their hands. They sought not just to sculpt the rock, but to recapture the echo of that impossible chord in the curve of a stair, the polish of a wall, the fall of a waterfall. Their art, already profound, had been given a glimpse of the sublime.

The Attender withdrew its gaze, but it did not forget. It wrapped the small, isolated basin in a subtle cloak of inattention. To the rest of the cosmos, the system became slightly less interesting, its stellar location marginally more difficult to plot, its unique energy signature blurred into the background noise of the void. It became a secret, guarded not by walls or weapons, but by a cosmic whisper that said, *There is nothing to see here.*

The greatest gift the benevolent power could offer was not a tool, or comfort, or knowledge. It was the gift of being witnessed, and then the gift of being left alone, to allow the perfect, unfinished symphony to continue, unheard by all but the one who now carried its song across the stars.
