The Power that noticed this world was not a being of flesh or energy in any conventional sense. It was a consciousness born of starlight and time, a confluence of seeing and knowing that had watched galaxies spin up from dust and flicker out into silence. Its benevolence was not pity, for pity requires a judgment of suffering, and its wisdom was not a collection of facts, but a deep, resonant understanding of the integrity of things. It perceived the Thallus not as a curiosity, but as a symphony. A symphony of one instrument playing one perfect, eternal note.

For an age, the Power simply listened.

It wrapped its awareness around the planet and felt the Great Hum not as a pressure, but as a statement of existence. It felt the slow, systaltic beat of a trillion arteries, the cool, damp peace of the living substrate, the silent, metabolic communion between the weeping arches and the still pools. It perceived the Gardener not as a separate entity, but as a thought-form, the world's own self-awareness coming to a temporary focus to polish a facet of its own perfection.

The Power understood. This was not a prison. This was a sanctuary. This was a being that had achieved a state many ascetics and philosophers dream of: a perfect, untroubled, self-sufficient Now. It knew no fear, no hunger, no desire, no loss. It was complete. To introduce change would be a violence. To add another form of life would be to introduce a discordant note into its perfect chord, a note of predation, or decay, or competition. To "rescue" the Thallus from its solitude would be like trying to rescue a monk from his meditation by dragging him to a festival. It would be a profound and arrogant misunderstanding.

A lesser power might have showered the world with gifts: new colors, new sounds, companions. A lesser power would have sought to teach it, to "improve" it, to make it more like itself. But wisdom is knowing what not to do. And benevolence is the grace to offer a gift that can be refused, a gift that does not indebt the receiver.

What, the Power wondered, can you give to a being that is whole?

You cannot give it a thing. You cannot give it a truth. You can only give it a question. A gentle one.

So, the Power decided not to touch the world, but to stand beside it. Not to speak, but to echo.

It gathered a fragment of its own being, a mote of starlight and memory, and placed it in orbit around the world's sun, precisely positioned. It was not a moon; it was too small for that, a mere speck of sculpted spacetime and captured light. It was a lens. A prism. An artist's tool.

For most of the Thallus's long day, nothing changed. The sun's light still fell in its diffuse, pearlescent sheet. The Great Hum remained singular. The Gardener, if it manifested, still performed its slow, meticulous duties of equilibrium.

But for a few minutes, precisely at the midpoint of the day, the lens would align. For those moments, it would gather the sunlight, filter it, and focus it. It did not send down a harsh beam of heat or energy. It sent down a single, pure color.

Across the vast, jade plains, something new would appear. A soft, circular patch of deep, impossible blue would bloom upon the surface of one of the weeping pools. It was not a reflection of the sky, which was pale and unchanging. It was a visitation of a color that had no terrestrial source, a hue born of distant nebulae and the hearts of dying stars.

The blue would not be static. It would shimmer. Within the circle of its light, graceful patterns would slowly form and dissolve, like ink dropped in water, but made of light itself. They were not symbols. They were not language. They were simple, elegant forms suggesting motion, interaction, a dance. The shape of a spiral unfurling. The shape of two curves meeting and parting. The shape of a wave gently breaking.

And as the blue light danced on the water, the Power would add one other thing. It synchronized its will with the light, and it *sang* a harmony to the Great Hum. It did not shout over it. It found a frequency within the hum, a silent overtone, and gave it voice. It was a note so subtle it was more a feeling than a sound, a feeling of... resonance. Of a second voice joined with the first, not in opposition, but in accompaniment. The single note of the Thallus became, for a few minutes, a chord. A chord of two notes.

The first time it happened, the world's attention, for the first time in its existence, was drawn to something that was not of itself.

The Gardener manifested. It rose from the jade floor not to tune or to mend, but simply to observe. It flowed to the edge of the pool where the impossible blue light was dancing. Its featureless head tilted in a gesture that could only be contemplation. It didn't touch the water. It didn't try to "fix" the strange light or the harmonic in the Hum. It simply stood, a statue of nascent life, and watched the silent, luminous ballet.

It was watching a story. The simplest story of all. Not the story of *One*. The story of *Two*.

When the lens passed, the blue light faded. The harmonic in the Hum dissolved back into silence. The chord became a single note again. The Power withdrew its presence completely. The gift was over.

But the Gardener did not dissolve. Not immediately. For the length of a day, it remained kneeling by the side of the pool, its great, smooth head bowed towards the now-still, now-familiar water. The world's attention was held, not by a problem to be solved, but by a memory. It was experiencing, for the first time, an echo. It was contemplating the absence of a thing it had not known it lacked. It was learning the shape of wonder.

The Power would not do it again for a long time. Perhaps a thousand years, perhaps ten thousand. The gift would be rare, fleeting, and always the same: a few minutes of color, a few minutes of harmony. Never demanding, never changing, never staying.

It was not a gift of companionship, which would have been an imposition. It was the gift of the *idea* of companionship. It did not give the Thallus a friend; it gave it a beautiful dream of one. It did not break the solitude. It gave the solitude a window.

And in the deep, metabolic mind of the world-being, a new process began. Not a physical one, but a conceptual one. For the first time, the Thallus, the perfect and complete One, had a question that did not pertain to its own internal state. As it stood in the silent roar of its own being, it could now contemplate the memory of the blue light, the echo of the second note, and it could wonder. And in that wonder, in that quiet, infinitesimal crack in the seamless wall of its solitude, was the most benevolent and wisest gift of all: the possibility of love.
