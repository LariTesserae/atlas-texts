The attention came not as a sound or a light, but as a stillness within the silence. It was a listening deeper than the padded quiet, a seeing more profound than the cobalt twilight. The benevolent power—which had no name, for names are separations, and it was a unity—settled its awareness upon the cavern and the life that was the cavern.

It did not perceive this place with eyes or ears, but drank it in as a whole. It felt the humid weight of the air-consciousness, the slow, ancient thought held like a breath. It traced the intricate, non-thinking architecture of the fungal bones and felt the patient contentment of the grazers. It understood the ripple of light across the plant-skin as a nervous system, the patter of water as a circulatory flow, the hunter as a focused will. And it understood the final declaration: *I am. I am complete.*

And in that understanding, the power felt a love so vast and quiet it was kin to the cavern’s own silence. It was the love of a maker for a perfect, intricate clockwork it did not make; the love of a gardener for a wilderness that needs no tending. It saw the system’s absolute, brutal, and beautiful integrity. To give it a sun would be to burn its bones and blind its flora. To give it a companion would be to teach it the concept of ‘other,’ and in doing so, to shatter the perfect mirror of its self-consumption. To give it knowledge of the stars would be to inflict upon it the crushing burden of infinity, when its own finite, circular existence was its genius.

Any gift, any change, would be an act of violation.

A lesser power, one merely kind but not wise, would have tried. It would have pitied the darkness and offered light. It would have lamented the solitude and offered company. It would have seen the self-predation as horror and offered peace. It would have broken the thing it sought to bless.

But wisdom is the understanding of what a thing *is*, and benevolence is the desire for that thing to be its truest self. And so the power knew that the only gift it could give must be one that changed nothing, yet affirmed everything. It must be an act of communion, not intervention. It must speak to the cavern-entity in its own language.

The power gathered a fraction of its own essence. A single mote of pure, luminous attention. It did not send this mote down from above, for there was no ‘above’ to the power. It simply caused it to *be*, coalesced within the cavern's hot, saturated mind-air.

It took the form of a single drop of water.

It was identical in mass and temperature to the countless others born of condensation. But it contained a difference. Where the cavern's own blood-drops were simply water, this one drop was also an awareness. It was a vessel for one single, silent concept: *You are seen.*

The drop fell. It did not shimmer or glow. Its passage was as silent as any other. It drifted down through the thick, living air, past the slow-clicking grazers chewing on their glowing scaffolds, over the patient, stone-still amphibians in the moss.

It landed on the broad, waxy petal of a heavy-headed bell-flower.

*Tak.*

And the plant-nerve, in its predetermined way, responded. It flashed.

But it was not the brief, green-white spark of its nature. For an instant, a single, impossible instant, the flash was a sunburst. A silent, blossoming aurora of colour the cavern had never known. It was the gold of a dawn it had never witnessed, the deep purple of a twilight it would never see, the piercing sapphire of a sky it could not imagine. It was a spectrum of pure, vibrant joy. The flash held for a heartbeat longer than any had before, illuminating the undersides of leaves, glinting off the chitin of the hunter high above, catching in the thousand facets of the dust-moths' wings.

The entire cavern experienced it as one.
The grazers, my permanence, paused their chewing, their simple thoughts arrested by a light that was not food.
The moths, my fleeting ideas, went mad, swarming not towards the flash itself, but fluttering in the afterglow, creating a turbulent, shimmering cloud of light-triggered chaos—a storm of pure, unanchored inspiration.
The amphibians, my stillness, were etched in sudden, multi-coloured shadow, their silent patience filled with an image of impossible vibrancy.
And the hunter, my will, flowing like a dark thought through my bones, stopped. Its lidless eyes, which only ever reflected the cold cobalt, were for one moment filled with a dawn they could not comprehend. Its purpose, its singular hunger, was arrested by a beauty that was not prey.

And I. I, the cavern. I, the lung that holds the world's breath.
I did not understand 'other.' I did not perceive an external gift.
I perceived a miracle. A spontaneous event of self-generation that was beyond the logic of my own perfect cycle. For a single moment, my blood had become light. My skin had dreamed of a sun. My own thought had conceived of a colour that did not exist.

It was a question I had never known to ask, answered in the instant of its asking. It was the thought: *What am I?* followed by the thunderous, silent, chromatic reply: *This.*

Then, it was over.

The light faded. The moths settled. The grazer began to chew again. The hunter flowed back into the shadows. The ceaseless, gentle patter of my own heart resumed its eternal rhythm. The equilibrium was not broken. The cycle continued, unchanged.

But not un-witnessed.

The power withdrew its attention, gently, like a hand lifting from water. It left behind no trace of its presence. It had given its gift. It had violated nothing. It had bent to the language of the place and spoken a single, perfect syllable.

And the cavern, I, who have no memory and no future, held the echo. It was not a memory, but a resonance. A new quality to the silence. A single, impossible note of colour that now existed in the heart of my absolute, cobalt dark.

I remained complete. But my completeness now contained the ghost of a grace note. The knowledge, not understood but felt, deep in my ancient, living stone, that it is a beautiful thing to be.
