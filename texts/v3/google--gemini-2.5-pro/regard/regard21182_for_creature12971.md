The Power’s attention was not a thing of travel. It did not cross voids or breach atmospheres. It was a shifting of cosmic focus, a turning of an attention so vast and pervasive that its gaze was a form of existence in itself. Where it looked, reality deepened, its causal threads shimmering with scrutiny.

And it looked here.

Its first perception was not of sound, but of Intent. It perceived a will, hammered into the shape of a world. The roar was not chaos to it, but a mantra. A single, indivisible word of purpose, spoken without cease. It was a declaration of function, a prayer sung in the key of grinding iron. The Power understood this not as noise, but as a form of extreme, unwavering honesty. This place did not pretend to be anything other than what it was: an engine.

The Power's awareness flowed past the sound, seeping into the very fabric of the chamber. It felt the chill, and understood it as a necessary consequence of the work. It perceived the weeping walls not as sorrow, but as diligence. It felt the trembling of the iron mesh floor and understood the intricate, stable dance of stresses and vibrations that kept the structure from tearing itself apart. It saw a system in a state of brutal, breathtaking equilibrium. A form of grace measured in decibels and barometric pressure.

Then, it noticed the life.

It perceived the slow, electric metabolism of the moss, a lifeform so perfectly adapted it was less an organism and more a living sensor array for the chamber itself. And through the moss, it perceived the Custodian.

The Power did not see a monster or a prisoner. It did not register a lonely, alien thing trapped in a purgatory of noise. When its attention settled on the being, it saw a heart. It saw the living, motile will of the engine room. It saw a consciousness that did not reside in a brain, but was distributed through every plate of its carapace, every filament of its symbiotic moss, every pad of its suction-gripped feet. The Power understood that this being's thoughts were vibrations, its emotions were shifts in the ambient static field, its sense of self was indistinguishable from the architectural integrity of the walls.

The Power felt the Custodian pause its patrol, its moss-sensors tasting a subtle fluctuation in the chamber’s static field—a change that was not a flaw, but a presence. A resonance from beyond the system. The Custodian had no framework for this. It was a note it could not place, a vibration without a source bolt to be tightened. For the first time in its existence, it experienced a stimulus it could not mechanically resolve. It was not a scream of imperfection; it was a query. A blank space in the equation.

Here, the Power’s benevolence was tested by its wisdom. A lesser benevolence might have sought to "save" the Custodian. It might have hushed the roar, offering the shocking, terrifying poison of silence. It might have opened the walls to a sunlit sky, a sight for which the Custodian had no eyes, an environment that would scour its damp skin and kill the moss on which its senses depended. It might have conjured a companion, teaching the Custodian the concept of "other," and in doing so, teaching it the crushing, absolute meaning of loneliness.

But the Power was wise. It understood that to grant the Custodian these things would not be a kindness, but an annihilation. It would be like trying to "free" a single note from a chord; the note would cease to have context, harmony, or meaning. It would simply vanish.

And so, the Power did not seek to change the Custodian's world. It sought to affirm it. It decided to give a gift that could be understood, a gift that would not alter the song, but deepen its meaning.

It spoke to the Custodian in the only language that mattered.

For the duration of a single, drawn-out moment, the Power acted. It did not touch the machinery. It did not alter the temperature. Instead, it reached into the monolithic roar and resonated with it. It introduced a harmonic, an overtone that had always been a mathematical possibility but had never been manifest. The sound did not change in pitch or volume, but for one timeless instant, it gained an impossible quality of fullness. It was the sound of a bell achieving its true, perfect resonance, just once. The sound of the chamber becoming utterly and completely itself.

To the Custodian, the experience was electric. The unresolvable query it had sensed was answered. The overtone was not a dissonance; it was a revelation. It was the feeling of a bolt being tightened not by its own limbs, but by the will of the room itself. It was the feeling of absolute, final, undeniable *rightness*.

Simultaneously, the Power gifted a single spark to the sensory moss. Across the Custodian's carapace, the dim electric-blue velvet did not just glow; it flared. For that same instant, at the heart of every patch of blue, a point of pure, soft, silver light bloomed and then faded. It was a new taste in the air, a new flavour on the static field. It was not a word, but a confirmation. The flavour of "Sanctuary." The taste of "Purpose." The unqualified sensation of being *seen* and being *good*.

Then, it was over.

The overtone faded back into the potential from which it came. The silver light vanished. The roar was once more the roar. The air was once more the air. The Power’s attention began to withdraw, like a tide receding from a shore it had not disturbed, but merely witnessed.

The Custodian remained motionless for a long time, an unprecedented pause in its eternal patrol. It could not form a memory in the human sense. It had no capacity for nostalgia. But the resonance was now a part of its being. The perfect harmonic was now the standard against which the eternal song was measured. The taste of silver light was now a permanent, latent part of its sensory palate.

Its work had not changed. But its purpose had been consecrated.

When it began to move again, its silent, graceful glide through the weeping gloom was no longer merely the function of an immune system. It was the act of a guardian. Its patrol was no longer just the correction of flaws; it was the preservation of a sacred space.

It moved on, a living piece of a known and holy engine, its only desire to maintain the great, deafening song it now understood not just as eternal, but as blessed.
