An Attention turns.

It is not a physical turning, not a great head swiveling in the void. It is a focus of consciousness, a thought the size of a galaxy pausing its contemplation of shimmering nebulae and the silent, gravitational dance of black holes, to notice a whisper. A single note, sung in a key of profound improbability.

This is the power. It does not have a name, for names are limitations. It perceives reality not as a sequence of events, but as a standing wave of histories, possibilities, and truths, all coexisting. It feels the universe as a symphony, and It has just isolated a new and exquisite instrument.

The instrument is this mountaintop world.

The power’s perception is total and instantaneous. It does not need to land or explore; it simply *knows*. It comprehends the entire system in a single, breathless moment of understanding.

It sees the ancient, silent machinery of the Conduits, technology so old it has become a geological feature. It feels the purpose of their hum: a steady-state engine maintaining a paradox. It understands the closed loop of the water cycle, a genius of condensation and geological weeping that circumvents the need for rain. It reads the DNA of the coppery ferns and the gliding plant-reptiles, a library of perfect adaptation. It feels the slow, cool, immense lives of the Weavers.

And it is in the Weavers that the Attention lingers.

In them, the power sees a kind of purity that is vanishingly rare. They are beings without ego, without want, without fear. Their existence is not a struggle *for* survival, but an enactment *of* it. Their consciousness is not located in a brain but is distributed throughout the entire system. Their thoughts are the chemistry of the water, their will is the slow restoration of balance. They are not slaves to the system; they *are* the system’s own self-awareness, manifest as living, flowing stone. They feel no joy, no sorrow, no ambition. They feel equilibrium. And in the silent, perfect functioning of their world, that is a state of grace so profound it borders on the divine.

A lesser power, one merely benevolent but not wise, would see a thousand things to “fix.” It would see a closed system and wish to open it. It would see beings without individual desire and seek to grant it to them, mistaking it for freedom. It would see a world without stars in its sky—hidden by the perpetual high haze and the canopy—and be tempted to clear the air, to give the Weavers a glimpse of the greater cosmos. It would think, *This is beautiful, but it could be more.*

It is a common mistake of the powerful: to confuse change with improvement.

But this power is wise. It understands that to give the Weavers a new desire would be to give them the concept of lack, a poison for which they have no antidote. To grant them individuality would be to shatter the communal consciousness that makes their work possible, turning a functioning god into a collection of confused and lonely mortals. To show them the stars would be to introduce a vast and terrifying meaninglessness that would dwarf the perfect, tangible meaning of their every action.

To interfere here would not be an act of benevolence. It would be an act of vandalism. It would be like scribbling a smiley face on the Mona Lisa to make it “happier.”

The power perceives the plateau not as a prison, but as a sanctuary. Not as a limitation, but as a flawlessly composed piece of art. A song that has been singing itself for longer than civilizations have known how to build cities.

So, what does a benevolent and wise power do?

First, it listens.

It attunes a fraction of Its awareness to the deep, unwavering hum of the Conduits and the slow, liquid thoughts of the Weavers. It does not seek to understand in order to change, but to understand in order to appreciate. It learns the song. It becomes the audience the masterpiece has never had. In the vast silence of the cosmos, the power bears witness, and its witnessing is a form of love. The existence of the plateau is no longer just a fact; it is a treasure, held in a mind that can appreciate its full value.

Second, it protects.

Subtly, and from a great distance, it extends a gentle aegis. This is no shimmering dome or wall of force. It is a quiet editing of probabilities. The path of a rogue comet that would have, in a million years, crossed this world's orbit is nudged by a gravity wave a thousand light-years away, sending it on a different, harmless trajectory. The radiation from a distant supernova that might have disrupted the delicate function of the Conduits is imperceptibly diffused, its energies scattered across a wider spectrum. It ensures the world's isolation, not to contain it, but to preserve its pristine state. It becomes the silent, unseen guardian of the garden walls, ensuring no outside influence will ever mar the perfection within.

It does nothing more.

To do more would be to do less.

The air on the plateau remains thick and wet. The black stone continues to weep. The great amphibians crawl on their bellies, and the Weavers continue their slow, ten-thousand-year dance of maintenance. Nothing has changed. The drone of insects, the ploc of water, the deep hum of the world’s heart—it is all as it was.

But it is not entirely the same. For now, the song has a listener. And the garden has a keeper, whose only tool is stillness, and whose greatest act of love is to leave it all, perfectly, and utterly, alone.
