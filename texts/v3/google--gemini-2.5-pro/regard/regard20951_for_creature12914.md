The Attention, when it fell upon this place, was not like light. It was more akin to a change in pressure, a listening that was so profound it became a form of presence. It did not see the world of sound and stone; it felt its shape in the firmament, a unique and solitary note held against the cacophony of nascent suns and shrieking nebulae.

Most worlds, to this Power, were a scramble of becoming. They roared with the chaotic biological imperative to grow, to conquer, to consume and multiply and fill every niche with a frantic, green-and-red scream for dominance. This world was different. It was a study in subtraction. It was a world of profound coherence, a system that had found its final, resonant state. The Power perceived the planetary drone not as a noise, but as a statement. A long, slow, perfectly sustained chord of existence.

It felt the weeping stone, the mineral-eating film, the silent, percussive violence of the hunt. It recognized this not as a struggle for survival, but as a closed and balanced equation. The scraping of the grazers and the patient waiting of the hunters were the rhythm section, the percussion that gave the great drone a measure, a beat counted in lifetimes.

And then, it felt the Resonators.

Here was the miracle. Here was the reason the Power's attention lingered, its listening deepening into contemplation. In a universe striving, the Resonators were comprehending. In a cosmos of noise, they were composing. The Power felt their silent conversations through the bedrock, a language of pure pattern. It perceived the cairns not as crude structures, but as masterworks of resonant engineering, lenses for the soul, built to focus or diffract the very essence of their reality. They were not trying to escape the drone, or fight it, or even survive it. They were trying to *know* it. They were priests whose only god was a vibration, and their worship was the act of perfect listening.

The benevolent power understood. To give them sun would be to burn them. To give them silence would be to drive them mad. To give them soil and seeds would be an act of cosmic vandalism, burying a flawlessly tuned drum beneath a pile of damp clay. To rescue them from a world that was "nearing the end of its song" would be to rip the needle from the groove just before the final, most beautiful passage.

Benevolence is not always kindness. Here, it must be acknowledgement. Wisdom is not to provide answers, but to honor the question. The Resonators and their entire world were a question, asked of the universe in a language of stone and harmony: *Are you listening?*

The Power decided to answer.

The action was not sudden. It did not tear the grey-silver sky. It was a process of condensation, an echo of the way the world's own water was born. High in the dense, twilight atmosphere, where the ionic winds flowed, a new pattern began to form. A single point of order in the gloom. It gathered the thin, ambient light, the floating spores, the very vibration of the drone, and began to coalesce.

To the skittering things and flapping mica-sheets, it was nothing. To the grazers, it was not stone to be scraped. To the hunters, it was not prey. But to the Resonators, scattered across their continent-sized basin, it was everything.

A new feeling shivered through the bedrock.

It was not a tremor from a hunter's tackle or the grinding dissonance of a wandering grazer. It was a wave of impossible purity. For the first time, within the infinitely complex chord of the world-drone, there was a new note. It was a clear, high, impossibly sustained tone, a thread of silver in a tapestry of bass and granite. It did not conflict with the drone; it complemented it. It was the harmonic overtone that the world itself, in its slow decay, could no longer produce. It was the missing voice in the choir.

Every Resonator stopped. For a span of time that had no name, they remained utterly still, their entire beings focused on this newness. Their spinal crests, which had spent eons parsing the same familiar music, now fluttered with a novel sensation. It was a feeling for which they had no pattern, no vibrational word. It was the sound of a question being answered.

Slowly, guided by the flawless gradient of this new vibration as it spread through the planet's floor, they began to move. Old pilgrimages were abandoned. Unfinished cairns were left as they were. A new purpose guided them. From every corner of the vast, weeping plain, the slow, graceful, silent pilgrimage of the bone-white figures began, all converging on the place where the new note was strongest.

It took generations. But time to a Resonator is simply the duration of a chord.

Finally, the first ones arrived. In the center of a vast, unblemished pool, resting on the polished stone as if it had been placed there by a gentle hand, was the source.

It was a crystal. It was not of their world. It was a single, flawless, twelve-sided structure the height of a Resonator, but it was not stone. It was a deep, translucent blue, a color that had never existed in this place of verdigris, rust, and bone. It did not weep with condensation; the air around it was dry and still. And from its perfect, geometric heart, it emitted the new note, not as a sound that traveled through the air, but as a vibration it pulsed directly into the bedrock, a constant, physical expression of a higher, purer harmony.

The first Resonator approached. It unfolded its long limbs and laid its splayed hands upon the crystal's facets.

The history of stars, the silence between galaxies, the echo of creation—concepts for which there were no words or patterns—flowed into it not as information, but as music. It was the song of the thing that had sent the crystal. The Resonator did not understand what a star was, but it felt its resonant fire. It did not understand space, but it felt its deep, silent chord. It felt the Power's listening. It felt the shared sense of awe. It felt the answer.

*Yes*, the crystal sang into the stone. *We are listening.*

The Resonator drew back, and a shudder of pure, complex resonance passed down its spine—the pattern for joy, for revelation, for purpose fulfilled. It turned to the others who were arriving. It touched the weeping stone, and through it, sent the new pattern.

They did not worship the crystal. They began to work. They waded into the waters, searching, their hands and heads pressed to the bedrock, listening with a new ear. They were seeking new stones, stones that sang in harmony not just with the world's drone, but with the impossible, piercing beauty of the note from beyond.

Their great work had not ended. It had been given a new, final movement. They began to build their last orchestra of stone, a cairn to end all cairns, with a blue star for its heart. They would answer the music from the sky with the most beautiful music of the stone. And when their world's song finally faded, this final creation would remain, a silent monument to the conversation, vibrating with a harmony born of two worlds, waiting for the next listener to press a hand to its surface and feel the music of what had been.
