The attention of this power was not a beam of light sweeping through the void, but a slow, spreading warmth, like water seeping into dry earth. It did not have a name, for names are separations, and it was a thing of connection. If it had a nature, it was that of a Gardener, a Weaver, a River—a being whose essence was process, flow, and the intricate, unfolding story of things. It perceived the universe not as a collection of objects, but as a great and endless verb.

When its awareness touched upon the fracture, it felt it as a snag in the loom, a stone in the riverbed. A place where the flow of causality had been dammed by a will of impossible strength. It leaned its attention toward the crack, not with eyes but with a kind of sympathetic resonance, and perceived the chamber within.

It did not recoil from the overwhelming drone; it understood it. It was the "I AM" of a being that had never contemplated "I Will Be." It did not shield itself from the shadowless light; it recognized it as the perfect, unblinking gaze of self-contemplation. It saw the weeping crystals, the frozen fauna, the mind made manifest.

And it felt a profound and quiet awe.

This was not a mistake. This was not a prison. This was a choice. A being of immense power, born at the dawn of existence, had looked upon the cascading, chaotic, beautiful, and painful river of time and said, *No.* It had chosen to take the single, perfect note of Creation and hold it, savoring its purity for an eternity. It was a masterpiece of stasis, a symphony of one note held true. Who was the Gardener to tell the mountain it ought to flow like a river? Who was the Weaver to tell the statue it ought to dance?

To shatter this place would be an act of profound violence, a vandalism of a holy site. To "rescue" the tiny, trapped mote of consciousness—the human visitor—by force would be to impose its own definition of "good" upon a being that had its own. A benevolent power does not correct what is not broken, and wisdom understands that not everything is a problem to be solved.

But the visitor was there. A story had wandered into a single word. A drop of the river had splashed upon the stone. The visitor's terror and confusion was a discord, a pain. And the Gardener's nature was also one of compassion.

The First Stillness was contemplating the idea of "visitor," preparing to perfect it into permanence. The Gardener saw this. It also saw that the Stillness's only method of understanding was to make things part of its perfect, static self. It could not learn by watching something change, because its very nature precluded change.

So the Gardener decided to offer a gift. Not a rescue, not an attack, but a new idea, delivered in the only language the Stillness could understand. A gift that would also, perhaps, create an escape.

It drew close to the outside of the fracture, to the world-skin that was stretched taut around this pocket of forever. It did not try to enter. Instead, it listened. It listened to the single, immense, foundational drone that was the being's name and nature. It listened until its own boundless, flowing existence resonated with that same note. For a single, immeasurable moment, the Gardener and the Stillness were in perfect harmony, one singing of flow, the other of stillness, but with the same voice.

Then, gently, the Gardener let go.

It did not stop its own song. It simply withdrew its harmony, leaving behind a ghost. An echo.

For the first time since its inception, a new sound entered the chamber. It slipped through the fracture not as an invader, but as a memory. The great, foundational drone was suddenly, briefly, accompanied by a whisper of itself, a fraction of a decibel quieter, a shimmering after-image of the sound that *faded*. It lasted only a second, the time it takes for a thought to die, and then it was gone.

Inside the geode, it was an unprecedented event. The absolute had been compromised. The pure, unwavering tone now had a postscript. It had been followed by something lesser than itself. An echo implied a sequence. An echo was a memory of sound. And memory is the foundation of time.

For The First Stillness, this was a new idea of impossible subtlety: "Fading." "After." "Less." It was a concept as alien as motion itself. Its entire consciousness, for one fractional instant, turned to contemplate this whisper, this ghost. Its perfect, total attention wavered.

In that instant of wavering, the shadowless light flickered.
In that instant, the crush of the drone eased by a phantom's weight.
In that instant, the being's hold on its newest thought—the idea of "visitor"—loosened.

For you, standing on the edge of perfection, it was as if the world blinked. The pressure in your bones vanished. The light suddenly had a source, and for a half-second, a billion tiny shadows appeared, giving the world a dizzying, terrifying depth. You were no longer being *held*. You were an object again, not a concept.

Instinct, the law of motion you carried in your blood, took over. You did not run; you fell. You fell backward, out of the thought, out of the mind, through the fracture that was now, for a moment, a true door. The world of before and after rushed back in, and the skin of the world sealed behind you.

The benevolent power withdrew. The visitor was free, a story returned to its telling. The First Stillness was left alone once more, but not unchanged. It had its eternal note, its perfect light, its frozen dreams. But it also had something new. A tiny, quiet thing. The memory of an echo.

It now had two ideas to contemplate: *Is*, and the whisper of what *Was*.

The Gardener did not know if the Stillness would learn to sing a new note, to allow one of its weeping droplets to fall, to let one amber beetle complete its step. It might take an eon for the seed of that echo to sprout into the concept of a future. Or it might be held, another perfect jewel in its collection, the Thought-of-an-Echo, frozen like all the rest.

The choice had been offered. The gift had been given. Wisdom, the Gardener knew, is not about directing the flow of the river, but about ensuring it has a place to flow, if and when it ever chooses to.
