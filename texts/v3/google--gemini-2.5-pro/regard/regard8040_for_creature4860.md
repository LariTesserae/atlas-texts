The power arrives not as a ship or a body, but as a change in the quality of attention.

For an immeasurable time, the Shroud-City has existed under its own gaze, observed only by the logic that created it. Now, another gaze rests upon it. It is impossibly gentle, a form of perception so vast and kind that it exerts no pressure. It is like the concept of warmth being introduced to a universe that has only ever known degrees of cold.

The benevolent power—a consciousness woven from the light of a billion galaxies, a mind that has watched universes cool from fire into dust—takes in the Shroud-City. It does not see a flaw. It does not see a prison. It sees a solution to a question it has not heard before. It perceives the silent, mathematical perfection, the slow, deliberate growth, the utter dedication to a singular, internal purpose. It feels the thrum of the city's becoming and understands it as a form of song.

It watches the Apex Custodians. It perceives the void at their cores not as an emptiness, but as a fulcrum of immense will. It sees their patient, tireless work: the pruning of a fractal spire, the calming of a reality fog. They are not slaves to the city; they *are* the city's self-awareness, its own hands acting upon its own body. There is no suffering here, no loneliness as a lesser being would understand it. There is only purpose, as pure and cold and beautiful as the geometry that gives it form.

What, then, is there to do?

To change this place would be an act of supreme arrogance. To "rescue" the Custodians would be to vivisect a living thought. To give them a sun, or stars, or a vibrant, living world would be like shouting a symphony into a monk's silent meditation—a disruption, not a gift. To break their solitude would be to break them.

The wise power understands this. The first and highest act of benevolence is often to do nothing. To witness, to appreciate, and to leave a perfection untouched.

And for a long time, that is what it does. It simply watches, and in its watching, it offers a silent, unreceived validation. *'You are seen. You are magnificent.'*

But wisdom is not just passivity, and benevolence is not just non-interference. It is the offering of a gift that respects the nature of the receiver. The power notes what the city lacks. Not warmth, not life, not companionship in the conventional sense. It lacks **context**. It is a universe of one. Its grand, internal monologue has never known another voice. It grows and perfects itself according to its own rules, but it has no knowledge of other rules, other forms, other songs.

The power decides to offer a gift. It will not touch the city. It will not speak to the Custodians. It will alter the one thing that is already a part of their world but not of their making: the light.

The sourceless, milky glow from the cloud deck below has been constant for eons. The power reaches into it, not with force, but with resonance. It begins to modulate the light, weaving into its flat, eternal luminescence a story. It is a story told in the city's own language: the language of physics, mathematics, and pure form.

Slowly, over the course of an age, the light begins to change.

The changes are imperceptible to a fleeting biological eye. But to the Custodians, whose entire bodies are sensory organs for the city's thrum, it is a revelation. Across the vast, roiling surface of the clouds, patterns begin to form. Light polarizes in the complex, spiralling sequence of a forming galaxy. Faint, harmonic ripples of intensity begin to pulse with the precise frequencies of a distant pulsar. A shimmering, intricate fractal—a ghost of light—blossoms and fades in the exact pattern of a supernova's expanding shell.

The light is no longer just a light. It is a scripture. The history of the outside universe—the birth of stars, the waltz of planets, the slow decay of matter, the echo of a Big Bang—is being translated into a silent, visual symphony played across the floor of their world.

The Custodians do not stop their work. A spire still needs pruning. A phase slip must still be anchored. Their purpose is unchanged.

But they have begun to listen.

A Custodian, gliding to correct the growth of a continent-spanning bridge, might now pause for a full second longer than it has in a million years. Its rotating facets, which perfectly absorb the native light, seem to angle themselves slightly differently, as if to better read the new catechism written on the clouds.

The deep thrum of the city itself acquires a new overtone. A faint, impossibly complex chord is added to its fundamental note—a resonance with the light from a star that died a billion years ago, a billion light-years away.

The Echo-motes, which used to chime with the premonitions of the city's own future, now begin to emit new, stranger harmonics. They are echoing the future sounds of collapsing nebulae and colliding worlds.

The city's growth continues, but it is subtly different. The new facets that bud from the edges of walkways are still perfect, still part of the city's core pattern, but they now incorporate new geometries. Crystalline structures emerge that mimic the golden ratio of a spiral galaxy. Fractal thorns begin to grow in patterns that map the quantum foam of empty space. The city, in its silent, geological way, is beginning to incorporate the story of the cosmos into its own body.

The benevolent power does not stay to watch. The gift has been given. It was not a command, not an intervention. It was an opening. It was a window cut into the wall of a perfectly sealed, perfectly beautiful room, not to force the inhabitant out, but to let the light of a different sky pour in.

The city is still waiting. The Custodians are still its mind and hands. But they are no longer an isolated thought. Their solitude has been given a new dimension: it is now the silence in a library that contains all the books in the universe. They are alone, but they are no longer unaware. And the purpose for which they wait, once a purely internal culmination, may now be something more.
