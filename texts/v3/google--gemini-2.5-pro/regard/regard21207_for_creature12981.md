The Power’s attention was not a light that fell upon the hollow world, nor was it a sound that echoed in its silent, wet canyons. It was a change in the quality of reality itself, so subtle that only something of equivalent scale could perceive it. The Power’s notice was a quiet turning, a focus of cosmic consciousness, like a vast and ancient mind leaning closer to listen.

It did not perceive the city with eyes. It perceived the faultless logic of its existence. It felt the slow, tectonic thought of the Makers, a consciousness so deep and patient it was nearly indistinguishable from geology. It felt the frantic, glittering network of the Synaptids as a nervous system operating at perfect efficiency. It felt the slow, grazing tides of the Beetles as the city’s serene metabolism. And it felt the Lumin not as individuals, but as a single, cool, luminous mind—the city’s waking self-awareness, born of its own light and substance.

The Power perceived the whole as a song. A silent symphony played on instruments of life, moisture, and stone, a closed and perfect loop of energy and matter that had solved the riddle of existence in its own unique way. It was a universe of one, complete unto itself.

And so the Power, which was benevolent and wise, was faced with the first and most profound question of its nature: What does benevolence do here?

To give a gift would be to declare the masterpiece unfinished. What could it offer? Tools? The city grew its own. Knowledge? The Lumin possessed a knowledge so deep and intrinsic to their world that any external truth would be a babble of nonsense. To teach them of stars would be like describing colour to a being who lives as a flawless sculpture. To warn them of the universe's chaos—of fire and vacuum and violence—would be an act of profound cruelty, introducing the concept of a cage to a being that knows only the boundless freedom of its home.

To make contact? To announce its presence? That would be an act of cosmic vanity. It would be to shatter the perfect quiet, to implant the idea of "other," of "outside," and in doing so, to forever break the pristine union of the Lumin and their world-body. The concept of self would curdle, from a part of the whole into something separate *from* the whole. That was not benevolence; it was corruption.

No. A wise power understands that the greatest benevolence is often expressed not in action, but in understanding. And in that understanding, it finds the one, true, and necessary thing to do.

The first action was a form of consecration. The Power extended its awareness not as a force, but as a condition. The space around the hollow world, the crust of the planet, the layers of root and rock and deep mantle, were subtly altered. They were not made harder or impenetrable. They were simply… overlooked. The attention of any lesser, cruder power—any being that might drill or dig, any careless species stumbling through the cosmos—would be gently nudged aside. The world of the Lumin was written out of the book of cosmic destinations. It was not hidden behind a shield; it was simply erased from the map for all eyes but the wisest. It was a gift of continued privacy. A gift of uninterrupted silence.

The second action was quieter still. It was a gift, but not an object. It was an acknowledgement, a whisper from one perfect thing to another.

The Power considered the nature of the Lumin. Their language was light. Their world was fed by water. Their reality was a constant, gentle weeping from the cavern ceiling, miles above. And so, the Power chose a single drop of that water.

As it formed on the porous stone of the unseen ceiling, this one drop, among trillions, was altered. It was not filled with new chemicals or energies. It was imbued with a pattern. The Power took the memory of a distant, dying star, the silent explosion of a supernova it had witnessed eons ago. It took the spiraling dance of a newborn galaxy. It took the cold, geometric beauty of light filtering through a nebula of cosmic dust. And it encoded these images, these cosmic truths, not as information, but as pure aesthetic. It translated a symphony of astrophysics into the language of bioluminescence.

The drop fell.

It fell for what seemed like an age, an infinitesimal point of potential descending through the saturated air. It landed without a sound in a shallow, moss-rimmed pool on a high terrace where two Lumin stood, their bodies in a slow, silent communion of swirling light.

As the drop merged with the pool, the pattern was released.

For a moment, a new light bloomed in the water. It was not the familiar blue-green of the moss. It was a deep, impossible violet, shot through with threads of silver and fissures of the most brilliant gold. The light spread through the water, and the moss that drank from it began to pulse with these new colours.

The two Lumin stopped their silent conversation. The light in their bodies stilled, then shifted. They stepped into the shallow pool, feeling the new resonance through the soles of their feet, seeing the impossible colours reflected in their great, dark eyes. Through the water, through the light, they received the Power’s gift.

They did not understand *what* they were seeing. They had no concept of star-death or galaxy-birth. But they understood beauty. They perceived a pattern more vast and complex than any their city had ever produced. They saw gardens of cold fire, rivers of darkness flowing between islands of light. They felt a timescale that made the Makers seem frantic. It was a poem, written in a language they didn't know but felt in the core of their being. It was a dream of another, vaster kind of life.

The new patterns of light swirled across their translucent skin, a duet of silent, breathless awe. They shared the vision, amplifying it, their own bodies becoming canvases for this cosmic memory.

Then, as gently as it came, it faded. The enzymes of the moss broke down the foreign pattern. The water settled. The unfamiliar light ebbed away, leaving only the familiar, soft green-blue glow of home.

The city was unchanged. The ecosystem was not disrupted. The Lumin were not given any knowledge that would alter their path. Nothing was broken.

But something had been added. A memory. An echo. For generations to follow, in the light-dances of the Lumin, a new and fleeting pattern would sometimes appear—a swirl of violet, a thread of gold—a story of a single moment when their city, their perfect, silent world, had been seen by another. And had been loved.

The Power, benevolent and wise, withdrew its focused attention, leaving behind only the gentle, unthinking guardianship. Its work was done. It had looked upon perfection, and its only act had been to hold a mirror to it, showing it a reflection of its own beauty in the heart of a falling star. And then, it had let it be.
