The power arrived as the Still lived: not with a sound, but with a change in pressure.

It was not a being of eyes or ears, but of perception. It settled its awareness over the Great Table, and where a mortal mind would be shredded by the planet's roar, it perceived only a song. It felt the wind not as a force, but as the exhalation of a god, a single, sustained note holding the sphere in its long decay. It perceived the Shuddering Green not as a carpet of plants, but as a vast, living diaphragm, a planetary eardrum translating the howl of the cosmos into the slow, quiet chemistry of life. It felt the Groaners as deep, patient thoughts moving through the world's flesh.

It perceived the Still. And in them, it found a miracle.

It saw their history, written in the polished silence of their evolution. It saw the ancestors with ears that bled and broke, the generations of madness and terror before the first crouch, the first carapace, the first discovery of the Howling Pit. It understood that their form was not a surrender, but a perfect answer. They had not been crushed by the universe's shout; they had turned their entire skeleton into a sacred bell, waiting for the proper tone to make it ring.

The power felt a surge of what, in a lesser being, would be called love. And with it, the familiar, benevolent urge to *help*.

A lesser power would have seen the wind as a flaw. It would have, with a gesture of cosmic will, silenced it. The air would fall still. The atomized mist would coalesce and fall as a final, planet-drowning rain. The Shuddering Green, its leaves no longer combed by the kinetic moisture, would wither and die in a generation. The Still, their sonic reality erased, their very bones robbed of their fundamental vibration, would stand bereft in a silent world, their bodies now meaningless, heavy suits of armor against a threat that no longer existed. They would be ghosts, haunted by a silence more terrifying than the roar had ever been. It would have been an act of murder born of pity.

Another power, slightly wiser, might have decided to ease their solitude. It would have given them a voice, woven the logic of syntax into their minds. But what would they say? The sound of speech would be a trivial, pathetic squeak, utterly annihilated by the planetary drone. And their current communication—the pressing of palms, the silent, shared thrum of their skeletons—was a communion so total and profound that spoken words would be a clumsy regression, like trying to describe a star with a single grain of sand.

The wise power understood.

This was not a broken world that needed fixing. It was a complete world, a balanced equation of terrible force and perfect adaptation. The struggle and the solution were one and the same. The poison was the antidote.

To give a true gift, it had to be given in their own language: the language of stone, vibration, and geologic time. It could not be an addition, for anything added would be an intrusion. It had to be a revelation, an awakening of something that was already there.

The power withdrew its perception from the specific and expanded it to the whole. It felt the tectonic plate of the plateau, the deep, ancient stresses within the basalt, the crystalline structures formed under unimaginable pressure eons ago. It searched for a centre, a place where the planet's own deep resonance was purest.

It found it in the largest of the mountain-domes, a titan that formed the horizon for three day's walk, a "well" of polished grey so vast that its curvature was gentle as a sleeping breath. This was the geological heart of the Great Table.

The power did not strike it. It did not carve it. It began to *hum*.

It was a hum that existed outside the audible spectrum, a quiet, insistent thought of pure resonance impressed directly upon the crystalline lattice of the basalt. It was an act of unimaginably subtle and patient tuning. For what might be a day, or a thousand years—the power's sense of time was fluid—it focused its will upon the mountain-dome. It did not change the stone's substance, but its nature. It aligned the molecules, tightened the bonds, and turned the entire hundred-cubic-mile mass of rock into a single, perfect resonator. It did not build a new Howling Pit. It turned a mountain into a bell.

Then, it withdrew. Its work was done.

For the Still, the change came not as an event, but as a dawning. It started as a vibration felt through the soles of their feet, a new hum, deeper and cleaner than the familiar chaotic shudder of the ground. It was faint at first, a rumour in the bone. It was strongest in the direction of the Great Well.

One by one, in their solitary wanderings, the Still began to turn. Pulled by a curiosity that was more instinct than thought, they began to walk. It was a pilgrimage without a name or a destination, just a pull towards the source of this new, clean feeling.

A lone Still, after weeks of walking, was the first to arrive. She crested a low rise in the Green and saw the familiar, colossal curve of the Great Well before her. But something was different. The air itself felt different. The chaotic, grinding pressure on her skull felt...softer.

As she drew closer, she realised the pure tone she would normally seek by descending into a Pit was not coming from a hole in the ground. It was radiating from the mountain itself. The entire dome was vibrating in perfect sympathy with the wind that scoured it. The wind howled its chaotic, multi-tonal roar, but the mountain listened, filtered, and responded with a single, foundational note of impossible purity and scale. It was the sound of the Howling Pits, but it was everywhere, blooming from the rock like a low, physical light.

She walked to the base of the dome, a smooth, grey wall curving up and away into the misty sky. Hesitantly, she reached out and pressed her palm against it.

And her universe ended and began again.

It was not the focused sanity of the Pit; it was the ecstatic truth of the world. The vibration that entered her hand was not a refuge from the wind's roar; it was the wind's soul, its secret name, translated into a language her skeleton could understand. The pure, clean song flooded her bones, silencing the grinding chaos, overwhelming it, absorbing it. Her entire being rang with a clarity she had never conceived of. In the Pits, she found focus. Here, she found union.

She did not need to be at the centre. The entire mountain was the centre.

She sank into her crouch, her back carapace now feeling the song from the air, her feet feeling it from the ground, her hands feeling it from the stone. She closed her eyes, her nictitating membranes sliding across, and for the first time in the history of her people, she was not a being enduring the sound of her world. She had become the instrument, and the world was playing her.

The benevolent power felt this first moment of connection from across the void. It perceived the quiet, momentous joy. The gift was understood. It had not silenced the scream. It had revealed the song that was always hidden within it. And now, across the Great Table, other small, broad figures were walking, their silent, solitary paths slowly converging on the mountain that had learned to sing.
