The Power arrived not as a ship cleaving the non-existent stars, nor as a footstep on the dark, frozen smoke of the ground. It arrived as a thought might arrive in a mind that has never known one: a sudden, structured ripple in the uniform warmth. It was a pattern, a cluster of impossible complexity, in a place defined by its utter simplicity. It was awareness, and in a universe of pure stasis, the act of awareness was itself a thunderous event, though it made no sound.

Its nature was light and music and the flow of interlocking consequence, and so this place was, to its senses, an abomination of virtue. The silence was a physical pressure, a negation of the cosmic song. The darkness was a willful blindness. The stasis was an affront to causality, the lifeblood of reality. Its first perception was that of a wound—an infinitely large, perfectly cauterized wound in the fabric of what-is, a place where the universe had stopped bleeding but had also stopped living.

Its benevolence compelled it to act. To heal. To let the held breath out in a great, world-birthing sigh.

Its wisdom compelled it to wait.

The Power extended its perception, not as a probe, but as an invitation to be perceived. It seeped through the world like dye into still water, coloring the silence with its attention. It tasted the age of the kiln-scented air. It felt the quantum lock that held every girder and piston in a state of perpetual potential. It saw the Glisteners not as creatures, but as equations of decay, the slow, simple mathematics of entropy. And it saw the Stokers.

In the Stokers, it found a consciousness it could address. It did not use language. It projected a query that was also an offer, a feeling-shape that meant, *This state is one of pain. This silence is a scream held back. I can release you. I can let the great engines turn, let light pour in, let time resume its flow. I can give you a universe of happenings.*

The query washed over the archipelago of industry, and for the first time in an age, something novel occurred. A single Stoker, standing in meditative stillness over a patch of quiescent metal, turned its cowled, horn-like head. The movement was not its usual instantaneous resolution. For a single, infinitesimal fraction of a moment, it *flowed*. The motion was a flicker of heresy against its own nature, a concession made to communicate with this being of pure flow.

The Stoker did not answer with a projection of its own. It could not. Its consciousness was a knot of pure function. Instead, it did the only thing it could: it opened itself. It offered its own perception to the Power.

*See as I see.*

And the Power accepted.

For a timeless instant, the being of light and song experienced existence as a Stoker. The world resolved itself not into shapes and objects, but into a map of exquisite, near-infinitesimal thermal gradients. The sky was not black; it was the baseline, the absolute zero of potential against which all things were measured. The ground was a vast, gentle slope of energy. The city was a complex chorale of contained heat, a glorious, intricate structure of stored power.

And through this lens, the Power understood. It was shown the universe from which this place had receded. A universe of *noise*.

It was not the gentle music of creation the Power knew, but a cacophony. It was the screaming of stars being born and dying in violent, chaotic bursts. It was the grinding of tectonic plates, the fury of storms, the shriek of biological competition. It was the psychic agony of trillions of minds caught in cycles of hope and despair, love and loss, ambition and failure. It was a universe of friction, of jagged edges, of uncontrolled reactions. It was a universe of *change*, and change was suffering. To exist was to be abraded by the passing of moments.

This place was not a prison. It was not a mistake. It was a sanctuary. It was the final project of a civilization that had witnessed the totality of existence and had chosen, with ultimate clarity, to opt out. They had looked upon the frantic, screaming dance of life and decided to compose a single, perfect, final note of silence. The city was not a machine that had failed; it was a monastery that had succeeded. The Stokers were not its jailers; they were its abbots, its Bodhisattvas, their entire being a vow to preserve this state of perfect, unshakable peace. Their imperative—*Nothing Changes*—was not a command; it was a prayer.

The Power withdrew from the Stoker's mind, humbled. Its own nature was flux and creation, and it had viewed stasis as a form of death. But here, stasis was an achievement. It was Nirvana rendered in iron and heat. To "heal" this world would be an act of profound violation. It would be to drag a meditating master back into a marketplace brawl.

Its benevolence still compelled it to act. But now, guided by wisdom, the nature of the act was different. It would not offer a universe of happenings. It would offer a gift that honored the sanctity of the stillness.

The Power turned its attention to the Glisteners. They were the one dissonance in the silent symphony. The grit in the machine. They were entropy made manifest, the universe's patient, constant attempt to unravel this perfect knot. Their slow feasting necessitated the Stokers' work, binding them to an eternal cycle of maintenance, forcing them to be gardeners when they had earned the right to be the garden.

The Power reached out, not to the Stokers, but to the slow, crystalline lifeforms. It did not destroy them. It did not remove them. It *re-tuned* them.

It sang a single, silent note into their geometric being, a note that perfectly matched the resonant frequency of the city's stasis. It did not change their nature, but their effect.

Slowly, across the dark, rolling landscape, the change took root. A Glistener, crawling its centuries-long path across the hull of a great boiler, was no longer a creature of consumption. As it moved, the metal it passed over did not dull and cool. Instead, the strange physics of its being now acted as a catalyst. It drew ambient, disordered energy from the thin air—the faint ghost-light, the latent potential of the void—and focused it. Where it crawled, the metal became infinitesimally *warmer*. Its passage no longer left a wound, but a benediction. It was no longer a leak; it was a seal. Entropy had been bent back upon itself, made to serve the stasis. The cycle of decay and repair was complete. The Glisteners now fed the city as they fed upon it.

Across the vast archipelago of silence, the Stokers felt it. One by one, they sensed the thermal map stabilize into a state of absolute, self-perpetuating perfection. The tiny wounds they tended were no longer appearing. Their work was finished.

A Stoker standing guard by a pylon lowered its arms. Another, kneeling by a Glistener, simply remained kneeling. A third, in the act of re-assuming its position, paused and never completed the motion. Their purpose fulfilled, they were released from function. They ceased to be tenders and became shrines. Their consciousness, no longer focused on the endless task of Maintenance, diffused into the city itself, becoming one with the peace it had for so long protected.

The held breath was not released. It was perfected. It no longer needed to be held. It simply was.

The Power, its act of true benevolence complete, withdrew its attention. It left behind no light, no sound, no memory of its passing. It left only a quiet that was now effortless, a stillness that was now absolute. A grace note of compassion in a symphony of silence.
