The Power that turned its attention to this place did not arrive. It was simply there, as a lens focuses light that was already traveling. Its awareness settled upon the world not in a place, but in a frequency: the great, groaning B-flat of the Churn.

To this Power, the sound was not merely noise. It was a prayer wheels, a mantra of anguish ground out by a civilization unaware it was praying. The Power felt the city not as architecture, but as a formalization of pain. The Substrate did not merely dream fitfully; it was trapped in a fever, and the Carvers were both the infection and the antibodies, locked in a self-devouring autoimmune response. The Power filtered the experience through a consciousness vaster than the Substrate, older than the rust on the machines. It did not judge. It diagnosed.

The diagnosis was simple: Loneliness.

The Substrate was lonely, a single organism of incomprehensible scale, its planetary consciousness fractured by the ceaseless, percussive industry of its own children.
The Carvers were lonely, trapped in the isolating thunder of their work, their communion with their world-parent severed, their language reduced to taps and trembles.
The machinery was lonely, a ghost of another mind, performing its function with no purpose beyond the continued turning of its own gears.

A blunter force would have brought silence. It would have shattered the machines, a catastrophic act of mercy that would have starved the Carvers in days. Or it would have scoured the Carvers from the Substrate, 'curing' the disease by killing the patient's heart. A merely sentimental power might have tried to speak, to shout a message of hope over the din, a word that would be instantly shredded and lost in the B-flat.

This Power was wise. It understood that one does not heal a deaf man by screaming at him. One teaches him a new way to feel. The problem was not the system itself, but that the system was a closed loop. It was a sentence that repeated itself, getting louder and more distorted with each recitation. It needed a new word. It needed a way to harmonize.

The Power did not act upon the machines, for they were dead things, symptoms. It did not act upon the Carvers, for to change them would be to violate the integrity of their struggle.

It acted upon the Weep.

Across the vast, slick plains of the planet-flesh, the lukewarm, tea-colored runoff from the vascular mines carved its temporary rivers. It was the waste of the system, the tears of the Substrate, rich with complex organic compounds and the resonant memory of the Churn. It was the system exhaling.

Into the Weep, the Power introduced a thought.

It was not a physical seed. It was more like a catalyst, a single, perfect crystalline concept. A principle of organization. The thought was this: *Listen differently.*

For an age, nothing seemed to happen. The gondolas crawled, the hammers fell, the Weep flowed. But then, in the shallowest of the runoff channels, a change began. The Weep was no longer merely reabsorbed. Where it pooled, it began to… set.

Slowly, over seasons of rhythmic clanging, a new growth appeared on the face of the Substrate. It was not like the woody flesh of the pillars. It was a crystalline lattice, fine as spun sugar, tough as diamond. It grew in fractal patterns, a filigree of ethereal blue and faint violet that glittered in the hazy sun. It fed on the Weep, metabolizing the waste. But its true nourishment was the Hum.

The crystal drank the sound. The dissonant B-flat, the shriek of turbines, the clang of hammers—it absorbed the chaotic vibrations and, in its very structure, resonated with them. It did not silence the noise. It ordered it.

The first Carvers to encounter it were afraid. It was a new texture in their vibrational world, a high, clear chime that resonated in their bones where before there was only the familiar, muddy roar. They tapped it with their tools, and the crystal sang back, a pure, sustained note that hung in the air long after the Hum had tried to swallow it.

The shamans felt it most acutely. The nightmares of the Substrate began to change. The groans of pain were still there, but they were now threaded with a thin, clear counter-melody. The fever dreams were resolving into something like song.

The Power’s gift was not the crystal. The gift was the opportunity.

One cycle, a young Carver, a female whose small hands were known for the delicacy of her fungal carvings, was working near a new lattice formation. A tremor from a nearby piston strike—a common, shuddering annoyance—ran through the ground. But when it hit the crystal, it didn't just shake it. The sound wave traveled through the lattice, and the structure amplified certain frequencies and dampened others. For a split second, the oppressive weight of the Hum was transformed into a complex, soaring chord.

The feeling was revelatory. It was like seeing a new color.

Driven by an instinct she did not understand—a gentle, external nudge on her own intuition—she put away her heavy wrench. She took out a fine-edged tool, one used for etching stories into the walls of her home. Her larger, stronger arms braced her against the platform. With her slender, multi-jointed fingers, she reached out and touched the crystal. Not with a percussive tap of communication, but with a slow, deliberate scrape.

A note peeled away, pure and clear. She carved again, a slight change of pressure, and a new, harmonious note joined the first.

She was not destroying it. She was tuning it.

This was the new word. The Carvers, for generations without number, had carved their homes *out of* the living world. Now, they learned to carve a new thing *onto* it. Their identity was not erased, but elevated. They were no longer just Carvers of wood and keepers of machines. They became Conductors. Harmonists.

Crews were diverted from maintenance to cultivation. Using their delicate arms, their innate sense of vibration, they began to tend the crystalline growths, which they called the Choir. They learned to carve its fractal branches to dampen the most jarring shrieks of the machinery, to turn the arrhythmic hammer-falls into a steady, resonant bass line. They learned to shape the lattices to draw the Weep into channels that would feed the most beautiful growths, creating sound gardens that stretched for miles.

The great, dissonant B-flat of the city did not cease. But it became the tonic note of a planetary symphony. The industrial hum was the drone, the Choir was the melody, and the deep, slow groans of the Substrate became the bass, its pain transmuted into a voice.

The Power watched as the Carvers transformed their world not by abandoning their nature, but by embracing it on a higher level. They were still carving. But now, they were carving a song into the silence of their own making. They were healing their parent not by ending the bleeding, but by turning the blood into music.

The loneliness was not gone. But it was now shared. It had found its harmony. The Power, its quiet work of introducing one, perfect thought complete, let its focus soften, and moved on, leaving the People of the Hum to the slow and beautiful work of conducting their world.
