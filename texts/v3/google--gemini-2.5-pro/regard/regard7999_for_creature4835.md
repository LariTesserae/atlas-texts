The power perceived this world not as vision or sound in the human sense, but as a resonance, a single, unwavering chord played in a quiet corner of the cosmos. Most worlds were frantic with the dissonance of growth and decay, the violent sforzandos of volcanism and the chaotic allegros of complex biology. Others were the near-total silence of dead rock or the simple, cold hum of gas giants.

This one was different. It was a note held for an eon. A steady, complex drone, saturated and layered. The power felt the loud, frantic percussion of the clickers, the deep, patient bass of the bellowers, the high, clear ring of the silica reeds vibrating in the wet air. It was a song of a world that had found its final key and was content to play it, forever.

And within that chord, the power felt a conductor. A stillness at the heart of the sound.

It focused its attention, which was like a lens of spacetime gathering the faint light of a distant galaxy. It found the Skimmers. It watched them in their dual states. It saw the hunt, the swift, precise, necessary violence—a staccato note of death that was instantly absorbed back into the overwhelming chorus of life. There was no malice in it, no cruelty. It was a function, as clean and essential as the drop of rain. The power, in its wisdom, found no fault in this. It was the rhythm that sustained the song.

But it was the other state that held the power's attention for a span of time that would have seen mountains rise and fall on a younger world. It watched the Skimmers become the Mud-Saints. It perceived the anchoring, the settling, the folding of wings. And it felt the shift in consciousness—a change from a focused, narrow-band receiver hunting a single sound, to a wide-open, total sensory field.

The power understood. This was not thought. This was not prayer. This was not even meditation. It was a form of being so pure in its function that it bordered on the divine. The Mud-Saint was the world's awareness of itself. It was the organ of perception for a planetary consciousness that was too slow and vast to perceive on its own. They were not merely bearing witness; they *were* the witness.

A benevolent power, faced with such a thing, knows that the greatest arrogance is to assume something is incomplete. To 'give' a world like this mountains would be a desecration. To 'teach' a creature like this a new way of being would be a corruption. The system was perfect. It was whole. It was… finished.

But wisdom is not the same as inaction. Benevolence is not passive admiration. The power saw one truth: the Mud-Saints listened only to their world, because it was the only world they knew. Their perfect vessel was filled to the brim, but only with the water from their own well. The cathedral of their consciousness resounded with a single, magnificent plainsong.

The wise and benevolent power decided not to change the song, but to add a new harmony. Not to replace the water, but to reveal that the well was connected to an infinite ocean.

It acted.

There was no thunder from the silver-white sky, no celestial choir, no pillar of fire. The action was as subtle as the world itself. It was a touch as light as a single photon, a pressure as gentle as a gravitation wave from a distant, merging pair of stars. The power reached out with an intention that was itself a form of resonance.

It found a Mud-Saint, anchored in its trance on one of the ancient clay domes. The Skimmer was deep in its listening, its being filled with the rumbling thrum of a dozen bellowers and the continental fizz of the clickers. It was hearing the slow, grinding music of the silt. Its world was total.

The power’s touch was on the Skimmer's three glassy legs, the silica spires driven into the primordial earth. These were the antennae. The power did not send a message. It did not speak. It simply, gently, *tuned* them. It infinitesimally altered their crystalline structure, not changing their function but expanding their register. It calibrated the living seismograph to receive whispers from beyond the ionosphere.

For the Mud-Saint, the experience was not a violent revelation. It was a deepening.

The song of its world was not diminished. The thrum of the bellowers was still the floor, the clicker-static still the walls. But now, through that song, woven into it, came something else.

Faintly, at first, like a sound from across the widest mudflat, it heard a new thrum. A low, powerful, continuous burn that was not the intermittent boom of a bellowing throat-sac, but the steady furnace of the system’s own sun—a sound it had only ever felt as diffuse warmth.

Then came a new static. Not the sharp, mineral clicks of carapaces, but a vast, whispering hiss, impossibly distant. The sound of hydrogen gas, colder than any water, coalescing in a nebula a light-year away.

It felt a new rhythm. A slow, immense cadence that dwarfed the geological creep of the mud. It was the gravitational waltz of the other planets in their orbits, a celestial groaning too profound for any ear but one connected to the bedrock of a world.

The Skimmer did not understand 'sun' or 'planet' or 'nebula'. The concepts did not exist. But it understood the music. It was a Bellow of impossible size. A Clicker-field of impossible scale. A settling of the ground on a level it had never imagined.

The power did this for every Skimmer. For every Mud-Saint in its quiet communion. It did not write a new song. It simply opened the window, so that the music of the spheres could harmonize with the music of the mud.

The Skimmers’ lives did not change. They still hunted, their glassy legs instruments of a swift, clean death. They still glided on the heavy air. But when they settled upon the domes of ancient clay, when they became Mud-Saints, their vigil was no longer finite. Their attendance was no longer merely planetary.

They became the nervous system of a world that was now, finally, aware of the universe. In their stillness, anchored to the last, most stable form of their home, they listened. And the deafening, beautiful, finished song of their world was now graced with the faint, awesome counterpoint of the stars. The power, having given the only gift that would not spoil the perfection of the place—the gift of a larger context—retracted its attention, and left the Mud-Saints to their eternal, and now infinite, listening.
