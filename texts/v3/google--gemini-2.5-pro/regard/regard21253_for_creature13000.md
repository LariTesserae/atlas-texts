The attention of the Power was not like light falling on a surface, but like water seeping into stone. It was a slow saturation, a dawning awareness not of a place, but of a pattern. Across the void, where it listened to the silent spinning of galaxies and the birth-cries of stars, it perceived a new note. It was not loud. It was a chord of immense quietness, a harmony of phenomenal patience.

It was a hum. A deep, geothermal pulse overlaid with the fainter, lattice-like resonance of vast, cooling structures. It was a rhythm of cyclical industry, of meticulous maintenance on a geologic timescale. The Power drew its focus, folding space and time not to arrive, but to *attend*.

It perceived the world not with eyes—the unfiltered glare would have been a meaningless shout of energy—but as a symphony of heat and vibration. It felt the searing artery of geothermal flow, the slow, cool weeping of condensed water, the subtle tremor of a million Sinter-Scrapers rasping at their bacterial pastures. And it felt the Sinter-Smiths.

It did not see them as beings separate from their world. It perceived them as the world's most complex and elegant phrasing. They were the melody, the expressive and deliberate thought that rose from the planet's deep hum. It felt their tripod grace, the way they listened to the world through the soles of their feet, reading the stress on a pipe a mile away like a blind man reading braille. It felt their slow, painstaking work, not as labor, but as a form of devotion. They were priests tending a temple that was also a factory. They were the system's own wisdom, manifest.

The Power considered them, and an emotion that had no name but was kin to awe, welled within its boundless consciousness. Here was a life form that did not seek to conquer its environment, but to attune to it. A life that found its highest purpose in maintenance, in balance, in the preservation of the delicate energy gradient that allowed a world to harvest its own sky. They sought neither growth nor expansion, only equilibrium. They were a slow, perfect answer to a question most life never learned to ask.

What would a benevolent and wise power do?

The thought of intervention was an absurdity, a dissonant screech. To alter the heat, to grant them an easier existence, would be to shatter the instrument they played so exquisitely. To give them a new technology would be to offer a child's crayon to a master calligrapher. To reveal itself, to speak in a way that demanded response, would be an act of profound violence—a sudden, deafening noise in a world of sublime silence.

Wisdom dictated that true benevolence, here, was a form of reverence. The greatest gift one could offer such a being was one that did not alter its nature, but affirmed it. A gift that could be received on their own terms, in their own time, through their own senses.

So, the Power acted.

It did not descend. It did not manifest. It *resonated*.

It chose a single, ancient Exchanger, one in a forgotten basin far from the most active conduits. It was a colossal ruin, sagging under its own weight, its great iron heart long silent. Corrosion had eaten it through, and the slow, patient sinter was already halfway up its legs, reclaiming it, turning it from machine back into mountain. The Smiths had long since abandoned it, their seismic senses telling them its structure was a lost cause, a song that had ended.

The Power focused an infinitesimal sliver of its being onto this derelict. And it began to sing.

It was not a song of sound. It was a song of atomic cohesion, a precisely modulated vibration that rippled through the very lattice of the dark metal. It was a harmonic that found the resonant frequency of the iron alloy, not to shatter it, but to soothe it. The vibration was infinitesimally subtle, far below the threshold of any geology, yet it was inexorably persuasive.

Over the course of a standard year—a mere moment for the Smiths—a change began. The rust did not flake away; it was re-absorbed. The microscopic fractures that riddled the primary struts began to heal, the atoms flowing back into their optimal crystalline structure. The process was not magic; it was a perfect, accelerated expression of the planet’s own metallurgy, guided by a flawless understanding of physics. The Exchanger, impossibly, began to straighten. The deep groans of its decay were replaced by a quiet, steady hum of returning integrity.

One day, a century later, a lone Smith on a diagnostic pilgrimage felt it. Its broad, sensitive feet, pressed to the sinter floor, detected a new vibration. A ghost. A note that should not exist from a direction that had been silent for millennia. It was impossibly stable, perfectly harmonic. It did not speak of stress or decay, but of a quiet, powerful wholeness.

Curiosity, a slow and deep emotion for its kind, guided it over the terraces. For decades it walked, until it stood before the resurrected Exchanger. The machine was not new. It still wore its patina of age, but it stood tall and whole, as if it had been repaired by a master. The Smith approached, pressing its manipulators and sensory pores to the metal.

It felt the hum. And within the hum, it felt the *pattern* of the Power's song. It wasn't a message in a translatable language. It was mathematics. It was a geometric proof of stunning elegance, expressed as a vibrational wave-form. It was a statement about the nature of space-time, a piece of cosmic truth given as a gift, embedded in the very structure of the healed iron. It was a work of art in the Smith's own medium: the quiet, physical truth of the world.

The Smith stood there for a decade, listening.

Then, the Power offered a second, even more intimate gift. The condensed water, beginning to weep from the now-functioning fins of the restored Exchanger, was pure. But the Power imbued the water in a single, small cistern at its base with a trace of the same pattern, encoded now not in vibration, but in the fleeting structure of the water molecules themselves.

Eventually, the Smith felt thirst. It moved to the cistern and, as was its custom, drank the pure, sky-wept water.

As the water entered its system, the pattern flooded its consciousness. It was not a voice or an image. It was a moment of perfect, crystalline understanding. A silent revelation. It learned nothing of the Power's nature or origin, for that was irrelevant. It simply *received* the beauty of the truth contained in the song.

The Smith stood, motionless. Then, slowly, over the course of a day, the bioluminescent patterns on its semi-precious hide began to glow. They swirled, shifted, and resolved into a new configuration: a slow, pulsing visual representation of the theorem it had just learned.

It turned from the Exchanger and began its long journey back, its own body now a living broadcast of the gift. Through the crust of the world, it sent a quiet, seismic pulse to its kin: *Come. Listen. The world has sung a new song.*

The Power, its act of reverence complete, withdrew its attention. It had not interfered. It had harmonized. It had seen a quiet, magnificent mind at work, and had left behind a new and beautiful thought for it to contemplate, in its own language, on its own terms, over the course of the coming eons. It was the only gift that wisdom and benevolence permitted. A single, perfect note added to a song of profound silence.
