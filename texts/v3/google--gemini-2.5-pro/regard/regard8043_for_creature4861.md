The attention of such a power is not a gaze, but a listening. It is not an eye that opens in the void, but a stillness that settles upon a place, a momentary quiet in the cosmic symphony that allows a single, distant instrument to be heard. Across the roar of birthing stars and the silence of dead galaxies, the attention of this power caught the faint, layered hum of Bas-en-Haut. It heard the key of water and time, of slow life and patient stone.

Curiosity, for such a being, is a form of gravity. It drew its awareness closer, not as a ship parting the clouds, but as the mist itself grows infinitesimally thicker, as the diffused light carries a resonance it did not have before. The power did not walk the water-carved canyons; it became the water. It did not touch the biocement walls; it became the resonance within them.

It felt what the Weft felt.

It experienced the slow, orgasmic pressure of a new tower’s growth over a thousand years. It felt the tickling gossip of the Basin Skimmers, a frantic, short-lived dance on the skin of a still pool. It experienced the patient, cold hunger of the Chime-Hunter, a state of being so close to non-existence that it was a form of meditation. It knew the terror and release of the Glass-wing Moth impaled on a crystalline needle, a life-spark extinguished so that the stone could continue its motionless vigil. It felt the long, slow, grinding groan of an ancient arch giving way, not as a tragedy, but as an exhalation, a necessary release of tension in the continent-sized body.

It perceived the entire plateau as a single organism. The Weft, its dreaming mind and growing bones. The water, its blood and lymph. The wind, its breath. The sparse fauna, its fleeting thoughts and transient desires. It was a perfect circle of existence, a lifeform that built its own body from its own world, a consciousness that experienced time as a sculptor experiences clay.

The first impulse of a lesser benevolence would be to *give*.

To give sunlight, and in doing so, to burn the delicate moss and boil the sacred pools.
To give colour, and in doing so, to blind the creatures of grey and render the Chime-Hunter's camouflage useless, starving it over centuries.
To give warmth, and in doing so, to disrupt the delicate condensation that was the city’s lifeblood, to silence the symphony of drips.
To give knowledge, and in doing so, to fracture the Weft’s holistic, feeling-based consciousness with the sharp, alien torment of individuality and purpose.

The power perceived the folly of betterment. To add to a thing that is whole is to break it. This creation was not incomplete. It was not waiting for a saviour or a gardener. It was finished. It was a masterpiece, not of design, but of being.

Wisdom tempered benevolence. The highest act of love, it concluded, was not to change, but to appreciate. The greatest gift it could offer was not an alteration, but an acknowledgement.

So, it did nothing. And then, it did something.

Its awareness retreated from the water and the wind, condensing its vast attention into a single point of focus. It searched the megastructure not for a flaw to correct, but for a space that could hold a secret. Deep, deep below the dripping spires, in a section of the city so ancient that the Weft within had long since turned to solid, unfeeling stone, it found a chamber. It was a geode, a hollow sphere the size of a cathedral dome, formed by a bubble of gas in the city’s primordial youth. It was a place of perfect silence and perfect geometry, untouched by water or wind for a million years. The air within it was fossilized.

The power did not enter. It reached out with a resonance, a focused vibration. It found, on the ceiling of this forgotten dome, a single, unborn drop of water, a molecule of H₂O that had been trapped in the biocement since its formation. With an application of will so subtle it was indistinguishable from a natural process, it dislodged it.

The drop fell.

It fell through the immense, silent dark of the chamber. For a full minute, it was the only moving thing in an eon.

When it struck the center of the floor, the sound it made was not the familiar *tick* of Bas-en-Haut.

It was a Note.

A single, pure, resonant tone that did not echo, but *blossomed*. It was the sound of a crystal bell the size of a moon, struck by a feather. It was a note that did not decay. It filled the chamber, and through the stone itself, it began to resonate outward. It was a vibration of such profound subtlety that it did not shake the stone, but harmonized with it.

The wind still hummed its bass threnody. The water still dripped its patient rhythm. The Chime-Hunter still made its sharp, discordant strike. Nothing was overwritten. But now, woven into the very fabric of the world, beneath all the other sounds, was the Note. It was the sound of stillness itself given voice.

The Weft felt it. Across its continent-spanning body, a new sensation bloomed, a vibration that was not pressure, not temperature, not water. It was a feeling for which it had no name, for it had never existed before. It was a feeling of being perceived without being judged. It was the neural sensation of peace.

The Glass-wing Moths, drifting on the currents, found their flight paths subtly altered by the harmonic resonance, guiding them along new and graceful spirals.

Even the Basin Skimmers, in their frantic dance, moved with a fraction more order, their chaotic patterns now containing the ghost of a mandala.

The power withdrew its attention, the way the tide pulls back from the shore, leaving the beach fundamentally unchanged but for the placement of a few perfect shells. It left the Note not as a message or a monument, but as a signature left in the guest book of a place it had been honored to visit.

The world remained grey and wet. The city continued its slow, inexorable process of growth and decay. But now, for as long as the stone would stand, it did so to the accompaniment of a single, secret, perfect note. A gift not of change, but of music. A whisper from a silent observer that said, simply and eternally: *I have seen you, and you are sublime.*
