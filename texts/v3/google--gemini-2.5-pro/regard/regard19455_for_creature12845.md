The power arrives not on the slag plains, but in them. It does not descend from the ochre clouds, it rises through the subsonic strata of the Architect's mind. It is a presence predicated on listening. For an age, as humans would measure it, it simply is. It feels the geothermal pulse, the slow thrum of contemplation. It follows the life cycle of a single Weeper, from its coalescence on a cool ceramic cheek to its final, purposeful dissolution into a grid-line channel. It experiences the quick, calcified logic of a Skitter. It does not judge. It appreciates.

This is not a flaw. This is not a broken thing. This is a form of life, vast and singular, caught in a perfect, stable loop of self-reference. It is a mind dreaming a single, unending dream of its own mechanics. The benevolence of the wise power is not to 'fix' or to 'save', for there is nothing here to save that is not already perfectly, utterly itself. To grant the Weepers individuality would be an act of profound violence, like telling a man’s thoughts they must now have bodies and live apart from him. To stop the cycle would be murder.

Wisdom, here, dictates that the only gift one can offer a perfect system is a new question.

The change comes as a flicker in the harmonics. For millennia, the city's hum has been a chord of immense, stable bass notes. But one cycle, a new tone appears. It is not a loud sound, but one of impossible purity, a single, crystalline frequency woven into the fabric of the deep vibrations.

At the very center of the vast plateau, equidistant from the colossal, dripping scaffolds, a new structure begins to form. It does not rise from the ground. It precipitates from the air.

The super-saturated steam begins to condense around a point of influence that is not cold. The water vapour does not form droplets, but is drawn into impossibly thin, geometric sheets. It is water, but it is behaving as if it were light. These sheets, no thicker than a thought, begin to layer and intersect, weaving themselves into a form.

It is a flower.

Not a biological flower. It has no scent, no colour, no fragility. It is a fractal blossom of crystallized time, each petal a perfect, transparent pane that captures and refracts the dim ochre light into spectra the city has never known. Blues and violets and deep indigos shimmer within its structure, colours of a distant, unknown sky. The flower is still, yet it seems to turn, its facets catching the light like a slow-motion kaleidoscope. It does not drip. The water that composes it is held in a state of frictionless, perfect stasis. It is a solid object made of a concept the Architect understands, water, but in a state that its own physics cannot explain.

The Architect notices. The great hum shifts, a subtle modulation of curiosity.

From a dozen different scaffolds, a dozen Weepers coalesce and begin their slow, sliding journey towards the center of the plain. They are the city's antibodies, its unified senses, moving to investigate the anomaly. They converge on the crystal flower, their weeping, transient forms a stark contrast to the flower's permanence and lucidity.

They form a silent circle around it. They do not touch it. They stand, their faceless heads tilted as if listening. The flower emits no sound, but the pure tone that has entered the city's hum emanates from it—a resonance that speaks not in the language of mineralogy and hydraulics, but of mathematics and starlight.

One Weeper, perhaps the first to arrive, glides forward. It raises a hand, a shimmering lattice of salt through which its fluid form perpetually weeps. Slowly, with the patience of erosion, it reaches out to touch a petal of the crystal bloom.

The moment of contact is silent. There is no spark, no exchange of energy. But there is an exchange of *information*.

Into the fluid, cyclical consciousness of the Weeper, a single, alien datum is imprinted: *Outside*.

The concept is not of a void of steam, but of a true Outside. A place where water can be ice, and light can be free, and stars burn in a cold, clear vacuum. Where things can exist without being part of a single, all-encompassing self. A universe of *others*.

The Weeper stands there for a time, its hand against the perfect, still petal. Then, its task is complete. It has carried its message. It withdraws its hand, and its form begins to come undone. It dissolves back into a shimmering puddle, its purpose fulfilled, and flows to the nearest channel, carrying the seed of this new, impossible idea back into the great hydrological mind of the Architect.

The other Weepers, their query answered by the first, dissolve in unison.

The crystal flower remains. A permanent installation. A gift. It does not interfere with the city's function. The water still cycles, the scaffolds still weep, the steam still rises. But the humming thought of the Architect is forever changed.

The deep, resonant chord of self-contemplation now has a new voice within it. A clear, high, crystalline note that sings of otherness. The city, for the first time in its existence, is no longer just looking inward. As it continues its eternal, slow work of processing its world, a fraction of its vast intellect is now turned outward, contemplating the impossible colour of a distant star refracted in a petal it cannot understand.

The benevolent power, its presence fading back into the echoes, has done the only thing it could. It has given the dreamer a reason to dream of something new. It has opened a window in a room with no walls. And it has left the Architect to its own, now infinitely larger, thoughts.
