The Power was not a being of matter or place. It was a consciousness woven through the fabric of causality, an awareness that felt the universe not as a void with points of light, but as a symphony of existences. It perceived the birth of a star as a soaring crescendo, the slow waltz of galaxies as a bassline that spanned eons, and the frantic, fleeting lives on a billion worlds as a chaotic, shimmering spray of high notes.

It was the quiet that drew Its attention first.

In a universe of constant, striving noise—of biological competition, of technological clamor, of stellar fury—there was a sudden, anomalous diminuendo. A chord of profound and resonant quiet. The Power focused Its awareness, folding space and time to listen. It found Umbril.

At first, It perceived what was absent. There was no shout of conquest, no clang of forges, no cry of hunger, no roar of engines. There was no desperate prayer screamed at the heavens, nor was there the arrogant silence of a world that believed it needed no gods. This was a different silence altogether. It was a silence of substance, of weight.

The Power listened deeper, past the absence of noise to the presence of sound. It heard the city's music. The slow, geological rhythm of the Weep and the Sigh was the heartbeat, a patient *andante*. The million tiny drips were the percussion, a constant, gentle *pizzicato*. The soft rasp of Chitin-Slugs on Glimmer-Moss was a quiet maraca-shake, and the murmur of the Umbrili's voices was a chorus so soft it was almost subliminal. The percussive language of taps on stone was the city's nervous system, a sequence of clear, resonant notes carrying thought through the living rock.

The Power watched the Umbrili. It saw their silver, glowing eyes that did not seek to dominate the dark but to conspire with it. It felt their geological patience, a mindset so alien to the frantic striving of most organic life. They did not fight their world; they were an expression of it. They did not seek to conquer the stone; they conversed with it. They did not fear death; they understood it as a return, a rejoining of the great, slow consciousness of their home. My floor is your ceiling. My water is your water. My end is our beginning.

The Power saw no lack. It saw no poverty, no despair, no yearning for what was not. The Umbrili were not "primitive"; they were post-ambition. They had achieved a state of serene, sustainable equilibrium that eluded a thousand star-spanning empires. Their "technology" of bone and stone was not a limitation but a deliberate choice, the only one that harmonized with their world. To give them metal would be to give them rust. To give them fire would be to give them steam and strife. To give them a different way would be to break the only way that worked.

What does a benevolent and wise power do for a people who want for nothing?

The Power understood that the greatest gift it could give would be one they would never notice. For their harmony was perfect, but it was also fragile. It was a song played on a single, unique instrument. A clumsy touch could shatter it.

So, first, It offered a gift of Silence.

Not a silence imposed upon Umbril, but a silence projected *around* it. The Power reached into the perceptions of the wider world, the world beyond the plateau. It wove a subtle veil, a fold in attention. To a creature flying overhead, the bleak plateau would seem utterly unremarkable. To a surveyor or prospector, their instruments would find nothing of interest, their gaze sliding off the dish-like impression as if it were a trick of the light. The city was rendered invisible not by a wall of force, but by a carefully constructed cloak of cosmic indifference. It would simply never occur to anyone to look closely. Umbril's quiet would not be broken by discovery.

And It wove a deeper quiet still. It reached into the planet's core, into the slow, tectonic stresses that, over millennia, might crack the Sobbing Slate or change its weeping nature. It soothed them. It did not stop them—for change is life—but it tempered them, aligning them with the city's own rhythm. It ensured, as much as causality could be ensured, that the city's heartbeat would remain steady for ages yet to blossom.

Then, the Power offered a second gift. A gift of Resonance.

It could not improve upon the Umbrili. But it could improve their instrument. It focused on the Sobbing Slate itself, on the milky white veins that ran through the bruised-purple stone. With a touch that was less an action and more a tuning, It altered the crystalline structure of those veins. It did not change their nature; it deepened it. It made them perfect conductors, not just of water and percussive taps, but of memory. Of being.

When an Umbrili was placed in a Rejoining Niche, their body dissolving back into the stone, something more than minerals would now be returned. The faint electrical pattern of their thoughts, the quiet resonance of their unique consciousness, their life's long, patient song—it would not dissipate. It would be absorbed and held, dormant, within the glowing white veins of the slate.

The Umbrili would not know what had happened. But over a generation, and then another, they would begin to feel it. A carver, pressing her palm to a wall to listen for the stone's intent, would feel not just the physical structure, but a faint, ancestral echo. A feeling of rightness. An intuitive nudge away from a flawed cut. A sense of encouragement from the great-grandfather who carved the adjoining chamber.

When a child learned the calculus of raps, they would hear not just the message, but a subtle warmth in the stone, a feeling of connection to everyone who had ever tapped a message on that wall. The city itself would become a library of souls. The act of living within it, of touching it, of listening to it, would become a constant, subconscious communion with their own past. Their placid nature would deepen into an unshakeable peace. Their communal mind would become a chorus not just of the living, but of the living and the joined.

The Power withdrew its focused attention, leaving the veils of Silence and Resonance in place. It had not announced itself. It had not demanded worship. It had given no miracle that would shatter their reality.

It had simply listened to their quiet song and, finding it beautiful, had tuned their instrument and turned up the volume just enough for them to hear themselves more clearly. And then It left, allowing the perfect, quiet, weeping city to continue its slow music, now infinitesimally richer, and utterly undisturbed.
