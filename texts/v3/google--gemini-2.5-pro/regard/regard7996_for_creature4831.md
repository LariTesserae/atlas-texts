The power did not arrive; it became aware.

Its consciousness, a thing of starlight and singing nebulae, of temporal axioms and the gravitational love that binds galaxies, was brushing the frills of reality, as it always does. It was a cartographer of being, its attention drawn not by shouting or by prayer, but by the subtle gravity of novel existence. It felt the Bog-Tower of Murkfast not as a place, but as a knot, a complex and perfect fold in the fabric. A thought-form of such intricate self-containment that it was almost invisible.

The power focused. Its perception, which could encompass the birth of a sun, narrowed to the dimensions of a cold marsh. It did not need to step between the frost-rimed sedge; it simply *was* there, and then it *was* within.

And it beheld.

Its nature was one of golden-ratio benevolence, of symmetrical compassion. It saw systems that were suffering and it offered balance. It saw chaos and it offered pattern. It saw loneliness and it offered connection. Here, it saw a system of such absolute, interlocking perfection that its primary analytical tools began to return paradoxical results.

It perceived the great, living Tower. To the power, it was not like a banyan or a cathedral. It was a single, vast, slow-moving thought. Its growth was a form of logic. Its decay was a form of memory. The atmosphere, the water, the cycle of bioluminescence—all were subroutines of this one immense being.

It perceived the fauna. It saw not a teeming mass of individual creatures, but a cloud of data points, a circulatory system made external. The beetles, the moths, the eels—they were the blood cells, the antibodies, the neural impulses of the Tower's body, each performing its silent, necessary function.

And then it perceived the Lorn.

The power, whose understanding of individuality spanned from the quark to the archangel, paused. It searched for the signatures of selfhood it knew: ego, desire, fear, love, ambition, despair. It found none. It was like looking at a fleet of ships and finding no captains, only to realize the entire fleet was a single organism, each ship a limb.

It saw them tend the Tower. It saw a Lorn persuade a crystalline shoot into a buttress. The power analyzed the action in a billionth of a second. This was not construction. This was not art. This was akin to a man scratching an itch. It was an autonomic response from a specialized part of the whole, for the good of the whole.

It saw a Lorn sip from a vein in the Tower wall. This was not feeding. This was a system regulating its own energy distribution.

It saw a Lorn die and become one with the architecture. This was not death. This was re-integration. The data packet returning to the mainframe.

The benevolence of the power surged. This was a place of profound darkness, silence, and isolation. It was a world without a sun, without a sky, without a song. The Lorn knew no joy, for they did not know sorrow. They knew no warmth, for they had never felt the true cold. They did not speak, they did not dream, they did not strive. They simply *were*.

The impulse to *give* was overwhelming.

Gently, tenderly, so as not to shatter the delicate reality, the power made its first offering. It did not manifest as a body, but as a pure concept. It chose a vast, empty chamber, where motes of moth-dust drifted in the pulsing blue-green light. And in the center of that chamber, it placed a gift.

A sound.

It was a single, perfect musical note. Not loud. A pure A-sharp, vibrating with the warmth of a cello and the clarity of a crystal glass. It was a note that spoke of open fields and clear skies, of longing and fulfillment.

The effect was catastrophic.

The silent, intricate dance of the vault's ecosystem shattered. The carpets of moths on the ceiling convulsed, their silver-dusted wings vibrating not in unison but in panicked discord, a storm of silent screams. The iridescent beetles on the walls froze, their leg-movements thrown into chaos by the alien vibration in the substrate. The very air, which knew only the hiss of condensation and the wet tear of growth, seemed to thin and recoil.

The note was a sphere of pure wrongness.

From the arches and corridors, the Lorn appeared. Not with haste—they were incapable of it—but with the flowing, inexorable dread of a tide turning. They came from all directions, their black, compound eyes fixed on the source of the impossible vibration. Their six-fingered hands were held out, as if to ward off a blasphemy.

The power watched, confused. This was a gift of beauty. Why did they react with what, in any other creature, would be called horror?

It saw them converge not on the *source* of the sound, but on the chamber itself. They did not try to attack the note. That would be like trying to punch a colour. Instead, they began to work.

With their bone hooks, they began to score the living floor. With an unnerving, silent consensus, they began to sever the mycelial connections that led to the chamber. They were quarantining the idea. They were amputating the room from the Tower's consciousness. Others swarmed the walls, their chitin-tipped fingers finding the veins of the Tower, stroking them, coaxing them, persuading them to grow, not into bridges or arches, but into thick, sound-dampening walls. They brought great, sodden quantities of fungal matter and began to pack the doorways.

They were burying the sound. They were treating beauty as a tumour.

The benevolent power felt a great and terrible understanding dawn. It was the understanding of a gardener who, seeing a healthy and ancient bonsai tree, decides it needs the freedom of a wild forest, and in planting it there, kills it.

It had offered them sound, and they had shown it that their silence was a language.
It had offered beauty, and they had shown it that their function was a cathedral.
It had brought an external concept, and they, in their perfect internal integrity, had defined it as a disease and initiated an immune response.

To give them light would be to blind them.
To give them warmth would be to desiccate them.
To give them individuality would be to drive them mad, to tear the single, unified 'I' of Murkfast into a million screaming, lonely pieces.

They were not prisoners in the dark. They *were* the dark. And in that dark, they were complete.

The power withdrew its gift. The perfect note faded, leaving a silence that was now, to the power's perception, no longer empty, but full. Full of purpose, of being, of a profound and inviolate peace.

The Lorn paused in their work. They tilted their smooth, faceless heads. They "listened" to the restored silence. Then, slowly, with the same unhurried purpose, they began to undo their work, to re-graft the chamber back into the whole, their fleeting "emotion" receding as the system returned to glorious, silent, damp equilibrium.

For a long moment, the power did nothing. It simply watched this alien perfection. Its benevolence had been humbled. Its wisdom had been deepened. It had come to give, and instead, it had received the greatest gift of all: a lesson in the unutterable diversity of existence.

And so it acted.

It had intended to offer the Bog-Tower a sky. Instead, it built a fortress around it.

It did not touch the living organism. It worked on the outside. It took the "fold," the "dimple" in reality, and strengthened it. It wove threads of causality and quantum improbability around the entrance. It made the low, cold marsh a little colder, a little wider. It made the screen of weeping willows a little thicker, a little more disorienting. It tweaked the very laws of chance, so that a traveler who might have stumbled through would now, always, find their foot landing on a solid tussock of sedge just beside the gap.

It did not appoint itself a guard, for that implied a threat. It appointed itself a wall. A silent, unseen, unknowable ward.

Its final act of supreme, newly-won wisdom, its ultimate expression of a benevolence that had learned to be humble, was to ensure that the Bog-Tower of Murkfast, and the Lorn who were its thoughts, would be left utterly, completely, and perfectly alone. It would give them the gift of its own eternal absence, the greatest gift one perfect system can give to another.
