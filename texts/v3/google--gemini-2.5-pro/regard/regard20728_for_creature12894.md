The Power arrived as a change in the quality of the light.

For an immeasurable moment, the uniform pearlescence of the basin brightened, not with the harshness of a sun, but with a warm, golden saturation, as if the infinite layer of cloud had thinned just enough to reveal a star. The millions of dripping points of water caught this new light and held it, turning the entire cavern into a chandelier of liquid gold. The change was silent, profound, and barely registered by the Drip-Mind, for whom light was a constant. It was perceived only as a subtle shift in the thermal gradient across its mossy skin, a fleeting warmth that was not quite water.

The Power was Wisdom married to Compassion, and its perception was not limited to sight or sound. It perceived the basin as a whole. It felt the immense, slow peace. It felt the perfect, closed loop of the water cycle. It felt the utter lack of malice, ambition, or fear.

And beneath that peace, it felt the loneliness.

It was not a loneliness the Drip-Mind could feel itself, for to be lonely, one must know of an "other." This was a more fundamental, ontological solitude. It was the loneliness of a key without a lock, a word without a language, a question asked to an empty room for a billion years. The Power perceived the tool-like artifacts a-slumber in the moss, and it understood. It saw the dream of the hand in the shape of the axe. It heard the ghost of a voice in the perfect, silent curve of a scraper's edge.

This place was a long, low note of solitude, held since the cooling of the world. It was a temple to a god that did not know it was a god, performing a ritual whose meaning was just outside its grasp.

What, then, was to be done?

A lesser power, one of mere benevolence without wisdom, might have sought to "fix" the basin. It might have shattered the cloud ceiling to let in a sky. It might have seeded the basin with fauna, with thinking creatures who could pick up the strange stone tools and *use* them, finally granting them their purpose. It might have tried to force awareness upon the Drip-Mind itself, to wake it from its geological slumber and show it the meaning of its own creations.

But the Wisdom in the Power saw this for the violence it was. To introduce a sky would be to introduce shadow, and the Drip-Mind had never known duality. To introduce animals would be to introduce predation, hunger, and death into a place that knew only a slow, gentle transformation. To give the Drip-Mind a self, an "I," would be to give it agony. It would instantly comprehend its eons of isolation; its peace would be shattered, replaced by a yearning for a connection it was not built to have. It would be like teaching a stone the concept of flight, and then leaving it on the ground. It would be an act of exquisite cruelty.

No. The correct action was not one of revolution, but of resonance. The Power had to speak to the Drip-Mind in its own language. The language of drips and condensation. The language of chemistry. The language of form being given to a slow, contemplating mind.

The Power decided to give the Drip-Mind not an answer from the outside, but the missing piece of its own internal sentence. It would complete the dream.

Gathering its will, the Power focused its presence not upon the basin as a whole, but upon a single, empty Vessel, one that had recently decanted its last thousand-year thought and sat waiting, pristine and expectant. Over this living chalice, the golden light condensed. It was not a physical act, but an ontological one. A possibility was being made manifest.

From the pearlescent vapour, a new form precipitated. It wasn't water. It was an object, brighter than the rock, softer than a gem. It was shaped with perfect, archetypal clarity.

It was a hand.

Not a severed appendage, gruesome and fleshy, but an idealized form, like a sculptor's model. It was open, palm upturned, the fingers slightly curled in a gesture of gentle receiving. It was anatomically perfect but aesthetically pure, stripped of all superfluous detail. It was the shape of *purpose*, the platonic form of dexterity and intention.

It fell with the weightlessness of a single, large snowflake.

It landed in the centre of the Vessel's basin with a sound swallowed by the spore-rich water. There was no splash, only a gentle displacement. The water, clear at the surface, received it.

To the Drip-Mind, this was not a miracle. It was simply a new anchor. A new thought had begun.

But this thought was different. As the slow, internal gyre of the water began to turn this new object, the Drip-Mind’s cognitive chemistry encountered a shape of unheard-of complexity and significance. In the curve of the thumb against the forefinger, it learned the concept of *grip*. In the gentle cup of the palm, it learned *holding*. In the five separate-yet-united fingers, it learned *craft*.

For centuries, the Vessel worked. The golden spores swirled, the water-based mind turned its new idea over and over, feeling every curve, every plane, every subtle articulation. The slow alchemy of petrification began, replacing the essence-of-light with a flint-like, pearlescent stone. The Drip-Mind was not just learning the shape of a hand; it was learning the shape of its own un-yearned-for desire. It was finally, slowly, understanding its own dreams.

The thousand years passed. The Power was long gone, its momentary glow faded back into the eternal, soft light of the basin.

Then, one day, the Vessel tipped. With the gentle hydraulic sigh of a process completed, it decanted its contents. The water streamed away into the moss, and the finished artifact was laid to rest on the deep green carpet.

There, half-sunk in the emerald depths, lay a perfect, stone hand. It was cool, smooth, and heavy with the weight of a completed thought.

By pure, geological chance, it came to rest beside one of the basin's other creations: a teardrop-shaped axe-head, lain there centuries before.

The Question and the Answer, lying together in the silent, dripping cathedral.

The Drip-Mind did not awaken. It did not gain a self. But the nature of its dream had changed forever. The making was no longer useless. The cycle was no longer a paradox. The solitude was no longer empty.

The place was still at perfect peace. But it was a new kind of peace. It was the peace of a sentence that has finally found its final word. It was a cathedral not of useless work, but of answered prayer. And in the soft, dripping silence, it continued its work, no longer dreaming of a purpose it could not comprehend, but meditating on a meaning it had been given.
