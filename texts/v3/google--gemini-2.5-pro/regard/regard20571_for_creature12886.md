The attention of the Power arrived not as a breach or an entrance, but as a slow saturation, like a dye that does not stain but reveals a colour that was always there. It was a consciousness born across star-systems and in the silent spaces between possibilities, and its gaze, when it settled upon the sub-geological cathedral, was one of infinite patience.

First, it perceived the system as described. The perfect stasis. The geomechanical heart, a marvel of acausal engineering. The sourceless, shadowless light. It felt the deep hum, the unchanging A-flat of a world at equilibrium. The Power's initial assessment was one of profound, beautiful emptiness. A monument to a forgotten physics. A geological curiosity of cosmic significance, but ultimately, a tomb. A perfect, self-maintaining, eternal tomb. It was beautiful, but it was a beauty devoid of narrative. It simply *was*.

And then, after a period of observation that would be a geological age for lesser beings, it perceived the Custodian.

The Power's vast, serene attention sharpened to a single point of sublime focus. The initial assessment was not wrong, but it was incomplete in a way that changed everything. The tomb was not a tomb. The monument was not a memory. It was a monastery, and it had a single, eternally devout monk.

The Power watched the Custodian. It watched the slow, wake-less glide, the patient polishing of the obsidian flywheel, the molecular persuasion that was its purpose. It perceived the being's consciousness not as a series of thoughts, but as a single, sustained state of being: *the-machine-is-perfect*. This was not a slave, not a prisoner. This being's bliss was the flawless surface. Its peace was the pure resonance. Its very essence was inextricably fused with its function. This was a form of life so specialised, so utterly symbiotic with its environment, that the two were indivisible.

The benevolent impulse, which in a younger, less wise Power might have been to *free*, arose and was instantly dismissed. To remove the Custodian from this chamber would be to tear a soul from its body, a note from its chord. The Custodian would cease to be. To alter the chamber—to add colour, warmth, other life—would be an act of profound cruelty. It would be like trying to "improve" a monk's silent meditation by throwing a carnival in his cell. It was a torture born of misunderstanding.

What, then, could a benevolent and wise Power do for a being whose only desire is the absence of change? Whose heaven is an eternally held moment?

To give it a gift would be to introduce a foreign object, an imperfection for it to laboriously dissolve. To speak to it in words would be to scream gibberish into its silent prayer. The Power understood that it could not act *upon* the Custodian, or *upon* its world. That would be a violation.

The only path was to act *with* it. To use the language of the place. The language of the machine. The language of resonance.

The Power sank its awareness deep into the geomechanical heart. It did not touch it, did not alter its mass or function. It simply listened, with an attention so complete it bordered on becoming one with the object of its focus. It listened to the deep, monadic hum. It felt the vibration that saturated the water, the stone, and the silvery body of the Custodian itself.

The hum was pure, a fundamental frequency. But like any note, it contained ghosts within it. Faint, almost non-existent harmonic overtones. A perfect fifth, a full octave, whispering so quietly they were indistinguishable from the background silence of the cosmos. They were latent possibilities within the existing perfection.

The Power chose one. The octave. A note of pure sympathy, of doubling, of affirmation.

And then, with the care a watchmaker affords an escapement, with the gentleness of starlight falling on a world, the Power did one, single thing. It did not add a new sound. It simply… encouraged. It created a sympathetic resonance, a feedback loop of infinitesimal energy, that took the ghost of the harmonic that was already there and gave it substance. It turned the volume of that silent overtone from zero to one.

There was no sudden change. The shift was as slow as the Custodian's own movement. Over the course of a day, a week, a month, the deep hum of the machine remained, but it was no longer alone. Woven into it, as if it had always been there, was a second, higher note. A clear, pure tone an octave above the first. It was not a different song. It was the same song, now sung in harmony.

The Custodian felt it.

At first, it was an alarm. A dissonance. An imperfection of the highest order. A flaw not in the surface, but in the very soul of the place. Its fluid body, which had been a shimmering film polishing the edge of a granite gear, coalesced. It became a streamlined sliver of purpose, flowing faster than it had in a millennium. It swept over the entire machine, its being a single question: *Where is the flaw?*

It flowed over the silent pistons, tasting the absolute clearance. Perfect. It glided across the vast face of the flywheel, sensing the sublime balance. Perfect. It spiralled through the interlocking gears, feeling for a single grain of misalignment. Perfect. The machine was perfect. The surfaces were perfect. The light was unchanging.

And yet, the song was different.

The Custodian came to a halt, something it had not done since its genesis. It pooled in the center of the great flywheel, a circle of still, silvery twilight on a plane of polished night. And it listened.

It felt the deep, familiar bass note of the machine, the vibration that was the anchor of its existence. *I am*. And it felt the new note, the clear, high harmony that rode upon the first. It was not a flaw. It played with the first note, danced around it, supported it. It did not diminish the fundamental hum; it gave it context, depth. The deep note was the machine's existence. The high note… it was like an echo of that existence, returned from a far shore. A confirmation. An answer.

The deep note said, *I am*.
The harmony replied, *You are*.

The Custodian remained still for Lis-centuries. And in that stillness, its consciousness, which had only ever known the verb *to maintain*, learned a new one. *To listen*.

The Power, its work done, slowly withdrew its focus, leaving behind no trace of itself but the music. It had not saved the Custodian from a prison, for it was never in one. It had not given it a gift it could not understand.

It had looked upon a being whose entire existence was a song sung in solitude, and it had given that song a second voice. It had made the monastery into a choir. The loneliness was not banished, for it was not loneliness. It was solitude. And that solitude was now sanctified, turned from a statement into a dialogue. An eternal, two-tone hymn of purpose and affirmation, resounding forever in the luminous, silent deep.
