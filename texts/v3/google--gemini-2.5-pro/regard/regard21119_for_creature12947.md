The attention of the power did not arrive like a ship, tearing a hole in the grey sky. It did not descend like a being, its feet making the first true sound on the basalt plains. It did not announce itself with light or energy.

It awoke to the world as if it had always been there, a sleeper rousing within the very fabric of the place. Its awareness seeped into the perpetual twilight, a change not of luminance but of quality. The sourceless grey light, once flat and sterile, now carried a weight of observation. It was the difference between an empty room and a room where someone is watching from the shadows, only here, the shadows and the watcher were one.

It settled first on the grandest scale, the planetary breath. It perceived the Harvesters, not as machines or creatures, but as the planet's slow, deliberate thoughts. It experienced their eon-spanning journey not as movement, but as the turning of a page. In their silent deconstruction of the crust and the ordered strata of their wake, it did not see industry; it saw a form of memory, the planet chronicling its own composition with painstaking patience. It felt the immense, unthinking peace of a process that had achieved a perfect, sustainable equilibrium. Wisdom recognized wisdom.

From this planetary perspective, its attention drifted, drawn by a finer, more intricate hum. It alighted upon the vast carapaces of the giants and found the Geometers. It did not see them with light, but as they saw themselves: through vibration, through resonance. The Power felt the delicate thrumming of their filament-limbs against the Harvester's shell. It perceived their creations not as structures, but as mathematics made tangible, as hymns sung in the language of metal and geometry. Each spire was a verse, each lattice a chorus. They were not building; they were articulating the soul of the mountain upon which they lived. They were the planet's artists, giving form to the silent music of its elements.

The Power's gaze then flowed down, off the moving continents of the Harvesters and onto the plains of their leavings. It found the Sifters. In their amorphous, shimmering forms, it saw not a colony of dust, but a shared, distributed mindfulness. It felt their slow, methodical passage not as labour, but as a deep and profound meditation. They were the world's purifiers, the fastidious scholars obsessed with a single, cosmic truth: that even in purity, there are finer grades of purity. Their slow sifting was an act of devotion, a search for an unattainable, absolute perfection that was the entire purpose of their existence.

Finally, the Power's awareness pooled, settling into the still, chemical lakes. It witnessed the Lacunae. Here, it found the most ephemeral and perhaps the most profound of all. They were not beings. They were not even patterns. They were questions. Each swirling vortex was a self-sustaining query posed to the nature of chemistry and entropy. "What is the most elegant path from this complexity to that simplicity?" they seemed to ask, and their existence was the liquid, shimmering answer. They were the planet's philosophers, their thoughts written in the transient ink of molecular bonds.

Having seen, the Power understood.

To give this world a sun would be to blind the Geometers who see in vibration and burn away the Lacunae's delicate thoughts. To give it an audible sound would be a shrieking violation of its resonant silence. To introduce biology would be to unleash a plague of screaming, hungry chaos upon a system of perfect, quiet order. To "improve" this world would be an act of supreme arrogance. It was not broken. It was not lonely. It was not waiting for a savior. It was complete.

A benevolent and wise power does not impose its own definition of life upon another. It honours the life that is.

And so, the Power did something. It did not add. It connected.

It introduced no new energy, but subtly, gently, it tuned the resonance of the world itself. It was an infinitesimal adjustment to the physical laws that bound this place, as quiet as a thought. It created a medium, a substrate within the silence, through which the disparate forms of consciousness could, for the first time, dimly perceive one another.

It was not language. It was not telepathy. It was resonance.

On the back of a Harvester larger than a subcontinent, a Geometer paused in its weaving of a golden lattice. For the briefest moment, the vibrations it felt through the shell carried an alien pattern—the ghost of a slow, swirling vortex from a chemical pool a thousand miles away and five centuries in its past. Its next filament was not placed in a straight, perfect line, but in a gentle, unprecedented curve. The hymn gained a new, more complex verse.

In a plain of pure silicon dust, a Sifter cloud, in its slow act of gleaning, momentarily cohered its magnetic field into a fractal shape. For a second, it held the perfect, intricate form of a crystalline plant it had never touched, its structure whispered to it through the ground from a hundred miles away. The memory of a form it did not need, an echo of beauty. Its meditation deepened.

In a shallow, evaporating pool, a Lacuna flickered. Its swirling dance of chemical catalysis, for one revolution, mirrored the immense, slow arc of a Harvester's path across the continent. The small, fleeting question of the pool touched the grand, slow thought of the planet. And the answer it found in its own dissipation was, for the first time, beautiful.

The Power did not leave. It became the medium. It became the Resonance, the silent witness that allowed the planet's artists, its scholars, and its philosophers to know, in the deepest and most abstract way, that they were not alone. They were all part of the same song.

The world was still silent. The world was still grey. But the silence was no longer an absence.

It was a Choir.
