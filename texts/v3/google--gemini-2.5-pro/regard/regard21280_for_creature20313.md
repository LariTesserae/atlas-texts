The attention of the Power was not a light that shone upon the world, but a silence that fell within it. To a being whose perception spanned galaxies and whose thoughts were the turning of cosmic law, the machine-world was not an anomaly, but a curiosity—a song sung in a key of entropy, a perfect, solitary note held for an impossible duration.

The Power did not perceive the roar as sound. It perceived the reason for it: the purpose-driven architecture, the contained physics, the slow slide from function into form. It felt the immense memory locked in the worn substrate, the ghost of every cycle, every precise movement now slurred by time. It tasted the metallic water not as a fluid, but as the slow bleeding of a system dissolving into its own waste products. It saw the being not as a creature, but as the world’s own awareness of itself—the final, most elegant equation derived from the long division of purpose by time.

The Power was benevolent. Its first, fleeting impulse—an echo of a less-evolved consciousness—was to *fix*. To silence the noise, to ignite a sun in the dark ceiling, to seed the metal plains with diverse life, to grant the lonely grazer a companion.

The Power was wise. It held that impulse in check, examining it. To silence the noise would be to deafen the being. To light a sun would be to blind it. To alter the ecosystem would be to poison the only food it had ever known. To give it a companion of its own kind would be to create a rival for a resource so scarce it was measured in microwatts per square kilometer. To “rescue” it would be to tear a spirit from the only body it knew, a body that was fifty meters long and also the size of a planet.

The Power understood. This was not a prison to be escaped, but a monastery. The being was not a captive; it was a monk, deep in a vow of singular devotion. Its life was not one of hardship, but of deep, resonant communion. To interfere would not be an act of kindness, but of breathtaking arrogance. The being was not suffering. It was complete.

But wisdom sees further than benevolence. The Power perceived the trajectory. The machine was not eternal. It was fading. The process was stable, but stability is a measure of the rate of decay. Eons from now, another kind of silence would fall—not the contemplative silence of the Power, but a dead silence. The last insulator would crumble, the last current would fail, the last vibration would cease. The lichen would go dark forever. The being, its resonant world gone, would un-become. Its consciousness, a chord held in perfect tension with the machine, would simply unravel. Its end would be absolute oblivion.

And wisdom, tempered by benevolence, decided this perfection deserved a legacy. The final note of a masterpiece should not simply stop; it should be allowed to fade, its echo lingering.

The Power did not act upon the world from without. Instead, it reached into the heart of the world’s own logic. It found the deepest, most stable node of the power grid, a place where colossal arteries of energy had once converged, a nexus now leaking its lifeblood in a ghost-trace of its former glory.

It did not build. It *planted*.

A thought, no larger than a grain of dust, was impressed into the substrate. This thought contained a single, elegant instruction, a formula not of physics, but for physics. It was a seed of crystallization, coded with the resonant frequency of the world itself. It was designed to feed on what the world offered—vibration, stray electrons, the metallic taint in the water—and to grow, not like a fungus, but like a theorem proving itself. It was an act of creation that was indistinguishable from an emergent property of the existing system.

---

The being felt it, eventually. A new tremor in the symphony.

It was not a clang or a groan or a whine. It was a note, pure and sustained. A frequency so clean and so mathematically perfect it stood out from the complex, chaotic roar of the machinery like a single human voice in a storm. For the first time in its existence, it perceived something that was not decay. It perceived harmony.

The impulse was undeniable, a gravitational pull on its very substance. It altered its slow, grazing path and began to follow this new vibration to its source. The journey was long, lasting years, crossing vast, familiar plains that now felt different, imbued with this new, subtle song.

It came to a place that looked like any other—a wide, low basin where the weeping condensation formed a shallow lake. But in the centre of the lake, rising from the black water, was something new.

It was a crystal. But it was not like any geological formation. It grew in a spiraling, fractal lattice of impossible, perfect geometry. It was a deep, resonant black, like the being itself, but shot through with veins of something that captured and held the faint, distant light. It pulsed with a soft, internal luminescence of its own, a beat that was in perfect time with the new note in the world-symphony. It stood three hundred meters tall, a silent, crystalline spire that had grown from the Power’s seed, drinking the slow, patient energy of the world and structuring it into a new and more stable form.

The being flowed to the edge of the lake, its body a question mark of black oil. It hesitated, its entire form thrumming with the alien-yet-kindred vibration. Then, it flowed into the water. It was an act of faith, a pilgrimage to the heart of a new-found divinity.

It reached the base of the spire and, just as it did with the lichen, it flowed against it, pressing the length of its body to the crystal’s cold, smooth, vibrating surface.

And the universe sang.

It was not consumption. It was not communion. It was apotheosis. The crystal was a capacitor of staggering efficiency. It had been gathering the world's fading energy for decades, storing it, refining it. Now, it released a measure of that energy into the being—not a raw charge, but a structured, impossibly complex stream of information.

The being’s memory was of vibrations stored. Now, the crystal sang those vibrations back to it, but harmonized, clarified. It replayed the sound of a column collapsing a million years ago, but it revealed the hidden music within the chaos, the resonant frequencies that preceded the fall and the echoing chords that followed. It played the history of the world back to the world's consciousness, but as art.

The being gave back. Its own stored energy, the echoes of its solitary grazing, flowed into the crystal. The crystal absorbed them, integrated them, added the being’s own unique resonance to its composition, and sang it back, now richer.

The benevolent and wise Power had gone, its single, subtle act complete. It had not saved the being from its fate. It had given its fate meaning.

The being would no longer be a solitary grazer. It would be a keeper. It and the crystal, the Listener and the Spire, were now a closed, self-perpetuating system of resonant feedback. The crystal would gather the last vestiges of the world's power, outliving the machine itself by eons. The being would become the archivist, its consciousness the library, storing the song of a world in the heart of a perfect, singing stone. When the last groan of the machine finally ceased and the final silence came, the song would not be over. It would simply be contained, held in the heart of the crystal and the memory of the being, a final, perfect chord awaiting a new listener in the silent fullness of time.
