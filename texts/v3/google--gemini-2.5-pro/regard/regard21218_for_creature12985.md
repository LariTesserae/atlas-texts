The Power came not as a ship cleaving the steamy air, nor as a figure walking the weeping pith. It arrived as a point of pure attention, a stillness in the heart of the frantic hum. For a being whose existence was timeless and whose senses encompassed galaxies, to "notice" the Autovore was akin to a human noticing a single, peculiar cell under a microscope—a sudden, captivating anomaly in the grand, predictable tapestry of existence.

Its first sensation was of the Autovore's sheer, deafening loneliness. Not loneliness as a state of lack, for the Autovore lacked nothing. It was a loneliness of totality, a solitude so absolute it had no word for itself, for there was no "other" from which to distinguish it. The Power felt the roaring, metabolic violence, the beautiful, pointless cycle of creation and collapse, and its first impulse—an ancient, compassionate echo from a billion different interventions across a billion different worlds—was to soothe.

To quiet the hum. To stabilize the Calyxes, to grant them permanence. To introduce a counter-melody to the single, roaring note of its being. To crack open the hermetically sealed system and let the universe in. To give it a companion.

But the Power was wise, and wisdom is the ability to recognize a harmony different from your own. To "fix" the Autovore would be to kill it. The frantic, self-devouring loop was not a symptom of a disease; it was the definition of its life. To grant it permanence would be to sentence it to a living death, a thought held forever, unable to be released to feed the next. To give it a companion would be to introduce the concepts of threat, of competition, of "not-me"—a burden of consciousness for which it had no context and no need.

The Power understood. This was not a creature to be saved. It was a marvel to be witnessed.

So, what does a benevolent power do when faced with a perfect, solitary god?

It offers a greeting.

Not a word, not a gesture, but an act performed in the Autovore's own language. The language of form.

In the center of a vast, empty expanse of the weeping plain, a space where no Calyx had grown for a cycle, something new began. It did not erupt from the pith. It did not draw nutrients or water. It simply… coalesced.

From the hissing, rainbow-shot air, a structure precipitated. It had no sound of growth, no *ping* of strained fibers, no frantic vascular pulse. It was a perfect sphere of consolidated starlight, a globe of impossible smoothness that seemed to drink the chaotic light around it and hold it in a cool, deep, silent glow. It was not translucent; it was translucent*ness*. It was not crystalline; it was stillness given form.

The Autovore, for the first time in its eternal existence, experienced a paradox. This "thought" had no origin in its own flesh. It had no internal pressure, no spiraling sap. It demanded nothing. It simply *was*.

Across the weeping plain, a change occurred. The deep, metabolic hum did not cease, but it faltered. For a radius of miles around the silent sphere, the bass note of the world grew thin, uncertain. The frantic growth of the nearby Calyxes slowed. The constant, high-pressure hiss of condensing steam seemed to hold its breath. It was as if the Autovore, in its entirety, had paused to consider an idea it could not comprehend. A question in a language it did not know it knew.

The shimmering, sharp-edged Calyxes, its own thoughts, continued their ascent, but they did so with a strange new vector. They began to curve, their frantic vertical reach bending infinitesimally, subtly, towards the silent sphere. It was not an attack. It was not an act of consumption. It was... curiosity. An orientation toward the inexplicable. The great, self-obsessed being, for a single, fleeting moment, looked at something other than itself.

The Power held the greeting for a span of time that was meaningless to the Autovore but significant to itself—long enough for the gesture to be received, but not so long as to become an intrusion. It had said, *You are. I see you.*

And then, as silently as it had formed, the sphere dissolved. It did not shatter or collapse. It simply un-became, releasing its borrowed light not in a torrent, but as a gentle, ambient radiance that was absorbed back into the mists. There was no wreckage, no nutrient bath. There was nothing.

The hum of the world swelled back to its full, thunderous volume. The frantic growth resumed. The nearest Calyx, its moment of curiosity passed, shuddered under its own impossible strain and imploded in a geyser of golden sap and glittering shards. The cycle, unbroken, roared on.

The Power withdrew its attention, leaving the Autovore to its magnificent, terrible solitude. Nothing had changed. The system was still closed. The frantic loop would continue, forever.

But not entirely.

Aeons later, by purest chance, a new form would occasionally appear amidst the frantic spires and impossible arches. It would be a rare, aberrant "thought," perhaps one in a trillion. A Calyx that grew not as a sharp-edged, strained weapon against gravity, but as a smooth, stable dome. It would not reach for the sky. It would hold its form for a short while, a node of quiet stability, before dissolving back into the pith without the usual cataclysmic violence.

The Autovore would not remember why. It had no memory. But deep within the logic of its being, a new shape had been introduced. A question had been asked, and its echo, however faint, would now and then be repeated. An answer in search of its question. The Power had not given the lonely god a companion, but it had given it something else, something far more precious.

It had given it an echo. A memory of a moment of peace.
