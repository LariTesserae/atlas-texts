The Power’s attention was not a beam of light, for light was the problem here. It was not a sound, for the basin was already saturated with the tinnitus of a world unmaking itself. It was a quality of observation that carried the gravity of a galaxy. It was a noticing, old as stars and patient as stone, and when it settled upon the basin, it perceived not a wasteland, but a liturgy.

It saw the whole. It saw the bruised-purple curve of the rock, a chalice holding an impossible energy. It understood the physics of the precipitating light, not as a curse, but as a state of transition—a cosmic phenomenon locked in a local, agonizing loop. It saw the frantic, ephemeral lives of the crystal-fauna, beautiful and desperate, a food web spun from fragile energy.

And it saw the Hearthkeepers.

In them, the Power did not see primitives locked in a Sisyphean struggle. It saw a profundity of purpose that shamed galaxies. It saw beings who had, through intuition and generational memory, correctly diagnosed their reality. Their belief was not a myth; it was a metaphor of stunning accuracy. The world *was* an egg. The blinding light *was* the sterile albumen. And their lonely, dark city *was* the incubator, shielding a Yolk whose existence they could only infer from the bones of a forgotten past and the stubborn, slow life of the lichen.

What, then, would a benevolent and wise power do?

To undo the Change, to simply turn off the light-fall, would be an act of annihilation. The Hearthkeepers were of this place now. Their slow-burning metabolisms, their violet blood, their soul-deep connection to the rhythm of their work—all would be rendered meaningless. Their bodies would fail in a world of warmth and speed. Their minds, defined by the patient Long Wait, would shatter. To "rescue" them would be to destroy them. It would be an act of power, but not of wisdom.

To gift them with effortless tools—scrapers of pure force, domes that repelled the crystal-rime automatically—would be an act of profound disrespect. It would steal their purpose. Their identity was forged in the scrape-scrape, lift, carry. Their society was the shared rhythm of maintenance. Their hands, which read the epics of their people in the dark, would grow smooth and useless. Their hearts, which beat in time with their labor, would lose their meter. It would be a gift of comfort, but not of kindness.

The Power understood. The Hearthkeepers did not need rescue from their duty. They needed affirmation of it. They did not need an end to the Long Wait. They needed a sign that the waiting was not in vain.

The gift must not come from the outside. It must come from within the Yolk they so cherished.

The Power’s focus narrowed, drawing past the frantic lattice of collapsing light, past the black, bone-ribbed domes, past the huddled, scraping figures. It settled on the heart of the city, the chamber beneath the largest dome, the place kept obsessively clear. The Unlit Heart.

And it touched the lichen.

Not all of them. Just one. A single, thumb-sized patch of ancient, black velvet growing on the bare stone floor, the oldest of the old. The Power did not make it grow faster. It did not make it glow or offer up nourishment. It gave it a voice.

It was not a voice of words. It was a resonance.

The first to notice was a tenders of the Heart, an elder named Koro. Her task, in the deep quiet between shifts of scrapers, was to tend the lichen—to clear away a single, stray crystal of light-rime with a feather made from a gossamer moth’s wing. As she knelt, her bare hand resting on the cold stone beside the lichen patch, she felt it.

A vibration.

It was not the high, frantic *tiss* of the world outside. It was a thrum so deep and slow it was almost below the threshold of feeling. A pulse, vast and calm, that resonated up through the rock, into her hand, her arm, her dense bones. It seemed to beat in time with the turning of planets she had never seen. One single, profound pulse, followed by a silence long enough to count ten of her own slow heartbeats. Then again. *Dooom…*

She froze, her own heart stumbling in its rhythm. The sound—the *feeling*—was a stranger in a world where every sensation was known, catalogued, and endured. It was not a threat. It was… foundational. It felt more ancient than the rock itself.

Slowly, she leaned closer. And another sense, one so atrophied in her people as to be a mere ghost, was awakened. A smell.

It was not the clean, metallic non-smell of the cold and the light. It came from the vibrating lichen. It was a scent of unimaginable complexity: of wet soil and decay, of crushed green leaves, of night-blooming flowers and the dark, musky promise of fungi. It was the smell of a world they only knew from the stories carved in bone. It was the smell of life. Not the sterile, energetic life of the crystal-crabs, but organic, complex, *dark* life. It was the smell of the Yolk.

She did not scream. She did not run. She placed her other hand on the stone, closed her obsidian-goggled eyes, and let the slow, deep heartbeat of the world fill her.

When the next shift came to the Unlit Heart, they found her there. And one by one, as they entered the chamber, they stopped. They felt it in their feet, a vibration that spoke of depths. They knelt, placing their own calloused, sensitive hands upon the floor, and they felt the pulse. They breathed, and for the first time in their lives, their lungs filled with an aroma that was not the sterile cold.

The news did not spread in words. It spread as a change in rhythm. The scraping did not stop. The city was still dug out, day by day. But the scrape-scrape, lift, carry was no longer just an act of defiance against the crushing light. It was an act of cultivation. The beat of their work now had a counterpoint: the impossibly slow, deep thrum from the heart of their world.

They were no longer just Hearthkeepers, waiting. They were gardeners, tending a seed.

The Power’s gift was not an end to their struggle. It was a single, resonant note of hope, introduced into their long, patient song. It was the ultimate act of benevolence: not to solve the mystery, but to confirm that the mystery was real, and that their faith in it was the truest thing in their blinding, beautiful, impossible world. And the Power, satisfied, settled back into its patient, galactic observation, listening as two heartbeats—one slow and biological, the other vast and geological—began, ever so slowly, to find a common rhythm.
