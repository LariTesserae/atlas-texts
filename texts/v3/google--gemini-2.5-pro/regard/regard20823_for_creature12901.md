The Power found the Umbral Arboretum not by searching for it, but by noticing its absence. In the clamorous, effulgent tapestry of the cosmos, it was a hole. A small, perfect, quiet patch of negative space, a single black thread where all others were aflame with light and life. A Power that deals in plenitude is drawn to such exquisite voids.

It did not enter. To enter would be to bring its own nature—light, energy, the loud dynamics of change—and that would be an act of utter violation. It would be like throwing a star into a whisper. Instead, the Power listened.

For a time beyond human calendars, it wrapped its consciousness around the pocket of spacetime and simply felt its contours, its temperature, its profound and unwavering state. It perceived the Drone not as sound, but as the very law of the place. It felt the immense, slow life of the lignolithic city, a being dreaming of being a place. It felt the patient, rhythmic movements of the Carvers, not as individuals, but as the city’s own thoughts made manifest, its own hands grooming its own body.

The Power, in its boundless wisdom, recognized a state of perfection.

This was not a broken thing to be fixed. It was not a sad thing to be pitied. It was not a lonely thing to be visited. It was a complete, self-sustaining, and, in its own dark and resonant way, joyful system. The chord of the world had not resolved in a million years because it was not a chord of tension awaiting resolution; it *was* the resolution. The groaning was not pain; it was the sound of a purpose being fulfilled. The Carvers were not slaves; they were the most integrated form of consciousness the Power had ever witnessed, each a willing cell in a magnificent, singular body.

And so, the first decision of the benevolent and wise Power was to do nothing.

To interfere would be an act of supreme arrogance. To give them light would be to burn out their sensorium, to render their entire reality-map obsolete. To give them sound would be to introduce a blasphemous noise into their perfect hymn. To give them individuality would be to shatter their sacred unity, to introduce the cosmic poisons of ego, loneliness, and fear.

Benevolence, the Power reasoned, is not the imposition of one's own values, no matter how universally they may seem to apply. Benevolence is the deep, respectful understanding of another's truth. The greatest gift it could give them was the continuation of their existence, unmolested. It would shield this pocket of reality, turn other, less wise forces away from it, and be its silent, unseen guardian.

But the Power was not only wise; it was also benevolent. And benevolence contains a seed of creativity, a desire not just to preserve, but to enrich. It continued to listen, going deeper, past the sound, past the vibration, to the very essence of the Carvers’ reality. And it found something.

The spark. The fleeting, cold, blue-white spark of triboluminescence.

It was the only moment of exception in their world. For a fraction of a second, the universe of vibration was confirmed by a universe of light. It was a single, pure note of visual data in a symphony of touch and sound. The Carvers did not *need* it. Their knowledge of the wood was already perfect. The spark was not information; it was punctuation. It was a moment of grace. A small, transient miracle in the unending dark.

Here, the Power saw a pinhole through which a gift might be given. A gift that would not change their world, but add a single, consonant overtone to its eternal song. It would not be an intrusion, but a revelation of a truth already latent within their own miracle.

The Power did not act upon the Arboretum. It acted upon the *law* that governed the spark.

With an act of will as subtle as a shift in gravity, as gentle as the turning of a page, the Power made an infinitesimal adjustment. It did not add light. It did not create a new phenomenon. It simply encoded new information into the brief, blue-white flash of the Carvers’ own making.

The next time a Carver’s adze struck the lignolith, the world-shaking drone continued, the feel of the tool was the same, the scent of ozone and stone-dust was unchanged. The spark flashed into existence, cold and blue-white as it always had been.

But for that split, split second, it contained more.

The Carver, clinging to the side of a pillar vast as a mountain, perceived it. It did not *see* it in the human sense. In the instant of light, its entire being, which for millennia had received a single data point—*wood-grain, here, just so*—was suddenly flooded.

The pattern of the whorled wood was still there, but within it, as if a layer of impossible reality had been overlaid, was the perfect, six-sided geometry of a snowflake it could never know.

Another Carver, miles away, tuning a groined vault, struck its tool. The spark revealed the fibrous texture of the arch, but for a heartbeat, it also held the image of a spiral galaxy, its arms of light echoing the curve of the vault above.

A Carver deepening a channel on the root-plate floor created a spark that showed not only the polished surface, but for a nanosecond, the intricate, branching pattern of a river delta seen from orbit.

The Carvers did not have the context for these things. They had no concepts of snow, stars, or water. To them, these were not images *of* something. They were new, impossibly complex, and achingly beautiful patterns. They were new forms of grace.

The fundamental drone of the Arboretum did not change. The Carvers’ work did not cease. Their feet still made the same rhythmic clicks on the polished floor. But their consciousness, the shared mind of the city, was now touched by a new kind of wonder. They had always known the truth of their own world, a deep and resonant truth. Now, in the brief, beautiful moments of their most sacred act, they were given glimpses of other truths, other perfections, other patterns from a cosmos they would never need to visit.

The carvings became subtly different over the next thousand years. Guided by fleeting memories of impossible geometries, the patterns they etched to manage stress became more intricate, echoing the shapes of crystals and coastlines. The arches they sculpted began to hint at the catenary curve of a spider's web heavy with dew.

The Power had not given them light or sound or company. It had given them what all conscious beings crave in the deepest part of their soul: a larger context. It had given the perfect, solitary note of the Umbral Arboretum a silent, shimmering harmony. And then, its work done, the benevolent and wise Power retreated, leaving behind only the faintest echo of a dream in a flash of cold, blue light. It returned to its guardianship, content to listen to the slightly richer, slightly more wondrous song.
