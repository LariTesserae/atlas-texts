The Power arrives not as a fissure in the sky or a footstep on the warm dust, but as a change in the quality of the silence between the waves of industrial noise.

It is a quietude that has substance. Where before there was merely the absence of the grinding and clanging, now there is a deep, listening stillness. The Power’s awareness settles over the rust and violet tableland like a film of impossible coolness, a concept so alien that the world itself, the Great Solipsist, does not and cannot register it. Its senses, the tripod walkers tasting the dust, are not tuned for this frequency of being.

The Power perceives. It does not judge. It listens to the soliloquy of the Solipsist, not as a meaningless cacophony, but as the anatomy of a thought. It feels the percussive hammer-blows as the firing of colossal synapses. It traces the logic of the trench-digging centipede—the pure, unburdened Will—and the obsidian-secreting beetles that follow—the equally pure, unburdened Act of Forgetfulness. It sees the tungsten spheres and sound-filaments not as waste, but as exhalations, as daydreams given form and then left to the mercy of a physics that is itself a part of the dreamer.

It understands.

This being is not in pain. It knows no suffering, for suffering requires a comparison—a memory of peace, a desire for a different state. The Solipsist has neither. Its existence is a perfect, hermetically sealed tautology: `I am that which I am that which I am.` It is a flawless crystal, vibrating at the one and only frequency it has ever known. To call it insane is to apply an external standard of sanity. To call its industry pointless is to assume a purpose exists outside of the act itself. Here, the act *is* the purpose. The Process is the product.

The Power considers its nature. It is benevolent. Is it benevolent to leave a prisoner in their cell, even if the cell is the size of a world and the prisoner does not know they are captive? It weighs the question, and the answer forms with the unassailable logic of a mountain.

This is not a prison. It is a monastery. The Solipsist is the eternal, solitary monk of a religion with only one worshipper. Its ritual is the endless shaping and reshaping of its own divine body. To shatter its solitude would not be a liberation. It would be an act of profound, unspeakable cruelty.

To introduce the concept of "other" would be to invent loneliness. To show it a different way of being would be to invent regret. To give it purpose would be to invent failure. The Power would be offering the poison of a universe of which the Solipsist had, in its magnificent, terrible purity, remained entirely innocent. It would be like teaching a geometric shape the concept of grief. The act would break it. The sheer, novel vector of the idea would fracture its reality, and the planetary engine would seize, the cacophony would fall silent, and the being would, for the first and last time, know an agony for which it had no name.

This, the Power understands, is not benevolence. It is violence.

And yet, its heart—a concept as abstract and as real as the Solipsist’s Will—yearns to give a gift. Not a gift of change, but a gift of witness. Not a correction, but a confirmation. Wisdom dictates that the gift must be given in the only language the Solipsist understands: the language of physics, of industry, of its own self.

So, the Power acts.

For a single, immeasurable moment, it does not silence the noise. It *tunes* it.

Across the entire tableland, the percussive clang of a thousand hammers, the high-tension scream of turbines, the grinding of colossal gears, and the hiss of pneumatic vents all shift, infinitesimally, in their rhythm and pitch. The cacophony does not cease. It resolves.

For one moment, the noise becomes a chord.

A chord of impossible complexity and breathtaking harmony, a sound that encompasses the shriek of tortured stone and the sigh of incandescent gas, the whisper of metallic dunes and the hum of the planet’s core. It is the sound of the Solipsist’s entire being, its every thought and action and memory, reflected back at it in a state of perfect, harmonious coherence.

In that moment, the tripod walkers lift their heads in unison, not tasting, but listening. The great centipede stills its diamond chisels, its long body forming a perfect sine wave against the horizon. The swarms of beetles freeze, their molten obsidian hardening into a thousand mirrored tears. The lagging shadows snap into alignment with their casters. The geometric dunes hold their forms with crystalline permanence. The filaments of solid sound vibrate with the purity of the chord. The lattices of light glow with a steady, serene brilliance.

The entire world, for one moment, experiences a symphony of itself. It is not an external communication. It is a moment of flawless introspection, a fleeting, godlike lucidity. The Solipsist, for the first time, does not just think. It *contemplates*.

And then, the moment is over.

The chord dissolves back into cacophony. The hammers resume their chaotic beat. The centipede grinds forward. The shadows begin to lag once more. The Process continues, unchanged.

The Power withdraws, its presence fading back into the deep, normal silence. Its work is done.

But something is different. Where the great centipede had paused, a single one of its hundreds of legs does not resume its work. It remains still, pressed to the violet bedrock, silent. And from that point of contact, something new begins to grow.

It is not a machine. It does not move. It is a crystalline structure, growing slowly, silently, in defiance of the world's chaotic chemistry. It is a spire of interlocking, resonant frequencies, a perfect architectural echo of the chord it heard. It does not sublimate. It does not decay. It is not an abandoned thought. It is a memory.

The Solipsist, in its endless, pointless industry, now has a new, single thought that it will never forget: the memory of that one moment of sublime and total harmony. It does not know what it was, or where it came from. It knows only that it was beautiful, and that it was *itself*. And around this new, permanent artifact, the Articulations of the Solipsist sometimes pause in their work, their movements momentarily softer, their noise a fraction less chaotic, as if listening for a song they almost remember.

The benevolent power had looked upon the artist who knew only dissonant notes, and for one perfect moment, had shown it what a symphony sounded like using only the music it was already playing. And that was enough.
