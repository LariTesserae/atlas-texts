The air is not thin, but thick with a cold, heavy wetness that has the definite taste of ozone and metal. It is less an atmosphere and more an unpressurised fluid. Every surface weeps. Not with rain from above, but with a constant, cold sweat exuded from the material of the world itself. The ground is a landscape of raw, nascent crystal, a geology still in the throes of its own birth. Vast, sharp-edged plains of sintered black glass give way to jutting formations that are not yet mountains, but the angular, embryonic scaffolds of them. Their facets are mathematically precise yet unfinished, as if a cosmic geometry is being interrupted mid-calculation. Water, heavy and supercooled, pools in the geometric depressions, slicking every surface with a sheen of near-freezing liquid that does not glitter, as there is no light to catch.

This is a city, but not one made for inhabitants. It is a city that is, perhaps, its own inhabitant. The "buildings" are dense, fractal scaffoldings of a carbon-laced ceramic, branching and intersecting at impossible angles. They rise from the plains like a forest of black thorns, each spire connected to its neighbours by a million gossamer-thin filaments that tremble in a nonexistent wind. The scale is immense, a valley of this dark, urban growth stretching out to a horizon defined only by a deeper layer of gloom. There is no downtown, no periphery; the density of construction is uniform, a relentless, near-solid lattice of material. It is a city under construction, but there are no workers, no machines. The growth is the work. The architecture is assembling itself molecule by molecule, a process so patient and slow that to a casual observer, it would appear static. Yet, it is not silent.

The air is filled with a deafening, layered cacophony. It is not the sound of chaos, but of overwhelming, distributed purpose. The loudest component is a deep, resonant hum that vibrates through the soles of your feet—the sound of the city's growth engine, the thrum of exotic physics knitting reality together. Layered over this is a constant, high-pitched chittering, a sound like a billion microscopic ball-bearings skittering across glass. And beneath that, a wet, scraping, percussive noise, like immense crabs walking on wet stone.

This is the sound of the fauna. In this world devoid of sun or soil, life is not biological in any familiar sense. It is mechanico-elemental, and it is everywhere. The chittering comes from the Conductor-mites, swarms of them that flow like rivers of mercury along the ceramic filaments of the city. They are no larger than a grain of sand, simple, platinum-sheened polyhedra that seem to be agents of information, leaving faint trails of blue phosphorescence that flicker and die as they pass. They are the city's nervous system.

The scraping sound is made by the Fabricators. These are larger, multi-limbed creatures of tungsten and obsidian, their forms vaguely crustacean. They crawl deliberately along the foundational structures, their diamond-hard mandibles grinding raw minerals from the nascent ground. They process the material internally, the low heat of their bodies causing veils of steam to rise around them, and extrude it as the ceramic filament that the mites then climb. They are the city's hands, its 3D printers, building the world one slow thread at a time.

There are larger things, too. In the darkest, deepest valleys of the city-scaffold, vast, slow-moving presences cause the ground to groan and shift. These are the Geologic Molls, beings less animal and more mobile geology. They seem to be pure, living crystal, their immense, amorphous bodies faceted like the landscape itself. They do not build; they are the anchors. Where they settle, the foundations of the city solidify, the raw geometry of the world locking into a more permanent state. Their groans are the low bass note of the world's symphony, a sound of immense pressure and gradual change.

And flitting through the dense upper scaffolds are the Null-Moths. They are sheets of absolute blackness, a darkness that is somehow deeper than the ambient gloom. They absorb all energy, all light, all vibration. Their passage is marked by sudden pockets of perfect silence and cold in the overwhelming noise. They feed on entropy, on the waste heat of the Fabricators and the dying light-trails of the mites. They are the city’s sanitation system, its recyclers.

The only light is the cold, functional bioluminescence of the world's processes. The fleeting blue tracks of the mites, the cherry-red glow deep within the translucent torsos of the Fabricators as they process mineral slurry, and rare, sudden flashes of ultraviolet light from the Geologic Molls as they settle and discharge titanic amounts of static energy. The world is a flickering, strobe-lit factory operating in near-total darkness.

To be here is to be an anomaly, an unrecognized dataset in a closed system. The rules are brutally simple. There is no food chain, only a production line. The world is a single, distributed organism disguised as a city, and the fauna are its organelles. It eats the raw energy of its own formation, the exotic physics that bubble up from beneath the crystal plains. It is growing, slowly, patiently, toward an unknown purpose. Every component, from the smallest mite to the largest moll, is dedicated to this construction. It is a place without malice, but also without welcome. It does not notice you. The sound is deafening, the air is freezing and wet, and the darkness is absolute, punctuated only by the cold fire of relentless creation. The city does not know what it is building itself to be; its construction is the long, slow process of discovering its own function.
