This is a place without sight. There is no light, and therefore no a priori direction, no up or down established by a sun or sky. You find your bearings by other means.

First, by the air. It is not air so much as a low-temperature steam, thick and warm and absolutely saturated with water. It has the weight and presence of a blanket. On your skin, it doesn’t evaporate; it simply joins the existing sheen of moisture, beading and trickling in runnels that have nothing to do with sweat. To breathe is to feel a cool wave moving into you, tasting of ozone, wet chalk, and something metallic, like blood in the back of the throat. The atmosphere is dense, pressing, a medium that feels just shy of being a liquid.

Second, by the ground. It is not rock, not soil, not mud. It is a yielding, porous, warm surface, something like cork that has been soaked for a thousand years. It is a world in a state of pre-formation. Call it geo-flesh, an un-stone that is just beginning to decide what it will become. It is everywhere damp, not from puddles on its surface but from water weeping out of its very substance. Pressure from your foot causes a sigh of mineral-rich water to well up around your boot, and when you move, the ground slowly, audibly thirsts it back in.

This chamber is not large. It is a hidden pocket, a geological geode high in the heart of a mountain. The walls are not walls but the same nascent material as the floor, growing inwards. Space is not carved out of rock here; it is the void left between material that is actively, if imperceptibly, coming into being.

The chamber is dense with these emergences. Everywhere, smooth, featureless lobes of the geo-flesh swell into the space. Bulbous columns, lacking the tell-tale drip-lines of stalactites, reach for one another in the dark, their surfaces slick and warm. Crystalline structures grow directly from these soft surfaces, not like quartz crystals in a void, but like splinters of bone pushing through skin. They are sharp, fibrous, and hum with a faint, internal resonance if you happen to brushing against them. This is a landscape under construction, but the construction has been stalled at the moment of genesis for an eon. It is a state of perpetual potential, never resolving into a finished form. Stable, but raw.

There is sound, but it is deeply quiet. There is the constant, soft *weep* of the world-stuff exuding its water. There are patient, fat drips falling from the larger lobes into unseen pools, the sound a soft, absorbent *plup* rather than a sharp splash. And underneath it all, a hum. It is too low to be a distinct note, felt more in the bones and teeth than heard with the ears. It is the sound of the geology itself, the sound of slow crystallization, of molecular bonds locking into place over centuries. It is the single, unchanging, foundational sound of the world's own creation, playing on a loop.

Life here is an afterthought, an opportunist. There is no green. In the thin, weeping films of water grow veils of what might be mycelia or bacterial mats. They are ghost-pale and cling to the curves of the geo-flesh, thriving on the dissolved minerals. Their faint, musty smell is a minor chord in the overtone of rock and water. Here and there, a single, pallid stalk, no thicker than a thread, rises a few inches and puts forth a delicate, translucent cap before dissolving back into the damp.

Rarer still are the grazers. Wandering across the soft floor are creatures like flattened, many-legged woodlice, their shells a polished, pearlescent white. They are blind, silent, and move with a slow deliberation, scraping the fungal veils from the rock with complex, unseen mouthparts. Their presence is so scarce you may only find the faint, clean trail they leave behind on a slimy surface.

But you are not the first of your kind here. The evidence is scattered, and as quiet as everything else. In one alcove, the ground has been smoothed and compressed into a sleeping-pallet. A single-fired clay pot, unglazed and dark, sits half-sunk into the soft floor, its inner surface slick with collected mineral water. The most telling artifact is a tool: a shard of the fibrous crystal that grows here, its base wrapped tightly in woven strands of the pale mycelia to form a handle. It is a simple pick or knife.

Someone comes here, or came here. Perhaps they still do. They don't build; they barely leave a trace. They come to harvest. In certain protected hollows of the geo-flesh, the weeping is so slow and the mineral content so high that the water emerges not as a liquid, but as a viscous, jelly-like pearl. They form over decades, soft and warm, capturing the low hum of the chamber within them. The harvesters, the quiet ones, come with their bone-crystal knives to delicately scoop out these geological tears, leaving behind a shallow, concave wound in the world that immediately begins to weep, and to heal. It is a place of infinite, static birth, and they are its patient midwives.
