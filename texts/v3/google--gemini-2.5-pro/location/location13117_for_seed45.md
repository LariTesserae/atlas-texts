The silence is not an absence, but a presence. It is a thick, warm blanket in the near-total darkness, a substance with weight. The air, if it can be called that, does not move. There is no wind to scour these heights, no breath of cooler air from a distant valley. The warmth is constant, radiant, emanating from the ground itself—a deep, geothermal hum you feel in the soles of your feet and the core of your bones. It is the warmth of an oven slowly cooling, or perhaps, slowly heating.

You are standing on a plateau of what might be obsidian or cooled basalt, but it feels wrong. It's too smooth, too sheer, lacking the grittiness of rock. It is a surface of pure, black mineral, seamless and uncracked. Across this plain, scattered with the thoughtful emptiness of a zen garden, rise the true features of this world.

They are not mountains. Not yet. They are the process of mountains, captured in a single, ongoing moment. Spires, needles, and impossible blades of the same black material are actively extruding from the ground. They grow. Not with the slowness of geology, but with the terrifying, silent speed of a crystal forming in a saturated solution. The growth is turbulent, chaotic. A new needle, thin as a hair and a hundred meters tall, might pierce the dark in a matter of minutes, glowing a dull, angry cherry-red at its tip from the friction and heat of its own creation. Then, its growth might halt, its inner light fading to black over the course of an hour, becoming just another silhouette against a starless void.

The scale is immense. The plateau you are on is merely a ledge on a far greater structure, one that falls away into unseen depths. Across a chasm that could swallow a city, other, similar plateaus are birthing their own forests of spires. Some spires grow straight and true, others corkscrew, some blossom at their tips into complex, fractal florets of glass before their own weight causes them to implode in a slow, silent cascade of black shards. The sound is not an explosion, but a muffled, percussive *thump* that travels through the solid ground, a single heartbeat in the quiet. The air is too thin, too alien, to carry sound with any real force. You hear mostly through the rock. High-pitched *tinks* and *pings* of cooling mineral. A deep, resonant groan as a massive new foundation is laid somewhere in the dark.

There is no erosion here. No water to carve canyons, no wind to sandblast faces. Every edge is razor-sharp. Every surface is new. This world is being born, not dying. The raw geology is on full display, a riot of creation without purpose.

Occasionally, the growth creates forms that mock intention. Two spires, rising in proximity, might intersect and fuse, forming a perfect archway a kilometer high. A wall of glass might extrude in a clean, straight line for miles, a defensive curtain for a non-existent city, before it shatters under its own internal stress, leaving behind a field of what looks like megalithic rubble. These are not ruins; they are failures of formation, geologic ghosts. This accidental architecture is the only mark of anything other than the raw, vertical drive to exist.

And on the surfaces of the older, cooler spires, something else is happening. Where the internal heat has finally subsided, a delicate, filamentary substance begins to precipitate out of the strange atmosphere. It is not a plant, not a fungus. It is a mineral frost, but it gathers in patterns that look organic. It forms fine, white webs in the corners and joints of the black glass, clings to sharp edges like luminous fur, and catches the faint, ambient glow from the newer, hotter formations. It is pre-life, or non-life, a pattern of deposition that mimics biology. This acrition-frost is the only thing here that is not black or glowing red. It is a quiet, ghostly white.

There is no sense of up or down in the sky, only a uniform, lightless ceiling. The only light comes from the land itself: the fierce but short-lived glow of creation, and the faint, collective starlight of the white mineral frost. The world is a field of embers, endlessly renewed.

It feels empty of will. No creature skitters across these plains. No predator waits in the shadows of the spires. It is a world of pure physics, of chemical potential unfolding into kinetic reality. But the silence, the warmth, the feeling of the rock humming under your feet, the sight of a new mountain being born before your eyes—it doesn't feel dead. It feels embryonic. It is the sound of a world being written, the volume turned down so low you can only feel the vibration of the pen.
