The sound is a solid thing. It is the first wall you hit, a pressure against the skin and in the bones before you can even register it as noise. It is a physical presence, a deafening, unending, complex chord of a billion living things. It is the grinding groan of immense, woody structures straining against their own weight, the wet, percussive dripping from a million leaves, the overlapping, frantic, stridulating panic of countless insects, and the bass hum of something vast and resonant at the center of it all. The sound is the atmosphere, the medium through which everything else moves. To be here is to be inside a bell at the moment it is struck, and the moment never ends.

You stand in a chamber. Despite the overwhelming vegetation that constitutes the world, the space itself is profoundly empty. It is a cathedral of impossible biology, a single, hollow grove the size of a town square. The ground is not soil but a springy, damp trampoline of woven root and living moss, slick with condensation and giving slightly underfoot. From this living floor rise the walls: immense, interwoven trunks of something akin to banyan or mangrove, spiralled and braided together in a feat of purposeful, ancient architecture. They are not separate trees, but pillars and buttresses of a single, city-sized organism. The colour is not the vibrant green of a young jungle, but the deep, weathered tones of old bark, dark mosses, and leprous, pale fungi that trace patterns like cracked porcelain. This is a settled place, a form that has found its equilibrium over millennia. It does not grow so much as it *endures*, breathing on a geological timescale.

Above, the canopy is a dense latticework of leaves and branches, so thick that it forms a complete ceiling. Yet, light is normal, even bright. It filters through in thick, hazy shafts, as if through the stained-glass windows of a church, illuminating motes of pollen, spores, and glittering water droplets that hang in the warm, viscous air. The temperature is that of a hothouse, a constant, humid warmth that makes the air feel heavy and cloying in the lungs. Water is not a river or a lake, but a pervasive condition. It weeps from the living walls, running in resinous, slow trickles down the bark. It gathers in dark, mirror-like pools on the lowest points of the root-floor, perfectly still except for the dimple of a falling drop.

The emptiness of the vast central volume is a lie, a trick of the eye. The space is swarming with abundant fauna, but they are not made of flesh and bone in any conventional sense. They are made of the sound. The reality here is thin, and the deafening noise bleeds through, congealing into temporary life. Shimmering, insect-like things flicker in the shafts of light—they are not dragonflies, but knots of high-frequency vibration, impossibly fast and sharp-edged, their wings the visual artifact of a piercing shriek. Larger shapes drift through the open air: slow, cetacean forms that are the physical manifestation of a deep, resonant hum, their bodies blurring at the edges as the note wavers. They are echo-fauna, acoustic ghosts.

This ecosystem is brutally active. A predator-prey dynamic exists, but it is one of frequency and amplitude. A swarm of chittering, staccato "insects" will be scattered and consumed not by a mouth, but by the arrival of a broad, low-frequency wave that passes through the chamber, its very presence dissolving their coherence. What you see is a momentary snapshot of the soundscape. As the light shifts and a different part of the plant-city groans into a new position, the chord of the chamber changes, and the fauna within it warp, dissipate, and are born anew. A creature might be a memory of a sound that has already passed. A flock of flute-like calls might coalesce into a brief, soaring "bird" before the sound fades and it unravels back into the vibrating air.

This is a city, but it has no citizens, only the architecture and the phenomena it generates. The woven trunks form colonnades. Higher up, you can see balconies dripping with ferns, and bridges of living vine arcing across impossible gaps between spiraling towers. It is a metropolis built by a silent, non-technological intelligence for a population that is created and destroyed by the city's own voice.

To stay here for any length of time is to feel your own senses begin to break down. The constant, physical pressure of the sound begins to feel like a touch. You might see a flicker in your peripheral vision and be unable to tell if it was a sound-creature or just an optical remnant of the overwhelming noise. The boundary between your thoughts and the environment thins. A sudden memory might seem to manifest as a shimmer of light in the corner of the chamber. The very space feels unstable; a far-off buttress might appear to ripple and twist, not because it is moving, but because the sound waves between you and it have bent the light, offering you a glimpse of what it looked like a moment before, or a moment from now. You are not an observer here. You are an instrument, being played by the place. And the song is everything.
