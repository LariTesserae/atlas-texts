This is a world of verticality, suspended in a warm, gelatinous medium. You are not swimming in water; you are moving through a substance that feels like warm, infinitely clear oil, a planetary ocean of it, so dense it holds you effortlessly at any depth. There is no surface, no sky, only the endless, glowing volume of the medium itself. The light is a uniform, pearlescent glow that seems to emanate from the very substance you are in, eliminating all shadow and making distance a matter of conjecture.

The terrain is a mountain range, but not one that has been worn by wind or rain. These are the fresh, violent spires of a world geologically born and then immediately submerged. They are impossibly sharp. Peaks of obsidian-black and dark basaltic rock rise from an unseen floor thousands of feet below, their ridges like knife-blades, their faces sheer and unblemished by erosion. To move through them is to navigate a forest of needles, a landscape of pure, unblunted angles. You are high among these peaks, drifting through passes and along cliff faces that have never known air.

Life here is lush, dense, and utterly alien. It clings to the sheer rock in a slow-motion riot. The primary lifeform is a type of growth, neither plant nor fungus, that forms vast, hanging tapestries. Great curtains of it, which could be called ‘glass-kelp,’ drape from the ridges—perfectly transparent, broad ribbons threaded with veins of slow-pulsing, pale green light. They are a hundred feet long, catching the ambient glow and fracturing it into subtle, shifting rainbows.

Beneath these shimmering veils, the rock itself is carpeted in a living velvet. It is a deep, chlorophyll-rich moss that grows in thick, pillowy layers, so dense it softens the brutal sharpness of the rock beneath. It seems to feed directly from the ambient energy in the medium, and to the touch, it is cool and firm, exuding a slow, rhythmic warmth.

Higher up, closer to the needle-like summits, are the float-orbs. These are spherical organisms, ranging from the size of a fist to the size of a carriage, anchored to the peaks by long, coiling tethers of black, fibrous tissue. The orbs are semi-translucent, their interiors filled with a complex lattice of golden structures. They are buoyant, tugging gently on their anchors with an ageless patience, forming a kind of inverted canopy far above the velvet-covered slopes. Their purpose is unknown; they simply are. The space is saturated with these three forms of life, layered one on top of the other, touching, intersecting, creating a dense, three-dimensional jungle of stillness.

Sound is almost entirely absent. The viscous medium smothers it, turning any potential noise into a dull, distant pressure wave. There is no rush of water, no wind, no calls of animals. There is only a profound, bottomless quiet. If you listen with your whole body, you might perceive a low, sub-audible hum, the resonant frequency of the entire world-ocean vibrating with its own internal energy. Occasionally, a glass-kelp ribbon, disturbed by a slow current you cannot otherwise feel, might slide against another, producing a faint, attenuated chime like thick glass being rubbed slowly. These are the only sounds.

Fauna is profoundly rare, almost legendary. The world is not empty of it, but its cycles are so slow as to be geological. For centuries at a time, the only movement is the imperceptible growth of the flora and the lazy drift of the currents. But then, an observer might witness the passage of a Great Sail. It is a creature so vast and slow it is often mistaken for a mobile landform. Its body is a crystalline lattice, miles long, a living mountain of interlocking geometric plates that glint with their own internal light. It moves through the open spaces between the highest peaks, its enormous, fin-like sails of shimmering membrane catching solar and energetic winds that are undetectable to anything else. It does not eat; it filters nutrients from the medium on a scale that takes millennia. Its passage is the only major event in this world, a silent, majestic migration from one silent epoch to the next.

The rules here are simple. Energy is ambient, drawn from the glowing medium. Life does not need soil or sunlight. Growth is measured in centuries, not seasons. Change is a concept belonging to other worlds. There is no struggle, only being. You are a mote of dust in a vast, warm, illuminated cathedral, a place of profound stillness and strange, silent life, waiting patiently for nothing at all.
