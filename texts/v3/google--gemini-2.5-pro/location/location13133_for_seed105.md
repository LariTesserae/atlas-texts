This world is drowned.

There is no surface, no shore, only the crushing, eternal calm of a world made liquid. The "air" here is a dense, gelid fluid, unimaginably cold, holding the pressure of a depth that has forgotten sunlight. We are high up, on the spine of a massive, ancient mountain range, yet the weight of the world-ocean above is a physical presence, a constant pressure on the bones, a hum felt deep in the teeth.

The peaks and ridges are not sharp. Eons ago, before the Inundation, they were carved by wind and ice into grand, sweeping forms. Now, those forms have been settled, smoothed, and softened by millennia of immense, quiet pressure. The rock is a dark, slick basalt, worn to a dull sheen, its surface cool and unyielding to the touch. These are the bones of a dead world, now the foundation for a new and stranger one. Vast, open valleys fall away into utter blackness on either side of the ridge, chasms so deep that they seem to be fractures in reality itself.

From this ancient rock grows a silent forest. Or rather, a singing one. There is no animal life here—no fish, no crustaceans, no slithering things in the silt. The world is devoid of movement, of hunger, of predation. But it is not devoid of life. This is a kingdom of flora, lush and strange.

They are called Chime-stalks. They grow in sparse, scattered groves, leaving vast expanses of bare, resonant rock between them. They are not green; they are the colors of deep-sea bioluminescence and cold fire. Tall, vitreous stalks, some thick as an old tree and others thin as a whip, rise from the stone. They are translucent, their insides filled with a complex lattice of crystalline filaments that pulse with a soft, internal light—a cold, shifting glow of cobalt blue, faint violet, and bone-white. They are not photosynthesizing; they feed on telluric currents, drawing a slow, powerful energy directly from the geological activity of the planet's core.

And they are loud. The silence of the deep does not exist here. The world is filled with sound. It begins with a constant, low-frequency groan, the sound of unimaginable pressures shifting and grinding on a tectonic scale. It is a drone that vibrates through the liquid, through the rock, through you. Overlaid on this foundation is the music of the Chime-stalks. The currents of the deep, slow as weather fronts, move through the groves, causing the stalks to sway. As they bend, their internal crystal structures scrape and resonate against each other, producing a symphony of clear, overlapping tones. It is not a gentle wind-chime melody; it is a massive, resonant, sometimes dissonant chorus of singing glass, echoing for miles across the abyssal plains. Sometimes a single, massive stalk will discharge a build-up of energy, and a deep, sonorous *BOOM* will roll through the valley, its light flaring in a silent, momentary blue starburst.

The weather of this place is the current. Slow, inexorable rivers of slightly colder or denser fluid drift through the landscape, their passing marked by a change in the pitch of the Chime-stalks' song and a shimmering distortion in the distant, glowing groves. Occasionally, a deep geothermal vent will rupture on a valley floor. A plume of super-heated, mineral-rich fluid will billow upwards for days, and the flora in its path will enter a frenzy of growth, their light and sound intensifying into a roaring, incandescent pillar.

Scattered amongst the groves, and standing alone on bald promontories, are the only signs of craft. They are simple, powerful structures of stacked stone. No mortar, just immense, dark slabs of basalt, lifted and placed with an impossible precision. Some are tall, hollow monoliths, like unlidded sarcophagi stood on end. Others are low, circular walls, like listening dishes aimed at the sky that will never be seen. They are weathered, ancient, sharing the same settled permanence as the mountains themselves.

There are no inhabitants. There is nothing to hunt, nothing to eat. The builders are a ghost, their purpose an enigma. A prevailing theory, if one could call it that, is that these structures were not dwellings. They are acoustic instruments. They are resonance chambers, amplifiers, focusing lenses built to capture and augment the deep song of the world. They are primitive technology designed not to conquer nature, but to listen to it more intently. Here, on a high ridge, one such stone circle stands. To sit within its perimeter is to feel the tectonic groan of the planet become a clear, physical vibration, and to hear the chiming of a distant grove, miles away, as if it were singing directly into your skull.

This is a world of active, vibrant loneliness. It is a garden without a gardener, an orchestra without a conductor, a cathedral without a god. It is dynamic, its light and sound in constant flux, a living system of rock and botany in a cold, eternal dance. But with no eyes to see it, no minds to comprehend it, save for the faint memory of its builders fossilized in stone, the world simply sings its loud, strange, beautiful song into the crushing, luminous dark.
