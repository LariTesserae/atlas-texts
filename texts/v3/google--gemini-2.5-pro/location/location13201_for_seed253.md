The Sound is the first thing, and the last. It is not a collection of noises but a single, omnipresent fact. It is a bass thrum that is felt more than heard, a pressure on the bones and in the fluid of the eyes. This is the choral drone of a billion billion lives, a resonant hum that has saturated the very medium of the world for uncounted eons. It does not rise or fall; it simply is.

This world is a liquid. Not water, but something denser, more viscous, that holds pressure and temperature with an unyielding stability. It is cool, the kind of deep, patient cold that leaches warmth over millennia, not moments. There is no surface, no sky. There is only the medium, and what stands within it.

And what stands is everything. This is a place of impossible verticals. Immense pillars, trunks of a primordial forest petrified into a substance harder than stone, rise from unseen depths and ascend into an equally unseen gloom. These are the world's mountains, its continents, its entire geology. There is no rock here, no primal crust. The substrate is life. The pillars, some as wide as mountain ranges, are the lithified remains of a single, ancient botanical kingdom. Their surfaces, worn smooth by the slow, inexorable currents of eternity, are like polished bone. They are not gray or brown but have the deep, layered iridescence of fossilized wood, pearlescent sheens that are invisible in the crushing dark.

Though the "vegetation" forms the entirety of the solid world, the structures themselves are sparsely placed. Between the colossal pillars are chasms of open fluid, voids so vast they could swallow weather systems. To cross from one pillar to the next is a journey of days, a slow drift through absolute blackness, guided only by the unchanging thrum of the world-sound. This is the landscape's great contradiction: it is entirely full, yet mostly empty.

This is a built world, but it was not built by hands. It is the architectural output of a biological process that reached its zenith and then froze in a state of perfect, unchanging equilibrium. The pillars are not simple columns. From their sides extend great, sweeping buttresses of the same petrified material, arching out into the void to connect with a neighboring trunk miles away, forming galleries and aqueducts of breathtaking scale. The pattern is not random; it follows an ancient, organic logic, like the branching of a vascular system or the filigree of a diatom's shell, magnified to a geological scale. There has been no growth, no collapse, no significant erosion for longer than the concept of time has meaning. The world has settled into its final, most stable form.

The darkness is total, a physical presence. Yet, the world is not without light. The fauna, who are abundant, are the light. The surfaces of the great pillars are not bare. They are carpeted in vast, continent-sized mats of sessile organisms, living tapestries that pulse with a cold, rhythmic bioluminescence. Patches the size of cities glow in soft violet, then fade as a neighboring region brightens in ethereal blue. These waves of light are slow, hypnotic, and as unchanging in their pattern as the drone is in its frequency. They reveal the sheer faces of the pillars, the gentle curve of a continent-sized arch, the silent, gaping mouths of ancient passageways bored through the petrified wood.

Through the great open spaces between the pillars drift the true inhabitants. These are not fish, not leviathans, but something else. They are shoals, massive, cohesive clouds of smaller organisms that move as one. A single shoal might be miles long, a river of living light flowing through the blackness. They are the source of the Sound. Each individual creature produces a single, resonant note, and their combined chorus, amplified and carried by the dense medium, creates the eternal thrum. They drift in permanent, cyclical migrations, tracing the same invisible pathways between the pillars that their ancestors traced a billion years ago. They feed on microscopic matter shed by the light-carpets, and in death, their bodies dissolve back into the medium to nourish them in turn. It is a closed system, perfect and time-invariant.

There is no technology here. Nothing is made or unmade. The only process is life, and the only change is the slow, ceaseless, predictable procession of the light-shoals through the chasms of the dead-plant titans. To be here is to be suspended in a final, ageless thought, a world that has already happened and is content to simply be, forever. The pressure is a constant embrace, the cold a final stillness, and the sound and the light are the steady, eternal pulse of a world dreaming of itself.
