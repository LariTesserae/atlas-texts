The first thing is the sound. It is not a collection of noises, but a single, monolithic drone that has the physical weight of a waterfall. It is a pressurized hum, a complex chord of a million million tiny frictions, a standing wave of stridulation that fills the vastness and presses in on the ears. It is the sound of a system in perfect, deafening equilibrium, and it never, ever changes.

This place is a nation underground. Not a cave, not a tunnel, but a void carved out by some immense and recent fracture in the world's bedrock. The scale is that of a broad mountain valley, miles long and perhaps a mile wide, but sealed beneath a ceiling so high it is lost in the permanent dimness. The terrain is newborn, geologically speaking. The floor is a pavement of obsidian-like stone, shattered in places into razor-edged shards and plates. Great pillars, hundreds of feet thick, rise to support the unseen roof, their surfaces not worn smooth by time but still showing the raw, angular grain of the snap that made them. Everywhere are sharp angles, clean breaks, and surfaces of conchoidal fracture that gleam like black glass. The geometry is one of violence, frozen at the moment of its creation.

The air is cool and absolutely still, carrying a permanent, clammy dampness. There is no rain, no rivers, no pools. Instead, every surface is coated in a greasy film of condensation. Water beads on the stone facets, catching the faint light, but never gathers enough mass to drip. It is a world of perpetual dew, the moisture exhaled by the rock itself, trapped and endlessly recycled in the stagnant atmosphere. The chill is not the biting cold of ice, but the deep, preservative coolness of a root cellar.

Sparsely, across the colossal floor and climbing the lower flanks of the pillars, are the only plants. They are not plants in a familiar sense, but sprawling, map-like colonies of phosphor-lichen. They grow in flat, continent-shaped patches, glowing with a cold and stagnant viridian light. This is the only illumination. It is a dim, sourceless glow that does not cast sharp shadows but rather creates a world of soft, graded darkness. The light is as constant as the sound, a biological fuel cell that has reached a perfect, unwavering output.

And between these glowing patches, in the vast spaces of polished stone, is the fauna. The source of the sound. The architects of the equilibrium. They are overwhelming. Not in size, but in sheer, unthinkable number.

They are six-legged things, the size of a human hand, with carapaces of polished dun and grey that make them nearly invisible against the stone when they are still. Call them the Percussives. Their movements are the second layer of the sound: the dry, infinite clicking of millions of tarsal claws on stone. They move in vast, slow rivers of bodies, flowing around the shattered pillars and across the plains of black rock in organized, predictable currents. There is no panic, no frenzy. There is only the Work.

In the mandibles of almost every Percussive is a tool: a sharp-edged flake of the obsidian-like rock, broken from the fractured terrain. This is their technology. They use these simple blades for a single purpose: to scrape the glowing lichen from the rock. They move in their millions to the edge of a viridian patch, and in unison, they begin to shave the luminous growth away. The scraping sound—a high, silicate shushing—is the third and highest frequency of the great drone.

They are not destroying the lichen. They are harvesting it. The lichen grows back at the precise rate at which it is consumed. The population of the Percussives is perfectly calibrated to the lichen’s regenerative capacity. When a Percussive dies—its lifespan measured in some unknowable but rigid unit of time—its body is immediately set upon by its kin. The carapace is cracked open, the flesh consumed, the component materials put back into the system. For every one that falls, another hatches from a crevice, its shell hardening, its instinct already driving it to find a stone shard and join the Work. The population does not fluctuate by a single individual.

The emptiness of the place is a part of its fullness. The vast spaces between lichen patches are not truly empty; they are fallow fields waiting for the slow, inexorable migration of the harvesters. The silence between the pillars is not silence; it is simply a place where the drone has a different acoustic reflection.

There are no rules here but one: the system must not change. The birth rate equals the death rate. The rate of consumption equals the rate of growth. The output of light is constant. The temperature is constant. The sound is the endless, perfectly repeating anthem of this stability. To stand here is to feel time itself dissolve, to be an observer in a world that achieved its final state moments after its creation and will now hold that state for eternity, a self-sustaining engine of chitin, stone, and cold light, humming its one and only note into the deep.
