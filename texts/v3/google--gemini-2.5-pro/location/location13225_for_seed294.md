You are suspended in a medium denser than water, colder than ice, yet which refuses to freeze. It is not liquid in the way of a sea, but a supercritical fluid, a state of matter that is a gas and a liquid at once, pressing in from all sides with a uniform, immense weight. To breathe it is to feel your lungs fill with a substance like cold, heavy silk. The silence is profound, but it is a charged silence, the quiet of a room where a voltage is building.

This is a world of impossible architecture. There is no ground, no rock, no soil. Everything is made. Immense, flat-topped pylons of a vitreous, obsidian-like material rise from the abyssal depths below and vanish into the gloom above. They are impossibly ancient, their once-sharp edges rounded by eons of flow into soft, organic curves. They feel not like worked stone, but like something grown and then petrified. These pillars are scattered across a vast, open space, separated by kilometers of the churning, pressurised fluid. Connecting some of them are causeways, drooping arches hundreds of meters wide, their surfaces worn glassy-smooth. It is a metropolis designed for giants, now submerged and scoured by time into a geological formation. This is the final, stable state of a forgotten creation.

But stability is a lie. The fluid medium, the "atmosphere," is in a state of perpetual, silent chaos. This is the source of the world's energy. Immense, invisible currents and vortices form and collapse without warning. Pressure fronts, tangible as moving walls of force, sweep through the open spaces, their passage causing the very fluid to flicker with a dim, internal luminescence—a cold, blue-green triboluminescence that provides the only light. The world is a dim, strobing gloom. One moment, you can see the distant silhouette of a dozen pylons; the next, a shift in pressure snuffs out the light and you are in absolute black, feeling only the titanic, grinding forces of the medium as it tears against itself in shear zones.

There is no plant life. Nothing grows on the black, glassy surfaces of the constructs. The entire ecosystem is driven by the turbulence. Anchored to the pylons and causeways are what might be mistaken for mineral deposits, but are in fact life. These are the Siphons, beings of living ceramic and gel. They are shaped like bells, their outer shells the same polished black as the structures they inhabit, but their inner surfaces are a matrix of pulsing, crystalline fibers. They feed directly on the pressure changes, flexing and resonating with the passing fronts, converting kinetic force into the energy of life. When a major vortex passes, their resonant hum can be felt through the dense medium, a low, guttural vibration that is more felt in the bones than heard.

Far more numerous are the Glass-Skaters. They travel in sparse, sweeping shoals through the open voids between the monoliths. They are not swimmers; they are riders of chaos. Each creature is a shard of jointed, flexible carapace, perhaps two meters long, shaped like a hydrofoil. They are eyeless, navigating by sensing the minutest pressure gradients. As a shear zone approaches, they adjust the angle of their bodies with sharp, clicking sounds, catching the wave of force and being hurled at immense speed across the dark. Their passage is a series of these staccato clicks and the faint, high-pitched whine of their carapaces stressing against the flow. They are silvery and reflective, and when the fluid flashes with light, a whole shoal can ignite in a momentary, breathtaking constellation of scattered stars.

The turbulence dictates all. The ancient, built world is the static stage, but the dynamic fluid is the play. To be here is to exist in a state of constant, violent change that is utterly silent. You feel a pull as a new current establishes itself. In the distance, a pressure wave illuminates a vast, hanging archway you hadn't seen before. A shoal of Skaters, caught unawares by a collapsing vortex, are sent tumbling end over end, their clicking frantic and disordered. On the surface of a nearby pylon, the Siphons begin to glow more brightly, their deep hum intensifying as they drink deeply from the invisible storm. There is no up or down, only away from the greater pressure and toward the lesser. There is no safety, only the brief, calculated stability of aligning oneself with the howling, silent forces that have ground this artificial world into a new kind of nature.
