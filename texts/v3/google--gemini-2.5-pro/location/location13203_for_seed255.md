The sound is the first thing, and the last. It is not a single note, but a grinding, tectonic chord played by a world in the act of becoming. It is a bass-heavy groan of immense pressure, the high, tearing screech of crystalline structures failing, and the constant, percussive shudder of something massive being settled into place. The air is thick with this noise, a physical presence that presses on the ears and vibrates through the soles of the feet.

This is a place of low elevation, a basin or a vast, shallow concavity. But the ground is no natural substrate. It is a floor, a pavement of dark, semi-vitrified material, like slag from a colossal, primitive kiln. It is uneven, cracked in places, and everywhere slick with a cool, persistent dampness. There is no weather in the conventional sense, no rain, but the cool air is so saturated with moisture that condensation weeps constantly on every vertical surface, gathering in the crazed network of fissures on the floor to form shivering, ink-black pools.

From this made-ground, the world rises. It is not built in the sense of discrete blocks being laid one upon another. It is extruded. Countless columns, walls, and angular buttresses push slowly, inexorably, upwards from the floor. They are dense, packed so tightly together that in many places the gaps between them are no wider than a hand's breadth. This is a forest of architecture, a city of unthinking pillars. The scale is open—one can sense the basin stretching for miles in every direction—but the local experience is one of claustrophobia, of being hemmed in by the slow, loud birth of these structures.

The material of these growths is a kind of ceramic-chitin hybrid, rough and abrasive to the touch, colored in bands of bruised purple, dull grey, and oxidized black. They are not inert. This world is forever emerging, raw and unfinished. The sound is their growth. The upward, grinding pressure causes parts of the structures to fracture, shearing off in razor-edged sheets that fall to the floor with a ringing crash, adding to the debris field. The constant dampness finds its way into these fractures, and the slow, hydraulic pressure of this captured water groans deep within the pillars.

There is no sun here, no moon. The darkness is near-total, but the world is not without light. The process itself is the source of illumination. Where the pressure is greatest, where a new edge is being forced into existence, thin, crawling lines of stress-luminescence flare to life. They are veins of angry orange and cold electric blue, tracing the immense forces at work. They flicker for a moment, illuminating a small pocket of the oppressive dark, and then fade, their brief revelation swallowed once more by the gloom. These fleeting, localized strobes are the only way to see, making navigation a disorienting act of memorizing the landscape in shattered, momentary glimpses.

It is a world without vegetation. No moss clings to the damp surfaces, no lichen exploits the mineral wealth. The air tastes clean, sterile, like a deep cave, with only the sharp tang of ozone from the fracturing material and the smell of wet clay.

Life is rare, and it is not a incidental occupant but a part of the system. Scuttling in the tight warrens between the growing columns are the Groove-tenders. They are low, armored creatures, like multi-legged trilobites the size of a human hand. Blind, they navigate by sound and by sensing the vibrations in the floor. Their purpose seems to be to scour the newly-formed material, their hardened mandibles grinding away imperfections and weak points on the surface of the columns. Their scraping is a high, chittering descant that rides above the deep groaning of the world's growth, another layer in the deafening symphony. They are a maintenance crew, or perhaps a form of symbiotic parasite, their existence inextricably linked to the world's constant, loud self-construction. Moving among them, but far, far rarer, are larger things, silent walkers that glide through the labyrinth on padded feet, their forms never fully caught by the fleeting light, leaving only the sense of a vast, quiet shape passing in the dark.

The rules of this world are the rules of its making. There is only one direction: up. There is only one state: becoming. The floor is the past, a solid record of what has been extruded and has ceased to grow. The active zone of growth is the present, a place of immense sound and kinetic force. The open, dark space above the columns is the future, a void waiting to be filled. To exist here is to be a witness, or a component, in a single, vast, and tireless act of primitive construction, a project with no apparent architect and no discernible end.
