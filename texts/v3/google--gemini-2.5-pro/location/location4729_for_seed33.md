This is a place of impossible height. You are standing at the top of the world, on a needle-thin peak that should not be able to support its own weight, let alone the structure that surrounds you. The air is thin, cold, and utterly dry; breathing is a conscious effort, a drawing in of a substance that feels more like a vacuum than a gas. There is a faint, metallic taste to it, like ozone or cold-forged iron. The temperature is a constant, biting cool that seeps through any covering, a cold not of ice, but of deep space.

This is a city.

The ground beneath you is not rock, not exactly. It is a surface of interlocking, dark grey facets that absorb the dim light. It is one of a million such platforms, ledges, and walkways that constitute the Shroud-City of the Apex. There are buildings here, but they were not built. There are towers, but they do not follow the logic of engineering. They have *grown*. Imagine a forest of pure geometry, a metropolis extruded by the planet’s core and weathered by eons of silence.

The scale is bewildering. A broad, empty avenue—too wide to see across clearly in the perpetual twilight—sweeps away into the distance, connecting this peak with another, miles away. It is an unsupported bridge, a ribbon of darkened crystal hanging over a chasm of unfathomable depth. Far below, the world is lost in a permanent, roiling cloud deck that has never been pierced. The only light comes from this cloud layer itself, a diffuse, sourceless luminescence, the colour of a dying ember seen through milk glass. There is no sun, no moon, no stars.

The structures around you are vast and hollow. Towers that resemble fractal arrangements of obsidian thorns climb into the dimness, their highest points dissolving into the gloom. Walls are not solid; they are membranes of frozen paradox, surfaces you can see *through* but not *into*. Their angles are wrong, unsettling the eye and giving a faint sense of vertigo even when standing still. The entire city feels ancient, each surface worn smooth, not by wind or water—there is none—but by the slow, inexorable friction of time itself. It is a finished form, a settled state, yet it is not static.

This is the central rule of the Shroud-City: it is alive. The city breathes, but it does not inhale air. In the profound, near-total silence, you can feel more than hear it: a slow, deep thrumming that resonates directly in the bones. It is the sound of growth. Look closely at the edge of a balcony, and you may perceive, over the course of an hour, a new facet budding from the surface, a tiny crystalline tetrahedron pushing itself into existence with silent, unyielding force. This is a place of non-biological, non-technological construction. The city is an act of geology, a mineral ecosystem whose primary organism is itself.

The emptiness is a presence. The vast plazas are devoid of statues, the kilometre-long halls are without furniture, the silent districts are without inhabitants. The density of objects is infinitesimally low. You could walk for a day and see nothing but the repeating, emergent patterns of the architecture and the yawning, empty space between them.

But the world is not entirely unpopulated. Fauna is rare, but it exists, born of the same impossible physics as the city. You might see a Null-glider, a creature that looks like a sheet of rippling, heat-hazy air, coasting silently on the imperceptible currents between the towers. It has no discernible anatomy, no face, no limbs. It is a two-dimensional entity moving through three-dimensional space, feeding on stray motes of light or perhaps on the very concept of distance. On a ledge, you might find a cluster of Echo-motes, crystalline polyhedra that slowly rotate, emitting faint, harmonic chimes that are echoes of sounds that will happen a thousand years from now. They are less animals and more living instruments.

The greatest activity here is the weather, but it is not meteorological. It is ontological. The reality of the Shroud-City is not stable. There are tides of existence, currents of possibility that flow through the empty avenues. Without warning, a distant spire might waver, becoming translucent, its form unspooling into a lattice of shimmering lines before slowly re-coalescing. This is a "reality fog" rolling in. At other times, a section of the city might experience a "phase slip," its geometry folding in on itself, a courtyard becoming, for a few minutes, a four-dimensional space where looking left brings you back to where you started, before it snaps back to normality with a silent shudder that you feel in your teeth.

To be here is to exist in a state of quiet awe and constant, low-grade disorientation. The silence is broken only by the subsonic thrum of the city's growth and the rare, ghostly chime of an Echo-mote. The air is still and cold. The view is an endless vista of dark, crystalline structures set against a sky of dim, eternal light. This is not a city that was abandoned. It is a city that is waiting; a metropolis built by no hands, for no one, whose only purpose is to continue its silent, impossible becoming at the cold, dry, empty roof of the world.
