The first thing is the sound, and the second is the pressure. They are inseparable. The sound is not a noise that travels through air to reach an ear; it is a concussion that travels through the water to resonate in your entire body. It is a deep, rhythmic, industrial beat, a colossal heart of iron and steam. *Thump-shudder... clank. Thump-shudder... clank.* A slow, multi-ton rhythm that has been beating for millennia. It is so constant, so foundational, that it has ceased to be sound and has become a law of physics for this place, like gravity. The pressure is the medium for this sound, the cool, heavy liquid that fills everything, pressing in on all sides with the weight of a deep lake.

You are in a chamber, submerged. Its scale is that of a great hall or a small cathedral, and there is no bedrock, no natural stone. The floor is a pavement of vast, basalt-dark ceramic tiles, each the size of a wagon, now smooth as river stones. Their grout lines are deep, eroded canyons where the water has worked for ages. The walls are the same material, curving up and over to form a vaulted ceiling high in the dimness, a perfect, constructed vault now scarred with millennia of slow decay. Everything is rounded, edges softened, corners worn away into gentle curves. This place was made, but the water has been unmaking it since time immemorial.

The source of the light and the thunder is the Engine. It occupies the center of the chamber, a monument of pitted, dark alloy and what might have once been brass. It is a machine of incomprehensible purpose and staggering endurance. A single, house-sized piston, slick with a faint, rainbow sheen of ancient lubricant, drives a flywheel twenty meters across. The *thump-shudder* is the piston’s stroke; the *clank* is the sound of colossal tumblers or gears locking into place deep within its housing. The Engine is not hot to the touch, only slightly less cold than the surrounding water, but it leaks a steady, invisible energy into the environment. Perhaps it is a colossal pump, circulating the water of this closed world, or a filter, or a power source for a city that has been dark for a million years. Its function is lost. Its functioning is not. The only significant light comes from a series of thick, quartz-like lenses set into the Engine's casing, which glow with a dim, steady, amber light, as if illuminating gauges no one will ever read again.

Life here does not grow; it accretes. There are no plants. The closest thing is a sparse, velvety patina of chemotrophic bacteria that coats the Engine's warmer plates and clings in patches to the walls. It feeds on the microscopic particles of metal and ancient chemicals the Engine bleeds into the water. This faint dusting is the bedrock of the entire food chain.

The water is not empty. It is filled with shoals of tiny, iridescent creatures, part-crustacean, part-mollusc, that glitter like clouds of scattered copper dust. They navigate by sensing the weak electrical field of the Engine, and they graze endlessly on the bacterial film.

Hunting these clouds of dust are the true inhabitants of the chamber: fish. They are abundant. Pale, blind, and sleek, they move with a nerveless grace. Long, whisker-like barbels trail from their jaws, tasting the water, sensing the pressure waves of their prey and the unceasing pulse of the Engine. Schools of them, a hundred strong, swarm the walls in tight formation, their bodies identical, their movements synchronized. They are called Glimmer-Jacks by no one, because no one is here to name them.

Larger things hunt the Glimmer-Jacks. Solitary predators, long and thin as spear-shafts, hang motionless in the water, their sides the same color as the pitted ceramic walls. They are Current-Knives, all muscle and patience. They do not see, they do not hear in the conventional sense. They *feel* the rhythm of the chamber. They feel the constant *thump-shudder... clank* as the baseline of reality. It is the disruption to that rhythm—the frantic, scattering pulse of a panicked school of smaller fish—that triggers their explosive, arrow-like strike.

The whole chamber is a closed system in perpetual, slow motion. The Engine tirelessly erodes itself, feeding the bacteria. The bacteria feed the grazers. The grazers feed the hunters. The hunters die, and their bodies are slowly broken down by the patient water, their minerals returned to the system to be circulated once more by the apathetic churning of the great machine. The dim amber light from the Engine's heart catches the silver-white flash of a hunting Current-Knife, illuminates a spiraling galaxy of copper dust, and reflects off the endless, weeping, ceramic walls. And through it all, the beat goes on. *Thump-shudder... clank.* A pulse in a world without a sun.
