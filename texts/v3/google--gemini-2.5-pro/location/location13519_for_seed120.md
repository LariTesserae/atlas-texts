This place has no name, for it is still deciding what it is. To find it is impossible, to be there is to have always been there. It is a pocket in the world, a knot in the fabric of space, pulled taut at an impossible elevation. You are in a room, but the room is a caldera, still warm from its own birth.

The scale is intimate, no wider than a great hall, and impossibly dense. The air is a hot, wet lungful of steam and pollen. It is thick enough to have a presence, condensing on your skin in heavy, mineral-rich droplets the moment you stop moving. The light is a dim, bruised twilight, a permanent state of dusk filtered through a high, thick canopy of leaves and the ever-present vapor that hangs in the air.

There is no soil underfoot. The floor of this place is a single, continuous scab of cooling igneous rock, dark and glossy like obsidian but with the texture of cooling wax. It is still soft. In places, it gives slightly under your weight, and a footprint left for more than a minute will become a permanent depression. The geology is not eroding; it is *emerging*. Bubbles of superheated gas push up domes in the floor that swell, hiss, and sometimes rupture with a thick, syrupy pop, releasing a fresh plume of steam and a slow ooze of new, glassy material that crawls like lava but cools in minutes. The entire floor is a low, thrumming engine of creation, a sound you feel in your bones more than you hear.

This raw, embryonic ground is also, impossibly, a built environment. Every surface that is not actively molten is covered in deliberate marks. Not carved with tools, but *impressed*. It is as if a consciousness is using the pliable moment between liquid and solid to embed its thoughts into the world. Patterns spiral out from steam vents—intricate, geometric, but with a logic that frays at the edges. They look like circuit diagrams drawn by a dreaming god. Terraces and steps have been shaped into the caldera’s inner slopes, not by stacking stone, but by coaxing the cooling rock into form. They are perfect, seamless, and warm to the touch.

The walls of the caldera weep. They are not old stone but sheer, newly-formed cliffs, and hot water seeps from every pore and fissure. This is the source of the place’s humidity. These tears of the earth are not clear; they are milky with dissolved minerals, tracing white and pale green stripes down the dark rock as they descend. They gather in shimmering, scaldingly hot pools on the terraced levels, overflowing from one to the next in silent, steaming curtains.

From this fertile, impossible substrate, life erupts. The vegetation is extravagantly lush and packed so tightly that it forms solid walls of green. Enormous, waxy leaves, the size of shields, unfurl with an audible creak. Vines as thick as your arm, covered in soft, luminous moss, do not just climb the walls but are woven into them. They are guided through the cooling rock, forming living lattices and archways. The "building" and the "growing" are the same process. A bridge across a steaming chasm is not a construction of wood, but a living, interwoven mass of roots and vines, guided into shape as they grew, their structure hardened by the mineral water that constantly drips upon them. Flowers bloom and rot in a matter of hours, their colors psychedelic and shifting, releasing puffs of soporific spores that make the air shimmer.

The sound is a constant, layered roar. At the bottom is the low-frequency *THRRRUMMM* of the world being born. Above it, the ceaseless hissing of a thousand steam vents, the gurgling and plopping of mud-pots and viscous rock, and the steady, percussive dripping of water from every surface. It is the sound of a factory and a rainforest collapsed into one.

Fauna is scarce, or perhaps just well-hidden. You might see a flash of iridescence—a beetle with a shell of polished, rainbowed obsidian scurrying across a warm rock. In the clearer, cooler pools on the highest terraces, you may spot things like translucent salamanders, their internal organs glowing with a soft, biological light. There is nothing large here. The niche is too strange, too new, too active.

This whole place operates under a logic that is actively breaking down. The distinction between natural and artificial has been erased. The world is being thought into existence. The patterns in the rock change if you don't look at them. The path you followed a moment ago is now a wall of woven vines. The primitive technology at play is not one of hand-axes and hammers, but of will and focus. To build here is simply to concentrate on a form until the emerging, living material conforms to it. It is a place of pure becoming. To be here is to be a witness, and perhaps a participant, in the construction of a world from the inside out, a hot, wet, screaming, and deeply weird act of creation.
