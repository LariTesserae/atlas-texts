This world is a womb of light and noise, a cavern so vast its ceiling is a remote concept, a shimmering, nebulous haze lost in the glare. There is no sun, yet the light is absolute, a brilliant, clean, day-bright luminescence that pours from the very structure of the place. It is a world beneath the surface, but it is not dark.

The source of the light is the geology itself, which is in a state of frenzied, perpetual birth. From the cavern floor and, presumably, down from the invisible ceiling, rise and descend immense, fluted pillars of a crystalline substance akin to selenite or mica. They are not static. You can see them growing. The light is a byproduct of their formation, a cold fire locked in their molecular assembly. Where the growth is fastest, the light is blindingly white; where it is slow, it is a soft, steady glow like a gas mantle. The pillars cluster in dense forests, their edges razor-sharp, forming canyons and thickets of solid light. The air shimmers with the heat rising from the floor and the constant shedding of microscopic, incandescent motes. The atmosphere is warm, thick, and wet, tasting of hot stone and ozone. Water condenses on every surface, running in trickles down the crystal flutes to gather in shallow, steaming pools on the uneven floor. These pools are supersaturated with minerals, their edges caked with delicate, glittering salt-frost.

There is no soil, no plant, not a single leaf or stem. The entire ecosystem is founded on this rampant lithogenesis. The floor, in the open spaces between the light-pillars, is a chaotic jumble of half-formed crystal plates, sharp-edged flakes, and mounds of glowing dust. And it is moving.

This is a world defined by its fauna, which is overwhelming, a constant, churning biomass that fills every niche. The base of the food chain is a creature that consumes the new-formed crystal. They are the Scrabblers: fist-sized, six-legged arthropods with carapaces of pearlescent, heat-resistant shell. Their mandibles are hardened silica, and they swarm in glittering tides over the brightest, newest growth on the pillars, gnawing away at the glowing surface. The sound of their scraping is a primary component of the cavern’s deafening thrum—a high-pitched, pervasive staticky sizzle, like a billion tiny drills.

Hunting the Scrabblers are the Glass-Snakes, translucent, metre-long predators that move with liquid speed. Their bodies are little more than digestive tracts sheathed in flexible, silicon-based muscle. When they have recently fed, their entire form glows from within, illuminated by the half-digested light of their prey. They hunt in packs, flowing over the crystal formations like living mercury, their passage marked by the frantic scattering of the Scrabbler swarms.

The air itself is territory. Huge, manta-like creatures called Skimmers, with wingspans of ten or fifteen metres, ride the thermal updrafts that boil up from the hotter sections of the floor. Their undersides are covered in fine, hair-like cilia that filter the air for the luminous dust and smaller airborne organisms. Their calls are the other dominant sound: deep, resonant, bell-like tones that reverberate through the vast open spaces, somehow both mournful and territorial. They are a constant presence overhead, their massive shadows momentarily dimming the glare from the floor below. The density is claustrophobic; you are never more than an arm's length from some chittering, slithering, or pulsating thing. Sessile organisms, like fleshy, lung-like sacs called Breath-Bags, cling to the less-luminous rock faces, rhythmically inflating and deflating as they filter the air, their collective sighing a wet, organic bass note under the sizzle and the ringing calls.

The entire system is turbulent. A section of floor might suddenly buckle and thrust upwards, birthing a new pillar in a surge of blinding light and a sound like cracking ice on a continental scale. This geological instability sends shockwaves through the ecosystem. Scrabbler swarms will migrate in an instant, a glittering river changing its course to feast on the new energy source. Glass-Snakes will follow, and the Skimmers will alter their flight paths to patrol the new updrafts. It is a world of constant, violent, creative chaos.

And amidst it all, there are faint, scattered signs of intention. A narrow bridge, woven from the flexible, metallic fibers of some unknown creature's cocoon, stretches across a deep, narrow chasm between two pillar-clusters. Its craftsmanship is simple, a primitive but effective solution to the treacherous terrain. In a small, sheltered alcove, the ground has been cleared of sharp debris, the crystal dust swept into a neat circle. Leaning against the wall is a single tool: a long, sharp shard of the dark, non-luminous obsidian-like rock that sometimes forms here, its handle wrapped in cured Skimmer-hide.

These are not the marks of masters of this world. They are the traces of survivors, of beings carving out a precarious existence in a place that is too loud, too bright, and too ferociously, unstoppably alive. They are the quietest things here, whispers of order in a cathedral of creative bedlam. To be here is to feel like a loose particle in a supercollider, a witness to a creation event that never ends, deafened by the roar of a world being born and eaten in the same instant.
