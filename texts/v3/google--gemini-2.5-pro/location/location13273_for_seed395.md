The world is a single, continuous city, built by no one.

It spreads over a low, barely undulating basin of planetary scale. There is no soil, no sand, no water-carved rock. The ground itself is a raw, emergent geology, a pro-lithic crust still warm from its own formation. It is a vast plain of dark, glassy material like obsidian, shot through with veins of metallic sulfide that gleam like frozen lightning. Here and there, the surface puckers into smooth, low domes or buckles into pressure ridges miles long—the slow, tectonic exhalations of a world under construction. The air is warm, thick, and carries the faint, clean scent of hot stone and ozone.

From this black-glass foundation, the vegetation grows. It is not life as we know it, for it does not drink. It is lithotrophic, a form of life that metabolizes the mineral substrate directly. It is a forest, a jungle, an entire biosphere of crystalline growth. Vast, silent thickets of silica-fronds rise hundreds of feet, their structures like translucent, branching fulgurites. Their surfaces are smooth and cool, catching the normal, yellow-white light of the sun and breaking it into slow-drifting rainbows that dapple the ground below. Between them, the space is packed with dense, geometric bushes of metallic salt-crystals, some forming perfect cubes of pyrite-gold, others sharp dodecahedrons of garnet-red. There is no empty space. Every available surface is colonized by this slow, silent explosion of geological life.

This is the "city." The growth is not random. It is organized. The crystalline "trees" align themselves into vast, colonnaded halls that stretch to the horizon. The crystalline "bushes" form dense, labyrinthine walls that divide the landscape into districts and corridors. Great, arching buttresses of milky quartz leap between the tallest spires, forming a continuous, interlocked canopy overhead—a living roof that spans a continent. It is urban in its structure, in its density, in its sheer, overwhelming presence, but it is entirely natural. It is a single, colonial organism or a symbiotic collection of them, growing into architectural forms. The "streets" are not paved; they are simply the paths of least resistance, channels where the growth is less dense, floored in the gleaming black substrate.

There is abundant life here, but it, too, is waterless. Creatures of articulated geology move through the silent crystal canyons. Herds of what might be called grazers, beings the size of cattle but shaped like trilobites, crawl slowly along the bases of the quartz spires. Their undersides are fitted with rows of diamond-hard teeth that scrape and grind the nutrient-rich crystal, the sound a faint, rhythmic rasping that is quickly swallowed by the immense quiet. Their carapaces are mosaics of shale and schist, perfectly camouflaged against the darker growths.

Hunting them are sleeker, faster predators. They are built of obsidian and mica, their forms blade-thin and jointed like insects. They move in silent, fluid bursts, their feet making no sound on the glassy floor. They do not kill with tooth and claw in the traditional sense; they are agents of structural failure. They identify stress points in a grazer's carapace and strike with focused, percussive force, shattering a leg joint or cracking the shell open to get at the softer, energy-rich crystalline matrices within.

The world is profoundly silent. The atmosphere is too dense and the structures too solid to carry sound well. There are no bird calls, no insectile buzzes, no rustling leaves. The only noises are the quiet, infrequent sounds of process. The deep, sub-audible hum of the planet's crustal annealing. The high, momentary *tink* as a crystal, cooling too quickly, fractures. The soft, grinding scrape of a grazer feeding. The almost imperceptible creak of the city itself as it grows, a sound on the edge of hearing, like a glacier moving.

This place is intensely dynamic. The "city" is in a constant state of becoming. On one horizon, the raw crust is still cooling, outgassing plumes of hot, metallic vapor. Here, the first pioneer crystals are taking root, small, sharp needles of bismuth and antimony. In the heart of the continental structure, parts of the city have reached the end of their lifecycle. The great quartz spires slowly lose their internal cohesion, turning cloudy and opaque before shattering in a slow, silent cascade of crystal dust that settles and is reabsorbed by the substrate. This decay provides fresh, processed material for new growth. Predator and prey enact their slow-motion ballet. Light shifts, catching new facets, throwing new patterns of colored light across the silent plazas. It is a world, an ecosystem, an entire civilization running on a clock of geological time, a cycle of mineral turning into life, and life returning to mineral, all without a single drop of water.
