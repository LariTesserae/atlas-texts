This place is a secret kept by the deep rock.

It is not an ocean, not a sea, but a Vesica: a perfect, almond-shaped cavity of fluid no larger than a cathedral, suspended in the absolute black of the planet's deep crust. The "water" here is not water. It is a denser, colder, life-sustaining medium, a vitreous humour that pillows every movement and muffles all sound to a near-total silence. To be within it is to be held, suspended in a solution both amniotic and glacial. The pressure is immense, but the fluid's viscosity makes it feel less like a crushing weight and more like a heavy blanket. Gravity is a gentle suggestion, a compass pointing toward the Vesica's thicker-shelled bottom.

The terrain is the city, and the city is the terrain. It is a single, continuous structure, a settlement of biological architecture rising from the floor of the Vesica. But this is not an ancient city of stone. It is a nascent metropolis of bone-ivory and living nacre, actively and silently under construction. There are no straight lines. Towers, resembling the hollowed femurs of impossible titans, curve and spiral upwards, their surfaces still slick with the mucus of their recent extrusion. Buttresses of pearlescent shell, shimmering with a faint, internal light, connect spires with delicate, web-like lattices. The "streets" are not-quite-solid causeways, broad channels of semi-congealed material where new layers are slowly being deposited. The density is startling; structures lean into one another, merge, and bifurcate, creating a three-dimensional labyrinth that is almost entirely without empty space. It feels layered, over-saturated, a coral reef dreaming of becoming a metropolis. Because it is still emerging, the geology is raw. Surfaces are soft in places, hard in others. You might touch a wall and find it firm as quartz, but the wall next to it yields like cartilage, a new archway just beginning to harden.

There is no sun here, no sky. The atmosphere is the cold, thick fluid. The only light is a dim, sourceless phosphorescence, a *lume* that emanates from the city itself and its inhabitants. The bone-white structures seem to have absorbed millennia of faint biological light, and now they give it back, a passive, milky glow that paints everything in shades of ghostly opal and pale, bruised violet. Where new growth is occurring, the light is brighter, a soft throb of liquid pearl. The cold is a constant presence, a deep, cellular chill that slows everything. It is a cold that preserves.

The silence is the defining rule of this world. It is a profound, holy quiet. There are no voices, no clatter of tools, no wind. The only sounds are subliminal: the wet, viscous *slide* of a creature moving along a wall, the infinitesimal *crackle* of a new filament of bone hardening into place, the deep, subsonic pulse of the entire biome breathing as one. You do not hear these things with your ears, but feel them in your bones as subtle shifts in the ambient pressure.

The builders and residents are one and the same. They are the abundant fauna, a silent populace of molluscan architects. These creatures are soft-bodied, translucent beings, shaped like elongated teardrops. They drift through the fluid with slow, elegant undulations of a membranous fin that encircles their bodies. Within their glassy flesh, organs pulse with a slow, rhythmic bioluminescence—the power source of this world. They have no faces, no eyes in a way we would recognize, but their entire forward surface is a complex sensory membrane. From a set of spinnerets near their base, they exude a liquid protein that, upon contact with the cold medium, slowly crystallizes into the nacreous, bone-like substance of the city. They are slow, deliberate, working with the unthinking coordination of a hive. They glide through the dimness, brushing against a surface, depositing a filament of new matter, sculpting a finial with a gentle press of their own bodies, then drifting on. They are building their own tomb, their own cradle, their own world.

Life teems in the city's dense latticework. Glass-fin swimmers, no larger than a thumb, dart in shoals through the hollows of the bone-towers, their own fleeting lights like scattered sparks. Armoured, shrimp-like skitterers crawl along the causeways, cleaning the surfaces of any unwanted microbial film, their hundreds of tiny legs making no sound in the thick liquid. These smaller creatures are the prey, the food source for the architects, who absorb nutrients directly through their skin from the fluid, a thick broth enriched by the life and death cycles of this closed system.

Vegetation is sparse, an afterthought. Thin veils of pallid, spectral algae hang from the undersides of arches, swaying almost imperceptibly in the non-currents. Patches of luminous fungi, glowing with a cold blue light, cling to the junctions where spires meet, their mycelial networks threading through the very bone of the city, partners in its construction. They are not a foundation of the ecosystem, but an ornamentation upon it.

This place is intensely dynamic. It is a single, massive process: the process of its own creation. The city grows from a central node near the Vesica's floor, a "heart-pearl" of unimaginable density and brightness, spreading ever outward and upward. The architects work in silent, harmonious waves, their movements dictated by some inaudible biological rhythm. One cycle may see a new tower extruded; the next may see the thickening of a major artery-road. Predator-prey dynamics are slow, patient dramas of ambush and absorption. The entire world is an organism, a city-embryo gestating in the dark womb of the planet, growing, living, and shimmering in its profound, eternal silence.
