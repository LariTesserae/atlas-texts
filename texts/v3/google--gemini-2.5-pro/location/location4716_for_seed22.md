This is a place of sound.

Before sight, before touch, there is the sound. It is not merely loud; it is a geological force, a constant, world-defining pressure that invades the bones and makes the teeth ache. It is the absolute baseline of existence here. The sound is a deafening, multi-layered drone, a superposition of a million frequencies at once. There is a deep, resonant hum that feels like the spinning of a planet-sized engine, a bass note that vibrates up from the soles of the feet. Layered over this is a finer, sharper lattice of crystalline chimes and whistles, a constant, decorative filigree of noise. And woven through it all is a high-frequency wash, like the hiss of a trillion dying sparks or the static of a radio tuned between worlds. Silence is not a concept here; it would be a vacuum, a wound in the fabric of the real.

Your eyes adjust to the perpetual twilight. The light is dim, as if filtered through layers of smoked glass, but what it illuminates is a scene of impossible contradiction. You are standing in a vast, open basin, the scale of a broad river valley stretching towards a hazy, indistinct horizon. Above, there is no sky, only a ceiling of impenetrable, bruised-purple cloud, or perhaps the underside of some colossal architecture. From this gloom, immense structures descend.

This is the city. But it is a city of ghosts, an urban armature built from light and resonance. Towers that would dwarf any on Earth are mere suggestions, shimmering scaffolds of pale green and blue energy that flicker at the edge of hearing. Their forms are sharp-edged, crystalline, and impossibly complex, like the blueprints of a mad god rendered in captive lightning. They are patently *new*; there is no wear, no dust, no decay. They look as if they were switched on a moment ago. Yet despite this metropolis of light, the place is profoundly, fundamentally *empty*. These towers have no walls, no floors, no occupants. They are volumetric projections, their complex geometries defined by standing waves in the omnipresent sound field. You could walk through one, if you could bear the shrieking intensification of the localized frequencies that define its "matter." The city is an immense and intricate piece of infrastructure for a civilization that has no mass.

Weaving through and around this sonic architecture is the vegetation. It is riotously lush, a jungle of pure information. Great, weeping willow-like trees are composed of cascading streams of golden-red light, their "leaves" just momentary, brighter sparks that drip into the air and fade. Underfoot, a carpet of phosphorescent moss-analogue pulses in rhythm with a sub-harmonic of the main drone. Ferns made of crackling violet electricity unfurl in fractal perfection. This flora is the source of the dampness. The intense energy fields of the plants cause the thin, warm air to shed its moisture. A constant, fine condensation beads on every shimmering leaf and filament, coalescing into heavy, slow-motion drops of silver liquid that fall with a soundless splash, evaporating almost as soon as they touch the ground, their moisture instantly reclaimed by the humid atmosphere. It never truly rains, but everything is perpetually slick with this technological dew.

The ground itself is one of the few solid things here. It is a floor of sharp, black volcanic glass or obsidian, fractured into vast, plate-like shards with razor edges. These newborn geological plates seem to float on a deeper, unseen medium, and they ring like bells, transmitting the world's deep hum directly into your body.

And through this empty, holographic jungle-city, the fauna moves. They are abundant but weightless, untethered from physics as we know it. Shimmering, eel-like concatenations of data, hundreds of meters long, swim lazily through the "air" between the towers of light, their bodies rippling with rainbow shifts of color. They are distractions, errors in the grand broadcast. Flocks of smaller creatures, like birds made of pure acoustic feedback, dart and swerve in tight formation, emitting piercing chirps that are audible even over the din. They are not chasing insects; they are chasing rogue frequencies, consuming glitches in the signal to sustain their own fleeting, programmed existence. On the ground, things like six-legged panthers made of shadow and static stalk through the glowing undergrowth, their footfalls making no sound, yet their passage causing the light-plants to recoil and dim.

To exist here is to be a ghost in a machine of unimaginable scale and purpose. The world is a dynamic, active system. The Carrier Wave—the fundamental sound—modulates in slow, ponderous cycles. Over the course of a day, a tower might dissolve into a spray of sonic mist while a new one is drawn into being nearby. A whole sector of the jungle might shift its resonant frequency, its color palette changing from cool blues to hot oranges. The predator-prey cycle is not about flesh, but information. The static-panthers hunt and "consume" the informational signature of smaller data-fauna, causing them to decohere into a burst of meaningless noise.

This place is not natural. It is the product of a technology so advanced it is indistinguishable from a spiritual plane. Perhaps it is a computer's memory made manifest, a storage system where data has taken on ecological form. Perhaps it is a terraforming project that has stabilized in this halfway state between matter and energy. The rules are simple and absolute: do not trust your senses of substance and void. That empty space beside you might be the "solid" boundary of a sonic edifice, and stepping into it could shred your constituent atoms. That shimmering, beautiful flower of light might be a focused node of such intense sound it could boil your blood. The only safety is on the black glass floor, in the gaps between the projections, a transient being walking through a deafening, vibrant, and utterly hollow cathedral.
