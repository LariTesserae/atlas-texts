There is no surface. To speak of depth here implies a top that can be left behind, a boundary between one medium and another. No such boundary exists. The world is a single, unbroken volume of a substance that is not quite water. It is heavier, more viscous, and absolutely, fundamentally cool. The coolness is not of ice, but of a perfect, entropic equilibrium, a temperature that has not varied by a thousandth of a degree in a million years. It is a coolness that pulls heat from any foreign body with a slow, inexorable thirst.

You are high on a mountaintop, but the mountain is drowned. Its jagged, freshly-birthed peaks, which should have been scoured by wind and ice, are instead suspended in this quiescent fluid. Here, on a plateau just below the primary summit, rests a city. Or a district. A single, contained ward.

The structures are not built *of* anything you can name. They are blade-thin and obsidian-dark, absorbing all light and pressure. They are impossibly sharp. Where geology and time would have conspired to round every edge, here the corners of plazas and the tips of spires could slit the fabric of reality. This is an architecture of subtraction, as if the buildings were not added to the rock, but are the result of everything *else* being carved away with perfect, atomic precision. They rise in sparse, lonely arrangements—a spire here, a low-slung data-crypt there, a cantilevered platform hanging over a chasm that drops into the mountain's core. The spaces between them are immense, deliberate voids of the thick, dark liquid.

This is an urban place. There are streets—not for walking, but for navigating. They are delineated by the faintest lines of internal luminescence, cold white-blue light that bleeds from hairline fractures in the otherwise seamless black ground. The light wells up from whatever impossible power source fuels this stasis, tracing paths, outlining plazas, and creating grids of light that don't illuminate so much as define the darkness. The light has no warmth, offers no comfort. It is geometry made visible.

There is no life here. No algae films the perfect, non-eroding surfaces. No schools of fish glide through the plazas. It is a mineral world, constructed and then sealed. But it is not entirely empty. Once, perhaps in a week of patient observation, you might see a presence. It is not an animal. It is a slow, geometric custodian, a creature of faceted chrome and shadow, perhaps a kilometer long, that glides through the silent avenues with a purpose one can only guess at. It seems to be polishing the already perfect surfaces, its passage the only motion this realm ever experiences. It has no eyes, no mouth, no apparent biology. It is a piece of the city's infrastructure that has been given the semblance of life, an impossibly advanced janitor for a pristine tomb.

The only sound is a hum. It is so low and pervasive that it is felt in the bones more than heard. It is not the thrum of machinery, but the sound of an equation held in perfect balance. It is the sound of absolute stability, the standing wave of a technology that has arrested time, decay, and chaos. This sound is the city's one, unchanging rule: nothing changes. Nothing degrades. Nothing is born, and nothing dies.

The physics here are a form of magic. The pressure at this depth should crush these delicate-looking structures, but they stand. The absolute stillness of the medium is a violation of thermodynamics, a pocket of the universe where entropy has been told to wait outside. The city was not built to be lived in by beings of flesh and blood. It may be a library, its structures the solid-state memory of a civilization that transcended physical form. It may be a prison for a single, conceptual entity. It may be a monument to a forgotten victory, or a perfectly preserved crime scene.

To be here is to be an anomaly, a warm thing in a cool, dead place, a dynamic process in a field of absolute stasis. The sparseness of the towers makes you feel exposed, not to predators, but to the sheer, overwhelming emptiness. The city does not threaten. It does not welcome. It simply *is*, a dark, sharp, and eternal thought suspended in the cold heart of a drowned mountain, waiting for nothing at all.
