There is no silence here. There is only the Sound.

It is a chord, immense and unwavering, played on engines the size of mountains. It is a bass note of titanic furnaces, a resonant harmony of a trillion whirring gears, and a high, sharp overtone of pressurized steam shrieking from pipes that lattice the horizon. The Sound is the first and last fact of this place. It vibrates in your bones, a constant, physical presence that has long since deafened any ear that might have evolved to hear it. It is so total that it has become the background radiation of existence, the very texture of the air.

This is the City, and it is built across the rooftop of the world. The geology is a scream held in stone. Peaks of black, glassy rock, impossibly sharp, rise to altitudes where the sky bruises to indigo. These are not worn, ancient mountains; they are new, as if they were violently extruded from the planet's mantle yesterday and then frozen in place. The word for their formation has been forgotten, but the result is a landscape of knife-edge ridges, sheer cliffs that drop for miles, and chasms so deep and narrow that they have never seen the sun. The scale is hysterical. A single mountain face could contain a continent. A valley could swallow a sea.

And across this violent, frozen topography, the City is draped. It is less a concentration of buildings and more a distributed network of industrial organs. Here, a refinery complex clings to a vertical cliff face, its metal hide sweating a perpetual sheen of cold condensation. Kilometers away, visible only as a flicker of light across a gulf of empty air, a power station hums, its pylons drilled directly into a needle-like spire of obsidian. They are connected by bridges, structures of heroic and pointless grace. These are not simple spans; they are enclosed tubes of riveted iron and thick, weeping glass, sometimes arcing for a hundred miles through the thin, frigid air, their internal lamps a lonely string of pearls against the vastness.

Space here is the dominant material. The City is urban in its interconnectedness, but hollow in its soul. Between the great industrial nodes, there is nothing but altitude and mist. The air is cold enough to make your lungs ache, and it is saturated with water. A constant, condensing fog clings to the lower regions, filling the chasms with a roiling, opaque sea. Higher up, the moisture manifests as a constant weeping. Water seeps from the porous black rock, tracing silver paths down sheer faces. It drips from the rusting bellies of the great machines, forming slushy pools of pristine, mineral-heavy water that never freeze solid and never drain away. Every surface is slick, filmed with a layer of cold moisture.

There is life, but it is not animal. The absolute lack of fauna is a silence deeper than the Sound. No insects drone, no birds cry, no predators stalk the metallic gantries. The only life is a lush, defiant carpet of vegetation. Mosses, thick as fleece and disturbingly vibrant in shades of toxic green and electric blue, grow in the perpetual damp of the rock faces. Lichens with a metallic, iridescent sheen colonize the very plating of the factories, their slow, patient growth a testament to an age that never seems to pass. In the crevices where soil has been manufactured or has gathered over unthinkable spans of time, strange, hardy ferns unfurl, their fronds waxy and tough, glistening with a permanent dew. They grow on the precipices, on the bridges, on the dormant smokestacks, a green fur softening the hard lines of industry.

This is the central weirdness, the rule that governs all else: the place is static. It is a frozen equilibrium. The engines run, the furnaces burn, the pistons drive, but they *produce nothing*. The City is not manufacturing goods or processing resources for some unseen consumer. Its entire, monumental industry is turned inward, its one and only purpose the maintenance of its own state. The staggering energy output is used to counteract all change. Microscopic repairs are made to the rock faces to prevent erosion. The water that weeps from the stone is collected, purified, and pumped back into the rock in a perfect, closed loop. The temperature is held at a precise, chilling degree. The Sound is not the noise of production; it is the noise of stasis. It is the roar of a world locked in a battle against time, and winning.

The light is normal, coming from a distant, white sun, but it feels alien here. It casts sharp, black shadows that stretch for miles. It glints off the wet surfaces of the endless machinery, making the City shimmer like a wet reptile. At dawn and dusk, the light refracts through the atmospheric moisture, and for a moment, the sky can fill with impossible colors, the grand, empty spaces between the thrumming towers painted with hues for which there are no names.

To be here is to be an anomaly, a fleeting error in a system of perfect permanence. You would stand on a gantry of cold, gridded iron, looking out over a chasm of churning mist. Above you, the dark architecture of a converter complex blots out the sky, its deep thrumming vibrating through the soles of your feet. The air smells of cold ozone, wet rock, and hot metal. There is nothing to wait for. The next shift will not begin, because it never ended. The bridge you crossed will not decay. The blue-green moss at your feet will not grow or wither. The titanic, continental chord of the Sound will not change its key. It will play on, forever. This is the City’s purpose and its curse: to exist, deafeningly, emptily, in a single, unchanging moment. This is the Last Chorus.
