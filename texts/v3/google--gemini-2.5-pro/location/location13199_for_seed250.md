There is no surface. Not anymore, or perhaps not yet. You are suspended in a medium that is not quite water, though it has its weight, its pressure, its cool embrace against the skin of whatever form you inhabit. It is a liquid continuum, solvent and vast, stretching for distances that defy geography and beggar the mind. This is an ocean at high altitude, a world-sea held aloft by principles that have no name in common physics, its floor a rumor kilometers below, its sky a concept lost to the crushing, luminous depths above.

The light is here, but its source is everywhere and nowhere. It is a diffuse, silver-blue glow, as if daylight has been dissolved into the very liquid of the world. Far-off things are shrouded in a cerulean haze, their forms softened and made dreamlike by the sheer volume of illuminated fluid between you and them. The scale is planetary; a feature that seems a hand's breadth away might be a full day's journey, its true size that of a mountain range.

And it is loud. Not the sound of life, for there is none. No clicks of crustaceans, no songs of whales, no chittering of unseen things. This is a deeper, more fundamental noise: the sound of creation. It is a constant, bone-deep hum that modulates in pitch and intensity, a resonant frequency that seems to hold the entire medium in a state of barely-contained tension. This is the background noise of the universe, amplified and made immediate.

Then, the song shifts. In the far, blue distance, the steady hum climbs into a thunderous, grinding chord. A region of the water, kilometers wide, begins to churn. It is not a current, but a roiling, self-contained storm of physics. The liquid there goes opaque, curdling like milk, shot through with veins of blinding white light. The sound is immense now, a bass note that vibrates through your core, the scream of matter being torn from energy, of atoms being introduced to each other for the first time.

You are witnessing a birth. This is an act of geogenesis, raw and untamed. From the heart of that turbulent cloud, a structure is precipitating into existence. It is not eroding; it is emerging. A vast, skeletal spire of something that is not quite rock and not quite metal begins to knit itself together. Facets of what looks like smoked quartz the size of city blocks crystallize around a humming, metallic lattice. It grows upward and outward, an embryonic mountain range built in minutes, not millennia. The process is violent. Chunks of the nascent structure shear off, dissolving back into the liquid with a hiss, while other sections solidify, still glowing with the internal heat of their formation.

The world is, for the most part, empty. Between these titanic, ephemeral acts of creation lie voids of staggering size. You can drift for weeks and see nothing but the endless, silver-blue medium and hear nothing but the patient, fundamental hum. The density is near zero; there are no floating motes of dust, no debris. There is only the liquid, and the things that are being born from it.

But it is not entirely barren. Where a newly formed spire has cooled, its residual energy leaking out into the water like a thermal vent, something grows. It is not kelp or coral. They are long, hyperfine filaments of what appears to be spun glass or silicon lace, anchored to the new geology. These crystalline reeds grow in sparse, isolated clusters, reaching meters into the water. They sway not in currents, but in response to the resonant frequencies of the world, vibrating in sympathy with the deep song of creation. They are the only true stationary things here, and they are themselves temporary, feeding on the afterglow of formation until the energy is spent and they too dissolve back into the liquid potential of the world.

The rules of this place are simple. Matter is not a constant; it is a temporary state, a violent precipitation from the liquid substrate. The sound is the announcement of this process. The tech is the process itself—a cosmic or nanoscale engine of creation so far beyond an artifact that it has become a law of nature, a self-sustaining turbulence that builds and unmakes reality. There is no ground, only temporary anchors. There is no life, only the slow, simple response of crystal-vegetation to energetic events.

Another formation event begins closer by. The hum rises to a painful shriek. The water around you thickens, the pressure shifts, and you are pushed back by an invisible wave of force. A new geodesic scaffold flashes into being, a web of impossibly thin, strong alloy that glows a dull cherry red. It is a world under construction, a foundry the size of a planet. You are a mote of dust in an engine, a silent witness in a womb that is delivering a cosmos of raw, screaming geology into the cool, quiet, and utterly vast emptiness.
