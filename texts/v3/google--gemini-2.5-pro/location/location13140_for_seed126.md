This is the Lattice. There is no ground, only the uppermost layer of a structure that descends into immeasurable, hazy depths. What you stand on is not rock or soil but a seamless, vitrified composite, a pavement of a soft, bone-white ceramic that stretches to every horizon without a single joint. The scale is planetary. You could walk in a straight line for a lifetime and never leave it, only perceive the faintest curvature against the impossible geometry above.

This world is ancient, and its erosion is not the work of water or wind, but of time itself. The perfect flatness of its creation is gone, softened by eons of slow, internal decay. The surface is no longer a perfect plane but a landscape of vast, gentle swales and swells, like a sea of milk frozen mid-ripple and then sanded smooth for a billion years. Here and there, the surface is pocked with shallow, glassy craters, the result of ancient energy discharges that melted the ceramic and let it cool into concave lenses. At the lowest point of these basins, you can sometimes see down into the next layer of the Lattice: a dimmer, ghost-image of the world you are on, crisscrossed with its own patterns, another continent of structure floating a mile below.

The entire world is a source of light. There is no sun, no moon, no stars. The light is a property of the material itself. It is a brilliant, shadowless, pearlescent glow that issues from the very ceramic beneath your feet and from the incomprehensibly vast ceiling of the next layer up, a shimmering, indistinct veil of light miles above. This creates a sky of uniform, radiant silver-white. The atmosphere is dense but thin, a high-pressure mix of inert noble gases that does not carry sound well. The world is wrapped in a profound quiet. The only sound is a deep, subsonic hum that you feel in your bones more than you hear with your ears—the vibration of the Cattice itself, the sound of a machine the size of a planet, still running. It is always warm, a constant, dry heat that radiates from the luminous floor.

Vegetation is sparse, and it is not biological in any terrestrial sense. It is crystalline. In the vast, shallow valleys, where cosmic dust and particles shed by the Lattice itself have settled over millennia, growths of "filament-lichen" appear. These are not soft, but brittle, fractal dustings of silicon that grow in slow, exquisite patterns, feeding on ambient energy. From a distance, they look like patches of pale blue or rust-red frost. More striking are the Spire-Fronds. These are solitary, needle-thin towers of pure, translucent crystal that grow directly out of the ceramic, sometimes reaching thirty feet in height. They grow in response to energy leylines running through the Lattice, tapping directly into its power. They chime, faintly, when the Lattice's energy flow shifts—a single, high, pure note that hangs in the thin, quiet air for a moment before fading completely. This is one of the only sharp sounds ever heard here.

The Lattice is not static. It is an active, dynamic system in a state of impossibly slow metabolism. Its "weather" is not meteorological, but energetic. The most common event is a Harmonic Tide. This is a visible, silent wave of increased luminosity that sweeps across the plains of the Lattice, moving at the speed of a running person. As it passes, the Spire-Fronds glow brightly from within and the filament-lichen shimmers, storing the energy. The hum of the world deepens to a low thrumming during a tide.

Rarer, and more significant, are the Recalibration Cascades. Without warning, a region miles wide will begin to subtly change. Geometric patterns of hairline cracks will appear in the ceramic floor, glowing with intense blue or violet light from the layers below. Sections of the "ground" may slowly, almost imperceptibly, rise or sink over the course of days, creating new swells and basins in the ancient topography. These are the processes of the Lattice's eternal self-repair and reconfiguration, a system so old its maintenance cycles are geological in their pace.

Fauna is exceedingly rare. For weeks, you might see nothing. Then, in the twilight of your own perception, you might spot a Glass Skipper. It is a creature perhaps the size of a dragonfly, apparently composed of warped, refractive crystal. It does not fly so much as it skates on the flows of energy, propelled by the Harmonic Tides. It moves in complete silence, its body bending and distorting the light passing through it, making it exceptionally difficult to see unless it crosses directly in front of you. They seem to feed by grazing on the filament-lichen, shattering the delicate crystalline structures with tiny, precise vibrations, consuming the stored energy. Their life cycle, their intelligence, their origin—all are unknown. They are simply a quiet, fleeting presence in this vast, constructed silence.

To be here is to be inside a machine that has outlived its creators by so long that it has become its own ecosystem, its own geology, its own world. The density of the place is not one of clutter, but of layered presence; you are always aware of the infinite strata above and below. Everything is made, but it is so old that the distinction between "natural" and "artificial" has been eroded into meaninglessness. The Lattice is. It hums, it glows, it shifts, it provides a floor for the almost-nonexistent life that skims its surface. It is a monument to an intelligence so vast and ancient it is now indistinguishable from a law of physics, a silent, luminous, and eternal engine idling at the end of time.
