This place exists only in the heart of a new mountain. Not a cave within it, but a chamber that is the mountain's purpose. The peak, an impossible spire of jagged, unweathered stone, scrapes the edge of the stratosphere. The air outside is a crystalline, killing cold, too thin to breathe. But here, inside, the world is different. It is a room, the size of a small cathedral's nave, carved not by hand or machine, but seemingly extruded from the mountain's own substance.

The terrain is a floor of polished, jet-black basalt, so smooth it seems liquid. It is not level. It swells in low, broad curves, like a frozen exhalation. From this floor rise countless slender pillars of the same material, sharp-edged and perfect. They are not columns holding up a roof; there is no roof, only the mountain's peak coalescing into a single point far above. These pillars form a dense, layered forest of stone, arranged in lattices and grids that suggest an urban plan, a city for beings with no breadth. The geometry is unnerving, full of repeating, fractal patterns that draw the eye into infinite, unsettling recursion. This is the city. An infrastructure with no inhabitants.

The material is everything. The walls, the floor, the pillars—all are a variant of obsidian, born not from cooling lava but from some other, more precise process. It is young stone, its edges so acute they might cut careless flesh. Embedded within the black matrix, visible just beneath the surface, is a lattice of pale orange filaments. They pulse with a slow, dim, biological rhythm, like the nadirs of a sine wave. This is the source of the only light, a faint, crepuscular glow that casts no true shadows, only deeper and shallower pools of darkness. The effect is of being inside a vast, sleeping circuit.

The air is hot. Not the dry, scouring heat of a desert, but a thick, oppressive humidity that clings to the skin. It’s a terrarium heat, baked-in and constant, radiating from the stone itself. The high elevation means the air is thin in pressure, yet heavy with moisture. This paradox creates a stifling atmosphere. Water, the only non-mineral presence here, manifests as a constant, slick condensation on the cooler upper reaches of the pillars. The heat from below meets the cold seeping through the stone from the stratospheric outside, and the air weeps. Fat, heavy droplets of distilled, perfectly pure water form and fall with an unnerving slowness in the thin air, landing on the basalt floor with a soft, resonant *plink* that echoes through the quiet.

There is no life. No plants, no animals, no microbes in the soil, for there is no soil. The world is pre-biological, or perhaps post-biological. Yet, it is not static. The entire chamber is a single, active machine. The pulsing filaments in the rock are its calculations. The subtle shifts in the heat are its processing cycles. This is a computer made of a mountain. A lithic engine performing some colossal, silent task. The "city" is its tangible interface, its logic gates and memory registers made manifest as architecture.

The only sounds are the products of this process. A foundational hum, so low it is felt more in the bones than heard with the ears, is the baseline of existence here. It is the sound of the Summit-Engine thinking. Layered over this is the intermittent, melodic dripping of condensation, each drop a percussive note in a slow, arrhythmic composition. Occasionally, a new sound will break the pattern: a sharp, crystalline chime that rings out from a specific pillar, as if a calculation has resolved. The chime hangs in the humid air for a moment before being reabsorbed into the foundational quiet.

The rules of this place are simple and absolute. Matter is data. The mountain is not a geological feature; it is a hard drive. The heat is not a byproduct of pressure, but of processing. The water is a cooling system, a passive condenser cycle. The "city" is not for living in; it is for thinking in.

To be here is to be an anomaly, a foreign body in a seamless system. The space is pocket-sized relative to the world, a single hidden chamber, yet it feels infinitely vast because it is so empty of anything that is not itself. The architecture seems to watch, its perfect angles and repeating forms a kind of passive surveillance. You are a mote of chaotic, biological dust in a sterile, ordered, and utterly alien mind. Its purpose is unknown. The result of its long, slow calculation is a mystery. Does it even know you are here? Or are you simply a momentary error, a fleck of noise in the signal, soon to be corrected and forgotten by the slow, hot, quiet thought of the mountain?
