This is a landscape of impossible sound.

Before sight, before the chill seeps into your bones, there is the sound. It is not the roar of a waterfall or the howl of wind, for there is no wind here, no moving water. It is a drone, a colossal and unwavering chord of grinding bass and dissonant, crystalline harmonics that fills all space. It has no source and no echo. It simply *is*, a fundamental property of the air, the rock, the very fabric of this place. The sound is so constant and all-pervading that after a time, the mind begins to treat it as silence, a crushing, heavy silence that makes the ears ache and the teeth vibrate in sympathy. It is the sound of a mountain range being turned to dust and reformed in the same instant, an eternal process frozen in a single, deafening moment.

The land itself is a conclave of peaks at the world's ceiling. These are not sharp, young mountains. They are ancient, worn down by an erosion that was not of water or ice, but of time itself. Their flanks are hewn into impossible, flowing geometries, as if a sculptor of incomprehensible scale had worked them when they were soft as clay, and they had then been instantly petrified. The stone is a dark, lustreless basalt that seems to drink what little light exists, but it's veined with thin, shimmering filaments that pulse with a faint, internal luminescence, a cold blue-white light that seems to thrum in time with some unheard rhythm beneath the great, deafening drone.

The air is thin, cold, and carries a peculiar dampness. It is not fog or mist, as these would imply movement. Instead, a permanent, near-invisible aerosol of supercooled moisture hangs in the absolute stillness. It does not fall as rain or condense into dew. It simply clings. Every surface is coated in a slick, gelatinous rime, a clear substance that never freezes and never evaporates. It has the texture of soft cartilage and feels unnervingly organic to the touch. Underfoot, the ground is not soil but a fine grit of the same black basalt, which gives way to larger, shattered plates of rock that lie like the scales of a dead leviathan.

Vegetation is a generous term. On the shaded faces of the mountains, away from the direct non-light of the sky, grow crystalline structures. They are not alive in any biological sense. They are formations of faceted, smoky quartz and a mineral that resembles tarnished silver, and they grow in fractal patterns, branching out from the rock like frozen lichen. They grow with imperceptible slowness, perhaps a millimeter a millennium, fed not by sun or water but by the omnipresent sound, which they seem to metabolize into their own complex, silent forms.

There are no structures here, not in the way one thinks of buildings. But scattered across the high, flat saddles between the peaks are arrangements of stone. These are the Echo Cairns. They are simple piles of the local rock, yet they defy the rules of this place. A cairn might cast a shadow in a direction that has no light source. Another, when viewed from the left, appears to be a neat stack, but from the right, is a scattered mess of stones ten feet apart. Most disturbingly, if you stand between two of them, the great drone of the landscape momentarily silences in one ear, creating a giddy, nauseating vertigo. They are not artifacts left by people; they are scars in reality, points where the physics of the world became permanently snarled, frozen in a state of contradiction.

This is the core truth of the peaks: reality is established in its brokenness. The weirdness is not an event; it is a geographic feature. There are zones, as clearly defined as riverbeds, where gravity is slightly weaker, causing your steps to feel unnaturally buoyant. There are sightlines across valleys that are not Euclidean; the far peak you see is actually behind you, its light bent around a curve in space. These anomalies are as fixed and unchanging as the mountains themselves. They are the laws of this domain.

Fauna is nearly non-existent. The only hint of anything that might be called an animal is a rare and terrifying phenomenon. Sometimes, a flicker of motion is perceived at the edge of vision. It is never a creature of flesh and bone. It is a ripple in the air, a localized distortion of light and space, like a heat haze in the freezing cold. These "Phantasms" drift with profound slowness across the plateaus. They have no physical form, but where they pass, the crystalline lichen shatters into dust and the drone of the world momentarily dips in pitch. They are akin to weather patterns in a place with no weather, the landscape's slow, digestive process made manifest. They are utterly silent, their presence known only by the subtle warping of the one great Sound.

Time does not pass here in a conventional way. The bruised, purple glow of the sky never brightens or dims. The rime never melts. The sound never changes. To be here is to be outside of progression, locked in a single, eternal, deafening frame of existence. It is a hollow place, a void defined by its own impossible noise, on a mountain range so old and so strange that it has forgotten the laws of the universe it was born into.
