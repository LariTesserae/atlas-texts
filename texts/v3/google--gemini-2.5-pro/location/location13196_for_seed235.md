The first thing is the sound.

It is not a collection of noises; it is a single, monolithic fact. A chord of impossible magnitude, struck once at the beginning of time and held in perfect, unending sustain. It has no direction. It is not carried by the air; it *is* the air, a pressurization of the atmosphere into a physical, vibrating presence. It presses on the skin, thrums in the bones, resonates behind the teeth. To be here is to be inside a bell that will never cease ringing. At first, it is deafening, an agony that erases all thought. But there is no "at first." There is only the eternal is-ness of the sound. After a time that does not pass, the mind gives up trying to hear it and simply submits to being *in* it. It becomes the substratum of all other sensations.

The second thing is the heat, a wet, suffocating blanket. The air is solid with moisture, hot enough that to breathe is to feel the lungs coated in warm fluid. The light is flat and pearlescent, seeming to emanate from the thick, white cloud-ceiling that is also the sky. There is no sun, no single point of origin for the light or the heat. They are simply properties of the place, as fundamental as the sound.

This place is a plateau, a vast tableland suspended at an impossible altitude. The edges are lost in a swirling, opalescent mist that never moves. From this plateau rises a city.

It is not a city that was built. It is a city that *calcified*. It is an urban arrangement of living, or once-living, things that have become mineral. Towers of jade-green and malachite pierce the atmospheric haze, their forms suggesting a slow, organic extrusion rather than construction. They are fluted, ribbed, and buttressed with sweeping arches of what looks like solidified, moss-colored resin. Their surfaces, slick with perpetual condensation, are not stone, not wood, but something in between—a fibrous, crystalline material that seems to have grown into its final, unchangeable shape eons ago.

These towers and spires form a dense, layered metropolis. They are connected by bridges that are not laid but are woven from thick, glassy lianas, now hard and immobile as quartz. Canyons between the towers serve as streets, their floors paved in a mosaic of mossy, kidney-shaped stones that fit together with biological precision. There is infrastructure here: channels and aqueducts carved into the living architecture, but the water within them does not flow. It sits, perfectly clear and still, a mirror held up to the weeping, green-sheened walls. Water perpetually oozes from every surface, condensing from the saturated air, yet it never gathers into puddles or trickles downwards. It simply clings, a universal, unmoving dew.

This is a world of lushness without life. The forms are those of vegetation—the towers are like petrified, gargantuan fungi; the linking structures are like fossilized vines; the ground cover is like moss turned to stone. But nothing grows. Nothing decays. There are no leaves to fall, no roots to spread. The entire ecology is frozen in a state of perfect, established verdure. It is a photograph of a jungle, rendered in three-dimensional, city-sized form. There are no animals. No insects drone, no birds call, no creatures rustle in the undergrowth that isn't undergrowth. The only movement is a lie told to the eyes.

This is the source of the weirdness, the breakdown. Reality here is inconsistent. Look at a spiraling tower, and its geometry seems to shift, not as if it's moving, but as if your memory of its shape from one moment to the next is unreliable. Distances are fluid. A canyon that appears a stone's throw away might take an eternity to cross, the space between you and it stretching like heated glass. Or you might blink and find yourself on a bridge you were only just observing from miles away. Perspective does not obey its own rules; a distant spire might be perfectly in focus while a wall beside you is a blurry smear.

It feels as if the entire landscape is a memory, a recording being played back without the dimension of time. The deafening sound is the carrier wave for this static image of a world. It is the hum of the projector, the resonance of a crystal that holds a single, complex thought. All the energy of the place—the intense heat, the high potential energy of the altitude, the biological imperative of the lush forms—is perfectly balanced, creating a system in total, roaring equilibrium. Nothing can happen here. A rock dislodged would not fall; it would hang in the air, a new, permanent feature of the scene. A footstep would make no sound, swallowed by the monolithic chord.

This is a city of ghosts where there were never any people. It is a jungle that has never known an animal. It is a world screaming at the top of its lungs, forever, about nothing at all. To exist here is to be the only anomaly, the only thing that is not part of the eternal, unchanging, deafening chord. You are a discordant note in a symphony that has no room for you.
