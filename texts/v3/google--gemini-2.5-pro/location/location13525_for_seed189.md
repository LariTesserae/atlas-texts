This place is not found, but breached. You don't descend into it; you crack it open. From the tight, mineral-smelling dark of a fissure below the surface, you push through a final membrane. It feels like tearing wet parchment, and the air that meets you is warm, humid, and thrumming with a sound so profound it is more a feeling in the bones than something heard by the ears.

You are inside. Inside a single, hollow, living thing.

The space is a chamber, an ovoid maybe forty paces across, a hidden pocket in the deep earth. Yet there is no earth here. The floor, walls, and ceiling are one continuous, living surface. It is a material that is neither bark nor flesh, a smooth, semi-translucent shell of pale green and amber, webbed with a network of brighter, citrine veins carrying a slow-pulsing light. This is the source of the chamber's illumination: a steady, bright, daylight-equivalent glow that casts no distinct shadows, as it emanates from every surface. The light feels clean and vital.

The chamber is almost entirely empty. The vast, open air at its center is the defining feature, a shocking void cradled within this shell of absolute life. This emptiness creates an unnerving scale. The space feels intimate, like a room, but the perfect curve of the walls and the lack of any corners make distances deceptive. Staring across the chamber can induce a soft vertigo, a sense of falling into the featureless light.

But the emptiness is not total. Crossing the void are impossibly thin filaments of the same luminous plant matter. They stretch from wall to wall, floor to ceiling, in long, taut lines and gentle catenary curves. Some are as fine as spun silk, others as thick as a wrist. They form a vast, three-dimensional web, a minimalist architecture that delineates the void. They are freshly grown, so new that they seem to shimmer with residual energy, their surfaces sharp and perfectly formed. Walking through the space requires navigating this lattice, brushing past strands that vibrate with the chamber's deep hum.

The sound is immense. It is a biological roar. A deep, resonant baritone note holds steady, the thrum of sap or nutrient fluid circulating at pressure through the chamber's walls. This is the bass note of the world. Layered over it are sharp, percussive cracks, like ice fracturing. These are the sounds of growth—a new filament anchoring itself to the far wall, a structural rib thickening, a sudden expansion of the chamber itself. The place is in a constant, audible state of becoming. The sound is so loud it muffles your own footsteps, your own breathing. Echoes are stillborn, swallowed by the living walls before they can form.

The air is warm and damp. The plant-shell breathes. Its inner surface is slick with condensation, a constant, gentle perspiration. Droplets form, merge, and trickle down the curved walls in meandering paths, carving temporary, dark channels in the glowing surface before gathering in shallow, perfectly clear pools on the floor. The water is sweet to the taste, energizing in a way that feels both nourishing and deeply alien.

This is a system with almost no fauna. Life is monopolized by the great plant-vessel. But if you look closely at the walls, near the pools of water, you might see them: tiny creatures, the size of a fingernail, like silver mites. They have too many legs and move with a fluid, multi-jointed gait, scraping nutrients from the slick surface with minuscule, iridescent mandibles. They are the only native inhabitants, skittering through the endless, quiet light.

But they are not the only ones to have been here. There are marks of intrusion. On one wall, a wide, shallow arc has been scraped away, the wound already healing over with a brighter, scar-like tissue. The tool-marks are rough, made by something like sharpened flint or obsidian, showing where someone methodically harvested a sheet of the living material. Several of the thicker filaments have been severed, their cut ends dangling and slowly dripping a viscous, glowing sap that hardens into amber tears on the floor below. This is the work of a hand, a mind. Someone comes here to gather, to use this impossible place for their own primitive ends, wielding simple tools against a reality that should defy them.

And reality is thin here. Stare too long at the web of filaments and they will seem to shift, rearranging themselves when you are not looking directly at them. The path you took to cross the chamber may not be the path you take to return. The constant, shadowless light can play tricks on the eye, making the far wall seem to rush towards you, or recede into an impossible distance. The air itself feels charged, and a deep breath can leave you feeling dislocated, as if you are simultaneously here and somewhere else entirely, your thoughts echoing with a faint, silent hum that matches the chamber's resonant frequency. You are inside a lung, a heart, a seed. You are a parasite in the belly of a god you cannot see, in a room that is growing around you, a temporary void in a universe made of plant.
