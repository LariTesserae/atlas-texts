This place is a sound before it is a sight. The sound is a constant, overwhelming, abrasive hum. It is not the deep thrum of machinery, but a high-frequency, complex, layered static, like a million tiny workshops operating at once. It is the sound of grinding, of scraping, of infinite, furious patience. It fills the air, pressing in from all sides, a wall of noise that makes the silence between your own heartbeats feel like a profound emptiness.

The air is cold and carries a wet, mineral tang. It is thick with a gloom that is not quite total darkness. A diffuse, sourceless twilight percolates through a dense, permanent fog, revealing a forest of impossibilities. You are standing at an extreme altitude, but not on a peak. You are *in* it. This is a grove of stone, a tight cluster of crystalline spires that stab upward into the curdled sky. They are packed together, their bases nearly touching, creating a labyrinth of narrow, vertical canyons. The scale is intimate, like a cathedral’s nave, if the cathedral had been shattered and compressed.

The spires themselves are geologically adolescent. They are not worn or rounded by time but are things of sharp, aggressive angles and clean, fractured planes. They look as if they were violently thrust from the planet’s core only yesterday and then frozen in place. The material is not granite or basalt, but something like smoky quartz or obsidian, shot through with veins of dull, metallic ore. Its surface is slick, perpetually weeping a cold condensation that gathers and drips in a steady, maddening rhythm, a slow percussive beat beneath the all-consuming static. This constant dampness pools in the narrow gaps between the spires, forming still, black puddles that never seem to evaporate.

There is life here, but not as it is commonly understood. No trees, no grass. The sparse vegetation is a thin, greasy film that coats the spires, a kind of lithotrophic rime that gnaws infinitesimally at the crystal. It glows with a barely-perceptible phosphorescence, a sickly green-white light that contributes to the gloom rather than alleviating it. This is the bedrock of the ecosystem. This is the pasture.

And the grazers are the source of the sound.

Every surface is alive. The overwhelming fauna of this place is a species of crustacean-like, six-legged creatures, each the size of a human hand. Their carapaces are the same color as the dark crystal they inhabit, faceted and sharp-edged, a perfect camouflage. They are a tide of bodies, a living sheath coating the spires. Tens of thousands of them, hundreds of thousands, are visible in any given glance, moving with a slow, deliberate, ceaseless industry. This is the "density" of the place: the rock and the life upon it are one continuous, near-touching mass.

They are the source of the noise. Each creature clutches a shard of the same dark crystal in its two foremost appendages—a primitive, found tool. With these sharp-edged scrapers, they methodically plane the phosphorescent rime from the spires. The sound is the collective noise of millions of crystal shards scraping against a crystal mountain, a high-pitched, chittering, grinding symphony of work. This is their technology, their existence. They scrape, they eat, they breed, and when they die, their faceted husks tumble down into the dark crevices to be slowly covered by the droppings of their kin, forming a new stratum of compressed detritus.

There are no predators, no grand dramas. The a-dynamic nature of this world is absolute. The fog never lifts. The temperature never changes. The sun is a forgotten myth. The only cycles are the slow dripping of water and the grinding, generational creep of the scrapers up the spires. The weirdness is in this stability: a place of such violent, young topography inhabited by an ecosystem of absolute, unchanging routine. The world is loud with the noise of life, yet nothing ever *happens*.

This is a world ruled by a single, unyielding verb: to scrape. The air vibrates with it. The light is made of its byproduct. The ground is paved with its dead. It is a place of extreme verticality overcome by a horizontal purpose, a dark, cold, wet factory whose only product is its own continuation, humming its one and only note into the endless, foggy dark.
