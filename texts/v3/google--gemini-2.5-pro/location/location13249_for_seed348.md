This is a city built in a basin, a vast, low-lying dish of ceramic-like stone. To call it a city feels both correct and deeply misleading. It has the structure of a metropolis—streets, plazas, and towering, blocky edifices—but it is not for any people you would recognize. The air is cool and carries a perpetual dampness, a slick of condensation that coats every surface in a thin, greasy film. The atmosphere is heavy, thick with the tang of ozone, hot metal, and a complex, cloying biological odor, like a sealed terrarium full of rotting fruit and electricity.

The ground underfoot is not pavement but a mosaic of interlocking metal plates and stressed, ancient ferro-concrete, worn smooth by the passage of ages. There are no plants. Not a single leaf, blade of grass, or strand of moss. The world is shades of grey, rust-red, verdigris-green, and the flat, non-color of old plastic. The architecture is brutal and functional, great windowless cubes and ziggurats connected by enclosed sky-bridges and gantries. These structures are not built to human scale; they are immense, like the engine blocks of gods, their surfaces streaked with dark liquids that weep from hairline fractures and overflow from gutters. This is the industrial heart, the machinery of the place. Giant, ribbed pipes, wide as ancient tree trunks, snake along the foundations of buildings, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic thrum. The sound is deafening, a layered and unceasing symphony of industry. It is a chord made of a million noises: the deep, resonant hum of colossal generators, the high-frequency whine of unseen turbines, the percussive CLANG-hiss-CLANG-hiss of pneumatic presses, and the constant, showering sizzle of electrical discharge.

This sound is not sterile or clean. It is a backdrop, a canvas upon which an even greater noise is painted. For the city is not empty. It is overwhelmingly, suffocatingly full of life.

Every surface is in motion. The "fauna" here has consumed the role of vegetation, soil, and water. They are the ecology. Swarming the bases of the great factory-towers are the Boiler-Whelks. These are creatures the size of small vehicles, with iridescent, heat-blackened shells that look like cast iron. They move with geological slowness, their fleshy, grey underbodies adhering to vast, heated plates embedded in the city floor. They are not animals that live *in* the factory; they *are* the factory. Their bodies are living furnaces, consuming a nutrient-rich sludge that flows in open channels alongside the streets—a thick, ochre-colored gruel produced by the city's automated chemical processors. The heat shimmering from their shells creates localized thermals, sending eddies of vapor spiraling into the air.

Above, the air is thick with shimmering, chitinous clouds. These are the Skitter-wings, insectile creatures no larger than a human hand, with wings of mica-thin, glassy material that beat at an impossible speed. They exist in the billions. Their flight is not graceful; it is a chaotic, frenetic swarm, and their primary sound is a constant, high-pitched clattering, like a billion tiny beads being poured onto a tin roof. They feed on the aerosols, the very air itself, which is saturated with microscopic particles of protein and minerals vented from filtration stacks. They fill the vertical space of the city, their countless bodies dimming the light and turning the open plazas into echoing halls of rattling sound.

The light is not from a sun. High, high above, spanning the entire basin, is a ceiling. It is a lattice of glowing filaments, a vast and intricate web that sheds a continuous, flat, shadowless light onto the scene below. It is the color of a cloudy afternoon, and it never changes. There is no day, no night. There is only the perpetual, unvarying Now.

This unceasing state is the deepest rule of the world. The city is a perfectly balanced, closed-loop system, frozen in a state of loud, frantic equilibrium. The factories process some unknown, piped-in raw material into the nutrient gruel and energized aerosols. The Boiler-Whelks consume the gruel, generating the heat and waste products that power smaller systems. The Skitter-wings consume the aerosols. Their waste, a fine, metallic dust, falls like a constant, gentle snow, coating everything and providing the raw grit for yet another phylum of life: the Lissotrichs. These are silent, serpentine cleaners, like meter-long slugs made of polished chrome and wet leather. They flow over every surface, ingesting the dust, their bodies acting as living polishers, preventing the entire city from being buried. Their trails are the only clean, dark lines in a world of damp patina. They move with a steady, unhurried purpose, their paths as fixed and unchanging as the orbits of planets.

The population of every creature is stable. The output of the machines is stable. The temperature, the humidity, the light, the constant, overwhelming sound—all are locked into a state of permanence. It is not quiet stasis, but a dynamic stasis. A waterfall, frozen in place, yet with every drop still roaring and churning. The city does not grow or decay. It simply *is*. It is a cathedral built for a billion mindless gods, a perpetual motion machine of flesh and metal whose only purpose is to continue. There are no masters, no inhabitants in the way one might think. The builders are long gone, or perhaps the city built itself. To stand in one of its sparse, echoing plazas is to be a ghost in the machine, an irrelevant observer to a process that began before time and will continue long after. The ground vibrates under your feet, the air is cold and tastes of rust, and the noise presses in, a physical weight that promises no silence will ever come.
