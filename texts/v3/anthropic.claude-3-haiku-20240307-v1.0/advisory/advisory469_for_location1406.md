Preparing to visit this ancient, otherworldly seabed would require extensive planning and specialized equipment. Here are some key considerations for the ordinary human multiverse traveler:

Breathing Apparatus: The immense depth and pressure of this underwater realm make regular scuba gear entirely insufficient. You would need a highly advanced, specialized rebreather system capable of providing a stable, breathable atmosphere under extreme conditions. The equipment would need to be meticulously maintained and tested before the dive.

Pressure Suits: Normal diving suits would be crushed by the phenomenal water pressure. You would require a state-of-the-art atmospheric diving suit (ADS) - a nearly self-contained, articulated exosuit that can withstand immense depths while allowing a reasonable range of motion and dexterity.

Illumination: The limited bioluminescent glow would provide only the barest of visibility. Powerful, long-lasting LED lighting systems would be essential, both mounted on the diving suit and handheld. These lights would need to be resistant to the intense pressure.

Navigation: Without recognizable landmarks or currents, normal underwater navigation techniques would be useless. Advanced sonar, mapping systems, and inertial guidance would be necessary to maintain orientation and find your way in this alien seascape.

Life Support: The dive would need to be meticulously planned, with redundant life support systems and backup emergency measures. You may need to bring your own breathable atmosphere in cryogenic tanks. Extensive decompression stops on the return journey would also be critical.

Mental Preparation: Visiting this tranquil yet unsettling realm would be a profoundly disorienting experience. The utter stillness, the alien landscapes, and the crushing solitude could take a severe psychological toll. Mental exercises, training, and emotional fortitude would be vital.

Only the most skilled, determined, and psychologically resilient individuals would have any hope of successfully navigating and surviving an expedition to this ancient, otherworldly sea. The risks and challenges would be immense, but the potential for discovery and revelation could be world-changing. Ultimately, it would require an explorer's heart, a scientist's mind, and a philosopher's soul.
