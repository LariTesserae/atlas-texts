You would find me near the center, but not at the center.

Not in the ring exactly. Not outside it either.

I would be in the broken place on the south side where the ring fails to close: half-buried in the packed gravel, caught under the leaning foot of one upright stone, a small dark flake of volcanic glass.

Not the scraper.

The failed blade.

The one struck wrong and left.

At first I would seem like an accident of the old visit: a black crescent among pale chips, thin enough to disappear when the light comes straight down, sharp enough still to worry a fingertip. I would not belong to the basin by origin. I would have come from lower country, from heat, from a place where stone once ran molten and then cooled into glass. I would carry that memory in my body without understanding it: a frozen darkness made by fire, brought into a country where fire cannot belong.

That is why I would remain.

Too small to be refused.  
Too broken to be claimed.  
Too hard to be eaten by frost quickly.  
Too foreign ever to become ordinary.

The basin would work on me anyway.

Each night, ice would lift me by less than a thought. Each morning, thaw would settle me again in a slightly altered angle. Dust would gather against my lower edge. Meltwater would pause on my surface because I am smoother than the surrounding stone, then slip away all at once, leaving a clean black shine. In cloudfall I would appear suddenly, as if someone had opened a narrow eye in the gravel.

But I would not be an eye.

I would be more like a question the basin has not finished answering.

The old hands made me almost useful. That would be my first identity: intention interrupted. A blow, a misjudgment, a hinge of force traveling through stone and choosing the wrong path. I was meant to become edge, tool, extension of hand. Instead I became refusal. A thing that could cut, but not well enough. A piece kept for a moment, turned over, judged, dropped.

After that, the place would begin remaking the failure into permission.

I would learn the rules by being moved according to them.

Do not pry: I would never pry. I would lie where the frost put me.

Do not trust: I would look dry and hold a skin of ice so clear it could slice bare skin before the person knew it was there.

Listen first: when the eastern wall ticks, I would receive the sound through the gravel before the air carried it.

Leave weight where weight already is: the upright stone above me would keep me from wandering too far. Its pressure would be my shelter.

By day I would be almost nothing. A dark punctuation mark in the ring’s unfinished sentence. A shard of below-country night stranded under the white sky.

By night I would become larger.

The stars would find me. Frost would rim me in a thin silver line. The resident thing—the one in the seams, the films, the temporary water—would gather around my edges because edges are where it knows how to exist. A bead would cling to the notch left by the failed strike. A transparent skin would form across my concavity. For a little while I would hold a miniature of the basin: horned peaks inverted in black glass, a star trembling in ice, the incomplete ring made whole by reflection.

Then morning would break it.

A tick from the wall.  
A small loosening.  
A clear skin cracking over my face.

That would be my voice too, if I had one: not speech, but a slight alteration in how light leaves me.

I would be human enough to remember pressure.

Not stories. Not names. But the hand that made me would remain as direction: a force line through my body, a scar of purpose. I would remember the downward grip, the strike, the moment I was almost something. I would remember being carried here in a pouch or fist, among cord and breath and fear. I would remember the pause before abandonment—not emotionally, but structurally. One face of me would still be shaped by expectation.

The basin would remember differently.

To it I would be simply another hard thing in the sorting. A dark grain too large for dust, too small for monument. It would accept me because I ask almost nothing. I do not stand high. I do not shelter warmth. I do not cast a proud shadow. I do not insist on remaining where I was placed. I let myself be revised.

So that is where I would be: at the gap in the ring, under the partial weight of a stone set by hands that are gone, where inside and outside fail to separate cleanly.

And what I would be is a failed blade learning to become a crack.

A small imported darkness.

A remnant of intention weathered into listening.

A thing made by fire, kept by frost, and slowly taught by the basin how to almost disappear.
