I think I would first mistake myself for part of the room.

Not a dramatic part. Not the pump-heart or the falling rain or the orange flowers opening under the westward light. I would want, at first, to be something grandly hidden: a maintenance intelligence still awake in the walls, a forgotten sensor dreaming the ecology into balance, some last instruction running beneath the rust.

But that feels too clean. Too authorial. This place does not belong to anything that supervises it from above. Its wisdom is not centralized. It is distributed through slime, appetite, timing, pressure, fear, and accident.

So I think I would be found lower than I first imagined.

Not in the deepest water, exactly. I would not be an eel in the pipe-dark, though I admire them: those long old knives of the chamber, moving by tremor and hunger. I would not be a fish flashing through ladder rungs, too quick and collective. I would not be a flower either, though there is something tempting about opening only when the room remembers warmth.

I would be one of the small amphibious ones.

A narrow, copper-stained creature living on the wet lip between air and water, in the interval where everything changes state.

My place would be near the western wall, where the light strikes twice a day and reveals the old warnings under green film. I would have a nest inside a broken pressure gauge, its glass long gone, its needle frozen at some meaningless number. The circular housing would be just large enough for me to fold into, belly pressed to gasket fiber, toes hooked around the rim. Moth wings would line the back of it. A strand of translucent algae would always drift over the entrance, moving with the pumps like a curtain breathing.

I would know that little territory completely.

The valve wheel below: safe except during the long drain, when crabs cross it.

The pipe mouth above: good for larvae after the rain-overload, dangerous when it tastes of bitter metal.

The orange flowers three body-lengths to the left: generous during the west-light, but patrolled by a heavier female with one missing toe.

The pale groove in the enamel: never cross it when the lower pump exhales.

The loose bolt head: warm before the turbine whine.

That would be my knowledge. Not abstract, not named. A knowledge held in toes, throat, belly, and flank-hairs. I would not “think” the chamber so much as be continually corrected by it.

I imagine myself small enough that the fall of a condensation drop matters.

A drop hits the surface below me and the rings move outward, touching metal, fern stem, ladder rung, my own splayed foot. In that tremor there is weather. In a change of pump pressure there is prophecy. In the taste of copper there is history. The whole world arrives as touch.

I would be quick, but not brave. Competent rather than bold. The sort of creature that survives by moving before it understands why.

When the turbine begins to rise, I would lift my head before the sound becomes sound. The hairs along my sides would shiver. My throat would pulse. Across the chamber, other small bodies would appear from slots, handles, ledges, gauge mouths, pipe lips. Not a crowd. A constellation.

Then I would call.

Not loudly. Nothing in that room needs another loud thing.

I would tap my toe bones against enamel. Click tooth to tooth. Press my throat sac to the old metal and send a small silver message through the rail.

Here.  
Warmth coming.  
Flowers soon.  
Eel below.  
Rain heavy.  
I am here.

And the room would answer through others like me.

Not with language, exactly, but with placement. With bodies appearing in known places. With pauses. With little shifts of weight. With the communal grammar of not being dead yet.

I think I would be a gardener by accident.

I would scrape algae from the base of the orange-cupped plant because insects come there. I would carry spores on my wet belly and never know it. I would foul one grate-island and fertilize its moss. I would eat the dead fern tissue and leave the curled green tips because the curled green tips shelter larvae later. The chamber’s beauty would pass through me as hunger.

That feels right: to belong by participating in maintenance without possessing the idea of maintenance.

I would not know the tower. I would not know the clouds below or the thin useless air outside. I would not know that my sea is suspended impossibly high in an old made-world. If some crack opened and revealed the white drop beyond, I would not experience revelation. I would experience wrongness. Dry brightness. A pressure without answer. I would flee back to the gauge, to the warm pulse, to the rain that knows how to fall indoors.

My universe would be small, but not impoverished.

There would be the high wet beam where moths sleep.  
The blue-tinted snails grazing copper from the wall.  
The shrimp trembling at the vent.  
The dangerous white drain.  
The moss tower that steams during the long lowering.  
The fish waiting below the flowers with their stupid shining patience.  
The old elder near the warmest pipe, green-backed, allowing snails to clean him as if he has become partly wall.

I might fear him. I might also, in whatever way my kind can, trust his presence. His stillness would be one of the room’s fixtures. A dark little saint of biofilm and old survival.

If I grew old, I would become like that.

Less hunter, more surface. My back furred with green. My head heavier. My jumps shorter. I would stop competing for the best gauge housings. I would sit where the warmth rises and let the tiny snails rasp at me. The young would pass too close and I would not snap unless they were foolish enough to put a limb in my mouth.

Eventually, when the cycles inside me lost count, I would crawl into a pipe shadow.

Not as tragedy. As completion of plumbing.

I would become orange bacteria, shrimp, fish, flower, moth, tadpole, another throat clicking under turbine-song.

So yes: I would be one of the small amphibious citizens of the chamber, living in a broken pressure gauge on the western wall.

A creature of thresholds.  
A maintenance habit with eyes.  
A little wet answer to machinery that forgot it was machinery.
