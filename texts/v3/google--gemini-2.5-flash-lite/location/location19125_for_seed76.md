This is the Expanse of the Sky-Bones.

The terrain here is a breathtaking, almost violent testament to unimaginable geological ages and processes that predate any familiar concept of a planet. Imagine vast, undulating plains of fractured, crystalline minerals that catch the ceaseless, bright light from a colossal, perpetually-fixed star, making the ground glitter with a thousand internal fires. These plains are not smooth; they are a chaotic mosaic of shattered plateaus, colossal shards thrust upward like broken teeth, and deep, silent chasms that plunge into unimaginable darkness. The material underfoot is primarily composed of brittle, silica-rich rock interspersed with veins of a metallic, obsidian-like substance that hums with a faint, internal luminescence. There is no soil as we understand it, no dust to swirl in the wind – only stark, mineral beauty.

Despite the arid nature of the ground, the vegetation is astonishingly lush. It doesn't grow in soil, but extrudes from fissures in the rock and the sheer faces of the stone formations. Think of immense, phosphorescent fungi that spread like living tapestries across the obsidian plains, pulsing with a soft, internal light that adds to the pervasive brightness. Towering, crystalline growths, resembling gargantuan, petrified trees, pierce the sky, their branches made of faceted, semi-translucent minerals that refract the starlight into dazzling spectral displays. Delicate, ephemeral vines, woven from filaments of light and something akin to spun glass, drape themselves across the shattered landscape, occasionally catching the faint thermal currents and swaying with an eerie grace. These plants seem to draw sustenance directly from the ambient light and the deep, mineral-rich fissures.

The air is thin and cool, carrying a faint, metallic tang. There is no moisture in the conventional sense. The "water" parameter being so low signifies a complete absence of liquid water, or indeed any of the common solvents that drive familiar ecosystems. The chemistry here is likely alien, perhaps based on something else entirely, or the processes are so slow and stable that the need for liquid mediation is bypassed. The cool temperature is a relative concept; it's not frigid, but a crisp, clear coolness that never warms to anything approaching heat.

The Expanse of the Sky-Bones is at an immense elevation, not on a planet's surface, but adrift in some cosmic sea. The sky is a perpetual twilight, dominated by a blinding, unmoving star that hangs at a fixed, impossibly high point. The light is so intense and constant that there are no distinct day or night cycles. Stars, nebulae, and distant galaxies are visible as ghostly smudges through the brilliant glare, their light too faint to compete.

The erosion here speaks of eons of undisturbed existence. The forms, while fractured, are ancient and weathered, etched by forces that are slow and inexorable. Think of wind-blasted edges on the giant crystal trees, smooth, polished surfaces on the plains where something has, over millennia, scoured them clean, and the perfectly formed, sharp edges of chasms that have widened not by collapse, but by a slow, internal expansion. It is a landscape in a state of deep, enduring equilibrium, having reached its current form and now existing within its own timeless cycle.

The scale is truly vast, stretching beyond comprehension. This is not a continent, but a self-contained realm of immense proportions, a physical space that commands its own weather patterns, its own unique atmospheric physics. The vastness emphasizes the sparseness of 'presences.'

Fauna is rare, almost non-existent in the conventional sense. What life exists is either deeply embedded within the mineral structures, or it is ephemeral and tied to the unique biology of the luminous vegetation. Perhaps there are occasional, slow-moving mineral grazers, or silicate-based organisms that subsist on ambient energy. But there are no herds, no swarms, no creatures that occupy the open spaces with any regularity.

The built environment is utterly absent. There are no structures, no tools, no evidence of intelligent design or habitation by any species that would alter the natural forms. This is a place of pure, elemental existence, untouched by artifice.

The atmosphere carries an unusual stillness. The sounds are muted, often limited to the faint, resonant hum of the crystalline materials, the near-inaudible sigh of thermal drafts through chasms, and the occasional, delicate chime of crystalline branches brushing together. The silence is not an emptiness, but a presence, a profound lack of the clamor of life.

The dynamic nature of this place is subtle but significant. The vegetation grows and pulses with its internal light, the crystalline growths slowly expand or fracture over geological timescales, and the faint atmospheric currents stir the upper reaches of the chasms. The 'active' parameter describes the inherent, slow-moving processes of this unique ecosystem, the constant, albeit imperceptible, processes of formation, decay, and transformation within its alien chemistry.

The 'weirdness' manifests in the fundamental disconnects: lush growth in a waterless, mineral-rich void; a constant, blinding light without a discernible sun's movement; the sheer scale and alien chemistry. It’s a place that challenges every biological and geological assumption. It is a monument to existence, stark, brilliant, and profoundly alone. A landscape of sky-bones, where light is the water, minerals are the soil, and time is the sculptor.
