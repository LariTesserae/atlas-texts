The Sky-Ocean is a world of perpetual twilight, a realm where the very air has forgotten the touch of solid ground. It is a planet utterly saturated with water, not as continents and seas, but as a boundless, breathable medium stretching from what would be its surface, kilometers deep, to an edge of cosmic mist. This is a submerged world, but not one of crushing pressure and crushing dark. Instead, the water here is strangely buoyant, less a liquid and more a viscous, shimmering gradient of atmospheric density, thick enough to support colossal structures, yet thin enough to allow for unimpeded movement.

Vegetation, in the terrestrial sense, is utterly absent. There are no roots to grip, no leaves to photosynthesize in the dim, diffused light. The world is, in this regard, a stark mineral or constructed one, a canvas of sculpted stone and alien alloys that have been shaped over eons of slow, deliberate change. The erosion rate of 2.8/3 speaks to an ancient, settled state, but not one of gentle weathering. Here, erosion is a process of ceaseless, monumental dissolution and reformation, driven by the deep currents and the very nature of the world's materials. Imagine a primordial ooze that has, over unimaginable time, hardened into crystalline structures and vast, planar surfaces, only to be slowly, exquisitely worn away by the endless flow.

The temperature is a constant, biting chill, a deep cold that permeates the water itself. This isn't the sharp, stinging cold of ice, but a profound, pervasive frigidity, like being perpetually submerged in glacial meltwater. Yet, this cold doesn't freeze; it simply *is*, and life has adapted to it.

Elevation here is a concept turned on its head. The world is "high" in that it exists far above any implied planetary core, a vast, open expanse of mid-range scale, stretching out in every direction beyond immediate comprehension. One can drift for days, weeks, even years and still behold the same grand, unhurried vistas of impossible architecture and geological formations. But "openness" also describes the sparse density – vast abysses of nothingness dotted with islands of impossibly intricate construction.

This is a built world, utterly and completely. There is no natural substrate to be found. Everything one perceives, from the towering spires that pierce the perpetual gloom to the intricate latticework that forms the very foundations of this submerged realm, is the product of creation. It is an environment entirely made, an artificial cosmos. The tech level is paradoxically zero – no devices, no tools as we understand them. The builders and inhabitants of this place, whatever they are, operate on principles beyond our comprehension, shaping their world through sheer will, biological manipulation, or forces we cannot quantify.

Light is a scarce commodity, a dim, diffused luminescence that struggles to penetrate the depths. It is not the sharp, directional light of a sun, but a general, ambient glow, like a perpetual moonlit night filtered through a miles-thick fog. This dimness, however, is punctuated by the abundant fauna. Creatures of all shapes and sizes, adapted to this sub-aquatic, cold, and sparsely lit existence, flit and drift through the water. They are the vibrant pulse in this otherwise serene, ancient landscape.

The "weirdness" factor of 0.7/3 hints at something unusual, but not outright alien. The fauna itself likely contributes to this – bioluminescent organisms casting their own ethereal light, creatures with forms that defy terrestrial biology, or perhaps their behaviors are simply so alien as to be unsettling.

The soundscape is mostly quiet, a muted symphony of displacement and distant hums. The water muffles sharp noises, leaving only the soft rush of currents, the groaning of ancient structures shifting under unseen pressures, and the occasional, resonant call of a deep-dwelling creature.

But the most defining characteristic of the Sky-Ocean is its dynamism. The 3.0/3 rating signifies a world in perpetual, chaotic change. This is not a passive, still world. Its currents are not gentle flows but Raging torrents, its structural integrity is constantly tested, and its inhabitants, the abundant fauna, are engaged in an endless, frenetic dance of existence. Imagine tectonic plates shifting not on land, but within the very fabric of this super-dense atmosphere, causing seismic ripples that distort the light and churn the water into temporary maelstroms. Towering spires might sway, chasms might open and close, and the very geometry of the constructed world might subtly reconfigure itself with startling speed. This is a universe in a constant state of becoming and undoing, a place where stability is a fleeting, beautiful illusion.
