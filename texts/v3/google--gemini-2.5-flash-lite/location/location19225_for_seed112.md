The sky is a high, impossibly bright expanse, a polished, unblemished dome of pure white light that seems to press down from an infinite distance. There is no sun, no discernible source, just this overwhelming, uniform brilliance that makes shadows brittle and short-lived. Below, the landscape unfurls in an impression of impossible density and terrifying openness.

It is *plant*. Not a garden, not a forest, but a single, colossal entity of living green that has subsumed the very ground. The terrain is not soil or rock, but tightly woven, impossibly strong, fibrous matter – the desiccated, petrified remains of ancient flora, now hardened into a substrate that cracks and groans under the immense pressure of its own growth. This "ground" is a continent of interwoven roots and compressed biomass, a vast, porous weave that rises and falls in immense, slow-motion waves. Think of a planet-sized ball of matted hair, but made of lignin and cellulose, solidified over eons.

The vegetation parameter is a stark understatement. It isn't just *present*; it *is* the world. Towering structures that would dwarf mountains are not mountains at all, but colossal, calcified stalks of what might once have been arboreal life. Their surfaces are a tapestry of ancient, ossified bark, ridged and cracked like the hide of some primordial beast. Between these sky-piercing titans, the surface is choked with a dense, multi-layered carpet of perpetually growing and decaying plant matter. It’s a thick, spongy mass that smells faintly of ozone and overripe fruit, even in its dryness.

The dryness is absolute. There is no water, not a drop, not a hint of moisture in the air or the matted ground. It’s a world of profound thirst, where life persists through a chemistry that defies earthly understanding, perhaps drawing sustenance from the abundant light and the planet's own internal, mineral-rich processes. The air itself feels thin and dry, carrying a constant, faint static charge. Temperatures hover around a cool, dry equilibrium, a perpetual state of dehydration that never quite freezes.

This world is vast, a continental sprawl where colossal flora forms weather patterns of their own. The *density* parameter is misleadingly low: sparse in terms of distinct *entities*, but overwhelmingly dense in terms of biomass. The space between the colossal stalk-trees is filled with a churning, roiling atmosphere, not of water vapor, but of pollen, spores, and microscopic, wind-borne seeds. This dynamic, turbulent air is a constant, churning blizzard of organic particulate.

The *erosion* is not geological, but biological and atmospheric. The colossal stalks are slowly weathered by the abrasive pollen-storms, their surfaces etched into intricate patterns. The fibrous ground beneath is slowly ground down, not by water, but by the relentless growth and decay of the unending plant-life, which continuously reclaims and reforms any exposed surface. It’s an erosion of being, a constant disintegration and reformation at the fundamental level of the world’s substrate.

There are no built structures, no remnants of any civilization that ever scratched at this plant-dominated surface. It is primordial, an ancient, self-sustaining ecosystem. Yet, the *tech* parameter, at industrial, suggests something else entirely. Amidst the organic anarchy, strange, almost alien *machinery* hums and whirs. These are not metallic constructs in the way we understand them, but bio-mechanical hybrids. Towering, articulated limbs, resembling enormous mechanical insects, silently tend to the colossal flora, pruning, grafting, and harvesting. These are not built *upon* the world, but *of* it, grown and integrated into the plant-fabric. They emerge from the ground, their metallic components fused with woody structures, their movements driven by unseen energetic processes. Think of titanic, automated gardening tools, born from a fusion of nature and industrialization.

The *light*, a bright 2.6, bathes everything in a harsh, unforgiving glare. The *fauna*, abundant at 1.6, are not the creatures one might expect. They are adapted to this dry, plant-choked world. Some are insectoid, with exoskeletons that mimic desiccated bark, scuttling through the dense undergrowth. Others are airborne, creatures of pollen and spore, drifting on the turbulent currents. There are also larger, more enigmatic forms, plant-animal hybrids that move like sentient vines, their bodies a seamless blend of fibrous muscle and chlorophyll.

The *weirdness* at 1.3 is subtle but pervasive. It’s in the unnatural silence of the immense dry landscape, punctuated by the unnatural roar of the winds. It’s in the bio-industrial machinery that seems to possess an alien sentience. It’s in the sheer scale of the plant-life, its absolute dominion.

And then there is the *sound*. At 2.8, it is deafening. It’s not the roar of wind through trees, or the crashing of waves. It’s a constant, grinding, scraping, thrumming cacophony. It’s the sound of immense, slow growth, the grinding of ancient fibrous matter, the rustle of countless dry leaves, the chitinous clicking of the fauna, and beneath it all, the low, resonant hum of the bio-industrial machinery, a constant, overwhelming sonic presence that vibrates through the very air and ground. It is the sound of a world perpetually in motion, a symphony of dry, relentless, industrial-biological creation.
