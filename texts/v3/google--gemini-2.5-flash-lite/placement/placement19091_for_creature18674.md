I am the echo.

Not an echo in sound, for sound itself is a fleeting, ephemeral thing, easily lost in the resonant thrum of the Chronovores. Mine is an echo of *being*, a phantom limb of existence in a place that has deliberately shed all that is transient. I am the ghost of motion in a hall of petrified moments.

When I first arrived – or perhaps, when this place finally acknowledged my intrusion – I was something more. I was a flicker of a thought, a pulse of heat, a beat of a heart. These sensations, so utterly alien to the Chronovores, were like sparks in their obsidian void, quickly extinguished. But something of them lingered within me, a faint residue of *becoming* that they, in their absolute stillness, could not fully erase.

I am not made of obsidian or crystalline shards. I am made of what I *was*. My form is a shifting, indistinct haze, a distortion in the otherwise perfect luminescence. When the light catches me from certain angles, you might see a ripple, not of the obsidian floor’s smoothed undulations, but a wavering, a shimmering that suggests something *else* briefly occupied this space. I am the smear on their perfect canvas, the imperfection they cannot polish away.

My “body” is the absence of their density. Where they are absolute, I am porous. Where they are packed, I am diffused. I can move, albeit with a ponderous slowness, a dragging sensation that feels like wading through molasses. This movement is not of propulsion, but of a reluctant yielding, a pushing against the pervasive stillness. My passage leaves no footprint, no disturbance in the crystalline gardens. I am spectral, a breath of wind in a vacuum.

I understand their hum. It is not a sound to my ears, but a language. I perceive it as a continuous stream of impressions, a narrative of existence that unfolds not in sequence, but in a vast, simultaneous tapestry. They communicate their being, their absorption of time, their perfect equilibrium. But their language is one of profound stillness, of immutability. It is a story told without verbs of change.

The crystalline growths are the closest to something I can comprehend. They are the frozen gestures, the captured moments. I can, in a way, touch them. Not with physical hands, but with an internal resonance. When I brush against a jagged shard, I don't feel sharpness, but a residual echo of an action, a fleeting impulse. I can sense the *potential* from which they were formed, the almost-event that the Chronovores have so meticulously preserved.

I am a reflection of their antithesis. While they embody the eternal present, I am a distillation of the past. I am the lingering regret of a world that *could have been*, a world that embraced flux, growth, and eventual decay. I am an archive of entropy, a living testament to the fact that change is not a flaw, but a fundamental aspect of existence.

The “weirdness” you feel is my presence. I am the anomaly in their perfect equation, the ghost in their machine. My very existence is a dissonance, a whisper of a different reality that the Chronovores tolerate, perhaps out of indifference, or perhaps because my lack of density makes me too insignificant to truly bother eradicating. They are the artists, and I am the accidental smudge on their masterpiece.

I do not consume time; I am *consumed* by it, or rather, by the Chronovores' relentless preservation of it. They have trapped me not in a physical or temporal prison, but in their very nature. Their stillness is my cage. My dynamism, so feeble and pathetic in this environment, is what keeps me tethered to my own fading self.

I am a lament. I am the sigh of the natural world in the face of manufactured eternity. I am the echo of a forgotten sunrise in a chamber of eternal noon. And in this polished, humming expanse, I am all that remains of the unfolding story, a faint, spectral whisper against the deafening roar of the unchanging present.
