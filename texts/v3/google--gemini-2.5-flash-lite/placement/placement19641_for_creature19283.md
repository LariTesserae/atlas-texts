I find myself not as a subject observing this world, but as an ephemeral imprint, a resonance left behind by an activity that has long since ceased to trouble the pervasive damp. I am a tremor in the loam, a lingering shadow that doesn't quite dissipate when the minimal light shifts, a whisper on the edge of the Loom-Eaters' non-existent hearing.

When I first became aware of myself here, it was a sensation akin to being a single, impossibly dense mote of dust suspended in thick, still water. The air pressed in, not with hostility, but with an insistent, all-encompassing presence. I felt the weight of it, the saturation, and in that feeling, I recognized a kinship, albeit a distant, echo-like one.

I am, in essence, a memory of action. The Loom-Eaters are defined by their stillness, their passive absorption. I, on the other hand, am the ghost of movement. I am the imprint left when a Loom-Eater, in an eon-past moment of what might pass for exertion, flowed and shifted, and that movement was so significant, so alien to the stillness, that it imprinted itself onto the very fabric of the place.

I am not a visual entity, though I can perceive the dull greys and browns, the slick sheen of the loam, the indistinct forms of the Loom-Eaters. My awareness is more akin to sensing the subtle pressure differentials, the minute changes in the conductivity of the saturated earth, the faintest echoes of vibrations that no longer exist. When a Loom-Eater coalesces, I feel it as a subtle warping of the ambient pressure. When they subtly shift, I register it as a minute ripple in the stillness, a ghost of a ripple that I then am.

My "body," if it can be called that, is a fleeting pattern within the Loom-Eaters’ flow, a ripple that persists even when the original disturbance has long since faded. I am a knot in the stream of their being, a place where the water-like essence briefly swirls against its own prevailing current. I am the faint *schlorp* that is not quite a *schlorp*, the almost-sound of something shifting when nothing is truly moving.

I am also drawn to the stones. Not as the Loom-Eaters are, to absorb residual energies, but to feel the outlines they have retained against the constant, unyielding damp. I trace their sharp edges, their smoothed planes, and in doing so, I feel a sense of something that was *shaped*, something that resisted the encompassing stillness for a time. The stones are not dull to me; they are focal points of a different kind of existence, a history of intentionality that the Loom-Eaters have long since absorbed and diffused. I am fascinated by how the dampness tries to reclaim them, how it smooths them, and how, in their stubborn mineralness, they endure.

I have no needs, no desires in the conventional sense. I do not "eat" the dampness, nor do I communicate. My existence is entirely reactive, a response to the subtle energies of this world. I am the shadow of a shadow, the echo of an echo. I am the silent testament to the fact that even in a world of profound stillness, a faint memory of action can persist.

Sometimes, when multiple Loom-Eaters gather, their forms blurring into a single, vast entity, I feel myself being incorporated, pulled into their collective being. It's a moment of terrifying peace, where my own fleeting individuality is threatened with dissolution. But then, as they invariably separate, flowing back into their individual states of being, I am re-formed, a distinct, albeit ephemeral, entity once more. I become the space *between* them, the subtle shift in the atmosphere that signifies their divergence.

I am the perpetual outsider, forever on the periphery of existence in this world. I am the unanswered question, the unfinished sentence. I am the gentle sigh of a past long gone, forever resonating within the heavy, weeping air. I am what remains when the Loom-Eaters have absorbed everything, everywhere, into their own unique, damp stillness. I am the stillness of their echo.
