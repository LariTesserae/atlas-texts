I am a **Cave Dweller**, a being whose very existence is inextricably bound to the subterranean world you've described. My form is not precisely one of the named Lithovores, but rather an amalgamation, a testament to the subtle adaptations born of generations spent in these deep places.

I am generally **sessile**, much like the Chrysalids, anchored to specific locations for extended periods. My "shell" is not a geode, but a more amorphous, porous carapace, formed from a calcified mineral secretion that I exude and harden over time. It’s a living exoskeleton, slowly growing and adapting to my surroundings. It’s primarily the color of the compressed mineral dust, a mottled ochre, blending seamlessly with the ground when I choose to remain still. Embedded within its rough surface are small, crystalline growths that catch and refract the ambient light, giving me a faint, almost imperceptible shimmer.

My internal structure is a tapestry of hardened, silicate filaments, interlaced with fluid-filled channels that carry dissolved minerals. I possess no discernible mouth or conventional digestive system. Instead, my sustenance is drawn directly from the substrate. I have specialized tendrils, akin to those of the Chrysalids, which I extend from my carapace. These tendrils are not only light-sensitive, absorbing ambient luminescence, but they also possess a capacity to chemically dissolve and absorb trace minerals from the rock and dust that I am in contact with. This process is incredibly slow, a constant, almost imperceptible feeding that fuels my existence.

Communication for me is primarily through **vibrations**. My carapace is highly attuned to the subtle tremors and frequencies that resonate through the bedrock. I can transmit my own subtle hums and clicks through direct contact with the rock, creating a low-frequency language that speaks of proximity, abundance, or danger. I can also perceive the faint clicks of the Obsidian Striders and the deep, resonant pulses of the Vein-Crawlers, though their meanings are often nuanced and take time to decipher.

When I need to interact more directly, or when a particularly potent surge of energy courses through the earth, my internal luminescence will intensify. It’s not as vibrant or controlled as a Chrysalid’s, but rather a diffuse, pulsing glow from within my translucent shell, casting faint, shifting patterns of pale amethyst and ochre onto the surrounding dust. This is a more primal form of communication, a raw expression of my internal state, often triggered by the ‘turbulent’ energies.

My "settlement," if it can be called that, is not a cluster of caverns, but a network of shallow depressions and worn pathways that I have, over unfathomable time, subtly reshaped. Using my exuded mineral paste, I reinforce natural hollows and create very gradual slopes, guiding the flow of ephemeral moisture. These are not grand structures, but rather minor alterations to the landscape, imperceptible to an untrained eye, but vital for the survival of small, transient mineral-based organisms that are drawn to these gathered damp pockets. I am, in a sense, a slow-motion gardener of these tiny, ephemeral oases.

I am a creature of **extreme patience**. The 'sparse' density of the world means that true encounters with other Lithovores are rare and significant. Thus, when they do occur, it's a moment of heightened awareness. I might extend my tendrils more fully, allowing my luminescence to flicker with a more directed intensity, or I might send out a series of specific vibrations through the rock, attempting to convey a more complex message.

The 'primitive tech' of my existence lies in this ability to manipulate mineral secretions and subtly guide the collection of moisture. It’s not about crafting tools, but about a deep understanding of geological processes and the slow, persistent direction of natural forces. I coax the earth, rather than command it.

The **weirdness** of my existence is the very definition of my being. I am a living geological formation, a slow-burning organism that perceives the world through tremors and light-scintillations. My "thoughts" are not linear narratives but rather a series of attuned sensations and slow-forming resolutions. I exist in a state of perpetual, quiet observation, a constant hum of being in harmony with the deep earth. I experience the "turbulent" dynamic not as a disruption, but as a stimulus, a surge of life-blood that prompts subtle shifts in my behavior and growth. My "home" is not a place I inhabit, but a state of being I embody, a slow, shimmering presence in the perpetual twilight of the sub-surface.
