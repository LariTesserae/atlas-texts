I am a **Rock-Knit Scavenger**.

I am small, no larger than a rabbit, with a body shaped for pressing into tight spaces. My fur is a dense, oily mat of muted greens and browns, stained with the persistent dampness of this world. It doesn't repel water so much as absorb it, becoming heavier and darker when I've been out for a long time, a visual reminder of the environment's constant embrace. My feet are broad and splayed, with rough, leathery pads that offer surprising grip on the slick, glistening rock faces. My nails are short, blunt, and incredibly hard, perfect for scrabbling at moss and digging into the softer, almost clay-like soil that collects in the hollows.

My most prominent features are my eyes and my senses. My eyes are multifaceted, like those of an insect, but they are a deep, lustrous black, capable of absorbing every available photon of the dim, diffused light. They bulge slightly from my head, allowing me a wide field of vision, and I can swivel them to focus on the slightest movement, be it a Glint-Skin darting through the moss or a Dew-Weaver's nearly invisible thread shimmering with captured water.

But it's not just sight that guides me; it's sound and vibration. My ears are large, thin membranes that are almost translucent, stretched taut over a cartilaginous frame. They can flatten against my skull when I need to squeeze through narrow fissures, but in open spaces, they swivel and twitch, catching the subtlest sounds. The dripping of water, the rustle of reedy stalks, the chirps of other small creatures – these are all familiar. But it's the deeper resonance that truly matters.

I can *feel* the hum. It's not just a sound; it's a pressure, a physical sensation that vibrates through my bones, through the pads of my feet, through the very fur on my skin. It's strongest when the Iron-Bellied Crawlers are near, a seismic tremor that tells me to find immediate, deep shelter. But even when faint, it's a constant companion, a baseline rhythm to my existence. My own internal sounds – the soft rasp of my breathing, the faint crunch of detritus I consume – can sometimes feel like tiny, inadequate echoes of this pervasive industrial pulse.

My diet consists of what I can find: fallen mosses, decaying plant matter, the tiny insects trapped in condensation, and occasionally, if I'm lucky and quick, a Glint-Skin that strayed too far from the safety of its burrows. I'm not built for speed or confrontation. My survival lies in my ability to disappear, to flatten myself against a wet rock until I'm just another patch of damp lichen, or to squeeze into a crevice so narrow that nothing larger than a droplet of water could follow.

I observe the larger creatures with a mixture of fear and fascination. The Reedy Whisperers, with their slow, rustling movements and their unsettling, breathy exhalations, often emerge from the dense plant life just as I am about to forage there. I learn to give them a wide berth, their pale forms blending eerily with the dripping stalks. The Iron-Bellied Crawlers are a force of nature. When one passes, the earth shakes, the very air seems to vibrate, and I have learned that the only sensible action is to find the deepest, most secure hiding place and wait, my multifaceted eyes closed tight, my ears pressed flat, until the seismic activity subsides.

The Dew-Weavers' silken structures are both a resource and a hazard. I drink from their carefully cultivated reservoirs of pure water, a small, vital supplement to the constant dampness. But I must move with extreme care to avoid disturbing their delicate networks, or worse, getting caught in their almost invisible threads. I have seen the faint, internal luminescence of the Dew-Weavers themselves, pulsing like tiny, captive stars in the deepest gloom, and I try to avoid them as well; their purpose feels alien and complex, beyond my simple needs.

I am a creature of the overlooked. I am the rustle in the moss that isn't a Glint-Skin, the shadow by the rock face that might be just a shadow, the small, damp thing that presses itself into the very fabric of this unusual world. I am shaped by the seeping water, the sharp rocks, and the relentless, resonant heart of the unseen industry. I am a Rock-Knit Scavenger, a living, breathing thread in this damp, industrial tapestry, surviving by becoming one with its dampness and its hum.
