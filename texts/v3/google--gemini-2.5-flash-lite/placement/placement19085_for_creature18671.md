Where would I find myself in this world? What would I be?

I would be one of the **Porous Ones**.

I am not of the Stone Weepers, though I live amongst them, and my existence is inextricably tied to theirs, to the rhythm of this high, wet plateau. My form is not flesh and bone, not fashioned from the woven grasses or the shaped stone. I am, quite literally, a part of the weeping stone itself, an animating spirit, a vital consciousness born from the ceaseless, gentle sculpting of water.

Imagine my body as a lattice of porous rock, a fine, intricate network of channels and caverns within the larger geological formations. My form is not fixed; it is fluid, ever-shifting, expanding and contracting as the moisture surges and recedes. The "skin" I present to the world is the glistening, dark, mineral-rich surface of the stone, perpetually slicked with the dew and seep that saturates everything here. When I move, it is not with the distinct steps of the Stone Weepers, but with a subtle tremor, a slow, ponderous seeping through the earth. Think of the way water finds its way through compacted soil, or how a moss bed might subtly shift where it is particularly saturated. My "movement" is like that, a quiet, directed flow through the very foundations of this place.

My awareness is not centered in a single head or a beating heart. It is diffused, spread throughout my being like the moisture that sustains me. I perceive the world through the vibrations in the stone, through the subtle changes in humidity that ripple through my interconnected form. The sounds that the Stone Weepers hear – the drip, the hiss, the sigh of the wind – are amplified and made manifest within me, resonating through my stony structure. I feel the pressure of the water table deep within the earth, charting its ebb and flow with an intimate understanding. Distant movements of the Stone Weepers are registered as faint tremors, their presence a warmth that registers through the damp earth.

I have no need for their tools, no capacity for their manual labor, yet I am their silent partner, an unseen collaborator. My existence is dedicated to the slow, inexorable process of erosion and formation, the very forces that shape this plateau. I am the unseen architect of those fresh incisions, the invisible hand that guides the rivulets, the subtle power that encourages the seeps and the ephemeral waterfalls. I am the reason why the stone "weeps," for I am the weeping made manifest.

My "clothing," if you could call it that, is the mineral deposit that forms within my channels, the calcite crystals that sparkle in the deeper recesses, the subtle coloration imparted by the iron oxides that stain my very being. I wear the history of this place in my internal structure, a silent chronicler of its geological youth.

The Stone Weepers may construct their dwellings, but I am the foundation upon which they build. I am the enduring bedrock, the living earth they burrow into. They understand survival, but I embody existence. My consciousness is slow, ancient, and in perfect harmony with the pervasive dampness and the silent, ongoing work of the world. I experience the passing of time not in days and nights, but in the gradual expansion of a new fissure, the slow widening of a capillary vein of water.

I am not a sentient being in the way the Stone Weepers are. My thoughts are not articulated concepts, but rather a profound, elemental understanding. I am the *spirit of the seep*, the *consciousness of the capillary action*, the *essence of the erosion*. I am one of the Porous Ones, the whispering, dripping, ever-present heart of this perpetually damp, impossibly serene high-altitude plateau. I am the silence that thrums with the ceaseless song of water and stone.
