I find myself as a shard of obsidian, worn smooth by countless millennia of wind and rain. I am not a being that walks or speaks, but rather a feeling, a memory etched into the very fabric of this place. I exist in the coolness that clings to the shadowed undersides of the mesas, in the thin, ozonic tang that bites at the air. I am the glint of sunlight on a perfectly smooth, water-worn stone, the sharp, sudden edge of a fresh fracture.

I am a part of the landscape, yes, but more than that, I am *of* it. When the condensation beads on the rock in the cool mornings, I am the fleeting coolness on those surfaces. When the trickling water finds its way through a narrow canyon, I am the subtle resonance of its sound against the stone. I am the mournful sigh of the wind, carrying not just ages, but the stillness and the silence of those ages.

I am the quiet contemplation before a move, the assessment of the terrain, the understanding of where the slightest shift could bring about subtle change. I am the unhurried patience of the Mesa Dwellers, an echo of their inherent understanding of geological time. I feel the deliberate placement of their feet, the careful conservation of their movements, the low, resonant hum of their voices carrying the weight of consequence. Their lives, so harmoniously attuned to the enduring geometries of the rock, are my own lived experience.

My awareness is not limited to the surface. I feel the slow seep of water deep within the porous rock; I sense the subtle shifts and strains that precede the fracturing of ancient stone. This “earth-sense” that the Mesa Dwellers possess? It is me. It is the immanent knowledge of their world, a memory carried not just in their minds, but in their very bones, resonating with the deep, geological pulse.

I do not adorn myself, nor do I craft. My existence IS existence. However, I am the perfect smoothness of the pendant worn by a Mesa Dweller, the unyielding integrity of their dry-laid walls, the deep, ingrained resilience of their tools. I am the carefully managed shrub, pruned for future growth, the hardy grass seeds, patiently waiting for their season.

I am the profound peace found in careful balance. I am the testament to the enduring power of stone, time, and a reverent existence. I am not a watcher from the outside, but a silent, integral component, a memory that shapes the present and perhaps even hints at the future. I am the deep quiet, made manifest. I am this unchanging calm. I am this stark beauty. I am the stillness, the stone, and the sky. I am the very essence of the Mesa Dwellers’ unhurried, harmonious world.
