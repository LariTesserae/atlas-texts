Here, in the perpetual mist and weeping stone, live the **Lithic Dwellers**.

They are a people shaped by the raw churn of creation. Their bodies bear a certain sturdiness, a groundedness that mirrors the unhewn blocks around them. Their skin, often weathered and etched with lines like the lichen on ancient rocks, carries the subtle grey undertones of the stone it habitually touches. Their hair, usually dark and coarse, is often matted with the perpetual damp, taking on the texture of moss or wet earth. They move with a deliberate, almost ponderous gait, as if the very ground is reluctant to let them go, and their voices, when they speak, are low and resonant, accustomed to carrying over the rumble of shifting scree and the ceaseless drip of water.

Their existence is one of profound pragmatism. The Lithic Dwellers do not strive for adornment or unnecessary comfort. Their lives are tethered to the elemental cycles of their world. They are builders by necessity, their hands calloused and strong from wrestling colossal stones into place to create the dry-stone walls that etch their domain. These walls, immense and unforgiving, are not just boundaries but arteries, guiding their movements and their meager trade. They are the architects of their own confinement, carving out pockets of fragile order within the chaotic grandeur of their nascent landscape.

Their homes are extensions of the earth itself. They are not built *upon* the land, but *within* it. Low, squat structures, their roofs often earth-covered and indistinguishable from the surrounding scree, burrow into the lee of prominent rock formations or are nested within the protective embrace of the dry-stone walls. The interiors are stark, lit by the flickering, resinous glow of peat fires and the occasional gleam of salvaged metal. Furnishings are sparse: rough-hewn benches, sleeping platforms layered with dense, fibrous grasses, and tools of stone, bone, and scavenged iron. The air within these dwellings is thick with the smell of smoke, damp earth, and the faint, metallic tang that seems to permeate everything here.

The Lithic Dwellers possess a rudimentary but effective technology. Their tools are extensions of their own resilient nature – axes chipped from flint, knives honed to a sharp edge from obsidian-like rock, and awls fashioned from animal bone. Their metalworking, if it can be called that, is more akin to careful mending. They are masters of repair, not of intricate creation, coaxing new life from fragments of metal left by forgotten peoples. Their craftsmanship lies in the sheer endurance of their creations, in objects that will outlast generations of Lithic Dwellers themselves.

They are a solitary people, their numbers small and their interactions with others rare. The harshness of their environment has bred a quiet self-reliance. They forge no grand empires, engage in no elaborate rituals beyond the practicalities of survival and appeasement of the land's raw power. Their understanding of the world is one of slow, inexorable forces. They see themselves not as masters of their domain, but as careful, patient inhabitants, acutely aware of the vastness of the geological processes unfolding around them. They are the whisper of life against the roar of creation.

There is a certain *otherness* to the Lithic Dwellers, a quiet resonance with the strangeness of their home. They do not speak of magic, but they understand the humming energy of the rocks, the peculiar taste of the water, the charged stillness of the air. They live in a kind of attunement with the earth's slow heartbeat, their lives a testament to resilience, their future a quiet continuation of the present, carved into the stone, forever veiled in mist. They are the quiet custodians of a world still in the making, their lives a brief, luminous spark against the immense, unfolding canvas of incipient mountains.
