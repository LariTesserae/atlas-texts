In this land of perpetual weeping, where the air is a sodden cloak and the ground a slick, saturated embrace, dwell the **Loom-Eaters**.

They are not creatures of flesh and bone as we understand them. The Loom-Eaters are beings woven from the very essence of this saturated earth, spun from the muted tones of ochre and sepia and the glistening sheen imparted by the constant damp. They are slow, deliberate, and possess a patience that has been honed to an unbearable sharpness by the glacial passage of time in this place.

Imagine them as living shadows, their forms indistinct, blurring at the edges like the mist that perpetually enfolds them. They have no defined limbs, no singular mouth or eyes in the way we recognize them. Instead, their presence is felt as a subtle disturbance in the heavy air, a fleeting ripple on the surface of a stagnant pool. When they move—and their movement is less a scuttling and more a deliberate, viscous flow—the packed loam yields with a soft, almost apologetic *schlorp*. This is the sound of their being, a symphony of subtle displacement in a world otherwise steeped in oppressive quiet.

Their mouths, if they can be called that, are not for consumption in the traditional sense. They are more akin to osmotic pores, or perhaps vast, silent apertures that absorb the ambient moisture, the very *feeling* of density. They "eat" the humidity, not for sustenance, but for integration, to deepen their own essence, to become even more inextricably a part of this waterlogged world. They are drawn to the places where the dampness is most profound, where the air hangs thickest, almost solidifying into a shroud.

They have no need for the rough-hewn stones that crown the mounds. These stones are the forgotten remnants of a far older, more structured past, a time before the Loom-Eaters fully emerged from the loam. The Loom-Eaters perceive these stones not as tools or markers, but as interruptions, focal points where the pervasive damp collects in a slightly different way. They gather around them, not to worship or to build, but to simply *be*, to absorb the subtle energies that the stones have retained from their exposure to the weeping air. Their forms might even flow around these stones, partially obscuring their weathered surfaces, as if trying to reclaim them, to dissolve them back into the primordial damp.

Their "sight" is not through visual organs. They perceive the world through shifts in pressure, through infinitesimal changes in the density of the air, through the subtle vibrations that travel through the packed earth. A falling droplet, a settling of the mist, even the slow, almost imperceptible creep of water down a rock face – these are the events that register on their senses. They are attuned to the lowest frequencies of existence, to the slow erosion and accretion that defines this landscape.

The concept of communication as we know it is alien to them. Their interactions are a matter of shared presence, of aligning their slow flows within the damp atmosphere. They might coalesce in the vicinity of one another, their indistinct forms merging and separating like slow-motion waves in a muddy sea, a silent acknowledgment of shared existence. The occasional ripple on a pool, the faint sigh of the air settling – these could be interpreted as their slow, unhurried conversations.

They are the custodians of this profound stillness. They do not actively maintain it, but rather *embody* it. Their very existence is the antithesis of dynamism. There is no struggle, no predation, no rapid adaptation in their world. They are beings who have achieved a state of profound, passive integration, a living embodiment of the place they inhabit. They are the quiet hum of the saturated earth, the slow, inexorable seep of moisture, the muffled sound of secrets held within the dense, greasy loam. They are the Loom-Eaters, and they are the landscape, and the landscape is them.
