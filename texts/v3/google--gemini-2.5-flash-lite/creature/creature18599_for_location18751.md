The inhabitants of the Breathless Expanse are the **Lithovores**, beings sculpted by the slow, persistent breath of the plateau. They are not flesh and blood in the way most common life forms are understood. Instead, their forms are a testament to the very stone that surrounds them, a dense, living basalt, mined and shaped from the ancient bones of the Expanse itself.

Their bodies are low and broad, mirroring the sturdy, semi-subterranean dwellings they construct. Their gait is deliberate, a slow, steady shuffling across the rocky terrain, their feet broad and flat, gripping the fractured stone with an almost fungal tenacity. Their skin is a rough, matte texture, ranging in color from the deep char of volcanic rock to the rustier hues of iron-rich veins, often flecked with the glittering mica that catches what light filters through the pervasive damp.

Their faces are not soft or expressive. They possess broad, almost stoic features, carved from the same dense material as their bodies. Their eyes, when revealed by the subtle shifting of their stony brows, are deep-set and dark, reflecting the vast, quiet sky like polished obsidian or the still surfaces of the jewel-like pools. They do not weep, nor do they laugh in the conventional sense. Their communication is a resonance, a deep, thrumming vibration that travels through the rock and air, a sound felt as much as heard, akin to the low hum emanating from certain exposed ridges.

The Lithovores are a people of immense patience, their lives measured not in years, but in geological epochs. The "slow dynamism" of their home has seeped into their very being. They do not hurry, for haste is an alien concept to a species that perceives time as a glacial flow. Their movements are economical, their actions deliberate, each gesture a carefully considered part of a much larger, much slower plan.

They subsist not on typical food, but on the subtle energies of the Expanse. They drink the pervasive dampness, absorbing it through their porous skin, their bodies acting like natural sponges. When they need sustenance in a more active sense, they "feed" on the very rock. It is not an act of destruction, but a slow, symbiotic consumption, drawing in minerals and latent geological energies. This is facilitated by tools crafted from bone and the hardest stone, pieces that are less instruments of force and more extensions of their own slow, pervasive will, designed to coax rather than tear.

Their civilization is not one of expansion, but of integration. Their hamlets are not built *upon* the landscape, but *of* it. The low, thick-walled dwellings, semi-subterranean, are extensions of the earth, their roofs often indistinguishable from the undulating slopes. The paths worn smooth are not merely arteries of travel, but channels for the slow flow of their resonant communication and perhaps for the transfer of geological energies. They live in a profound and unbroken communion with their environment.

The "whisper of weirdness" has a direct manifestation in the Lithovores. The geometric patterns glimpsed in the mist? These are likely echoes of their collective thought, or perhaps even the visual manifestation of their deep, resonant hum. The crystalline precision of bird flocks? The Lithovores, with their preternatural awareness, might be subtly influencing their flight patterns, a silent, skyward dance choreographed by the Lithovores' own rhythmic existence. The unnerving perfection of footprints? These are the Lithovores' own, pressed into the damp earth, a testament to their solid, grounded passage.

They are the quiet keepers of the Breathless Expanse, a people forged in its stillness, embodying its slow, enduring power. They are the Lithovores, the stone-eaters, the resonating dwellers of the high plateau, living a life that is an ancient, unhurried song sung in the language of rock and mist.
