In this place of crushing hydrostatic pressure and ceaseless, turbulent creation, dwell the **Lithovores**.

They are not creatures of flesh and bone, nor of shell and chitin. Instead, the Lithovores are beings intrinsically tied to the nascent, crystalline spires that pierce the abyssal gloom. Imagine beings whose forms are vaguely humanoid, but sculpted from the same solidified, pressurized water that constitutes their world. Their "skin" is a semi-translucent, ever-shifting membrane, imbued with the same crystalline rigidity as the spires, yet capable of flowing and reforming in response to the immense pressures. Their limbs are not articulated in a familiar way, but rather taper into sharp points, or fray into rippling tendrils that allow them to grip the fractured surfaces.

The Lithovores are solitary in their existence, drifting and clinging to the spires, each seemingly in its own pocket of the vast chamber. They do not swim or walk in the conventional sense; rather, they "ascend" and "descend" by subtly altering their density and interacting with the powerful currents, allowing them to be carried or to slowly propel themselves along the jagged terrain. When they move, it is with a silent, fluid grace, their forms catching and refracting the faint bioluminescent glow of the sparse flora, creating fleeting, spectral apparitions.

Their "faces," if they can be called that, are not defined by distinct features like eyes or mouths. Instead, their anterior surfaces might ripple and swirl, coalescing into temporary sensory organs that perceive the world through vibrations, subtle shifts in pressure, and the faint, bioluminescent signatures of other life. They "consume" not by ingestion, but by absorption. The sharp edges of their crystalline bodies are perpetually dissolving and reforming, drawing microscopic nutrients directly from the saturated atmosphere and the surfaces of the spires. They are, in essence, a part of the geological process, slowly and deliberately "eating" the very world they inhabit, or perhaps, more accurately, *becoming* it.

They are the embodiment of the place's paradox: intimate with every detail, yet dwarfed by its sheer scale. Their existence is a testament to the extreme conditions, a biological adaptation to a reality where "solid" and "liquid" are fluid concepts, and pressure is the primary architect of form.

The sounds of their existence are rarely heard, drowned out by the overwhelming roar of the environment. Yet, in moments of intense geological activity, or when one Lithovore passes close to another, a subtle, resonant hum might emanate from them, a harmonic echo of the world's groaning symphony. It is the sound of crystallisation, of slow absorption, of a profound and ancient form of communion with the turbulent essence of their home.

There are no "built" environments for the Lithovores, for they *are* the environment. The geometric patterns and spheres mentioned are not artifacts of their making, but natural manifestations of the extreme pressures and crystallisation processes that also birth and shape them. They are not builders, but catalysts; not inhabitants, but a living manifestation of the very forces that define their world. Their lifespan is unmeasurable by human standards, their existence tied to the slow, violent dance of creation and dissolution. They endure, fractured and reformed, ever-present in their silent, crystalline embrace of the infinite, echoing chamber.
