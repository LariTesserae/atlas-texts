They are the **Xylosians**, a people born from the humid embrace of their world. Their skin, where it is visible beneath the dense, water-repellent foliage they adorn themselves with, tends towards a deep, mossy green or the mottled grey of wet obsidian, allowing them to blend seamlessly with their environment. Their eyes, large and dark, are adapted to the perpetual twilight of the lower canyons and the diffused light of the upper reaches, capable of discerning even the faintest glimmer of luminescence.

Their **form** is an echo of the terrain itself – adapted to extreme elevation and precarious footing. Many Xylosians possess a secondary, prehensile limb, a third arm or a flattened, gripping tail, that allows them unparalleled agility on the sheer cliffs and clinging vines. Their feet are broad and calloused, with splayed digits that grip stone like an anchor. There is a remarkable fluidity in their movements, a slow, deliberate grace that belies their raw physical strength, necessary for navigating the dense, often vertical, landscape.

Their **culture** is inextricably linked to the water that saturates their world. They are masters of its flow, not through grand engineering, but through an innate understanding of the weeping rock and subterranean channels. Their habitations are not built upon the land, but *within* it, carved into the porous obsidian, their entrances often concealed by cascades of iridescent moss or strategically grown vines. These dwellings are kept perpetually cool and humid, reflecting the ambient conditions outside, and are often imbued with the faint, internal light from the rock itself, casting a soft, pulsating glow.

The **tech beyond modern** is not a tool they wield, but a symbiotic relationship. They have learned to attune themselves to the subtle energies of the luminous veins in the rock, using them as a form of low-level communication, a shared resonance that allows for a profound, almost telepathic, connection across distances. This connection extends to the flora; they coax, rather than command, the plants, encouraging their growth for shelter, sustenance, and the creation of intricate, living tools. The coordinated growth patterns they observe in a specific type of vine are, in fact, a form of direct, if slow, interaction with their kin.

They are **not boisterous**; the constant hum of their world provides a natural buffer to overt displays of sound. Their communication is a layered affair, a blend of soft, melodic vocalisations, subtle shifts in skin iridescence (a language learned from the plants themselves), and the almost imperceptible pulses of the rock veins. Loudness, in their world, is often a sign of distress or imminent danger.

The **Xylosians are observers**, deeply attuned to the slow, patient evolution of their planet. They do not seek to conquer or control, but to exist in harmony with the immense forces that shaped their home. Change comes not through sudden revolution, but through gradual adaptation and a deep, generational understanding. They are the keepers of the ancient secrets of Xylos, living embodiments of its humid, verdant, and profoundly alien spirit, their lives measured not in days, but in the slow creep of moss across stone and the patient, luminous pulse of the world itself.
