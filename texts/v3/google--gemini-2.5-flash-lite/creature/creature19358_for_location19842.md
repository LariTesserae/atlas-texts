The denizens of the Sky-Ocean are beings of profound adaptation, shaped by a world that is as much fluid as it is structure. They are the living currents within the built cosmos, the vibrant pulse in the perpetual twilight.

Imagine first **The Drifters**. They are the most ubiquitous, a testament to the world's vastness and the sparse distribution of its true substance. Picture amorphous forms, akin to immense, gossamer jellyfish, their bodies composed of complex, semi-permeable membranes that subtly shift in opalescence. These membranes are not merely protective layers, but are in constant communion with the surrounding water, absorbing nutrients and expelling waste with an unconscious grace. Their internal structures are delicate, filamentary networks of bioluminescent nodes, pulsing with a cool, internal light that ranges from soft blues to vibrant greens. This light serves not only as communication – a languid flickering to signal presence or mood – but also as a lure, attracting smaller organisms that become sustenance. They move with an unhurried, almost meditative drift, propelled by minute adjustments of their internal density and subtle undulations of their bell-like forms. They are the living clouds of the Sky-Ocean, vast and gentle, existing in a state of perpetual, passive exchange with their environment.

Then there are **The Sculptors**. While the descriptions suggest a built world, there are no builders in the traditional sense encountered. This is where the "tech level zero" comes into play. The Sculptors are not architects with tools; they are the very agents of construction and deconstruction. They are beings that embody the world's mineral essence, their forms often resembling intricate crystalline growths that have achieved sentience. Some possess massive, stony bodies, slow-moving behemoths that are themselves like mobile geological formations, their surfaces encrusted with mineral deposits and smaller, symbiotic life forms. They operate on principles of molecular manipulation, their very presence causing the surrounding water's density to alter, its minerals to precipitate or dissolve, thus shaping the world's architecture over eons. Their erosion rate of 2.8/3 is a testament to their deliberate, yet slow, impact. They might not build with hammers and chisels, but with focused currents, gravitational shifts within their own being, and directed biological secretions that act as catalysts for mineral formation. Their actions are monumental, slow-burn artistry that defines the very landscape.

The **Current Riders** are the masters of its dynamism, the embodiment of the 3.0/3 chaos. They are sleek, serpentine creatures, their bodies impossibly streamlined, possessing a resilience that allows them to navigate and even harness the raging torrents of the Sky-Ocean. Their scales are not hard and fixed, but are rather a mosaic of overlapping, incredibly tough, yet flexible bio-armor plates that shift and ripple with their movements. Bioluminescence here is not soft and inviting, but sharp and electric, a series of strobing, almost aggressive bursts of light that seem to cut through the gloom, signaling intent and power. They possess multiple pairs of highly articulated fins, capable of both rapid propulsion through the densest currents and intricate maneuvering in constricted spaces. Their "weapons" are not claws or teeth in the conventional sense, but focused sonic pulses that can disorient prey or even momentarily destabilize the very structures they inhabit, creating temporary pockets of lesser density they exploit for speed. They are the apex predators, the dynamic force that ensures no part of the Sky-Ocean ever truly settles.

The **Whisperers of the Deep** are the true enigmas, the source of the "weirdness" factor. They exist in the rarer, thicker abysses, where the light is even more attenuated and the cold more profound. Their forms are often described as fleeting, paradoxical. Some observers speak of ephemeral, glowing ribbons that weave through the water, their bioluminescence a cool, intelligent shimmer that seems to absorb the surrounding light rather than emit it. Others describe them as being more akin to clusters of intelligent sound, their very existence perceived more through resonance and vibrational patterns than visual cues. It's whispered that the Whisperers communicate not through light or sound, but through direct psychic entanglement, their "weirdness" stemming from a consciousness that operates on principles utterly alien to linear perception. They might possess sensory organs that detect gravitational anomalies, shifts in dimensional fabric, or the very "intent" of the water itself. Their bioluminescence, if it can be called that, might be a byproduct of their deep-sea communion with the world's fundamental forces.

Finally, there are the **Symbiotic Clusters**, too numerous and varied to be a single species, but too integral a part of the ecosystem to ignore. These are the smaller organisms, the "fauna that flit and drift." They exist in a million forms, from microscopic bioluminescent plankton that coat the structures like living frost, to intelligent, pack-hunting schools of metallic fish that shimmer like liquid mercury. Many of these smaller creatures are inextricably linked to the larger beings. The Drifters might host colonies of nutrient-processing lichens on their inner membranes. The Sculptors may be covered in specialized mineral-extracting barnacles. The Current Riders might have their streamlined bodies adorned with bio-luminescent algae that provide camouflage or warning signals. These clusters are the mundane wonders, the constant hum of biological activity that underpins the grandeur and the chaos of the Sky-Ocean. Their sheer variety and the intricate ways they are interwoven with the larger life forms contribute significantly to the unsettling, yet breathtaking, alienness of this submerged world.
