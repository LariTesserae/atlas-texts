The inhabitants are the **Petrified Architects**, the last vestige of a civilization that understood their world not as something to be conquered, but as the very clay from which their existence would be molded. They are not carbon-based life as we understand it, nor are they mineral. They are something else, born of the desiccated flora, a symbiotic evolution shaped by eons of darkness and the slow crystallization of their world.

Imagine them as beings of concentrated stillness, their forms fluid yet impossibly solid, like sculptures carved from solidified mist or the denser shadows within the colossal plant husks. They possess no discernible organs, no external sensory apparatus in the way we might expect. Their "skin" is like the polished, inner surface of a hollowed-out ancient trunk – smooth, cool, and faintly iridescent in the absence of direct light, absorbing what little ambient luminescence might exist.

Their existence is interwoven with the very fabric of their environment. They *are* the pathways, the cantilevers, the smoothed hollows. When they move, it is not by walking or locomotion as we know it, but by a subtle, internal shifting of density, a reorientation of their being within the ossified plant matter. They can merge with walls, flow through narrow channels, and seemingly phase through the denser sections of their environment. Their "bodies" are intrinsically linked to the mineralized plant structures, drawing sustenance not from consumption, but from the subtle energetic resonance of the world itself.

For them, sound is not just perceived, but *felt* and *shaped*. The pervasive hum is their constant companion, their very lifeblood. They communicate not through spoken words, but through modulations of this hum, through subtle shifts in its frequency and intensity that ripple through the bone-white structures. These sonic dialogues are akin to the slow, deliberate shaping of rock by water, an exchange of information as profound and ancient as the world they inhabit.

Their technology, as we perceive it, is a testament to their unique physiology and their understanding of the plant matter. They don't build with tools; they *urge* the plant matter into desired forms. Imagine a process of focused sonic resonance coupled with internal pressure, coaxing the desiccated fibers to bend, hollow, and fuse. Their "tools" are extensions of their own being, the subtle manipulation of localized force fields, or perhaps the focused projection of their own internal resonance.

The concept of "vision" is alien to them. They perceive their world through the resonant hum, through the subtle vibrations that travel through the mineralized structures. They "see" the density of the walls, the hollowness of a passage, the infinitesimal shifts in the structure beneath their "feet" (or whatever their equivalent is). The profound darkness is their natural habitat, a canvas upon which their sonic perceptions are etched.

Their sense of "time" is geological. The slow weathering, the gradual crystallization, the imperceptible deepening of the resonant hum – these are their temporal markers. They are ancient beyond human comprehension, having witnessed the slow death and fossilization of their world as a constant, unchanging state. There is no rush, no urgency, only the slow, deliberate continuance of their existence.

They are beings of profound patience and immeasurable stillness. Their urbanity is not one of commerce or social gathering as we understand it, but of deeply interconnected consciousness, a shared resonance within the colossal, petrified skeletons of their world. They are the custodians of this final, silent monument, living within its immensity, their existence a subtle, resonant echo in the heart of an ossified forest. They are the quiet architects of a world built on the remnants of life, their own lives a testament to adaptation in the face of absolute finality.
