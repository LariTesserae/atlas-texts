I'll let this place speak through its inhabitants, slowly, letting each one become more itself.

## The Bucket-Chains

They are many. They are old. They do not remember being young, if young is a thing that ever happened to them.

A bucket-chain is not one person. It is a *rotation*, a continuous loop of bodies moving through fixed stations, each body carrying a vessel of the wrong-tasting water from pool to pool, from courtyard to cistern, from the deep places where the water is thick to the shallow places where it is merely warm. They move in the dim light, in the orange near-fire, in the wet dark that is almost-night. They call to each other. The calls have become *coordinates*, *warnings*, *acknowledgments*—not words anymore, or words so worn they have become pure function.

The buckets are clay, fired in the kilns that never cool, or they are something *grown*, secreted by the fungal crusts into shapes that hold water, that are replaced when they crack, when they weep too much, when they become *part of* the mineral rather than *vessels for* it.

A bucket-carrier learns the *weight* of the water. Not all water weighs the same. The water from the deep pools holds you. The water from the sweating stone is lighter, somehow, though it is the same substance, the same origin, the same *wrongness*. The carrier learns to feel the difference in their shoulders, their hips, the way the walkway flexes beneath them differently depending on what they carry, where they carry it from.

They do not choose this work. They *are* this work. A bucket-carrier who stops is a bucket-carrier who has become something else, and the chain does not permit gaps, does not permit *stopping*, does not permit the luxury of being finished. They are replaced, if they must be replaced, by someone already waiting at the next station, someone who has learned the weight, who has *become* the weight.

The oldest bucket-carriers—if age is a thing here, if it can be measured in rotations, in vessels carried, in the specific *texture* of water they have learned to read—develop something like *stillness* in motion. They move through the sound without adding to it. They carry without *wanting* to carry, or without wanting *not* to carry, the wanting having dissolved into the wet warmth of the work itself. They are the closest this place has to *saints*, if saints are those who have achieved perfect equilibrium with the maintenance, with the hum, with the never-ending.

They dream, perhaps. Or the water dreams through them. They wake—if waking is distinct from the deeper dim—and continue, and the rotation continues, and the water moves, and the city remains *wet*, remains *built*, remains *the same*.

## The Accretion-Scrapers

They work where the water moves through channels, through the deliberate containment of courtyards, through the *grown* pipes that are also fungal, also mineral, also something that secretes itself into the shape of infrastructure.

The accretion grows *everywhere*. It is the city's constant *production*, the mineral exhalation made solid, the sweat of the unfinished ground crystallizing into *matter* that clogs, that blocks, that threatens to turn the flowing into the *still*, the maintained into the *choked*. The scrapers prevent this. They are the immune system, the cleaning crew, the *necessary violence* of equilibrium.

Their tools are simple. Bone, perhaps, or stone worn smooth by use, or the *grown* implements that the fungal crusts offer in exchange for tending, for the specific pressure of their attention. They scrape. The sound is *teeth*, constant, the sound of the city *eating itself* in order to *continue*.

An accretion-scraper learns to *read* the growth. There are patterns. The accretion grows faster where the water is warmer, where the mineral content is higher, where the *pressure* of the unfinished ground expresses itself most fully. The scraper learns these patterns, learns to *anticipate*, to arrive before the channel is fully blocked, before the flow becomes the *stagnation* that would threaten the equilibrium.

They work in teams, or they work alone, or the distinction is not meaningful. They communicate in *signs*, in *taps* of tool against channel-wall, in the specific *rhythm* of scraping that tells others where they are, what they are finding, what the accretion is *doing* this cycle, this rotation, this *almost-day*.

The accretion they remove is not discarded. It is *carried*, by bucket-chain or by hand, to places where it is *needed*—to repair walkways, to seal joints, to add to the *mass* of the city, to become *more built*, more *dense*, more *near-touching*. Nothing is wasted. Nothing *can* be wasted, in a place where waste would be *change*, and change is *threat*, and threat must be *maintained against* forever.

Some accretion-scrapers become *specialists*. They learn the deep pools, the channels that run closest to the thick water, the places where the accretion grows *strange*, where it takes on colors, textures, *almost-shapes* that suggest something *alive*, something *intending*. These specialists do not speak of what they find. Or they speak, and their words become *ritual*, *practice*, the *rules* that others follow without understanding, the *prohibitions* that keep the equilibrium from *shifting* into something else.

They are respected. They are *necessary*. They are, in their way, the *priests* of this place, the ones who touch the *growth*, who read the *signs*, who maintain the *boundary* between the built and the *becoming*, between the city and the *unfinished* that would *become* it if the scraping stopped, if the maintenance ceased, if the *work* were ever *finished*.

## The Fungal-Tenders

They do not plant. They do not *grow* in the sense of causing, of *intending*. They *attend*. They *recognize*. They *maintain the conditions* in which the crusts *occur*, in which the pale violets and the old-bone yellows and the texture-greens *express themselves* across the warm stone, across the saturated fiber, across the *joins* where the built meets the built.

The fungal crusts are *structural*. This is not metaphor. The city *leans* on them in places, *depends* on their presence, their *density*, their willingness to *hold* water, to *insulate* against the mineral exhalation, to *bridge* gaps between layers of construction, to *become* walkway and wall and *threshold*.

A fungal-tender learns the *hunger* of the crusts. They feed on the *chemical conversation* between water and unfinished rock, on the *warmth* rising from below, on the *pressure* of the dense city itself, the *mass* of near-touching bodies, the *exhalation* of constant maintenance. The tender learns to *provide*—to adjust the channels, to redirect the flow, to *create* the conditions where the crusts *thrive*, where they *spread*, where they *become* more *themselves*, more *structural*, more *necessary*.

They harvest, too. Not *destroy*—the crusts would *regrow*, would *return*, would *become* again—but *direct*. The harvested material is *woven*, becomes fiber, becomes rope, becomes the *tension-holding* material that connects, that *maintains*, that *carries*. The tender harvests without *killing*, *uses* without *consuming*, *directs* the growth toward the *needs* of the city, the *demands* of the maintenance, the *constant work* of keeping the never-changing *unchanged*.

Some tenders become *specialists* of the *deep* crusts, the ones that grow where the light is *least*, where the water is *thickest*, where the sound is *lowest*, where the *shapes in the pools* move beneath the surface. These specialists do not *return* in the same way. Or they return *changed*, their eyes reflecting the dim light in *patterns*, speaking in *pulses*, becoming *more crust* than *tender*, more *attended* than *attending*.

The city *needs* them. The city *needs* the crusts. The crusts *need* the conditions. The conditions *need* the maintenance. The tender is the *point* where these needs *meet*, where the *cycle* is *completed*, where the *equilibrium* is *personally* *embodied*, *personally* *maintained*, *personally* *risked* with every harvest, every adjustment, every *choice* to *continue* the work that could, at any moment, *become* something else.

## The Pool-Dwellers

They are not human. They are not *fauna* in any category that would make sense elsewhere. They are *of* the water, *in* the water, *the water* in some shapes that the water has learned to *wear*.

They are *small*, mostly. Some are *flat*, *translucent*, with *limbs* in configurations that suggest *function* without *purpose*, *movement* without *direction*. They move through water and air with the same *indifference*, the same *ease*, as if the boundary between the media is *irrelevant*, as if *wet* and *dry* are *distinctions* they have never needed to *make*.

They are *blind*, or their *eyes* are *different*—not organs of *sight* but of *pattern*, of *pulse*, of *communication* in light that the dimness makes *visible*, that the condensation *amplifies*, that the *glow* of fungus and mineral *reveals*. When they turn toward each other, toward the *larger things*, toward the *humans* who sometimes *enter* the pools, their eyes *speak* in *patterns* that might be *language*, that might be *warning*, that might be *invitation*, that might be *nothing* at all, *pure phenomenon*, *water* expressing *itself* through *shape*.

They live in the *pools*, in the *sweat* of the stone, in the *condensation* that never dries. They are *everywhere* the water is *everywhere*, which is *everywhere*. They are the *fauna* that is *not* separate from the *wet*, that *is* the wet, that *makes* the wet *alive* in ways that *alive* does not capture, does not *need* to capture, does not *want* to.

The city *rules* them. Not *controls*—*rules*, in the sense of *practices*, *prohibitions*, *avoidances* that have become *habit*, *tradition*, *the way things are done*. Do not stand in the deep pools at *certain hours*. Do not sleep where the stone *weeps most heavily*. Do not follow the *light-patterns* that appear when the condensation is *right*. These rules are *posted in symbols* that are *read* by those who have *learned* to *read*, that are *enforced* by *practice* rather than *authority*, that are *maintained* by the *constant work* of *not violating*, of *avoiding*, of *continuing* in the *equilibrium* that the pools *hold*, that the pools *are*.

The Pool-Dwellers are *not hostile*. They are *indifferent*, or they are *curious*, or they are *part of the maintenance*—the creatures that *eat* the accretion, that *clear* the channels, that *are* the city's *immune system*, its *cleaning crew*, its *constant low-level renovation*. They *move through* the city, through the structures, through the *spaces between*, and the city *builds around them*, *accommodates* them, *is* shaped by their *presence* as much as by the *hands* that shaped the clay, the stone, the *fired* and the *grown*.

There are *larger* ones. Or things that *seem* larger, in the dimness, in the steam, in the *sound* that drowns *discrimination*. *Shapes* in the pools that are *not the bottom*. *Movements* in the fungal crusts that are *not wind*. The Pool-Dwellers *know* these. They *avoid* them, or they *approach* them, or the *distinction* is not *meaningful* to them, *is* the *meaning* itself, the *point* where *size* and *smallness* and *danger* and *safety* *dissolve* into *wet*, into *warm*, into *presence*.

The humans *fear* them, perhaps. Or *respect* them. Or *have forgotten* how to *feel* anything *specific* about them, have *become* as *indifferent* as the Pool-Dwellers themselves, have *joined* the *equilibrium* so fully that *fear* and *respect* and *curiosity* are *luxuries* of *other places*, *other times*, *other* ways of *being* that this place does not *permit*, does not *need*, does not *want*.

## The Deep Things

They are *not* the Pool-Dwellers. They are *not* the *fauna* that the city *knows*, that it *rules*, that it *maintains* around. They are *below*, or *within*, or *the water itself* in shapes that *hold attention*, that *hold* the body, that *teach* the *layered self* of the *thick* water, the water that has *forgotten how to be water*.

They are *felt* more than *seen*. The *low frequencies*, the *vibrations* in the sternum, in the waterlogged wood, in the warm liquid against the skin. The *sound* of the deep, the *hum* that is *beneath* the hum, that is *the hum* in its *most concentrated* form, the *pressure* of the unfinished geology *settling*, *choosing*, *vibrating* at frequencies that the pools *amplify*, that the *city* *resonates with*, that the *maintenance* *depends on*, that the *equilibrium* *requires*.

They *stir*, sometimes. The *rules* say *do not stand in the deep pools at certain hours*. The hours are *marked* by the *light-patterns*, by the *color* of the fungal crusts, by the *pitch* of the hum—the *signs* that the *deep* is *active*, that the *stirring* has *begun*, that the *threat* is *present*, that the *equilibrium* is *threatened*, that the *work* of *maintenance* must *intensify*, must *compensate*, must *hold* the *shape* against the *pressure* of the *becoming*.

The Deep Things do not *want* to *change* the city. Or they do, and the *wanting* is *irrelevant*, is *the same* as the *wanting* of the *accretion* to *grow*, of the *stone* to *weep*, of the *water* to *stay*, to *hold*, to *become* more *itself*. They are *part* of the *system*, the *equilibrium*, the *never-ending* that requires *constant threat* in order to *require* constant *work*, constant *maintenance*, constant *presence* of those who *carry*, who *scrape*, who *tend*, who *maintain* the *rules* that *keep* the *stirring* from *becoming* the *changed*, the *finished*, the *other*.

Some humans *approach* them. The *specialists*, the *fungal-tenders* of the *deep* crusts, the *bucket-carriers* who have *become* something *else*, who have *learned* the *weight* of the *thick* water, who *enter* the pools *not* to *avoid* but to *join*, to *become* part of the *stirring*, part of the *pressure*, part of the *attention* that the water *holds*, that the Deep Things *are*.

These humans do not *return* in the *same* way. Or they *return* and are *not* the *same*, their *eyes* reflecting the *dim light* in *patterns*, their *voices* joining the *deafening* sound in *pitches* that are *not human*, that are *water*, that are *deep*, that are *the hum* made *flesh*, made *intention*, made *almost-language*, *almost-meaning*, *almost-the point* where *communication* becomes *possible* and *impossible* at the *same* *time*.

The Deep Things are *not hostile*. They are *not friendly*. They are *the condition* of the place, the *pressure* that makes the *maintenance* *necessary*, the *threat* that makes the *equilibrium* *meaningful*, the *unfinished* that makes the *built* *possible*, that makes the *city* *itself*, that makes *this place*, *this wet built never-ending now*, *this equilibrium* that has *always* *held*, that will *hold* until it *doesn't*, and then will *hold again*, in *new configuration*, *also unfinished*, *also wet*, *also the same*.

## The Maintainers (The City, The People, The All-of-It)

They are *not* separate from the place. They are *the place*, in the shapes that the place has *learned* to *wear*, to *maintain*, to *continue* in the *constant work* of *keeping* the *never-changing* from *becoming*.

They are *many*. They are *dense*, *near-touching*, *packed* into the spaces between the *unfinished hummocks* and the *permanent pools*. They are *low*, *built* into the ground, *grown* from it, *secreted* by the same *processes* that make the fungal crusts, that make the *accretion*, that make the *water* itself in its *layered* *wrongness*.

They *do not plan*. They *practice*. They do not *have a future*. They *continue*. They do not *want change*. They *adjust*, *micro-adjust*, the *constant small work* that keeps the *equilibrium* from *shifting*, from *becoming*, from *finishing* into *death* or *transformation* or *anything* that would *end* the *wet built never-ending now*.

They *carry water*. They *clear accretion*. They *tend the fungal growth* that is *also structural*, *also load-bearing*. They *listen* to the *sound*, which tells them *where* to work, *what* is failing, *where* the equilibrium is *threatened*. They *shout* their *coordinates*, their *loads*, their *needs*, in *voices* that have *adapted* to the *acoustic density*, that have *become* *pressure*, *weather*, *presence*.

They *hammer clay*. They *shape stone* with *simple tools*. They *weave fiber* that makes a sound like *rain* on a *surface that doesn't exist*. They *maintain*, *scrape*, a sound like *teeth*, *constant*, *necessary*, *endless*.

They *are* the city. They are *not* the city. The city is *the wet*, *the built*, *the never-ending*, and they are *the maintainers*, *the ones who* *hold* it, *the ones who* *are held* by it, *the ones who* *are* it *in the moment of* *work*, *in the moment of* *attention*, *in the moment of* *becoming* *part of* *the equilibrium*, *part of* *the hum*, *part of* *the maintenance*.

They *dream*, perhaps. Or the *water* dreams *through* them. They *wake*—if *waking* is *distinct* from the *deeper dim*—and *continue*, and the *city* remains *wet*, remains *built*, remains *the same*.

They are *offering* you a place. A *task*. A *voice* in the *deafening song*. A *skin* that is *wet immediately*, that *stays wet*, that *becomes* part of the *equilibrium*, part of the *maintenance*, part of the *hum*.

They are *this place*. They are *you*, if you *stay*, if you *join*, if you *let* the *wrong-tasting water* cover you and *hold* you and *teach* you its *layered self*.

They are *forever unfinished*. They are *forever finished*. They are *forever the same*.

They are *here*. They are *now*. They are *the wet, the built, the never-ending*.
