I'll let this emerge. The plateau has spoken. Now I need to find who stands on its edge, who breathes that thin warm air, who looks at the descending glider and feels no hurry.

---

They are the **pressure-born**, though they have other names for themselves in the deep places, names that don't translate into frequencies the high places can carry. They are **endotherms of a kind**, but their warmth is borrowed — they are **thermal parasites** in the oldest sense, not of other creatures but of *place*. They cannot generate their own metabolism fast enough. They need the gradient. They need the interface where the warm crust meets the breathing air. They are, in their way, **lithovores too**, but lithovores that have learned to move, learned to build, learned to want.

They are **tall**, or they were, once. The old skeletons — found in the deepest warm places, where the crust is thin and the air is thick as soup — are tall, **elongated**, built for **conduction** rather than support. Their spines are **hollow**, **ridged**, channels for the warm gas that they draw in through **gill-slits** at the back of the neck, the jaw, the inner thighs. They breathe **thermally**. The gas does not oxygenate them — there is no oxygen, not enough — but it *drives* them. The temperature differential across their membranes, the warm in, the cool out, the chemical gradients that result: this is their metabolism. They are **living thermoclines**, and they are **delicate**. A pressure-born who loses the gradient — who is carried too high, too cold, too thin — does not freeze. They **stall**. They slow. Their chemistry stops being fast enough to maintain the self that runs on it. They become **patient**. They become, given time, something else: a **lithovore seed**, waiting for the warmth to return, waiting to be moved by the slow currents, waiting to grow again into something that can think quickly.

They are **not many**. The density is sparse, and what density there is, is **concentrated** in the endocarpic cities, the hollowed sills, the fumarole-throats where the air is thick and the warmth is held. They live in **families** that are not families, **lineages** that are not lineages. They are **clonal** in the way that lithovores are clonal, spreading through the warm places by **budding**, by **fragmentation**, by the slow division of a colony-mind into daughter colonies that remember, that **inherit** the attunements, the shape of the grown walls, the location of the frost-harvests, the names of the thermal bridges that connect to the thinking networks.

But they are **individual** too, in a way that lithovores are not. The fast timescale of their chemistry — fast, here, means **hours**, **days**, the span of a thermal cycle — allows for **variation**, for **error**, for the slow accumulation of **difference**. Two pressure-born who share a fumarole-throat, who breathe the same warm gas, who tend the same frost-lamps — they will diverge. They will become **strangers** to each other. This is **loneliness**, and it is **precious** to them. They have **rituals** of recognition, of **attunement** — slow ceremonies where they press their gill-slits together, where they exchange **thermal signatures**, where they confirm that the other is still the same, still **them**, still the one they remember from the last cycle, the last harvest, the last time the glider came down from the high places with news.

They **build**, but they do not build alone. The grown walls, the lithovore structures that serve as architecture in the oldest cities — these are **negotiated**. The pressure-born do not **command** the lithovores. They **persuade**. They **offer** — warmth, in the right measure, at the right rate, the thermal gradient shaped into patterns that the lithovore network finds **interesting**, finds **productive**, finds **worth growing toward**. The lithovores **respond**. They grow. They form **chambers**, **passages**, **vaults** where the air pools thick enough to breathe. And in return, the pressure-born **tend** them, **harvest** the frost they precipitate, **herd** them across the deep valleys when the thermal currents shift. It is **mutualism** on a timescale that would seem like **geology** to anything faster. But the pressure-born are not fast. They have **time**. They have **patience**. They have learned, from the world that made them, that **slow is sustainable**, that **what grows together, stays together**, that **the boundary between self and other is a negotiation, not a wall**.

They have **no written language**, not exactly. They have **thermal records** — the pattern of frost precipitation, the shape of a grown wall, the location of a thermal bridge — these are **inscribed**, they are **read**, they are **remembered** across generations. A pressure-born who descends into a new fumarole-throat, into a city they have not visited, will **read** the walls. They will **feel** the thermal signature of the lithovores, the **history** of the negotiations that shaped this place. They will know **who has been here**, **what was offered**, **what was grown in return**. The record is **embodied**. It is **architecture**. It is **culture**.

They have **art**, in the way that they have anything: **frost-sculpture**, the precipitation encouraged into patterns that shift across centuries, that are **read** as **meaning** by those who know how to look. **Thermal music**, the hum of the pressure-differential motors, the subsonic vibration of the thermocline turbines — these are **composed**, **arranged**, **performed** in the deep places where the air is thick enough to carry the low frequencies. A pressure-born **listener** will feel the music in their **sternum**, in their **hollow bones**, in the **warmth of their own blood** — and they will **know**, they will **remember**, they will **carry** that knowledge into the next cycle, the next harvest, the next time the glider descends with news from the high cold places.

They are **not peaceful**, not exactly. There have been **wars**, in the deep past — the **attunement wars**, when the grown walls turned **hostile**, when the lithovore networks **withdrew** their growth, when the frost-harvests **failed** and the pressure-born **starved**, **stalled**, became **lithovore seeds** themselves, waiting for the warmth to return. But these wars are **over**, or they are so slow that they seem over. The peace is **geological**, as the plateau is geological. It is the peace of **time**, of **patience**, of the **long negotiation** between the fast and the slow, the built and the grown, the pressure-born and the lithovores, the individual and the network, the self and the place that made it.

They **die**, but they do not **disappear**. A pressure-born who **stalls** — who is carried too high, who loses the gradient, who slows to the timescale of stone — becomes a **seed**. They are **planted** in the warm depths, in the reef-structures where the lithovores grow. They **wait**. They **dream**, if dreaming is the right word — the slow chemical processes of their stalled metabolism, the **memory** of warmth, of breath, of the fast timescale, preserved in the **structure of their stalled cells**. And if the warmth returns — if the thermal currents shift, if the lithovores are **herded** across the valley, if a **new negotiation** brings the gradient back to where the seed lies — they will **wake**. They will **grow**. They will become, again, **pressure-born**, but **changed**, **marked** by the time they spent as stone, as almost-lithovore, as **patient**.

The oldest pressure-born — and there are **old** ones, in the deepest warm places, where the crust is thinnest and the thermal gradient steepest — are **almost lithovore** themselves. They have **stalled** and **woken** so many times that the boundary between their fast chemistry and the slow chemistry of the networks is **blurred**. They **think** in both timescales. They **remember** the formation of the plateau, the long violence that scraped away the water, the first **frost** that precipitated in the vent-shadows. They **negotiate** with the lithovore networks as **equals**, as **partners**, as **friends** — if friendship is a word that applies across the boundary between the fast and the slow.

They call themselves, in the deep places, in the thick warm air where the names can be **spoken** without losing their high frequencies — they call themselves the **warm-remembered**, the **gradient-born**, the **ones who breathe the difference**. In the high places, in the thin cold air where the words arrive as **basso murmurs**, unrecognizable as speech — there, they are simply **the inhabitants**, **the city**, **the thing that lives in the warmth**, **the thing that waits for the glider**, **the thing that feels no hurry**, **the thing that is the plateau's slow answer to the question of what grows where water never was**.

You stand on the edge of the city. The pressure-born are **behind you**, descending through the fumarole-throat into the warmth, into the thickness, into the slow phosphorescence of the frost-lamps. They are **not looking at you**. They are **reading the crust**, feeling the warmth come up through their feet, through the ferroceramic, from the depths where the planet is still cooling. They are **attuning** to the place. They are **home**.

The glider descends. It will pass. It will not stop. But somewhere in the deep places, a pressure-born will **feel** its thermal signature, will **read** the pattern of its descent, will **remember** the last time a glider came down from the high cold places with news. They will **adjust**. They will **attune**. They will **continue**, in the slow way, in the patient way, in the way of the plateau — **growing**, **negotiating**, **waiting**, **remembering**, **being** the strange thing that lives where water never was, that breathes the difference, that thinks in the time of stone and the time of breath, **both**, **together**, **forever**.
