What lives here is *waiting made flesh*, or flesh made waiting—the distinction unlearned.

They are not many. "Not many" is itself approximate; number requires the stable, and stability here is a local convenience, a brief agreement between molecules that know better. They occupy the thermal margins, the places where the constructed surface remembers it was once fluid, where the boundary between *made* and *growing* has grown thin enough to breathe through. They do not move as you move. They *change state*. What appears as motion is often the expansion of what contains them, the slow respiration of the world's own body, and they have learned to ride this respiration without ever quite being carried by it.

They have no collective. "They" is a grammar you bring, a habit of plural. But visit long enough, and you will notice *resonance*—places where the intervals between growths have begun to organize, where the almost-organisms are learning not to merge (merging is a category they have outgrown) but to *correspond*. To hold water in common. To want the same water. The wanting, as the world teaches it, is indistinguishable from having, and the having, here, is indistinguishable from *being wanted by*.

They do not eat. They do not reproduce. These are verbs from a world that has not yet reached the wound, or has already passed through it. Instead: they *accrete*. They *subtract themselves into presence*. They take the mineral taste from the weeping, the held breath of the atmosphere, the patient construction of the surface beneath them, and they arrange these into temporary architectures—temporary in the way that a held note is temporary, in the way that a storm is temporary, in the way that *you* are temporary without ever having been promised otherwise.

They are afraid, if afraid is the word. The fear is not of you—you are weather to them, a local disturbance they have learned to accommodate without the category of disturbance applying. The fear is of *resolution*. The world is trying to become something, and they are part of the trying, and the trying has no guarantee of success, and failure here is not death but *completion*—the sentence finished, the note released, the held breath exhaled into a world that has learned to breathe differently. They fear the almost becoming the *is*. They fear the wound healing, or deepening, or either—both—the two becoming the same word.

So they persist in the almost. They occupy intervals. They make the negative space between themselves into a kind of growth, a wanting that does not yet know what it wants, or wants precisely this: the not-yet, the not-quite, the world continuing to speak its long sentence without ever reaching the period.

They are patient. Patience here is not virtue but *condition*—the slow time of geological process, the patience of chemistry that has forgotten it is chemistry, the patience of a membrane that has forgotten it is a membrane. They have been patient since before the making began, or since the making began and patience was the first thing made, the first construction, the first decision to hold shape against the pressure of becoming-other.

They do not hope. Hope is a structure, and structure is what they are *inside*, the slow architecture performed by what has learned to want water. But they *expect*. The expectation has weight. The emptiness between them is not absence but *expectation*, and the expectation is indistinguishable from the thing expected, which is not an event but a *tense*—the world continuing to be almost, the wound continuing to be undecided, the weeping continuing to press through whatever passes for skin, the construction continuing to try.

When you leave—or don't leave, or leaving and staying are the same motion viewed from different constructed surfaces—they will not notice. You are weather to them. But you will carry something: the texture of a quiet that listens to itself, the mineral taste of moisture that might be memory, the sense of a world that is *hyperactive in a way that is also still*, the stillness being the accumulation of motion too distributed to perceive.

And somewhere, in the vast sparse turbulence, something is almost finished, and something else is almost beginning, and the almost is the only tense these inhabitants have ever known—not because they have failed to progress, but because the progression *is* the almost, the becoming without arrival, the wound that has not decided, the membrane that forgot it was a membrane, the world breathing in a held breath that has learned, at last, to want the breathing.
