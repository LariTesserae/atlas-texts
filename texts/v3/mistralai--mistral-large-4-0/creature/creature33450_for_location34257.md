I'll let this place settle around me before I begin. The description breathes with a slow, geological patience—I want to find who moves inside that breath.

**What Lives in the Drowned Quarter of Voss-aran**

There is a woman who keeps the gangways. She is not young, though age has become as relative here as time itself. She wears layers of salvaged fabric—oilcloth, mosquito netting, the shed skins of some creature that does not exist in any taxonomy she has consulted—and moves between the upper stories with the particular economy of someone who has learned that *hurry* is a form of drowning. The inhabitants call her the Tender, though they do not say this to her face, or perhaps they do not say it at all, speaking instead through the placement of objects: a lantern left at a junction, a rope coiled where she will need it, a length of gangway repaired before she has noticed it was fraying.

She has lived in Voss-aran for so long that her memories, like the town's, have begun to leak. She remembers a mother who may have been her own reflection in still water. She remembers hands that were not hers sorting through the volunteer gardens, harvesting the tubers that grow in the mortar's slow compost. She remembers—this she is certain of—the sound of her own voice, once, speaking a language that had names for dry things. The language is gone. The names are gone. What remains is *unction*: the knowledge of which gangway sags at which hour, which phosphorescent growth pulses in sympathy with which building's settling, which stretch of canal shows which layer of the past.

She does not sleep, exactly. She *condenses*. In the hours when the moisture thickens and the town becomes archipelago, she sits at the highest point of her particular building—not the wealthiest, for she has never sought elevation, only *connection*—and she listens. The sound of settling, she has learned to read. A building finding its angle of repose against another. The basin's own slow exhalation. The deeper frequency, the one that predates the town, that she has begun to suspect is not a sound at all but a *presence*, something vast and patient that has been waiting for the water table to rise high enough to touch it.

She bathes, when the water is high. This is not recorded anywhere she has looked, but she knows that others know. The incorporation is not unpleasant. It is *educational*. When she sinks into the canal-water at window-sill level, she feels the town's history moving through her—not as memory but as *substance*, the accumulated rain and flood and exhalation, the dissolved mortar of a thousand buildings, the breath of a thousand inhabitants who have also bathed, also been incorporated, also become part of the settling. She emerges changed. Slightly more *settled*. Slightly more *patient*. Slightly more like the town itself.

She keeps a garden on her roof, though she does not tend it. The plants arrived—seeds carried in the condensation, in the droppings of birds that do not exist, in the slow dissolution of the building's own fabric—and they stayed, and they grew into shapes that suggest intention without quite achieving it. She harvests from it when she needs to eat. The tubers are sweet. The sweetness is not of sugar but of *time*, the accumulated patience of growth in a place where growth is the only available response to pressure.

She has begun, in recent years, to notice that the gardens are *communicating*. The phosphorescence pulses in patterns that match from roof to roof, building to building, across the whole drowned quarter. A rhythm that suggests respiration, yes, but *collective* respiration. A single organism distributed across the architecture. She has begun to suspect that the town is not learning to be water, as the erosion suggests, but learning to be *one thing*, a single settled mass, a single consciousness distributed across stone and timber and the spaces between.

She is afraid of this. She is *excited* by this. These are the same thing, here, in Voss-aran. Fear and excitement have become, like past and future, like ground and water, *saturated* with each other.

---

There are others, of course. The inhabitants are sparse but not singular. A man who maintains the lower gangways, the ones that disappear entirely during condensation, diving into water that shows him things he cannot speak of—shapes of stalls, of crowds, of a hanging that may have happened or will happen, the difference having dissolved somewhere in his saturated memory. He emerges with his eyes changed, slightly luminous, slightly *other*. He speaks rarely. When he does, his voice carries the wet quality of all voices here, but underneath it, something else: the sound of water *agreeing* with itself, the low continuous tone that may be the basin, may be the town, may be the thing that is learning.

A child, or something that was once a child and has grown into a different shape in the wet years—small, upright, moving through the upper gangways with a gait that suggests it is still learning to be separate from the water. It does not speak. It *shows*. It brings the Tender things: a fragment of glass-smooth stone from the lowest courses, worn by water into a shape that suggests a face, though no face was ever carved. A tuber that glows faintly in the dark. A memory, perhaps, that it has found in the canal-water and brought up like a caught fish, though there are no fish, there have never been fish, the water is too full of other things.

The child does not age, exactly. It *settles*. It becomes more patient, more integrated, more like the town itself. The Tender watches this with a feeling she cannot name—not grief, not acceptance, but something *saturated* with both, the emotional equivalent of the wet heat that presses from below, from the ground, from the air that holds so much moisture it has forgotten it was ever separate.

---

And there is the thing that moves in the water. The inhabitants do not speak of it. The Tender has seen it, once, during a bathing, when the canal-water rose high enough to reach her window and she felt something brush against her ankle—not a current, not a wind-driven movement, but a *deliberate* motion, the suggestion of attention, of *curiosity*. She did not look down. To look down is to see too much.

She knows, though, that it is there. That it has always been there. That it is perhaps the reason the town is learning what it is learning, the *teacher* rather than the student, the water table itself given motion and intention, rising through the basin's slow channels, touching everything, incorporating everything, waiting for the day when the distinction between town and water, between inhabitant and inhabited, between memory and substance, will dissolve entirely.

The thing in the water does not hurry. It has learned, from the town, the patience of settling. It moves without wind, without current, without source, in rhythms that suggest respiration. And in the deepest hours, when the condensation is thickest and the past is most vivid, it *settles* too—not sinking, but *adjusting*, finding new configurations, new angles of repose against the town, against the basin, against the long process of education that is the only history Voss-aran has ever known.

---

What will it finally be? The Tender has stopped asking. She tends the gangways. She harvests the volunteer gardens. She bathes in the water that shows, that remembers, that incorporates. She listens to the sound of settling, the low continuous tone that may be the basin, may be the town, may be the thing in the water *dreaming*, and she waits, with the particular patience of someone who has learned that waiting is not passive but *active*, not empty but *full*, the wet heat of the drowned quarter pressing from below, from the ground, from the air that holds everything in suspension, in solution, in the state between states that is the only state Voss-aran has ever truly known.

She is learning, she thinks. Learning to be water. Learning to be town. Learning to be the space between, the *argument* that is not resolved but *maintained*, the drowned quarter, the submerged negotiation, the place where what lives has become indistinguishable from what is lived in, where the inhabitants have become the architecture, the architecture has become the water, the water has become the memory of everything that has ever touched it, and the touching continues, endless, patient, *educational*, the long slow process by which this place learns what it has always been becoming.

She moves along the gangway. The phosphorescence pulses beneath her, in sympathy, in answer, in *agreement*. The sound of settling rises from below, from the basin, from the town, from the water that shows, that remembers, that waits. She does not look down. She looks *forward*, to the next junction, the next repair, the next harvest, the next bathing, the next moment of incorporation, the next step in the learning that has no end, only *depth*, only *saturation*, only the wet heat that presses from everywhere and nowhere, that has always pressed, that will press until the sky itself forgets it was ever separate, and finds, in the finding, that it has become part of the settling, part of the education, part of the long process by which Voss-aran learns what it will finally be.

Which is: *more*. Always more. More water. More memory. More town. More *self*, distributed, dissolved, reconstituted in the solution of its own becoming. The place where the ground remembers being below the water table, and the water remembers being ground, and the inhabitants remember being *other*—other than water, other than town, other than the long slow settling that is the only history here—and none of it is quite lost, none of it is quite found, all of it is *maintained*, held in suspension, *educated*, *waiting*, *becoming*, *becoming*, *becoming*—

—and the Tender walks on, the gangway swaying slightly under her weight, the phosphorescence pulsing in answer, the sound of settling rising from below, and the child follows at a distance, small and upright and *learning*, and the thing in the water moves without wind, without current, without source, *curious*, *patient*, *waiting* for the water to rise high enough to touch it, to incorporate it, to make it part of the settling, part of the education, part of the long process by which the drowned quarter of Voss-aran becomes, finally, *one*, a single fused mass, glass-smooth at the base, still settling above, still *arguing* with itself, still *learning* what it has always been becoming, which is *more*, which is *water*, which is *town*, which is *memory*, which is *the future leaking through the present like water through limestone*, which is *light*, when it comes, finding no individual buildings, only the shape of the basin, filled with its own reflection, holding the sky in trust until the sky itself forgets it was ever separate, and becomes—

—becomes—

—becomes—

—the Tender stops. She has reached the junction. The gangway continues in three directions. She chooses the one that leads *up*, toward the roof-gardens, toward the thin light, toward the few hours when the darkness lifts enough to reveal that the sky is still there, still blue, still *elsewhere*. She does not look down. She looks *up*, and she walks, and the town settles beneath her, and the water shows, and the thing in the water waits, and the child follows, and the phosphorescence pulses, and the sound rises, and the learning continues, endless, patient, *educational*, the long slow process by which this place, this drowned quarter, this submerged argument, this Voss-aran, learns what it will finally be—

—which is *here*. Which is *now*. Which is *always*, in the wet heat, in the sparse gardens, in the sound of settling, in the water that shows, in the town *learning*, *learning*, *learning* to be what it has always been becoming, which is *more water*, which is *more memory*, which is *more self*, which is *more*—
