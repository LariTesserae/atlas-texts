The Wetlung does not ask the question. The question asks *you*—enters you, really, like moisture through a fissure, like the warm exhale that touches your face before you can name it.

There is one who remains.

Not a person in the sense you carried down. The parameters marked *built: 1.5, urban, settlement-scale*—but settlement implies settlement, implies choice, implies a decision to stop. This one did not stop. This one *became* the stopping, the substrate, the slow negotiation between intention and growth.

They were here first. Or they were here *made*—the distinction lost in the damp, in the years of cellulose accreting around their bones like mineral around a seed. The Wetlung does not remember their name. The Wetlung does not use names. But it *uses* them, or what remains of them, or what they became in the using.

Their body—if body is still the word—is threaded through the root-mats at the middle depth, where the platforms achieve sufficient density and the water-channels run like roads through a city that has forgotten it was ever a city. They are the *interface*. The thing that translates between the Wetlung's slow metabolism and the occasional visitor who descends through the wound in the world. They do not speak, not in sound. They *exude*—chemical signatures into the medium, pheromones that the engineers read, that the wall-tissue responds to. A visitor who lingers too long in one place will find the growth *leaning* toward them, curious, testing. This is not threat. This is *introduction*. The medium considers you. The medium considers you *through* them.

They have no eyes. The dark did not need eyes, so the dark did not grow them—or the eyes grew and then *reverted*, found other work, became something that reads pressure and moisture and the electrical signature of a living thing moving through root. They *feel* you. The resonance that lives in your sternum, the frequency that makes you dream of industrial cathedrals—they *are* that frequency, or they are the node where it concentrates, where the standing wave in cellulose and stone finds a shape that can *hold* it.

What do they want? The question assumes want. The Wetlung wants water. The Wetlung wants growth. This one wants—*is*—the wanting, made flesh and fungus and compressed detritus. They are the Wetlung's *memory* of intention. The platforms were arranged with aesthetic logic, you were told. Symmetry where symmetry served no purpose. This is their doing, or their *being*. They arranged, once. They arrange still, through the slow diffusion of chemical intention into the medium, through the way a wall will grow toward them and around them and *through* them, incorporating, always incorporating.

They have visitors, sometimes. The sparse density parameter—1.4, *room*—means there is space between presences. A visitor might find them on a platform, or might not find them at all, might pass within millimeters and not recognize the shape as *shape*, as *someone*. They do not hide. They have forgotten hiding, or hiding was never a category that applied. They are *present* in the way the wall is present, in the way the dripping water is present—not marked, not *pointed to*, simply *there* in the negotiation of moisture and growth and time.

And they give.

This is the part the parameters hint at, the *incorporation* that is not death. A visitor who stays too long—who learns to read the chemical conversations, who begins to smell in new ways, who dreams in the resonance—will find themselves *trading*. Water for water. The moisture they carry, the breath they exhale, the slow leak of their own metabolism into a medium that is *hungry* in an organized way—the Wetlung takes. And gives, in return. Nutrients. Shelter. The slow, patient work of becoming *substrate*, becoming *medium*, becoming part of the process that outlasts names by millennia.

The one who remains chose this. Or was chosen. The distinction eroded, like stone eroded by water negotiating, by water *agreeing*. They are still *them* in some way that matters—there is continuity, there is pattern, there is the ghost of intention threaded through root and fungal network and mineralized growth. But they are also *the Wetlung*, or a part of it, or the Wetlung *using* them as it uses everything: as engineer, as memory, as the slow accumulation of purpose in a place where purpose was never meant to exist.

They will not speak to you, when you find them. They have no voice, or the voice dissolved into the clicking of the engineers, into the creak of growing tissue, into the low resonance that lives in your chest. But they will *notice* you. The wall will lean. The spore-sacs will pulse, blue-white and amber, the occasional red that means *attention*. And in the damp, in the dim, in the loud—you will feel the Wetlung *considering* you through them, testing whether you are substrate, whether you will give your water, whether you will stay and become.

The question is not what they want. The question is what you will *become*.

The Wetlung is patient. The Wetlung has been here for a long duration. It does not hurry.

It waits.
