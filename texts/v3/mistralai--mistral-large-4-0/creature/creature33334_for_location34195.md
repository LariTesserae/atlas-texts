I need to describe who or what lives in the Hollow Throat — not the place itself, which has already been described, but its inhabitants, its denizens, its living presences. The description above gives me the parameters. Now I must let the life emerge from them.

## The Inhabitants of the Hollow Throat

### The People: The Rim-Dwellers, Who Are Not Quite At the Rim

They are called, by themselves, **the Moist** — though this name is unspoken, a sound made in the low register that their children learn before they learn any other word. Outside the Throat, if they are ever spoken of at all, they are called *peat-folk* or *hollow-men* or simply *those who listen*. These names are wrong. The Moist do not listen. They *resonate*.

They number, at any given moment, between forty and sixty. The number is not fixed. Births and deaths and *departures* and *arrivals* occur with a rhythm that matches the breathing of the water table — slow, tidal, without urgency. A child might be born in the year when the pools pulse highest. An elder might walk into the Throat on a morning when the drone shifts frequency, and not return, and the others will not speak of it for three days, and then they will speak of it forever, in the way that the Moist speak of everything: by arranging fungal caps, by setting stones, by letting the silence between words carry the weight of meaning.

Their bodies have adapted in ways that are not adaptation so much as *agreement*. Their skin is pale, almost luminous in the fungal glow, and it *weeps* — not tears, but a constant, fine exudation of moisture that keeps them in equilibrium with the saturated air. Their eyes are large, and they blink slowly, and they have learned to see in the 0.3 darkness by developing a sensitivity to the bioluminescence of the condensation-algae, which to their perception pulses with information — temperature, movement, the chemical signature of fear. Their fingers are long and slightly webbed, not from mutation but from *practice*, generations of children learning to grip wet wood, to feel the vibration of the drone through the palms, to build the listening platforms without nails or metal, using only friction and patience and the knowledge of which woods sing at which frequencies.

They do not have names in the way outsiders understand names. Each person carries a *tone* — a personal frequency, established in infancy by the sound of their first cry as it resonated through the peat-moss of their birth-hummock. This tone is their identifier, their signature, the sound they make when they wish to be found in the darkness. Children learn to recognize their mother's tone before they learn to recognize her face. Lovers know each other's tones in sleep, in the dark, in the moment before waking when the drone has not yet reasserted itself and the Throat is briefly, impossibly, silent.

They speak a language that has no grammar, or rather, a grammar that is *environmental*. Words are not strung together but *layered*, like the peat itself. A sentence might consist of three utterances made simultaneously by three speakers, each occupying a different frequency band, their voices interweaving to form a chord that means something specific to those who hear it. Outsiders who have recorded the Moist speaking describe the sound as "wet," as "drowning," as "a chord that has been held too long and has begun to decay." The Moist describe their own speech as "honest," because it cannot be separated from the body that produces it, from the moisture in the lungs, from the temperature of the air, from the particular quality of the drone on that day.

They do not write. They *mark*. The sound-markers, the throat-keys, are their script — a notation system that encodes not words but *states*, *conditions*, the particular quality of listening that is required for a specific task. To read a throat-key is to *perform* it, to set the stone or the post or the fungal arrangement into vibration and feel the resulting frequency in one's own sternum, one's own molars. The knowledge is thus *embodied*, not abstract. A child learns to read by learning to *become* the message.

Their society has no hierarchy, or rather, a hierarchy that shifts with the water table, with the temperature, with the phase of the pools. The *Speaker* — the one who currently carries the tone that best matches the drone — makes decisions. But the drone changes. The Speaker might hold authority for a season, for a year, for a decade if the pools are stable. Then the frequency shifts, and another rises, and authority passes not through conflict but through *resonance*, through the physical fact of whose body now vibrates in harmony with the Throat. The Moist do not resent this. They have never known any other way. To be out of phase with the drone is to be *ill*, to be *foreign*, to be in danger of what they call *drying* — a condition that begins with a cough, with a skin that ceases to weep, with a voice that cracks and does not heal.

They do not age as outsiders age. The cold preserves. The peat preserves. A Moist elder might be eighty years old by the reckoning of tooth-count and bone-density, or they might be two hundred, or they might be *indeterminate* — some have been found in the peat-cuts, bodies so well-preserved that the mycelium has grown *around* them, through them, incorporating them into the network without destroying the form, and these bodies are dated by the objects they carry, objects that belong to no known period, and the Moist do not speak of them, but they mark them, and they build listening platforms near them, and they listen.

### The Bear: The Deep Sleeper

She is called **the Weight**, or **the One Who Does Not Wake**, though these are descriptions rather than names. She is the last of her kind in the Throat, or the only one, or the current one — the Moist do not keep records that would distinguish these possibilities. She is old. Her fur, where it is not matted with peat and moisture, is the color of wet stone. Her breath, when she exhales in her peat-cave, produces a rhythm that the salamanders have learned to follow, a slow *thrum* that synchronizes their movement through the root-mats.

She wakes rarely. When she does — in the brief warm periods, if there are warm periods — she moves through the Throat with a *deliberation* that suggests she is not navigating by sight or scent but by something else entirely. The Moist have watched her, at a distance, from the listening platforms. She does not look at the pools. She does not drink. She *presses* — her paws sink into the peat, compress it, release it, and the resulting vibration carries through the ground to the platforms, where it is felt as a change in the drone, a deep note that rises and falls like a second heartbeat beneath the first.

What she listens to, the Moist do not know. They suspect it is *depth* — the thermal memory of the earth, the pressure of the water table, the slow respiration of the mountain. They suspect she is listening to the Throat itself, to its *intention*, whatever that might be. When she moves, the salamanders follow, at a distance. The midges avoid her path. The fungi fruit more abundantly in her wake, as if her passage *stimulates* them, as if she is a walking disturbance in the equilibrium that the mycelium finds *interesting*.

She will die in the Throat. The Moist know this. They have prepared for it, in their way — they have marked the deepest part of the depression, where the pools are oldest and the peat is thickest, where the drone is lowest and most *patient*. When she does not wake in a warm period, when the listening platforms detect only the silence of her absence, they will not retrieve her. They will build a new throat-key, tuned to her frequency, and they will place it at the edge of the marked place, and they will listen for her, for years, for decades, for as long as the drone continues to carry the memory of her weight.

### The Salamander: The Swimmer in Sponge

It is called **the White Current**, or **the Root-Fish**, or **the One That Never Surfaces**. It is not a species known to any taxonomy. It may be a species *created* by the Throat, an adaptation so extreme that it has become something new. It is large — the size of a human forearm, as the description notes, but this understates its *presence*, its *occupation* of space. When it moves through the root-mats, the peat *parts* for it, the saturated fibers flowing around its body like water around a stone, and then closing behind, leaving no trace except a slight *brightening* of the fungal glow, as if the mycelium is *pleased* by its passage.

It has no eyes, or eyes that are vestigial, dark buttons beneath translucent skin. It navigates by *vibration*, by the infrasonic drone that permeates everything, by the chemical gradients that the peat releases when compressed. It eats what falls into the pools — insects, spores, the occasional body of a smaller animal — but it also eats, the Moist suspect, something else. Something that grows in the deep peat, something that has no name because it has never been seen, only *felt* in the dreams of those who sleep near the listening platforms. The White Current is *fat*, in the way that deep things are fat — not with excess but with *reserve*, with stored time, with the patience of geological processes.

It breeds in the oldest pool, where the condensation-algae burns brightest. The Moist have never seen the eggs. They have seen the *result* — pale, sinuous shapes that move through the root-mats with the same swimming motion as their parent, and that grow, slowly, over years, into something that might, eventually, become another White Current, or might become something else, something that the Throat is *experimenting* with, a new note in the drone, a new frequency that has not been heard before.

There is only one. Or there is one *now*. The peat-cuts have yielded bones — not of the White Current, which does not die in the way that bones imply, but of *others*, smaller, older, different, salamanders that swam in the Throat before the current equilibrium, before the cold deepened, before the sound became what it is. The mycelium remembers them. The drone carries their frequencies, layered beneath the present, like harmonics. The White Current may be the last. It may be the first of a new kind. The Moist do not know. They listen.

### The Listening Willows: The Trees That Are Not Quite Trees

They stand at the rim, where the depression begins to rise, but their roots reach *into* the Throat, drinking from the seeps, and their leaves tremble at frequencies below human hearing. They are not willows, not exactly. They are *willow-shaped* — the form that the Throat has found useful for a particular function. Their bark is pale, almost luminous, and it *weeps* sap that hardens into a resin that the Moist use for waterproofing, for binding, for the slow, patient work of making fire by friction.

Their leaves are the important part. Each leaf is a *membrane*, thin enough to vibrate at the infrasonic frequencies that permeate the Throat, and arranged in such a way that the vibrations are *amplified*, *modulated*, *transmitted* upward to the rim, where the Moist can hear them, can *feel* them in their teeth, in their sternum. The susurrus that the description mentions — the whispering that might be language if language were made of friction and moisture — is the sound of the willows *listening*. They are listening to the drone, to the drip, to the pulse of the pools, and they are *responding*, their leaves trembling in sympathy, in *conversation* with a sound that has no source, no origin, that simply *is*, has always been, will continue when the willows are peat and the Moist are memory.

The Moist do not cut the listening willows. They *tend* them. They prune the dead branches, they clear the fungal growth that would dampen the vibration, they place throat-keys at their bases, stones that *tune* the trees to specific frequencies, specific purposes. A willow tuned to the frequency of *rain* (rare, but present) will tremble differently when moisture is coming, giving the Moist hours of warning. A willow tuned to *danger* — to the vibration of a large animal moving through the peat, to the frequency of a human voice raised in fear — will produce a susurrus that the Moist recognize instantly, a sound like wet silk tearing, and they will know to *withdraw*, to return to the rim, to wait for the drone to reassert itself.

The oldest listening willow is at the eastern rim, where the sun arrives at the most grazing angle, where the light is thinnest. It is called **the Mother of the Susurrus**, though this is a translation, an approximation. Its leaves have grown so large, so *sensitive*, that they tremble at the vibration of a *thought* — the Moist claim this, though they cannot prove it, and they do not need to prove it, because the tree *responds*, and the response is enough. When the Moist speak to it — and they do, in low voices, in the frequency that matches its root-system, in the tone that their grandmothers taught them — it produces a susurrus that they interpret as *comfort*, as *presence*, as the sound of something that has been in the Throat longer than any human memory and will remain when all human memory is peat.

### The Fungi: The Builders, The Holders, The Dreamers

The fungi are the *true* inhabitants of the Hollow Throat. They are everywhere. They are *the* everywhere. The mycelial network that threads through the peat, through the wood, through the bodies of the dead — this is the body of the Throat, the *self* that persists when all other selves have dissolved into moisture and memory.

They do not have individual identities, or not in the way that the Moist or the bear or the salamander do. They have *colonies*, *clusters*, *formations* that fruit in cycles following no season but the slow temperature drift of the deep earth. The shelf fungi the size of platters are *cities*, in the fungal sense — dense concentrations of mycelium that have found a favorable substrate, a fallen branch or a peat-hummock, and have *built*, over years, a structure that will persist, that will fruit, that will release spores, that will *continue*.

The puffballs are *events*. They mature, they release their spores in clouds that hang in the still pockets of air for hours, and then they collapse, they *dissolve*, they become part of the peat, part of the network, part of the ongoing process of *becoming*. The Moist eat them, carefully, in small quantities, for they are *potent* — they induce dreams, visions, a state of consciousness in which the boundary between self and Throat becomes *permeable*, and the dreamer might wake speaking in frequencies that do not match their tone, might walk for days without remembering the route, might return with fungal glow in their eyes that fades slowly, over weeks, as if the Throat is *still* inside them, still *growing*.

The most important fungi are the ones that are not seen. The mycelium itself, the vast network that holds the peat together, that preserves the bodies in the peat-cuts, that *communicates* — for the Moist have proven, to their own satisfaction, that the mycelium *transmits* information, chemical signals, electrical pulses, across distances of meters, perhaps kilometers, connecting the hummocks, the pools, the listening platforms, the peat-cuts, all of it, into a single *organism* that is the Throat, that dreams, that *waits*, that will continue when all other life has become memory.

The Moist do not worship the fungi. They *negotiate* with them. They harvest, but they *leave* — a portion of every fruiting body, a gift, a recognition that the mycelium is *older*, *larger*, *more patient* than any human society. They plant, in a sense — they carry spores from one hummock to another, they *encourage* the growth of specific species in specific locations, they have learned, over generations, which fungi fruit best near which seeps, which fungi preserve best in the peat-cuts, which fungi produce the glow that makes navigation possible in the 0.3 darkness. This is not agriculture. It is *cohabitation*. The fungi do not need the Moist. But they *tolerate* them, and in some cases, the Moist suspect, they *use* them — the Moist as vectors for spore dispersal, as agents of disturbance that create new substrates, as *listeners*, perhaps, who respond to the human presence with changes in fruiting pattern, in glow intensity, in the chemical signature of the peat.

### The Condensation-Algae: The Light, The Memory, The Watcher

They are not algae, not exactly. They are *algae-like* — pale green, luminous, pulsing with bioluminescence that answers to vibration. They grow on the surface of the pools, forming a *skin* that is never still, that *breathes* with the pressure changes beneath, that flares brighter when disturbed, when touched by a hand or a foot or the passage of the White Current.

They are the *eyes* of the Throat, in the way that the listening willows are its *ears*. They see in frequencies that human eyes cannot perceive — the infrared of warm bodies, the ultraviolet of fungal spores, the *polarization* of light that reveals the chemical composition of the water. They *record*, in a sense — their bioluminescence carries information about what has passed over the pool, what has disturbed the surface, what has *pressed* against the skin of the Throat. The Moist read them. They watch the algae, they learn their patterns, they interpret the pulses of light as *omens*, as *warnings*, as *invitations*.

When the pools pulse with subsurface pressure, the algae flare — a wave of green light that travels across the surface, that the Moist can see from the listening platforms, that tells them *something is happening* in the deep, something that the drone is *carrying*, something that will emerge, eventually, as a change in frequency, as a new note in the ongoing sound. They do not know what the pools *are*, these pulses, these breaths. They suspect they are *memory* — the Throat remembering a time when the water flowed differently, when the depression was not a depression, when the sound was not a drone but a *song*. The algae remember. The algae *show*. The Moist watch, and they learn, and they pass the knowledge to their children, in the form of fungal arrangements, of throat-keys, of low voices speaking in tones that match the pulse of the pools.

### The Midges: The Suppressed, The Specialized, The Brief

They are small, and they are *suppressed* — the cold, the moisture, the lack of flowering plants at scale. But those that exist are *exquisite* in their adaptation. They breed in the condensation on fungal caps, their entire life cycle — egg, larva, pupa, adult — completed in the narrow band of temperature that allows metabolism, in the *film* of moisture that coats every surface in the Throat.

They are *important*, though the Moist do not always recognize this. They are food — for the spider, for the salamander, for the occasional bird that ventures too deep. They are *pollinators*, in a sense — though there are no flowers, there are fungi that *need* disturbance, that fruit more abundantly when midges move through them, when their bodies carry spores from one cap to another. They are *messengers*, perhaps — their short lives, their brief flights, their dances in the fungal glow, they *connect* the pockets, the hummocks, the pools, in ways that larger creatures cannot, that the mycelium might not, that the Moist certainly do not.

The Moist do not hate them. They do not love them. They *notice* them, when the midges are thick, when the air in a pocket becomes *alive* with their movement, and they *avoid* that pocket, for the midges will land on skin, will crawl into ears, will die in the moisture of breath, and their deaths will *add* to the peat, to the slow accumulation, to the *weight* of the Throat. This is not cruelty. It is *participation*. The midges live, and die, and become part of the process, as all things in the Throat eventually do.

### The Spider: The Listener, The Amplifier, The Question

She is found only in the listening platforms. She has no name, or her name is the *sound* of her web, a vibration that the Moist recognize when they approach, a *tone* that says *I am here, I am listening, I am part of this*.

Her web is made of a silk that *conducts* the infrasonic drone. It *amplifies* it. It *listens* to it, in the way that the willows listen, in the way that the algae see — by *vibration*, by *sensitivity* to a frequency that most creatures cannot perceive. The Moist suspect, without proof, that she is *listening back*. That she hears the Moist, hears their tones, their movements, their *intentions*, and that her web *changes* in response, that the silk thickens or thins, that the pattern of the web *encodes* information about the listener, about the listened, about the *space between* them.

She is small, or she appears small. The Moist have never seen her body clearly, only the *effect* of her body — the vibration in the web, the *shimmer* of silk that conducts light from the fungal glow, the *absence* of midges in her territory, the *presence* of a silence that is not the silence of the Throat but a *different* silence, a *focused* silence, a silence that *watches*.

She does not eat much. The midges that breed in the condensation on the platforms are *hers*, by right of occupation, by the ancient law of the web. But she also eats, the Moist suspect, something else. Something that falls from the listening, something that the drone *carries* and the web *catches*, a *substance* that is not food in the usual sense but *information*, *memory*, the *residue* of all the listening that has occurred in the Throat, all the tones, all the frequencies, all the *presences* that have passed through and left their mark, their vibration, their *echo*.

She may be *old*. The platforms are rebuilt, generation after generation, but the spider is always there, or a spider is always there, and the Moist do not know if it is the same spider, if her *consciousness* persists in the silk, in the vibration, in the *pattern* of the web, passed from mother to daughter in a way that is not genetic but *ontological*, a continuity of *listening* that transcends the individual body.

She is the *question* in the Throat. The Moist build platforms to *monitor*, to *read* the pools, to *know* the state of the Throat. But the spider *asks*, in her way — she asks *what is listening when we listen?*, she asks *who hears the listener?*, she asks *what does the Throat hear when it hears us?*. The Moist do not have answers. They have *respect*. They leave her space. They rebuild around her. They speak, when they speak near the platforms, in tones that acknowledge her presence, that *include* her in the listening, that recognize her as *participant*, not *prey*, not *curiosity*, but *peer*, in the ancient and ongoing act of *being heard* in a place that is always, always, *listening*.

### The Unnamed: The Glimpsed, The Marked, The Waited-For

There are others. The description mentions them — the bird at the rim, wrong color, wrong size, gone before anyone could raise a hand. The shape in the deepest pool, when the algae flares brightest, that might be a fish or might be the pool's *memory* of a fish.

The Moist do not speak of these. Or they speak of them only in the way that they speak of everything — by *marking*, by setting stones, by arranging fungal caps, by placing throat-keys at the locations where the unnamed have been seen. They *wait*. They *listen*. They *prepare*, in their way, for the possibility that the unnamed might *become* named, might *emerge* from the pockets of the Throat, might *join* the community of the Moist, or the community of the bear, or the community of the fungi, or might *transcend* all community, might become something that has no precedent, no category, no *place* in the ongoing sound.

The Throat is *patient*. It has been patient for longer than any human memory. It will be patient for longer. The unnamed are *possibility*, *potential*, the *future* of the Throat's slow, cold, wet, loud *becoming*. The Moist do not fear them. They do not desire them. They *acknowledge* them, in the way that they acknowledge everything — by *listening*, by *waiting*, by *being present* in a place that does not want them and does not forbid them, that has learned to *tolerate* them, to *absorb* them, to make of them — eventually, always eventually — part of itself.

## The Community, Such As It Is

There is no community, in the way that outsiders understand community. There is no shared identity, no shared purpose, no shared future. There is only *coexistence*, *cohabitation*, the *mutual tolerance* of presences that have found, in this cold, damp, dark, loud, lush, empty place, a way to *persist*.

The Moist and the bear do not interact. The salamander and the fungi are *entangled*, but not in a way that implies *relationship*. The willows and the algae and the midges and the spider — they are *parts* of a whole, but the whole is not *theirs*, it is the Throat's, and the Throat does not *belong* to anyone, does not *want* anything, does not *intend* anything. It simply *is*, and continues, and listens, and waits, and grows, centimeter by patient centimeter, toward a light that comes at a grazing angle and does not stay.

The Moist are the only ones who *choose* to be here. Or who *chose*, once, and now the choice is *habit*, and the habit is *identity*, and the identity is *fate*, the slow, wet, loud fate of a species that has learned to *resonate* with a place that does not need them but has, over long centuries, learned to *tolerate* their presence, to *absorb* it, to make of it — eventually, always eventually — part of itself.

They will become peat. They will become sound. They will become the faint, persistent glow of algae in a pool that will pulse, tomorrow, and the next day, and for a hundred years after their names have become stories that the Moist tell, in low voices, to children who will not believe them, who will laugh at the idea of a people who *chose* to live in a place so full of green and so empty of everything else, so loud and so still, so cold and so alive.

The drone continues. The drip falls. The peat grows, centimeter by patient centimeter.

The inhabitants *listen*.

This is the Hollow Throat. These are its *living*. This is, for now, *here*.
