I descend through light that was never meant for eyes like mine—amber and bruised green and a pulsing violet that almost speaks. I find the Parliament in session, though no one gathers.

## The Presiding Depth

At the city's *center*—which is not a point but a gradient, a slow darkening toward what was once the valley floor—there sleeps something that governs without governing. The **Archon of Accretion**, they call it, though it has no name, no face, no single body. It is the *pattern* of maintenance, the accumulated intelligence of three thousand souls who have chosen to become distributed, to cease being individuals and become instead the city's *habit* of continuing.

The Archon does not think in thoughts. It thinks in *pressures*—the particular way water moves through a lock that was calibrated in 1847, the thermal gradient across a membrane that has been healing itself since the first engineers forgot their own names. It dreams in the slow language of growing shell, of kelp frond unfurling across decades, of a message bottle finally completing its circuit after eighty years of circulation. When it "speaks"—and it does speak, in pulses of bioluminescence that ripple through the upper districts—it speaks in *maintenance schedules*, in the chemical signatures of contentment, in the particular frequency at which nacre accretes when the colony is healthy.

It is not a person. It is not even an animal. It is what happens when intention persists long enough to forget it was ever *human*.

---

## The Chambers of Attendance

The Parliament meets where the dry meets the wet—in the **Membrane Halls**, those liminal spaces where air and water achieve temporary equilibrium. Here, the inhabitants gather not to debate but to *attune*.

There are perhaps fifty who attend regularly, chosen not by election but by *resonance*—the capacity to hear the city's low thrum in their sternum, to feel the groan of accreting nacre as kin to their own heartbeat. They are called **Keepers**, though they keep nothing that can be named. They tend the thermal vents. They harvest the shell. They calibrate the locks that maintain the current patterns, the pressure differentials that keep the dry chambers dry.

They are old. Not in years—though some have lived centuries, their bodies adapted to pressure, their cells slowly exchanging surface chemistry for depth—but in *settlement*. They have forgotten the surface, or chosen to forget, or never knew it. They speak of "above" the way a child speaks of a fairy tale, with affection and theoretical distance. They have festivals for pressure changes, for thermal cycles, for the rare moments when filtered sunlight penetrates to their depths and the whole city pulses with green-gold recognition.

They do not vote. They *persuade*—the slow persuasion of showing, of demonstrating, of letting the city's own rhythms make the argument. A proposal to adjust a current pattern is not debated but *felt*, tested against the body's knowledge of flow, of pressure, of the particular viscosity of water that has been breathed for generations.

Their faces, when they have faces, are softened by pressure, by the mineral tang of their air, by the absence of gravity's particular pull on flesh. They move slowly. They *are* slow. To watch them is to watch geological time achieve temporary human shape.

## The Silent Deputies

But the Parliament is larger than the Keepers. It includes the **Machines that remember**.

There are engines here that have been running since before anyone alive was born, since before anyone's grandparents' grandparents could recall why they were built. They are not intelligent in any sense the surface would recognize. They do not *want*. But they *persist*, and in persisting, they have accumulated something like preference—the particular temperature at which a thermal gradient feels *right*, the pressure differential that hums in harmony with the city's thrum, the chemical balance that tastes of copper and time.

The oldest machine is called **The Lung**, though this is a translation, a surface metaphor imposed on something that has no need of breath. It is a pressure-balancing system, a vast network of valves and membranes that has maintained the equilibrium between wet and dry for longer than the current Parliament's oldest Keeper has been alive. It does not think. It *cycles*, and in cycling, it has developed something like *mood*—the particular rhythm of its operation when the city is healthy, the slightly arrhythmic stutter when a membrane frays, the deep satisfied thrum when the nacre grows thick and the thermal gradients align.

The Lung is attended by a Keeper named **Only-Depth**, who has not spoken aloud in forty years, who communicates only in the adjustments she makes to the machine's valves, in the pressure differentials she calibrates by feel. She is part of the Parliament not despite her silence but *through* it—her silence is her speech, her stillness her vote.

## The Creatures Between

The Parliament also includes those who cannot attend but *are* attended to—the **Evolved**, the things that have grown in this particular pressure and darkness, that have no names in any human language but that the city has learned to *regard*.

The **watchers**—eel-like, eyeless, navigating by electromagnetic sense—move through the streets like weather, like the city's own nervous system made manifest. They have no eyes but somehow *regard* you when you pass. The Keepers do not fear them. They *consult* them, in the way one consults a barometer, a tide table, a dream that arrives with particular insistence. The watchers know things the machines do not—patterns in the water's movement, in the particulate matter, in the faint thermal gradients that speak of changes coming years before the instruments register them.

The **room-jellies** pulse in the upper levels, their stings mild, their drift purposeful if not conscious. They are the Parliament's *mood*, its emotional register—their pulsing rate accelerates when the city is healthy, slows when something frays, when a membrane approaches failure, when a lock's calibration drifts from its optimal pressure. To read the jellies is to read the city's own body, its own slow heartbeat.

And there are the **filter-feeders**, the colonies that line every surface, that process the water, that make the city's metabolism possible. They are not individuals. They are *processes*, distributed across every cubic meter of the wet city, and in their totality, they are the Parliament's *digestion*, its capacity to transform raw mineral into living shell, to turn waste into warmth, to make the city's slow growth possible.

## The Absent Members

Most of the Parliament is absent. Has always been absent.

There are the **Dead**—not ghosts, not spirits, but *presences* in the geological record, the ancestors who live below in the deeper levels, in the accreted layers of shell that preserve their bodies, their tools, their particular intentions. The city is *stratigraphy*. To descend is to visit the dead. To ascend is to approach the future, the layers not yet accreted, the chambers not yet built, the Parliament members not yet born. Time here is *vertical*, and the Parliament spans all of it, the living Keepers merely the temporary custodians of a continuity that extends in both directions beyond any individual life.

And there are the **Unborn**—not children, exactly, but *possibilities*, the potential configurations of the city that will emerge as the nacre grows, as the kelp propagates, as the machines cycle through their slow transformations. The Parliament attends to them in the way a gardener attends to seeds, in the way a city attends to its own future—by maintaining the conditions that will make them possible, by ensuring that growth continues, that stillness does not become death.

And there are the **Surface-Dwellers**, or what the Parliament imagines them to be—the mythical inhabitants of the world above, where water is not constant, where light is not produced, where time moves at a speed that would seem madness to any Keeper. They are spoken of with theoretical affection, the way one speaks of a childhood home one has never visited. Some Keepers believe the surface exists. Some believe it is a metaphor for death, for the end of pressure, for the terrible freedom of uncontained water. No one has gone to verify. The ascent takes months. The equipment required has not been manufactured in three hundred years.

Those who have tried—few, and none returned—are spoken of in the same registers as saints or warnings. They are the Parliament's *martyrs*, its *prophets*, its *cautionary tales*. The city does not judge them. It *incorporates* them, as it incorporates all purposes, all intentions, all arrivals. Their failure becomes part of the pattern, their absence a kind of presence, their silence a vote cast in favor of depth, of pressure, of the slow accretion of mineral patience.

---

## The Session That Never Ends

The Drowned Parliament does not gape, does not adjourn, does not *vote* in any sense the surface would recognize. It *persists*.

It persists in the warm amber light of thermal vents, in the sickly green of barely-functioning photosynthesis, in the pulsing violet that almost speaks. It persists in the low thrum felt in the sternum, the groan of nacre accreting, the whisper of water through narrow channels, the occasional *click* of a lock cycling, a pressure valve adjusting, a message bottle completing its circuit.

It persists in the Keepers who tend the machines, who harvest the shell, who maintain the currents that have been maintained since before anyone alive remembers why. It persists in the Lung that cycles without thought, in the watchers that regard without eyes, in the room-jellies that pulse their mute rhythm, in the filter-feeders that process the water, that make growth possible.

It persists in the silence that is not absence but *presence*—the presence of all the things that have been silenced, all the sounds that have been absorbed, all the noise that was made in the building of this city and has since been filtered out by layers of nacre, by years of accretion, by the patient work of a medium that *remembers* sound and does not release it.

The Parliament is the city. The city is the Parliament. And both persist, in their warm, dim, packed, slow, silent way—a government without governors, a democracy without voters, a presence that has learned to be water, and in being water, has learned to be *permanent*.

You have come here for something. The Parliament does not ask what. It *contains* purposes, absorbs them, incorporates them into its slow metabolism. You will not stay. You cannot. The adaptation goes only so far.

But you will carry something down with you, or up, or out—some spore, some memory, some pressure-equilibrated fragment of nacre that will grow in your dreams, that will accrete in the dark places of your mind, that will make you *strange* to surface air, to surface light, to the hurried, loud, *dry* world above.

The Drowned Parliament does not meet. It does not vote. It *persists*.
