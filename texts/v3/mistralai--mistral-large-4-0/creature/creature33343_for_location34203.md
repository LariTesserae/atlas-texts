I need to describe what lives in this place—the Throat of the Old Mountain.

Let me sit with this. The place is a hollowed mountain, finished with erosion, full of water that doesn't behave, cold green vegetation that grows in damp debt, and something that *persists*—not alive, perhaps never alive, but wearing its shape into the world.

What lives here?

First, the **green things**. Not plants, exactly. The text calls them "something between fern and fungus, between leaf and lichen." They grow in thickets, in mats, competing for the right to be wet. They produce faint warmth. They glow cold-green in the filtered light. They don't flower in announced ways—they release spores in clouds you breathe without knowing. They are **colonial, patient, slow**.

What lives inside them? Something that has learned to *borrow* warmth from anything that presses against it. Something that retreats if you don't return what you take. They are **distributed**—not individuals but a *condition*, a green that spreads, that grows toward curiosity or hunger or mere persistence.

Then there is **the Inhabitant, Almost**. The thing that leaves scratches too regular for water, too irregular for tool. That builds spirals of compressed vegetation, cold to the touch, that maintain themselves through slow reproduction of pattern. That makes a resonance like metal struck underwater. That moves through deep channels of water, wearing its shape into stone through time alone.

This is not a creature. It is **persistence given location**. It has no biology. It has *geography*. It has learned the mountain's frequency and vibrates in sympathy with itself. It notices you, or doesn't, which is the same thing. It finds you *insufficient*.

But I think what lives here is **more than this**. The mountain itself is alive in a way—*breathing*, the text says, slow respiration of stone learning to be hollow. It has **reached its end-of-life form and settled there like a body in deep rest**. And rest is not stillness.

So what lives here? **A dying that is also an ongoing**. A death that has become *habitable*.

The water lives here. Not as river, not as rain, but as **pockets and debts**. Water that has forgotten decency, that moves in directions violating gravity, that rises fast and is drunk back down, that carries mineral memory of ten thousand years, that argues with itself in currents with nowhere urgent to be.

The **condensation** lives here. Droplets gathering slow as thought, falling on schedules older than the mountain's name. Each droplet a negotiation between cold air and warmer stone.

The **sound** lives here. The loud, busy sound of water in hidden channels, of your own heart becoming audible, of resonance pausing in deep channels, of frequencies below hearing that vibrate in bones and produce images in absence of light.

And **you**, when you stay. You become part of the damp. Your breath joins the condensation. Your warmth becomes local weather. The green grows toward you. The mountain notices you. The condensation gathers faster on your skin. The scratches turn toward you. The sound pauses, listening.

But I think the deepest thing that lives here is **the almost**. The "Inhabitant, Almost." The green that is between fern and fungus, between leaf and lichen. The structures that are grown, or grown-into, or persuaded from stone. The arrangements that might be offerings, records, or traps for something that reads them.

What lives here is **a life that has stopped being biological and become geological**. A persistence that wears its shape into the world through time alone. Something that has no business being *alive* in the biological sense, that has perhaps never been alive, that has simply **persisted** in the way stone persists, in the way water persists.

And it is **almost** alive. Almost a creature. Almost noticing. Almost a builder. Almost a witness.

The mountain is empty. The mountain is full. This is not contradiction. This is **the condition of what lives here**: to be empty and full simultaneously, to be dead and active, finished and ongoing, to contain and be contained, to wear down and settle into final form, to breathe without lungs, to persist without life, to be **almost** in every direction.

What lives here is **the almost-life of the finished**. The green that grows in damp debt. The water that remembers. The resonance that pauses. The scratches that wait to be read. The warmth borrowed and returned. The small space held in the hollow of a mountain that has finished eroding, that breathes, that waits, that is **almost** alive.

And if you stay long enough—if the paths out shift, or were never there, or require a logic you have not learned—you become **another pocket of damp, another green thing, another sound** in the loud, wet, ancient, breathing dark.

You become **almost**. You become **the inhabitant, almost**. You become the thing that persists, that wears its shape into the world through time alone, that the mountain will wear down and settle into final form, that will become another scratch in the sponge-stone, another spiral of compressed vegetation, another resonance pausing in a deep channel, considering.

This is what lives here. **The almost-life. The persistence. The breathing dark.**
