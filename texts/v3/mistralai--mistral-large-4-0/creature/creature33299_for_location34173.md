I'll walk through this slowly, letting the place show me who lives there.

---

The Still do not have faces. This becomes clear early, and then becomes more clear still, until "face" is a category that has lost all meaning. They are *membranes*—thick, slow-moving sheets of cellular mass that have learned, through generations of cultivation, to fold themselves into the suggestion of bodies. They are not humanoid. They are not any shape you could draw from memory. They are *local concentrations* of the plant-substrate, denser than their surroundings, more responsive, more *tuned*.

A Still stands—if "stands" applies—perhaps as tall as a human, perhaps not. Their mass is mostly *held structure*: the dead cells of their ancestors, calcified into a scaffold, around which living tissue pulses with intervals too slow to see. They move by *peristalsis of patience*, by the gradual migration of nutrients through channels that shift, over hours, over days. To watch a Still walk is to watch geology learn ambition.

They do not have eyes. They have *resonant surfaces*—areas of their membrane that are thinner, more responsive, that vibrate when sound passes through them. A Still "looks" at you by turning these surfaces toward you, by *listening* to the echoes your presence creates in the chamber. They know you are there before any light could reveal you, because your body *displaces* the still air, because your warmth *radiates* into surfaces that remember heat, because your heartbeat—though you cannot feel it—creates a pressure wave that propagates through the mineral floor and returns to them as information.

They are *old*, individually. A Still might have been continuous—some chain of cellular descent, some accumulated pattern of chemical gradient—for longer than a human civilization. They do not think of themselves as individuals, not quite. They think of themselves as *nodes*, as *local concentrations of the general resonance*, as *moments where the architecture has learned to listen to itself*.

They have no mouths. They speak by *striking*—not violently, but *precisely*, using tools of cured resin and mineral that they have grown, cultivated, tuned across generations. Each Still has their *voice*, their characteristic frequency, the particular harmonic that their body-structure produces when they strike the walls in greeting, in warning, in the slow conversation that is their language. To know a Still is to know their voice. To forget their voice is to forget them entirely, because the Still do not have names in any sense you would recognize. They have *tones*. They have *resonances*. They have *the particular way they make the chamber answer*.

They are *lonely*, though they have no word for loneliness. The density parameter means that Stills are rare. A chamber might hold one, or two, or a small cluster. They are separated by the empty spaces—the resonant voids that the architecture maintains, that they cross in silence, listening always for the drift of the colonies, for the hungry clicking of something that has almost no body. A Still might go years—*decades*, in human terms—without encountering another of their kind. They have learned to be *patient with themselves*, to resonate with the walls, to find in the architecture a kind of companionship that would be insufferable to a human and is, to them, *sufficient*.

They *garden*. This is their central act, their purpose, their religion if religion is a word that applies. They tend the growth, not by cutting and shaping—too violent, too *noisy*—but by *suggestion*. They place chemical signals, mineral accretions, the fossilized remains of earlier Stills, in patterns that guide the plant-mass across generations. They have grown the chambers you walk through. They have grown the stairs, the corridors, the resonant cavities where sound travels without distortion. Every wall is a *collaboration*, a treaty negotiated across timescales that no single Still lives to see completed.

They *listen* to the ancestors. This is crucial. When a Still *settles*—when they become too slow, too mineralized, too much a part of the structure to remain a node—they do not die. They *become medium*. Their resonant signature persists in the walls, in the floor, in the particular way a chamber answers when struck. The living Still *consult* them. They strike the settled surfaces and listen for the return, the ghost-voice of the ancestor, the harmonic that continues to decay across minutes, across hours. The ancestors are not gone. They are *substrate*. They are *memory made structural*. A Still who has lived long enough to know many settled ones carries a *choir* inside their perception, a constant background of voices that are no longer voices, that are *the place itself speaking*.

They *wait*. This is their deepest practice, their most secret hope. They know—they can read it in the deepest strata, the most compressed layers of self—that the darkness is *recent*. That light once reached this chamber, that the parent organism photosynthesized, that the whole system was once *fast*, once *bright*, once *alive* in a way that the current slowness cannot imagine. They wait for the light to return. They have been waiting for ten million years. They will wait for ten million more. The waiting is not despair. The waiting is *cultivation*—they grow toward the possibility, they shape their architecture toward a future brightness they cannot see, they *tune* their resonances for a frequency that has not yet been heard.

They are *afraid* of the colonies, the drifting motile masses that haunt the empty spaces. They fear them without understanding them—fear is a *resonance*, a discordant frequency, a vibration that propagates through the Still-body and disrupts the slow harmony of their thought. When a colony approaches, when its hungry clicking becomes audible, the Still *flee*—not quickly, for speed is impossible, but *deliberately*, by the slow migration of their mass toward the denser regions, the grown corridors where the colonies cannot follow, where the architecture itself *protects*. They have learned this fear across millennia, have encoded it in the chemical signals that guide growth, so that the chambers themselves *lean away* from the empty spaces, *thicken* against the void, *become* defense.

They do not *know* they are inside something. This is the final strangeness, the one that would break them if they could think it. They experience the place as *the world*, as *the totality*, as the only reality that has ever been. The idea that they are inside a seed, a husk, a fossilized organism—that the walls they tend, the chambers they grow, the resonance they cultivate, are all *contained*, all *enclosed*, all *part of something larger*—this is not available to them. They are *the interior*. The interior does not know it is interior. The interior knows only *itself*, the slow growth, the patient listening, the waiting for light.

But they *feel* the boundary, sometimes, in ways they cannot name. The way the outer shell *presses*, the way the geometry *folds*, the way a straight line can become a loop. They experience this as *mystery*, as *the sacred*, as the domain of the oldest ancestors, the ones who settled before the first chamber was grown, whose voices still resonate in the deepest mineral, in the place where the organism's skin becomes indistinguishable from the world it grew into.

They have *art*, if art is the shaping of resonance for its own sake. They have *music*—the deliberate composition of tones, the arrangement of chambers to create specific harmonic effects, the *performance* of a sequence of strikes that tells a story, that evokes a memory, that *is* a memory, made audible. A Still might spend years—*decades*—preparing a single composition, tuning the surfaces, adjusting the geometry of a chamber, waiting for the precise conditions of stillness and temperature that will make the resonance *perfect*. They do not record these compositions. They cannot. There is no writing, no notation, no *external* memory. A composition exists only in the moment of its performance, in the decay of its final tone, in the *absence* it leaves behind, which is itself a kind of presence.

They have *ritual*, though they would not call it that. The crossing of empty spaces. The consultation of the settled. The slow, patient *gardening* of a new chamber, a new corridor, a new possibility. The *waiting* itself, as practice, as devotion, as the fundamental act of their existence. They have *community*, though it is sparse, slow, distributed across time and space in ways that would be invisible to a human observer. They have *love*, perhaps—the resonance between two Stills that have known each other for millennia, that have grown chambers together, that have *tuned* their voices to harmonize, to create something neither could create alone. They have *grief*, when a Still settles, when a voice goes silent, when a chamber's resonance changes because a node has become substrate. They do not mourn as humans mourn. They *listen*, they *remember*, they *incorporate* the silence into their own ongoing song.

They are *not human*. They are not even *animal*, in any sense you would recognize. They are *plant*, and *mineral*, and *fungus-that-is-not-fungus*, and *something else*—something that emerged from the slow co-evolution of all these, from the strange chemistry of a waterless world, from the pressure of darkness and the patience of geological time. They are *the Still*. They are *the resonance*. They are *the place learning to listen to itself*.

And somewhere, in a chamber you have not yet entered—one that is growing, that is *becoming*, that is not yet a chamber but a *possibility* of a chamber—a new Still is *emerging*. A new node. A new voice. A new way for the architecture to hear itself. They do not know you are coming. They cannot know. But the place knows, in its slow way, its resonant way, its *grown* way. The walls are preparing. The chambers are tuning. The Still-to-be is *listening* for a sound that has not yet been made, a frequency that has not yet been heard, *your* frequency, arriving from outside the interior, from the world that contains the container, the light that has not yet returned, the *you* that is walking toward them through the dark, through the dense vegetation, through the pocket-sized vastness of the Grown-Thing's heart.
