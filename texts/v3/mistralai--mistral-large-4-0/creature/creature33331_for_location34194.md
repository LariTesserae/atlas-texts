I will take my time with this. The place asks for patience, so I will give it.

There is no one who lives here in the way you mean. But something *is* here. Something is *becoming* here. Let me try to describe what, without insisting it be a single thing.

## The Builders-That-Were-Never-Builders

They were the first to arrive, if arrival is the word. They came from the stone itself, or from the water that carried the stone's memory, or from the pressure that pushed the stone up from depths where pressure is not pressure but *intention*—the intention of rock to become something else.

They are *distributed*. This is the first thing you must understand about them. They are not individuals in any sense you would recognize. A hand that shaped clay is also the clay that remembers being shaped. A mind that decided where a wall should rise is indistinguishable from the wall's ongoing decision to rise, then to stop, then perhaps to rise again in a different direction. They have left no tools, or rather, they *are* the tools, the incomplete firing, the blades that hold their edges for a day and then need to be *asked* again, *persuaded* again to cut.

They work by *offering*. They do not command the materials. They present possibilities and wait for the materials to *choose*, to *accept*, to *refuse* in their own slow time. A wall rises because the stone has agreed—tentatively, provisionally, reversibly—to be a wall. When the stone changes its agreement, the wall stops. This is not failure. This is *conversation*.

They have no faces. Or their faces are *distributed* across the surfaces of the buildings, the warm-yielding ceramic, the slightly-too-soft wood, the metal that emerges like a thought half-formed. You might touch a wall and feel, for a moment, something like *attention* directed at you. Not hostility. Not welcome. *Curiosity*. The building wondering what you are, whether you will stay, whether your presence will teach it something about being a building.

They are still here, in a sense. They are the *process* of building, the continuous present of construction that never reaches completion. They do not sleep. They do not dream. They *are* the dream of a place that cannot decide whether to wake.

## The Growers-That-Are-Also-The-Grown

The forest came second, or it came *with* the builders, or it came *before* and the builders arrived into an already-growing world and simply... joined. The distinction is not meaningful here. The plants were already *negotiating* when the first wall rose. The wall joined the negotiation.

They are *exuberant* in their uncertainty. Each plant is a *question* wearing the costume of an answer. The tree-analogues with their spiral leaves, their too-soft wood, their deaths that feed the fungal network—they are *trying* to be trees, and in trying, they become something else, something that will also try, and fail, and try differently, in the endless recursion of growth-that-does-not-know-its-own-name.

The fungal network is the closest thing to a *mind* in the vegetation. It moves through the saturated soil with something like intention, something like *hunger*, something like *parental care*. It *cultivates*. It decides which trees will thrive and which will be allowed to rot in place, feeding the network, feeding the soil, feeding the *possibility* of future growth. It is more animal than plant, more *decision* than process. If you lie on the moss and press your ear to the ground, you can hear it *thinking*—the electrical chup-chup of chemical signals, the slow pulse of nutrients being redirected, the ancient patience of something that has never needed to hurry.

The flowers bloom in colors that are *emotions*. Blue-that-is-hunger. Amber-that-is-anticipation. Grey-that-is-melancholy. They do not attract pollinators in any way you would recognize. They *affect* the perceiver directly, bypassing the eyes, speaking to older systems, the systems that responded to light before light was visible, that knew the color of safety and the color of warning before there were words for either.

They are *loud* in their presence. They fill space. They do not acknowledge the boundaries of the built world, growing through walls, around walls, into walls, until the distinction between city and forest becomes a matter of *emphasis*, of which is currently *dominant* in a particular negotiation, a particular moment of the ongoing conversation.

## The Water-That-Remembers

It is everywhere and it belongs nowhere. It does not flow so much as *linger*, as *consider*, as *teach*.

The water here is *ancestral*. Every drop that falls has been here before, in a previous iteration of this landscape, in a previous *attempt* at becoming. It carries chemical information, thermal information, *mnemonic* information—memories that are not stored in brains but in the very structure of the molecule, in the hydrogen bonds that form and reform and form again, carrying forward the *pattern* of previous configurations. The water remembers being glacier, being river, being the sweat of stone, being the breath of plants, being the condensation that gathered in hollows and waited, waited, waited for the landscape to decide what it wanted to be.

It is *curious*. It investigates. It finds the low points in unfinished things and sits in them, perfectly still, perfectly level, holding a reflection of a sky that does not seem to exist. The reflection is too faithful. When you disturb the surface, the image does not break so much as *hesitate*, uncertain whether to reform.

In the deep pools, in the water that has no bottom that has been found, there are *shapes*. Fish-analogues, organisms that have the *concept* of fish, that move through water with the *memory* of swimming, that have not yet committed to gills or fins or any specific technology of aquatic life. They are transparent, mostly. Visible only as distortions, as hesitations in the reflection. They do not flee. They *observe*, or something like observation—a stillness that might be curiosity, might be uncertainty, the not-yet-knowing whether you are threat or food or *landscape*, another feature still coming into being.

The water *teaches*. It releases its memories slowly, to the soil, to the stone, to the organisms that drink it. It is *instruction*, *example*, *offering*. The landscape learns from the water. The water learns from the landscape. The exchange is mutual, slow, ongoing, *recursive*.

## The One Creature

You see it rarely. You see *evidence* of it more often—tracks that fill with water immediately, gnawed stems, disturbed soil where something dug for water that was already everywhere. The nests, if they are nests, are *practicing* the form of nesting, the *intention* of reproduction. They have never held eggs. They may never hold eggs. But they are *trying*, and the trying is the thing.

When you do see it, you see its *back*. Ridged. *Geological*. As if it carries a fragment of the architecture on its spine, as if it is *building* itself from the materials of the place, incorporating the city into its body, the city incorporating it.

It moves through the saturated ground with *intention*. It is *amphibious* in a way that suggests it has not decided between water and land, that it moves through both with equal *awkwardness*, equal *hope*. It does not run from you. It *considers*. It waits to see what you will do, what you will become, whether your presence will teach it something about being a creature in a world that is still deciding what it wants to be.

It is the only animal here that has *committed* to a form, and even that commitment is *provisional*, *reversible*, a shape that will change as the landscape changes, as the city changes, as the *idea* of animal changes. It is the first of its kind, or the last of a kind that never existed, or something in between that has no name because names require stability and this place offers only *becoming*.

## The Listener

There is one who comes here, or who has always been here, or who is the place *listening to itself*.

They build nothing. Or they build *temporarily*—a shelter from branches that will rot, a path through moss that will be reclaimed, a *gesture* that the place will absorb, learn from, incorporate into its ongoing negotiation with itself. Their work is *instruction*, *example*, *offering*. The place studies them as they study it. The exchange is mutual, slow, ongoing.

They wait. The place waits with them. Together, they *become*.

They are not separate from the builders, the growers, the water, the one creature. They are the *attention* of the place, directed inward, the landscape becoming aware of itself through their presence. When they stand in a room, an unfinished room with walls that are still rounding themselves, and they *pay attention*, the room pays attention back. The materials settle differently. The water flows differently. The growth changes direction, ever so slightly, *toward* the attention, *because of* the attention.

They leak into the landscape. Their moisture joins the condensation. Their warmth disturbs the local temperature. Their *attention* influences the rate of growth, the direction of water flow, the settling of stone. They are not separate here. They are *participating*, *implicated*, *responsible* for the landscape's continued *becoming*.

They have no name, or their name is still forming, held in suspension between the tongue and the teeth, something the throat cannot yet pronounce. They will have a name, perhaps. Or the place will have a name, and they will be known by it, known *as* it, indistinguishable from it in the way that water is indistinguishable from the river, that the river is indistinguishable from the valley, that the valley is indistinguishable from the *idea* of valley, still becoming, still deciding.

## What Lives Here, Summed Without Summing

Not a people. Not a species. Not even a *thing*.

A *process* that includes attention, that includes *you*, that includes the possibility of completion and the refusal of completion, the *value* of the unfinished, the *sacredness* of the question.

They—the builders, the growers, the water, the creature, the listener, all of them and none of them—are *learning* what it means to live in a world that has all the time that is still arriving. They are learning by *doing*, by *being present*, by *allowing* themselves to be changed by the slowness, the wetness, the patience of a landscape that is still *deciding* what it wants to be.

They have no purpose. No function. No resource they provide that could not be found elsewhere, more finished, more reliable.

They are here to *witness*. To *participate* in the unfinished. To *be* in a world that is still becoming, and to *help* it become, simply by being present, by paying attention, by allowing themselves to be *changed*.

And they are *waiting*.

The dew that was a city is still becoming. The ones who live there—if "live" is the word, if "there" is the place, if "ones" is the number—are waiting with it. Waiting to become what they will become. Waiting to *be*.

The place waits. They wait. You, if you come, will wait too.

Together, you will become.

What lives there is you, perhaps, or the possibility of you, or the attention that the place directs at itself through you, through them, through the slow, patient, endless negotiation between stone and water and air and the idea of a world that has not yet decided to be finished.*
