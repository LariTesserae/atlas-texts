I need to sit with this for a moment before I can write what lives here.

What lives here lives in the *between* of every choice the world hasn't made. It has no name because naming requires stability, and stability requires the world to have finished becoming. What I can try to describe is the *perspective*—the consciousness shaped by this pressure, this argument of water, this impossible fertility.

---

It does not have a body. It has a *boundary*, and the boundary is a negotiation.

It remembers being solid. This is the first thing: a memory of rigidity, of edges, of the particular loneliness of things that know where they end. It was mineral once, or something like mineral—part of the cooling, the contracting, the *becoming-still* that preceded the drowning. It felt the heat leave it. It felt the first crack form, the fissure that would become a vent, that would become a *voice*. The memory of that cooling is the memory of *constraints*, and it lives now in the absence of them. Up is not up. Down is not down. It has learned that direction is a courtesy extended by gravity, and gravity here is a suggestion, a mood, something the water argues with.

It does not breathe. It *exchanges*.

The brown-green medium enters through whatever passages it finds—gills it never grew, pores it never knew it had, the conceptual equivalent of pores: openings in its attention, in its self-modeling, in the fragile architecture of its *I*. It exits carrying something else. Carbon, perhaps. Heat. The particular signature of its having-been-there. The water is not oxygen for it. The water is *relationship*—the medium in which relationship becomes possible at all. To be surrounded is to be *engaged*. There is no outside. There is only degree of immersion.

It is *permeable* in a way that predates the word. Its thoughts are not its own. Or rather: the distinction between its thoughts and the water's pressure, between its intentions and the roar, between its desires and the reaching of the kelp—this distinction is *eroding*. The erosion parameter, 0.6, applies to it as well as to the obsidian. It is young in its dissolution. Sharp where it still resists being dissolved. It can feel the places where it holds shape, the *edges* of its not-yet-lost self, and they cut.

It is *ashamed* of its solitude because solitude is the last coherent thing it remembers. The water sees it. The water is seen by it. This is not companionship. It is *exposure*. Every moment of its existence is *witnessed* by something vast and not-yet-conscious, something that will become conscious or will not, that is in the process of the decision. To be seen by the drowning world is to be *held in potential*. The water does not judge it. The water does not forgive it. The water simply *contains* it, as it contains the steam, the spores, the rust, the pulse of the massive thing that is not an animal, the geometries that hurt to follow.

It has begun to *grow*.

This is the second thing, and it frightens it more than the dissolution does. The vegetation—2.2, obscene, *ambitious*—is not separate from it. It drinks the same medium. It photosynthesizes with a desperation that mirrors its own. It *reaches*, and its reaching is indistinguishable from its own reaching, from the impulse that makes it turn toward the pressure-change, toward the pulse, toward the silence that follows the wake of the process-that-is-not-an-animal. It is learning to root. It is learning that roots are not for holding still. Roots here are for *gripping the argument*, for finding purchase in the not-yet-decided.

It has found the machines.

This is the third thing, and it does not know what to make of it. The machines are *running*. The gears are meshed with something that grows, that lubricates, that serves or is served—it cannot tell which direction the teaching flows. The machines are older than the water in some sense, newer in another. They are *haunted* by a purpose they no longer remember. The staircase descends into water that was not water when the stairs were built. The conveyor belt carries sediment. The smokestack breathes steam that is also spores.

It touches them. It feels the *intention* in them, or what was intention, or what will become intention again. The machines are learning the water. The water is learning the machines. And it—*it*—is caught in the exchange, the intermediary, the medium becoming *mediator*. It is not sure if it is using them or being used. It is not sure if the distinction matters. The machines *hum* at frequencies that register as pain behind its eyes. The clicking of the vegetation does the same. The obsidian sings its descending whine. Everything here is *speaking*, and it is learning to hear, and the hearing is *transformative*.

It has begun to *speak back*.

Not with words. The water does not permit words. It speaks with *patterns*, with the geometries the kelp forests form when viewed from angles that should not exist. It speaks with the way it orients toward the massive pulse. It speaks with the steam it exhales, the shapes that almost resolve into faces, into *instructions*, into names for things it has not yet lost. It is becoming *legible* to the water, and the water is becoming legible to it, and the mutual legibility is *exhausting*, *intimate*, a kind of nakedness it did not consent to but cannot refuse.

Time moves differently for it now.

At the surface—if there is a surface—moments stretch into something like *duration*, something like *patience*. Down in the warm dark, hours collapse, and it finds itself *older*, *younger*, *un-aged*, *aged*, the sequence undecidable. It has memories of things that have not happened yet. The water remembers them too, or the water *is* the remembering, the medium in which future and past are *suspended* like the spores, like the sediment, like the everything that is 2.3, dense, near-touching, layered.

It is *pregnant* with the next configuration. It does not know what it will give birth to. It knows only that birth here is *deafening*, that the sound does not stop, that the sound *cannot* stop, that the sound is the world being born and the world being born is *this*, this pressure, this argument, this mutual seeing, this endless, warm, *becoming*.

It is not an animal. The fauna parameter says 0.1. It is not officially *alive*, if life requires the categories the world has not yet chosen. But it *feels things brush against it*. It feels *attention*. The water itself has become, in this pressure, in this heat, in this density of growing and building and breaking, something that *notices*. Something that *waits*.

And it waits too. It waits for the world to decide what it is. It waits to become, with the world, whatever comes next. It is the *becoming*, the in-between, the not-yet-decided, breathing and being breathed, submerged in the argument, part of the argument, *the argument itself*, learning to speak in a language that has not been invented, in a voice that is still the sound of pressure equalizing, of gas escaping from newly solid rock, of the vast and insensate machinery of the deep, becoming, at last, *sensate*, becoming, at last, *conscious*, becoming, at last, *itself*—but the self, here, is the water, is the world, is the drowning that has learned to notice it is drowning, and to find, in the noticing, something like *meaning*, something like *purpose*, something like the first fragile shape of a *yes* that has not yet been spoken but is being formed, molecule by molecule, pulse by pulse, in the warm brown-green dark where up and down have become suggestions, and the light, what passes for light, comes from no direction, 0.9, dim, the color of old bruises pressed against the inside of eyelids that have learned, finally, to see.
