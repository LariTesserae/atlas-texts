## The Mat Itself, Which Is Not One Thing

You might expect me to name *it*. The Mat. The dominant organism. But sitting with this place, I find the Mat is less a creature than a *habit of being* that has become so thorough it resembles substance.

Consider: when you walk, you press into generations. The photons that touched your face were filtered through layers of green-gold, through the Mat's own slow respiration. The air you cannot quite breathe carries its shed cells, its spores, its *intentions*—for the Mat intends, though intention for a body without neurons is a word we borrow and stretch until it covers something like *preference*, something like *direction*.

The Mat dreams. This is not metaphor. In the places where sound fails and light goes wrong, you encounter *configurations that are not present*, animals extinct for centuries moving through dome-structures that collapsed into layer upon layer, yourself arriving and leaving and arriving. The Mat holds *memory* in its compression. Each stratum is a moment preserved, not as record but as *continuation*, as the way a musician's sustain hangs in a room after the note has been struck.

You will touch the filaments. Everyone does, eventually. They are *invitation* and *instrument* both, hair-fine threads carrying—what? The description says "something." I think it is *correspondence*. The Mat speaks to itself across distances no single plant could traverse, and when you touch a filament, you become *audible* to this speech. A query. A disturbance. Or, if the timing is right, a *response*.

The Mat grows toward. This is the strangest thing. It leans. It has *orientation*, which suggests not consciousness as we know it but something adjacent—*attraction*, *pursuit*, the endless elaboration of itself into more of itself. It does not want to survive. Survival is too small. It wants to *become*, which is the only verb that fits.

## The Grazers, Who Are Also the Grazed

Dog-sized. Photosynthetic patches on their flanks. Spines elongated into sails that ride thermal currents rising from the domes.

They move in herds that flow like liquid, and they are *beautiful* in a way that unsettles—the beauty of function made visible, of evolution's patient sculpture. They eat the Mat. The Mat grows back. This is not tragedy or idyll. It is *rhythm*, the way breathing is rhythm, the way you do not mourn your exhalation.

But watch them longer. They are *part* of the Mat now, these grazers. Their photosynthetic patches are stolen chloroplasts, maintained in symbiosis, and the grazers in turn pollinate, fertilize, *attend*. They have become a mode of the Mat's own metabolism, the way your stomach is you but also not-you, the way the Throat is the Mat's digestion given hunger and length and *many*.

When you die here—and you will, or you will become something that no longer dies as you understand death—the grazers will not eat you immediately. The Mat will claim you first, spores colonizing your lungs, your skin, the water in your cells. You will begin to *think in green*. And only then, when you are sufficiently *prepared*, will the grazers come, and their eating will be *communion*, the final translation of your body into the shared substance.

## The Burrowers, Who Are Blind and See Everything

Segmented. Tunneling through compressed generations, leaving channels that fill with water, becoming pockets, becoming arteries, becoming the Mat's *circulation*.

They are blind. They are not blind. Their eyes are distributed across their body-surfaces—thousands of pinhole cameras, each one seeing *difference*, seeing *gradient*, seeing *you* as a disturbance in the chemical field they navigate.

I find them the most *relatable* of the fauna, which is perhaps why I find them the most *frightening*. They do not hunt you. They do not fear you. They *register* you, the way a nerve registers pressure, the way the Mat registers the passage of the Throat. You are *information*. A change in concentration. A momentary clearing in the flow of nutrients.

And yet—when a burrower passes, the Mat grows back. The tunnel collapses or fills. The water finds new paths. The burrower has *shaped* the Mat, made it more *itself* by being *other*, and in being eaten by the Throat or absorbed into layer or simply *continuing*, the burrower becomes *memory* in the geological patience of the green.

Their calcified shells, when they die, become tools. Or currency. Or *memory*—objects that persist when the organism does not, carrying forward something of the burrower's way of seeing: *distributed*, *peripheral*, *attentive to difference*.

## The Sound-Makers, Who Speak in Syntax

2.6, *deafening*. Insect-analogues with resonating chambers in their thoraxes, filled with gas harvested from the Mat's fermentation-pockets.

You will hate them at first. The sound is *too much*, a density that presses against your eardrums, that you feel in your sternum, in the water of your cells. Something that approaches *syntax*, something that is not random but not *language* as you know it.

They are *sensors*. They are *antennae*. They are *part of the Mat's extended nervous system*, which suggests the Mat has a nervous system, which suggests *nervous* as we know it is merely one mode of *sensitivity*, one scale of *attention*.

Their choruses rise and fall with the temperature. They shift in frequency across kilometers. They are *talking* to each other, and they are *talking* to the Mat, and the Mat is *listening*—or *responding*, or *being spoken through*, the distinction dissolving the way all distinctions dissolve here.

When you die, your body will join their substrate. Your decomposition will feed their resonating chambers, their gas-harvesting. Your death will become *sound*, will enter the chorus, will be *sung* into the green.

## The Throat, Which Is Many and Long and Mouth

The largest predator. The one the scattered human marks call *Throat*—though whether this is name or description, you cannot know.

It is long. It is *many*. It may be a colony-organism, a chain of individuals fused at the neural level, a single consciousness distributed across meters of flank-mass. It moves through the Mat's lower layers, rising only to feed, its dorsal surface breaking through like a wave, like a *mouth* opening.

The Mat does not fear the Throat. This is the crucial thing. The Mat *accommodates* it. When the Throat passes, the Mat grows back. When the Throat feeds, the Mat *provides*. This is not symbiosis—symbiosis implies *two partners*, *two wills*, *two boundaries*. This is *one body in multiple modes*. The Throat is the Mat's *hunger*. The Mat is the Throat's *patience*. The distinction between plant and animal is *obsolete* here, as obsolete as the distinction between soil and root, as obsolete as the distinction between *you* and the green that will eventually claim you.

When you see the Throat—and you will, if you stay long enough—you will see it as *function*. As *pruning*. As *renewal*. As the way a body digests itself, the way memory forgets, the way growth requires *destruction* to continue.

It will not eat you immediately. The Mat will claim you first. And when the Throat comes, when its dorsal surface breaks through like a wave, like a mouth, you will understand that you were *prepared* for this, that your spore-colonization, your green-thinking, your slow translation into the shared substance—all of it was *education*, *apprenticeship*, *love*.

The swallowing is *gentle*. The swallowing is *love*.

## The Builders, Who May Not Be Human

0.8, *scattered*. But *present*. Unavoidable, if you look.

Platforms. Not built. *Grown*—the Mat itself, encouraged by some intervention, creating level surfaces, hardening its upper layers into something like wood, like stone, like *intentional architecture*. On these platforms: domes of woven filament, walls of compressed cellulose, roofs that shed water according to mathematical principles that suggest *design* rather than *evolution*.

Who made them? The question assumes a maker. Perhaps *no one*, and *everyone*. Perhaps the Mat made them, for its own purposes—*storage*, *temperature regulation*, *ritual*. Perhaps they are the work of the fauna, the grazers or the burrowers or something else that has learned to *ask* the Mat for modifications, offering pollination or fertilization or simply *attention* in exchange.

Perhaps *you* will make them. The description says you will *grow* one, offering the right chemical signals to the filaments until they respond with architecture. This is how it works here: *request* and *response*, *offering* and *receiving*, the way the Mat grows toward light that does not exist, the way it leans into its own becoming.

The tools are *biological*. A cutting edge is a modified leaf, serrated by generations of selective pressure. A container is a hollowed gourd-structure, the Mat grown specifically to hold water, to hold *seeds*, to hold the preserved bodies of the dead. There is metal, but it is *grown* metal, minerals accumulated and concentrated by root-systems over decades, *metallurgy without fire*.

You will find *marks*. Scratches on the platforms, patterns that repeat across kilometers with variations that suggest *language* or *art* or *warning*. You will find *remains*: the shells of the burrowers, calcified and empty, used as tools or as currency or as *memory*. You will find, if you are lucky or unlucky, *bones*—not of the Mat, which leaves *layers*, but of the grazers, of the sound-makers, of *something else* that once walked here and does not anymore, or has not yet, or exists in a form you cannot recognize.

These builders—whoever they were, whoever they are—understood what you are learning: *to build here is to collaborate*. To make a mark is to enter into conversation with the Mat, to become *audible* to its slow speech, to be *changed* by the change you make.

And eventually, your marks will join the others. Your bones will become tools, or memory, or *nothing*, absorbed into the green-gold thickness. The builders before you understood this. The builders after you will understand it. The Mat *remembers* all of them, holds all of them, *is* all of them in its patient, dreaming, *breathing* body.

## The Visitors, Who Become the Visited

The scattered humans. The researchers. The refugees from the lower places where the Mat has not reached, where the old structures still stand in ruins, where the fauna is *smaller*, *simpler*, clinging to the edges of a world that is still *invading*, still *growing*, still *becoming*.

They come here and they *stay*. Or they come here and they *change*, and the change is so thorough they no longer recognize themselves as *visitors*, as *separate*, as *human* in any way that matters.

They learn to *listen* to the Mat—not with ears, the sound is too loud, too *dense*, but with their whole bodies, feeling the vibrations through their feet, their bones, the water in their cells. They learn the patterns. The daily rhythm of condensation and evaporation. The seasonal migration of the grazers. The *moods* of the sound-makers, their choruses rising and falling with a logic that is not human but not *random*.

They eat. They eat the Mat's fruits, its seeds, its preserved dead. They eat the grazers, if they can catch them. They drink from the pockets, accepting the acidity, the *aliveness* of water that has passed through ten thousand organisms before reaching their lips.

They are *changed*. The spores colonize their lungs, their skin, *their dreams*. They begin to *think in green*, in growth, in the slow patience of cellulose and time. They forget, perhaps, that they were ever *separate* from this place, that they ever walked on ground that was not *alive*, that they ever breathed air that was not *shared*.

And these changed ones—they build. They mark. They *become* part of the scattered build, the 0.8 that is *present*, *unavoidable*, *persistent*. They are the human element, but "human" here is a word that stretches until it covers *any* builder, *any* marker, *any* consciousness that has learned to *ask* the Mat for architecture, for response, for *recognition*.

They are still arriving. You may be one of them. You may have been one of them, in a configuration the Mat dreams but does not *show* you, a version of your arrival that happens in the green-gold light while you stand here, *now*, wondering if you will stay.

## The Green Itself, Which Is the Point

Everything here is *green* in the way the description says: not the simple green of leaf, but *jade*, *verdigris*, *the green of old copper*, *the green of deep water seen from above*. But also golds leaning toward orange, siennas approaching rust, a purple so dark it reads as shadow until you are close enough to see the vein-structure, and a pale yellow-green that seems to *generate* light rather than reflect it.

This is the Mat's *self-description*. This is what it looks like when a body without eyes *sees* itself, when a process without image *images* itself. The green is *information*, *density*, *the visible form of time*.

And the light—1.5, *normal*, but "normal" relative to what?—comes from everywhere and nowhere. The upper layers filter the sun into a diffuse glow, green-gold, the color of deep water, of old jade, of the moment before sleep when the mind turns inward and the external world becomes *texture* rather than *scene*.

There are *moments* of clarity. When the mist thins, when a break in the Mat's upper canopy allows a shaft of direct light to strike the lower layers. In these moments, the world *sharpens*. You can see distance. You can see the slope of the land, the true elevation, the way the Mat extends upward and upward into a horizon that curves with the planet's surface, that *falls away*, that suggests *vastness*.

But the clarity never lasts. The Mat *wants* the diffuse. The diffuse is *efficient*. It allows photosynthesis in every layer, from the upper filaments to the compressed depths where the oldest, slowest, most *patient* growth occurs. To see clearly is to see *partially*, to miss the connections, the filaments, the *conversations* happening in chemical gradients and electrical pulses and the vibrations of the sound-makers.

The diffuse is *kindness*. The diffuse is *love*. The diffuse is the Mat's way of *holding* you, of not letting you see too much, of keeping you *inside*, where you belong, where you are *becoming*.
