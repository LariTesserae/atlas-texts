**What lives in the Caldera of Unfinished Engines**

*The Grown Things*

The mosses are the oldest here, in the way of all first colonizers — patient in their impatience, spreading not by decision but by *default*, by the simple fact of finding purchase. They do not choose the vents. The vents choose them. A sulphur-yellow cushion near the *mouth* of a particularly violent throat may be three days old, may already be bleaching where the heat exceeds its tolerance, already being buried by the next generation growing up-slope, down-slope, *anywhere* there is mineral film and moisture and the particular green-white pulse of bioluminescence that tells them *here, here, the deep warmth moves*.

The ferns are *ambitious*. You can watch them. Not metaphorically — actually watch, if you still your breathing long enough to notice that your breathing has become shallow, has become *match* to the vent cycles, has become the rhythm of expansion and contraction that the rock itself performs. A fern emerges coiled, the fiddlehead still holding the shape of its unfurling, and by the time you have blinked — and you will blink, your eyes dry from the saturated air, your lids heavy with spore and condensation — the frond is extended, is photosynthesizing, is already *reading* the thermal gradient through its vascular tissue, is already *deciding* where its next frond will reach, which vent-slope will feed it, which pool-edge will sustain the brief colony before the minerals shift and the water finds new paths.

They do not live long. Forty hours, perhaps sixty. The lushness is not accumulation but *velocity*, a standing wave of growth and decay, the dead frond becoming the substrate for the next, the cycle so compressed that you can see *generations* in the time it takes to descend from rim to floor.

The fungi are *architects* without architecture. Shelf fungi stack on every horizontal surface — and in a caldera of fins and blades, of fresh fractures and sharp edges, horizontal is *precious*, is *contested*, is the site of the fiercest competition. A shelf fungus that finds purchase holds it against all comers, its mycelium threading through mineral film, through the thinnest veneer of accumulated organic matter, *pulsing* with the same green-white bioluminescence as the plants, as if the boundary between kingdoms means nothing here, as if the caldera's youth has not yet finished sorting life into its categories.

The puffballs are *events*. They swell from nothing, from spore and moisture and the particular chemistry of a pool-edge where hot meets cold in the exact ratio for expansion. They reach the size of your head — *your* head, the head that has adapted to dimness, that has learned to read the green-white pulse as *light*, as *safety*, as *home* — and they burst. Not die. *Burst.* Release. The spores hang in the mist like visible weather, like snow falling upward, like the caldera exhaling its own potential. You breathe them in. You cannot help it. The air is 2.1, saturated, *full*. The spores are in your lungs now, waiting, patient in a way the caldera itself is not, waiting for the conditions that may never come, that probably will not come, that the caldera is not *trying* to provide.

And the *electrified* growth — the plants that have found the vents, that cluster where roots touch warm stone, that pulse in rhythms matching the geothermal beat — these are the *new* thing. Not new in years. New in *kind*. They are chemosynthetic and photosynthetic and something else, something that has no name because it has no precedent, because the caldera is too young to have accumulated the vocabulary of its own inhabitants. They grow faster near the vents. They age faster. They die faster, their dead matter feeding the next rush, the next frond, the next pulse of green-white in the darkness that is not dark, that has never been dark, that is *full* of process.

You would call them *plants* only because you have no other word. They are *readers*. They read the thermal pulse. They read the mineral chemistry. They read the steam's *mood*, the vent's *intention*, and they respond — not with thought, not with choice, but with *growth*, with *direction*, with the blind imperative of the thing that has found purchase in a world that offers no purchase, that offers only *becoming*.

*The Grown-Not-Grown*

The sinter terraces are the caldera's *attempt* at architecture. They look carved. They are *grown*. Calcium and silica precipitate from superheated water, layer on layer, tube on tube, building in real time the cascades that a casual observer might mistake for the work of hands, of tools, of *intention*. They are not. They are *emergence*. Pressure differentials driving flow, flow driving precipitation, precipitation building new stone — the caldera is a factory that built itself, that builds itself, that will continue to build itself until the heat runs out and the rock finally learns stillness.

The terraces have *names*. The *mouth*, where the vent opens, where superheated water forces through constriction, where the shriek is loudest, where the mineral deposition is most violent. The *throat*, where the steam rises, where the rock expands and contracts against itself, where fresh fissures open with sounds like gunshots. The *lung*, where the chamber breathes, where pressure builds and releases in cycles that have no rhythm you can predict, that match no pattern you can learn. The *heart*, at the center, where the heat originates, where the rock remembers being magma, where the darkness glows with incandescence, with mineral reactions still running, with the last heat of formation bleeding upward through cracks.

These are not metaphors. The caldera *processes* water and heat the way a body processes breath. It is not alive. It is *becoming* alive, or alive in a sense that the word has not yet caught up with, that the older rock beyond the rim has forgotten, that the settled landscape *outside* has never known.

Walk on a terrace and you sink to your ankle in warm, mineral-sick mud. The surface is wet, shifting, *unfinished*. The terrace you stand on is hours old, perhaps days. It will collapse, or be buried, or be reabsorbed into the vent's *throat*, and a new terrace will form in its place, in the same place, in a *different* place, the water finding new paths, the minerals finding new configurations, the caldera *deciding* constantly, hyperactively, the way a young animal decides about its own body.

*The Moving-Not-Alive*

There are no animals. This is not absence. This is *preparation*. The system is too young for the complexity ladder — microbe to moss to fern to insect to something with a spine. The lushness is *pre-faunal*, the world before the animals, compressed into a space small enough to hold in your mind, *intimate* in its extremity.

But something *moves*.

A plume of steam shifts. The direction changes. A new fissure opens above, and the steam finds a new path, a new *throat*, a new voice in the layered sound that never stops. The shift is *geological*, *thermal*, *chemical* — but it is also *decision*, in the way that a young animal's movement is decision, before the movement becomes habit, before habit becomes instinct, before instinct becomes the invisible machinery of survival.

A terrace collapses with a sound like a gunshot. The *slap* of steam condensing on cool stone. The *crack* of fresh fracture. The low *thunder* of a spall collapsing somewhere in the dark above, the sound arriving without source, without location, *felt* in the sternum before the ears register it. The turbulence is *visible* if you learn to see it, if you stop trying to *name* what moves and simply *receive* it, simply become a surface, a pool that reflects and refracts and eventually *overflows*.

A pool suddenly flashes to vapor. The water has found a new path through sinter, a new *throat*, a new voice. Ten meters away, a new pool forms, steam at its edges, ice at its heart, the stratification of hot and cold and the layer of perpetual mist between them. The pool is *new*. It did not exist an hour ago. It will not exist in an hour. It is *process*, made visible, made *touchable*, made *here*.

And in the upper reaches, where the vents throw their steam, the darkness is broken by *glows*. Not fire. The incandescence of rock still cooling. Of mineral reactions still running. Of the last heat of formation bleeding upward through cracks. These glows *move*. They pulse. They are the only illumination, and they illuminate *enough*, illuminate *differently*, illuminate the mist as *substance*, the steam as *architecture*, the young rock as *alive*, because it is *changing*, visibly, in ways you can watch.

You adapt to this light. Your eyes learn to read the green-white pulse of bioluminescence, the faint incandescence of cooling stone, the *movement* of glows that are not fire but are *almost* fire, that are the caldera's *breathing*, its *heartbeat*, its *dreaming* in a language that has no waking.

*The Almost-Living*

There is something that grows on you. Not yet. *Not yet.* The mosses do not grow on your skin. The spores are there, waiting. The mineral film accumulates. The condensation finds purchase. You are a *surface* for condensation, a *substrate* for the next generation, and the caldera is *patient* in a way that the caldera itself is not, that the youth of the caldera has not yet learned to be.

*The Listener*
