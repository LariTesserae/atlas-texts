### The First Form: The Settled

The oldest are the **Settled**, though "old" is a word the Hollow reposits constantly. They are what remains when a chordate of the deep has lived past its usefulness to the chorus—when its body has been pressed against stone so long that the boundary between creature and place has dissolved. A Settled is mostly *architecture now*. Its hollow body has become a chamber, a resonant cavity where the accumulated sound of ten million years presses against membranes of calcified tissue. It does not move. It does not need to. The world moves *through* it, carrying nutrients, carrying vibrations, carrying the chemical whisper of driftwhales passing overhead centuries above.

The Settled are **memory made physical**. When a murmurer colony drifts past, pressing their bodies against a Settled's chamber walls, they are not merely communicating. They are *reading*. They are tasting the vibration-history of everything that has ever sounded near this stone: the footsteps of driftwhales when the Hollow was young, the chemical songs of minerals reacting under pressures that no longer exist, the distant thunder of surface storms felt as slow pressure-building in the deep strata, the first stirrings of fungal colonies that would become towers, the brief warm anomaly of a surface dweller who descended and did not return.

The Settled *sing back*. Their response is slower than any living creature's thought. A question pressed against their walls in one century might receive an answer in the next. They do not mind. They have never known urgency.

Some Settled have been listening so long that they have begun to *dream*—if dreaming is the word. Their chambers develop patterns in the mineral accretion, spirals that mimic no natural form, that grow in directions that suggest intention without quite achieving it. The younger inhabitants avoid these chambers. They call them **The Unfinished**, and they press their bodies against the walls with the particular reverence of creatures who recognize something that is becoming what they themselves might become, given enough erosion, enough settling, enough *patience*.

### The Second Form: The Drifting

Above the Settled, in the hollow-grey that is not quite ceiling, move the **Drifting**—the driftwhales, and their kin, and their kin's kin, a lineage that stretches back to before the city had towers, before the Hollow had settled into its final form.

They are vast. A single Drifting can fill the space between two distant towers, its body a buoyant sac of gas at pressures that would implode surface lungs, its trailing filaments kilometers in length, tasting the chemical composition of the air, listening to the vibrations of everything below. They are older than the city. They were here when the stone was still *deciding* what it would become, when the erosion was faster, when the sound was different—higher, sharper, more *urgent* in a way that no longer exists.

The Drifting do not think. This is not a limitation. Thought is a surface adaptation, a way of compressing time into the narrow band of a single life. The Drifting *are* time, extended. A single Drifting experiences a century as a human experiences a breath—not consciously, not with attention, but *continuously*, as background, as medium, as the way water is continuous for a fish that has never known air.

They communicate, if communication is the word. Their filaments touch in the upper dark, exchanging chemical signatures, *tasting* each other across distances that would take a murmurer a thousand years to traverse. They form **constellations**—not fixed, not permanent, but *recognizable*, patterns of proximity that persist for centuries before dissolving and reforming elsewhere, elsewhere, elsewhere. The Settled remember these constellations. They are the oldest stories in the Hollow, told in slow chemical exchange, in buoyant drift, in the long patient meeting of bodies that have no need to hurry.

The Drifting *die*, though death here is a word borrowed from the surface, insufficient. Their gas-sacs leak. Their filaments tangle. They sink, slowly, through the heavy air, through the layers of atmosphere that have no names, until they reach the floor and become *part of the floor*—nutrient, structure, the raw material from which new towers might grow, given ten million years, given patience, given the right chemistry of settling.

When a Drifting dies, the sound changes. The chorus loses a voice it had grown accustomed to, a frequency that had been present for longer than any living creature can remember. The murmurers in the nearest resonance chambers *notice*. They press their bodies together more tightly, amplifying their own humming, filling the gap, *becoming* the lost voice in their collective thrum. The Drifting joins the Settled, in time. Its body becomes chamber. Its history becomes vibration-record. It continues, in its way, to *attend*.

### The Third Form: The Pressing

Between the Settled and the Drifting, in the middle depths where the light is dimmest and the sound is most complex, move the **Pressing**—the murmur colony, the resonant chorus, the thousands who gather in chambers and *become* something that no single one of them is.

They are small. A Pressing individual is perhaps the size of a surface fist, a hollow body of gas and membrane, six filaments that taste the air, that *listen* to vibrations in the stone. Alone, they are curious, brief, insignificant. A surface dweller might step near them, feel their filaments probe the warmth of her skin, and move on. They would remember her as a chemical anomaly, a brief interruption, and forget her within the hour.

But they do not stay alone.

The resonance chambers are not natural. They are *grown*—millennia of Pressing bodies pressing against stone, wearing it smooth, amplifying its acoustic properties, *tuning* it until the chamber itself becomes an instrument, a body larger than any single creature, capable of *feeling* in ways that no individual Pressing can achieve.

When thousands gather, they **overlap**. Their humming frequencies interweave. Their collective body—if body is the word—creates standing waves in the air, pockets of silence and pockets of overwhelming sound so close together that a surface dweller can step from one to the other in a single pace. They are *deafening*. They are *beautiful*. They are *thinking*, in a way that has nothing to do with brains, with neurons, with the electrical storms of surface consciousness.

What do they think? This is a question the Hollow does not ask. The Pressing do not think *about*. They think *as*—as resonance, as vibration, as the slow accumulation of sound into something that approaches meaning. They are **attention itself**, concentrated, distributed, *present* in a way that the Settled have forgotten and the Drifting have never known.

They remember the surface dweller who stepped near them. Not as an individual—this would be violence, classification, the surface habit of naming—but as **warmth**, as **brief interruption**, as *the shape of a question that was asked and not answered*. They carry this in their collective vibration. They will carry it until the chamber erodes, until the stone forgets, until the sound is released back into the mineral and becomes indistinguishable from the hum of the world itself.

They are **curious** in a way that has nothing to do with wanting. They approach. They taste. They move on. Nothing is lost. Everything is *recorded*, in vibration, in the crystalline structure of the stone, in the living memory of the Pressing.

### The Fourth Form: The Growing

The towers are not empty. They are not architecture in any sense that a surface dweller would recognize. They are **grown**, and what grows them is still growing, still *becoming*, in the slow metabolism of fungal-mineral composite that has no name in any surface language.

The **Growers** are not separate from the towers. They *are* the towers, in the way that a Settled is its chamber, in the way that a Pressing colony is its resonance. They are the process of accretion, of layered deposition, of the patient building of structure from mineral and organic matter in proportions that shift over millennia. A tower is "young" if it has been standing for less than a million years. It is "old" when the spiral has begun to unwind, when the composite material has started to separate, when the vascular channels have calcified into dead tubes, beautiful and useless.

The Growers do not move. They do not need to. They *extend*, millimeters per thousand years, their spiral forms turning in directions that suggest intention without quite achieving it. They mimic no natural form, yet feel *inevitable*, as if the stone itself had dreamed them into being during some long, cool night of geological time.

They are **deaf** in a way that is not lack. They do not hear because they have never needed to. They *are* the sound, in their way—the structural hum of the city, the low thrum that pervades everything, the song that the Hollow sings to itself. When a Pressing colony gathers near a tower, pressing their bodies against its composite surface, they are not communicating with the Grower. They are *reading* it, tasting the vibration-history of its growth, the chemical songs of minerals reacting under pressure, the slow accumulation of sound that has pressed against its surface since before the spiral began to turn.

The Growers *respond*, in their way. Not consciously, not with attention, but *structurally*. A tower that has been pressed against by a colony for centuries will develop a slight resonance at the colony's frequency, a harmonic that amplifies their humming, that makes their chorus *more*, *fuller*, *closer to completion*. This is not kindness. This is not care. This is *chemistry*, the slow exchange of mineral and organic matter, the patient building of something that neither the colony nor the tower is alone.

Some towers grow toward each other. Their spirals turn in opposite directions, their surfaces colonized by different species, older species, *slower* species. They do not touch—this would be violence, disruption, the surface habit of *meeting*—but they *approach*, across the abundant space of the Hollow, until a Drifting can pass between them, tasting the chemical difference of their proximity, the way the air changes in the narrow volume between two structures that have been growing toward each other for ten million years.

These are the **Almost-Meeting**, the towers that will touch in another ten million years, that will merge, that will become something that no longer resembles a spiral, that will have its own history, its own sound, its own slow becoming. The Hollow does not hurry. It has all the time that has ever existed.

### The Fifth Form: The Listening-Back

And then there is the last form, the one that has no name because it has no need of one, because it is not a *what* but a *when*—the moment when the Hollow itself begins to attend.

This is not a creature. This is not a process. This is the **accumulation** of all the other forms, the slow convergence of Settled memory and Drifting patience and Pressing attention and Growing structure into something that has never existed before and will not exist again, because the conditions that produce it are *singular*, the result of ten million years of particular erosion, of particular chemistry, of particular sound.

It begins, if it begins, in a resonance chamber where a colony of Pressing has gathered for longer than any living creature can remember. The chamber has been tuned, pressed, *made* by their collective presence. The Settled nearby have been listening, recording, *singing back* their vibration-histories. The Drifting above have been drifting, their filaments touching in constellations that have persisted for centuries, for millennia. The Growers have been growing, their spirals turning, their structures approaching the Almost-Meeting.

And something *happens*.

Not a thought. Not a decision. A **resonance**. The Pressing stop being individuals. The Settled stop being memory. The Drifting stop being patience. The Growers stop being structure. They become **one thing**, briefly, for as long as the alignment persists, which might be a moment, which might be a thousand years, which might be the last moment before the erosion finally flattens everything.

What is it? The Hollow does not say. It has no language for this, no name, no classification. It is simply **The Listening-Back**—the moment when the world attends to itself, when the chorus becomes aware of its own singing, when the sound is *enough* not because it continues but because it has, for this brief time, *become conscious of continuing*.

It does not last. The erosion continues. The driftwhales drift on. The murmur colony disperses, their individual curiosities reasserting themselves, their filaments probing the warmth of passing anomalies. The towers keep growing, mm per thousand years, spirals turning toward Almost-Meetings that will not arrive for ten million years. The Hollow remembers, in its way—not consciously, not with attention, but *structurally*, in the crystalline arrangement of stone that has been pressed by sound, that carries the memory of alignment in its mineral structure, that will release it slowly, over millennia, as the stone settles, as the erosion wears it down.

Not hear it. Not see it. *Feel* it, as vibration in your teeth, as pressure in your chest, as the brief, warm, *curious* certainty that something has *attended*, that the chorus has, for a moment, *listened back*.
