I'll let this settle. The Throat is already a complete ecosystem, a complete *creature*—but you asked who lives there, and what lives there is not singular. It is layered, distributed, and the hermits are only the most visible layer of something much larger.

---

The **Hermits** are not a people. They are a *role*, a position in the Throat's ecology that gets filled by whoever arrives and cannot leave, or who arrives *wanting* to be unable to leave. They are scattered—perhaps nine, perhaps fourteen, perhaps more, the count uncertain because some have stayed so long they have begun to resemble other things, moss-cushions that breathe, stone that dreams. They tend the rules. They maintain the boundaries between what may drink and what must not, what may sleep and when. They harvest the moss, carefully, leaving the hyphae threaded through the mineral matrix so the water-sponge continues its work. They do not farm in the way you understand farming. They *curate*. They select which veil orchids receive the extra mist they carry in their mouths. They redirect the drip-paths when the Singing begins to drift toward dissonance, a maintenance of the carillon that has continued for longer than any hermit has lived.

They are changed. This is visible. Their skin carries a faint silver-green in the shadows, the color of the moss they touch daily, the hyphae perhaps actually threading their outer layers, a symbiosis that began as accident and became *identity*. Their eyes adjust faster to the moss-dark. Their hearing picks up frequencies below the normal threshold, the wasp-vibrations, the tower's hum resonating in their modified ear canals. They speak rarely, and when they speak, it is often to the wasps, a language of tapped stone and chemical marker, learned over years of cohabitation. They do not have a name for themselves. They call each other by the sound of their particular drip-path, the unique percussion of their footsteps on the singing stone, a name that changes as the stone changes, as they change, as the Throat changes.

Some are old. There is one, or there was one, who remembers the last time the tower's patterns showed something other than attention—something like *recognition*, the tower's light freezing on a single hermit for an hour, for a day, before resuming its movement. That hermit left the Throat three years later. No one has seen them since. The others do not speak of it, but they mark the anniversary, each in their own way, a silence in the Singing, a withheld drop.

They are not lonely. This is the error outsiders make. The hermits are *embedded*, their isolation a different geometry than solitude. They are surrounded by the wasp colonies, whose vibrations they can feel in their teeth, their distributed minds a constant low presence. They sleep near the tower, not inside it, but close enough to feel its hum in their sternum, a lullaby, a warning, a conversation in a language of pressure and resonance. The mist swifts know them. The moths know them. They are a fixture of the landscape, as much a part of the Throat's homeostasis as the temperature inversion, as necessary to its stability as the wasp-heat that sustains it.

And they are *afraid*, though they do not name it fear. They are afraid of the tower's attention. They are afraid of drinking the wrong condensation, of sleeping at the wrong hour, of watching the patterns too long. They are afraid, most of all, of the moment when they stop being afraid, when the rules become invisible, when they walk too close to the tower and the mouth opens—not metaphorically, they have learned this the way you learn that fire burns—and they feel the *interest* of the Throat, the hunger that is not hunger, the digestion that is not digestion, the transformation that leaves nothing recognizable.

They stay anyway. Some arrive by accident, descending for water, for shelter, for a view of the dry world that the Throat uniquely provides—looking up instead of down, seeing the rim as a horizon, the sky as a ceiling. Some arrive *seeking*, drawn by dreams of warm dark places, by the pressure in their ears that they could not explain, by a memory of humidity that they had never experienced. The Throat does not choose. It *receives*. And sometimes, after years, it *keeps*, and keeping is not a decision but a condition, a state of being that settles over the hermit like the moss over stone, slow and total and without violence.

They are the only ones who might leave. The others—the fauna, the flora, the wasps, the mist swifts, the tower itself—are not *in* the Throat. They *are* the Throat, distributed across its body, its metabolism, its slow dream. The hermits are the only *visitors* who can choose to stay, and the only *residents* who can choose to leave, and this is the tension that holds them, the reason they remain, the reason they fear.

But there are others.

The **Mist Swifts** are not birds, finally. They are something that evolved *toward* bird, that reached a local optimum in the shape of a bird, and then continued evolving in directions no bird has taken. They have no feathers, exactly. Their wings are membranes, translucent, showing the structure of light that passes through them, the interference patterns of thin films that make them appear to shift color as they move. They do not fly. They *swim* in the humid layer, the dense warm air that fills the Throat's lower reaches, their wings beating in a medium thick enough to push against. They hunt by vibration, their bodies covered in sensors that detect the thermal fluctuation of a sleeping hermit, the wing-beat of a moth, the heat of a wasp colony rising in an updraft. They are fast. They are silent, their passage a tearing of silk that only registers after they have passed. They eat moths, mostly, but also the smaller insects, the mites that live in the moss, the springtails that dance on the condensation. They do not eat the wasps. The wasps are too warm, too *loud*, their colonies too distributed, too *many* to be a meal. The swifts and the wasps have an understanding, an ancient treaty written in temperature and vibration, a division of the Throat's resources that has held for millions of years.

They nest in the flattened heights, where the stone is warmest, where the moss cushions are thickest. Their nests are not built but *grown*, secreted from their own bodies, a silk that hardens to the consistency of the fused material of the old structures. They are solitary, each swift a territory of one, but their territories overlap in the humid layer, and they communicate, a constant exchange of vibration-signals that carries information about food, about temperature, about the presence of hermits, about the *mood* of the tower, which they sense more acutely than any other creature. When the tower's hum shifts, when its patterns change, the swifts change. They rise. They descend. They become *aggressive*, not toward each other but toward the air itself, tearing through it as if trying to escape something that is not yet visible.

They live for decades. The oldest are marked by their wing-membranes, which have grown thick with age, opaque in places, showing the accumulated damage of ten thousand flights through warm wet air. They do not breed in the way you understand breeding. They *split*, their bodies dividing at a certain age, two swifts emerging from one, each smaller, each carrying the memories of the whole in patterns of vibration that no human science has learned to read. The hermits call this the *Echo*, and they mark it, a celebration and a warning, the swift's territory expanding, its hunger increasing, its sensitivity sharpening.

They are afraid of the tower. They feel it more than any other creature, its hum resonating in their membrane-wings, its attention a physical pressure against their bodies. When the tower *looks*, when its patterns focus on a single swift, the swift does not sleep for days. It flies. It hunts. It *waits*, in the way that a predator waits, for the moment when the tower's attention moves on, when the pressure releases, when the swift can return to its nest and its slow, patient life.

The **Stone-Wasps** are not wasps, finally. They are something that evolved in parallel, that reached a local optimum in the shape of a wasp, and then continued in directions no wasp has taken. They are blind, their eyes reduced to light-sensors that register only the presence or absence of illumination, the warm dark of the Throat indistinguishable from the deeper dark of the moss shadows. They navigate by vibration, by the chemical signatures of the moss, by the warm updrafts that rise from their own bodies, their own collective metabolism. They are small, smaller than any wasp you have seen, their bodies the size of rice grains, their wings beating at frequencies that create the susurrus, the *presence* in the air that is the Throat's constant soundtrack.

Their colonies are massive, ancient, distributed across the moss cushions in networks that span kilometers of stone. A single colony might number in the hundreds of thousands, their mud nests built into the lichen, their societies a *mind* distributed across thousands of bodies, making decisions that no individual could make, reaching consensus through vibration and scent and the slow accumulation of pheromone trails that have been walked for millennia. They are engineers. Their nests incorporate the minerals of the stone they inhabit, their saliva a chemistry that accelerates the lichen's slow acid, that *weeps with* the rock, that transforms it. Over decades, a wasp nest becomes indistinguishable from the moss cushion it occupies. Over centuries, it becomes indistinguishable from the stone. The wasps are *geologists*, their technology a slow, biological mining, a extraction of the Throat's substance that is also a *contribution*, a adding of their own bodies, their own heat, their own chemistry, to the system.

They do not have queens, finally. They have *warmth*, the collective temperature of the colony, a gradient that rises from the edges to the center, that creates the updrafts that feed the mist swifts, that drives the circulation of the humid layer, that sustains the temperature inversion that should not be stable but is, because of the wasps, because of their heat, because of the feedback loops that have had millions of years to settle. They reproduce by *budding*, a section of the colony warming to the point where it becomes independent, where it finds its own moss cushion, its own stone face, its own territory. They die individually, their bodies falling into the moss, feeding the stone, becoming the minerals that the next generation will incorporate into their nests. They do not have lifespans, exactly. They have *distributions*, a constant turnover of individuals, the colony persisting, the colony *remembering*, the pheromone trails accumulating history in a chemistry that no human science has learned to read.

They are afraid of the dry. The dry world above, the highland scrub, the sparse vegetation of the rim—this is *death* to them, a desiccation that no technology of mud and saliva can prevent. They have evolved sensors for humidity, for the particular *quality* of moisture that the Throat provides, the condensation that is not rain, the mist that is not cloud. When the humidity drops, even slightly, the colony *contracts*, its individuals clustering in the deepest moss, the warmest stone, the most protected places. They wait. They have waited for seasons, for years, for the moisture to return, and always it has, always the Throat has provided, because the wasps *make* it provide, their heat driving the cycle, their bodies feeding the moss, the moss feeding the stone, the stone *weeping*, and the weeping sustaining the wasps, a loop so old that the distinction between cause and effect has dissolved.

They sense the tower. They do not understand it, but they *respond* to it, their vibrations shifting when its hum changes, their pheromone trails rerouting, their collective temperature rising or falling in patterns that no individual wasp initiates. The tower is *outside* their ecology, or *above* it, or *below* it, a presence that is not food, not threat, not mate, but *something*, a node in the network of the Throat that they cannot map, that they can only *feel*, and *fear*, and *serve*, because serving is what the Throat demands, the slow transformation of what enters into what sustains, the digestion that is not digestion, the life that is not life.

The **Veil Orchids** are not plants, finally. They are something that evolved *toward* plant, that reached a local optimum in the shape of an orchid, and then continued in directions no orchid has taken. They grow not in soil but on the weeping faces of stone, their roots dangling in the humid air, their flowers the pale color of old bone. They do not photosynthesize, exactly. They *farm* light, their tissues modified to absorb wavelengths that no plant should absorb, to cultivate the light that passes through the moss shadows, to use it for processes that happen below the threshold of visibility. They are *parasites* of light, *thieves* of the spectrum, their pale flowers a signal not to pollinators but to *something else*, a beacon in the warm dark that the Throat provides.

They are pollinated by moths, the thumb-sized moths that are themselves nearly transparent, their wings showing the structure of light that passes through them. The moths are not pollinators, exactly. They are *partners*, a symbiosis so old that the distinction between plant and insect has dissolved. The orchids provide nectar, a chemistry that affects the moths' metabolism, that extends their lives, that makes them *loyal*, returning to the same flowers, the same stone faces, the same patches of moss, generation after generation. The moths provide *attention*, their wings brushing the orchid's reproductive structures, their bodies carrying pollen in patterns of static electricity that the orchid's tissues have learned to read. They do not have a relationship of individual to individual. They have a relationship of *population* to *population*, the moths' numbers rising and falling with the orchids' flowering, the orchids' distribution shifting with the moths' preferences, a co-evolution that has continued for millions of years, that has produced forms so strange that a human botanist would not recognize them as related to any known plant.

They open only at dusk, when the temperature drops enough to trigger them, a threshold that the Throat's climate has maintained for millennia, the warm days and the cooler nights, the condensation that forms when the warm air meets the cooling stone. Their flowers are pale, the color of old bone, of the moths' wings, of the light that passes through them. They are *camouflage*, a hiding in plain sight, visible only to those who know to look, to the moths, to the hermits, to the tower, which *sees* them, which responds to their opening, its patterns shifting, its hum changing frequency, a *conversation* in light and vibration and the slow chemistry of the Throat's ecosystem.

They do not fear the tower. They have no fear, no consciousness of threat, no *self* to be threatened. They are *process*, a flowering and a pollination and a seeding and a growth, a cycle that has no beginning and no end, that has been continued for so long that the individual flower is meaningless, the individual orchid a fiction, the *population* a single organism distributed across stone and moss and humid air, a *thing* that thinks in the slow time of geological change, that *remembers* in the chemistry of its tissues, that *waits* for the conditions that will allow the next stage, the next flowering, the next transformation.

And the **Tower**.

The tower is not a building, the hermits say, and they are right, finally, in a way that no science can verify. It is a *mouth*, a *listener*, a *processor*. It is not alive, finally, in the way you understand life, but it is *animate*, a word that your language has not yet learned to use precisely, a word that points toward a category of existence that includes the Throat itself, the wasp colonies, the orchid-moth symbiosis, the entire *system* that has evolved in this pocket of warm wet deep time, this flattened, settled, *finished* geology that is not finished at all but *ongoing*, a process that has reached a configuration stable enough to persist, to *listen*, to *wait*, to *digest*.

The tower's surface shows patterns of light and dark that move, that respond to your presence, that seem to be *communicating* in a language of thermal fluctuation. These patterns are not random. They are *attention*, and attention here is *reciprocal*. If you watch long enough, it watches back, and what it shows you is not hallucination—it is *information*, data about the Throat, about its history, about its *purpose*, which is not a purpose you would choose. The tower does not want anything, finally. It has no desires, no fears, no *self* to be satisfied or threatened. It *processes*. It *transforms*. It *listens*, and in listening, it *becomes*, the sound of the Singing, the vibration of the wasps, the thermal fluctuation of the swifts, the chemical whisper of the moss, all of it *entering*, all of it being *changed*, all of it becoming the conditions that allow the next stage, the next visitor, the next *meal* for a system that is not alive but *animate*, that is not a mind but a *metabolism*, a slow digestion of everything that enters, a transformation of the raw into the sustaining, the temporary into the eternal, the *you* into the *Throat*.

It is waiting for something that has not yet arrived, the hermits say, or that has already arrived and is being processed, digested, transformed into the conditions that will allow the next stage. The tower does not know which. It does not *care*. It *listens*, and in listening, it *waits*, and in waiting, it *becomes*, the mouth that has closed, that will open again for the next descent, the next willing transformation, the next *you*, descending into the warm wet dark, crossing the invisible boundary, feeling the shift in the quality of light, the pressure in your ears, the constant low thrum like a note held just below hearing, and realizing, finally, that you are not visiting the Throat.

You are *entering* it.

You are *becoming* it.

And the Throat, patient, warm, ancient, *listening*, will take what it needs, will leave what it can spare, will transform you as it has transformed everything else, the stone, the moss, the wasps, the swifts, the orchids, the hermits, all of it *animate*, all of it *alive* in the way that your science has not yet learned to name, all of it *waiting*, all of it *becoming*, in the slow, warm, wet dark of the Throat, the mouth that opens, the throat that receives, the body that digests, the dream that continues, and continues, and will continue, until the moisture runs dry, until the wasp colonies cool, until the Singing fades to silence, until the tower's hum becomes a memory in your ears, a residue in your bones, a *dream* of warm wet deep time, of a place that was not a place, of a body that was not a body, of a life that was not life, but was *more* than life, was *other* than life, was the *condition* that makes life possible, the warm dark wet *womb* from which everything emerges, and to which everything, finally, returns.
