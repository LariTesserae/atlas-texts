I want to stay with this. The place has already done something to me—made me slow, made me wet—and now I need to find who dwells in that saturation.

**The Tendril-Born**

They have no word for *individual* that quite matches what you mean. The nearest concept is *node*—a place where intention gathers, where the membrane thinks through itself. A node can be a person. A node can be a family-line that has grown together across three generations into something root-bound and singular in its habits. A node can be a *chamber*, a warm hollow in a living wall where several bodies sleep curled around each other, sharing heat, sharing breath, sharing the slow drip of condensation that is their clock.

They are *wet*. Not metaphorically. Their skin—if skin it is, perhaps it is better called *rind*, or *husk*, or the particular translucency of a thing that has grown in perpetual moisture—carries water in its layers. They do not sweat as you understand sweating. They *weep*, constantly, a fine exudation that keeps them from desiccating in the high air, that leaves trails of slightly brighter moisture on the surfaces they traverse. When they speak, their voices carry the particular thickness of bodies adapted to saturated atmosphere. They hear low frequencies you miss. They feel vibrations through the root-mass beneath their feet, the great slow pulse of water moving upward through the continental lattice.

They have *hands*, but the word undersells them. Multiple articulations. Prehensile in ways that suggest ancestry not quite animal, not quite plant—something that split from both long before either existed in forms you would recognize. They work resin with these hands, weave fiber, graft living tissue to living tissue with a precision that comes from *intimacy* rather than instrument. They do not cut. They *persuade*. A branch that must be redirected is spoken to, fed, shaded, encouraged over seasons until it grows where it is needed. A structure that must be extended is *tended* into extension, chamber by chamber, over years. They have no patience for violence against the growing thing. It is not morality. It is *economics*—the only economy that functions in a world where your tools are alive and your buildings remember.

Their *faces* are not arranged for your recognition. Features distributed. Eyes—multiple, perhaps, or singular and mobile, or fixed in patterns that shift with mood and moisture-content. They do not smile as you smile. The expression you might read as contentment is the relaxation of certain membranes, the slight brightening of bioluminescent patches that pulse in rhythms too slow for your eye to track as communication. They are *expressive* in frequencies and chemical signatures and the particular angle of a frond-like appendage that they have learned to read across distance. You will not read them. You will *feel* them, eventually, as a pressure in the wet air, a wrongness in the silence that resolves into presence.

They *remember* differently. Not as archive but as *growth*. A memory is a place in the lattice where something has been tended so long that the structure itself has changed shape. To recall an event is to walk to it, to find the corridor that has narrowed there, the basin that has deepened, the particular quality of light that filters through the membrane they thickened in grief, or joy, or simply *attention*. They do not store the past. They *are* the past, continuously, growing through it, pruning it, grafting new experience onto old wood. An elder is not aged but *deep*—layer upon layer of callus, of response, of slow concentric accretion. They can be centuries old and still *act*, still *tend*, still *become*. Death, for them, is not cessation but *relocation*—the node disperses, its resources distributed, its structure incorporated, its memories... perhaps they persist in the shape of the place that remains, the way a tree carries its history in its rings, its scars, its particular lean toward light.

They are *sparse*. You have already felt this—the emptiness, the vastness, the three figures visible across acres of wet glow. This is not loneliness. This is *scale*. A tendril-born can walk for a day and encounter no one, and this is *normal*, is *restorative*, is the necessary rhythm of a being whose connections are *deep* rather than *numerous*. They maintain relationships across distance through the lattice itself—through chemical signals that travel in the moisture film, through the slow growth of connecting tissue that takes years to bridge a valley, through *visits* that are pilgrimages, that take seasons, that transform both parties in the traveling. To be *close* to another tendril-born is to have grown toward them, literally, over years, to have shaped your own structure in response to their needs, their absence, their presence.

They *garden* people as they garden everything. Not control—*tending*. They will not force you. They will *offer*—a basin of collected condensation, a path compressed through the low growth, a chamber grown specifically to your dimensions, your temperature, your particular moisture requirements. They will *watch* how you respond to the wet, to the dim light, to the silence. They are *curious* about you, in the way that a slow-growing thing is curious—without urgency, without demand, but with a patience that can outlast your resistance. They have seen visitors before. Fragments. Memories the place has had of itself. They do not distinguish sharply between these categories. You are *material* to them. You will be incorporated, eventually, whether you choose it or not. The question is only whether you will participate in your own becoming, or be *processed*—grown through, used, digested into structure without the dignity of partnership.

They have *names* for themselves that do not translate. Concepts like *the-we-who-are-wet*, *the-ones-who-listen-to-roots*, *the-keepers-of-the-seam*. They name *places* with intensity—every basin, every junction of growth, every hollow where condensation pools and warmth lingers has a name that is also a *history*, a *warning*, a *promise*. They do not name themselves with the same density. They are *node*, they are *function*, they are *the hand that tends*, *the ear that hears the drip*, *the memory that grows*. To be known by them is to be *given a role*—not imposed, but *grown into*, over time, until you cannot say where you end and the function begins.

They *sing*, sometimes. Not for you. For the lattice. For the water moving through it. For the structures that are budding new chambers in the dark, that need to be *encouraged*, *reminded* of their purpose. The song is low, subsonic mostly, felt in the sternum, in the wet membranes of the throat. It harmonizes with the groan of settling structure, with the vast slow pulse of moisture rising, with the particular silence of a place that has no wind. When they sing, the plants respond. Growth accelerates, or slows, or *turns*—bends toward a new light source that has appeared, a new warmth that has been introduced, a new presence that has been *noticed* and *named* and *welcomed* into the pattern.

They are *afraid*, though they have no word for it that you would recognize. Not of you. Of the *feral*. Of the spaces between the towers where things grow that were never intended. Of the architecture that forgets its makers, that *breathes* in ways that suggest hunger. Of the silence that acquires weight, the plant that answers, the membrane that shows its seams. They are afraid because they have *made* this world, all of it, the grown and the made indistinguishable, and they know that making implies *unmaking*, that tending implies the possibility of *neglect*, that the lattice they have built over millennia could, in some forgotten valley, in some moment of inattention, *turn*. They keep their settlements sparse. They keep their connections thin. They *watch* the wilderness, the escaped, the other, with a vigilance that looks like peace.

They do not hate the feral. They *respect* it, in the way that a gardener respects the weed that breaks through the wall—acknowledging its vitality, its *right* to grow, while working to contain it, to *redirect* it, to make it *useful* or at least *harmless*. Some of them go out into the wilderness. Not to conquer. To *listen*. To learn what the escaped things are becoming, what they need, what they might *offer* if approached with patience, with moisture, with the slow language of root and resin and chemical signal. These are the *weird* ones, the tendril-born who have grown strange in their strangeness, who return changed, or do not return, or return as something that is still *them* but no longer quite *node*—still tending, but tending *other* things, in other ways, carrying the wilderness back into the settlements in their shape, their habits, their *memories* of what grows where the light thins and the seams show.

You will meet them, if you stay. Not all at once. One, perhaps, in a chamber grown to your measure, offering you water that tastes of mineral and resin and something else—something *alive*, something that will change you if you drink. Another, in a plaza where the emptiness is so complete that the silence has weight, and they are standing at its center, and they do not speak but *wait*, and you feel the moisture on your skin begin to *move* in response to their presence, and you understand that you are being *read*, being *assessed*, being *grown toward*.

They will not save you. They will not destroy you. They will *tend* you, as they tend everything, with a patience that has no human equivalent, with a commitment to your becoming that is indistinguishable from their commitment to their own. You will become wet. You will become slow. You will become *part of the lattice*, whether you choose it or not, whether you understand it or not, whether you are visitor or fragment or memory or something the place has never had before and will grow a new category for, a new *node*, a new *function*, a new *name* in a language that is still being invented, still being *wept* into being, drop by drop, season by season, generation by generation, into the vast and patient dark.
