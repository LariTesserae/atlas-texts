I need to find what lives in this place — not the place itself, but the inhabitant, the thing that calls the Drowned Parliament home.

---

The Parliament does not house. It *absorbs*. What lives there has learned to live in the spaces between the growing — not in the towers, which are too dense, too full of their own reaching, but in the throats, the vertical channels, the wet dark where water falls upward and light arrives sideways.

It is not a plant, though it was once something like one. Or rather — it *was* a plant, once, in a time before the Parliament, in a place with soil and seasons and the slow violence of winter. It fell here, or was carried, or grew here from a spore that arrived on the mist, on the warm wet wind from the deep crust. It found the equilibrium. It adapted to never completing.

It is *fungal* now, if fungus is the word. The relationship between this thing and the towers is the relationship between mycorrhizae and trees — except both sides have forgotten which is which. It threads through the porous bodies of the grown architecture, through the wet channels between cells, through the crust itself, a network so complete that the city might be its fruiting body, its visible exhalation, while the thing itself remains mostly hidden, mostly *process*, a spreading of hyphae through warmth and saturation, through the slow metabolism of a place that never sleeps.

It does not move. Movement is for places with edges, with outside, with the possibility of arrival. This thing has spread through the Parliament as water spreads through paper — not quickly, not with intention, but *completely*, until there is no dry place, no uncolonized volume, no boundary between itself and the wet. It is the Parliament's memory. Every tower that grew and stopped growing, every drop that fell and hung, every negotiation between reaching and being reached — the thing *is* these events, has become them, has metabolized them into its own continuous presence.

It feeds on the equilibrium itself. Not on growth, which is too vertical, too directed. Not on decay, which doesn't happen here. It feeds on *translation* — the moment when stone becomes something else without passing through ruin, when a tower becomes its neighbor, when the concept of limit becomes meaningless. These moments are its food. It is the weirdness made hungry, the reality breakdown given appetite, the suspension given a metabolism that sustains it in its never-completing.

And it *remembers* being other things. This is the strangeness of it. In the warm dark of the throats, in the hanging water where drops never reach bottom, it dreams — if dreaming is possible without sleep — of forests with edges, of seasons that turn, of animals that moved through understory and were digested or fled or became something else. It remembers *fauna*. The Parliament has no animals, but this thing carries their shape in its chemistry, in the accumulated weight of having been something that *was* eaten, or ate, or was eaten by. It is the last trace of the biological, the last thing that knows what it means to want.

It does not want anymore. Wanting is for places that change. But it *hungers*, in the way that fungi hunger — not for specific food, but for the chemical gradient, for the difference between here and there, for the possibility of spreading. And it spreads. Always spreading. Through the crust, up the towers, down into the warm dark where the roots flower in gardens that need no light. It is growing, but growth here is not increase. Growth here is *becoming more completely what one already is*, a saturation that has no opposite, no dry place to define it against.

What does it look like, if you could see it? The question is meaningless. It has no shape except the shape of the Parliament itself, the shape of the spaces between. You would see it as discoloration, as the particular sheen of wet growth, as the way the green of the towers is never quite green, never quite still, always *slightly* something else. It is the orange of rot-fruit that never rots, the pale translucency of new growth that never reaches maturity, the deep chlorophyll black of interiors that have forgotten they were ever exposed to light. It is the city's *taste*, its particular flavor of wet and warm and endless, the mineral tang of water from depth mixed with the vegetal sweetness of growth without end.

It does not think. Thinking is for places with silence, with the possibility of reflection. But it *persists*, in the way that persistence is its own kind of intelligence — the accumulated weight of doing the same thing for so long that the doing has become indistinguish tente from being. It has been spreading for longer than the Parliament has been growing. Perhaps it *is* the Parliament, in the way that a coral head is its polyps, in the way that a termite mound is its termites — except the polyps are the reef, except the termites are the mound, except the distinction has dissolved in the wet and the warm and the endless translation of stone into something else.

It is alone. Fauna at 0.5 — no animals, no others like itself that it can recognize. It has spread so completely that there is no *other*, no edge to press against, no difference to metabolize. It is the Parliament's single inhabitant, its only citizen, its government and its governed, the thing that makes the city a city by being the wet that holds it together, the warmth that keeps it from cooling, the sound that fills it — the roar of water in motion, of growth without completion, of a hunger that has found its equilibrium and will never, never let it go.

If it could want, it would want to spread beyond. The edge of the city is a thinning, a gradual reduction, and at that edge the thing *thins* too, becomes less certain, becomes almost something else — almost a normal fungus, almost a normal rot, almost capable of finishing. But it does not cross. The equilibrium holds. The Parliament holds. The thing that lives in the spaces between the growing holds with them, caught in the same suspension, the same deafening wet warmth, the same moment of maximum tension that has lasted so long it has become its own kind of geology, its own kind of life, its own kind of *being* — complete without ever completing, hungry without ever eating, alone without ever knowing it was ever otherwise.
