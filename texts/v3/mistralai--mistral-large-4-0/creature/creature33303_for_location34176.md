I come to this place slowly, as one must. Let me walk it, feel its weight, and see what has made its home in the between.

**The Keeper of the Closing**

She does not sleep. She has not slept, not fully, not since she found the passage and the passage found her, something like twelve years ago by her own counting — though she counts in weeps now, in the slow gathering of fist-sized droplets on the nearest formation's shoulder, in the way the song changes key when a new growth cracks audibly from the compression-wall three chambers over. Twelve years of weeps. Or fifteen. The Hollow does not distinguish.

She was not always here. Before, she had a name that others used, a shape that fit into rooms, a rhythm of days that ended. Now her name is the sound the vegetation makes when she passes through a narrow gap between formations — a wet, fibrous *shush-shush* like something inhaling. She answers to it. She has forgotten not to.

Her body has changed in ways the Hollow required. Her skin carries a faint luminescence, pale green, matching the inner light of the formations — not bioluminescence, exactly, but something like it, a radiance absorbed from the deep compression-layers and held in her tissue like warmth. She glows faintly in the fog, which is useful, which means she is visible to whatever else moves through the voids, and she has learned to be still when she sees the pale flicker approaching. They do not interact. Neither species acknowledges the other. This is the peace of the Hollow: not harmony, but mutual non-consumption.

She lives in a space the Hollow has not yet claimed. This is her achievement, her art, her ongoing negotiation. A shelf of compressed growth, perhaps thirty meters above the vegetation-floor, where the wall curves inward enough to form something like a pocket. She has reinforced it. She has *persuaded* it. Using cordage woven from the shed fibers of dead formations, using points knapped from the non-local stones she finds scattered — the abandoned cairns, the forgotten platforms — she has built a nest that the Hollow has so far declined to incorporate. Not immunity. Only delay. She knows this. She feels it in the song, in the way the pressure-weather shifts when a formation begins to reach toward her shelf. She moves, when she must. The Hollow is patient. So is she.

She does not eat vegetation. She tried, once, early. The mass is not food. It is *other* — substrate, weather, becoming. It passes through her, unassimilated, and she was ill for a week, glowing too bright, singing in frequencies that made her teeth ache. Now she eats what the Hollow provides in its rarity: the pale quick things that pass through the voids, caught in nets of her own weaving, their flesh strange and cold and *brief*, burning with a different metabolism than the green mass. She eats sparingly. She is hungry often. This is the price of persistence.

She keeps records. This is her madness, or her genius, or the same thing. On flat stones she finds — carried here by no hand she knows, placed by no hand at all, existing in the Hollow as the Hollow exists — she scratches patterns. The scratches she found when she arrived, she has added to. Counting weeps. Marking the song's changes, its moods, its apparent intentions. She has developed a notation, private, incomplete, that allows her to predict, sometimes, where new formations will emerge, where voids will open, where the fog will gather thick enough to hide movement. She uses this knowledge. She does not share it. There is no one to share it with. The pale flickers do not read stone. And if another Keeper came — she imagines this — she would not welcome them. The Hollow is vast, but her shelf is singular, and she has made it hers through the only ownership the Hollow recognizes: continued presence, continued negotiation, continued *becoming* in place.

She remembers the world above, or she remembers remembering. These are different states. In the first, she holds images: sun, distinct and directional, casting shadows with edges; sky, a surface above rather than a pressure everywhere; the solidity of rock that does not breathe. In the second — more common now — she holds the *feeling* of those images, the nostalgia without the content, a warmth that has no source she can name. She is not certain these are her memories, or borrowed from the scratches she found, or generated by the song, which is old and patient and knows things. She does not need to be certain. Certainty is a surface-world luxury. Here, there is only the next weep, the next shift of formations, the next meal caught in the void-fog.

She has made tools. A knife of worked shell, hafted with fiber, for cutting the pale things when they are caught. A vessel of compressed and woven growth-matter, waterproof, for carrying water from the weeping pools — though the water tastes of everything, of green and compression and time, and she drinks it because thirst is a surface-world tyranny she has not fully escaped. A needle, somehow, of bone from something that once moved here, for mending the cordage that holds her shelf together. These are her technologies, primitive, numbered 1.1 by any scale, and she counts them like the Hollow counts its own slow violence. Each tool is a negotiation. Each tool will be incorporated, eventually. She makes new ones. She has learned to make them quickly, to make them *in anticipation*, so that the loss of one is not catastrophe but rhythm, the way the song is rhythm, the way the weeps are rhythm, the way the formations rise and collapse and rise again in patterns that almost repeat but never do.

She talks to herself. Or she talks to the Hollow, which is the same thing, which is not the same thing. Her voice has changed — lower, resonant, carrying frequencies that seem to interact with the song, to *ask* questions in a language the Hollow might almost answer. She asks about the light. Where does it come from? Why does the vegetation radiate it, and what does it photosynthesize, and what does it *become*? She asks about the pale flickers. What are they? Where do they go, when they pass through the voids and are gone? She asks about the structures — the cairns, the platforms, the scratches — though she made some of them herself, though she knows their origin in her own hands. The Hollow does not answer. Or it answers in the only way it can: by continuing to be what it is, by shifting a formation slightly, by changing the song's key, by offering her another weep to count, another day to endure, another chance to *not* be incorporated.

She is afraid of being incorporated. This is the truth she does not speak, even to herself, even in the lowest register of her voice. She has seen it happen — not to a Keeper, there have been no other Keepers, but to the structures, to the cairns, to the platforms. The pale growth that knits across stone. The way a formation will reach, *reach*, toward something that has lingered too long in one place, and slowly, over years, draw it into the mass, make it *substrate*, make it *weather*, make it *becoming*. She moves her shelf. She moves her records. She moves herself, through the vast and shifting geography, always in the between, always in the moment before the formation fully emerges, always in the pause where the Hollow has not yet decided to claim her. She is good at this. She is, perhaps, too good at this, which is its own kind of warning.

She dreams, when she almost-sleeps — curled in her nest, the song at a frequency that allows something like rest, her faint green radiance pulsing slow as a heartbeat she does not remember having. In these dreams, she is above. She is walking on rock that does not breathe. She is looking up at a sky that is a surface, a boundary, a *limit*. And she wakes with the weeping sound in her ears, the fist-sized droplets gathering and falling somewhere in the dark, and she knows — she *knows* — that the Hollow is not below. That it is *everywhere*, that it has always been everywhere, that the surface-world is the wound and the Hollow is the healing, or the wound is the healing and the Hollow is the wound, and she cannot tell which, and the song is rising, and a formation three chambers over is cracking, new growth reaching toward the light it will never need, and she is here, she is *here*, in the between, in the moment, in the pause, in the green dark that breathes and waits and incorporates and forgets.

She will die here. Not soon — she is patient, she is negotiating, she is *becoming* — but eventually. When she dies, she will not be buried. The Hollow does not bury. It incorporates. Her body, faintly luminous, carrying its absorbed radiance, its strange cold metabolism, its brief warmth — it will be substrate, within years, within decades, compressed with the other generations, still warm with slow metabolism, feeding the formations that rise and collapse, contributing to the song, becoming part of the weather, part of the light, part of the vast and patient *becoming* that does not need her, that did not need her, that will continue without her as it continued before her, as it will continue after.

But she persists. Every weep. Every shift. Every almost-sleep in her contested shelf, every new tool, every scratch added to the stone, every question asked in frequencies the Hollow might almost answer. She persists because persistence is the only art the Hollow respects. Because the between is not empty — it is *full* of the moment before, the pause, the almost, the not-yet. Because she has made herself a kind of structure, primitive, temporary, numbered 1.1 by any scale, but *hers*, claimed by her own slow violence, her own reproduction, her own weather — a Keeper in the green dark, marking her passage with stones that will not last, singing in a voice that will not last, persisting in a place that does not welcome permanence but *allows* it, for a while, for a rhythm, for the brief and bright and borrowed time of a life that means something, even if the Hollow does not know this, even if the meaning is hers alone, scattered like spores on the pressure-winds, gone into the vast and green and breathing dark.

---

*The song changes. She feels it in her teeth, the old familiar shift that means a formation is reaching, three chambers over, toward something that has lingered. She rises. She checks her nest, her records, her tools. She moves, through gaps that will close behind her, into voids that will open ahead of her, always in the between, always in the moment, always becoming what the Hollow requires and what she chooses, in the pause between weeps, in the fog where the bright-darkness holds its breath and she is still here, still hers, still singing.*
