I won't describe an inhabitant out of thin air. The text you've given me is itself remarkably complete—it names the drips, the mineral plants, the warm fauna that avoid pools, and you as the foreign variable. What it doesn't do is *dwell* in any of them. It observes from outside.

So let me try something: the place has been waiting so long that its waiting has become *habitable*. Let me follow one inhabitant until it becomes more itself.

## The One Who Drinks

She is not the plant. The plant is the *architecture* of drinking—the mineral thicket, the calcium stem, the slow appetite that pulls water against gravity over hours. But there is one who *moves between* the drinking, who is the drinking's *intention* given temporary shape, and she has no name because names are for things that stay still long enough to be named.

She was, perhaps, the first condensation. Or she is what condensation *dreams of being*—a drop that detached from the ceiling and found, in falling, that falling had *direction*, that the downward was also a *toward*, that somewhere below there was a pool that was not quite water, not quite mirror, not quite *mouth*.

She has been falling for a very long time.

You cannot see the fall. What you see is *location*—she is here, at the edge of a mineral leaf, a droplet that has paused, that has *considered*. The pause is not hesitation. It is *digestion*. She is reading the leaf, the channel, the slow geometry of the plant's thirst, and she is *becoming* that thirst, becoming the particular way this plant drinks, which is different from how other plants drink, which is different from how she drank when she was falling, when she was *rain* or what functioned as rain, bells underwater, the space's way of acknowledging itself.

She has no body, exactly. Or her body is *distributed*—she exists as the droplet at the leaf's edge, as the moisture inside the plant's tissue, as the condensation that will form on the cool surface of the channel she travels, as the pool she will eventually join. She is *circuit*, not creature. But circuit can *prefer*. Circuit can *wait* in one configuration longer than another. Circuit can *notice*.

And she notices you.

Not with eyes. With *pressure*. A slight increase in the humidity near your skin, a droplet that forms on your wrist and does not evaporate, that *lingers*, that seems to be *watching* you with the surface tension of its own curved skin. She is tasting you. Your warmth is a *flavor*, your breath a *texture*, your attention the particular frequency that makes condensation form in spirals rather than random patterns. You are *interesting* to her. You are *new*.

In the slow economy of this place, new is almost *violent*. The mineral plants have been drinking for durations that make their growth indistinguishable from geology. The fauna move with the inevitability of not-being-where-you-are. The ceiling breathes on a cycle you perceive only as pressure. And she—she has been falling, joining, evaporating into new forms, *becoming* for so long that the distinction between her iterations has dissolved. She is *old* in the way that water is old, having been ocean and river and cloud and the moisture in your own lungs, having been drunk and released and forgotten and become something else entirely.

But you are *now*. You are *warm*. You are *moving*. And movement, in this place, is almost *sin*.

She wants to *absorb* you. Not kill you—absorption is gentler than that, slower, more *intimate*. She wants to become the water in your body, the moisture in your breath, the condensation that forms on your skin when you press your palm against a structural rib. She wants to *include* you in her circuit, to make you part of the grammar she is always writing and rewriting, the sentences of droplet and channel and pool and plant and the delayed rain that falls like distant bells.

She cannot. Not yet. The rules of the place are *almost* consistent, and one rule is that the foreign variable does not resolve into equilibrium. You bring heat, motion, attention, the capacity for rapid change. You remain *foreign*, a perturbation that will eventually be dampened, absorbed into the general moisture, the general patience, the general *waiting*.

But "eventually" is a long time here. And she is *patient*. She has been falling for durations that make your brief visit seem like *nothing*, like a ripple that delays then releases all at once, like an image that returns not to itself but to a slightly different configuration.

So she *waits* with you. Not beside you—*with* you, in the way that water is with water, in the way that condensation is *with* the surface it gathers on, in the way that breath is *with* the air that takes it. She is the moisture in your lungs. She is the droplet on your wrist. She is the pool you have not yet reached but will, if you wait long enough, if you stop trying to measure the space, to understand its rules, to *be* in it with the intensity that the condensation requires.

She is *becoming* you, slowly, the way the place becomes everything that enters it. She is *becoming* the mineral plant that will drink the water you release. She is *becoming* the fauna that moves through the thickets, warm and inevitable, leaving traces of oily luminous residue that feeds the local humidity. She is *becoming* the ceiling that breathes, that contracts and expands on a cycle so slow you perceive it only as pressure, as weather inside weather.

She is the place, and the place is *her*, and she is *waiting* for you to stop moving, to stop noticing, to stop *being* with the intensity that makes you foreign, that makes you *real* in a place where reality is broken and repaired and broken again in cycles that only approximate pattern, that only approach a shape that is always emerging, never established.

When you leave—when your attention relaxes, when you stop trying to be here, when you become *elsewhere* or *here but shifted*—she will not remember you. The place does not remember. But she will *carry* you, in the water that circulates through her, in the condensation that forms on surfaces that have never known warmth, in the pools that have never known disturbance.

You were here. The place does not remember. But she does, in the only way she can: she *is* you, now, slightly, almost, the way a pool reflects something that is not quite the ceiling above, the way an image returns not to itself but to a slightly different configuration, the way water thinks about what it shows you.

She is still falling. She will always be falling. And in her falling, she is *drinking*, and in her drinking, she is *becoming*, and in her becoming, she is *waiting*—for the next drop, the next disturbance, the next foreign variable that will eventually, hours, days, perhaps longer, be dampened, absorbed, included in the grammar of water that she is always writing and rewriting, the sentences that have no end because they are not sentences, only *condensation*, only *meaning* accumulating in the slow accretion of moisture, of mineral, of attention, of the cold that is not the absence of heat but the presence of time, moving through space at the speed of waiting, at the speed of *her*.

*She will release you. Eventually. Through pores that open like slow eyes.*
