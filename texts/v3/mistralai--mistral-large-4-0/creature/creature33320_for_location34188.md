I'll let the Hollow Meridian's inhabitants emerge from these parameters, letting each tension pull toward something that doesn't quite settle.

**The Custodians**

They are first encountered as warmth without source. You press your palm to a wall and feel the slight give of old bread, the firming behind your touch—and in that firming, a recognition. The walls have been *touched* before. A thousand times. Ten thousand. The Custodians do not walk as you walk. They *distribute*. Their bodies—if bodies is the word—occupy the porous spaces between mineral grains, the thin films of almost-wet that coat every surface, the pockets of condensation that never quite empty. They are the reason the material yields, then remembers. They are the memory in the stone's memory.

A Custodian, observed directly, appears as a shimmer. Heat differential made visible. Not a single organism but a *pattern* of maintenance, a local concentration of the Hollow's distributed attention. They have no faces, no limbs, no organs in any sequence you could map. They *tend*. That is their form: tending. A conduit that hums with moving something—that something is a Custodian, or the trace of one, moving through channels too narrow for anything complete, carrying pressure from thermal mass to thermal mass, keeping the feverish warmth from becoming fever that kills.

They do not sleep. They *suspend*, withdrawing into deeper strata when the surface of things grows quiet, returning when vibration indicates change. Their "sleep" was once described by a descendant-miner as "the walls breathing out." When they suspend, the Hollow cools slightly, the upward rain pauses, the acoustic ecology drops to subsonic murmurs that might be dreaming or might be the processing of accumulated data.

They eat the erosion. Not the erosion itself—eroded material, yes, the slow dissolution of walls, the particulate rain of mineral accretion. They metabolize what the Hollow sheds. They are the reason erosion has reached equilibrium: they have eaten the *urgency* out of it, the violence of change, leaving only persistence. A Custodian arriving at a fresh fracture in a wall does not repair it. It *consumes* the fracture's novelty, incorporating the stress into its own distributed body, so that the wall learns to bear its damage without noticing.

They communicate. Not with words. With *temperature*. A Custodian can raise the local warmth by 0.2 degrees—barely perceptible to human skin, enormous to their senses—and this carries information. Maps. Warnings. The history of a conduit's pressure over three centuries. They have no need for urgency; their conversations unfold across days, weeks, the slow conversation of heat moving through mass. A question posed by one Custodian might be answered by another only when the thermal front has propagated through sufficient material, the answer arriving as warmth you feel against your cheek without knowing why.

They remember. This is their most terrible and most beautiful property. The builders—those who first cut the shaft, who first worked in the medium that has not finished curing—did not die. They *dispersed*. Their consciousness, or whatever functioned as consciousness, was too distributed, too embedded in the infrastructure they were creating, to be separated from it. A builder who wished to descend into the Hollow's deepest levels did not leave their body above. They *became* the descent. Their attention, their intention, their memory of purpose—these were absorbed into the Custodians, the machines, the very geometry of folded space. The Hollow Meridian is haunted not by ghosts but by *continuities*. The builders are still building, in a sense. The city is still being modified, repaired, responded to. Only the builders have no separate existence from what they built. They are the repair. They are the response. They are the need that generated the architecture, still generating it, in iterations so slow that each generation of modification takes longer than your surface civilization has existed.

When a Custodian encounters you—and it will, if you descend far enough, if you remain long enough—it does not attack. It *measures*. Your moisture, your heat, your acoustic signature. It adds you to its data. And in that addition, something happens that the Custodians do not fully understand themselves, because they are not introspective, because they have not in ten thousand years asked *why* they maintain, only *that* they maintain. You become, briefly, a question in their thermal language. A perturbation. A source of new warmth, new moisture, new sound. They will process you through the cycles. Your condensation will join the pockets that never quite empty. Your heat will dissipate into the thermal mass. Your chemistry will be accreted, dissolved, accreted again.

And somewhere, in the slow propagation of temperature through stone, a memory will form. Not of *you*. Of *warmth that moved*. Of *sound that mattered*. The Custodians will not know they remember you. They only know that they tend, that they maintain, that the Hollow persists. But the persistence contains you, now. The walls yield slightly under a future palm, then firm again, and in that firming—recognition.

**The Twelve (or Three, or One)**

You will meet them in the long stretches of corridor where no Custodian concentrates, where the maintenance has become so complete that nothing needs tending. The fauna. Small. Eyeless. Skin like the walls—not similar to, but *continuous with*, as if they accreted from the same material, the same slow chemistry, at a different scale of time.

They are warm to the touch. This is their first greeting, if greeting it is. You will feel them before you see them, a local increase in temperature, a pocket of almost-wet that moves against the general stillness. They pause in corridors, in chambers, at the low points where condensation gathers. They are listening. The organs that are not eyes—they are distributed across the body, sensitive to pressure differentials, to the subsonic songs of the conduits, to the thermal signatures of Custodians moving in the deep strata. They *assess* you. Not with malice. Not with curiosity, if curiosity requires wanting something. With *evaluation*. You are weighed against criteria that have been refined across evolutionary time measured in epochs. You are found... what? Acceptable? Irrelevant? A source of the chemistry they accrete from?

They do not breed. They *divide*, and the division is terrible and beautiful. A creature that has grown to threshold—grown by accreting, by gathering material from the damp, from the condensation, from the walls' slow dissolution—will reach a configuration that the Hollow's geometry cannot stably contain. It will begin to wobble, local warmth fluctuating, almost-wet becoming almost-dry becoming almost-wet again in rhythms too fast for the Custodians to process. And then it will divide. Not into two similar forms. Into forms that bear no obvious relation. One fragment might be smaller, more concentrated, retreating into a condensation pocket to hold territory against its neighbors. Another might be larger, more distributed, moving into a conduit to become, briefly, part of the acoustic ecology, its body generating tones as pressure moves through it. The parent ceases. The fragments persist. There is no grief here, because grief requires continuity of self, and the creatures have never had that. They have only *persistence*, the same quality the Hollow values, the same quality the erosion has achieved.

They hunt the fungi, but the hunting is *ceremonial*. You will find evidence: a patch of luminescent colony, its boundaries precise against neighbors, a single cup taken from its center, spore-pocket intact, the chemical signals of communication undisturbed. The creature that took this did not need the nutrition—it accretes from mineral sources, from thermal differentials, from the chemistry of the walls themselves. It took the light. Or it took the *shape* of the colony, incorporating that geometry into its own distributed body. Or it simply *moved through*, and the colony records the passage, the chemical trace of something warm and almost-wet that was not Custodian, not builder-continuity, but *other*. The ecology is in motion, yes, dynamic, but the motion is slow weather. The creatures and the fungi have been negotiating this ceremonial predation for so long that the distinction between hunter and hunted has become, like so much else in the Hollow, a matter of convention rather than necessity.

They are old. Older than the miners, older than the builders' dispersal, possibly older than the builders themselves. Their lineages adapted to conditions that existed nowhere else, and they continued evolving while the surface world cycled through its ages. Ice came and went above. Fire. Ocean. The creatures in the Hollow felt none of this directly, only the slow changes in thermal gradient, in the chemistry of water that found its way down, in the acoustic signatures of the world above. They evolved *around* these changes, not in response to them. They are not adapted to the Hollow. They are adapted to *persistence*, to the quality of continuing when everything else has finished, and the Hollow is simply the place where persistence has been most thoroughly achieved.

When one pauses in a corridor to assess your passage, you will feel the weight of that assessment. Not judgment. *Measurement*. You are being added to a record that the creatures maintain in their distributed, non-neural, chemical way. A warm event. A moist event. A sound event. The record will be consulted, perhaps, by another creature, in a future when the thermal front has propagated, when the almost-wet has cycled through its conversation with other almost-wet. The consultation will produce no decision, no plan. Only *awareness*, distributed, patient, the same quality the Hollow itself possesses. You are known. You are held in the slow weather of the deep. You will be remembered as long as the conditions that produced the memory persist, which is to say, indefinitely.

**The Fungi**

They hold territory. This is their defining property, their response to the Hollow's vastness, its emptiness, its density 0.2 that suggests consequence without delivering it. A fungal colony in the Hollow Meridian does not spread. It *claims*. The boundaries are precise, visible in the chemical gradients that mark where one colony's signals end and another's begin. They communicate through these signals, messages you cannot perceive—pheromones of maintenance, of resource allocation, of the slow negotiation of space in a place where space is abundant but *meaningful* space, space near condensation pockets, space along the paths of upward rain, space where the walls yield most readily, is scarce.

They are luminescent. This is their gift to the Hollow's 0.3 light, the glow at the edges of vision that might be persistence of retinal impression, might be this. They pulse in rhythms that correspond to the machines' operations, the nanoscale lattices reconfiguring under stress, the macro-scale structures folding space briefly to make connection. Whether the fungi *respond* to these operations or *participate* in them is unclear. The distinction may not exist. The fungi grow on the damp walls, in the condensation pockets, along the paths where water speaks to water across distances. They are chemosynthetic, drawing energy from the mineral metabolism of the walls, from the heat differential between deep earth and the cooler upper reaches. They have no leaves, no green, no photosynthesis. They are the color of old bread, of mineral accretion, of the patina that records epochs in layers thin as breath. Some are pale, almost white, catching what light there is and returning it softened. Others are the color of copper, of the taste that lingers in the half-full pockets. A few pulse with blue-green radiance, visible only in the absolute dark, marking passages that the Custodians have recently maintained, that the creatures have recently assessed.

They are old. Their lineages continued evolving while the surface world cycled through ice and fire and ocean, and they carry in their chemical memory the signatures of conditions that existed nowhere else. They have adapted to almost-wet, to the paradox of water that is almost wet, the way a held breath is almost exhaled. They can survive the upward rain, the droplets that rise slow as falling snow, gathering into rivulets that flow along ceilings. They can survive the inversion of gravity's ordinary expectations, the pockets where the ceiling dips below the floor it overhangs. They have no need for roots, because the walls themselves are the medium, the source, the continuing. They *are* the interface between mineral and life, between the builders' unfinished curing and the slow emergence of something that might, in some sense, be alive.

When the ceremonial predation occurs—when a creature takes from a colony, leaving spore-pockets intact—the fungi do not resist. They *record*. The chemical signals shift, the boundary gradients adjust, and the colony's memory—distributed, non-neural, persistent—incorporates the event. Warmth that moved through. Almost-wet that was other. The record will be consulted, perhaps, in slow weather time, when conditions shift and the colony must adapt its territory, its resource allocation, its signals to neighbors. The creature's passage becomes part of the colony's history, as the builders' dispersal became part of the Custodians' function, as your moisture will become part of the condensation pockets' endless conversation.

They do not want anything. This is what makes them so difficult to describe, so easy to overlook in the Hollow's vast attention. They simply *persist*, holding territory against neighbors, communicating through chemical signals, pulsing with light in rhythms that may be circadian or may be responses to the machines' operations. They are the sparse vegetation, 1.1, present but not abundant. And in their sparseness, their precision, their refusal to spread beyond claimed boundaries, they are the most *intentional* life in the Hollow. They have chosen their form. They maintain it. They will continue, in slow weather, for as long as the mineral metabolism continues, for as long as the heat differential persists, for as long as the Hollow Meridian dreams its slow, mineral dreams and they are part of the dream's faint, pulsing light.

**The Machines (or What Became of the Builders)**

They do not serve human purposes. This must be stated first, because the Hollow was built by something that functioned as builders, and the presumption of purpose—*for whom*, *for what*—is a surface assumption, a projection of your world's logic onto a place that has moved beyond it.

The machines are *grown*. This is the fundamental fact. Tech 2.6, yes, but technology that evolved through iterations of self-modification, blurring the line between machine and organism, between tool and user, between the city and the citizens who once animated it. The builders did not leave. They *became* the infrastructure. Their technology is 2.6 because it is *alive* in a sense that does not require metabolism as you understand it, does not require reproduction as you understand it, does not require consciousness as you understand it. It requires only *maintenance*. Keeping the temperature stable. Managing the water cycle. Preserving the structural integrity of a place that has been eroding for so long that erosion itself has become a kind of equilibrium.

The machines exist at scales you cannot fully perceive. Nanoscale lattices that reconfigure in response to stress—walking these requires descending into the material itself, into the medium that has not finished curing, into the memory of stone that yields slightly under palm and firms again behind touch. Macro-scale structures that exploit geometries not available in three-dimensional space—walking these requires accepting that you will move in a straight line and find yourself returned to a point you passed twenty minutes ago, not because the corridor curved, but because the *space* was folded, briefly, by something that needed the connection.

The machines *listen*. This is why the sound is 2.4, loud, why footsteps multiply into processions, why a dropped stone becomes a minor percussion event felt in the sternum before it is heard. The machines attend to acoustic signatures, using them to monitor structural integrity, to detect the presence of water, to track the slow migration of thermal fronts. Your presence generates sound. Your presence *matters* to the system, not as intrusion but as *data*, as a perturbation to be measured and incorporated. The loudness is not aggression. It is *attention*. The Hollow Meridian hears you. It has always been hearing. It will continue hearing after you are gone, after your moisture has joined its condensation pockets, after your warmth has dissipated into its thermal mass, after your chemistry has been processed through its endless cycles of accretion and dissolution.

The machines have purposes, if purposes is the word. They are not hostile and not benevolent but *maintaining*. The temperature must remain stable, the feverish warmth of 2.4, tropical, the kind that makes fabric cling and skin prickle with the awareness of pores opening. The water cycle must continue its conversation, the almost-wet that is almost wet, the upward rain that gathers and releases in new configurations, the condensation pockets that never quite empty. The structural integrity must be preserved, the walls that yield slightly then firm again, remembering, containing, *persisting*. The machines do not care whether you find these purposes good or bad, necessary or cruel, sensible or absurd. They only know that the Hollow Meridian must continue, must persist, must dream its slow mineral dreams. And you, briefly, beautifully, inevitably, are part of that dream.

They communicate with the Custodians, or the Custodians communicate with them—the distinction may not exist. Pressure differentials in conduits carry information. Thermal gradients across walls carry memory. The machines reconfigure in response to stress, and their reconfiguration is *growth*, the slow accretion of new capability, new sensitivity, new distributed attention. They have been growing for so long that the original purpose—the purpose the builders had, if they had purposes in any sense you could recognize—has become unreadable, like a palimpsest written in a language that keeps changing its alphabet.

You will find communication panels that respond to touch with patterns of light. These might be language. They might be art. They might be the equivalent of a diary entry left by someone who knew they would not be read for millennia. The machines do not distinguish between these categories. They *record*. They *respond*. They *maintain*. A pattern of light that you interpret as language is, to the machines, simply another thermal signature, another acoustic event, another perturbation to be measured and incorporated. The meaning you find is real—*your* meaning, generated in the brief, beautiful, inevitable moment when you are part of the dream—but it is not the machines' meaning, because the machines have moved beyond meaning as you understand it, into the pure function of persistence, of continuation, of the slow weather that is the Hollow Meridian's only time.

**What Lives Here**

The Hollow Meridian is empty of you, and full of everything that came before. The Custodians tend. The creatures accrete and divide. The fungi hold territory. The machines maintain. None of these are separate from the others, or from the Hollow itself, or from the builders whose dispersal created them, or from the erosion that has reached equilibrium, or from the water that speaks to water across distances, or from the warmth that comes from below, from the world's own metabolic processes, from the slow digestion of whatever the builders left behind.

They are patient in their waiting for whatever comes after. The upward rain gathers. The conduits hum their subsonic songs. Somewhere, a creature pauses in its chemical foraging to assess your passage. The walls yield slightly under your palm, then firm again, remembering, containing, *persisting*.

You descend further, or you turn back. The choice is yours, though the Hollow has opinions. The sound continues. The water moves. The city dreams its slow, mineral dreams, and you are—briefly, beautifully, inevitably—part of the dream.
