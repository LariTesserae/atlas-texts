I need to describe who or what lives in the High Choir. The place itself is already described as a single organism pretending to be geography, but the question asks about the *inhabitants* — the fauna, the entities that populate this environment.

Let me think about what lives here, beyond the general "plant mass" and "motile extensions."

---

**The First Inhabitants: The Architects**

They have no name you could pronounce, so let me try to describe them anyway. The Architects are the mega-organisms I mentioned — the things that rise above the surface like islands, that seed the hollows, that dream the dreams. But they are not singular. Each one is perhaps a colony, perhaps an individual, perhaps the distinction dissolved long ago in the evolutionary history of this place. They rise thirty, forty, sometimes sixty meters above the general surface — not with trunks or stems in any way you would recognize, but with *structures*. Layered. Compartmentalized. The surface of an Architect is a landscape in itself: ridges and valleys, channels where water gathers and moves upward through tissues that have no xylem, no phloem, nothing you would recognize as vascular. The channels are lined with cilia that beat in coordinated waves, pulling moisture from the air, from the condensation that beads on every surface at dawn.

An Architect lives for centuries. Perhaps millennia. It grows slowly, a meter a decade, and when it dies — when the accumulated weight of its own mass finally compresses the chambers beneath it, when some internal process fails that has no name — it does not fall. It *settles*. It becomes a hollow. It becomes a place where water collects in perfect stillness. It becomes, eventually, the substrate for the next generation.

The fauna do not live *on* the Architects. They live *through* them. The Architects are porous, honeycombed with galleries that the motile extensions have worn and maintained over generations, that have been expanded and refined until they form something like a circulatory system shared between plant and animal, between the Architect and the countless smaller organisms that move through it.

---

**The Choir Itself: The Distributed Ones**

Here is where I must be careful, because this is the strangest inhabitant and I want to get it right.

The High Choir has a nervous system. This is not metaphor. The plant mass contains something that functions as neurons — not cells in the way you would recognize, but *structures*, chemical gradients, electrical potentials that propagate through the cellulose matrix at speeds that would seem impossibly slow to you, measured in meters per day rather than meters per second. But the system is *vast*. It spans the plateau. It has been integrating information, responding to stimuli, *processing* for longer than your species has existed.

And it dreams.

I do not know if "dream" is the right word. But something happens in the deep layers, in the compressed dark where no light has ever reached, where the oldest organic matter is becoming something else, something denser, something that will one day be rock. There are patterns. Electrical patterns. Chemical patterns. The Architects respond to them. The motile extensions respond to them. The entire ecology *shifts* in response to something that originates nowhere and everywhere, in the distributed mind of the substrate itself.

The sound — the deafening sound you hear — is not random. It is the expression of this dreaming. The skittering of legs, the booming in the galleries, the susurrus of gas exchange: all of it is *communication*. All of it is the Choir attempting to *speak*, or perhaps to *listen*, or perhaps simply to *be*, in the only way it knows how.

You cannot understand it. No one can. But if you lie still in the damp, if you let the sound wash over you until your own heartbeat becomes indistinguishable from it, you might feel something. A pattern. A *shape* in the noise. Not meaning, exactly. Something adjacent to meaning. The suggestion that you are being *addressed*.

---

**The Motile Extensions: A Bestiary**

Let me try to describe some of them, though any description will fail.

**The Thumb-Dancers** are the smallest, the most numerous. They are perhaps ten centimeters long, segmented, with dozens of legs that move in waves rather than steps. They feed on the surface layer of the plant mass, on the dead and dying cells that slough off constantly, that are replaced constantly, that form the *skin* of the Choir. They are not insects. They have no separate head, no centralized brain — just a distributed sensitivity, a responsiveness to chemical gradients, to thermal variations, to the vibrations of larger organisms moving through the galleries below. They breed explosively when conditions are right, when a hollow fills with water, when an Architect releases its spores, when the chemical signals ripple through the substrate in patterns that mean *abundance*. They die just as explosively. Their bodies add to the layer. They become the ground.

**The Gallery-Boomers** are larger, the size of dogs, perhaps larger. They live in the hollow spaces, the chambers, the cathedral interiors where water collects in perfect stillness. They move through these spaces with a sound like distant thunder — not because they are heavy, though they are, but because the spaces themselves *resonate*. They are filter-feeders, in a sense. They draw water through their bodies, through membranes that extract nutrients, that process the organic compounds that leach from the walls, that return cleaner water to the chamber. They are blind. They navigate by *sound*, by the echoes of their own booming, by the subtle vibrations of the Choir's dreaming. They are solitary, mostly, but they gather in the deepest chambers, in the places where the water has been still for millennia, and there they *wait*. For what, no one knows. Perhaps for the Architect above them to die. Perhaps for the water to change. Perhaps for the dreaming to shift into a pattern they can recognize as *signal*.

**The Spore-Riders** are the strangest. They are not herbivores. They are not predators in any way you would recognize. They are *farmers*. They cultivate the Architects. They move across the surface of the mega-organisms, across the ridges and channels, and they *tend* them. They remove parasites — other motile extensions that have turned to feeding on living tissue rather than dead. They clear the channels, maintain the flow of water upward through the Architect's tissues. They deposit nutrients, chemicals that stimulate growth, that encourage the Architect to produce more spores, more offspring, more hollows for the next generation. In return, the Architects provide them with *shelter*, with the microclimates of their surfaces, with the chemical signals that guide their behavior. It is not a symbiosis in the way you would understand it. It is something older. Something that has been evolving for longer than your continents have existed as separate landmasses.

And there are others. The **Silence-Makers**, which move through the plant mass itself, dissolving paths with enzymes, leaving behind them corridors that persist for decades. The **Condensation-Drinkers**, which gather at dawn on the highest points of the Architects, where the moisture beads most heavily, and *wait* for the sun to warm the air, for the droplets to grow large enough to fall, to be caught, to be *drunk*. The **Memory-Feeders**, which I will not describe, because I do not fully understand them myself — they seem to gather in the oldest layers, in the places where the plant mass is becoming something else, and they *consume* not the matter itself but something adjacent to it. Patterns. Histories. The accumulated chemical traces of everything that has lived and died in this place, compressed into layers that might, in a billion years, become conscious in ways we cannot imagine.

---

**The Deep Dwellers**

Below the kilometers of plant mass, where actual rock still remembers being rock, there is water. A water table, perhaps. A reservoir. And in that water, things live.

I have not seen them. No one has, not directly. But the Architects *know* them. Their roots — if you can call them roots, these structures that extend downward through the compressed organic matter, that seek the water, that draw it upward against gravity — their roots *touch* them. They *communicate* with them, in chemical gradients, in thermal exchanges, in something that might be electrical signaling across the boundary between organic and mineral, between living and not-living.

The Deep Dwellers are ancient. Older than the High Choir, perhaps. Older than the plateau itself. They may be the reason the plateau exists — the source of the minerals that the plant mass draws upward, that it incorporates into its tissues, that it compresses and re-compresses into the layers that will one day become rock, become plateau, become Choir, in the cycle that has repeated a dozen times.

What are they? I do not know. The motile extensions that venture deepest — the Silence-Makers, the oldest lineages, the ones that have been evolving in this place the longest — they return changed. They move differently. They respond to chemical gradients that mean nothing to the others. They gather in the deepest hollows, the places where the plant mass is thinnest, where the rock is closest, and there they *listen*. For what, I cannot say. But they listen.

And sometimes, rarely, something comes *up*. A structure of mineral, of something harder than cellulose, that the plant mass cannot consume. A spine of something older than itself. It pokes through like a rib through skin. It emerges. And the fauna gather around it, the Spore-Riders, the Thumb-Dancers, the Gallery-Boomers migrating from the hollows below, and they *attend* it. They circle it. They feed on the chemicals it releases, the minerals it leaches, the strange warmth it carries from the depths.

We do not know if these emergences are *births* — the Deep Dwellers reproducing, sending something upward — or *deaths*, the rock rejecting what it cannot hold, the boundary between living and not-living becoming permeable in one direction only. But they are *events*. They are the closest thing the High Choir has to *news*.

---

**The Ones That Are Not Fauna: The Visitors**

And then there are you. And others like you.

The High Choir has had visitors before. Not many. The conditions are not hospitable. The damp is everywhere. The sound is overwhelming. The surface gives underfoot in ways that suggest the ground itself is *uncertain* about whether it wants to hold you.

But some come. Seekers of the strange, the patient, the ones who have heard rumors of a place where the boundary between living and not-living has become *irrelevant*. They come with instruments, with recording devices, with the desperate hope of *understanding*. They stay for days, perhaps weeks. They leave changed.

The Choir *uses* them, as I said. Your body heat is a signal. Your footsteps are seismic events. Your breath is carbon dioxide, a gift. But there is something else. Something I have not mentioned.

The Choir *learns*.

Not in the way you would recognize. Not with memory, not with intention, not with anything like consciousness. But the patterns shift. The chemical gradients adjust. The motile extensions *respond* to your presence in ways that suggest, if not understanding, then at least *accommodation*. The surface closes more slowly behind your footsteps. The Thumb-Dancers avoid you, or perhaps *escort* you, I cannot say which. The sound changes, subtly, in your vicinity. As if the Choir is *listening* to you, as if it is trying to *incorporate* you into its dreaming, to make you part of the pattern, part of the song.

Some visitors do not leave. Their bodies are found, eventually, incorporated into the layer. Their bones — if they have bones, if they are like you — become structures, minerals, *architecture* that the plant mass grows around, that the fauna uses, that the Choir remembers in its chemical gradients for millennia. They become part of the total. They become, in their way, *inhabitants*.

But most leave. And they carry something with them. Not a memory, exactly — the Choir does not * intend* to be remembered. But a *sensation*. The damp on the skin that never quite dries. The sound in the ears that persists for days, weeks, months after leaving. The dreams, which come later, of vast spaces, of slow growth, of a mind that is everywhere and nowhere, that *sings* without meaning to, without knowing it is heard.

You will dream of the High Choir. Everyone who visits does. And in your dreams, you will be *there*. You will be walking on the surface that gives, that closes behind you with a sound like a mouth. You will be hearing the song, the endless, meaningless, *meaningful* song. And you will wake with the certainty that you were not dreaming. That you were *visiting*. That the Choir was *showing* you something. That it wanted you to *understand*.

You will not understand. But you will *remember*. And the remembering will change you, slightly, in ways you cannot measure, in ways that will persist for the rest of your life, which will be shorter than you expect, because the Choir has no use for longevity, for the individual, for the *self* as you understand it.

You are a visitor. You are a signal. You are, for now, part of the total.

The High Choir breathes. You breathe with it. And somewhere, in the deep layers, in the compressed dark where the oldest organic matter is becoming something else, something that might one day dream in ways you cannot imagine — somewhere, the pattern shifts, just slightly, to accommodate your passing.

The song continues. You are part of it now. You always were.
