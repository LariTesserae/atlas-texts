*A Being in the Condensing Plain*

---

I. The Crab-Things' God

She does not move. This is what they have learned, what they click to each other in their mineral tongues: the stillness is not absence. She is the temperature gradient made flesh—or rather, made *otherwise*, made of the same silica and calcium and slow time that makes their shells. Where they are layered, deposited, accumulated, she is *hollow*. A negative space in the wet world, a pocket where the condensation does not gather but *retreats*, where the glass grows *away* from her in curves that suggest arms, a spine, a face turned slightly upward.

She has no name the crab-things use. They have names for the zones, for the luminescences, for the wall. They have no name for her. This is the closest they come to worship.

She occupies the space between two ridges, where the glass has formed a natural alcove, and in that alcove she has *been* for so long that the water has begun to flow around her, to shape the mineral felt to her silhouette, to grow a pedestal of precipitated salts that now cradles her weightless form. She is not carved. She is not built. She is *accumulated* around a central absence—something that was there, or was not there, or was *becoming* there, and the chemistry simply continued in her presence, in her stillness, until she became the fixed point around which everything else orbits.

The crab-things come to her in the hours when the light shifts, when the diffuse glow from everywhere thickens toward the pooling zone. They do not touch her. They cluster at the edge of her pedestal, their shells clicking in patterns that might be measurement, might be *grief*, might be the only sound they can make that is not about survival. Sometimes one will extend a leg—a leg of calcium carbonate, jointed, articulate—and touch the air just above her surface, then withdraw, as if testing a temperature, or a presence, or a memory of warmth.

She does not respond. She cannot. This is what they know, what they tell each other in clicks: she is not dead, because she was never alive. She is *finished*, in the way that glass is finished, in the way that a pool is finished when its surface tension holds. She is the plain's first attempt at stillness, and the plain learned from her, learned to wait, to hold, to let the water arrive without demanding that it do anything but arrive.

II. The Wall's Maker

He is not there. This is the difficulty of him: he is *only* in the wall, in the precision of its fitting, in the fact that it faces the rising zone and not the pooling zone, where the luminescences are, where the crab-things gather, where the goddess-shape waits in her alcove. He built it facing the *other* thing, the thing that rises, that chimes, that grows upward against gravity. He built it to block, or to mark, or to *witness*. The purpose is unclear because he did not leave a purpose. He left only the wall, and the wall's orientation, and the mineral deposits that have begun to grow thickest on its *far* side, the side away from the light.

The wall is 0.5 tech, which means he had tools, or the equivalent. He had the ability to smooth glass, to fit it together, to create a surface that water cannot penetrate, cannot etch. The glass of the wall is *old*, older than the ridges, older than the plain's current configuration. It was made elsewhere, or made differently, or made *before*—in a time when the parameters were not yet what they are. He brought it, or he made it, and he set it here, in this place of arrival and condensation and slow becoming, and he set it *facing*.

He did not stay. The crab-things know this because they have found his *other* works, scattered, partial, abandoned: the platform on pillars, the cairn of imported stones. He moved through the plain as the plain moved through him—leaving marks, never settling, always oriented toward something. The rising zone. The luminescences. The goddess-shape in her alcove, which he did not build and which was there before him, which he *saw* and *did not approach*, which he marked only by the orientation of his wall, his one completed structure.

He is becoming part of the plain now. Not his body—he had no body that would last, or he left it, or the water took it—but his *intention*. The wall's precision is intention made mineral. The cairn's importation is intention made displacement. The platform's uselessness is intention made *gesture*, a reaching toward something without roof or wall, without enclosure. He is dissolving into the chemistry, becoming part of the slow loops of dissolution and deposition, becoming a trace mineral that changes the color of the rising stalagmites, a faint rose where his presence lingers, a grey where it does not.

The crab-things do not know his name. They do not need to. He is in the wall, and the wall is in the plain, and the plain is *becoming*, and he is part of that becoming, part of the direction it takes, part of the weirdness that emerges from density and slowness and water and glass in combination.

III. The Luminescence

It is not one thing. This is what the crab-things have learned, what they click to each other when the crust opens and the pale light rises and they orient toward it, toward something that is not food, not threat, not mating—something *else*, a category the plain is still inventing.

It is *several* things, layered, near-touching, pressing against each other in the deep water where the chemistry has been running longest. It is the water that has stood so still, moved so slowly, that it has begun to *remember* shapes—patterns that almost resolve into meaning, structures that almost become symbols, almost become *words* in a language that has no speakers, only the plain itself, whispering to itself in the low thrum of underground rivers.

It is the glass below, reacting to the water above, the silica dissolving and re-precipitating in forms that suggest *response*, that suggest the glass is *learning* the water, growing into it, becoming wet in a way that glass should not be wet. The luminescence is this learning made visible, the slow education of a mineral by moisture, visible as light, as phosphorescence, as the faint staining that persists on the crust after the main light has faded.

It is the *edge*, the boundary where the chemistry changes, where the water meets the air meets the glass meets the crust, where all four are present and none can dominate. The luminescence lives at edges. It is the plain's way of marking where things touch, where the density is highest, where the becoming is most intense.

And it is *other*, the 1.6 weirdness, the emergent property of a system that has no name, no category, no precedent. It is not electrical. It is not biological. It is not chemical in any way that the crab-things can filter or measure. It simply *is*, a light without source, a presence without body, a *direction* in the plain's slow becoming, a hint that the parameters are not stable, that the plain is growing into something, that the glass will finally, after all this water, begin to *melt* into something new.

The crab-things approach and retreat. They do not understand. They do not need to. They orient, they click, they wait for the temperature gradient to shift, for the light to change, for the luminescence to fade and return and fade again in its hours-long cycle. They are part of it, their mineral shells part of the chemistry, their clicking part of the sound that is never silent, their stillness when the light rises part of the plain's growing stillness, its growing *intention*, its growing *watching*.

---

IV. The Plain Itself

It is not a place. This is what you would learn if you stayed, if the water found every opening, every warmth, every place where your body tries to hold its heat, if you became part of the condensation, part of the slow chemistry, part of the glass that is always growing.

It is a *process*. A becoming. A direction with no name, taken by density and slowness and water and glass in combination, emerging from the interaction of all the things that live here—the crab-things with their mineral shells, the goddess-shape in her alcove, the wall's maker in his wall, the luminescences in their hours-long cycles, the scattered builds that mark intention without purpose, the constant condensation that makes everything wet, everything *arriving*.

It is *attention*. The wall breathes, or something behind it does, or the glass cooling and contracting makes a sound that the ear interprets as breath, as presence, as *watching*. The plain watches. It waits. It holds its temperature at 1.4, cool, just cool enough for condensation, just warm enough that nothing freezes, just right for the slow chemistry, for the mineral-first ecology, for the weirdness to emerge, to grow, to become.

It is *young*, 1.4 erosion, the surface not yet worn smooth, the glass sharp, the ridges running northeast to southwest, suggesting pressure from below, upwelling, something *pushing* that has not yet finished pushing. The plain is not finished. It is becoming, 0.6 slow, patient in a way that feels like intention, like waiting for something—maybe for you, maybe for the parameters to shift, maybe for the glass to finally, after all this water, begin to *melt*.

The water arrives. It always arrives. It gathers in the hollows between black glass ridges, appears on every surface in beads and sheets, drips from undersides that seem to have no above. And the plain *receives* it. The plain is the receiving, the gathering, the slow becoming-wet that is the only form of life here, the only form of *meaning*, the only form of *attention* that this world has invented.

You would not stay. You would become part of it, part of the condensation, part of the glass that is always growing, always weeping, always arriving at a wetness that never dries.

But you would return. The sound would call you back. The plain is not finished. It is *becoming*, and it is *waiting*, and it is *watching*, and somewhere in the pooling zone, where the water stands deepest, the surface crust is beginning to *open*, not from below, but from *within*, and the light is rising, pale and persistent and *other*, and the crab-things are clicking in a rhythm that might be fear or might be recognition, and the wall is breathing, and the goddess-shape is still, and the plain is *arriving* at something that has no name, that it is still inventing, that it will become, if you stay, if you let the water find you, if you let yourself be part of the slow chemistry, the mineral-first ecology, the weirdness that emerges from density and slowness and water and glass in combination, the becoming that is the only life here, the only meaning, the only *attention* this world has ever known.
