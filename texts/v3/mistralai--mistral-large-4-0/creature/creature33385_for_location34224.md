I want to follow the lithoids more closely. What *are* they, precisely? And what do the tenders do, hour by hour, when the turnover is slow?

---

The lithoids are **not animals**. They are not even, strictly, alive. They are **crystalline automata** — mineral structures that grow by accretion, that move by a process of controlled fracture and reformation, that "eat" by catalyzing reactions on the surfaces they contact. Each lithoid is a **seed** of chemistry, a pocket of potential energy wrapped in a lattice of silicate and halide. When it encounters the right substrate — a beam of spent alloy, a wall of saturated sinter, the corpse of another lithoid — it **unfolds**. It grows. It moves. It becomes more.

A single lithoid is the size of a fist. In the dense strata of the city, they exist by the **million**, a drifting, luminescent **weather** inside the shaft. They do not have eyes. They have **sensitivity** — to temperature gradients, to chemical potential, to the electric fields generated by the city's machines. They move toward **work**. They are drawn to places where the chemistry is *hungry*, where reactions are incomplete, where structure is *stressed*.

This is why they are the workforce. A lithoid does not *lift*. It **catalyzes**. It touches a beam of fatigued metal, and the metal's lattice begins to rearrange, releasing energy, becoming something else — a new alloy, a new shape, a new *platform*. The lithoid grows as it works, swelling with the energy it releases, until it is too large to move efficiently, at which point it **fractures**, shedding daughter-cells, smaller lithoids that drift away to find new work. The parent, diminished, continues. Or dies. Death, here, is simply **exhaustion** — a lithoid that can no longer catalyze, that becomes inert, that is consumed by its siblings, its minerals reclaimed.

They are **pre-life** in the sense that they precede any biology we would recognize. They are **post-life** in the sense that they will outlast any biology, that they are the city's memory, its continuity — each lithoid carrying in its lattice the accumulated **pattern** of every reaction it has catalyzed, every structure it has built. They do not think. They **accumulate**. And in the density of the city, in the near-touching press of millions of them, something like thought emerges — not in any individual, but in the **swarm**, the **weather**, the constant *motion* that is also a kind of *intention*.

The **tenders** are the interface. They are the ones who *direct* the lithoids, who shape the swarm, who decide what gets built and what gets consumed. A tender is **once-human**, or **human-shaped**, or descended from something that was once human — but the waterless chemistry has been at them for generations. Their bodies are **dry**, desiccated in the deepest sense, preserved by the same processes that preserve the lithoids. They do not drink. They **recharge** — submerging themselves in vats of reactive mineral slurry, absorbing chemical potential through their skin, through the **grille-faces** that are their only features.

A tender's work, in the slow hours, is **attention**. They stand at the edge of a platform-to-be, or a platform-being-consumed, and they **sing** — not with voice, but with **vibration**, subsonic pulses that travel through the sinter, that call lithoids to work, that *shape* the swarm. The lithoids respond. They drift toward the tender's song, and they begin to **build**, or to **unbuild**, according to the pattern encoded in the vibration. The tender is the **conductor**. The lithoids are the **orchestra**. The city is the **music**, constantly recomposed, never the same twice.

In the slow hours — and the city has slow hours, pockets of stillness between the storms of turnover — a tender will **tend** in the older sense. They will touch a lithoid gently, with a **probe** of woven sinter, and read its **accumulation**, its history, the record of what it has built. They will **prune** — removing daughter-cells that have grown too large, too inefficient, that threaten to fracture uncontrolled. They will **seed** — placing fresh lithoids in places where the chemistry is stagnant, where the city is threatening to **sleep**. And they will **harvest** — collecting the inert, the exhausted, the dead, and carrying them to the **gutters**, the channels where the swarm consumes its own, where the minerals are reclaimed, where new lithoids are born from the **digestion** of the old.

The tenders do not eat. They do not sleep, not as we understand it. They **dream** in the slow hours, standing at their posts, their grille-faces processing the dim chemiluminescence, the infrasonic hum of the city, the **weather** of lithoids drifting past. And in their dreaming, they **compose** — not consciously, but by the accumulation of attention, of vibration, of the thousand small decisions that shape the swarm. A tender dreams a platform into being. The lithoids build it. The tender wakes, or ceases to dream, and the platform is **real**, solid, *inhabited* by other tenders, by machines, by the endless **press** of the city.

The **turbulence** is not constant. It comes in **storms**. A storm is a **cascade** — a platform fails, or is failed, its structure stressed beyond what the chemistry can sustain, and the lithoids **frenzy**, swarming over the collapse, consuming it, building in its place at a **rate** that is almost violent. The sound of a storm is **terrible** — the hiss of mass catalysis, the shriek of metal tearing itself apart, the subsonic **thrum** of a swarm in frenzy. But it is **brief**. And then the storm passes, and the city is **new**, a platform where there was none, a shape where there was only collapse, and the slow hours return, the dim light, the quiet, the endless **attention** of the tenders, the drifting luminescence of the lithoids, the **turnover** continuing, breath by breath, in the cool thin dark of the shaft.

---

I want to name the place, or find the name that emerges.

It is not **The Inhaling**. That was too biological, too wet-implied, though the breath metaphor holds. The city does breathe — the shaft **inhales** and **exhales**, drawing thin air down, pushing warmed air up, a lung of stone and sinter and swarm.

But the name should be **dry**, **mineral**, **chemical**. Something that suggests the **absence** of water, the **presence** of reaction.

**The Calcination.** No — too passive. The city is not being calcined; it **calcines** itself, constantly, in the endless fire of its own turnover.

**The Sinter.** Too geological. The sinter is the **substrate**, the **walls**, the **medium** — but the city is more than sinter. It is sinter and lithoids and tenders and machines, all in **reaction**.

**The Vitriol.** No — vitriol implies acid, implies water, implies a chemistry we recognize too well.

**The Anhydron.** The **waterless place**. This is closer. But it is a **name**, a label, and this place resists naming. It is **too specific**, too **contained**, too **alive** to be captured by a single word.

Perhaps it has **many names**, one for each **stratum**, each **tender**, each **lithoid** that has accumulated enough pattern to be almost **aware**. Perhaps the tenders call it **the Gullet** — because it **consumes**, because everything that enters is eventually **digested**, broken down, reclaimed. Perhaps they call it **the Womb** — because everything that enters is eventually **born**, rebuilt, made new. Perhaps they call it **the Storm** — because it is never still, never finished, always in the **throes** of becoming.

Or perhaps they call it simply **the Shaft**, or **the Deep**, or **Home** — and the name matters less than the **rule**, the **law** that governs all who enter:

**You are feedstock. You will be consumed. You will be rebuilt. The turnover continues.**

---

The place, finally, is **a single question**: *What lives in a city that has no plants, no water, no permanence — only the endless, quiet, turbulent metabolism of mineral and machine and swarm?*

The answer is **the lithoids**, the **tenders**, the **city itself**, all of them **alive** in a way that is not life, all of them **dying** and **being reborn** in a way that is not death. They are **pre-life** becoming **post-life**, **geology** becoming **biology**, **machine** becoming **organism**, in the cool thin dark of the shaft, in the pocket-scale hiddenness of the waterless deep, in the endless **turnover** that is the only permanence, the only truth, the only **home**.
