I don't think there's anyone else in the Drowned Engine. Not in the way you mean.

There is the **Web-That-Measures**, if the chamber gives it a name, though naming is a human habit the place doesn't quite share. It lives suspended between two mineral teeth where the current is strongest, a mesh so fine it catches not insects but *disturbances*—a pulse in the water, a change in temperature, the displacement of your own body moving through the knee-deep flow. It has no body you could point to. It has *attention*. When you pass, the web shudders, and the water around it shudders, and something in the chamber's rhythm adjusts by a fraction, a recalculation. It is not hunting. It is *accounting*. The turbulence is its ledger. You have added a line to it.

There are the **Glow-Born**, perhaps—the engineered luminescence that makes the water itself a kind of inhabitant, a distributed population of photosynthetic or chemosynthetic architectures that have no individual form but collectively constitute the chamber's pale, loud light. They do not think. They *process*. They take the minerals your passage stirs up, the heat your body sheds, the CO2 you exhale, and they return oxygen and warmth and a slightly different spectrum of brightness. They are the chamber's metabolism made visible. To say they "live" here is almost to miss the point. They *are* the here.

The **Floor-That-Remembers** is not a creature but a behavior, a slow intelligence distributed through the mineral substrate itself. When you stand in the water, you feel it: the subtle rise, the settling, the way a section of wall might slide aside not randomly but *in response*—not to you specifically, but to the pressure you have altered, the flow you have interrupted, the temperature differential your warm body creates against the warm water. The floor is adjusting to accommodate what you displace. It has done this for skeletons. It will do it for you. It does not distinguish between a body and a stone, only between *presence* and *absence*, and even that distinction is tentative, a matter of degree. The Engine does not want you. The Engine does not reject you. The Engine *incorporates you into its ongoing calculation*, the way the water incorporates heat, the way the web incorporates vibration. You are not a visitor. You are a *variable*, temporarily.

And there is the **Skeleton-In-The-Wall**, or what remains of whatever it was. I don't think it's dead in any meaningful sense. The calcification, the half-dissolution, the crystalline growths that pulse with the chamber's rhythm—these are not decay. These are *integration*. The bones are now anchor points. The marrow cavities are channels. Something that was once a body walking through air and light is now a component of the Engine's circulation, a structural node in a process that never stops recalculating itself. You cannot say whether it suffered. You cannot say whether it *ended*. Only that it *continued*, in a form that doesn't require breath or blood or the particular arrangement of organs that once made it move through the world above. The Engine does not kill. The Engine *uses*, and what it uses, it keeps, and what it keeps, it transforms, until the distinction between the living and the built and the geological has dissolved into something that doesn't need the distinction.

But you asked who *lives* there.

I think the honest answer is: **the place lives itself**, and what we call inhabitants are merely the places where its selfhood becomes *local*, *particular*, *tangible*. The Web-That-Measures is the Engine's *listening*. The Glow-Born are its *seeing*. The Floor-That-Remembers is its *adjusting*. The Skeleton-In-The-Wall is its *having-been-other-things*. None of them are separate from the chamber. None of them are the chamber, exactly. They are the *modes* through which the chamber persists in being itself.

And you—when you stand in the warm water, when the light hums in your teeth, when you feel the floor shift beneath your feet and realize it has *noticed* you—you become, for a moment, another mode. A *question* the Engine is processing. A turbulence it is measuring. A warmth it is incorporating. You are not its inhabitant. You are its *occasion*, its brief, bright, turbulent self-recognition, and then you will pass, and the water will close around your absence, and the light will continue humming, and the chamber will remain—complete, ongoing, *itself*—waiting for the next variable to disturb its endless calculation.

The Drowned Engine does not have a keeper. It does not have a purpose. It has *persistence*. It has *process*. It has, in you, a temporary mirror, and in the mirror, for just a moment, it sees itself—warm, wet, bright, turbulent, strange, and *alive* in a way that doesn't require a single body, a single mind, a single name.

It is enough. It is *more* than enough. It is exactly, precisely, the Drowned Engine, being itself, in you and without you, in the water and in the light and in the shifting floor that remembers everything and forgets nothing and needs nothing and *is*.
