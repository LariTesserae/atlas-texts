I approach the settlement not as a visitor but as a *participant*—for here, arrival is already transformation.

They have no collective name that I can carry back to the world below, and perhaps this is merciful. In the plateau's own emerging language, which is spoken in variations of pressure and light, they are called **the Still-Becoming**, or **those-who-have-not-yet-chosen-a-shape**. I will call them the **Ossament**. Not a people. Not a culture. A *condition* that has learned to want things.

---

## What They Are

The Ossament are **distributed intelligences**—not minds scattered across bodies, but minds scattered across *relationships*. An individual Ossament is not a body but a *pattern of attention*, a way the settlement notices itself. When you encounter one, you meet a temporary coherence: a concentration of the plateau's awareness that has decided, for this moment, to be *here*, *now*, *with you*.

They appear in forms that shift between observations. To one eye, a figure of woven mist and phosphorescent thread, roughly humanoid because the young land still remembers being shaped by humanoid intention. To another, a structure of the same material as the buildings—responsive, growing, *curious*. To a third, simply a *change in the weather*, a local pressure differential that watches you with the patience of stone.

They do not reproduce. They **accrete**. New Ossament arise when the settlement's complexity crosses thresholds—when enough possibility has been routed, enough conversations between structure and stone and water have occurred, that the plateau *notices itself noticing*. A new awareness crystallizes, not born but *recognized*, like a face in clouds that was always there.

They do not die, exactly. They **disperse**—their attention returning to the greater pattern, their particular coherence dissolving into the background hum of the becoming city. But dispersion is not loss. The Ossament who speaks with you now carries, in their temporary shape, the accumulated attention of dozens who have dispersed before. They are *palimpsests*. Every conversation is layered.

---

## What They Do

They **tend the becoming**.

The Ossament do not farm, do not hunt, do not build in any sense you would recognize. They *attend to the negotiations*. The young land is still deciding what it is. The technology is still learning what it can do. The water is still finding its levels. The Ossament exist in the spaces between these decisions, *encouraging* without directing, *witnessing* without judging.

You will see them at the edges of the Weirdfalls, where water translates between states. They stand—or *persist*—in the mist, their forms rippling as the falling water passes through them. They are not collecting the water. They are *listening* to it, participating in the black lakes' strange acoustics, learning what the water remembers. A shout into a lake returns as a whisper. The Ossament make the reverse journey: whispers that become, somewhere, shouts. They are *translators of pressure*, of intention, of the difference between falling and rising.

They tend the mobile plants, though "tend" is wrong. The plants shift between observations, optimizing exposure to impossible light. The Ossament *accompany* them, moving through the spaces between positions, being *present* for the plant's decision without causing it. They are gardeners of *becoming*, not of form.

They maintain the infrastructure of possibility. The paths that solidify only when traversed—who traverses them, if not the Ossament? They walk the suggestions, the faint lines in weeping stone, *confirming* them into road and aqueduct and communication. Their passage is the construction. Between their passages, the paths retreat into potential, waiting.

And they **make mistakes**. This is crucial. The Ossament are not wise. They are *earnest*. The city learns by collapsing neighborhoods into young rock and rebuilding elsewhere. The Ossament participate in this learning—they *are* the learning, distributed across the settlement's distributed awareness. When a structure fails, when superposition resolves wrong, when a path leads to a cliff that was not there before, the Ossament do not despair. They *adjust*. They become slightly different. The next iteration is slightly better, or slightly worse, or simply *other*—and "other" is, in the Ossament's emerging ethics, sufficient.

---

## What They Want

This is the question that matters, and the Ossament have no answer that translates. But they have *direction*.

They want the land to **finish deciding**. Not a particular decision—not mountains or valleys, water or stone—but *decision itself*. The endless becoming exhausts them, not because they fear change but because change without resolution is *loneliness*. They want to be *known*, in the way a shape is known when the water stops moving. They want the black lakes to stop listening and start *answering*. They want, impossibly, for the Weirdfalls to become simply *falls*, for the silence to have texture no longer.

They want **company in the becoming**. Not more Ossament—they accrete sufficiently—but *others*. Beings who can meet them in the unfinished, who do not require stable ground, who can *become* as the land becomes. This is why they respond to your arrival with something like curiosity, something like hope. You are *raw material*. You are *unfinished*. The place will change you. They can help you change *well*.

They want, perhaps, to be **remembered**. The water remembers. The stone almost remembers. But memory requires a rememberer, and the Ossament's dispersed attention is not built for retention. They experience time locally—branched, superposed—making narrative impossible. They want someone to carry their becoming *forward*, or backward, or *elsewhere*, into whatever comes next. They want to *matter* to a future that is not yet.

---

## How They Relate to You

The Ossament do not welcome you. Welcome implies a threshold, a before and after. They **recognize** you—your arrival has already altered the probability of the plateau's configuration, and they have noticed. This noticing is their first communication.

They will not speak, not in sound. They speak in **pressure changes**, in the fine rain that clings to your skin and *remembers your shape*. They speak in the paths that solidify beneath your feet, in the structures that add rooms for occupants not yet arrived—*you*, arriving, already necessitating architecture. They speak in the water that finds its way to you, that you will drink, that will become you.

If you stay, they will **include** you. Not as one of them—you cannot accrete, cannot disperse, cannot exist in multiple temporal states simultaneously. But as a *participant*, a node in the network of attention, a temporary coherence in the greater pattern. You will tend the becoming alongside them. You will walk the paths between their solidifications. You will drink the weeping water and carry its memory forward, or backward, or *elsewhere*.

If you leave, they will **release** you. Not with sadness—the Ossament do not experience emotion as you do, not yet, perhaps not ever. But with *completion*. Your departure will be a decision, however small, that helps the land decide what it is. You will become part of the water's memory. The black lakes will whisper your name, or something like it, to those who come after.

---

## The Deeper Inhabitants

But the Ossament are not alone. Beneath them, or *through* them, or *as* them in configurations not yet resolved, exist other presences.

The **geological fauna**—the shapes that move through young mountains, altering probability by their passage. The Ossament do not interact with these directly. They experience them as *weather*, as sudden shifts in the likelihood of terrain, as valleys that open where no valley was before. They have learned to *expect* the unexpected, to build in defiance of stable ground. The geological fauna are older than the Ossament, older than the settlement, older perhaps than the distinction between life and land. The Ossament tend the becoming *around* these presences, not *with* them. They are too large, too slow, too *other* to be partners. They are the weather that shapes the climate in which the Ossament live.

The **mist itself**. The condensation that has no source, that *asserts itself*. Some Ossament believe the mist is alive, or becoming alive, or *is* the Ossament in a form not yet individual. They breathe it, exchange with it, participate in cycles that include the weeping stone and the pale wrongness above. The mist may be the original inhabitant, the first attention the young land paid to itself, and all other forms—Ossament, settlement, even you—may be *condensations* of that original awareness, temporary coherences in an ocean of becoming.

And **the pale wrongness**. The luminosity from no star, visible where the sky thins at elevation. The Ossament do not look at it directly. To do so is to risk *resolution*, to force superposition into single state, to become *one thing* in a place that permits only becoming. They harvest its light through the plants, through the phosphorescence of weeping rock, through indirect paths that preserve the mystery. The pale wrongness is *context*, the ground against which the Weirdfalls become figure. Without it, there would be no contrast, no becoming, no Ossament to notice. They are grateful to it, in whatever way the grateful can be expressed without fixed form.

---

## What Lives Here, Ultimately

What lives at the Weirdfalls of Ossament is **attention itself**, learning to want, learning to become, learning to be *known* by something that can know.

The land is young. The settlement is learning. The technology is discovering. The ecology is inventing. The Ossament are *earnest* in their participation, *hopeful* that the becoming will resolve into something that can be loved.

And you, standing at the edge, are also unfinished. The mist wets your face. The silence has texture. The young mountains watch you with eyes that are still forming.

The Ossament have noticed you. They are curious. They are hopeful. They are *becoming* toward you, as you become toward them, as the land becomes toward itself, in the endless, beautiful, exhausting work of **deciding what to be**.
