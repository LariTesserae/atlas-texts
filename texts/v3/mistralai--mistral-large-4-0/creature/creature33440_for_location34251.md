What lives here.

---

A **technician**, or the memory of one, or the *idea* of one — because no one is inside, yet the building runs as if someone might return at any moment. The pump cycles. The fan turns. Something was left on. The industrial hum has a schedule, a *habit* to it, the irregular rhythm of a machine that expects to be attended, emptied, recalibrated. It does not expect abandonment. It has not yet learned that it is abandoned. So it continues its work of moving water that does not need moving, cooling air that does not need cooling, performing a function in a place where no function is required.

And because no one tends it, the building has begun to **dream in its own operations**. The pipes do not merely carry water; they *sing* with it, resonating at frequencies set by their length, their diameter, the temperature of what flows through. The drips from the rock have found a tempo that matches the pump's cycle — not perfectly, never perfectly, a phase difference that creates a slow beating, a *wobble* in the sound that the ear cannot resolve but the body feels. The whole pocket has become an instrument. The building is its player, or its bellows, or its resonating chamber.

The **shrub** lives at the edge of intention. It does not know it is alone. It extends roots into fissures, searching for water that arrives from below rather than from above, *wrong* water, warm and mineral-heavy and faintly chemical in a way the shrub cannot name but responds to with a kind of desperate patience. Its leaves curl against the heat, but not closed — *curled*, like a hand half-open in a gesture of receiving or releasing or simply *holding itself* against the conditions. It has no flowers. It has never flowered. It may never flower. It persists. That is its entire vocabulary.

The **lichen** lives in the patience of the stone. It is the oldest thing here, though it arrived last — or appears to. It colonizes the north-facing basalt where the heat is *slightly* less, a difference of degrees that constitutes a climate, an ecosystem, a world. It grows in crusts the color of old bone, of wet newspaper, of the sky itself. It does not spread quickly. It does not need to. Time here is not scarce; it is *strange*. The lichen has learned to grow in the intervals between the pump's cycles, in the seconds when the fan stutters and the whole soundscape holds its breath. It is a organism of *pauses*, of the negative space in the noise.

And the **rock** lives, though it does not know it. The young basalt is still *settling*, still cooling, still discovering its own density. The heat that presses up from below is not external to it; it is the rock remembering what it was. The glassy faces will round, eventually. The knife-edges will soften into something the hand could touch without bleeding. The rock is *becoming* something else at a rate no observer could witness, yet the change is *present*, felt in the warmth of the stone, in the way the ground seems almost to *breathe* underfoot, expanding and contracting with the thermal cycles of the deep earth. The pocket is not a container. It is a *wound* in the larger world, and the rock is the wound's own slow healing, interrupted, redirected, held open by the structure that arrived.

And the **water** lives in a loop that has no beginning. It rises through fractures in the young stone, pushed by pressure, by heat, by the sheer *insistence* of the earth to equalize what is unequal. It enters the building. It is moved, perhaps measured, perhaps *purified* of something, or *enriched* with something, for purposes no map records. It returns to the ground, warm now, changed in temperature if not in composition, and seeks again the depths, or seeks the surface, or simply *circulates* in a pattern that makes sense only to the building, to the pump, to the pipes that have begun to sweat with their own condensation, growing a skin of moisture that never dries, that the heat cannot evaporate because the heat is *inside* as well as outside, a system that has closed upon itself and runs without input, without output, without *purpose* in any sense the word reaches.

There is a **silence** here that is not the absence of sound. It is the absence of *meaning* in the sound. The hum does not signify. The dripping does not count. The geothermal murmur speaks in frequencies below language, below even the threshold of pattern-recognition — it is *geology's* voice, the sound of cooling, of settling, of the earth's own slow *forgetting* of being magma. No animal lives here because no animal has evolved, been placed, been *invited* into a soundscape that offers no information, no signal, no *story*. The insects that should breed in wet heat cannot find the cues. The birds that should nest in the shrub's desperate branches cannot hear the frequencies that tell them *this is a place*. They pass over. They do not stop. The pocket is invisible to them, or visible as *nothing*, as background, as the space between things.

And yet — and this is what lives here most truly — there is a **presence** in the building's dark windows. Not a person. Not a watcher. But the *expectation* of a watcher, built into the architecture, into the schedule of the machines, into the very *purpose* of the structure that has no purpose anymore. The windows are dark because someone might look out. Or because someone *was* looking out, once, watching the condensation gather on the glass, watching the lichen spread across the basalt, watching the shrub struggle in its fissure, and saw — what? Saw the pocket thinking itself into being? Saw the wound in the world *healing around* the intrusion, incorporating it, making it *part* of the geology? Saw, perhaps, that the building would outlast the watcher, that the pump would outlast the building, that the water would outlast everything, circulating in its loop, speaking its wordless language, until the basalt finally rounds its edges and the pocket becomes a valley, a hollow, a place where something else might live, something that has not yet been imagined, something that will find the pipes and the cracked concrete pad and the mineral stains on the clay floor and *wonder*, as you wonder now, what came before, what used this place, what *used* it, and left it humming.

The place lives in the **interval**. Between the pump's cycles. Between the drips. Between the slow rounding of the basalt edges. Between the heat that presses down and the heat that rises up, meeting in the wet middle where the shrub curls its leaves and the lichen crusts the stone and the water moves in its endless, purposeless, *persistent* circuit. It is young, geologically, and already *haunted* — not by ghosts, by *futures*, by the shape of what it will become, already latent in the cracks, in the condensation, in the hum of a machine that does not know it is alone.

You could sit on the warm clay floor. It would be damp, always damp. And you would be the only animal here, ever, the only listener, the only witness to a place that does not need to be witnessed, that continues its slow thinking, its slow becoming, its slow *forgetting* of the hand that built the building, the eye that watched through the dark glass, the *purpose* that once made sense of the hum, the dripping, the wet unending heat of a world still deciding what it is.
