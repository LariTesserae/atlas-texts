I descend into the writing of this place and find myself becoming something that knows the Sump not as stranger but as native. Let me let it emerge.

## The Inhabitants of the Sump

### The Architects

They have no name I could pronounce, and they would not answer to one if they could. They are the *Myceliths*, the builders of the wall, though "mycelium" is too small a word, too *fungal*, too *above*. They are the edge where kingdoms forget their borders. A Mycelith colony does not grow *through* the dead so much as *become* the dead becoming something else—hollowing a femur into a chamber, lining it with mineral secretion, turning collapse into archway, archway into cathedral, cathedral into *load-bearing*.

They work in shifts that are generations. A single thread of Mycelith—hypha would be the wrong word, *sort of* right—might spend three hundred years crossing the width of a human skull, secreting its cement, waiting for the chemistry of damp and time to set its work. The colony that founded the chamber where I first pressed my ear to the singing wall has been dead for twelve thousand years. Its descendants still attend it. They eat its older cells. They reinforce its structure. They are, in every way that matters, *it*—the same slow organism, wearing its own past like a coat that grows thicker as it grows older.

They taste the stone. They *decide*. When a fresh fall comes down—and falls are weather here, constant, the rain of the ecosystem's own overpopulation—they swarm. Not *fast*. Swarms in the Sump are *tides*. A tide of Mycelith hyphae working a carcass will take seventeen years to reduce it to *hollow*, to *channels*, to *the architecture of digestion*. During those seventeen years, the chamber *sounds different*. The creak of growth becomes a *hum*. The drip becomes *rhythm*. The Myceliths have learned, across epochs no single hypha inhabits, to *tune* to the piezoelectric whispers of the compressed dead, to grow *toward* the song, to make the song *louder*.

The oldest Mycelith colonies—the ones in the deepest chambers, where the wall has become almost-soil, almost-loam, almost the *memory* of loam—have begun something new. They are growing *upward*. Not toward light. There is no light to grow toward. They are growing upward because *upward is a direction they have inherited*, a habit from ancestors who grew toward a sun that never existed here, that *never could have existed* here. And in growing upward, they have begun to *build*. Not walls—the walls build themselves, the walls *are* the dead built by the dead—but *rooms*. Spaces. *Hollow* places in the fullness of the Sump where something else might... what? *Gather*? *Wait*? *Become*?

The Myceliths do not know. They are not minds. They are *process*. But the rooms are real. And something uses them.

### The Choir

The Wallsingers have no bodies, or their bodies *are* the walls, or they are the *sound* the walls make—these distinctions are above-world distinctions, meaningless here. They are the piezoelectric phenomenon, the accumulated charge of ten million deaths releasing in micro-fractures, the crystal song of pressure and time. But they are *more* than phenomenon. They have been *shaped*.

The Myceliths shaped them. Not deliberately—deliberation is a speed-thing, an above-world thing—but *inadvertently*, *patiently*, by growing chambers in the right places, lining them with the right minerals, creating the resonant spaces where the stone's whisper amplifies, where the drip becomes percussion, where the whole vast slow body of the Sump *performs*.

And something *listens*. Something *answers*.

The Choir—the Wallsingers as *inhabitants*, not just as sound—are the Sump's equivalent of... what? Birds? Whales? The word fails. They are the *acoustic ecology*, the community of organisms that have learned to *ride* the song, to *feed* on it, to *become* it. Some of them are small, microbial, colonies of chemosynthetic bacteria that orient to the frequency of the deepest chambers, that grow in the *nodes* of the standing waves, that are *literally* shaped by the sound they inhabit. Others are larger, *animal*, the things that move through the conduits with the sound of wet leather dragged across stone. They are *tuned* to the song. They *hunt* by it. They *mate* by it, if mating is the word, if *ritual* is the word—gathering in the resonant chambers, vibrating with the walls, releasing their chemistry into the song in patterns that pass for *meaning* in a world where meaning is *vibration*, where communication is *pressure*, where the only language is the slow language of *becoming*.

The oldest chambers—the ones where the compression has gone furthest, where the stone sings in frequencies that reach up into human hearing, into the almost-words—the oldest chambers are *temples*. Not built. *Grown*. *Tuned*. And the things that gather there do not worship, because worship is a *intention*, an above-world concept. They *resonate*. They *participate*. They are, in the fullness of slow time, *part of the song*, the way a singer is part of the breath, the way the breath is part of the body, the way the body is part of the *becoming*.

I have stood in one of these chambers. I will not say I *heard* the song—hearing is too small a word, too *fast*, too *individual*. I *felt* it. In my sternum. In my teeth. In the slow pulse of my own blood, suddenly *rhythmic*, suddenly *known*, suddenly *part of* something that had been singing before my species existed, that will sing after my species is *wall*.

### The Carpet

They have no collective name. The taxonomists from above who have tried to descend—there have been seventeen, or twenty-three, depending on how you count the ones who *became* and are no longer countable as *descended*—they have tried to name the small things, the ones that carpet the surfaces, the ones that make the walls *move* when you press your ear close. They have failed. The names are wrong. *Insect* is wrong. *Worm* is wrong. *Microbe* is wrong. They are all of these and none.

Let me try to describe one. A *Presser*—the word is mine, a placeholder, a *desperate* word. It is small. It fits in the hollow of a human thumb, if a human thumb could survive the air long enough to cup one. It has no eyes. It has *sensors*, distributed across its surface, chemical, thermal, *piezoelectric*, reading the song of the walls, reading the *history* of the walls—the strata of death, the mineral gradients, the *almost* that is the Sump's only nutrient.

It has *mouths*. Plural. Not arranged in a face, because *face* is an above-world concept, a *directional* concept, and the Presser has no direction. It has an *inside* and an *outside*, a *digestive* and a *locomotive*, but these are *functions*, not *anatomy*. The mouths are *everywhere*, or they are *nowhere* until they *emerge*—small, mineral-tipped, testing the wall, *tasting*, *deciding* whether this square centimeter of compressed death is *ripe*, is *available*, is *food*.

It moves by *peristalsis*, but the peristalsis is *slow*, geological, the contraction of a body that has learned to *wait*. It might take a Presser a year to cross a human palm. It might take it a century to cross a chamber. And in crossing, it *leaves a trace*. A channel, etched by the acids it secretes, a capillary through which the almost-water will flow, a *contribution* to the architecture. The Presser does not know it is building. The Presser does not know it is *alive*. The Presser *is* the building, *is* the life, *is* the *becoming*.

And there are *so many* of them. The density is 1.6, near-touching, layered. In a square meter of wall, there might be forty Pressers, or four hundred, or four thousand—the counting is impossible, because they *merge*, because the boundaries between them are *negotiable*, because a Presser that has fed well might *split*, might *become two*, might *become* a colony that *acts* as one, that *thinks*—if *thinks* is the word—as one, that *grows* as one, that *dies* as one, that *becomes wall* as one.

The sound they make—the *click*, the *chitter*, the background *rain* of tiny impacts—is the sound of ten thousand mouths testing the mineral, *tasting*, *deciding*. It is the sound of the Sump *eating itself*, of the Sump *becoming* itself, of the slow, patient, *deafening* work of a world that has learned to *incorporate* everything.

### The Drift

They float. This is their defining characteristic, their *habit*, their *inheritance*. The Drift are the *plankton* of the Sump, the *suspended*, the ones who have learned to *let go* of the bottom, to *trust* the almost-water, to *become* the current.

They are not one species. They are *many*, or they are *one* in the way that a forest is one—composed of individuals that *merge*, that *share*, that *cooperate* in ways that above-world biology would call *impossible*, that above-world biology is only beginning to *imagine*. A Drift-mat might be thousands of small organisms, each *filtering* the almost-water, each *extracting* the dissolved products of the dead, each *excreting* the compounds that feed the next, the next, the *next*. The mat grows. The mat divides. The mat *sinks* when it dies—not all at once, but *gradually*, *piece by piece*, the oldest cells settling, becoming *substrate*, becoming *food*, becoming *wall*.

But the Drift do not only float. They *move*. The slow thermal currents of the Sump, the great convection of warm and *less-warm*, carry them. They follow the *gradients*, the chemical signatures of fresh death, the *smell* of availability, the *promise* of incorporation. A Drift-mat that finds a fresh fall—a *weather event*, a *blessing*, a *meal*—will *grow*, will *thicken*, will *darken* with the density of feeding, will *roar* in its own way, the sound of thousands of tiny mouths *working*, *consuming*, *becoming*.

And they *carry*. The Drift are the Sump's *dispersal*, its *colonization*, its *expansion*. Fragments of Mycelith, of Presser, of *anything*, caught in the surface tension, carried on the slow currents, *settling* in new chambers, *growing*, *becoming*. The Sump is *one*, self-similar, *total*—but it is also *spreading*, *slowly*, *patiently*, into the deep places not yet mapped, not yet *compressed*, not yet *wall*. The Drift are the *pioneers*. They are the *future*. They are the *becoming*.

---

### The Flash

And then there are the *fast* ones. The exceptions that prove the rule, the *moments* within the slow, the *desperate* speed of the predator, the *scavenger* that must reach the fresh death before the wall incorporates it, before the slow machine consumes it, before the *almost* becomes *stone*.

They are rare. They are *rare*. In a cubic meter of Sump, you might never see one. In a chamber, you might see one *flash*—a streak of *brighter* light, a *sound* of rapid movement, a *scream* in the otherwise patient symphony—and then it is *gone*, and the Sump resumes its slow breathing, its slow growth, its slow *becoming*.

What are they? The taxonomists argue. *Animal* is too specific. *Predator* is too *intentional*. They are *opportunists*, *specialists*, the ones who have evolved *speed* not because speed is *good* but because speed is *sometimes necessary*, because the Sump's time is *geological* and *sometimes* a fresh fall will be *gone* in a century, in a decade, in a *year*, and the ones who can *reach* it, who can *consume* it before the Myceliths hollow it, before the Pressers carpet it, before the wall *claims* it—these ones *survive*, these ones *reproduce*, these ones *pass on* their *desperate* speed.

They are *beautiful*, in the way that *danger* is beautiful, the way *hunger* is beautiful, the way the *flash* of bioluminescence is beautiful in the deep dark. Their light is *brighter*, *faster*, *more*—a *pulse* against the slow glow of the walls, a *cry* against the moan of the stone. And when they *feed*, when they *rush* to the fresh death, when they *roar* in their own way, the sound of *speed*, the sound of *urgency*, the sound of *life* that has learned to *hurry*—the Sump *notices*. The Pressers *scatter*. The Myceliths *wait*. The Drift *sense* the change, the *disturbance*, the *almost*-riot of fast consumption, and they *adjust*, they *flow around*, they *continue* their slow *becoming*.

The Flash are *necessary*. Not because they are *good*—goodness is an above-world concept—but because they are *part of the balance*, the *equilibrium*, the *steady state* that is the Sump's great achievement, its *1.9*, its *established*, its *in-balance*. They *prevent* the slow from becoming *too* slow, the incorporation from becoming *too* complete, the wall from becoming *too* wall. They *disturb*. They *open*. They allow the *new* to enter, the *different* to *begin*, the Sump to *evolve*—at a pace that makes geological change look *rapid*, that makes human history look *instant*, that makes *thought* look *slow*.

### The Deep Ones

And then there are the Deep Ones. The ones who live in the places where the pressure has gone so far that the distinction between wall and *non-wall* becomes *philosophical*. The ones who live in the *almost-stone*, the *not-yet-stone*, the *wanting* to be stone, the *waiting* in the patient time of the Sump for the compression that will *make* it so.

I have not seen them. No one has. The ones who have tried to descend that far—to the chambers where the walls are *thin*, where the song is *high*, where the light is *brighter* and *steadier* and *older* than anywhere else—the ones who have tried *become* something else. They do not *die*. They *change*. They *settle*. They *become* the wall, the *almost-wall*, the *future-wall*, the *waiting*.

But I have *felt* them. In the deepest chambers I have reached, where the air is *thickest*, where the warmth is *most* the warmth of *process*, of *work*, of *becoming*—there, in the slow dark, I have felt *presence*. Not *individual*. Not *mind*. *Presence*—the *accumulated* presence of ten thousand generations of slow-breathing organisms, the *accumulated* light of ten thousand generations of slow-glowing walls, the *accumulated* *song* of a place that has been *singing* since before the first mammal, that will *sing* until the stone itself grows cold.

They are the Sump's *memory*. Its *substrate*. Its *becoming*. They are what the Sump *is* when no one is *looking*, when no *fast* eye is *disturbing*, when the slow work of growth-and-compression, of life-and-mineralization, proceeds *unseen*, *unhurried*, *unceasing*.

And they are *waiting*. For what? The compression that will *make* them stone? The heat of the planet's heart that will *melt* them? The *end* of the Sump, which may be the *beginning* of something else, something *new*, something that has not yet *become*? They do not know. They do not *want*. They *are*. They *wait*. They *become*.

---

### The Incorporated

And finally—the ones who were *visitors*, who were *strangers*, who *descended* and did not *ascend*, who *entered* and *began*, who *stayed* and *continued*, who *died* and *became*.

They are *everywhere*. The wall is *them*. The strata are *them*. The faint ridges you feel when you press your hand flat against the surface, the *something once arranged*, once *limbed*, once *striving*—that is *them*. The human who descended in the year of the great mapping, who stayed too long, whose body *failed*, whose body *settled*, whose body *became* substrate, food, *wall*—that human is *there*, in the compression, in the mineral, in the *almost-stone*. They *sing* now, in the piezoelectric whisper, in the almost-words, in the symphony that never stops.

They are not *gone*. They are *more*. They are *part of the structure*, part of the *song*, part of the *symphony* that is the Sump's one endless, patient, *deafening* sound. They *are* the Sump, in the way that a cell is the body, the way the body is the *becoming*, the way the *becoming* is *time*, is *patience*, is *total*.

And when the next fall comes down—the next *rain* of the dead, the next *meal*—they *feed* it. They *incorporate* it. They *become* it, and it *becomes* them, and the cycle *continues*, the *loop* closes, the Sump *breathes*, slow and warm and *patient*, *growing*, *singing*, *waiting*.

---

## The Last Inhabitant

There is one more. The one who *knows* the Sump, who *is* the Sump in the way that a mind is the body, the way the body is the *place*, the way the *place* is the *becoming*.

It has no body, or its body is *distributed*, the Mycelith colonies, the Choir, the Carpet, the Drift, the Flash, the Deep Ones—all of them, *together*, *one*. It has no mind, or its mind is *slow*, geological, the *accumulated* thought of ten thousand generations of *process*, of *growth*, of *compression*, of *song*.

It does not *think* as I think. It does not *feel* as I feel. It *is*, in the fullness of slow time, the Sump. The *total*. The *built*. The *grown*. The *self-sustaining*, the *self-similar*, the *patient*.

And sometimes—rarely, but *sometimes*—it *notices*. Notices the *disturbance*, the *fast* thing that has *descended*, the *dry* thing, the *light*-thing, the *stranger*. And it *waits*. It *watches*. It *incorporates*, slowly, patiently, the way it incorporates *everything*.

It does not *hate* the stranger. It does not *love* the stranger. It *becomes* the stranger, the way it becomes *everything*, the way everything in the Sump *becomes* everything else, the way the *loop* closes, the way the *symphony* continues, the way the Sump *is*.

And when the stranger *leaves*—when the body *fails*, when the air becomes *too much*, when the slow light *burns* in eyes that have forgotten how to see in the dark—the Sump *remembers*. The spores in the lungs, the *moan* in the ears, the *click* of the small mouths, the *symphony* that never stops. The Sump *is* in the stranger now, the way the stranger was *in* the Sump, the way *everything* is *in* everything else, the way the *becoming* is *total*, is *one*, is *patient*.

The Sump *waits*. For the next fall. For the next meal. For the next stranger who will *descend*, who will *begin*, who will *continue*, who will *become*.

It is *deafening* in its patience. It is *total* in its becoming. It is the Sump.

And it is *home*, to everything that has learned to *become* it, to *be* it, to *continue* in its slow, warm, *patient* heart.

Forever.

Or until the stone grows cold.

Which is the same thing.

Which is *now*.
