**The humans of Vesper — 187, or 186, or 188**

They do not age. This is the first thing, and it is not a gift. They reach maturity in a single season — a *compressed* childhood, you said, an apprenticeship renewed every seven years — and then they stop. A forty-year-old keeper of records has been forty for as long as anyone can verify. But "verify" is doing strange work here. The keeper cannot verify their own age. They remember being younger — or they have the *memory* of being younger, which in Vesper is the same thing, because memory is the only record, and memory in a place without decay does not accumulate, it *overwrites*. The keeper remembers being thirty-five. They also remember being thirty-six, thirty-seven, thirty-eight, thirty-nine. These memories coexist. They have always coexisted. The keeper does not find this troubling. Trouble requires change, and the keeper does not change.

They are **short**. Not dramatically — perhaps fifteen centimeters below the average of the world they came from, or the world they will never leave. This is the neoteny you mentioned, but reversed: not adults retaining childish features, but adults *compressed* into a smaller form, as if growth itself had been negotiated with the equilibrium and the equilibrium had won the better bargain. Their hands are broad, their fingers long, suited to the trowel and the hammer, the working of soft stone. Their eyes are large relative to their faces, adapted to the constant bioluminescence, which is dimmer than daylight but *closer* — the lichen grows in wall niches, at arm's reach, and the eye that reads it grows to match. They **do not speak loudly**. The water-sound is always present, a hiss and murmur that fills the arcades, and a voice raised above it would be an intrusion, a kind of violence. It is **confirmation**. *You are still here. I am still here. The damp is still damp.*

They have **no names** in the way names are usually understood. Each person is known by their **function** — the Keeper of Records, the Trowel-Bearer, the Channel-Walker who inspects the arcades, the Lichen-Tender who harvests the bioluminescent gardens in the cellars. A child — if child is the word — apprentices to the Channel-Walker for one season, then the next, and one season the Channel-Walker does not appear at the appointed hour, and the apprentice *is* the Channel-Walker, has always been, will not stop being. The transition is not marked. There is no ceremony. The person simply continues, wearing the function like a garment that has become skin.

The Keeper of Records is always forty. Or always fifty. The number is not important, and this is the strangeness: **the number does not matter to them**. In a world of change, age is a measure of distance traveled, of time accumulated. In Vesper, age is a **label**, and labels do not accumulate. The Keeper has kept the records for as long as there have been records. The records are oral, passed from one keeper to the next, and the keeper is always the same age, and the records are always the same. In the year of the First Rain, the town began.* The Keeper reads these lines, has always read them, will read them at the next interval, and the meaning does not shift, because meaning requires change, and the Keeper does not change.

They **eat**. This is important. The equilibrium is not a suspension of need, only of change. They eat the lichen, the moss from the channels, the wool-dye that is also a medicine, the meat of the sheep when the sheep are brought down from the rim — an event so rare that it has no name, only a description: *the day the dry meat comes*. They drink the water from the channels, which is always moving, always the same temperature, always slightly mineral, tasting of stone and time. They excrete, and the water carries it away, and the water returns, and this circulation is not shameful, it is **the way of things**. The body is not a boundary in Vesper. They **sleep**. This is the only freedom, you said, and it is true. They sleep in shifts, or not in shifts — the records do not say. Some sleep more, some less. The difference is not remarked. A person who sleeps sixteen hours is not lazier than a person who sleeps four. They are simply **configured differently**, the way two stones in the same wall are configured differently, one bearing more weight, one less, both necessary, both permanent.

They **do not dream**, or they dream the same dream, which is the dream of the fish moving in the source-channel, the dream of rain that does not fall but is felt, the dream of a boundary that is crossed and not crossed, the dream of **leaving**. The dream is not remembered, or it is remembered as waking, and the distinction does not matter, because in Vesper there is no distinction between the states of the mind, only the states of the **moisture**, which are: slightly more, slightly less, the same.
