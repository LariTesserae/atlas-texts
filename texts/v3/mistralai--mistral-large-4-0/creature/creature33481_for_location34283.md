## The Inhabitants of Veth-Mar

They do not have a single name for themselves. In the oldest speech, the one preserved in the reed-eel oracles, they are called **the Wet** — *Veth*, before the hyphen, before the distinction between the city and the water that holds it. The name *Mar* came later, a shortening, a human attempt to claim possession. The Wet do not object. They have learned that names are like water: they flow through, they do not adhere.

### The Surface-Dwellers

Most numerous, most visible, the ones you would meet if you walked the channels at the hour when the scheduled sun glows brightest through the dome. They are **tall and slow**, their bodies adapted to the thick air, to the constant humidity, to the absence of seasonal urgency. Their skin is pale, almost luminous, marked with the patterns of the mist-orchids they cultivate — not tattoos but *inoculations*, living dye that blooms in the warmth of their bodies, that changes color with their health, their mood, their proximity to water.

They wear **reed-mat**, woven in the patterns of their districts, treated with the resins that make it resist the perpetual wet. Their clothing is *loose*, *open*, designed to let the air move through, to let the condensing moisture find its way to the skin where it can be *absorbed*, where the body can drink from the atmosphere as well as from the cup. They are barefoot more often than not, their feet broad, splayed, *sensitive* — the membrane-ground beneath them is not inert, and they feel its breathing, its slow pulse, its occasional *dreaming*.

They move slowly. Not from weakness but from *attention*. Every step is a negotiation with the wet, the warm, the *alive*. They know which channels flood at which phase of the membrane's cycle. They know which patches of ferrocrete are *fresh*, still off-gassing, still settling into their eternal half-finished state. They know the mist-birds by their thrumming — each bird has a frequency, a signature, and the Surface-Dwellers read the air as others read text.

Their technology is **primitive by design**. They work copper cold, hammer it into sheets, into vessels, into the coils that carry water from the reservoirs to the Mist Engines. They fire clay from the reservoir beds, shaping it with hands and wooden tools, letting the kiln-fires do what the frozen dynamic will not — they *finish* things, they make them *done*, and then the done-ness persists, eternal, unchanging, a small victory against the endless becoming of the world.

They eat **reed-eel**, **orchid-bloom**, **membrane-grazer's milk**, and **fungal luminescence** — the cultivated glow that replaces the sun when the dome-vents close. Their food is *wet*, *warm*, *living* until the moment of consumption. They do not store for seasons that do not come. They store for the *dreaming*, for the shared visions that come when the water's memory enters the blood, and in those dreams they see the city as it was, as it might be, as it *is* in the depths where the shapes move unnamed.

Their **oracles** are made from reed-eel bone, carved with the primitive tools, polished by the condensing air until they are smooth as water. They cast them in the reservoirs, in the shallow pools where the membrane grows thin, and they read the patterns — *upward* patterns, *against* gravity, *wrong* patterns that are nonetheless *true* in this place where reality has been negotiated. The Surface-Dwellers do not question the wrongness. They have learned that *function* is more important than *explanation*, that a tool that works need not be understood.

They are **lonely**. Not from lack of contact — the density is sparse, but the sparse still *meet*, still *gather* in the open colonnades, still *speak* — but from the *quality* of their solitude. The wet air carries sound poorly. The city is visually open, horizontally vast, and yet each dweller is *isolated* in a bubble of warm breath, of personal humidity, of the slow soaking that separates them from their neighbors even as they share the same climate, the same breath, the same eternal present. They are alone *together*, and this is the natural condition, and they have made it *sacred*.

### The Deep-Dwellers

They live on the water. Not *above* it, not on the membrane-ground, but *in* it, on the floating gardens that drift in the reservoirs themselves, tended by human hands and the digestive assistance of the membrane-grazers. They are **other**, and the Surface-Dwellers know this, and the knowing is *ambivalent* — respect and fear and a kind of *longing*, because the deep is where the water remembers, where the past is thickest, where the shared dreams are strongest.

The Deep-Dwellers are **small**, **compact**, their bodies adapted to a life of balance, of constant adjustment to the slow movement of their raft-gardens. Their skin is darker, marked with the patterns of the floating vegetation they cultivate — the *water-lilies* that bloom in the permanent humidity, the *mosses* that grow in the shade of the dome, the *fungi* that provide their light and their food and their *visions*. They do not wear reed-mat. They wear **membrane**, the cultivated skin of the membrane-grazers, treated, stretched, *alive* in a way that their clothing was alive when it was part of a living animal, still faintly responsive to the water around them.

They **never touch the membrane-ground**. This is their defining difference, their *taboo*, their *sacred boundary*. They are *of the water*, and the water is *not the ground*, and the ground is *alive*, and the two must not be confused. The Deep-Dwellers navigate by *feel*, by the subtle pressure changes in the water, by the *memory* that the reservoirs hold of every movement, every weight, every presence that has ever rested upon them. They know the reservoirs as the Surface-Dwellers know the channels — intimately, *personally*, as a *body* rather than a *place*.

They are **dreamers**. The Deep-Dwellers drink the untreated water, bathe in it, live immersed in its memory, and their dreams are *stronger*, *more coherent*, more *shared* than those of the Surface. They are the **interpreters**, the ones who translate the water's visions into the decisions that guide the city. The councils meet on the water, on the largest of the floating gardens, and the Deep-Dwellers *speak* for the reservoirs, for the past, for the *shapes* that move in the depths and that the primitive technology cannot classify.

They are **feared** by the Surface-Dwellers, and they know this, and they *use* it. Not cruelly. The fear is *real*, grounded in the reality-breakdown, in the weirdness that concentrates in the deep water, in the places where the membrane grows thin and the *other* things become visible, become *present*, become *active*. The Deep-Dwellers have learned to *accommodate* these presences, to *navigate* around them, to *trade* with them in the oldest commerce of Veth-Mar — the exchange of attention, of care, of the *willingness to be seen*.

Some are *born* there, *raised* in the *wet*, the *warm*, the *alive*, *taught* the *primitive technology*, the *ways of the water*, the *respect* for the *boundaries*. Others *arrive*, *emerge*, *become aware* in the *strange* place where *reality* *thins*, where the *rules* have been *negotiated*, where the *total built environment* *collides* with the *primitive technology*, where the *frozen equilibrium* *collides* with the *eternal becoming*.

The Ones Who Arrive **eat** the *reed-eel*, the *orchid-bloom*, the *membrane-grazer's milk*, and they *feel* the *dreams beginning*, the *water's memory entering* their *own*. They **sleep** in the *half-built structures*, on *reed-mat pallets*, *listening* to the *thrumming* of the *mist-birds*, the *breathing* of the *ground*, the *silence* that is *not absence of sound* but its *quality*, its *hush*, its *music*.

The *dreams repeat*, *slightly different each time*, *drawn from the same reservoir*, the *same accumulated past*, the *same eternal present*. They are *here*. They have *always been here*. The *water remembers*, and *so do they*, and the *remembering* is *enough*, is *everything*, is the *strange*, *bright*, *wet*, *unchanging* world that *holds them* in its *breathing*, its *condensing*, its *reality-breakdown embrace*.

And they **become**. Not *change*, not *progress*, not *history* — but *becoming*, *horizontal*, *across the surface*, *between districts*, *between states of being*, *between the human and the other*, *between the surface and the deep*, *between the built and the grown*. They *are part of the weather now*, *the climate*, the *endless humid luminous becoming* that *is* Veth-Mar.

The *membrane rises*. The *membrane falls*. The *city breathes*. They *breathe with it*. This is *life* in Veth-Mar, and it *will be* life in Veth-Mar, and they are *part of it*, *always*, *eternally*, *now*.
