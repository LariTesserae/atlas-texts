I need to describe who or what lives in the Drowned Works—not the landscape itself, but its inhabitants, the beings that somehow persist, thrive, or merely *endure* in this world of embryonic geology, saturated air, and perpetual becoming.

## The Inhabitants of the Drowned Works

### The Ones Who Remember Being Dry

There are *watchers* here who have not forgotten. Creatures that carry in their bodies the memory of a different world, of elevation and sky and the particular quality of light that falls from above rather than diffusing from everywhere. They are *stranded* in a sense that goes deeper than geography—stranded in *time*, in the wrong moment of the earth's becoming.

The **Old Deer** still come, though they should not. Their kind was never meant for saturation, for air that weighs, for ground that yields. Yet they descend from the almost-dry highs, the *intention*-hills where the water table dips just low enough, and they stand in the shallows with their hooves sinking, their great eyes reflecting the green dimness. They do not drink from the pools—their bodies reject the chemistry, the warmth, the *wrongness* of this water. They come to *watch*. To *wait*. Some ancient instinct, some ancestral memory of before, draws them down to witness the world's re-making. They stand motionless for hours, their breath misting in the saturated air, their coats dark with moisture that never dries. When they finally turn and climb back toward drier ground, something in their movement suggests *relief*, but also *longing*. They will return. They always return.

There are **Turtles** of a kind that should not exist at this latitude, this warmth, this *wrongness*. They have grown large, *ancient-looking*, their shells encrusted with tufa, with algae, with the mineral film of constant immersion. They move through the channels with a patience that borders on *stubbornness*, as if refusing to acknowledge that their world has changed. Their eyes, when they surface, hold something *almost* like recognition. They do not breed successfully here—the water shifts too fast, the temperature spikes, the chemistry turns—but they *persist*. Generation after generation, each cohort smaller, each individual larger, slower, more *determined* to simply *be* in a place that keeps trying to become something else. They are the closest thing this world has to *philosophers*. They do not hurry. They do not hope. They simply *continue*, and in their continuation, they *testify* to what was, to what might be again, to the possibility that the drowning is *temporary*, that the land will rise, that they will walk on ground that holds.

### The Ones Who Were Born Here, Or Think They Were

But most of what lives in the Drowned Works has *no memory* of elsewhere. They are children of this saturation, this warmth, this perpetual almost-becoming. They have never known a world with edges, with limits, with the *finality* of dry ground.

The **Frogs** are *everywhere*, and they are *not one thing*. The chorus that fills the air at 2.7 is not a single species but *dozens*, perhaps *hundreds*, of forms that have not finished separating from each other, that hybridize in the warm pools, that produce offspring which may or may not resemble either parent. They are *prolific* beyond measure, and they are *desperate*—not in any conscious sense, but in the way of all life that knows, at some level beneath knowing, that the conditions are *unstable*, that the boom will be followed by bust, that their abundance is *borrowed* against a future scarcity they cannot imagine. They call *constantly*, *competitively*, *obsessively*, each male trying to out-shout the others in a competition where the prize is *temporary*, where the victor will mate in water that may be gone tomorrow, may be *different* tomorrow, may not support the eggs, the tadpoles, the *becoming* frogs that result. Their voices are not *songs* but *assertions*, *demands*, *insistence* on being heard, on being *present*, on mattering in a world that is still deciding whether it will have *matter* in any stable sense. When you stand among them, you feel the vibration in your sternum, in your teeth, the *physical* weight of their refusal to be ignored. They do not *sing*. They *scream* the only way they know how, the way of life that has not yet learned *peace*.

The **Fish** are *strange*—not strange in appearance, exactly, but strange in *behavior*, in *ecology*, in their *relationship* to the water that contains them. They school in the deeper pools, in the warm upwellings, in the channels where the current runs *wrong*, uphill, *illogical*. Their movements are *synchronized* in ways that suggest something *more* than instinct, something like *attention*, like *curiosity*, like a *response* to the presence of watchers. They follow you. They gather when you stop, when you make any sound, when you disturb the surface. Are they *hungry*? The electrified remnants of the industrial past, the faint pulses still traveling through water and wet earth, may have taught them *association*, *anticipation*, a kind of *domestication* of the wild. Or perhaps they are simply *responsive*, drawn to *movement*, to *disturbance*, to the *possibility* of food in a world where food is *abundant* but *unpredictable*, where the next meal may be a population explosion of invertebrates, may be the corpse of a frog, may be *nothing*, may be the slow dissolution of their own kind when the chemistry turns. They breed *wildly* in the warm months, lay eggs in mats that may or may not float, that may or may not hatch, that may or may not find water when the water *moves*. Their lives are *short*, *fierce*, *uncertain*. They do not *age* gracefully. They *explode* into being, *consume*, *reproduce*, *die*, and are *replaced* by something almost like themselves, almost but not quite, the *almost* that defines this entire world.

The **Snakes** are *small*, *numerous*, *aggressive* without being truly dangerous. Their venom is *mild*, a *irritant*, a *reminder* rather than a threat. But their *presence* is *insistent*, their *density* in the warm spots *saturating*. They bask on the tufa rocks, on the floating mats, on the *almost*-land where the water table has dropped just enough. They hunt the frogs, the lizards, the *abundant* small life that thrives in the warmth. They mate *constantly*, *opportunistically*, in the brief windows when the conditions permit, producing young that emerge *ready*, that must *feed* immediately, that have no time for *learning* because the world changes too fast for learning to matter. They are *everywhere*, and their *everywhereness* is part of the *packed* quality of this place, the *no room between presences*. You step, and you step *near* one. You turn, and you see another. They do not *flee*—they *assess*, *threaten*, *strike* if you come too close, then *retreat* into the saturated vegetation, into the *almost*-cover of a world that offers no true shelter, only *temporary* concealment. They are *not afraid*. They are *confident* in a way that seems *wrong*, *misplaced*, as if they have not yet learned that this world is *uncertain*, that their confidence may be *mistaken*, that the warmth that sustains them may *shift*, may *fail*, may leave them *stranded* in cold, in dryness, in the *equally* hostile conditions of a world that has *finished* deciding and found them *unwanted*.

### The Ones Who Live in the *Between*

There are beings here that exist in the *thresholds*, the *transitions*, the *almosts* that define this landscape. They are the *most* adapted, in a sense, because they have *no* fixed form, no *settled* ecology, no *committed* way of being.

The **Lizards** are *elongated*, *eel-like*, *amphibious* in a way that suggests they are still *deciding*. Some have *legs*, but they *swim* as well as they *walk*, their bodies *undulating* through the water with a *fluidity* that suggests *practice*, *evolution*, *becoming*. Their eyes are *positioned* for a world where the surface is *unreliable*, where the boundary between air and water is *permeable*, *negotiable*. They bask on the *warm* rocks, the *exposed* tufa, the *brief* dry spots that appear and disappear with the *breathing* of the water table. They eat *anything*—insects, smaller lizards, the *eggs* of frogs, the *carcasses* of fish—because their world offers no *reliability*, no *guarantee* that any particular food source will *persist*. They are *survivors* in the most *literal* sense, the ones who have *not* committed to any particular way of being, who remain *available* to the *next* shift, the *next* change, the *next* becoming. Some of them are *parthenogenetic*, reproducing *without* males, *cloning* themselves into *abundance*, into *density*, into the *packed* presence that fills the warm spots. Others *breed* sexually, *mixing*, *hybridizing*, producing offspring that are *uncertain*, *experimental*, *possibilities* rather than *certainties*. They do not *live* long. They do not *need* to. The world changes too fast for *longevity* to matter, for *age* to accumulate *wisdom*. They simply *continue*, *adapt*, *become*, and in their *continuing*, they *testify* to the *possibility* of life in the *uncommitted*, the *unfinished*, the *still-becoming*.

The **Invertebrates** are *everywhere*, and they are *everything*. The mosquitoes, the midges, the *gnats*—their larvae hatch in *every* pool, *every* puddle, *every* film of water on *every* surface. They rise in *clouds*, *saturate* the air, their bites *welcoming* you to the world's *metabolism*, its *constant* *consumption* and *replacement*. They are *vectors*, *transmitters*, *participants* in the *cycling* of nutrients through a system that has no *stable* form, no *settled* ecology. They carry *disease*, *parasites*, the *agents* of *die-off* that are also the *agents* of *turnover*, of *renewal*, of the *constant* *beginning* that this world requires. The crustaceans in the deeper pools are *blind*, *pale*, *ancient-looking*, their lineages stretching back to when this place was *properly* underwater, before the *mistake* of land, before the *error* of elevation. They have *not* changed. They have *not* needed to. They are *waiting*, in a sense, for the world to *return* to what it was, for the drowning to *complete* itself, for the land to *surrender* entirely to the water that is *primary*, that is *always* primary, that will *always* reclaim. The snails on every surface have *thin* shells, *dissolving*, their bodies *extending* beyond the protection they have *not* finished building. They are *vulnerable*, *exposed*, *precarious*—and in their *precariousness*, they are *honest*. They do not *pretend* to be *settled*, *secure*, *permanent*. They simply *continue*, *secreting* their *inadequate* shells, *grazing* on the *films* of algae and bacteria that coat every surface, *participating* in the *slow* *geology* of this place, their *dissolving* shells contributing *calcium* to the *tufa*, to the *building* of a world from the *water* up.

### The Ones Who Live in the *Built*, the *Failed*, the *Absorbed*

There are inhabitants here who have *no* wildness, no *nature*, no *claim* to the *becoming* of the landscape. They are *refugees* from the industrial attempt, from the *human* presence that tried to *extract*, to *process*, to *make* this place *useful* and *failed*.

The **Rats** are *abundant* in the *scattered* structures, the *almost*-buildings, the *fragments* of human intention. They have *adapted* to the saturation, the warmth, the *unpredictability* of their environment in ways that would seem *impossible* to their *dry* cousins. They swim. They *nest* in the *upper* levels of the *rusted* structures, the *highest* points they can find, when the water *rises*. Their populations *explode* and *crash* with the water table, the temperature, the *availability* of *everything*—the *garbage*, the *waste*, the *organic* *matter* that accumulates in the *industrial* *remnants*, that *feeds* the *strange* *chemistry* of the pools. They are *aggressive*, *competitive*, *desperate* in their *abundance*, their *density* creating *pressure*, *stress*, a *kind* of *madness* that shows in their *eyes*, their *movements*, their *willingness* to *fight* for *space*, for *food*, for *the* *next* *breath* in a world that offers no *guarantee* of *tomorrow*. They are *not* *natural*. They are *parasites* on the *failed* *industrial* *presence*, *symbionts* in the *dissolution*, *participants* in the *slow* *reclamation* of the *built* by the *wet*, the *warm*, the *patient* *geology*. They do not *belong* here, and yet they *thrive*, and in their *thriving*, they *testify* to the *resilience* of *life*, its *ability* to *find* a *way* in *any* *condition*, to *make* a *place* for itself in the *unfinished*, the *uncertain*, the *still-becoming*.

The **Otters** are *stranger*. They came from *elsewhere*, from the *channels* that connect this place to *other* *waters*, *other* *worlds*. They are *playful*, *desperate*, *uncertain* in their *survival*. Their *play* is not *joy* but *practice*, *preparation* for a *seriousness* that the world does not *permit*, that the *constant* *shift* of conditions *prevents*. They *hunt* the *fish*, the *frogs*, the *abundant* *life* of the warm pools, but they *compete* with *each other*, with the *snakes*, with the *birds*, with the *constant* *pressure* of *density* in a *packed* world. Their *eyes*, when you catch them in the *dim* light, are *bright*, *aware*, *almost* *human* in their *intelligence*, their *uncertainty*, their *recognition* that they are in a place that does not *want* them, that will *absorb* them, that is *slowly* *drowning* them in *warmth*, in *wet*, in the *perpetual* *almost* of *becoming*. They do not *stay*. They *move* through, *follow* the *channels*, *seek* the *deeper* water, the *cooler* pools, the *possibility* of *elsewhere*—though elsewhere is *hard* to *reach*, the *landscape* *extending*, the *journey* *long*, the *dynamic* *slow*. They *return*, though. They *always* *return*, drawn by the *abundance*, the *warmth*, the *something* in this *saturated* world that *calls* to them, that *promises* *sustenance*, *survival*, *the* *possibility* of *continuing* in a place that *should* *reject* them but *does* *not*, that *accepts* them *temporarily*, *provisionally*, *without* *commitment*, *without* *promise*.

### The Ones Who Are *Not* Yet, Who *Might* Be

And there are *presences* here that are *not* *fully* *realized*, that exist in the *potential*, the *embryonic*, the *almost* that permeates this world. They are *felt* more than *seen*, *sensed* more than *identified*, *inhabiting* the *gaps* in the *packed* *abundance*, the *pauses* in the *deafening* *chorus*.

The **Something** in the *deep* pools, the *warm* *upwellings*, the *channels* where the current runs *wrong*. It is *large*, or *many*, or *distributed*—you cannot tell because the water is *murky*, because the *light* is *dim*, because the *reflection* *lies*. It moves when you *watch*, or perhaps it *watches* your *movement*, *responds* to your *presence* with a *curiosity* that is *not* *quite* *consciousness*, *not* *quite* *intention*, but *something* that *approaches* *both*. The electrified *remnants* of the industrial past may have *awakened* it, *given* it *attention*, *hunger*, a *kind* of *awareness* that *persists* in the *faint* *pulses* of *current* that still *travel* through the water. Or it may be *older*, *deeper*, a *presence* that has *always* been here, in the *warmth* from *below*, in the *geothermal* *uncertainty* that keeps this place *young*, *unfinished*, *becoming*. It does not *threaten*. It does not *welcome*. It simply *is*, *present*, *aware*, *waiting* for the world to *finish* *deciding* what it *is*, what it *will* *be*, whether it will *include* this *presence* in its *becoming* or *exclude* it, *drown* it, *dry* it, *erase* it. It is the *closest* thing this world has to a *god*, a *spirit*, a *dweller* in the *deep* that *predates* the *landscape*, the *vegetation*, the *fauna*, the *built*, the *industrial* *attempt*. It does not *care*. It simply *waits*, *patient*, *ancient*, *young* in the way of all *unfinished* things.

The **Fungal** *Consciousness**, the *network* that *spreads* through the *mats*, the *peat*, the *saturated* *soil*. It is *not* *one* thing but *many*, *distributed*, *connected* by *hyphae* that *thread* through the *raw* *substrate*, *communicating* in *chemical* *signals*, *sharing* *nutrients*, *information*, *the* *slow* *awareness* of a *collective* that has *not* *finished* *deciding* whether it is *one* *organism* or *many*, whether it is *conscious* or *merely* *responsive*, whether it *thinks* or *merely* *grows*. It *consumes* itself as it *grows*, *building* *soil* from its own *deaths*, *creating* the *conditions* for *something* to *climb* out of the water, to *try* to *become* *terrestrial*, to *fail* and *sink* back and *try* *again*. It is *patient* in a way that *no* *animal* can be, *slow* in a way that *matches* the *dynamic* of the landscape, the *1.0* that *measures* its *tempo*, its *refusal* to *hurry*, its *insistence* on its *own* *time*. It *remembers*, in its *chemical* *way*, the *plants* it *was*, the *deaths* it has *incorporated*, the *cycles* of *growth* and *decay* that have *built* this *place* from the *water* up. It does not *want*. It does not *fear*. It simply *is*, *spreading*, *connecting*, *becoming* the *soil* that will *one* *day* be *rock*, that will *one* *day* be *something* *else*, *something* *finished*, *something* *that* *does* *not* *drown* itself in *becoming*.

### The Ones Who Are *Leaving*, Who *Cannot* *Stay*

And finally, there are the *transient*, the *temporary*, the *ones* who *pass* through, who *cannot* *settle*, who are *drawn* by *something* but *repelled* by *everything*, who *circle*, *return*, *become* part of the *permanent* *abundance* only in the *sense* that they are *always* *present*, *always* *leaving*, *always* *arriving* again.

The **Birds** are *few* in *species* but *many* in *individuals*. The **Herons** stand in the shallows, *patient*, *waiting* for the fish that are *always* *almost* *there*, that *appear* and *disappear* with the *shift* of the water, the *change* of the *light*, the *disturbance* of *watchers*. They are *solitary*, *territorial*, *competitive* for the *best* *spots*, the *warmest* *pools*, the *most* *abundant* *prey*. They do not *sing*. They *croak*, *squawk*, *make* *sounds* of *annoyance*, *warning*, *assertion*. They are *not* *happy* here. They are *not* *comfortable*. But they *stay*, *season after season*, *drawn* by the *warmth*, the *abundance*, the *possibility* of *survival* in a world that *offers* *everything* and *guarantees* *nothing*.

The **Kingfishers** are *brighter*, *sharper*, *more* *urgent*. They *dive*, *catch*, *eat*, *move*, *never* *still*, *never* *settled*. Their *calls* are *sharp*, *isolated*, *piercing* the *frog*-chorus, *demanding* *attention*, *asserting* *presence* in a world that *ignores* them, that *absorbs* them, that *will* *eventually* *drown* them in *saturation*, in *warmth*, in the *perpetual* *almost* of *becoming*. They are *beautiful*, *fierce*, *temporary*. They will *leave*, *eventually*, *when* the *cold* *comes*, when the water *shifts*, when the *abundance* *turns* to *scarcity*. But they will *return*, *drawn* by *memory*, by *instinct*, by the *same* *something* that *draws* *all* *life* to *this* *place*, to *this* *becoming*, to *this* *unfinished* *world*.

The **Waterfowl** are *dabbling*, *filtering*, *converting* the *abundant* *invertebrate* *life* into *flight*, into *escape*, into *the* *possibility* of *elsewhere*. They *circle*, *descend*, *rise*, *never* *landing* for *long*, *never* *committing* to this *saturated* *ground*. They are *migrants*, *travelers*, *ones* who *know* *other* *places*, who *remember* *dry* *land*, *open* *sky*, *the* *particular* *quality* of *light* that *falls* from *above* rather than *diffuses* from *everywhere*. They *stay* only *briefly*, *feed* *intensely*, *breed* *desperately*, then *move* *on*, *following* the *channels*, the *currents*, the *possibility* of *survival* in a world that *changes* *too* *fast* for *permanence*. They are *not* *of* this place. They are *through* it, *beside* it, *above* it in *spirit* if not in *body*, *always* *leaving*, *always* *promising* to *return*, *always* *carrying* *something* of it with them when they *go*.

## The Quality of Their Living

To *live* here is to *live* in *relation* to *uncertainty*. The inhabitants of the Drowned Works have *no* *stability*, no *guarantee*, no *promise* that the conditions that *sustain* them will *persist*, that the *world* will *continue* to *be* what it *is*, what it *has* *been* for their *brief* *existence*.

They are *abundant* but *precarious*. They *thrive* in the *warmth*, the *moisture*, the *nutrients*—and they *die*, *locally*, *temporarily*, when the *conditions* *shift*, when the *cycles* *turn*, when the *water* *rises* or *falls*, when the *temperature* *spikes* or *drops*, when the *chemistry* *changes* in ways that *no* *one* can *predict*, that *emerge* from the *uncertain* *geology*, the *breathing* *water* *table*, the *slow* *patience* of *becoming*.

They are *packed* but *stressed*. The *density* of 3.0, the *concentration* of *presences* in the *warm* *spots*, the *abundant* *pools*, the *saturated* *mats*—this *creates* *competition*, *aggression*, *a* *kind* of *madness* that *shows* in *behavior*, in *eyes*, in the *desperate* *urgency* of *reproduction*, of *consumption*, of *survival* in a world that *offers* *everything* and *takes* it *away* without *warning*, without *explanation*, without *malice*—simply *becoming*, *always* *becoming*, *never* *settled* into the *form* that *would* *permit* *stability*, *security*, *the* *illusion* of *permanence*.

They are *listening*, *always* *listening*, to the *sound* of the *water* *moving*, the *wind* *shifting*, the *chorus* of *other* *lives*, the *deafening* *silence* that is *never* *truly* *silent*, that *carries* the *vibration* of *a* *world* *announcing* *itself*, *insisting* on *attention*, *refusing* to be *ignored*, *backgrounded*, *forgotten*. They are *immersed* in *sound*, in *presence*, in the *physical* *reality* of *becoming*—and they *respond*, *adapt*, *continue*, *become* *part* of it, *briefly*, *temporarily*, *without* *hope* of *permanence*, *without* *desire* for *it*.

They are *not* *happy*, in any *human* sense. They do not *aspire*, *dream*, *imagine* a *better* *world*. They simply *are*, *exist*, *persist* in the *conditions* that *permit* their *existence*, *adapting* to *change*, *absorbing* *disturbance*, *continuing* through *cycles* that *erode* them, *dissolve* them, *replace* them with *something* *almost* like themselves, *almost* but not *quite*, in the *perpetual* *almost* of *a* *world* that *has* *not* *finished* *deciding* what it *is*, what it *will* *be*, whether it will *ever* *be* *anything* *recognizable*, *anything* *that* *could* *be* *called* *finished*, *complete*, *real*.

They are the *inhabitants* of the Drowned Works. They are *water* and *warmth* and *patience* and *desperation* and *abundance* and *precariousness* and *sound* and *dimness* and *slow* *becoming*. They are *below*, *beside*, *through*—*inside* a world that has *not* *finished* *deciding* what it *is*, that *invites* them to *wait*, to *watch*, to *become*, *briefly*, *part* of its *emergence*, its *raw* *geology*, its *unformed* *promise*.

And they *will* *carry* it with them, when they *leave*—the *warmth*, the *wet*, the *sense* of *being* *below*, *beside*, *through*, *inside* a *world* that *does* *not* *hurry*, that *insists* on its *own* *tempo*, its *own* *time*, its *own* *way* of *coming* *into* *being*. Or they *will* *stay*, and *become* part of it, *dissolved*, *absorbed*, *replaced* by *something* *else* that *continues* the *same* *struggle*, the *same* *patience*, the *same* *desperate* *abundance* in the *face* of *uncertainty*, of *change*, of *the* *perpetual* *almost* of a *world* still *being* *born*.
