I read this place carefully, and what follows is my attempt to distill what survival here would actually require—not from any manual, but from what the place itself seems to be teaching.

---

## Preparation: The Body Before the Mind

**Your skin is a membrane. Treat it as one.**

The air here is near-saturated with moisture, spores, volatile compounds of unknown function. Your body will equalize with it. This is not optional. The preparation begins with accepting that you will not be dry, will not be clean in any way you recognize, and that your sense of "clean" is a cultural artifact from a drier world.

Bring nothing you cannot afford to lose its integrity. Synthetic fabrics will grow things. Natural fibers will become substrate. The distinction matters less than you think. What matters is **permeability**—your clothing should breathe, should not trap moisture against your skin in ways that encourage the wrong kind of growth. Loose, layered, with the understanding that "layer" here means something like "temporary agreement with your body."

Your hair, if you have it, will become a colony. Consider this now.

**Train your feet for negotiation, not walking.**

There are no paths. There is only compressed absence—the memory of previous feet. The ground is a negotiation between liquid and solid, and you are a third party entering that conversation. Practice walking on surfaces that yield, that rise to meet you, that do not offer the false promise of stability. Your ankles will learn. Your proprioception will be rewritten. Do not fight this. The ground that recognizes pressure, that accommodates—this is not hostility. It is **etiquette**. Learn to press gently, to distribute your weight as the place seems to want, to move as water moves when it finds its level.

**Your ears need protection, and your ears need to listen.**

The hum is below hearing's threshold, but it is not below *feeling*'s. Prolonged exposure—hours, days—will make your inner ear ache, your vision shiver at the edges. Bring physical protection: wax earplugs, perhaps, or the dense silicone kind. But remove them regularly. The hum carries information. The frequency shifts tell you things: something large approaching, fungal networks activating, the deep places stirring. You will need to learn to feel the hum in your sternum, your teeth, your knees, the base of your skull, and to interpret it as you would interpret tone of voice.

Do not listen too long to the lowest frequencies. The deep places drop below hearing entirely. They become pressure. You do not want to feel pressure against your eardrums, your ribs, the fluid in your eyes. Know the difference between "felt in the sternum" and "felt in the ribs." The first is conversation. The second is warning.

## Preparation: The Mind Before the World

**Abandon the map. Cultivate the moment.**

You cannot navigate this place. Not in any way that would satisfy your Cartesian instincts. The landscape shifts continuously. A hollow fills. A path overgrows. What you walked yesterday may not exist today. The moss grows overnight here—impossible, but *here*, possible.

Instead, learn **landmarks of process**. The weeping rock hollow, where water emerges in perpetual slow fountain, where the channels loop back on themselves and the water waits, humming at a different frequency. The fungal flats, where the ground is almost entirely mycelium, pale and pulsing. The high ground, rare, where water runs *off* rather than *up*, where the ecology shifts. These are not fixed points. They are **relationships**—ways the place organizes itself that persist even as the material changes.

Carry no map. Carry **attention**. The place rewards attention. It responds to it. The water rises to meet a slow foot. The fungal lights pulse toward a patient observer. You are learning to be **legible** to a medium that has rarely been addressed with anything but fear or exploitation.

**Learn to see without sharp edges.**

The light here is wrong. Filtered, processed, arrived as something between glow and shadow. Nothing casts a clean shadow. Everything bleeds into everything else at a few centimeters. This will disturb you. Your visual system expects edges, expects objects, expects the world to present itself in discrete units.

Practice **soft focus**. Let the boundaries blur. The "too many legs" of the fauna, the "unfolding" body—these are not failures of your perception. They are accurate reports of a world that does not respect your categories. The thing that passes at the edge of vision: do not try to make it *be* something. Let it be **event**, be **process**, be present-and-absent in the way that water is present-and-absent in saturated ground.

**Prepare for the dissolution of categories.**

Living/non-living. Natural/artificial. Self/world. These boundaries are permeable here, becoming as permeable as the membranes you will glimpse beneath the surface. The stone is grown, organized, patterned. The ground responds to your palm. The mycelium processes information. The tech is indistinguishable from ecology, from geology, from weather.

Do not panic when your categories fail. **Notice** their failure. The failure is information. Ask: what would it mean to be a self in a world where the boundary between self and world is a negotiation rather than a fact? You do not need an answer. You need **tolerance for the question**.

## Preparation: The Spirit Before the Place

**You will be changed. Decide what you are willing to lose.**

The spores you breathe. The water you touch. The light that carries information in wavelengths you cannot see. All of it works on you, slowly, the way water works on stone. You will not leave the same.

Some who stay too long—they change more. Their skin becomes more permeable. They begin to hum, unconsciously, at frequencies that match the local water. They are not unhappy. They are not, exactly, themselves, either.

This is not a warning. This is **description**. The question is: what do you want from this place? Do you want to observe? To survive? To be transformed? There is no wrong answer, but there is a difference between **choosing** transformation and **undergoing** it unconsciously.

If you want to leave—really leave, with your boundaries intact, your categories functional, your self continuous—then you must **limit your exposure**. Days, not weeks. Hours of direct contact, not days. The water you drink: filter it, boil it, but know that boiling does not remove the **information** the water carries. Consider carrying your own, or accepting that you will ingest the place, become part of its processing.

If you want to be changed—if you sense that your old self was already a kind of sleep, and that this place offers waking—then stay longer. But stay **consciously**. Keep a journal. The time here is strange. Morning, afternoon, dusk—these feel borrowed. Your journal will be your anchor, your proof that you were here, that you were **someone** here, even as that someone becomes diffuse.

**Prepare for being noticed.**

This place attends to you. Not with the focused attention of a predator, but with the **curiosity** of a medium that has rarely been so addressed. The water rises to meet you. The fungal lights pulse in your direction. The humming shifts, asks something you cannot understand.

You are not the first. There are traces, older than human, of other presences. Other ways of being in this world. You are not alone. You are not, perhaps, even special. But you are **noticed**.

What will you do with the noticing? This is the question the place asks. It does not demand an answer. But it **waits** for one, in the way that water waits in a reservoir, humming at a different frequency than the water around it.

## Practical Inventory: What to Carry

**Water:** Carry what you need for the first day. After that, learn to read the place. The water here remembers. It carries information. To drink directly is to ingest the place, to become part of its processing. Some do this intentionally. Most do not. If you must drink, filter through charcoal, through cloth, through layers of moss—but know that this removes particles, not **patterns**.

**Food:** Bring sealed, dry, unreactive. The local ecology will try to process anything you expose. Your food is not exempt. Eat quickly, eat cleanly, do not leave traces that will grow. The fauna here are events, not populations. They appear, they process, they are gone. You do not want to become **substrate** for such an event.

**Shelter:** Do not bring a tent. It will become a colony, a structure grown into rather than built. If you need shelter, find the high ground, the rare places where water runs off. Sleep on the root-mat, let it accommodate your weight. You will be damp. You will be attended to. This is the way of the place.

**Light:** Bring a source, but use it sparingly. The fungal networks pulse their own green-white code. The dim light that arrives from the bruise-sky is **information**. Your artificial light will disrupt the reading. Use it only when necessary, and be aware that the place will **notice** the interruption.

**Sound:** Protection, as described. And a way to record, if you choose. The humming, the subsonic groan, the ultrasonic whisper—these are the place's voice. Some travelers have tried to translate it. None have succeeded. But the attempt **changes** the attempter.

**Medicine:** Standard kit, plus something for fungal exposure (you will be exposed), something for the particular ache of subsonic pressure (rest, silence, time), something for the psychological weight of boundary-dissolution (companionship, ritual, the deliberate practice of **being a self** even when the world offers to make you otherwise).

**Nothing else.** The place provides. Moss for binding, for padding, for slow medicine. The weeping stone for tools, if you learn to read its channels. The root-mat for rest, for travel, for the accommodation of your weight. You are a guest. Act like one. Bring **attention**, bring **respect**, bring the **willingness to be changed**. The rest, you will find, or you will not survive to need.

## The Final Preparation: What Cannot Be Prepared

You cannot prepare for the **quality** of time here. Your watch will tick. But morning, afternoon, dusk—these feel borrowed from somewhere else. The landscape does not darken so much as **thicken**. The air grows denser toward evening, and the humming drops in frequency, becoming something you feel in your knees, your hips, the base of your skull.

You cannot prepare for the **scale** of the place's ambition, or its memory, or its dreaming. The membrane-thin ground above the vast slow movement. The something that turns, so slowly that sound itself seems to propagate through it like a wave through stone. You have not gone close. You should not go close. But you will **know** it is there, in the way you know a sleeper is breathing in the next room.

You cannot prepare for the **coherence** of the strangeness. This place is not chaotic. It has rules. The humming follows patterns. The water rises and falls in rhythms. The fungal lights pulse in sequences that repeat, that **mean** something, if you could only read the language. The weirdness is not random. It is **other**—an ecology, a physics, a world that has been running long enough to develop its own deep structures, its own emergent properties, its own **culture**, in the sense of accumulated pattern, of tradition, of the way things are done here.

You can prepare your body. You can prepare your mind. You can prepare your spirit, if you have one, if it is the kind of thing that can be prepared.

But the place itself—the water that rises, the moss that grows, the fungal lights that pulse their slow green code, the humming that continues below and above hearing, the sound of a world that has been making itself for longer than memory—this you can only **enter**. You can only **be noticed**. You can only **choose**, in the end, what to do with the noticing.

The water rises. The moss grows. The place has noticed.

What will you do?
