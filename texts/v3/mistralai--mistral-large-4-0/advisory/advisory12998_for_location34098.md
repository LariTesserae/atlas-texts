I'll prepare for this carefully. The Drowned Foundry reads to me as a place that doesn't care whether you survive—which means preparation is less about *beating* the environment and more about *becoming compatible with it*, at least temporarily. Here's what my intuition tells me:

---

## The Descent Itself

**The entry wound.** A vertical shaft barely wider than your shoulders, slick with growing minerals, carrying heat and the smell of rust-cooked meat upward. This is your first warning: the place *exhales*. Before you enter, I would:

- Study the shaft's moisture patterns. Where minerals grow fastest, the air flows most—this tells you about pressure differentials, about whether the city is "breathing in" or "breathing out" at that moment. Never enter during an apparent exhalation, if such a rhythm is observable.
- Lower a probe first. Not electronic—electronics will fail, or worse, *function* wrongly in this chemistry. A simple weighted line with samples of cloth, metal coupons, a vial to catch condensation. Retrieve it. Examine what happened to it.
- Descend slowly, *sideways* if needed. The shaft is narrow enough that you'll be in contact with the walls regardless. Wear something that can be discarded—outer layer of sacrificial material that the growing minerals will claim.

---

## What to Wear

Layering, but with a philosophy: **nothing irreplaceable, nothing sealed, nothing that needs to stay dry.**

- **Base layer:** Something that can absorb. The films will try to grow on you. Cotton, wool, natural fibers—materials that can host mineral deposits without trapping them *against* your skin in a sealed, rotting pocket. I'd wear something loose, with no tight cuffs or waistbands where moisture and chemistry can concentrate.
- **Middle layer:** Insulation that tolerates damp. Closed-cell foam degrades in strange chemistry; I'd want something like felted wool, or modern PrimaLoft-style synthetic that retains function when wet. The temperature swings—cool where walls remember the surface, hot where the deep earth speaks—will make you shiver and sweat in the same hour.
- **Outer layer:** A sacrificial shell. Something breathable but substantial—waxed canvas, perhaps, or a modern membrane that can be *peeled away* in sections. You will want to shed this. The city will mark it, claim it, and you'll want to leave that marking behind.
- **Feet:** Boots that can be *abandoned*. The pools rise to your waist. Your feet will be in water constantly—warm, cool, chemically strange. Waterproofing matters less than *drainage*. I'd want boots with aggressive tread for volcanic glass, and I'd accept that they'll be ruined, films growing in their seams, mineral deposits in their tread.
- **Hands:** Gloves, but *thin*. You'll need dexterity for climbing, for steadying yourself against weeping walls. The films will coat them. Accept this. Carry spare pairs in a waterproof pouch that you *know* will eventually fail.
- **Face and breath:** This is the hardest choice. A respirator with replaceable filters, knowing the filters will clog with mineral-laden vapor, knowing the air itself will become unbreathable over time *regardless* of filtration. I'd carry a rebreather if possible, but with the understanding that it extends your time rather than making it safe. The "too much of something, too little of another" will win eventually.
- **No plastics that seal.** No waterproof phone cases, no "dry bags" that pretend to be dry. Everything will be damp. Everything will *weepen* eventually. Plan for the moment when your "dry" storage becomes a pool of contaminated warmth.

---

## Light

You'll need multiple independent sources, and you'll need to *expect* strange behavior.

- **Primary:** A headlamp with a *warm* color temperature. The city filters light toward warmth anyway; matching this reduces disorientation, lets you see what the environment "wants" you to see. But also carry a *cool* source—the contrast will reveal what the warm light hides.
- **Secondary:** A lantern, not LED—something with thermal mass, something that *itself* generates heat. In this place, a warm light source is also a small thermal beacon, a point of difference the local life might notice or avoid.
- **Tertiary:** Chemical light sticks. They will last only hours, but they require no batteries, no switches, no *will* of your own. In a place where your own light behaves strangely, where it pools and changes color and presses against your eyes, sometimes you need light that is *stupid*, light that simply *is*.

And you must learn to use the city's own light: the phosphorescence in the volcanic glass, the blue flames at the vents, the pulsing that comes in patches and veins. This is dangerous—you're navigating by a light source you don't control, that might attract or repel the things that drift in warm pools. But the city's light is *honest* in a way yours isn't. Yours lies about the environment. The city's light reveals it.

---

## Sound and Silence

The hum will be constant. This is not something you can block. I would prepare:

- **Ear protection that *attenuates* rather than *blocks*.** You need to hear water rushing, mechanisms grinding, the *change* in the hum that signals something rare approaching. But you also need to protect the fine structures of your inner ear from a sustained low-frequency pressure that will fatigue you, make you nauseous, disturb your balance.
- **A way to *add* sound.** A small speaker, playing a frequency you choose—perhaps a counter-hum, perhaps white noise, perhaps music with a strong beat. Not to mask the city's sound, but to *orient* yourself within it. Your own heartbeat, amplified, can be an anchor when the city's hum becomes overwhelming.
- **Accept that you will carry the hum out.** It will not stop. This is not a preparation but a *warning*. You will hear it in quiet rooms. You will feel it in your sternum when you try to sleep. It becomes part of you, or you become part of it—the boundary already blurred.

---

## Navigation and Mapping

Traditional maps will fail. The city is too dense, too layered, too *folded* in on itself.

- **A line.** A spool of strong cord, biodegradable if possible—hemp, linen, something the city can *consume* if you lose it. Trail it behind you. Do not rely on memory, on "the way back felt different." Every direction is both in and out. The line is your only true north.
- **Markers that the city cannot absorb.** Glow sticks, weighted and placed at junctions, knowing they will fade. Carved notches in *your* walking staff, a personal tally. The city has its own markings—films in patterns, mineral deposits in shapes that "almost form meaning"—but you cannot trust them to be consistent, or to mean what you need them to mean.
- **A compass that lies.** It will spin, or point toward mass rather than north, or simply stop. Carry it, but know its unreliability. The city's density creates magnetic anomalies, gravitational *suggestions*, fields that pull your sense of direction toward the warm, the wet, the *alive*.
- **Time, not distance.** You will not walk far. You will walk *long*. Measure your journey in hours of breathable air remaining, in battery life, in the accumulation of film on your outer layer. When you have used half your resources, you turn back. Even if you have not "gone anywhere."

---

## Sustenance

- **Water:** Do not drink the city's water. This should be obvious, but the humidity, the *thirst* of warm damp air, will tempt you. Carry all water you need. Calculate generously—you will sweat, you will lose moisture through breathing this dense air, you will need to rinse films from your eyes and skin.
- **Food:** High-calorie, no preparation required, nothing that spoils. The heat will spoil things faster than you expect. The films will find any organic material you carry and begin to *process* it. Eat quickly, eat cleanly, leave no traces that could attract the larger things—the soft, gelatinous, *flowing* things that drift in warm pools.
- **Salt:** You will sweat out minerals. You will need to replace them. Carry electrolyte supplements, or plan to emerge *deficient* in ways that will take days to recover from.

---

## Health and Transformation

This is the preparation that matters most, and the hardest to accept.

- **Expect the films.** They will grow on your skin. They will not wash off. This is not infection in the medical sense—it's *colonization*, the city's chemistry finding purchase on your chemistry. Scrubbing will damage your skin, create entry points for actual infection. The films may be harmless. They may be protective, a temporary adaptation. They may be the first stage of something else. Monitor, document, but do not panic at their presence.
- **Expect the sound.** The hum will become *yours*. You will dream in it. You will wake with it already present, as if you never left. This is not psychological—it's physical, a resonance established in your tissues, in the fluid of your inner ear, in the *architecture* of your nervous system.
- **Expect the density.** The feeling of closeness, of walls pressing, of ceilings that sag into floors. This will affect your sleep, your breathing, your sense of your own body. Some travelers report *phantom* compression after leaving—the feeling of walls that aren't there, the need to turn sideways in open spaces. This can persist for weeks. It can be permanent.
- **Expect the temperature dysregulation.** Your body will struggle to maintain its narrow band. You will shiver in heat, sweat in cold. Your metabolism will work overtime. You will emerge depleted, possibly *changed* in your tolerance for temperature extremes.
- **The "above" that no longer exists.** This is psychological, but the city makes it physical. You will lose track of *up*. The wound in the stone, the vertical shaft—these are your only references, and they will feel *arbitrary*, chosen by convention rather than by truth. Prepare to emerge disoriented, to need time to re-learn the sky, the horizon, the *openness* of surface space.

---

## What to Carry That Is Not Physical

- **A recording device, knowing it will fail.** Not for the data—for the *intention*. The act of trying to capture the hum, the water, the *presence*. When it fails, you will have tried. This matters.
- **Something to leave behind.** An offering, a marker, a *gift* to the city. Not for the city's sake—it does not want. But for *yours*. The act of leaving something, of participating in the city's slow construction, of becoming *material* rather than merely *disturbance*. A lock of hair, sealed in glass. A photograph, printed on archival paper, left in a dry chamber. Something that will become a layer, a deposit, a film.
- **A plan for the return that assumes you will not be the same.** You will carry water on your skin. You will carry sound in your ears. You will carry *density* in your chest. Whoever meets you at the surface—if anyone does—will not recognize you immediately. Prepare them, if you can. Prepare yourself to explain what cannot be explained, to translate an experience that has no surface equivalent.

---

## The Final Preparation

Stand at the mouth of the shaft. Feel the heat rising, the smell of rust and cooked meat. Know that you are entering a place where time moves differently—not slower, but *rarer*, where events are cycles without progression, where the city does not *happen* but *is*.

Breathe deeply of surface air. You will not have this again for hours, perhaps longer. The air here will be thick, mineral-laden, *alive* with chemistry and biology and the slow metabolism of stone.

Descend sideways if you must. Let the minerals mark your clothing, your skin, your *expectation* of what cleanliness means.

And when you stand in that chamber where ceiling has collapsed into floor, where up and down are convention, where the hum is loud and the water rises to your waist—*participate*. Do not merely observe. The city does not contain you; you must *choose* to be contained, briefly, as the water is contained, as the films are contained, as the rare soft things are contained—drifting, sensing, *being* for a moment in a place that has forgotten the difference between living and built, between growing and made, between *now* and *always*.

Then leave. The way out is the way in, or another way, one of many. The city will not stop you. It does not *want* to stop you. It wants nothing. But it will *notice* your leaving, and it will *continue*, and you will carry its water on your skin, its sound in your ears, its *density* in your chest, and you will know that somewhere below, in a place that time has forgotten, something is still *singing*, still *weeping*, still *waiting* for the next disturbance, the next brief event, the next life that will come and go and become, for a moment, part of the endless, unchanging *now*.

---

You will not be prepared. No one is. But you will be *compatible*—temporarily, partially, at the cost of carrying something of the city back with you. That is the bargain. That is the only bargain the Drowned Foundry offers.
