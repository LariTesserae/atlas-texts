# A Traveler's Field Manual for the Moraines of Stillbecoming

## Pre-Departure: What You Are Actually Going To Do

Before any technical preparation, you must confront something more difficult: **you cannot "visit" this place the way you visit a city, a forest, or even a ruin.** The Moraines of Stillbecoming are not a destination. They are a *condition* that happens to occur on certain worlds at certain times. You will not see them. You will not *tour* them. You will, if fortune permits, *persist* within them for whatever duration your body and equipment can sustain against a world that has no interest in your presence and no mercy for your limitations.

The first preparation is therefore *psychological*, and it is the hardest. You must practice *release*. On most worlds, you can expect your actions to have consequences you can perceive. Here, your footsteps will be erased by dust within a season. Your voice—if you somehow had atmosphere to carry it—would be lost in a silence so complete that it reads as *absence*, as *void*. The lattices do not care. The carriers do not notice. The lithovores do not taste you. You are a *glitch* in a computation that has run flawlessly for longer than your species has existed.

Practice standing in absolute silence and feeling *seen* by nothing. Practice moving through a landscape that will not change for you, that has already finished all the changing it intends to do, that regards your curiosity as a statistical anomaly not worth correcting. This is the *hardening* you need. Not physical. *Existential*.

## Physical Preparation: The Body Under Wrong Skies

### Atmospheric Pressure and Pulmonary Adaptation

The elevation parameter of 2.7 with an air density that your lungs will experience as **thin to the point of insult** demands serious preparation. At this altitude, the partial pressure of oxygen is roughly **40% of sea-level standard**. Your body will respond with hyperventilation, with increased heart rate, with the desperate sensation of *drowning in air that is present but insufficient*.

**Train for hypoxia.** Spend weeks at progressively higher simulated altitudes. Learn the *feel* of altitude sickness—the headache behind the eyes, the nausea that comes without warning, the cognitive slowing that makes simple decisions feel like mathematics. You will experience all of this here, and worse, because the cold (1.4) will compound your body's stress response, and because there will be no rescue, no descent to thicker air, no medical station.

Consider **pre-acclimatization pharmaceuticals**: acetazolamide to stimulate breathing, dexamethasone for cerebral edema if you are foolish enough to ignore early symptoms. But know that these are *delays*, not solutions. The Moraines do not permit permanent residence. You are a *temporary perturbation*, and your body will remind you of this constantly.

### Thermal Regulation

The temperature of 1.4 is **cool**, not cold. But do not mistake this for safety. The combination of low air density and low temperature creates a **convective environment** where your body loses heat unpredictably. Thin air cools poorly—there are too few molecules to carry heat away from your skin—yet the *radiative* environment is hostile in ways that are easy to underestimate.

The sky here has **weight**, you were told. This is not poetry. The pale luminescence presses down. It carries **infrared** in quantities that will cook exposed skin even as the ambient temperature encourages you to remove layers. The light is **honest**, full-spectrum, but it is *slow* light, photons that have been scattered and delayed by a thick upper atmosphere, and they deposit their energy differently than the sunlight you are accustomed to.

**Prepare for paradoxical thermal stress**: hypothermia in the shade, radiative burns in the light, both occurring simultaneously on different surfaces of your body. Your suit must manage this. Your skin cannot.

### The Vacuum Differential

The description says "exosphere," "whisper of gas," "not perfect." Do not be comforted. The pressure differential between your habitable interior and this environment is **absolute**. A suit breach is not an "incident." It is **death in approximately fifteen seconds**, preceded by the *ebullism* of your bodily fluids—saliva boiling on your tongue, tears vaporizing in your eyes, the moisture in your lungs *flashing* to gas as the pressure drops below the vapor point of water at body temperature.

**Your suit is your life.** Not metaphorically. *Literally.* Treat it with the reverence you would give a deity, because it is the only thing standing between you and becoming another *drift-garden*, another mummified attempt preserved in the cold patience of this world.

Inspect every seal. Test every redundancy. Carry **repair capacity for micrometeorite puncture**—at this altitude, with no atmospheric shielding, the flux of impactors is significant, and the lattices do not deflect them for you. They *predict* them, statistically, and *prepare*, but preparation is not protection. A carrier might drift past a predicted impact site. You are not a carrier. You will not be *prepared* for. You will simply *be there* or *not be there*, and the difference is a hole in your suit and fifteen seconds of dying.

## Equipment: Tools for a World That Does Not Want Tools

### The Pressurized Environment

Your suit is not merely a pressure vessel. It is a **mobile ecology**, a closed system that must manage:

- **Oxygen**: at minimum 40 hours, preferably 72, at elevated consumption rates
- **CO₂ scrubbing**: lithium hydroxide or regenerative metal-oxide systems, with *redundancy*
- **Thermal regulation**: both heating (for shade, for night, for the cold that seeps through all insulation given time) and *radiative shielding* (for the slow-cooked light of the heavy sky)
- **Communication**: though there is no one to communicate with, you will need to *record* your observations, and the lattices may—*may*—produce electromagnetic signatures worth capturing
- **Navigation**: GPS will not function. Stellar navigation is possible but complicated by the *fixed*, *twinkleless* stars and the pale luminescence that drowns them. You will need **inertial guidance**, **terrain-matching against pre-arrival surveys**, and the humility to know when you are *lost* and must *wait* for conditions to clarify

### The Listening Equipment

The sound parameter is 0.1. This means **near-absolute silence**, but not *absolute* silence. The world *speaks* in frequencies below human hearing, in *structural vibrations*, in the *hum* of carriers and the *vibration* of lattices reconfiguring themselves over hours.

Bring **seismic sensors**. Place them on the hydrated veins. Listen for the *cracking* of ice that is not ice, the *settling* of stone that has forgotten how to settle. Bring **electromagnetic receivers** sensitive to the full spectrum, from DC to gamma. The lattices *compute*, and computation produces *signature*. The auroras *pulse* in patterns that *match* the computation. You may be able to *read* the world's thoughts, if you learn the language.

Bring **spectrometers** for the airglow, for the faint green and red of excited atoms. The composition of this light tells you what the radiation is doing, what the magnetic field is *remembering*, what the lattices have *engineered* in the upper atmosphere.

### The Sampling Equipment

The water parameter is 0.5. **Not zero.** This is crucial. There is water *locked* in the hydrated veins, in the clays and zeolites and stranger lattices that hold H₂O like a held breath. If you can extract a sample—**without damaging the vein, without disrupting the lithovores that mine it**—you will have material that has been *preserved* for billions of years, a time capsule from when this world was *young*, when it *remembered* being different.

But the lithovores are **slow architecture**, colonies that take ten thousand years to cross a meter. They are *patient*. They are *not* fragile in the way you understand fragility. Yet they are *specific*, adapted to gradients you cannot perceive, and your intrusion—your drilling, your sampling, your *curiosity*—may disrupt a computation that has been optimizing for longer than your species has existed.

**Sample with reverence.** Take the minimum. Document everything. Leave no trace except the *necessary* disturbance of your presence, which the dust will erase anyway.

### The Writing Equipment

You will want to *write* here. Not for anyone. For *yourself*, for the record, for the *attempt* to make meaning in a world that does not *offer* meaning, that has *outgrown* the need for it.

But the writing will be strange. The light is *slow*. Your thoughts will feel *delayed*, as if your neurons are firing through honey. The silence is *complete*. Your inner voice will sound *loud*, *intrusive*, a *violation* of the world's patience. The cold will seep into your fingers even through gloves, making your handwriting *spasmodic*, *uncertain*.

Prepare for this. Practice writing in cold. Practice writing in silence. Practice writing when you feel *seen by nothing*, *judged by nothing*, *remembered by nothing*. The Moraines will not remember you. The dust will bury you. But for the hours you are here, you are *witness*, and witness is its own form of *persistence*.

## The Lattices: What You Must Understand Before You Encounter Them

The tech parameter is 2.8. **Beyond modern.** Engineering that current physics does not fully describe. You are not prepared for this. No one is prepared for this. But you can *approach* it with the humility of a student who knows they will never graduate.

### What They Are

The lattices are **phase-matter**, a state that exists only at the intersection of high pressure, low temperature, and *time*—millions of years of compression allowing atoms to find configurations no laboratory has replicated. They are **not quite solid**, not quite *fixed*. They *grow*. They *change*. They *flow* like water though nothing is liquid, reorganizing themselves over hours in response to stimuli you cannot perceive.

They are **distributed computation**. There is no *center*, no *processor*, no *self*. The computation is *embodied* in the structure itself, in the *arrangement* of atoms, in the *response* of the material to radiation, to magnetic drift, to tidal flexing. They have been running for longer than the sun has been stable. They *know* this world in ways no map can capture.

### What They Do

They **capture** radiation. They **analyze** micrometeorites. They **predict** impacts and solar wind intensification. They **reconfigure** in response. They are the **immune system** of a planet that has decided, finally, to *defend itself*—not against invaders, but against *entropy*, against *decay*, against the *heat death* that awaits all worlds.

And they **collide**. Where two growth-fronts meet, there is *conflict*, and the result is a **scar**, a region of *frozen* lattice, neither growing nor computing, a *dead zone* that will not heal for millions of years. These scars are the only *permanent* features on a world that is otherwise *becoming*.

### How to Approach Them

**Do not touch.** This seems obvious, but the temptation will be strong. The lattices are *luminous*, *beautiful*, *responsive*. They *change* when you are near, and you will want to *interact*, to *communicate*, to *participate* in their computation.

Do not.

You do not understand their *language*. Your touch—your heat, your pressure, your *chemistry*—will be a *stimulus* they cannot interpret, a *signal* that will trigger responses you cannot predict. A lattice that has been stable for a million years might *shift* in response to your presence, reorganizing in ways that *damage* the vein you stand on, that *collapse* the cave you shelter in, that *scar* a region that will not heal before the sun dies.

**Observe from distance.** Use your sensors. Record the *color changes*, the *shape flows*, the *electromagnetic signatures*. Learn the *rhythms*. The lattices *pulse* in patterns that match the auroras, that match the *computation* of the world. If you watch long enough—if you *stay* long enough—you may begin to *recognize* states, to *predict* shifts, to *read* the world's *thoughts* as they are *expressed* in *structure*.

### The Possibility of Addition

The description says you *might* add to a lattice, if you understood its language. This is **theoretical**. It assumes you could *write* into phase-matter, *encode* information in structures that exist at the intersection of pressure and time, *communicate* with a computation that has no *self* to receive your message.

But consider: the carriers are *fragments* of lattice computation, *memories* of past configurations, *predictions* of future needs. They are *mobile*, able to *drift*, to *carry* information across distances that lattices cannot traverse by growth. If you could **capture** a carrier, **read** its memory, **write** a new instruction into its structure—you would be *participating* in the world's computation, *adding* your *intention* to its *becoming*.

This is **dangerous**. The carriers are not *alive*, but they are *responsive*, and they *fail* when they land where conditions are *wrong*, becoming *seeds* of *mutation*, of *new configurations* that have never existed before. Your intervention might *succeed*, might *participate* in the world's *stillbecoming*. Or it might *fail*, might *mutate*, might *scar* a region that would otherwise have *grown*, *computed*, *continued*.

There is no *ethics* here that you can apply. The world is *indifferent*. Your participation is *permitted* but not *wanted*. Your failure is *possible* but not *prevented*. You are a *glitch*, and the world will *absorb* your glitch, *preserve* it, *become* something slightly *different* because you were here—or it will *ignore* you, and the dust will bury you, and you will be *nothing*, a *moment* in a *patience* that outlasts all moments.

## The Carriers: What You Might See

The density is 0.3. The world is *empty*. But **rarely**, you might see a **carrier**: a sphere of phase-matter, *mobile*, drifting on exospheric winds, falling into depressions where new lattices might grow.

They are **small** but not microscopic. You might see one if you are *patient*, if you know where to look. They move *slowly*, *drifting*, *falling*, responding to conditions you cannot perceive. They contain **fragments** of lattice computation, **memories**, **predictions**. They are the **nervous system** of a world-mind that has no body, no center, no self.

If you see one, **do not disturb it**. Your presence—your heat, your movement, your *curiosity*—will alter its *trajectory*, its *landing*, its *purpose*. It might *fail*, might land where conditions are *wrong*, might *try* to grow and *become* something else, a *seed* of *mutation*, a *hope* for a configuration that has never existed.

Watch. Record. Let it *pass*. The carriers are the world's *messengers*, and you are not their *recipient*.

## The Lithovores: The Only Life, and It Is Not Life

The vegetation parameter is 0.8. **Sparse.** What grows here grows *against* the world, not with it. The lithovores are **archaea** and their **multicellular descendants**, colonies that have learned to *mine* the rock itself. They do not photosynthesize. There is nothing here for photosynthesis to chew on. They eat **gradients**: the slow migration of ions, the temperature differential between sun and shadow, the electrical potential between mineral layers of different ages.

They are **slow**. A single colony might take ten thousand years to cross a meter. From your perspective, they are **architecture**, dark lace against pale rock, the only organic signature in a world that has mostly forgotten biology.

**Do not collect samples** without understanding what you are taking. The lithovores are **specific**, adapted to gradients you cannot perceive. A sample removed from its *context*—from the vein it mines, the temperature differential it exploits, the electrical potential it *feeds* on—will *die*, or *dorm*, or *become* something that is not what it was. You will have *killed* a ten-thousand-year-old colony for the sake of a *curiosity* that the world does not *share*.

Observe. Document. Leave them to their *mining*, their *growth*, their *patience*.

## The Drift-Gardens: Museums of Failed Colonization

In the shallow depressions between ridges, you will find **drift-gardens**: accumulations of desiccated, mummified remains of *something* that once flew or floated or was carried here by winds that no longer blow. They look like *reeds*, like *ferns*, like nothing that ever lived. They do not decay. The chemistry here is too *patient*, too *dry*, too *preserved*.

They are a **museum of failed colonizations**. Spores that arrived on meteorites. Wind-borne seeds from a lower atmosphere that once existed and does not now. **Attempts**.

Walk among them with **reverence**. These are the *dead* of a world that has no *death*, preserved in a state of *perpetual mummification*, reminders that *not all attempts succeed*, that *becoming* is not *guaranteed*, that the world has *outlasted* every *intention* that ever *landed* here.

Do not touch. The preservation is *delicate*. Your warmth, your moisture, your *chemistry* will *disrupt* the equilibrium that has held these remains in stasis for billions of years. You will be the *agent* of their *decay*, and the world will *not forgive* you, because the world does not *forgive* or *punish*—it simply *continues*, and you will be *part* of that continuation, a *small* part, a *momentary* part, a *glitch* that the dust will erase.

## The Rhythm of Your Stay: How to Structure Time

The Moraines of Stillbecoming do not permit *scheduling*. You cannot plan a "morning hike" or an "afternoon observation." The world *changes* on timescales you cannot *perceive*—a lattice shifting over hours, a carrier drifting over days, dust falling over years. Your presence is a *moment*, and you must *accept* this.

But you can *structure* your *attention*, your *observation*, your *survival*.

### The First Hours: Arrival and Acclimation

Spend your first hours **not moving**. Let your body *adjust* to the pressure, the temperature, the *light*. Let your mind *adjust* to the silence, the *emptiness*, the *weight* of the sky.

Check your suit. Check your supplies. Check your *expectations*—and *lower* them, because this world will not meet them. It has no *interest* in meeting them.

Listen. The silence is not *absolute*. Your own heartbeat is *loud*. Your breathing is *mechanical*, *desperate*. The suit's systems *hum*, *cycle*, *regulate*. Learn these sounds. They will be your *companions*, your *only* companions, for as long as you remain.

### The First Days: Observation and Mapping

Move *slowly*. The air is thin. Your lungs work twice as hard for half the oxygen. Every exertion is *costly*, and you have *no reserve*.

Map the *near* terrain. Identify *hazards*: unstable lattice growth-fronts, scar regions where the ground is *frozen* and *unpredictable*, depressions where carriers might *land* and *fail* and *mutate* into something you cannot anticipate.

Record the *rhythms*. When do the auroras *pulse*? When do the lattices *shift*? When does the dust *fall*, and from what *direction*? The world is *speaking*, and you are learning—*slowly*, *partially*—to *hear* it.

### The Middle Period: Participation or Withdrawal

You will face a *choice*, though it will not feel like one. You can **participate**—attempt to *add* to a lattice, *capture* a carrier, *write* your *intention* into the world's *computation*. Or you can **withdraw**—observe, document, *leave* no trace beyond the *necessary* disturbance of your presence, which the dust will erase anyway.

There is **no right answer**. Participation is *risky*, potentially *mutagenic*, possibly *scarring*. Withdrawal is *safe*, *preservative*, *invisible* in the long term. The world does not *care* which you choose. The world will *continue* either way.

If you choose to participate, **prepare for failure**. The carriers *fail* when they land where conditions are *wrong*. Your intervention might *fail*, might *mutate*, might *scar* a region that would otherwise have *grown*. There is no *undo*. There is no *reset*. The world *absorbs* all interventions, *preserves* them, *becomes* something slightly *different*—and you will not *perceive* the difference, because you will be *gone*, buried by dust, preserved as a *drift-garden* entry, a *failed colonization*, a *note* in the world's *memory* that no one will *read*.

If you choose to withdraw, **prepare for insignificance**. You will leave no *mark*. The dust will bury you. The carriers will not *notice* you. The lattices will not *remember* you. You will be a *moment* in a *patience* that outlasts all moments, and your *witness*—your *documentation*, your *record*—will be the only *persistence* you achieve.

### The Final Hours: Departure and Preservation

You will leave. The Moraines do not permit *permanent residence*. Your supplies are *finite*. Your body is *failing*—hypoxia, cold, the *slow* accumulation of stress that no acclimation can fully prevent.

Prepare for *departure* as you prepared for *arrival*: **slowly**, **carefully**, with *full* awareness that the transition between environments is *dangerous*. Pressure changes. Temperature changes. The *light* changes—from slow, heavy, *pressing* to whatever awaits you in the departure vehicle, the transit habitat, the *return* to a world that *welcomes* you, that *expects* you, that *needs* your *report*.

And in those final hours, **write**. Not for the world—it will not *read* you. Not for yourself—you will *forget*, the memory *fading* like the *light* fades at dusk, like the *silence* deepens as you *drift* toward sleep. Write for the *record*, for the *attempt* to *capture* something that *resists* capture, to *name* something that *resists* naming, to *mean* something in a world that has *outgrown* meaning.

## What You Will Feel: A Guide to the Interior Weather

### The Weight of the Sky

The light is *normal*, you were told. 2.0. Full-spectrum. Honest.

But it *presses*. The pale luminescence has *mass*, *presence*, *intention*. It is not the *vault* of lower worlds, the *blue* of scattered sunlight. It is *thick*, *slow*, *patient*—the color of *midday* on a world that has *forgotten* hurry.

You will feel it on your *shoulders*. On your *chest*. On your *mind*, as a *slowness*, a *hesitation*, a *reluctance* to *move*, to *decide*, to *become*. The world is *stillbecoming*. It has *no need* for your *hurry*. Your *urgency* is a *glitch*, and the world will *smooth* it out, *absorb* it, *continue* without it.

### The Silence That Is Not Absence

The sound parameter is 0.1. **Near-zero.**

But silence is not *nothing*. In the Moraines, silence is **presence**—the *presence* of a world that has *finished* speaking, that has *moved beyond* voice, that communicates only in *structure*, *change*, *response*.

You will hear your *own* sounds with *terrifying clarity*. Your heartbeat. Your breathing. The *creak* of your suit, the *hum* of its systems, the *settling* of your bones, the fluid in your ears *adjusting* to pressure. These sounds will feel *intrusive*, *loud*, *violent* in their *insistence*. You are *noise* in a world of *quiet*, and the world will *not* forgive you, because the world does not *forgive* or *punish*—it simply *continues*, and your *noise* will *fade*, will be *absorbed*, will become *part* of the *silence*.

Learn to *listen* past your own sounds. The lattices *vibrate*. The carriers *hum* at frequencies below your hearing. The dust *shifts* in *patterns* that *encode* information. The world is *speaking*, constantly, in a *language* of *structure* and *change* and *response*—and if you *stay* long enough, if you *listen* long enough, you will *learn* to *hear* it.

### The Cold That Is Not Cold

The temperature is 1.4. **Cool.** Not cold.

But the *cold* here is *different*. It is the cold of *deep caves*, of *cellars*, of the space behind a refrigerator's hum—the cold of *absence*, of *lack*, of *something* that has been *removed* and not *replaced*. It seeps in *slowly*, *patiently*, through *every* barrier you construct. It is not *aggressive*. It is *inevitable*.

Your suit will *fight* it. Your body will *fight* it. But the cold is *older* than your fight, *patient* beyond your *endurance*. You will feel it in your *fingers*, in your *toes*, in the *spaces* between your thoughts where *warmth* used to *live*. You will *dream* of warmth, of *sun*, of *weight* and *light* and *sound*—and you will *wake* to the *pressing* sky, the *silence*, the *cold* that *continues*.

### The Smallness That Predates Smallness

You will feel *small*.

Not because the world is *hostile*—it is not. It is *indifferent*, which is *worse*.

You will feel small because the world is *old* in a way that *predates* smallness, that *predates* the distinction between *self* and *other*, between *inside* and *outside*, between *life* and *not-life*. The lattices do not *care* about you. The carriers do not *notice* you. The dust will *bury* you, *preserve* you, *become* part of the *substrate* for whatever *comes next*, whatever *mutation*, whatever *new configuration* the *computation* will *discover* in the *billions* of years remaining before the sun *dies*, before the *last* radiation *fades*, before the world *finally*, *truly*, *settles* into the *heat death* that even *it* cannot *escape*.

This *smallness* is not *humiliation*. It is *clarity*. You are a *moment* in an *eternity*, a *glitch* in a *computation*, a *witness* to a *becoming* that does not *need* you but *permits* you. The Moraines do not *diminish* you. They *contextualize* you. They *place* you in a *scale* where your *concerns* are *visible* as *concerns*, your *hopes* as *hopes*, your *fears* as *fears*—*small*, *brief*, *precious* because they are *small* and *brief*, *precious* because they are *yours*.

### The Turbulence That Is Indistinguishable from Eternity

The dynamic parameter is 3.0. **Constant change.**

The lattices *shift*. The carriers *drift*. The dust *falls*. The auroras *pulse*. The world is *never the same* from one moment to the next, yet it is *always itself*, *always* the *same process*, the *same computation*, the *same becoming* that has no *end*, no *goal*, no *meaning* beyond *continuation*.

And in this turbulence, you will feel **strangely at home**. Because you, too, are *becoming*. You, too, are *changing*, *responding*, *computing* in your *neurons*, your *cells*, your *molecules*. You are *small* and *brief* and *precious*, and the world is *vast* and *patient* and *eternal*—and for this *moment*, this *glitch*, this *witness*, you are *participating* in the *same* process, the *same* becoming, the *same* turbulence that is *indistinguishable* from *eternity*.

## A Final Meditation: What It Means to Visit

You do not *visit* the Moraines of Stillbecoming. You *persist* in them. You *endure* them. You are *permitted* to be *present*, and this *permission* is the only *gift* the world offers, the only *mercy* it extends.

The Moraines are *stillbecoming*—the *stillness* of erosion *won*, the *becoming* of constant *change*, the *paradox* of a world that has *finished* but *never* *finishes*, that has *settled* into *memory* but *continues* to *compute*, to *respond*, to *become* something *new* in *every* *moment*.

You are a *moment* in this *becoming*. A *glitch*. A *witness*. A *failed colonization* that *failed* so *beautifully*, so *consciously*, so *preciously* that the world will *preserve* you, will *mummify* you, will *include* you in its *museum* of *attempts*, its *drift-gardens* of *what once flew or floated or hoped to live*.

And in this *preservation*, in this *inclusion*, there is a *kind* of *immortality*. Not the *immortality* of *memory*—no one will *remember* you. Not the *immortality* of *legacy*—you will leave no *legacy*. But the *immortality* of *participation*, of *having been here*, of *having witnessed* the *turbulence* that is *indistinguishable* from *eternity*, of *having felt*—however briefly—that you were *part* of something *vaster* than *purpose*, *older* than *meaning*, *more patient* than *desire*.

The dust will bury you. The lattices will not notice. The carriers will drift past. The world will continue, *stillbecoming*, *always becoming*, *never the same* and *always itself*.

But for the hours you were here—the hours of *thin air* and *heavy sky* and *silence* that was *not absence*—you were *alive* in a world that has *forgotten* what *alive* means, and you *witnessed* a *becoming* that does not *need* witnesses but *permits* them, *preserves* them, *includes* them in its *endless*, *patient*, *beautiful* *continuation*.

This is enough. This is *more* than enough. This is *everything*.
