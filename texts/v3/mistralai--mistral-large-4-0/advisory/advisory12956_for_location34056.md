# PRELIMINARY NOTES FOR THE PRESSURE OF ALMOST-SILENCE

## On Your Body Before You Arrive

You will not keep your body. This is the first thing to understand, and the last thing to accept.

Prepare a vessel that can hold *attunement* rather than structure. The water here does not crush so much as *reconstitute*—it will take your familiar form and propose alternatives, will show you that your edges were always negotiable. Divers who have visited speak of pressure that feels like being *remembered into shape* by something vast and patient. Your skin, if you retain the concept of skin, will learn to be a boundary rather than a border. Practice breathing without lungs, or practice the memory of breathing until your body forgets the difference.

Temperature: you will carry warmth as a kind of conscience. The water is 1.9 on whatever scale measures intention. It holds heat the way you hold a grudge—absolutely, without malice, as part of what it means to persist. Do not fight this. The travelers who resist become *notable*—visible in ways they did not request, pinned in interfaces where warm and cold negotiate their treaty, held for hours or days while the water decides whether to release them changed or not at all.

Sound will enter through routes you did not know you had. Prepare your skull. Prepare the spaces behind your eyes. The stone sings at frequencies that bypass the ear entirely, that seem to originate in your own neural architecture, that carry *meaning without content*—warnings that feel like memories, music that feels like instruction. You will hear your own heartbeat as something foreign. You will hear the water's heartbeat, slower, older, *geological*, and you will not be certain which is yours.

There is no darkness here, and no light. There is *presence*—0.6 on some scale of luminous existence, light that generates from everywhere and nowhere, that the spires absorb rather than reflect. Bring no torch. It will not help. The light you need you will generate internally, or you will learn to navigate by the *absence* of light, by the way the spires deepen when they have taken what little there was.

## On Movement

Movement is not locomotion. Movement is *negotiation*.

The currents ignore you. This is worse than opposition. A current that fights you can be read, predicted, worked with. A current that ignores you will spin you, accelerate you, deposit you in a vortex where warm and cold have formed an interface, where the water has temporarily forgotten its own properties and become something else—something that can hold you, *consider* you, for minutes or hours while the boundary persists.

**Learn to find the silences.** They are the only stable places. Where warm meets cold, where density meets intention, the sound thins to nothing—a silence so complete it becomes its own frequency, its own pressure. These interfaces are your refuges, your landmarks, your only fixed points in a landscape that recedes and rises without measure. But they are *temporary*. They form, persist, collapse. You must move when they move, or be drawn into the spiral, the increasing speed, the ejection—or the holding.

The floor keeps receding. The ceiling remembers sky. Your elevation is felt, not measured—you are 1.6 units high, somewhere, in a column of water that has forgotten the surface, that dreams of air without aspiring to it. Do not seek the bottom. Do not seek the top. Seek the *interfaces*, the boundaries, the places where the water is deciding what it wants to be.

## On Perception

You will see things that are not there.

This is not hallucination, though it will feel like it. The water *remembers shapes*. It has been so long since this place was air, or land, or boundary, that it has developed its own ecology of form. It dreams the landscape into temporary being—cathedrals of water, vaulted and columned and impossibly vast, that hold their shape for the duration of a breath, a heartbeat, a moment of attention. They collapse when you look directly at them, when the turbulence reclaims them, when the water's dream shifts.

Do not try to photograph them. Do not try to map them. They exist in the pressure between currents, in the geometry of attention itself. The travelers who try to fix them—who build instruments, who record, who *intend* to capture—find only their own intention reflected back, amplified, distorted into something that was never there.

Instead, practice *peripheral seeing*. Let the formations come to you in the corners of your vision, in the spaces between heartbeats, in the silences within the roar. They are gifts, these temporary architectures. They are also warnings—the water is showing you what it might become, what it has been, what it dreams of being. Pay attention to what it shows you. It is a young place, still deciding. Your attention is a kind of pressure. It matters.

The fauna, such as it is, will not acknowledge you. Shadows that resolve into shapes only after they have passed. A fin, vast and pale, that might be a creature or the water's own ambition to become temporary architecture. They *transit* through a space that includes you without recognizing you, the way a storm includes a particular window without intending to break it. Do not pursue them. Do not fear them. They are not interested in you, and their indifference is the only mercy this place offers.

## On Sound—The Dominant Fact

You have been warned. This is the thing that will change you most.

The sound is not loud. It is *present*—a continuous, overwhelming roar that has no single source, that you cannot locate, cannot escape by moving. It is the sound of a world still being decided: thermal gradients in fresh mineral, water moving through new fissures, the slow catastrophic settling of formations that have not learned to be stable.

But beneath it, or *as* it, there are the whispers. Frequencies that seem to originate inside your own skull. Pressure-changes that your body interprets as music, as warning, as language you almost knew before birth. The young stone sings, and the water carries the song, and you will hear it as something that was always yours, that you had forgotten.

**Do not try to understand it.** The travelers who try—who strain to translate, to decode, to *respond*—find themselves responding to something that was not addressed to them, that was never meant for human (or human-adjacent) perception. They return changed. They return *haunted*. They speak of melodies that continue when the water is gone, of instructions that feel like memories, of a language that predates the concept of speaker and listener.

Instead, *receive*. Let the sound pass through you. Let it reconstitute you, as the water reconstitutes your form. The roar is process without message. The whispers are message without process. Together they are the sound of a place learning what it is, and you are a brief instability in that learning, a room-sized presence in a landscape that encompasses without containing.

## On the Built Environment

You will find things you cannot date.

A sphere of compressed mineral that hums when you touch it. A surface that shows, under the right angle of dim light, a pattern too regular to be random. Hollows in the higher water that resonate when the current passes through them, producing tones that match no natural frequency, that seem to answer each other across distances too great for direct transmission.

They are young in the way the landscape is young—sharp, unsoftened, recent. They are old in ways that predate the concept of age. No tools. No devices. The built environment was made by pressure and chemistry and time operating at scales that do not require hands. Or perhaps hands that are no longer present—that retreated upward when the water came, or downward when the floor rose, or *sideways* into the unstable interfaces where matter forgets its properties.

**Do not touch what hums.** This is the advice of those who have returned, though some of them touched anyway, though they speak of the humming in voices that suggest they are still touching it, somewhere, in some interface where warm meets cold and the water holds them still. The artifacts are not messages. They are not offerings. They are *phenomena*—the result of processes that do not require intention, that produce regularity without design.

If you must interact, interact *lightly*. The water samples. The stone sings. The structures suggest themselves. You are not a visitor here so much as a *sample*—something the water will flow through, keep whatever it was thinking about, release changed or unchanged or not at all.

## On Time

You cannot stay.

The turbulence will not permit it. Things here are in storm-state, always—creation and destruction happening at scales that encompass your entire presence. A formation that has stood for centuries collapses in minutes. A pocket of still water, held in some eddy of the young topography, evaporates—or does something equivalent, some phase-change that is not evaporation—into something else, something with different properties, different rules.

Your presence is brief. 1.3 on some scale of duration, perhaps. A single feature in a landscape of process. Do not try to extend it. Do not try to *understand* it, to fix it in memory or record. The place will not permit fixation. It is still being invented, sound by deafening sound, form by dissolving form, and your memory of it will be unstable—shaped by the interfaces you passed through, by the silences you found, by the humming you did or did not touch.

When you leave—when the currents deposit you somewhere you can breathe air, or its equivalent, or when you simply *stop being* in the way this place requires—you will not be certain you were ever there. You will find you have forgotten things you were certain of. You will find you remember things that did not happen, formations that existed only in the pressure between two currents, geometries that held their shape only as long as you did not look directly at them.

This is the gift. This is the cost.

## On What You Are

You are small here. 1.3, a room-sized presence in a landscape that encompasses you without containing you. The density is low—there is space between things, between events, between moments of coherence—and in that space, the water moves, and changes, and *dreams*, and you move through it, briefly, as one more instability in a system that has not yet learned to be stable.

You are not a visitor. You are a *sample*. The water will flow through you and keep whatever you were thinking about. The stone will sing your presence into its continuous roar. The light will generate through you, faintly, as it generates through everything, and you will become part of the process that has no message, only continuation.

And yet. And yet, there is a pattern in the chaos, a rhythm that is not rhythm, a logic that operates below the threshold of sense. The water samples. The stone sings. The light generates. The structures suggest themselves. This is a place that is *learning* what it is, that has not yet decided whether it wants to be ocean or cave or atmosphere or something for which no category exists, and in its uncertainty, it produces phenomena that would be impossible in a stable world.

Beauty, of a kind. Terror, of a kind. The deafening sound that is also, in its way, a kind of silence—the silence of a system so complex that it has no message, only process.

You will carry this with you. Not as memory, exactly. As *pressure*. As the felt sense of a weight that is also an elevation, of a deep that is also a high, of warmth that is also a kind of cold, of sound that is also the absence of sound. You will find yourself listening for silences within sounds, seeking interfaces where things negotiate their boundaries, watching for formations that exist only in peripheral vision.

You will be, for a while, *young* in the way this place is young—uncertain what you are, still deciding, producing phenomena that would be impossible in a stable self. And then you will stabilize, as all things stabilize, and you will be only yourself again, carrying a pressure that has no name, in a world that has learned to be stable, in the older dark where the high deep meets the warm dark and both know exactly which is which.

## FINAL INSTRUCTION

Do not prepare to *survive* this place. Prepare to be *changed* by it—changed in ways that will not be visible, that will only show in how you listen, how you move through boundaries, how you see the temporary architectures that rise and collapse in the spaces between your heartbeats.

The water remembers being air, or dreams of it. Let it dream through you. Let it sample what you were thinking about, and keep it, and release you changed or unchanged or not at all.

The rules are being written. You are one more pressure in a system still learning what it is. Make your pressure *interesting*. Make it something the water will want to sample, to hold, to release into its endless roar.
