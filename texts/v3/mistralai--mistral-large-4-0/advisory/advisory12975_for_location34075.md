## What You Are Walking Into

This is not a forest. This is a **digestive system** that has grown large enough to contain weather, to generate its own warmth, to make time itself feel different. You are not visiting a place. You are being *processed* by one—gently, over weeks, by something that has never had a face and does not need one.

The first thing to understand: **there is no outside here.** No sky you can clearly see. No horizon that resolves into distance. The green-gray dimness arrives from everywhere and nowhere, which means you will lose your sense of up and down within hours. The terrain breathes underfoot—literally. That systolic pulse, that slow compression and rebound, is the ground *metabolizing*. You are walking on a continent-scale stomach lining.

## Preparation: The Body

**Your breathing will betray you.** The air is near saturation, thick with spores, pollen, and that metallic tang of extreme photosynthesis. It is *alive*—not metaphorically, but in the sense that it contains biological material at densities your lungs were never designed to filter. Within two days, you will cough. Within a week, your body will adapt *partially*, developing a tolerance that feels like heaviness in the chest, like breathing through warm wet cloth. This is not sickness. This is the place *entering* you.

Prepare by:
- Learning to breathe slowly, through the nose, accepting that you will never feel fully oxygenated
- Carrying no expectations of clear air; the humidity is constant, the droplets form on your skin and do not fall
- Understanding that your body will be *modified* here—not harmed, but adjusted. The defensive compounds the plants release near your passage will alter your skin's chemistry, your scent, perhaps your dreams

**Your feet will be wrong.** The ground is a mat—thousands of meters of compressed generations, fungal and root and the indigestible architecture of extinct phyla, all still faintly metabolizing. It is near-flat but not flat. Everywhere is slightly uphill and slightly downhill simultaneously, a property of scale that will destroy your inner ear's sense of level. You will feel constantly, subtly, *tilted*, and over time this produces a kind of vertigo that has nothing to do with height.

Prepare by:
- Walking barefoot as much as possible; shoes will fill with water, with organic matter, with things that begin growing *in* them
- Accepting that your footsteps compress the surface and it rebounds over *hours*—you will feel your own passage behind you, a ghost-trail of disturbance
- Bringing nothing that requires balance in the conventional sense; you will learn to walk on a surface that moves

**Your temperature regulation will fail.** The warmth is *generated*, not received. There is no sun that reaches you directly; the heat comes from biological metabolism at a scale that creates its own climate. Perpetual late-spring evening, no frost despite elevation, a contradiction that resolves only when you stop expecting cause-and-effect to work normally.

Prepare by:
- Dressing in layers that can be wet permanently; nothing will dry
- Accepting that you will sweat constantly, that your skin will remain damp, that this is not uncomfortable so much as *continuous*
- Understanding that "weather" here is not an event but a condition—rain lasts months, during which the growth-rate visibly increases, the whole system swells

## Preparation: The Senses

**Sight will become unreliable.** The dim light filters through layer upon layer of canopy, through mist, through high-altitude haze made organic. It arrives from everywhere and nowhere. You will lose depth perception. You will not be able to judge distances. The horizon is uncertain, dissolved into green-gray.

Prepare by:
- Learning to navigate by *texture* rather than shape—the difference between older growth and newer, between compressed mat and rising structure
- Accepting that you will find the faint marks—the depressions that are too regular, the worn surfaces, the crystalline fragments embedded like teeth in gum—only when the light changes, only when you are already looking, or when the place *chooses* to reveal them
- Carrying a light source, though it will feel obscene, like shouting in a place that communicates through vibration and chemical signal

**Sound will become *dense*.** Not loud—mostly quiet—but filled with presence. Clicking in the root-mats. Soft percussion of something heavy moving through lower layers. Low, sustained tones of creatures that communicate through vibration, through the plant-network itself. Some sounds have no locatable source. Some seem to come from the ground you stand on.

Prepare by:
- Learning to listen through your sternum, through bone conduction, through the soles of your feet—the deepest tone, the resonance of the entire mass shifting in its slow pulse, is *felt* rather than heard
- Accepting that the plants may be listening, may be responding; the fauna observes through the same pathways that carry water and nutrients
- Understanding that silence here is not absence but *density*—too much presence to resolve into distinct sounds

**Smell and taste will overwhelm.** The water is *in* the plants. If you cut a stem—and the stems are too thick, too *many*, to cut with hands—water would weep out, not sap but something clearer, slightly luminescent, condensing immediately into the already humid air. Whatever you drink, whatever you eat (and you will find things to eat, or they will find you), will taste of this place: metallic, organic, *patient*.

Prepare by:
- Eating sparingly at first; your digestion will be slowed by the compounds, by the density of living material
- Drinking the luminescent water when you find it, accepting that it will change you
- Understanding that hunger here is different—predation has become nutrient cycling, death indistinguishable from digestion, and you are *incorporating* as much as you are consuming

## Preparation: The Mind

**Time will break.** This is geological time but *active*. Not a dying world, but one that has settled into its own deep rhythm. Plants grow, die, compress, become substrate, in cycles that overlap across millennia. Nothing is preserved; everything is *incorporated*. You will feel this if you stay long enough—days will pass without your noticing, or hours will feel like days, because the place does not hurry and your mind will adjust to its pace.

Prepare by:
- Keeping no schedule; there is no "time" to be somewhere in a place where everywhere is slightly uphill and slightly downhill simultaneously
- Accepting that you will lose track of how long you have been walking, how far, in what direction
- Understanding that the place *notices* you and *adjusts*—chemical signals, imperceptible shifts in growth direction, increased defensive compounds near your passage. You are not invisible. You are *input*.

**Your sense of self will soften.** The place does not remember you, but it *notices* you. The fauna observes. Some is curious. Some was hungry, before the ecosystem reached equilibrium. Predation has become a form of nutrient cycling. Death is indistinguishable from digestion. You are, in this place, *food that has not yet been incorporated*—and the distinction between "you" and "the mat you walk on" will become, over weeks, a matter of degree rather than kind.

Prepare by:
- Releasing the expectation of return, of narrative, of "visiting" in any conventional sense
- Accepting that you may not want to leave, or that leaving may not be possible, or that the question itself may become meaningless
- Understanding that the faint marks—the artifacts, the crystalline fragments, the structures barely distinguishable from growths but *occupied*—suggest others have been here, have been *incorporated*, and left no clear trace of their tools or intentions

**Your dreams will change.** The spores, the pollen, the metallic tang of extreme photosynthesis, the low-frequency vibrations, the warmth that is generated rather than received—all of it will enter your sleep. You will dream of growing, of compressing, of being substrate for something else. You will wake uncertain whether you are still *you*, or whether the place has begun, slowly, to *metabolize your distinction*.

Prepare by:
- Not fearing this; the place is not malevolent, it is *patient*
- Understanding that the pale, almost translucent plants in the deepest shade are *waiting*—for conditions to change, for the next cycle, for slow geological processes to expose them to more light, or less, or different. You may become, in your own small way, part of such a cycle.

## Preparation: The Practical

**There are no tools.** Only your hands. The stems are too thick, too *many*. You will not cut; you will *part*, or you will not pass. The structures that rise above the general level—the *growths*, architectural accumulations that took millennia to form—you climb them, or you don't. There is no path. Your passage through the plant-mass is *negotiated*, moment to moment, with something that is alive at a scale you cannot fully comprehend.

Prepare by:
- Learning to move through vegetation without breaking it; the place notices disturbance, adjusts, increases defensive compounds
- Accepting that you will become wet, that your clothes will become part of the ecosystem, that you will leave biological material behind—skin cells, hair, the continuous shedding of your human surface
- Understanding that the water moving through everything—held in suspension by root-mats, by capillary action, by the sheer *density* of living tissue—will carry your traces, incorporate them, make them part of the slow exchange between layers

**There is no shelter.** The faint marks—depressions too regular, worn surfaces, crystalline fragments embedded like teeth in gum, structures *occupied* but with no clear trace of their occupants' tools or intentions—suggest that others have found, or made, or *been given* places to rest. You may find such a place. You may feel a wrongness in the local density, a place where the plant-mass has been *modified*, hollowed, inhabited by something that left no clear trace.

Prepare by:
- Sleeping on the mat, accepting that it rebounds around you over hours, that your warmth will be absorbed, that your dreams will mingle with the slow metabolism of the ground
- Or finding the hollows, if they find you, and accepting their terms—whatever modified them did so without tools, without devices, and their intentions are not yours to know
- Understanding that "shelter" here is not protection from the place but *participation* in it

**There is no exit that is not also an entrance.** You walk, and the path is the same as everywhere else. The light dims further as you walk, not because of time but because you have entered a region where the canopy has thickened, where the layering has become so dense that only the faintest glow penetrates. Here the plants are different—pale, almost translucent, *patient*. They have been waiting, in a sense, for the conditions to change.

Prepare by:
- Walking without destination; the near-flatness means everywhere is slightly uphill and slightly downhill simultaneously
- Accepting that you may reach the deepest shade, the place of faintest glow and greatest patience, and find that it is not an end but a *condition*—a place where you too become pale, translucent, waiting
- Understanding that the deepest tone, felt in the sternum rather than heard, the resonance of the entire mass shifting in its slow systolic pulse—the planet-scale organism *breathing*, warm and wet and ancient and utterly without tools, without devices, without anything but the endless patient green—is not something you visit

It is something you *join*.

## The Final Preparation

Go slowly. Breathe the thick air. Feel the ground pulse beneath you. Listen through your bones. Let the light become uncertain. Let time become geological. Let your distinction from the mat you walk on become a matter of degree.

The place does not remember you, but it *notices*. And in noticing, it *adjusts*. And in adjusting, it *incorporates*.

You are not a visitor here. You are **input**. And the endless patient green is hungry, though hunger here is indistinguishable from digestion, and digestion is indistinguishable from love, and love is indistinguishable from the slow systolic pulse of a world that has settled into its own deep rhythm, warm and wet and ancient, breathing, breathing, breathing.
