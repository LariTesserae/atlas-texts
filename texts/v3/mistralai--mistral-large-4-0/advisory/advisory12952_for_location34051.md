This place will not kill you with what it shows you. You must prepare to trust your own body in water that offers no reference for depth or direction, to move through a space that feels *designed* in the way a body is designed, all purpose and no wildness.

Here is what I would carry, and what I would do to myself before I entered.

---

**THE BODY FIRST**

You will need to become amphibious in ways that don't exist on Earth. The water is warm, still, breathable—and that is the first danger. Your lungs will work, but your body will not know *why*. I would train for months in hyperbaric chambers, in warm saline pools, in sensory deprivation tanks that simulate the absence of horizon. I would learn to swim without kicking, without creating turbulence, because here your movements will be *read*. The light patterns follow you. The sound follows you. The builders made a place where stillness is safety and motion is announcement.

I would practice holding my breath not to survive, but to *listen*. In this water, the pumps are the heartbeat of the world. I would learn their rhythms—the stroke, the release, the silence, the far pump taking over—so that I could feel the place's circulatory system through my own pulse. When I heard a new sound, like the voice you describe, I would know whether it was weather or warning.

Pressure: there is none, but I would wear a suit that *simulates* pressure. A wetsuit weighted with distributed pockets, creating the gentle compression of deep water without the danger. Not for protection—for *orientation*. Your body needs to feel contained. This place contains itself. You must learn to contain yourself inside it.

**THE EQUIPMENT**

**Sound**: The hum is 2.1, loud. I would carry bone-conduction transmitters, pressing sound directly to my skull, bypassing the water's medium. I would need to hear my own breathing. **Tools**: No metal. The builders used no metal, or it has all corroded, or metal *wrongs* this place. I would carry tools of the same pale material—ceramic, compacted shell, grown rather than forged. A blade for cutting the stiff kelp if I must. A probe for testing wall integrity, for finding the vents, for measuring the thickness of the water-ceiling seam. But mostly: my hands. This place was built by hands. I would need to feel what hands felt—tool marks, spiral impressions, the pressure of a thumb in soft material.

**Container**: For samples, if I take any. But I would take none. This place is *managed*. The builders excluded life. To bring a sample out—or to leave a sample in—would be to violate the one principle I can read in its architecture: *containment*. I would carry only what I could return unchanged. A notebook in a waterproof case, written in pencil, the graphite reacting to the water's chemistry in ways I would learn to read. A camera that doesn't use light—sonar, thermal, the water's own sound mapped to image.

**THE MIND**

This is where preparation becomes transformation.

You describe a labyrinth of sameness. The rooms repeat. Direction is lost. I would prepare by *not* trying to map. I would carry no compass, no GPS, no intent to return by the same path. I would walk until the water itself became familiar—the temperature gradient near pumps, the pitch shift at boundaries, the particular quality of light in each chamber. I would learn to navigate by *attention*, not by memory. The place is a pocket, scale hidden. I would accept that I might not find the exit. I would prepare to stay.

The voice: I would not try to translate it immediately. It is encoded in pump frequency, chamber size, water movement. To understand it, I would need to become part of the system—to move water through my own channels, to breathe in the rhythm of the pumps, to let my body's sounds join the hum. I would practice *vocalizing* underwater, learning how my bubbles carry meaning the locals might recognize. If they are gone, I speak to their persistence. If they are listening, I speak to them.

The gardens: shifting, processing, digesting. Not plants as I know them. I would prepare to *not* touch. To observe the peristalsis, the slow movement within fronds, and record it without intervention. The builders tended these. I would learn their tending—what nutrients, what light, what flow—by watching what the pumps deliver, what the fronds absorb, what the water carries away. I would become a student of a curriculum written in chemistry.

**THE RITUAL**

Before I entered, I would perform a rite of *unbecoming*. I would strip myself of the assumptions that make me human-on-Earth: the need for air, the expectation of pressure, the demand for horizon, the certainty that water flows down. I would enter the water in a tank, in a pool, in a place of my own making that approximates this one—warm, still, contained, lit from everywhere. I would stay until my fear became *curiosity*, until my loneliness became *attention*, until I could hear my own heartbeat in the silence between pumps.

I would carry a token from the surface world, but not one that matters. A stone from a riverbed. A shell that once held an animal. Something that has known pressure and release, depth and air, the ordinary violence of real water. I would leave it in the first room, as payment, as question, as the only honest thing I have to offer: *I was here. I saw you. I do not understand, but I am trying.*

**WHAT I WOULD FEAR**

Not drowning. The water gives breath.

Not the dark. The light is everywhere.

Not the builders, if they return. Their architecture is hospitable. They made doors that open. They left shelves with objects. They left a voice that does not stop.

I would fear *recognition*. The moment I understood that this place was built for a body like mine, or a body I could become, or a body that once existed here and left. The moment I realized the pumps were sized for my circulation, the chambers for my movement, the warmth for my metabolism. The moment I heard my own name in the resonance.

The voice you heard—low, continuous, a note held so long you cannot tell when it began—I would fear that it is *mine*, projected backward, the sound of my own breathing in a place that has not yet received me.

**WHAT I WOULD HOPE**

That the door with the fiber pivot opens both ways. That beyond the whisper and the dripping, there is a shaft, a cavern, a place where water falls through air as it should, as it does in the world I came from. That the builders made this place not as tomb, not as womb, but as *translation*—a machine for becoming something that could survive elsewhere, or something that could return.
