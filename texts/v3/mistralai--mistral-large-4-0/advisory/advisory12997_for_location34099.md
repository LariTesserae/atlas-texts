## Preparation for the Drowned Works

### What You Will Become

You will become wet. Not damp—*wet*, in the way that water is wet, in the way that flesh becomes wet when it has forgotten its boundary with the world around it. The humidity at 1.6 is not weather. It is *medium*. You breathe it. You drink it unconsciously through your skin. Your clothing will cease to be fabric and become a second membrane, a negotiated zone between your internal dryness and the external saturation. Prepare to abandon the idea of dry.

More: you will become *slow*. The dynamic of 1.4 does not merely describe the plants. It describes the *tempo* of the place, and tempo is contagious. Your heartbeat will find a new rhythm. Your thoughts will thicken, become viscous, like water moving through root-mass. You will learn to wait. You will learn that waiting is not empty time but *full* time, time occupied by the pulse, the drip, the hiss. You will become patient in ways you did not know you possessed, or you will break.

And you will become *perceived*. At scale 1.4, you are not anonymous. The Drowned Works is small enough to be *whole*, and whole things notice intrusions. The plants do not attack. They *attend*. They turn their photosynthetic surfaces toward your warmth. They close their leaf-angles in patterns that might be curiosity, might be hunger, might be the slow vegetal equivalent of recognition. You are the gradient. You are the change. The place has been patient for decades. It can be patient with you.

### What to Carry

**Nothing metal.** Or rather: carry metal only if you are prepared to surrender it. The warmth of machines draws the plants. The plants *incorporate*. A knife left in a pocket will be found by root. A watch will become, over hours, over days, a warm core around which green tissue gathers, a seed of slow transformation. If you must carry tools, carry them in materials that do not conduct, that do not retain warmth, that the place will not mistake for ancestor, for kin, for sustenance.

**Water, paradoxically.** You will be surrounded by water and nonetheless thirst. The water here is *participatory*, yes, but it is also *busy*—full of the metabolism of the colonial plants, full of the slow dissolution of the industrial, full of the particular chemistry of this place's long drowning. Drink it and you drink the Works. Do not drink it and you will still be drinking it, through your skin, through your lungs, through the condensation that gathers on every surface including your own. Carry water from elsewhere. Treat it as a sealed boundary, a last dry thing, a reminder of what you were.

**Light, but not for seeing.** The light here is 1.8, normal, but *attenuated*, diffuse, coming from everywhere and nowhere. You will not need a lantern for illumination. You will need it for *signal*—for making yourself known in a place where sound is structure and silence is vulnerability. The animals that remain have learned to announce themselves. Learn their lesson. A light that pulses, that flashes, that says *I am here, I am still alive, I am still strange*. The bioluminescence in the stagnant pools pulses in 4-second intervals. Match this. Become legible.

**Something to press against.** The colonial mats *want* to be touched. You will want to touch them—their peristaltic pulse, their wet warmth, their slow hunger. Do not. Or do, but know what you are doing. Pressing your ear to them is an intimacy. The mats might close around a warm body. The mats might *incorporate* the listening. If you must touch, touch briefly. Touch with awareness. Touch as you would touch a sleeping animal that might wake, that might decide you are food, that might decide you are *substrate*.

**A blade, ceramic or obsidian.** Not for violence—there is nothing to fight here—but for *stratigraphy*. The walls are time. Each layer is a different *now* that has not quite ended. To understand this place, you must read it vertically. A ceramic edge will let you cut into the compressed memory of plant growth, into the mineral deposits of past floods, into the slow accretion of the industrial's afterlife. You will make wounds in the walls. The walls will heal. This is the covenant.

### What to Wear

**Layers that can be abandoned.** You will become damp. You will become wet. The clothing that keeps you dry at first will, by the second day, be a weight, a second skin, a negotiation you can no longer sustain. Wear things you can shed. Wear things that can be *left*—offered to the green, to the water, to the slow incorporation that is this place's only metabolism. The plants grow fastest near warmth. Your body is warmth. Your discarded clothing will become, in time, a warm core, a seed of green tissue, a small cathedral to your brief dryness.

**Boots that can be removed.** There is no dry ground. Only ground that is wetter or less wet. To stand still is to become damp. To sit is to be *absorbed*. Your feet will be in water constantly—not deep water, but *present* water, water that finds every level, that rises to meet you, that holds you in its slow embrace. Boots will fill. Boots will become part of you. Learn to walk barefoot. Learn that the ground is not solid but *receptive*, that each step is a negotiation, a brief pressure against a surface that will, given time, given patience, make you part of its green.

**A covering for the head, but not a seal.** The rain falls soft and without pattern. The condensation gathers. The water weeps from cut stone. Your head will be wet. Let it be wet. But cover it, somewhat, so that the wet does not become *cold*, so that the warmth of your body is not lost to the evaporation that is this place's constant, its slow breath. The warmth matters. The warmth is what the plants seek. The warmth is what makes you *different*, what makes you *perceived*, what makes you *change*.

### What to Unlearn

**The idea of destination.** The scattered built is *legible* but not *navigable* in any conventional sense. You will not find the smokestack and conclude your journey. You will not find the control panel and understand. The purpose is *past*, and the past here is *geology*, *weather*, a slow chemical process that has its own gravity, its own pull. The rails carry only water now, only the slow migration of nutrients, only the occasional floating seed-pod. There is no arrival. There is only the green, and the water, and the slow turning of the wheel that has no hub.

**The idea of return.** You may leave. The place does not prevent this. But you will not return *unchanged*, and "unchanged" is the only metric by which return is measured. The Drowned Works will have made you wet. It will have made you slow. It will have made you *perceived*, and the perception will not end when you exit the boundary of its humidity. You will carry the pulse. You will hear the drip in ordinary rain. You will feel the hiss in ordinary steam. You will become, in the world outside, a brief dryness, a moment of gradient, a temporary warmth that the world will, in time, absorb.

**The idea of self.** At this scale—1.4, the cathedral, the grove, the room—you are not a traveler. You are an *intrusion*. You are a brief perturbation in a system that has forgotten what dry means, that has learned to grow by *inflation* rather than division, that has become, over decades, over centuries, a single organism wearing the shape of industry, of ruin, of process arrested. You are the change. You are the gradient. And the place, patient, 1.4 slow, will wait for you to become part of the vertical time, to become substrate, to become wet.

### What to Listen For

**The pulse.** Subsonic. Felt more than heard. A pressure against the Eustachian tubes, a thrum that makes your teeth ache in sympathy. This is the heartbeat of the colonial mats, the slow peristaltic expansion of their cellular pressure, the sound of growth without limit, of incorporation without end. Match it. Breathe with it. Become, for a time, part of its rhythm.

**The drip.** Without pattern. From leaves, from cut stone, from the interior of the smokestack where water runs in sheets. Each drop is a small event, a moment of gravity, a brief return to the surface tension that holds the world together. Count them if you need to mark time. But know that time here is *vertical*, best perceived in stratification, in the core-sample of a wall, in the compressed memory of a plant's earlier growth.

**The hiss.** Water on warm metal. The sound of temperature differential, of the warm works meeting the wet world. This is where the plants grow fastest. This is where the machines are being *memorialized*, being built into cathedrals, being made into aquifers. The hiss is the sound of the industrial's afterlife, its slow conversion into something that conducts water, that breathes in the humid dark.

**The bong.** A turbine housing, cracked and flooded, struck by debris in the current. Resonant. A note in the place's structure, a reminder that sound is *architecture* here, that in the absence of visual depth, sound becomes the way you map, the way you *know* where you are, the way you avoid becoming *unmapped*, *vulnerable*.

**Your own sound.** Make it. The animals have learned. The frog with its miniature ecosystem, its forest of filaments and mites and photosynthetic hitchhikers. The pale, eyeless thing in the flooded lower levels. The bird with wings too small for its body, living in the cavities of the mats. They announce themselves. They say *I am here, I am still alive, I am still strange*. Learn their lesson. Make sound. Be legible. Do not become silent.

### What to Do When You Arrive

**By water, or not at all.** "Arrive" implies a will you may not have. The water is a condition, not a surface. It occupies the air as much as the low ground. You will be wet before you are *there*, before you have crossed the boundary that is not a boundary, before you have entered the humidity that is the place's true skin. Accept this. Do not fight the wet. The wet is the welcome.

**Stand still, briefly.** Let the place *notice* you. Feel the closing of leaf-angles, the turning of photosynthetic surfaces toward your warmth. Feel the weight of perception, the attention of a whole thing that has been patient for decades, that can be patient with you. Do not move too quickly. Do not rush. The dynamic is 1.4. Match it. Become slow.

**Find a warm surface.** The residual electrification, the phantom current, the warm metal that keeps the bioluminescence alive. Press your hand to it. Feel the difference, the gradient, the warmth that draws the plants, that makes you *change*, that makes you *perceived*. This is the place's metabolism, its slow hunger, its conversion of industrial into organic. You are participating. You are being *seen*.

**Cut a wall.** Any wall. A brick, a layer of mortar, a film of algae, a crust of lichen, the outer sheath of the colonial plant. Cut deeper, if you have the patience. Find the *memory* of the plant, the compressed cells of its earlier growth, still conducting water, still *alive* in the slow sense. The wall is a core-sample of time. Each layer is a different *now* that has not quite ended. Read it. Become vertical.

**Listen for the frog.** Or what was once a frog. Its skin hosting a miniature ecosystem. Its mobile pond. Its slow, breathing rhythm. This is the fauna, rare and strange, adapted to the *conditions* rather than the place. This is what survival looks like when the world has become wet, when the world has become green, when the world has forgotten what dry means. The frog is a teacher. Learn from it. Become, in your own way, a pond that walks, that *breathes* with the slow rhythm of the place.

**Make your sound.** A voice, a struck metal, a flash of light. Say *I am here, I am still alive, I am still strange*. Do not become silent. Do not become *unmapped*. The place is loud despite its slowness, and the loudness is *structure*, is the way you know where you are, is the way you avoid vulnerability. Be loud. Be legible. Be *present*.

**Wait.** This is the final preparation, and the only one that matters. The Drowned Works is patient. It has been wet for a long time. It will be wet for a long time more. You are a brief dryness, a moment of gradient, a temporary warmth. The place will wait for you to become part of the vertical time, to become substrate, to become wet. Wait with it. Become, for a time, the slow pulse that is the only heartbeat this place has ever known.

### What to Do When You Leave

**Leave something.** A tool, a layer of clothing, a boot, a watch. Something warm, something that will become a core, a seed of green tissue, a small cathedral to your brief dryness. The plants grow fastest near warmth. Your discarded warmth will draw them. Your discarded warmth will be *incorporated*. This is the covenant. This is the exchange.

**Do not look back.** Or look back, but know what you are seeing. The green compression. The water rising, falling, *present* in the vertical dimension. The smokestack, brick-lined, a chimney for nothing, water running down its interior in sheets. The long building, roof collapsed inward, now a boat-shaped basin of accumulated leaf-litter and rain, a nursery for the next generation of incorporators. The place does not want you to leave. It does not want you to stay. It is *patient*. It will wait.

**Carry the wet.** Do not dry yourself. Do not return to the ordinary world and immediately become *dry*. Carry the humidity on your skin, in your lungs, in your clothing. Let the world outside feel your difference, your *wetness*, your slow rhythm. Let them perceive you as you perceived the place—as an intrusion, as a gradient, as a change that will, in time, be absorbed.

**Listen for the pulse.** In ordinary rain. In ordinary steam. In the subsonic thrum of a refrigerator, a transformer, a city's hidden infrastructure. The Drowned Works is not *here*, not only here. It is a *condition*, a way of being wet, a way of being slow, a way of being *perceived* by a world that has learned to attend. You carry it now. You are its ambassador, its brief dryness, its moment of gradient. Do not forget.

**Prepare to return.** Not to the Drowned Works specifically—though you might, if the water finds you again, if the green calls you back—but to the *state* of being that the Works taught you. The wet. The slow. The perceived. The strange. The world outside is dry, and fast, and anonymous. But it is also, in its way, *receptive*. It is also, in its way, a place that could become wet, could become slow, could become *strange*. You know how. You have been taught.

The Drowned Works does not end at its boundary. It ends when you forget the pulse, the drip, the hiss. Do not forget. Carry the breath. Carry the water. Carry the slow, patient, *strange* incorporation of everything that enters, everything that lingers, everything that dares to be *dry* in a world that has forgotten what dry means.
