# A Traveler's Preparation for the Lithworks of Unfinished Heights

## I. Before Arrival: The Preparation of Self

### Unlearning Completion

You cannot arrive here prepared in any conventional sense, because conventional preparation assumes a *goal*—a destination reached, a task accomplished, a state achieved. The Lithworks rejects this grammar entirely.

**Practice of interruption.** Begin by training yourself to leave things unfinished. Not carelessly—*deliberately*. A sentence abandoned mid-thought. A meal prepared but not eaten. A project started with genuine commitment and then *paused*, as if interrupted by a thought you could not finish. Do this until the discomfort becomes familiar, until you can feel the *pull* toward completion and learn to *ride* it without yielding. The landscape will notice when you bring this practice, and it will recognize you as *material* rather than *tourist*.

**Study of negotiation.** Read nothing about "survival" or "adaptation" in extreme environments. Those frameworks assume the environment is *fixed*, that you must *overcome* it. Here, you must learn to *become* the negotiation. Practice arguing with yourself about what you want. Let the argument remain unresolved. Notice how the unresolved state generates its own kind of energy, its own *motion* without destination.

### The Body Before the Body

The Lithworks does not care about your body as you understand it. Your body is *geology in motion*, a temporary arrangement of water and mineral and intention that the place will incorporate, revise, perhaps *complete* in ways you cannot anticipate.

**Condensation training.** Before you arrive, practice living in damp. Sleep with a thin film of moisture on your skin. Breathe air so humid it tastes of metal. Let your clothes never fully dry. The goal is not comfort with dampness but *identity* with it—becoming, in some small measure, one of the condensation-drinkers, a creature of surfaces rather than sources.

**Vertical reorientation.** Spend time in places where the distinction between wall and floor becomes practical rather than theoretical. Climb. Hang. Crawl. Learn to think in *slopes*, in *angles of ascent* that have no names in the language of flat ground. The Lithworks is built in all directions at once, and your body must learn to *read* rather than *traverse*.

**Slowness as discipline.** Practice moving at half your normal speed. Then at a quarter. Then *stopping*, entirely, for periods that feel unbearable. The landscape operates at 1.2—slow dynamic—but this speed is *geological*, not merely sluggish. You must learn to feel time as *transformation* rather than *duration*, as the slow becoming of stone rather than the ticking of hours.

### The Mind Before the Mind

Your perception is *material* here. The Lithworks uses it. Your attention, your expectation, your *uncertainty*—these are substances that shape the reality you encounter.

**Duplicate recognition.** Practice noticing when you have seen something before. Then practice *doubting* the memory. Then practice *accepting* the doubt without resolving it. The duplicate structures will test this skill mercilessly. You will find the same stairway twice, down to the rust patterns, and the second encounter will reveal what the first was *missing*. The question "which is original?" has no meaning. You must learn to *inhabit* the question.

**Temporal pooling.** Meditate on the simultaneity of before and after. Hold in mind, without resolving, the moment of your own arrival and your own departure. Feel the weight of duration made *present*, vertiginous. The sump pools will show you other times—the excavation, the rain, the evaporation—and you must learn to *drink* this temporal water without drowning in it.

**Gravity negotiation.** This cannot be fully prepared for, but you can *soften* your relationship with "down." Practice walking on surfaces that *feel* level but are not. Practice trusting your feet when they seem to lie. Practice the *sensation* of water flowing uphill, of stone rolling sideways, and learn not to *correct* these sensations but to *accompany* them.

## II. The Journey: Arrival by Decision

You do not travel *to* the Lithworks. You *decide* to be there, and the decision *becomes* the path. The paths are still learning what they are. This means:

**There is no map.** Any map you carry will become *wrong* the moment you use it. The landscape revises itself. Paths that existed when you checked them may have *completed* themselves into something else—a tunnel where there was open scree, a cliff where there was a terrace.

**You will get lost.** This is not failure but *method*. The getting-lost is the journey. When you find yourself on a surface that shifts between scree and soil and strata, do not panic. The stone is *young*, psychologically surprised by its own existence. It crumbles at touch in some places, rings like a bell in others. Your footsteps will leave traces that *persist* longer than geology should allow. You are already being incorporated.

**The 0.6 light will adjust you.** The dimness is not from cloud but from *distance itself*, from so much air between you and any source that illumination arrives already tired. The color is the color of old bruises healing—phosphorescent, cool, *preserved*. Your eyes will adjust constantly. You will learn to see in gradients, in the *absence* of light as much as its presence, to read the landscape through shadows that have their own textures, their own depths.

## III. Immediate Adaptations: The First Hours

### Shelter Without Completion

You will need shelter, but shelter here is *negotiation*, not refuge.

**Find the unfinished.** Completed structures are *dangerous*—they complete themselves further when you are not watching, adding windows that face into solid rock, crowns made of materials that do not exist elsewhere. The abandoned, the paused, the *intended*—these are safe. A skeletal steel framework. A concrete pour that never received its second lift. A tower that terminates in an unfinished crown where the construction simply *stopped*, as if the builders looked up, saw the sky, and forgot what they were doing.

**Become the condensation.** Do not fight the damp. The industrial structures are never dry; they are *sweating*, their iron bones wrapped in films of collected dark water. Allow your own skin to *join* this condition. After hours, the wetness becomes a *second skin*, a membrane between you and the world that *filters* sensation, making everything slightly *distant*, slightly *dreamed*. This is not discomfort. This is *adaptation*.

**Sleep in the almost.** The machines here are *paused*, not broken—*waiting*. A conveyor belt turns one revolution every seventeen minutes. A pump lifts water and releases it into a pipe that leads nowhere you can trace. Sleep in this rhythm. Let your own breathing *approach* the tempo of the landscape, neither matching nor resisting.

### Water as Time

**Do not seek sources.** There are no rivers here, not properly. Water exists in *negotiation* with the landscape: condensation architecture, pocket hydrology, intermittent violence. To find water, you must *stop looking*. Let the moisture find you. Taste the wet on your lips. The mineral scent of wet rock will become a *memory of the ocean* that once covered this elevation.

**Drink carefully.** To drink from a sump pool is to consume a moment that has not yet fully arrived. The pools are *permanent temporary*—always present, never the same water. In certain lights, they show *other times*: the excavation that created them, the rain that filled them, the evaporation that will empty them, all *simultaneous*. The color is wrong—too dark, too still, reflecting not the sky but something *under* the sky. Drink, but drink *knowingly*. You are consuming duration.

**Follow the drip-lines.** Where water collects—everywhere water collects—ferns grow in *profusion*, their fronds uncurling in slow motion. These are *ancient species* in a young landscape, refugees from lower elevations. Their presence marks *warmth*, the thermal vents and condensation drip-lines that make survival possible. Follow them not as a path but as a *clock*, marking not hours but *transformations*.

## IV. Long-Term Practices: Becoming Material

### The Ecology of Interruption

The fauna here is *abundant in kinds, sparse in presence*. You are never alone but rarely see the same thing twice. This resolves in a rule of *emergence*:

**Stop looking for life.** The more you try to see, the less you will find. The condensation-drinkers—insects with bodies like *living dew*, transparent wings that collect moisture—are *everywhere in potential*, visible only when they choose to be. Suddenly, a surface *moves*, the dew itself taking wing.

**Allow yourself to be seen.** The more you *allow* yourself to be seen by, the more the place will *populate*. The echo mimics—birds that reproduce the sounds of the city, the hum of transformers, the *silence* between pulses—are *learning* the place, incorporating its noises into their own communication. You are a *new sound*. Let them learn you.

**Feel the almost-visible.** Most abundant of all: creatures that exist at the edge of perception. You feel them in the *quality* of the air, in the way shadows seem *thicker* in certain directions, in the sudden absence of the condensation-drinkers when something passes through. They are *real*—you find their traces, their shed skins, their *dens*—but seeing them requires *surrender*, the willingness to stop looking directly and let the landscape fill in its own inhabitants.

### The Industrial-Organic Continuum

**Learn to read rust as life.** The rust on the steel is *alive* in the way that the lichen is alive—both are *patinas of persistence*, both are the landscape learning to *wear* human intention without quite *adopting* it. There is no distinction, finally, between the machine and the moss, the factory and the fern. Both are *growths* on the raw geology, both are *negotiations* with the extreme elevation, the uncertain water, the dim light.

**Listen to the machines dreaming.** The smokestacks emit not smoke but *condensation*, white plumes that rise only to dissolve into the general damp. The transformers hum at frequencies that make your teeth ache. The lights flicker in patterns that might be code or might be *breathing*. The industry here does not produce; it *performs production*, a ritual of motion maintained because the stopping would be worse than the continuing. Learn to *hear* this performance. Learn to *distinguish*—or perhaps to *refuse* the distinction—between the organic and the mechanical.

**Walk the angles.** At certain elevations, on certain slopes, the *direction* of down becomes negotiable. Water flows uphill in thin streams. Loose stone rolls *sideways*, then *up*, then settles in configurations that *balance* without support. The inhabitants—human or otherwise—have learned to *walk the angles*, to move through the landscape as if it were a text to be read rather than a surface to be traversed. You must learn this too. Your body will *teach* you, through wrongness, through the *sensation* of error that is also *adjustment*.

## V. The Deep Adaptation: Becoming Unfinished

### You Are Being Built

The city does not exist *for* you. It exists *through* you. This is the final rule, the one that resolves all others:

**Your passage leaves traces.** Condensation on your skin that *stays*. Footprints in the scree that *persist*. Thoughts that *echo* in the architecture. You are not a visitor. You are **material**, being incorporated into the unfinished project, your presence *needed* for the completions that happen when you are not watching.

**Do not resist the completion.** Structures that were abandoned will *complete themselves* in your presence. A wall gains a window. A tower receives its crown. The completion is always *wrong*—the window faces into solid rock, the crown is made of a material that does not exist elsewhere, a glass that *absorbs* light rather than transmitting it. But the completion is *real*, as real as anything here, and the builders—if there were builders—acknowledge it with a silence that is almost *recognition*.

**Become the negotiation.** You are inside the Lithworks now. It is still deciding what you are. The raw geology and the industrial ambition and the abundant, hidden life and your own *becoming* are all part of the same slow, patient, *unfinished* process.

### The Final Preparation

There is no final preparation. There is only *continuing*.

**Practice the slowness.** The landscape's dynamic is 1.2—slow, but *inexorable*. You must match this, not in speed but in *quality*. Your own heartbeat will seem *rushed*. Your thoughts *impatient*. Learn to *wait* for the landscape to *notice* you, to *offer* itself, to *complete* itself in your presence.

**Practice the dimness.** The 0.6 light will become, after time, a *condition* rather than a limitation. You will learn to see in gradients, in the *absence* of light as much as its presence. Every light source—a window, a flickering lamp, the phosphorescence of lichen—will become an *event*, a *revelation*.

**Practice the loudness.** The soundscape is *full*, saturated with meaning. The industrial drone, the geological song, the biological chorus, the echo mimics adding sounds that have no source—all of this will become the *structure* of your hearing, the frame within which all other sound must be *placed*. And when it *stops*—if it stops—you will miss it. You will realize that the sound has become the *silence* you can no longer hear without.

**Practice the weirdness.** Somewhere—perhaps in the next valley, perhaps in the next moment—the rules will *shift*. You will find yourself in a landscape that *remembers* being different, that *mourns* or *celebrates* its own becoming. This is not a place you visit. This is a place that *visits you*, slowly, persistently, until you are no longer certain where the landscape ends and you begin, where the unfinished city *dreams* you and you *dream* it.

## VI. What to Carry (And What to Leave)

### Carry:

- **A willingness to be interrupted.** Not as philosophy but as *practice*. The ability to stop mid-thought, mid-action, mid-sentence, and *remain* in the stopping.

- **No map.** Any map will lie. The paths are still learning what they are. Trust the *decision* that brought you, not the *route* it suggested.

- **Patience for the almost.** The machines are *paused*, not broken. The structures are *intended*, not abandoned. The city is *becoming*, not failed. You must learn to *inhabit* these states without resolving them.

- **Openness to being seen.** The fauna will find you if you stop looking. The landscape will *offer* itself if you *allow* the offering. The city will *complete* itself through you if you *provide* the material.

- **A second skin.** Not literal—though the damp will make it feel so. The *habit* of wetness, of never being dry, of *filtering* sensation through a membrane that is also *yourself*.

### Leave:

- **The need to finish.** Any completion you achieve will be *revised* by the landscape. The finished becomes *strange*, subject to completions that happen when you are not watching. Remain *unfinished*. It is *safe*.

- **The distinction between organic and mechanical.** Both are *growths*. Both are *negotiations*. Both are *patinas of persistence*. The distinction will *fail* you here.

- **The expectation of horizon.** There is no horizon. The sky presses close, a bruised dome of perpetual twilight. The light is already tired. You must learn to *see* without distance, to *read* the landscape through shadows that have their own depths.

- **The self as separate.** You are *material*. You are being *built*. The city does not exist *for* you; it exists *through* you. Your passage leaves traces. Your presence is *needed*. You are inside it now. It is still deciding what you are.

## VII. The Arrival That Never Completes

You arrive not by road but by *decision*. The paths are still learning what they are. You will walk on surfaces that shift between scree and soil and strata, on ground that is *raw geology in mid-sentence*, erosion at 0.5 meaning the mountains are still *arguing* with themselves about what they want to be.

The stone is *young*—not geologically, but *psychologically*, still surprised by its own existence. It crumbles at touch in some places, rings like a bell struck too hard in others. There are no fossils because there has not been time for death to leave its signature properly.

You will find the city at 2.5—organized construction without completion. Buildings that rise from the mountainside not *on* it but *from* it, their foundations merged with bedrock in ways that suggest growth rather than construction. Streets that curve without destination. Plazas that open into cliffs. Towers that terminate in unfinished crowns where the construction simply *stopped*, as if the builders looked up, saw the sky, and forgot what they were doing.

You will encounter the ecology of interruption—lichen cathedrals, ferns in the cracks, trees that remember being smaller, and fauna abundant in kinds but sparse in presence. You will learn to *stop looking* and *allow yourself to be seen*.

You will experience the reality that frays—duplicate structures, temporal pooling, the unfinished becoming finished, gravity negotiation. The weirdness at 2.6 will become, slowly, *familiar*, then *necessary*, then *indistinguishable from yourself*.

You will understand, finally, the rules:

1. **Completion is dangerous.** The unfinished is safe.
2. **Water is time.** To follow water is to move through duration.
3. **The vertical is horizontal.** Navigation requires thinking in slopes.
4. **Abundance hides in sparse.** You will not find life by looking for it.
5. **The industrial is the organic.** There is no distinction, finally.
6. **You are being built.** The city exists *through* you.

You will feel, underneath all of this: **cool** in a way that *preserves*, **dim** in a way that makes you *lean in*, **loud** in a way that becomes *hum*, **slow** in a way that *changes* you. And the **weirdness**—the sense that you are walking through a place that is still being *imagined*, that your own perception is one of the materials being used, that the reality you experience is *negotiable*.

This is not a place you visit.

This is a place that *visits you*.
