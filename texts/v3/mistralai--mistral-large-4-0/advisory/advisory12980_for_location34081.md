This is a place that asks for *surrender of pace*, not of self. The description carries a particular quality I want to honor: this is not a dungeon, not a utopia, not a haunted house. It is a *settlement* in the deepest sense—something that has grown around and through the people who live in it, until the distinction between body and building has become negotiable. To prepare for staying here slowly, letting it reveal itself, you must prepare to become *legible* to a system that reads by touch, by sound, by the trace of moisture you leave on a surface.

## What to bring, and what to leave

**Leave behind anything that insists on being seen.** Mirrors, yes, obviously—but also polished metal, bright colors, synthetic fabrics that shed microplastics into the water systems. The hangings are woven from *biological* material: hair, scale, plant fiber harvested in darkness. Your clothing will be noticed. If you arrive in something that does not participate in this economy of shedding and being gathered, you will be *foreign* in a way that takes time to soften. Bring natural fibers if you can: untreated wool, linen, cotton that has been washed until soft. Even better, bring nothing you are unwilling to trade. The builders will want to know what you offer in the exchange of fibers.

**Bring water, but not much.** You will learn their cisterns. The water here is *managed*—collected, encouraged, made regular. Your own water, brought from outside, will taste different. Drink it first, while it is yours, then learn to read the damp. Which chambers weep at which hours. Which basins are safe and which carry something the builders avoid. The water moves in pulses. You will learn to feel the pulse in the floor before you hear it in the walls.

**Bring light, but dimmable, and expect to use it less.** The deepest chamber is harvested by feel. There is a plant fiber that grows only where no light reaches. The builders who gather it have developed a *tactile literacy*—they read the chamber by touch, by temperature gradient, by the particular resistance of air pressure in a space that has never known sun. You will not become this, not quickly. But you can practice: spend hours in darkness with your eyes open, useless, and learn what your skin knows. The damp on your face. The direction of air movement. The particular silence of a room full of hanging fabric versus a room empty of it.

**Bring something to record with, but not paper.** Paper curls in this humidity, grows things. The builders track time with knots and notches. Learn this. Bring cord, bring a knife you can use to cut notches in wood, bring charcoal if you must write—but know that the charcoal will be *read*. The walls are a palimpsest. Your marks will be added to something that does not forget.

**Leave your schedule.** This is the hardest preparation. The place operates on water-pulse and fabric-cycle and mineral-growth. The bellows are worked by hand in cycles determined by long observation. You will not be able to impose a rhythm. You can only *attend*—arrive at the bellows when they need working, arrive at the cisterns when the drips encourage, arrive at the hangings when they need rewoven. The calendar of knots is not a metaphor. It is *literally* how time is kept here, and you will be given a cord, and you will learn to cut your notches in it, and you will understand that your days will be measured by what you have touched, what you have changed, what has changed you in return.

## What to prepare in your body

**The skin must learn to be wet without being sweaty.** The temperature sits in that narrow band—alert to moisture, never quite crossing into perspiration. This is a *skill of the skin*, not just a tolerance. You will arrive with surface-world skin: too dry, too reactive, too *surprised* by humidity. The builders' skin has adapted. Yours will begin to. The damp will find the places where you hold tension—jaw, shoulders, the small of your back—and settle there. You must learn to release those tensions *before* the damp arrives, or the damp will teach you, painfully, through cramping, through the particular ache of moisture held in muscle.

**The ears must learn to *not* localize.** Sound here is strange. Voices arrive from wrong directions, transmitted through water-filled tubes. You will hear someone speaking from behind you who is three chambers away. Your instinct will be to turn, to locate, to *verify*. Resist this. The sound is true, but the direction is a *translation* the space performs, not a deception. Learn to hear the content without the geography. Learn to recognize voices by their *resonance* in the hangings, not by their position in space. This takes longer than you expect. You will be startled, repeatedly, by sounds that seem to come from inside your own body—the particular frequency of water dripping into a basin that has been tuned, over generations, to produce a pitch that matches the human chest cavity.

**The feet must learn a new floor.** The mineral accretion has been smoothed, then smoothed again, until it holds the memory of countless hands and feet. But it is not *even*. It undulates. It has been smoothed in the direction of water flow, of air current, of the particular traffic patterns of bodies moving through the Lung. You will arrive with surface-world feet: arches that expect flatness, ankles that expect predictability. Here, every step is a *negotiation*. The floor tells you where to place your weight by its temperature, by its slight give, by the sound it makes when you step—hollow here, solid there, resonant in the places where water moves beneath. You will fall. You will bruise. Your feet will adapt, slowly, developing a *reading* of this surface that no conscious instruction could teach.

**The breath must learn to be *heard*.** You are listened to here. The hangings adjust to your presence. The creatures turn their pale faces toward you. Your breath moves through fabric and mineral and bone and something else, and it is *noticed*. This is not paranoia. It is the actual condition of the place. You will become aware of your own respiration in a way that is uncomfortable at first—every inhale, every exhale, registered by the space, contributing to the small noises that make up the loudness of the place. Learn to breathe *slowly*. Learn to breathe *deliberately*. Your breath is a contribution to the soundscape, and the soundscape is *alive*, responsive, in a way that no surface-world acoustic environment is.

## What to prepare in your mind

**Abandon the category of "haunted."** The weirdness here is not supernatural. It is *unresolved*. The sealed chambers. The hangings that move against the air current. The sound in the deepest wet season that arrives from below. These are not ghosts. They are *incompletions*—places where the system of management, of accommodation, of slow negotiation with the mineral and the water and the biological, has not yet found a resolution. The predator in the deepest chamber is not a monster. It is *acknowledged*, in the way one acknowledges a season, a tide, a wound that will not close. You must learn to hold these without demanding explanation. The builders do not explain. They *accommodate*. They feed the predator. They seal the chambers and do not speak of them. They open the barriers rarely, for ritual, for punishment, for death, and find what is inside *changed*, though no one can say precisely how.

**Prepare for *incorporation*.** This is the deepest preparation, and it cannot be rushed. You are being *incorporated*, gently, into a system that was complete before you arrived. The hangings will adjust to your presence. Your hair, if you shed it, will be gathered. Your voice will become one of the resonances. Your body, if you stay long enough, will find its own hollow in the wall. This is not a threat. It is the *offer* of the place. The name, untranslatable, something like *the-hearing* or *the-being-listened-to*—it is accurate. You are listened to here, by everything, constantly, and the listening *changes* you. You must decide, before you descend through the chimney of warm air, whether you are prepared to be changed in this way. Whether you want to become *legible* to a system that reads by touch, by sound, by the trace of moisture you leave on a surface.

**Learn to *wait* in the way the place waits.** The creatures gather in the sealed spaces and press against the barriers, not in distress but in a kind of *waiting*. This is the model. You will find yourself in chambers where nothing happens, where the dripping continues, where the hangings move with air currents you cannot feel, where the creatures turn their pale faces toward you and *wait*. You must learn to wait with them. Not anxiously, not with the surface-world urgency of "what happens next." The place reveals itself slowly. To those who can wait. To those who have learned that *attention* is a form of time, and that time here is measured by what you have touched, what you have changed, what has changed you in return.

## The descent itself

You descend through a chimney of warm air. The temperature sits in that narrow band where sweat does not yet form but the skin knows it could. The body remains *alert* to moisture. This is the threshold. Above, the world has its rhythms: sun, rain, the predictable cycle of surface weather. Below, the water moves in pulses. You hear it before you see it: a distant percussion, drop after drop finding basin after basin, each impact slightly different in pitch, building a rhythm that is almost but not quite pattern.

The descent is not deep, but it is *under*. The hollowed volume in something older than stone—the calcified root-mass of a forest that died ten thousand years ago and became something like limestone, then something like bone, then something that *remembers* being wood. You will feel this in the walls. The walls are not carved. They are *grown*, or grown-over. Stalactites trained, coaxed, grafted into columns. Stalagmites hollowed out and lined with packed clay. The floor undulating with mineral accretion that has been smoothed, then smoothed again, until it holds the memory of countless hands and feet.

When you reach the floor of the first chamber, you will be *listened to*. The hangings will adjust. The creatures will turn their pale faces toward you. The water, somewhere, will find a slightly different path.

This is the beginning of your stay. Not the arrival. The *beginning*. The place will reveal itself slowly, to those who can wait, who can attend, who can become *legible* to a system that reads by touch, by sound, by the trace of moisture you leave on a surface.

You will not stay here long, perhaps. The damp will find you, eventually, will settle into your bones in a way that the surface world can never quite remove. But you will remember the sound—the particular quality of loudness that comes from so many small living things, so much movement in a confined space, so much *attention* being paid to every surface, every drop, every breath of air moving through fabric and mineral and bone and something else, something that grows in the dark and is harvested by feel and woven into the walls that breathe.

Or you will stay. And become one of the hangings yourself, in time. Your hair among the fibers. Your voice among the resonances. Your body finding its own hollow in the wall.

The place has a name, in the language of the builders, but it does not translate. It is something like *the-hearing* or *the-being-listened-to*. It is accurate.
