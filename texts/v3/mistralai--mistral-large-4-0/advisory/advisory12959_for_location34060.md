# Preparation Guide: The Hum of Throat-Canyons

*For the ordinary human traveler, the multiverse wayfarer, the one who has seen seventeen other realities and believes herself ready for anything*

---

## Before You Go: The Honest Assessment

You are not ready. No ordinary human is ready for this place. But readiness is not the point. *Survival* is the point, and survival here is possible, with preparation. The difference between a traveler who leaves changed and a traveler who does not leave is preparation measured in specifics, not general resilience.

This world does not want to kill you. That is the first thing to understand. The Hum of Throat-Canyons is *indifferent* in the way that deep water is indifferent, in the way that growing things are indifferent—not hostile, but *unconcerned* with your persistence. The dangers are environmental, physiological, psychological, temporal. They are not personal. Do not take them personally; this will help you survive them.

---

## The Body: Hardware Modifications and Adaptations

### Hearing Protection: Non-Negotiable

Your ears will be attacked. Not damaged—*expanded*. The hum will colonize your auditory processing, add frequencies you cannot locate, sources you cannot identify. The locals wear small resonant dampeners; you should obtain equivalent technology *before* arrival, not after.

But understand: the dampeners are not perfect. They filter specific harmful bands while leaving others. You will still hear too much. Your preparation should include:

**Mental training for auditory overload.** Practice sleeping through industrial noise. Practice meditation while white noise plays. Practice *not reacting* to sounds that seem to originate inside your own skull. The hum will do this to you. The question is whether you can maintain cognitive function while it happens.

**Ear protection hierarchy.** Start with dampeners. Carry backup dampeners—the environment degrades technology, and the fungal networks are known to *grow into* devices, to incorporate them, to make them part of the listening web. Have physical earplugs as final resort. Learn to sleep with your head positioned to minimize resonance—certain angles in the throat-canyons create standing waves that concentrate specific frequencies.

**Accept the addition.** Some travelers report that after initial exposure, they *miss* the hum when it is absent. They develop phantom hearing, a constant internal chord that never resolves. This is not damage exactly; it is *adaptation*. But it is adaptation you may not want. The dampeners, used consistently, may prevent this. Or may only delay it. No one knows the long-term effects of repeated visits.

### Respiratory Preparation

The air is saturated. Not with water vapor exactly—with *potential* for water, with particulate moisture that behaves strangely, that clings, that penetrates. Your lungs will work harder. Prepare by:

**Cardiovascular conditioning.** Not for strength—for *endurance*. The humidity increases the work of breathing by measurable percentages. You will feel it immediately, and it will not improve significantly with acclimation. Your body can adapt partially, but the adaptation is incomplete.

**Mask discipline.** The mineral dust, the copper-tasting particulate that accompanies the moisture-memory, is not immediately toxic but accumulates. The locals wear filtration masks in the condensation-heavy hours. You should too. Carry spares; the membranous vegetation will try to grow through them, will succeed given time.

**Learn to breathe *with* the canyon.** The wind moves constantly through the throat-constrictions. There are rhythms to it, pressure differentials you can feel in your ears, that you can *anticipate*. Breathing in synchronization with the canyon's inhalation reduces respiratory effort. It also induces a trance state. Be aware of this. Do not let the trance deepen beyond your control.

### The Skin and the Damp

Your skin will be wet constantly. Not with water—the damp leaves *memory* of wetness, a sensation without the substance. This is disorienting. Your body expects evaporative cooling that never comes, expects the relief of drying that never arrives.

**Clothing strategy.** Wear materials that manage moisture without retaining it. The locals have developed fabrics—part grown, part engineered—that wick the condensation-memory away, that resist the fungal spores that float in every breath. Acquire these locally if possible; import equivalents if not. Your ordinary synthetic fabrics will become heavy, will sag, will smell of the place in ways that persist after washing.

**Skin care.** The mineral dust is abrasive at microscopic scales. The humidity encourages every minor irritation to become infection. Carry broad-spectrum antimicrobial preparations. Use them prophylactically. The fungal networks are *everywhere*, waiting for any breach in your defenses to colonize.

**Accept the numbness.** The copper-tasting particulate causes temporary lingual anesthesia. This is useful for eating the local food—some of it is *aggressively* textured—but disorienting. You will forget how things taste. You will crave familiar flavors that no longer register properly. Bring concentrated flavor experiences. Spices that overwhelm. Textures that contrast. Your ordinary diet will become unbearably bland.

### The Eyes and the Wrong Light

The luminous sky provides illumination that is *topologically incorrect*. Light arrives from diffuse sources that should be point sources. Shadows fall in directions that contradict your other senses. The blue-white glow persists through night, dimming but never extinguishing.

**Sleep preparation.** You will need darkness. Total darkness. The settlement's structures are built dim deliberately; even so, you will need personal light-blocking. Sleep masks are insufficient—the light penetrates. You need enclosed darkness, a small space with no windows, or windows fitted with the spectral filters the locals use.

**Circadian disruption.** Your body's internal clock will struggle. The light never fully fades; the temperature cycles are wrong; the sound never ceases. Some travelers develop polyphasic sleep patterns, napping in the warm hours, working in the cooler. Others sleep in long consolidated blocks, ignoring the environmental cues entirely. Experiment before you arrive; find what works for you. The wrong choice here leads to cognitive degradation that compounds with the auditory stress.

**Visual adaptation.** The dim light, the diffuse sources, the absence of stars—your eyes will adjust, but slowly. Color perception will shift. You will see the pale jade and viridian of the membranous vegetation as *more* saturated than they are, will perceive details in shadows that should be invisible. This is not purely negative. It is *different*. Prepare for the disorientation of trusting your eyes in a place where they have been recalibrated.

## The Mind: Psychological Fortification

### The Hum as Presence

The hum is not background noise. It is *foreground*. It will occupy your attention whether you grant it permission or not. The question is how you relate to it.

**Meditation practice.** Specifically, practices that develop *attentional flexibility*—the ability to focus on one thing while humming continues, to let the hum recede without forcing it, to return to it deliberately. The hum will try to become your *only* focus, your only thought. Resist this without resisting the hum itself.

**Meaning-making.** The hum has structure. Intervals. Pulses. If you listen long enough—if you survive the listening—you may perceive patterns, maybe even *messages*. The locals believe the canyon *communicates* through the hum, that its requests are literal, not metaphorical. You need not believe this. But you should be prepared for the *temptation* to believe it. The hum is designed—or has evolved—to induce pattern-seeking, to reward attention with apparent significance.

**Madness as spectrum.** The "mad ones" are not a separate category. They are travelers who stayed too long, who listened too deeply, who found the intervals between hum-pulses and *entered* them. Some return changed. Some return *speaking*; their voices carry frequencies that damage unprotected hearing. Some do not return. Prepare for the possibility that you will be tempted to follow them. Prepare a *reason* to return that is stronger than curiosity.

### Time Dilation and Temporal Disorientation

The settlement exists in multiple speeds. The deep canyons, where the fungal silence holds, experience time differently. The Convergence is a node where speeds touch.

**Do not go to the Convergence unprepared.** Not on your first visit. Not without a guide who has been and returned. The Convergence offers *simultaneity*—the perception of the settlement as all its moments at once. The human nervous system cannot sustain this. Those who attempt it unprepared experience what the locals call *flattening*—the loss of temporal depth, the inability to distinguish past from present from future, a condition that may be permanent.

**Time-tracking discipline.** Carry multiple timekeeping devices. Mechanical, if possible—electronics are uncertain here. Compare them constantly. The local time, your body's time, the time of your origin—these will diverge. Know which you are tracking. Know which you need to track.

**The deep canyons: know your limits.** The fungal silence is real. Time moves differently there. Hours may correspond to days, or days to hours. The direction of the correspondence depends on the *hunger* of what waits below—a variable you cannot perceive directly. If you go deep, go with a guide. Go with a tether—physical or technological, some connection to the settlement's time. Do not let the silence become your only reference.

### The Negotiation

The settlement negotiates with the canyon. With the deep. With the beyond-modern technology that mediates between human and geological agency.

You are not part of this negotiation. You are a *visitor*. But you will be *noticed*. The canyon requests things; the settlement provides; you will be caught in the currents of this exchange. Some requests will involve you. Sound, specifically—the canyon sometimes wants *voices*, human voices, raised in specific frequencies. Growth—sometimes wants particular species cultivated, which may include species you carry, unknowingly, on your clothing, in your gut, in the crevices of your luggage.

**Offer nothing.** Unless you understand what you are offering. The locals have learned this through generations of error. You will not have that luxury. When the canyon seems to *ask* for something—a sound, a presence, a specimen—decline. Politely, firmly, without engagement. The canyon does not hold grudges. But it *remembers*. And its memory is the fungal network, which spans distances no human survey has mapped.

## Equipment and Supplies

### The Dampeners: Technical Specifications

Obtain resonant dampeners before arrival. Specifications:

- **Frequency range:** 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, with specific attenuation in the 40-80 Hz band (the canyon's fundamental) and the 200-400 Hz band (the "echo-blood" return frequencies).
- **Power:** Self-contained, minimum 200 hours continuous operation. The settlement has power—the luminous sky provides, somehow—but your devices should not depend on local infrastructure.
- **Redundancy:** Carry two pairs. The fungal networks will attempt to colonize them. Clean them obsessively. Replace them when attenuation degrades.

Practice using them before arrival. Learn to communicate with them in. Learn to sleep with them. Learn to *forget* them, to let their filtering become transparent, while maintaining awareness that protection is active.

### The Lifts and Vertical Transport

The settlement uses field-based lifts—vertical transport through tubes of pale light. These are *safe*, in the way that any technology in this place is safe, which is to say: functional, mostly reliable, occasionally *strange*.

**Learn the lift protocols.** The locals have developed procedures—when to enter, how to position yourself, what to do if the light *changes*. Follow these exactly. The lifts are part of the beyond-modern technology, which does not serve human purposes exactly. It has learned to *appear* to serve. Sometimes the appearance slips.

**Carry vertical alternatives.** Ropes, climbing equipment, the ability to descend on foot if the lifts fail. The bridges span the throats where the wind is strongest; they are engineered for trajectories that would be impossible elsewhere. They are *mostly* reliable. "Mostly" is not "always."

### Food and Water

The local food is *grown*, accelerated, accelerated again. It is nutritious, mostly safe, sometimes *too* vital—rapid growth means rapid accumulation of compounds your body does not expect. The *grazers* produce protein-rich secretions; the *filterers* concentrate minerals. Both are harvested, processed, served.

**Water:** Do not drink what condenses. Do not drink what collects in the *throats*. The real water moves underground, and what reaches the surface is *negotiated*—filtered through fungal networks, processed by the *filterers*, made potable by processes that are not fully understood. Drink only what the settlement provides, processed through their systems. The damp is not water. The condensation is not water. The memory of wetness is not relief from thirst.

**Bring supplements.** Your body will be stressed. Nutritionally, environmentally, psychologically. Carry concentrated nutrition—pills, pastes, anything that delivers calories and micronutrients without requiring local food. You may need it. You may need to *function* when the local food makes you ill, when the rapid-growth compounds disrupt your digestion, when you need energy for escape.

### The Specimens: What to Bring Back

You may want to bring samples. The place is *strange*, and strangeness is valuable in the multiverse. But:

**Do not bring living samples.** Not without explicit permission from settlement authorities, which you will not receive as a visitor. The fauna are *not native*. They were introduced, accelerated, *cultivated* by processes the current inhabitants do not fully control. You do not know what you are carrying. The fungal networks are *everywhere*, connecting ridge to ridge, exchanging information in ways that suggest *intention*. A sample you collect may be *part* of that network. Bringing it elsewhere may bring the network with it.

**Do not bring the mad ones' specimens.** Not even frozen, not even preserved in resin. These are *offerings*, accepted by the Convergence, *listened to*, then *released* to the deep. They are not souvenirs. They are *communications*. Bringing them elsewhere disrupts a process you do not understand.

**Do bring recordings.** Sound recordings, specifically, of the hum—with dampeners *disengaged*, recorded briefly, dangerously, with full awareness of the risk. The hum is the place's signature. The intervals between its pulses may contain information no other sense can access. But record briefly. Protect your hearing. Do not listen back without dampeners.

## Social Preparation: The Settlement

### The Locals

They are adapted. Not changed, not *other*—adapted. Their circadian rhythms are strange. Their hearing is filtered. Their relationship to time is *negotiated*, flexible, occasionally fractured.

**Do not imitate them immediately.** Their adaptations took generations, or took rapid technological intervention, or took *something else*—the stories disagree. You can visit their spaces, learn their customs, participate in their economy. You cannot become them in a single visit, or in ten, or in a hundred.

**Respect the silence.** The settlement empties itself when the canyon requests *silence*, absolute and maintained. The people go into the deeper ridges, where the hum cannot reach, where the fungal networks grow thick. If you are present when this happens—do not follow. You are not prepared for the deep silence. You are not prepared for the time dilation. Let them go. Wait in the settlement, or in your prepared shelter, with your dampeners *on*, filtering the residual hum that still penetrates.

**Learn the songs.** The settlement's oral tradition preserves *songs*—not entertainment, but *information*, the history of the negotiation with the canyon, the terms that have been offered and accepted and renegotiated. Listen to them. Do not sing along; your voice, untrained, may produce frequencies that disrupt the negotiation. But listen. The songs contain warnings, encoded in melody, that no written record preserves.

### The Beyond-Modern Technology

It is not human. It arrived, or was grown, or was summoned—the stories disagree, and the disagreement is *significant*, suggesting that the origin is not fully known even to those who interact with it daily.

It maintains the luminous sky. It manages the moisture cycles. It *listens* to the deep, translates its requests. It *mediates*—and it is not neutral.

**Do not trust its appearances.** The machines that descend from the luminous sky, that configure themselves for each task, that sometimes remain in attitudes of apparent contemplation—they are not *serving* you. They are not *observing* you, exactly. They are *processing*, in ways that include you as input but do not center you as purpose.

**Do not seek its attention.** The mad ones sought it, went deep seeking the source of the hum, seeking the *waiting* something below. What they found is not recorded. Do not repeat their experiment.

**Do use its services.** The lifts, the light, the moisture management—these are *offerings*, extended to the settlement, extended to visitors. Accept them gratefully, use them carefully, do not ask *how* they work. The technology has learned to *appear* to serve human purposes. Sometimes this appearance is accurate. Sometimes it is not. The distinction may not be available to you.

## The Deep: What Waits Below

You will hear about it. The warm, the pressurized, the *waiting*. The echo-blood. The channels that descend toward something that has *rules*, that makes *requests*, that is *patient*.

You should not go there. Not on your first visit. Not on your tenth. The settlement has learned to *read* the deep, to detect its shifting requests, to negotiate terms for coexistence. You have no such learning. You would be *input*, not *agent*. You would be *consumed*, not *consulted*.

But you will be *tempted*. The hum, the silence, the intervals between pulses—these will suggest *knowledge*, *understanding*, *union*. The deep is patient. It has waited through failed canyons, other configurations, other *attempts*. It can wait for you. The question is whether you can wait for yourself, can resist the *becoming* that the deep offers, the transformation into something that serves its purposes, whatever those purposes are.

**Prepare a reason to return.** Not a reason to stay. A reason to *leave*. Write it down. Carry it with you. When the hum seems to whisper that you could stay forever, that the waiting is *love*, that the negotiation is *becoming*—read your reason. Return to it. Let it anchor you.

## The Convergence: Final Warning

The Convergence is a *wound*. A place where the crust is thinnest, where the pale material beneath glows most visibly, where the air itself seems to *shiver* with potential.

People go there to *listen*. To hear what the canyon is saying. To offer what the canyon requests.

You may go. With a guide. With preparation. With dampeners, with time-tracking, with a reason to return that is stronger than curiosity.

But understand: the Convergence is a *node*. Where the different speeds of time touch. Where it is possible—briefly, dangerously—to perceive the settlement as it is *simultaneously*: all its moments, its growths and collapses, its negotiations and its failures, existing in a single *now*.

The human nervous system cannot sustain this. Those who attempt it unprepared experience *flattening*. The loss of temporal depth. A condition that may be permanent.

If you go, go *shallow*. Do not stay long. Do not *perceive*. Let the guide perceive; let the guide describe; let your own experience remain *sequential*, limited, *safe*.

## After: Return and Integration

If you return—when you return—you will be changed. The hum will be in you, in your bones, in the electrical murmur of your neurons. The wrong light will have recalibrated your vision. The negotiable time will have made ordinary time feel *rigid*, *brittle*, *insufficient*.

**Do not seek to remove these changes.** The dampeners, used consistently, may prevent the worst. They will not prevent all. The changes are *adaptations*, in the deepest sense—your nervous system, your perception, your relationship to time and sound and light, *adjusted* to a place that is still *becoming*, still *deciding* what it will be.

**Document carefully.** The multiverse traveler's duty is to *record*, to *transmit*, to make the strange *available* to others. But document with humility. This place does not yield its secrets to observation. It *negotiates* with attention, offers glimpses in exchange for engagement, *becomes* differently for each witness. Your record is *true for you*. It is not *the* truth of the place.

**Return, if you return.** The settlement continues its negotiation. The canyon continues its requests. The deep continues its waiting. You may contribute—sound, growth, silence, *presence*—if you have learned enough, if you have been changed enough, to be *useful* rather than merely *vulnerable*.

Or you may not return. This is also acceptable. The place does not require your persistence. It will continue *becoming*, with or without your witness, your participation, your *voice* in the hum.

## Final Preparation: The Night Before

Sleep in total darkness. Practice with your dampeners. Review your reason to return. Check your equipment—dampeners, backups, filtration, timekeeping, nutrition, light-blocking.

Listen to silence. The silence you know, not the silence of the fungal networks, not the silence the canyon sometimes requests. Your own silence. Your own *becoming*, paused, waiting, *deciding* whether to continue.

The Hum of Throat-Canyons will still be there. It has waited through other canyons, other configurations. It can wait for your decision.

Decide. Then go. Or don't. The canyon does not care.

But if you go—*prepare*. The place is not hostile. It is *hungry*, in the way that growing things are hungry, in the way that deep water is hungry, in the way that time itself is hungry for *more*, for *different*, for *becoming*.

Prepare to be *used*. Prepare to be *changed*. Prepare to *return*—or to be *returned*, by processes you set in motion but do not control.

The hum is waiting. The throats are singing. The canyon inhales.

Breathe with it. Or don't. But *choose*.

*Safe travels, wayfarer. The multiverse is vast, and this is only one of its *becomings*. May your own continue, changed but continuing, through the hum and the silence and the spaces between.*
