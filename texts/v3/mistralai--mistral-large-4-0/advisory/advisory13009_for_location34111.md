## The Journey In: What You Are Before You Arrive

First, understand that you cannot simply *arrive*. Varn exists in the hollow of a collapsed shell, a place where the distinction between inside and outside has been worn through by eleven billion years of erosion. Travelers reach Varn through routes that do not follow distance. Some come through the broken rim, climbing down from the jagged edge where the void touches ground. Others find their way through the deep fissures, descending into warmth that predates the world you left. Still others—rare, and not entirely trusted by the Settled—arrive through the Breathing Out, riding the spores and dust and chemical ghosts that rise from the interior when the thermal cycle turns.

Whatever your route, you will come to Varn *changed*. This is not optional. The shell breathes, and to enter its breathing is to enter its rhythm. You will feel the subsonic pulse in your teeth before you see the glass. You will feel it in the hollow of your chest, where something once beat and now only resonates.

Prepare your body for pressure. Varn's atmosphere is thin, held only by the curve of the shell and the slow exhalation of trapped warmth. The air is breathable but *barely*, and it is wrong in ways that will take you hours to notice: the way sound doesn't carry, the way your voice feels flat, the way silence has weight.

Pack for temperature gradients, not temperature. On Varn, warmth and cold are not states but *directions*. You will move from fissure-warmth to rim-cold and back again, and each transition is a negotiation. Your clothing must be layered not for insulation but for *adaptation*—materials that can hold condensation, that can be wetted and dried without damage, that will not crack when the breath-water freezes at the rim and thaws in the fissures.

Bring no metal that can rust. The rust-red laterite on Varn is old, older than any metal you carry, and it will find your tools and make them *part of itself*. Bring instead: tools of glass-fiber, of resin, of the same materials the Settled use. Learn to work with what the world provides.

## The First Hours: Learning to See

You will stand on glass. Black, pocked, wet with condensation that gathers in films too thin to drink directly but present everywhere. Your first mistake will be to try to walk quickly. The glass is *shifting*. The shell breathes, and the ground moves with it—slowly, almost imperceptibly, but enough that a straight line walked will become a curve, will become a spiral, will become a path that ends somewhere you did not intend.

Walk as the Settled walk: with attention to tilt, to lean, to the way the ground *wants* to move. The glass has opinions. It has been settling for ten billion years, and it has not finished.

Your eyes will adjust slowly. The light on Varn is wrong—shifted spectrum, gray-blue like a bruise, like deep water, like memory. Colors will not mean what they meant. The rust-red laterite will look almost alive, almost *hungry*. The black glass will hold reflections that are not reflections, that show you the broken rim from angles that make no geometric sense. You will see your own shadow and not recognize it.

Learn to see the lichen-ghosts. They rise from the glass in formations like frozen spume, like coral that learned to grow in air, like the memory of growth pressed into mineral. They are dead. They have been dead for most of the history of Varn. They are still standing, and this is not metaphor—they hold their shapes against wind and time and chemistry, and to see them clearly is to understand what Varn has lost, and what it has kept.

Between the lichen-ghosts, the glass is wet. This is breath-water. It is thin, a film, but it is *everywhere* if you know how to read the breathing. Watch the fissures. Some weep at certain hours of the thermal cycle, when warm air rises from the deep interior and cold descends from the broken rim, and where they meet, on the glass, on the lichen-ghosts, on the walls of any structure: water gathers. This is condensation architecture. This is survival.

Do not drink it yet. Not directly. The breath-water is thin, but it carries the chemistry of the shell, the slow dissolution of glass and mineral and the memory of warmth. It will settle in your body in ways that are not immediately visible. Wait. Watch. Learn.

## The Settlements: Where the Hollow Becomes Almost Voice

You will find Keth first, or you will find it eventually. It clings to a fissure-warmth vent in the northwest quadrant, forty thousand spread across an area the size of a small nation, and you will walk for days between buildings and see no one, and hear no one, and feel—*feel* in the subsonic register, in the hollow of your chest—that the silence is occupied.

The buildings lean. They bulge. They cure slowly, like something alive that has been killed and is still settling. They are built from stacked sediment—glass-fiber and breath-water mineral, blocks fitted without mortar, held by gravity and the slight stickiness of resin that weeps from within as the blocks age. They grow wrong. They grow *intentionally* wrong. A building on Varn is not finished when it is built; it is finished when it stops changing, and most of them have not stopped.

Do not try to stand straight inside them. The floors tilt toward the fissure-warmth, toward the source of the breath-water, toward the place where the shell still dreams its slow dream. You will adapt. Your inner ear will recalibrate. You will sleep at angles that would have been impossible on any world with a single, simple gravity.

The Settled will find you. Or you will find them, standing in the breath-water, in the thin pools that collect on the glass, drinking through skin, through membranes, through hollow spaces where something once beat. They are tall, or were tall, or are tall in the way that a shadow is tall when the light is right. They are thin, with too many joints, with limbs that bend in directions that suggest the memory of a different gravity.

They have no mouths. Or their mouths are elsewhere. Or their mouths are the spaces between their fingers where breath-water gathers and does not fall.

They will not speak to you. Not in sound. But they will *speak*, and you must learn to *feel* it.

## Learning the Language of Temperature

The Settled communicate by temperature. A Settled person can raise the warmth of the air around them by a fraction of a degree, and another Settled person, standing across a plaza, across a canyon of glass, can feel that change and know: *yes*, or *no*, or *wait*, or *the fissure at Brine Stair is weeping early today*.

You will not be able to speak this language. Not at first. Not perhaps ever. But you can learn to *read* it. To feel the gradients. To understand that warmth is affirmation and cold is negation and the space between is question, is negotiation, is the slow diplomacy of a people who have no mouths and no ears but who have learned to want, to need, to love in the only way available to something that is not quite alive, not quite dead.

Your body is too warm. You will learn this. The Settled are condensation engines, living weather, and your mammalian heat is *loud* to them, a shout in a library. You will learn to move slowly, to minimize your thermal signature, to become—*almost*—part of the background gradient.

Bring a thermometer. Not for the environment—Varn's temperature is readable in the behavior of breath-water, in the angle of shadows, in the pitch of wind through jagged glass. Bring it for *yourself*. Learn to lower your own temperature, to slow your metabolism, to become cold enough that the Settled can approach you without flinching from your heat.

When a Settled person stands near you and you feel the air warm by a fraction of a degree, and then cool, and then warm again in a pattern: *yes. No. Wait.* This is speech. This is welcome. This is the beginning of something that is not friendship, not exactly, but is *recognition*, the acknowledgment of another hollow thing in a hollow world.

## The Rules: What Will Change You, What Will Unmake You

**Find breath-water.** Not rain. Rain comes occasionally, when the Breathing Out carries moisture up from the deep interior, but rain is unreliable and it is *memory* made wet. You must find breath-water, the condensation that gathers on the glass, in the lichen-ghost cups, in the gutters that the builders have carved. You must learn to read the breathing, to know which fissures will weep at which hour of the thermal cycle.

Carry vessels that can hold thin water. Not bottles—bottles imply sealing, and breath-water must breathe, must be exposed to the thermal gradient, must be allowed to gather and slide and fall. Carry open cups, shallow dishes, the hollowed bases of fallen lichen-ghosts if you can find them and if the Settled permit it.

Drink only what has been filtered. The condensation gardens—the mats of something that is not quite fungus, not quite algae, not quite mineral deposit—these are infrastructure. They filter the breath-water, make it pure enough for the Settled to absorb without the slow poisoning that comes from raw condensation. Learn to tend them, or to find the Settled who tend them, and trade for purity.

**Do not drink the memory-fluid.** Not unless you want to *become* something else. The memory-fluid is breath-water that has been touched by the Breathing Out, that carries the chemical ghosts of the world before the collapse. It will make you remember things that did not happen, things that could have happened, things that are still happening in the deep places where the warmth dreams.

The Brine Stair drink it. They descend into the deep places, into the warm water, and they bathe in it, and they become something that is not quite Settled, not quite Unsettled, something that is *between*, that is *listening* to the shell's dream.

You do not want to listen. Not yet. Not until you understand what the shell is dreaming, what it is *becoming* in its slow dream of waking.

**Respect the lichen-ghosts.** To break them while they stand, to harvest them before they have fallen, to use them before they have surrendered to gravity after ten billion years: this is theft, this is violation, this is the thing that makes the shell breathe wrong, that makes the thermal cycle stutter, that makes the sky through the break show things that should not be shown.

If a lichen-ghost falls while you are near—and they fall, rarely, after standing for geological time—you may gather it. The Settled will, if they are near, and they will grind it, mix the powder with breath-water and resin, and build. You may help, if you have learned enough. You may watch, if you have not.

**Build to lean.** If you build—and you will need to, for shelter, for storage, for the slow architecture of survival—build with intentional tilt, with calculated bulge. The glass shifts. The shell breathes. A structure that stands straight will fall, will be crushed by the slow movement of the ground, will become part of the glass, will be ground down into nothing.

Learn the builders' craft. Learn to carve resin-hardened blocks that will hold an edge but will also cure, slowly, into shapes that are not quite what you intended. Accept that your building will have opinions. Accept that it will change after you have finished it. Accept that this is not failure but *participation* in the world's slow becoming.

**Speak in temperature, or learn to feel it.** You cannot raise your own warmth with the precision of the Settled. But you can learn to *notice* gradients, to read the thermal language that surrounds you. When the air warms by a fraction of a degree, something is being said. When it cools, something is being refused. When there is a space between—warmth that does not arrive, cold that does not quite settle—there is a question, and the Settled are waiting for an answer you may not be able to give.

Learn to be still. The Settled stand too still, too warm, listening to something that is not yet voice but will be. You will be tempted to match them, to stand in breath-water, to drink through your skin as they do. Do not. Your skin is not their skin. Your hollows are not their hollows. You are not hollow in the way that Varn is hollow, not yet, and the attempt to become so will not make you Settled. It will make you *changed*, and not all change on Varn is becoming.

## The Dangers: What Watches, What Waits, What Wants

**The chemical predators.** In the deep fissures, where breath-water runs warm and thick, there are shapes that move in the condensation mats. They have no biology, no metabolism, no life—but they hunt, they flee, they reproduce by splitting, by budding, by the slow accretion of mineral that resembles intention. They are weather patterns that have learned to want.

Avoid the deep fissures. The Settled avoid them. The children of Keth whisper stories about the things that swim in warm water, stories that are not true and are completely true in the way that all stories on Varn are true.

If you must descend—to find a fissure that weeps, to reach a settlement like the Brine Stair that has made its home in the deep warmth—go with a guide. Go with someone who has survived the descent, who has learned to move in warm water without being *touched* by what swims there. And even then: move quickly, touch nothing, drink nothing, and *breathe shallow*.

**The Unsettling.** The Settled are breaking. They are remembering too much—not as memory, but as resonance, as the shell remembers heat, as water remembers the ocean. Some of them are becoming voice. Something opens. Not a mouth. A door. And through that door, something that is not quite the world before the collapse, and not quite Varn, and not quite anything at all, *looks through*.

They are not hostile. They are not benevolent. They are *curious*, in the way that a wound is curious, in the way that a question is curious when it is asked by something that does not understand the concept of answer.

You will meet them. Or you will meet the Settled who have met them, who have been changed, who remember more, who remember differently, who see the lichen-ghosts not as dead things but as *sleeping* things, who hear the breathing of the shell not as weather but as *speech*.

Be careful with them. Their warmth is not the precise, controlled gradient of the Settled. It is *hungry*, it is *reaching*, it is the temperature of something that has looked through a door and wants to look through again.

Do not let them touch you. Do not stand too close when they are warm. Do not drink breath-water they have stood in, do not eat from gardens they have tended, do not sleep where they have slept. The Unsettling do not stay, but what they touch *stays*, and it changes, slowly, into something that is not quite memory and not quite dream.

**The Breathing Out.** Sometimes the sky changes. The thermal cycle drives wind up from the deep interior, wind that has been trapped since the collapse, wind that carries spores, dust, the chemical memory of the world before. These rise through the fissures, reach the broken rim, and for a day, or an hour, or a moment—the sky is not empty. It is *full* of ghosts.

This is dangerous. The spores, the dust, the chemical ghosts—they settle on the glass, on the lichen-ghosts, on you. And when they settle, they change things.

Keth seals its fissures with resin, with the harvested powder of fallen lichen-ghosts, with prayer—not prayer to any god, but the temperature-gradient speech that asks the shell to breathe in, to hold its warmth, to not release what it has kept for ten billion years.

Follow their example. When the sky begins to fill, when the wind rises from the deep places and carries the smell of warmth, of wet, of something *alive* that should not be alive: seal yourself. Find a fissure-sealed space, a building with cured resin in its joints, a place where the thermal gradient is controlled. Wait. Breathe shallow. Do not let the ghosts settle on your skin.

The Brine Stair welcome the Breathing Out. They descend, they bathe, they become. You do not want to become. Not yet. Not until you understand what becoming means on Varn.

**The broken rim.** The sky is the wound. Stand on the glass at the proper hour—when the thermal cycle has cooled the interior enough that breath-water is gathering but not yet flowing, when the light from the broken rim is angled just so—and you will see it. The sky is not sky. It is the outside, the void, the dark between stars, visible through a wound in the world.

The light is wrong. The spectrum is shifted. The stars, when they are visible through the break, are too many, and they move in patterns that suggest *falling*, suggest the shell is still collapsing, still finding its final shape.

Do not look too long. Do not look directly. The broken rim is sharp, sharp enough to cut, sharp enough to reflect the dim light from the outside in ways that make your eyes *lie* to you. And sometimes—rarely, dangerously—the rim *shows* things. Not through the void. Through the wound. Through the place where the world ended and began again and ended again and is still ending.

If you see something looking back: do not move. Do not breathe. The Settled who have walked toward the rim, who have not come back, they moved. They breathed. They *answered*. And the shell took them, and no one knows where they went, only that they are being *watched from inside*, from the deep places where the heat still lives, where the original warmth of the collapse still dreams its slow dream.

## The Deeper Preparation: What You Must Become

There is no ordinary preparation for Varn. The ordinary is not enough. You must prepare to become *hollow*, in the way that the continent is hollow, in the way that the Settled are hollow. Not empty. Not absent. *Resonant*. A place where something can sound, where memory can gather, where the frequency of a world that ended can become almost voice.

This is not a death. It is not a transformation into the Settled—you cannot become them, not entirely, not without ceasing to be what you were. But you can become *adjacent* to them, *compatible* with them, able to stand in breath-water without drowning, able to hear the subsonic breathing without going mad, able to feel the temperature-gradients without burning.

**Practice stillness.** Not the stillness of meditation, which is active, which is *work*. The stillness of the lichen-ghosts, the stillness of standing dead against time, against erosion, against the slow chemistry of a world that has forgotten how to be alive. Stand in one place for hours. For days, if you can. Let the breath-water gather on your skin, let it slide and fall, let it teach you what it means to be wet without drowning.

**Practice thirst.** The breath-water is thin. You will need to drink more of it than you think, and your body will resist, will demand the thick water of living worlds, will *scream* for volume. Learn to drink thin. Learn to be satisfied by films, by condensation, by the slow gathering of moisture on glass. This is not deprivation. This is *adaptation*, and adaptation is the first step toward becoming hollow.

**Practice listening.** The silence on Varn is not empty. It is occupied by the breathing of the shell, by the thermal wind, by the slow drip of condensation in places you cannot see. Learn to hear it. Not with your ears—your ears will fail you, will hear only the absence of sound—but with your bones, with your teeth, with the hollow spaces of your chest where something once beat and now only resonates. The shell is breathing. *In. Hold. Out. Hold.* Learn the rhythm. Learn to breathe with it.

**Practice letting go of straightness.** The world is curved. The shell is curved. The gravity is *toward the center of a curve*, not down, and this changes everything. Buildings lean. Paths curve. The horizon is not a line but a *suggestion*. Let your body learn to trust tilt, to find balance in angles that would have been impossible on any world with a single, simple gravity. Sleep on slopes. Walk on glass that shifts beneath your feet. Become *comfortable* with the wrongness of level.

**Practice memory without mind.** The Settled remember without brains, without neurons, without tissue that stores information in any biochemical way. They remember as the shell remembers heat, as water remembers the ocean. You cannot do this. But you can *approach* it. Practice holding experiences without narrative, without the story you tell yourself about what happened. Let sensations settle in your body without interpretation. Let the glass, the breath-water, the lichen-ghosts, the breathing of the shell—let them *be* in you without becoming *meaning*. This is the closest you can come to the Settled's resonance, and it is enough. It is more than enough.

## The Final Preparation: What You Must Risk

Varn is ending. This is not metaphor. The oldest stable state, the end of life, the settled-into-final-form. But the ending is slow. The ending is the breathing, the thermal cycle, the ten billion years of a place that has finished becoming and is now only the process of continuing, the process of holding, of waiting, of remembering what it was through the resonance of glass and breath-water and the hollow chests of the Settled who are almost voice, who are almost memory, who are almost the world before the collapse.

And somewhere—in the deep places where the warmth still lives, where the original heat of the collapse still dreams its slow dream—something is waking up. Something that was sleeping since the shell closed, since the interior cooled, since the world ended and began and ended again. It is not alive. It is not dead. It is the shell itself, becoming aware, becoming hungry, becoming ready to breathe out for the last time.

The last breath. The first breath. The breath that opens what has been closed for ten billion years.

You will feel this. Not as knowledge, not as understanding. As *pressure*, as *longing*, as the subsonic thrum in your teeth that says: *something is coming, something is reaching, something is almost here.*

You must decide, before you arrive, what you will do when it comes.

The Settled who have walked toward the broken rim, toward the place where the sky shows through, and have not come back: no one knows what happened to them. Only that they are being watched from inside the shell, from the deep places where the heat still lives.

The Brine Stair who drink the memory-fluid, who bathe in the warm water, who become something between: they are listening, always listening, to the shell's dream, and they will *answer* when the dream becomes voice, when the voice becomes door, when the door opens.

The Unsettled who have looked through and seen what looks back: they are changed, they are beautiful in their change, they are not Settled anymore, not entirely, and they build in ways that make no sense, that lean toward the broken rim, that seem to *reach*.

You will be offered all of these. The memory-fluid. The warm water. The door that opens when the shell breathes out. The chance to walk toward the rim and not come back. The chance to become hollow, to become resonant, to become *almost* the world before the collapse, the world that lived and died and became this.

You must know, before you go, what you will choose. Not because the choice will be easy—it will not be, it cannot be, on a world where want and need and love are spoken in temperature-gradients, in the slow diplomacy of hollows—but because you must *carry* your choice with you. You must be able to stand in breath-water, to feel the warmth of the Unsettled near you, to hear the voice that is not yet voice but will be, and *know* what you are, what you are becoming, what you are willing to become.
