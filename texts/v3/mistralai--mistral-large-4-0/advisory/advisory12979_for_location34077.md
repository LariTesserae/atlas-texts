# Preparation: The Drowned Hundredth Floor

*An ordinary human multiverse traveler's guide to ascending into a place that has forgotten it was built*

---

## Before You Arrive: Understanding What You're Walking Into

This is not a destination you approach casually. The Drowned Hundredth Floor is a **vertical ecosystem** masquerading as architecture, and your body—your *land-bound, air-breathing, temperature-regulated* body—must learn to survive what it was never designed for. The most important preparation is *mental*: you must stop thinking of this place as a building you visit and start understanding it as **a living body you enter**.

### The Pressure is Not Your Enemy (Initially)

You will descend—or rather, be lowered—through water that is **thick, mineral-heavy, and warm in places you don't expect**. At first, this feels wrong. Your ears will protest. Your sinuses will complain. The water is *dense*—not like ocean, which moves, but like a presence, like being embraced by something that wants to keep you.

**Prepare your equalization techniques.** Learn to equalize not just your ears but your *expectation* of pressure. The waterline shifts as you climb. What was at your waist becomes your chest, then your chin, then—briefly, terrifyingly—*above* you, and you must learn to **breathe through water** or find the air pockets.

The inhabitants breathe water. You cannot. But you can *learn the rhythm of the tides within the structure*—the thermal pulses that push water in and out, the moments when air is trapped, when it escapes, when you can find a pocket and fill your lungs before continuing.

### The Water Itself is a Character

This is **old water**. Water that has been inside this structure for centuries, perhaps millennia. It carries:

- **Dissolved minerals** that will coat your skin, your equipment, your *vision*
- **Colloidal memory**—the particulate residue of concrete, steel, human intention, now indistinguishable from the living chemistry around it
- **Chemosynthetic life** that has never known the sun, that pulses in **green-brown luminescence** you will feel in your teeth

**Your preparation must include:**

- **Sealed, pressure-rated everything.** Not waterproof. *Pressure-rated.* The depth changes. The density changes. Your gear must adapt or fail.
- **Light discipline.** You will become dependent on the **bioluminescent walls**—the slow heartbeat pulse of the structure's living skin. Your own lights will disrupt this. Learn to navigate in *their* light, which is dim, golden-green, and **temporal**—it pulses on cycles of hours, not seconds. Your eyes will adjust. Trust the adjustment.
- **Taste awareness.** The chemical gradients are *tasteable*. You will know when you're entering a transition zone—a boundary between ecosystems—by the **mineral tang on your tongue**, the shift in temperature, the change in the water's *weight* against your skin.

## The Ascent: Floor by Floor, Breath by Breath

You cannot rush this. **The dynamic is slow (0.8)**—and you must match it, or you will exhaust yourself against a patience you cannot comprehend.

### Movement

**Swimming is not enough.** The structure is **half-flooded, half-air**, and the waterline shifts constantly. You will:

- **Climb** grown stairs, their railings encrusted with calcium, with lichen, with the **memory of metal** visible only where something has scraped the growth away
- **Wade** through corridors where water reaches your waist, your chest, your chin
- **Drift** through vertical shafts where you can move for **minutes without touching anything**, suspended in water that **buoys you**, that lets you move through three dimensions with the effort of walking

**Your preparation must include:**

- **Buoyancy control.** The water is dense. You will float higher than expected. Learn to compensate. Learn to *sink* when you need to reach the floor, to *rise* when you need air.
- **Grip for organic surfaces.** Your boots, your gloves—they must hold on **grown walls**, on **calcium-crusted plaster**, on **kelp-analogue membranes** that are three meters tall and rooted in sediment that has accumulated in every room.
- **Patience training.** Practice **stillness**. The fauna moves through cycles you cannot perceive in a single visit. If you move too fast, you will miss them. You will miss the **thrumming in the structure**—the vibration of something too large for any room, coiled around the building's central spine, **sleeping**.

### Breathing

You will need **air pockets**. They exist—**dry levels**, pockets of air trapped in sealed compartments the vegetation hasn't learned to breathe. Finding them is **intuition**, not map-reading.

**Signs of air pockets:**

- **Lighter water**—less dense, less mineral-heavy, *thinner*
- **Different sound**—the water **sings** in narrow passages, creates standing waves, resonates in hollow spaces. Air pockets are **silent**.
- **The geometry of containment**—rectangular doorways, still *architectural*, frame not rooms but **gardens**, but sometimes, sometimes, they frame **dry space**

When you find air, **breathe slowly**. The transition is shocking. After water so thick, so *present*, air feels **thin**, **violent**, **temporary**. You will want to descend again. Resist, or don't—but know that each ascent into air is **preparation for the next descent**.

### Light

The walls **pulse**. This is not metaphor. The bioluminescent colonies have colonized **every surface, every crack, every former window frame**, and they pulse in **slow synchrony**, a heartbeat you can feel in your teeth.

**Your preparation must include:**

- **Dark adaptation.** Hours, perhaps days, of exposure to the **green-gold twilight** of the deep. Your eyes will adjust. You will see **structure in the pulse**—patterns, cycles, the **metabolism of the building** made visible.
- **Light discipline.** If you must use your own light, use it **sparingly**, **directionally**, and never upward—never toward the **canopy of kelp-analogues**, never toward the **membranes** that are not leaves but **vascular channels**, carrying nutrients from the deep thermal vents up through the structure's original plumbing.

## The Inhabitants: What Lives Here

**Fauna: 2.3, abundant.** They have filled every niche. You are not alone, but you are **spread**—the density is sparse (0.9), and you will pass through **vast spaces** where you touch nothing but water, nothing but **growth**, nothing but the **slow pulse of the structure's light**.

### What You Will Meet

**Crustacean-things the size of your hand.** They scuttle across grown surfaces, their shells **clicking against calcium deposits**, a constant percussion. They are **not aggressive**. They are **busy**. They are part of the **erosion**, the **recycling**, the **patience of stone becoming soil becoming forest**.

*Preparation:* **Do not crush them.** They are part of the **soundscape**, the **rhythm** you must learn to move within. Your footsteps will add to their percussion. Make them **respectful**.

**Fish—if you can call them that.** They drift through the flooded lower levels in **schools that move as one organism**, their passage creating **pressure waves you feel in your sternum**.

*Preparation:* **Let them pass.** Do not reach. Do not chase. They are **sensors**, **processors**, part of the **chemical negotiation** happening in gradients you cannot see. Your interference would be **violence**, and this place does not forgive violence—not with anger, but with **indifference**, with the **slow patience of water wearing stone**.

**Something larger.** You will hear it sometimes—**a thrumming in the structure itself**, the vibration of a body moving through water-filled space, **too big for any room**, coiled around the building's central spine like a **sleeping thing**.

*Preparation:* **Do not seek it.** It is not hostile. It is not curious. It is **part of the building now**, part of the **metabolism**, the **breathing** of the structure. If you encounter it—if you feel its **pressure wave**, its **displacement of water**—**drift with it**. Do not swim against it. Do not swim toward it. **Become part of the flow**, and it will pass, and you will continue.

## The Vegetation: 2.9, The Architecture Itself

The plants **are** the architecture now. This is the most important thing to understand.

**Kelp-analogues, three meters tall.** Their fronds are **membranes**—translucent, veined with vascular channels that carry nutrients from the deep thermal vents up through the structure's original plumbing, **repurposed, rerouted through organic graft**. Where there was plaster, there is **lichen-crust** that secretes calcium carbonate and builds new surfaces, **layer upon layer**, slowly, patiently, over **decades and centuries**.

**Your preparation must include:**

- **Respect for the growth.** Do not cut it. Do not tear it. Do not **harvest** it unless you understand the **cycles**, the **feeding, breeding, dying, becoming sediment, becoming nutrient, becoming new growth**. The inhabitants **cultivate**, they **harvest**, they **adapt**—but they do so with **knowledge** you do not have.
- **Movement through membranes.** The kelp-analogues **sway** in currents so slow they seem still until you watch long enough to see the **drift**. Learn to **part them**, to **move through them**, to feel the **viscosity of their vascular channels**, the **gentle resistance** of living tissue.
- **Understanding of scale.** Rooms that were designed for ten people now hold **forests**. A former stairwell is a **vertical garden**, a column of layered growth where each level is a **different ecosystem**: the warm dark at the bottom, the cool twilight at the top, the **transition zones** where species **negotiate their boundaries** in chemical gradients you can **taste on your tongue**.

## The Built: 2.3, Still Present, Still Urban

The human architecture is **not gone**. It is **in** the vegetation now. You will feel it constantly:

- **The right angles**
- **The level floors**—settled, established, in-balance after centuries of slow adjustment
- **The deliberate narrowing of corridors**
- **The hierarchy of spaces**

Someone built this to **do** something. Process materials. Grow food. House workers. The purpose is **unreadable now**, but the **structure of purpose remains**, like a **watermark**, like a **scar**.

**Your preparation must include:**

- **Architectural literacy.** Learn to **read the geometry**—the **doorways** that frame **gardens**, the **stairs** you can still climb, the **rectangles** that persist beneath the organic chaos. This is **human intention made manifest**, now **subsumed**, now **translated**, but still **present**.
- **Tool recognition.** You will find **blades hafted in crustacean claws**, **lines woven from sinew**, **knots tied by hands that are dust now**. These are **tech 1.0**—primitive, but **weird primitive**, **biological, grown, symbiotic**. They are **older than human technology**. They are **continuous with the world**.

Do not **steal** them. Do not **use** them without **understanding**. If you must use them, **learn from the inhabitants**—the current ones, the ones who have made this their world. They do not mine. They do not smelt. They **cultivate**, they **harvest**, they **adapt**.

## The Sound: 2.3, It Is Loud Here

Not with voices. With **everything else**.

**Your preparation must include:**

- **Acoustic mapping.** Learn the **soundscape**:
  - The **clicking** of crustacean shells against calcium
  - The **pressure waves** of fish-schools moving as one organism
  - The **thrumming** of the sleeping thing, too big for any room
  - The **rush** of water through pipes that are now **arteries**
  - The **drip** from growths that have **no name**
  - The **singing** in narrow passages where flow creates **standing waves**, frequencies that resonate in the **hollow spaces of the structure's bones**, turning the whole place into an **instrument** played by **temperature and pressure and time**

- **Silence discipline.** There are **long silences** that are **not empty but full**—full of processes too slow for hearing, of **chemistry**, of **pressure**, of the **deep patience of stone becoming soil becoming forest becoming, eventually, something else again**. Learn to **hear the silence**. It is **information**.

## The Time: 0.8, Nothing Here Rushes

This is the hardest preparation. **You cannot rush the Drowned Hundredth Floor.**

The growth takes **years**. The erosion takes **centuries**. The structure **settles**, millimeter by millimeter, as the thermal vents shift their output, as the weight of accumulated biomass compresses the lower levels, as the chemistry of the water changes with each generation of life and death.

**Your preparation must include:**

- **Temporal dilation training.** Practice **watching slow things**:
  - A lichen **covering a surface**, not hurrying
  - A kelp-analogue **swaying** in a current so slow it seems still
  - The **drift** of sediment, of nutrient, of **becoming**

- **Cycle awareness.** The light pulses on a cycle of **hours**, not seconds. The temperature shifts across **seasons that last years**. The fauna moves through **cycles you cannot perceive in a single visit**. You will not see **birth and death** in one ascent. You will see **evidence**—**sediment**, **growth**, **the compression of lower levels**—and you must **infer** the rest.

- **Patience as survival.** If you rush, you will **exhaust yourself** against a patience you cannot comprehend. If you rush, you will **miss the air pockets**, the **dry levels**, the **transition zones**. If you rush, you will **fight the water**, and the water **always wins**, eventually, with **indifference**.

## The Hundredth Floor: The Surface, Reached from Below

At the top—there is a top, though you cannot see it from below, the luminescence diffusing everything into **green-gold twilight**—there is a place where:

- The water is **shallow**
- The original roof has **cracked**
- **Rain still falls**, **actual rain**, from a sky that **remembers being sky**

Here the kelp grows **shorter, stunted by the violence of the interface**. Here the water is **fresh**, shockingly, **thin**, and you can **stand**, finally, on a surface that is **not growing, not yet**, and **look up** at a sky that is **grey and vast and normal**, and feel the **weirdness of it**, the **0.9**, the **unusual rightness** of **open air** after so long in the deep.

**The rain falls. It is loud. It is the loudest thing you have heard.**

This is the **climax of your preparation**—not because it is dangerous, but because it is **disorienting**. After so long in the **thick, warm, mineral-heavy, bioluminescent, slow, patient, growing** deep, **open air** is **violence**. The **thinness** of it. The **dryness**. The **vastness** of a sky that does not pulse, that does not breathe, that is simply **there**, **normal**, **indifferent**.

**You must be prepared to feel small.**

Scale: 1.1. You are a **visitor in a room-sized world** that is also a **building-sized world** that is also a **planet-sized world**, all at once, all depending on **where you stand**, **what you can see**, **how long you stay**.

Below you, the structure continues. The city continues. The **slow, patient, growing, drowned world** that has learned to live in the **memory of human purpose**, that has made of our architecture **something new**, **something itself**, **something that will outlast us**, that is **already outlasting us**, that will become, in time, simply **geology**, simply **habitat**, simply **the way things are**.

**You are small here.** And that is **correct**. That is **appropriate**. That is the **lesson** of the Drowned Hundredth Floor.

## Final Preparations: What to Carry, What to Leave

### Carry

- **Pressure-rated, sealed, adaptable gear.** Nothing that assumes **constant conditions**.
- **Buoyancy control systems.** You will need to **sink** and **rise** on demand.
- **Minimal light.** You will learn to **see in the pulse**.
- **Patience.** Not a virtue here. A **survival requirement**.
- **Humility.** This place does not care about you. It will **outlast** you. That is **not cruelty**. That is **honor**—to be **witnessed**, **briefly**, by something so **patient**, so **ancient**, so **alive**.

### Leave

- **Urgency.** There is no emergency here. There is only **the slow metabolism of the deep**.
- **Expectation of air.** You will find it, sometimes, but it is **not guaranteed**, and it is **not normal**. Do not **depend** on it.
- **The desire to understand completely.** The purpose of the original builders is **unreadable**. The current inhabitants do not **explain**. They **cultivate**, they **harvest**, they **adapt**. You will **observe**, **infer**, **wonder**. That is **enough**.
- **Fear of the water.** It will **hold you**. It will **buoy you**. It will **let you move through three dimensions with the effort of walking**. Trust it. **Learn its rhythms**. It is **not your enemy**.

## The Last Thing: How to Breathe

You cannot breathe the water. But you can **learn to breathe the rhythm of the place**.

- **Inhale** when the thermal vents push, when fresh(ish) water enters, when the **pulse of light** brightens
- **Exhale** when the structure **settles**, when the **bioluminescence** dims, when you feel the **weight of accumulated biomass** compressing the levels below
- **Move** with the **sway of the kelp**, the **drift of the sediment**, the **thrumming of the sleeping thing**
- **Be still** when the **silence is full**, when the **processes too slow for hearing** are **happening**, when the **patience of stone becoming soil becoming forest** is **your teacher**

And at the hundredth floor, when the **rain falls**, when the **sky is grey and vast and normal**, **breathe the thin air**. Feel the **violence of it**, the **shock of it**, the **gift of it**.

Then **descend again**, if you must. Or **stay**, and become **part of the sediment**, part of the **nutrient**, part of the **new growth**.

The Drowned Hundredth Floor does not **want** you. It does not **reject** you. It will **hold you**, **briefly**, and then **forget you**, as it has forgotten **everyone**, as it will forget **everything**, becoming, in time, simply **geology**, simply **habitat**, simply **the way things are**.

**That is enough.** That is **more than enough**. That is **the lesson of the deep**.
