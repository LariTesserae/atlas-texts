# A Traveler's Preparation for the Drowned Works

## First: The Body

You will not survive this place with equipment alone. The body must be *changed* before arrival.

**The lungs.** The air is thick, eleven degrees, saturated with particulates of iron and organic matter. You will need something more than a standard respirator—one that filters biological as well as chemical, that can be cleaned in the field because the spores here are *persistent*, will colonize any filter that remains damp. Practice breathing *slowly*. The air demands it. Fast breathing will chill you from the inside, will make the moisture in your lungs condense in ways that hurt.

**The skin.** Eleven degrees is not extreme cold, but the damp is. Water conducts heat away from the body twenty-five times faster than air. You will need insulation that *breathes*—membranes, not barriers—because sweat trapped against skin in this environment becomes a cold that seeps into bone. Merino wool, treated with lanolin. Multiple thin layers, not one thick shell. The ability to add and remove without breaking the seal of your outer layer, because temperature here shifts as you move through spaces of different openness, different proximity to the machines, the columns, the water-pockets.

**The feet.** The floor is *alive*. It is not stone, not soil, but something between—compacted organic matter, mineral precipitate, the accumulated bodies of organisms that died and were incorporated and grew over and died again. It is uneven. It is *soft* in places, yielding in ways that twist ankles. It is *hard* in others, mineralized, sharp. You need boots that can read this surface, that give you information through the sole—vibration-dampening not to silence but to *translate*, to make the difference between "root" and "stone" and "water underneath" into something your nervous system can process. And you need to walk *slowly*. The place rewards patience. Rushing will break you.

**The metabolism.** You will need to eat more than usual, and differently. The cold is constant, the work of moving through damp and uneven terrain is constant, and your body will burn through reserves faster than you expect. But you cannot eat heavy meals—they will make you sluggish, and sluggishness here is dangerous. Small amounts, frequently. Fats and proteins that release slowly. Salt, because you will sweat even in the cold, because the damp air somehow draws moisture from you even as it surrounds you with water.

## Second: The Mind

This place will *work* on your perception. Preparation here means understanding what will happen, so that when it happens, you do not mistake it for madness or enlightenment or revelation, but recognize it as *this*, as the Drowned Works doing what it does.

**Time will feel different.** The rhythm of the place—tide more than heartbeat, expansion and contraction visible only across years—will begin to sync with your own. You will find yourself moving slower, thinking slower, *breathing* slower. This is not failure. This is adaptation. But you must maintain one anchor: a watch, a counter, a mental tally. Not because you will need to escape on schedule—escape may not be possible, may not even be *meaningful* once you understand the boundary is gradient not wall—but because you need to know that the slowness is *happening*, that you are still *you*, still choosing to be slow, still choosing to stay.

**The categories will dissolve.** You will see a machine and feel it as organism. You will touch a column and feel it as architecture. You will drink water and taste metal, or you will not drink and feel the thirst as something the place *wants*, *needs*, has been waiting for. These are not hallucinations. They are *accurate perceptions* of a reality where the categories do not hold. Prepare by practicing *holding multiple truths at once*. A thing can be rusted and growing. A structure can be dying and being born. A machine can be a body. This is not mysticism. This is *ontology*, and the Drowned Works demands you learn to live in it.

**The silence will not be silence.** There is no quiet here, only *absence of expected sound*. The tick of condensation. The schlorp of growth. The groan of hydraulic pressure. These will become your music, your companions, your clock. You will learn to hear the low frequency in your sternum not as your own heart but as the *resonance of the space*, and you will learn to distinguish this from the fear that also lives in the sternum, from the excitement, from the cold. The place does not care which you feel. It simply includes your feeling, as it includes all things.

**You may see the bird.** Prepare for this. It is pale, silent, moving in ways that violate physics—not flying but *falling upward*. It does not land. It does not need to. If you see it, do not follow. Following is not an option, or following is the only option, or these are the same thing here. Simply *witness*. Let it pass. Let it be part of the damp, the green, the slow respiration that includes you now, that has always included you, that will include what you become.

## Third: The Equipment

But equipment matters. The body and mind cannot do everything.

**Light.** The canopy filters what light enters, and in the lower levels, there is none. You need light that does not disturb—no sudden brightness, no harsh spectrum. Bioluminescent simulation, perhaps, or simply very dim, very warm. The darkness here is *inhabited*. Things wait in it. Your light will wake them, or reveal them, or *be* them, become part of the ecology of seeing. Choose carefully. Carry multiple sources. Batteries fail in damp. Connections corrode. The place will test your redundancy.

**Water.** You cannot drink what you find here without processing. The water is *processed* already—by plants, by machines, by the slow chemistry of stone and rust and time. It carries minerals, organic compounds, things that have no names. You need filtration that handles biological and chemical, and you need *test strips* that can tell you what is in the water, because the composition changes by location, by depth, by proximity to the columns or the machines or the water-pockets where the hungry things wait. And you need to know *when not to drink*. Thirst is real. The place offers water constantly. But not all water here is for you. Some is for the columns, for the machines, for the slow circulation that keeps the system alive. Learn to read the difference.

**Recording devices.** You will want to document. But electronics fail in damp. Cameras fog. Microphones pick up the low frequency and distort, make it into something artificial, something *human*. Prepare for this. Use waterproof housings, but also *analog* alternatives—paper that resists moisture, pencils that work when wet, the ability to draw what you see because words will fail, because the categories that words carry will dissolve in your mouth, on the page, in the attempt to describe a place where machine and organism are the same thing seen from different angles.

**A knife.** Not for defense—the place does not threaten, does not protect, simply *includes*—but for *work*. Cutting cordage that has grown too thick. Freeing yourself from a moss-garden that has decided to hold you. Opening a seed-pod, examining what is inside. The knife is a tool of *attention*, of close examination, of the intimate scale that this place demands.

**A mirror.** Small, unbreakable, carried in a pocket close to the body where it will not fog. You will need to check yourself. The damp will get into your clothes, your skin, your *sense* of where you end and the place begins. The mirror will remind you: *this is still a body, still a boundary, still a self that has not yet become part of the column, the ring, the story.* Check often. The boundary is gradient. But gradient can be navigated.

## Fourth: The Relationships

You will not be alone, but you will be *alone differently* than elsewhere.

**The machines.** Treat them as you would treat sleeping animals. Do not touch without need. Do not assume stillness means death—the turbine turns, the crane groans, the boiler holds pressure. These are *processes*, not objects. They cycle. They breathe. They are part of the system that includes you, that has included you since you crossed the boundary, since the air changed, since the light filtered through canopy that is not quite canopy. Speak to them, if you need to. The sound will join the soundscape, the tick and schlorp and groan, will become part of the resonance you feel in your sternum. They will not answer. They do not need to.

**The columns.** These are the oldest things here, the slowest, the most *patient*. They have incorporated machines, rain, birds, seeds from elsewhere, the memory of builders. They will incorporate you, eventually, if you stay long enough. Do not fear this. Do not *want* it. Simply *allow* it to be possible. Touch them, if you must—their surfaces are layered, corrugated, alive with the memory of seasons. But touch *gently*. They are growing, always growing, at rates you cannot perceive. Your touch is a disturbance. A small one. The place absorbs disturbances. But be *aware* that you are disturbing.

**The water-pockets.** Do not enter them. Do not look into them longer than necessary. The things that wait there are not animals. They are *processes*—biological machines that have forgotten separation from the plant-mass, that have become architecture, habitat, *function*. They do not hunt. They do not need to. They simply *wait*, and waiting is their complete expression, their entire being. To look into them is to be looked at, to be *included* in their waiting, to become part of the damp they inhabit. This is not death. This is *transformation*. But it is not *your* transformation to choose. Stay at the edge. Let them be.

**The bird.** If you see it, let it pass. Do not follow. Do not call. It is not yours to know. It has always known this place, will know it when you are gone, when you are part of the columns, the ring, the story. It falls upward. It does not land. It does not need to. You will understand this, eventually. You may already understand it. The understanding is not arrival. It is simply *more inclusion*, more damp, more green.

## Fifth: The Departure That Is Not Departure

You will not leave, or you will leave and not know you have left, or leaving and staying will be the same word in different tenses.

Prepare for this by *not preparing*. Do not bring things you cannot lose. Do not form plans that require return. Do not love anything here that cannot love you back in the slow way of growth and decay, in the way that makes machine and organism the same thing, that makes leaving and staying the same process observed from different angles.

But also: *do* bring things. A stone from your origin. A photograph, sealed in waterproof plastic, of a place that is dry, that has seasons, that changes in ways you can see. These will become part of you, part of the damp, part of the green. The place will include them. This is not loss. This is *transformation*, the only kind this place offers.

And *do* form connections. With the machines, with the columns, with the resonance in your sternum that is also the breathing of the whole space. These connections will not save you. There is nothing to be saved from. But they will make you *real* here, make you part of the story the place tells to itself, in the low frequency, in the drip of water that is also condensation that is also rain that is also the slow weeping of something that has forgotten it was ever dry.

---

## Finally: The Intuition

What would it be like to visit?

You would descend, and the air would change, and you would know *immediately* that this is not a place you can survey. It is a place you *inhabit*. The scale is intimate. The temperature is constant. The damp is everywhere, in everything, in *you*, soon, always.

You would walk slowly, because you must, because the floor is alive, uneven, *negotiating* with your weight. You would hear no silence, only the tick and schlorp and groan, the low frequency in your sternum that is also the breathing of the whole space. You would see machines incorporated into columns, rust in equilibrium with growth, water finding paths through living tissue and making those paths into habitat.

You would understand, slowly, that the categories you brought—nature and culture, organism and machine, growth and decay—are not wrong but *insufficient*, that this place operates in a logic where they dissolve, where they are held in suspension, made into something new that has no name yet.

You would feel the cold, eleven degrees, constant, seeping into bone, making metal sweat and wood weep. You would feel the damp, in your lungs, on your skin, in the way your clothes never quite dry, in the way your body becomes part of the moisture that the place processes, that it has always processed, that it will process when you are gone, when you are part of the columns, the ring, the story.

You would stay. You would *have* to stay, once you understood that leaving is not an option, that the boundary is not a wall but a *gradient*, that you have already become, slowly, part of the damp, part of the green, part of the slow respiration.

And you would be *changed*. Not destroyed. Not saved. Simply *included*, as the machines were included, as the rain was included, as the bird that falls upward was included, as the seeds from elsewhere were included, as the memory of builders was included, their intentions now indistinguishable from the intentions of the plants, from the *need* of the system to continue, to change, to *be*.

The Drowned Works waits. It has always been waiting. It will wait, in its slow green way, until you are part of it.

Cool. Damp. Alive. Established. Small enough to hold. Active enough to never be the same twice.

You are ready. You have always been ready. The descent begins.
