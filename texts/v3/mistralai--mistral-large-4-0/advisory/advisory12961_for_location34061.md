I need to understand this place before I can advise on preparation. Let me sit with it.

The Anhydrous Cathedral is a chamber that has achieved *completion*. Water is not absent by accident but by exhaustion—this place *forgot* it, which is different from never having had it. The floor is baked by duration. The air is warm because the system cannot shed heat. There is no threat, only persistence. The danger is not death but *becoming*—settling into equilibrium, becoming another formation, another trace.

Gravity is slightly reduced. Time is not dilated but *completed* in its processes. The darkness has mass. Silence has texture. You are the only thing that changes.

What does an ordinary human multiverse traveler need?

## Preparation for the Anhydrous Cathedral

### The Body: What Must Change Before You Enter

**Hydration, inverted.** You are accustomed to carrying water into dry places. Here, water is *the contaminant*. Your breath adds moisture. Your skin will feel tacky, then dry, then normal—the place is *processing* you. Before entry, you must accept a different relationship with your own body's water. Drink normally. Do not overhydrate; you will not sweat in any conventional sense, yet you will lose moisture to the air through respiration and the slight permeability of skin. The air is *thirsty in a way that has no memory of satisfaction*—it will take from you without the desert's honest exchange of effort for relief.

Your body temperature will drift toward 2.3 on your scale—the ambient warmth of the chamber. This is not dangerous. It is *unnerving*. You will feel as if you are dissolving into the room. Prepare for this psychologically. Practice sitting in warm baths until your sense of self-boundary softens. The floor does not press back with full force; you will feel slightly lighter, as if the chamber is *curious about you*, testing whether you float away or settle.

**The ears.** The silence has weight. You will hear your own breathing as *loud*. The ringing that accompanies true silence will become a presence, not an absence. Some travelers find they cannot sleep in this place—the internal sounds become too pronounced. Bring no expectation of rest. Bring instead a practice of *active listening*: learn to hear the very low frequency hum in your sternum, the resonance of the chamber excited by your own movements. Become familiar with silence that has texture, that varies from dense to thin. If you can, practice in anechoic chambers, though none will prepare you for silence that *carries no echo at all*—sound here dies immediately, absorbed.

**The eyes.** The light is 0.2—darkness with texture. You will see the floor at your feet, formations at arm's reach, and beyond that, depth without boundary. The darkness presses down. You will feel the urge to crouch, to make yourself smaller. This is not metaphor; something in this place *communicates mass through absence*. Your eyes will adjust, but adaptation has limits. Do not rely on torch-equivalents; they will reveal too much and too little, and their batteries will drain in ways that feel *personal*, as if the light itself is being consumed by something. Bring instead a practice of *tactile navigation*—learn to walk with your hands extended, to read the floor through your palms. The ceramic reads as fine ridges, corduroy worn by patient feet. Learn to feel the difference between growth marks and erosion marks, even though the distinction has become irrelevant. You are not here to resolve; you are here to *witness*.

### Equipment: What to Carry, What to Refuse

**No metal.** The chemistry is acidic in slow motion. Sulfur-memory in the mineral deposits. Metal will not corrode dramatically—nothing here is dramatic—but it will *change*, surface by surface, in ways that accumulate. If you must carry metal, accept that it becomes part of the place's slow record. Ceramic tools, if you need them, should be made from the place itself—the floor is harder than it appears, and a scoop of local ceramic will outlast anything you bring.

**No water-bearing containers that seal.** The pressure differential is subtle but real. Sealed vessels may *weird* in ways you cannot predict—plastics becoming brittle, seals failing not from force but from *time's persistence*. Carry water in open, breathable containers if you must carry it. Better: carry *less* than you think you need, and trust the chamber's warmth. You will not overheat. You may feel as if you are *melting into* the warmth, but this is psychological. The actual thermal load is manageable.

**Light: minimal, and of a specific quality.** The formations are faintly self-luminous at wavelengths near the edge of perception. Your light will reveal this if it is of the right spectrum—*broad, dim, warm.* Harsh white light will destroy the texture of the darkness, will make the chamber into a *place* rather than a *presence*. Carry light that mimics the chamber's own—amber, low, diffuse. And carry *less* than you think. The darkness will teach you to see.

**Sound: bring none.** No recordings, no music, no communication devices that emit. The silence here is *occupied*. It has residents. Your sounds will die immediately, absorbed, but their *attempt* will disturb. If you must communicate with companions, use touch. Develop a vocabulary of pressure on the shoulder, the forearm, the palm—signals that transmit through the slightly reduced gravity, through the ceramic floor that does not echo.

**Recording devices: think carefully.** Cameras will capture the formations but not the *scale wrongness*, not the sense of enclosure without construction. Audio recorders will capture your own breathing, the hum in your sternum, and *everything else will be the machine's own noise, its own hunger for sound that this place does not provide*. If you must record, use devices that *fail gracefully*—that degrade into the environment rather than fighting it. And accept that your records will be *of your presence*, not of the place. The place does not wish to be documented. It has its own record, the scratches on walls too high to reach, the worn paths between formations, the tools shaped by abrasion against harder mineral. You are a question it has already answered.

### The Mind: What Must Be Prepared

**Time.** You will not experience time dilation. You will experience *irrelevance*. The processes that shape this place have completed, or have not yet begun. Your presence is a brief eddy in a current that has not changed in millennia. When you leave, the current will close behind you over years, centuries, perhaps longer. You will not see this. But you will *feel* it—the sense that your departure is not an ending but a *deferred consequence*, something the place will process long after you are gone.

Practice *non-narrative awareness*. Most human consciousness is storytelling—beginning, middle, end, meaning. This place offers none of these. It offers *persistence*. It offers *equilibrium*. Train yourself to experience without structuring, to perceive without interpreting. Meditation practices that emphasize *bare attention*, that release the impulse to name and categorize, will serve you better than any analytical framework.

**Loneliness.** Not the loneliness of absence, but the loneliness of *being the only thing that changes*. You will feel this acutely. The place does not miss you. It will not notice your departure except as a slight thermal disturbance, a brief redistribution of dust. Practice *voluntary solitude* before you come. Learn to be alone without needing the environment to *acknowledge* your aloneness. The chamber's indifference is not cruelty; it is *completion*. It has reached its own form and will not be moved from it. Your loneliness is *yours*—do not project it onto the place, which has no capacity for loneliness, only for persistence.

**Reverence.** You will feel it, if you are the kind who feels such things. Not religious reverence—there are no gods here, or the gods are the processes themselves. But reverence for the achieved. For the equilibrium that required so much time to establish. For the patience of stone and air and mineral. This reverence can become *desire to stay*, to settle, to become part of the equilibrium. Resist this. Not because the place is hostile—it is not—but because *you are not the place*. You are change. You are breath. You are the only thing that will leave. To stay is to *stop being yourself*, to become another formation, another trace, another mark on the wall that something might someday wonder about.

**The traces.** You will find them—abrasions in peculiar patterns, displacements of dust that look deliberate, worn paths between formations. Something walked here. Something still walks here, perhaps, at intervals so long you would not witness it. The fauna parameter is 0.5—the possibility remains but is unoccupied, like a chair with no one sitting in it. You may feel *watched*. This is not paranoia. The place is *aware* in a way that does not require consciousness—thermal gradients that respond to your warmth, mineral colonies that respond to vibration, the slow chemistry of air that carries the memory of sulfur. You are *registered*, even if no one is *registering*. Move with this awareness. Do not fear. Do not *invite*. The place has its own rhythms, its own slow purposes, and your intrusion is *tolerated*, not welcomed.

### Ritual: How to Enter, How to Leave

**Entry.** Do not rush. The threshold is real—you descend into this place, and the descent is *felt*, a change in pressure, in temperature, in the quality of light. Pause at the threshold. Let your eyes adjust to the texture of darkness. Let your ears adjust to the silence with weight. Let your body adjust to the slight reduction in gravity, the sense of floating, of being *curious* rather than *supported*.

Speak no words. Or speak only your own name, once, to *mark* your presence—not for the place, which does not listen, but for yourself, to establish that you are *here*, that you are *distinct*, that you will *leave*.

**Movement.** Walk slowly. The floor is ceramic in its exhaustion, baked by duration. It is not smooth. It reads as fine ridges, almost like corduroy worn down by patient feet. Learn to feel the difference between the ridges of growth and the ridges of erosion, even though the distinction has become irrelevant. You are here to *witness*, not to *resolve*.

When you encounter a formation—hollow, or solid, or banded with stripes of slightly darker material—do not touch it unless you must. The colonies that inhabit these formations are alive in a slow way. They respond to changes in air composition, to vibration, to the passage of something that disturbs thermal gradients. Your touch is a *disturbance*. It is not harmful, but it is *registered*, and the place is *closed*, *stable*, *time-invariant*. Your touch is an *event* in a system that has not experienced events in millennia.

If you find a tool—a scoop of ceramic harder than the floor, a bowl hollowed to hold something that no longer exists—do not use it. Do not *complete* it. The maker of these tools had a system, a syntax, a purpose. You do not know it. You will not learn it in the time you have. To use the tool is to *pretend* to membership, and the place will *feel* this pretense, will *register* it as the disturbance it is.

**Departure.** You will know when to leave. Not because the place will tell you—it will not—but because you will feel it: the warmth becoming *too warm*, the silence becoming *too present*, the sense of being *the only thing that changes* becoming *intolerable*. This is not fear. This is *recognition*. You are not meant to stay. You are not meant to *become*.

Gather your traces—your disturbed dust, your displaced thermal gradients, your brief eddy in the current. Do not attempt to *restore* them. The place will close behind you over years, centuries, perhaps longer. You cannot witness this. You can only *accept* it.

Climb. The ascent will feel different from the descent—lighter, perhaps, or *more weighted*, as if the place is *releasing* you, or *remembering* that it never held you. Do not look back. The darkness closes behind you. The silence resumes. The formations continue their slow existence.

### What to Bring Back

Nothing. Or rather: *yourself*, changed in ways you cannot yet name. The warmth you carry, the silence you will hear in ordinary rooms, the sense that you are *the only thing that changes* in a world that has stopped moving—these are yours. The place does not give gifts. It does not *withhold* them, either. You simply *pass through*, a question it has already answered, or will never bother to ask.

And somewhere, in the deepest part of the chamber, something adds another scratch to the record, or waits for no one, or has already forgotten that anything ever entered at all.

You were never there. You are *here*, changed, carrying the warmth of a place that forgot water existed.
