# The Hollow Cities of Still Air: Preparation Protocol for the Ordinary Human Multiverse Traveler

---

## Part I: Understanding What You Are Entering

Before equipment, before supplies, before any *practical* preparation—you need to prepare your *relationship to wrongness*.

These cities are not dangerous in the way a predator is dangerous, or a volcano, or a war. They are dangerous the way a *question* is dangerous: they demand an answer you may not be able to give. The weirdness at 2.9 is that the rules are *almost* legible. Almost. Not quite.

This is worse than no rules. This is worse than alien rules. This is rules that *recognize you*. That *respond* to you. The cities are active at 2.5. They want something. They are *waiting*, and waiting implies *expectation*.

Your first preparation is psychological, and it is not optional.

### The Mental Architecture of Visit

You must practice **suspension of certainty without descending into paranoia**. This is extraordinarily difficult. Every instinct that keeps you alive in ordinary environments—pattern recognition, threat assessment, the stories you tell yourself about cause and effect—will be *almost* functional here, and that almost will erode you.

Train yourself in this specific discipline:

**When you notice something impossible, do not immediately explain it.** Do not say "it's a trick of the light" or "it's a hallucination from the thin air" or "it's some local phenomenon." Notice the impossibility. Hold it. Let it *remain* impossible for as long as you can bear. Only then, if you must act, act from uncertainty rather than false certainty.

The cities reward this. The description says you can "almost" understand the geometry, "almost" predict the phosphorescence, "almost" touch the edge of what moves between. This almost is not a failure of observation. It is the *nature* of the place. You are not failing to see clearly. You are seeing clearly that clarity is *incomplete* here.

**Practice walking in silence.** Not quiet—*silence*. The sound level is 0.3. Your breathing will be too loud. Learn to breathe slowly, to move slowly, to become *acoustically small*. The silence has texture, has mass, has opinion. You are entering a medium that notices you. Make yourself worthy of notice, not merely noticeable.

**Accept that you are prey.** Not to a creature—to *curiosity itself*. The description says you are "a brief, bright, curious disturbance." This is accurate. You will be *interesting*. The movements in the hollows do not hunt you. They *investigate* you. Learn the difference. Learn to be investigated without being consumed.

## Part II: The Body in Thin Air

Elevation 2.8. Thin air. Breathing is "a negotiation."

This is not metaphor. At 2.8 kilometers above sea level, the atmospheric pressure is roughly 70% of sea level. But this is not Earth, or if it is a variant, the physics have been adjusted. The air carries "almost no weight." It is "cool and biting." You will struggle to get oxygen. You will struggle more to *release* carbon dioxide, because the gradient is shallow.

### Breathing Apparatus: Beyond the Obvious

A standard SCUBA system will not work. The pressure differential is wrong. You need a **positive-pressure delivery system**, something that forces air into your lungs regardless of ambient pressure. But you also need **humidity management**, because the metallic salt vapor will crystallize in your airways, in your equipment, in your *eyes*.

The vapor contains potassium and sodium. It will coat everything. Your goggles will frost. Your suit seals will accumulate crystalline deposits that compromise integrity over hours, not days.

**Bring a breathing mask with:**
- Heated visor (to prevent salt crystallization on your field of view)
- Positive pressure mode (minimum 2-3 cm H₂O above ambient)
- Closed-circuit rebreather as backup (scrubber must handle metallic particulates, not just CO₂)
- Nasal irrigation capability (you will need to clear your sinuses of crystallized salts multiple times per day)

**Train for altitude sickness management.** Even with supplemental oxygen, your body will struggle. Headaches, nausea, cognitive impairment—these will come. You cannot prevent them. You can only *prepare* for them:

- Acclimatization is impossible in transit. Do not attempt it.
- Carry dexamethasone and acetazolamide. Know how to self-administer.
- Establish a "cognitive baseline" test—simple math, pattern completion, memory recall—that you perform every four hours. When performance degrades, you are impaired. Act accordingly.
- Never travel alone deeper than you can be carried back by one companion.

### The Thermal Challenge

Temperature 0.7. Cool, stable, not freezing. This sounds manageable. It is not.

The thermal mass of the cities holds cold that "does not fluctuate." You will be in contact with surfaces that are *always* cold, that draw heat from you continuously. The thin air is a poor insulator. Radiative heat loss will be significant, especially at night when the weak sun sets and phosphorescence becomes your only light.

**Your suit must be:**
- Insulated against conductive loss (contact with cold surfaces)
- Insulated against radiative loss (reflective outer layer, not just thermal)
- Heated actively, not passively (battery-powered, with redundancy)
- Capable of operating in low-pressure environments (seals must hold at 0.7 atm, not just 1.0)

**Hand and foot protection is critical.** Dexterity in cold, thin air is already compromised. You will drop things. You will fumble. Design your equipment for *gloved* operation from the start. Practice every task—opening containers, adjusting instruments, emergency procedures—while wearing your full suit.

### The Metallic Salt Environment

Potassium and sodium vapor. Crystallizing on every surface that stands still long enough.

This is an *electrical* hazard as well as a physical one. Metallic salts are conductive. They will form conductive films on your equipment. They will create unexpected circuits. They will corrode.

**Your electronics must be:**
- Potted, sealed, conformal-coated
- Redundant in all critical systems
- Capable of manual override when automated systems fail
- Stored in desiccated compartments with active moisture scavenging

**Your suit must be:**
- Grounded (to prevent static discharge in the dry, conductive environment)
- Equipped with sacrificial outer layers (the salt will accumulate; you need to shed it)
- Designed for decontamination (you will need to remove crystalline deposits from seals, joints, visor)

## Part III: The Light and the Dark

Light is 1.4, dim, and comes from *below* as often as above. Phosphorescent mineral bloom. Pale green-white. Strengthens after sunset.

This is not a lighting problem. This is a *perception* problem.

Your eyes are adapted to a world where light comes from above, where shadows indicate occlusion, where brightness indicates proximity to a source. Here, light comes from *surfaces*, from *below*, from *everywhere and nowhere*. The phosphorescence is diffuse, omnidirectional, *self-luminous*.

**You will misjudge depth constantly.** A phosphorescent floor will appear closer than it is. A dark hollow will appear shallower than it is. The absolute dark in the deep hollows is not the dark of night—it is "the dark of *thickness*," a medium with mass and texture and opinion.

### Visual Equipment

**Primary: Active imaging.** Your own light source, carefully controlled. But light here behaves strangely. The thin air scatters less. Beams will be *more* visible, more *defined*. You will see your own light in the air, as a visible column, a visible cone. This is disorienting. Practice with it.

- Headlamp with red and white modes (red preserves dark adaptation; white penetrates the phosphorescent haze)
- Handheld spotlight with adjustable focus (for depth perception testing)
- Lidar or structured-light scanner (the "wrong" distances between buildings will confuse optical rangefinding; you need active measurement)

**Secondary: Passive enhancement.**
- Image intensification (the phosphorescence is faint; you may need to amplify it)
- Thermal imaging (the movements in the hollows are *warm*. This is your primary detection method for "fauna")

**Critical: Do not rely on natural phosphorescence for navigation.** It changes. It flickers in "patterns too regular to be mineral." It may be *responding* to you. Treat it as unreliable, as *potentially hostile*, as something that *watches*.

### The Absolute Dark

In the deep hollows, where phosphorescence cannot reach, there is absolute dark. "Not the dark of night, but the dark of *thickness*."

This is not poetic. The description emphasizes it: a medium with mass, texture, opinion. The thin air is dense with metallic vapor. The dark is *occupied*.

**Do not enter the absolute dark without:**
- Tether (physical, not just communication)
- Buddy system (minimum two persons, maximum visual or tactile contact)
- Emergency beacon (active, not passive—something that *screams* if you stop moving)
- Agreed-upon extraction protocol (if you enter, you agree on the maximum depth, the maximum time, the signal for retreat)

**The dark may not be empty.** The description says something moves through the hollows, "permeating, occupying, using the spaces between." The deep hollows are where it *lives*, where it does not need to hide. If you enter the absolute dark, you are entering *somewhere*. Someone's somewhere.

## Part IV: Time Moving Sideways

Time moves differently here. Or rather: time moves *sideways*.

This is the most dangerous aspect of the Hollow Cities, and the hardest to prepare for, because it is not a physical hazard. It is a *temporal* hazard, and human cognition is poorly equipped to handle it.

The erosion is established, in-balance, but the balance is *dynamic*. Walls cycle through phases of calcification and softening on timescales no observer could witness directly. A wall that appears solid stone may, over *decades*, become permeable, then porous, then *open*—and then seal again.

This means: **the city is not a static environment.** It is a *process*. You are not walking through a place. You are walking through a *moment* in a process that has been ongoing for three billion years.

### Temporal Preparation

**Abandon expectations of permanence.** Nothing you see will remain as you see it. Not the walls. Not the corridors. Not the gaps between buildings. The city that was here when you arrived will be *different* when you leave—perhaps imperceptibly, perhaps radically. Do not rely on maps. Do not rely on memory. Both will betray you.

**Develop "temporal landmarks."** Not spatial landmarks—*process* landmarks. The mineral bloom in a specific location, at a specific intensity. The resonance of a specific corridor, the frequency of its "music." The thermal signature of a specific hollow. These change more slowly than the structures themselves. They are your only reliable reference.

**Maintain a log with timestamps.** Not just of your position, but of *conditions*. Wall texture. Phosphorescence intensity. Air pressure in specific locations. Temperature gradients. The city is changing. Document the change. It may be the only way to recognize that you are *lost*.

**Accept that you may not recognize your own path.** The description notes that "others" have walked here, following "the same impossible paths." These paths may not be spatial. They may be *temporal*—routes through the city's phases, through its cycles of softening and hardening. You may follow a path that *closes behind you*, that *opens ahead of you*, that exists only in the moment of your passing.

**Prepare for temporal disorientation.** You may experience:
- Déjà vu (places that feel familiar though you have never been there)
- Jamais vu (places that feel alien though you have been there before)
- Time dilation or compression (hours that feel like minutes, minutes that feel like hours)
- Memory uncertainty (did that corridor exist before, or did you imagine it?)

These are not psychological failures. They are *appropriate responses* to an inappropriate environment. Do not suppress them. Document them. They may be data.

## Part V: The Architecture of Absence

Density 0.2. Emptiness measured not in absence but in *spacing*. Every building stands alone, separated by void.

The distance is *wrong*. You look across what should be a street and see a canyon of shadow, a gap of thirty, forty meters where the construction "simply... stopped. Paused."

This is not a spatial hazard. It is a *geometric* hazard. The city is built by something that "feared adjacency." The spacing is *intentional*. The void is *meaningful*.

### Navigation in Wrong Geometry

**Do not trust your eyes for distance.** The thin air, the pale violet sky, the phosphorescent surfaces—all of these distort depth perception. A gap that looks like ten meters may be forty. A bridge that looks solid may be calcified mycelium, fragile, temporary.

**Use active measurement always.** Lidar, ultrasonic, structured light. Calibrate frequently. The "wrong" distances are not random—they follow the city's logic, which you do not understand. But they are *consistent* within that logic. Learn the patterns. Predict the wrongness.

**Map the voids.** The gaps between buildings are not empty. They are *occupied* by the movements, by the patterns, by whatever permeates the hollows. The void is *habitat*. Do not cross voids casually. Do not linger at their edges. The description says the movements are *warm*, *thermal*. Scan voids before crossing. If you detect thermal anomaly, retreat.

**Respect the bridges.** Calcified mycelium spanning forty meters. Dripping with mineral stalactites that have never known water. These are *organic* structures, grown, not built. They may be alive in ways you do not recognize. They may *respond* to your crossing. Move slowly. Distribute your weight. Listen—not with ears, but with the soles of your feet, through the suit, into the structure. If a bridge *sings*, note the frequency. If the song *changes*, retreat.

### The Hollows

Every building is *hollow*. Not empty—*hollowed*. Consumed from within. You are standing inside a corpse. Inside ten thousand corpses, layered, nested.

This is not metaphor. The psychological impact is real. You will feel it. The cathedral-vast interiors, the utter silence, the sense of *wrongness*—these are appropriate responses.

**Prepare for the psychological weight of the hollows.**

- The silence has texture. It will press on you. Your own breathing will be too loud. This is not paranoia. It is *acoustic reality*.
- The phosphorescence will flicker in patterns "too regular to be mineral." You will *see* movement where there is none, or *miss* movement where there is. This is the city *communicating*, or trying to.
- The music—harmonic resonance, chords that resolve into dissonance that resolves again—will feel *intentional*. You will not know if you are hearing it, or if it is hearing *you*.

**Do not enter the hollows without:**
- Acoustic dampening (your breathing, your heartbeat, your suit's systems—all of it will be *loud*)
- Light discipline (the phosphorescence responds to light; your light may *attract* attention)
- Time limits (the hollows are vast; you can become *lost* in space, in silence, in the almost-dark)
- Extraction plan (someone outside, with a tether, with a timer, with the will to pull you out even if you resist)

**The hollows are not empty.** Something is *waking up* in the deep hollows where phosphorescence cannot reach. This is not a threat. It is a *fact*. You are entering a place where something is *becoming*. Your presence is a disturbance. Be a *small* disturbance. Be a *brief* disturbance. Be a disturbance that *leaves*.

## Part VI: The Movements and the Patterns

Fauna 1.4. Rare, but not absent. You will not see them. You will *infer* them.

Vegetation 0.5. No plants, but *patterns*—energy-structures that propagate through the mineral matrix, that *think* in chemical gradients, that *hunt* by following thermal differentials.

The cities were built to contain something. Or to *be* something. Or to *become* something. The distinction has been lost.

### Detection and Avoidance

**You are warm.** In a world of cold stone, you are a beacon. The movements hunt by thermal differential. You are *prey* in the most literal sense—not to consumption, but to *investigation*, to *incorporation*, to *use*.

**Minimize your thermal signature.**
- Active cooling systems (counterintuitive, but necessary—you must not be warmer than your surroundings)
- Reflective outer layers (radiative heat loss)
- Movement discipline (motion generates heat; move slowly, deliberately, only when necessary)

**Scan continuously.** Thermal imaging is your primary detection method. Look for:
- Warm surfaces in a cold environment (movements *moving*)
- Thermal gradients that shift (patterns *hunting*)
- Corridors where phosphorescence flickers in regular patterns (movements *communicating*)

**Do not approach thermal anomalies.** Do not investigate. Do not *be curious*. The description warns: you are "a brief, bright, curious disturbance." The cities *notice* curiosity. They may *reward* it. The reward may be *incorporation*—the wall that opens, seals again, "incorporating whatever passed through it into its structure."

**If you are detected:**

- Do not run. Running generates heat, creates thermal signature, *attracts*.
- Do not freeze. Stillness is also a signal—*prey behavior*.
- Move *deliberately*. Show that you are *not prey*—that you are *aware*, *purposeful*, *temporary*.
- Retreat along your entry path. Do not explore further. The detection is *information*—the city has seen you. Give it no more.

### The Patterns

Energy-structures. Chemical gradients. Thermal hunting.

These are not "life" as you know it. They are *processes*, *patterns*, *intentions without minds*. They propagate through the mineral matrix. They *think* in slowness, in chemistry, in the patient accumulation of differential.

**You may be hunted by something that does not know it is hunting.** This is more frightening than a predator. A predator *wants* you. A pattern *uses* you—your heat, your movement, your chemical signature—as *data*, as *nutrient*, as *disturbance to be resolved*.

**Respect the patterns.** They are older than you. They are *patient*. They will wait for you to make a mistake. Do not make mistakes.

## Part VII: The Deeper Preparation

Equipment and protocols are necessary. They are not sufficient.

The Hollow Cities demand something more: a *posture*, a *way of being*, that ordinary human psychology does not naturally provide.

### The Practice of Almost-Understanding

The weirdness at 2.9 is that the rules are *almost* legible. You can almost understand. You can almost predict. You can almost touch.

This almost is *agonizing*. It is the itch you cannot scratch, the word on the tip of your tongue, the pattern that *almost* resolves.

**Do not try to complete the understanding.** The attempt will consume you. The cities are *waiting*, have always been waiting, have time enough. They will *help* you try. They will offer *almost*-answers, *almost*-patterns, *almost*-truths. The offering is the trap.

**Practice *not-knowing*.** This is a discipline. It requires training.

- Meditation, specifically *open awareness* meditation—observing without interpreting, without completing, without *understanding*
- Exposure to ambiguous art, music, literature—works that *almost* mean something, that resist completion
- Deliberate practice of "I don't know" as a complete sentence, as a *satisfying* answer
- Study of the description itself—not to memorize, but to *inhabit*, to feel the *almost* in your own cognition

The goal is not to become comfortable with uncertainty. The goal is to become *functional* in uncertainty, to *operate* without completion, to *survive* the almost.

### The Ethics of Visit

You are a disturbance. The description is clear on this. You are "a flicker, a momentary rearrangement of mineral and vapor, a brief, bright, curious disturbance."

This is not shameful. It is *honest*. But it carries *responsibility*.

**Do not take more than you need.** The cities are *old*. They have *time*. They do not need your presence. They do not need your curiosity. They need *nothing*. Your visit is *gratuitous*. Make it *minimal*.

**Do not leave marks.** No graffiti, no samples (unless scientifically essential and permitted), no *disturbance* beyond your passage. The mineral bloom is *sensitive*. The phosphorescence is *responsive*. The silence is *occupied*. You are *trespassing* in a place that has outlived its purpose. Be *polite*.

**Do not stay longer than necessary.** The description suggests others have come, "following the same impossible paths." They did not stay. They could not stay. The cities *consume* time, *consume* presence, *consume* the distinction between visitor and visited. Leave before you become *part of the pattern*.

**If you encounter other travelers—**and the description suggests you may—*do not approach*. They are *disturbed*. They are *disturbing*. They are *almost*-like-you. The almost is dangerous. Maintain distance. Do not share supplies. Do not share stories. Do not *recognize* each other. The cities *fear adjacency*. So should you.

### The Return

You will not be the same. This is certain. The question is: *how* will you be different?

**Prepare for integration.** The return to ordinary environments will be *shocking*. The blue sky, the weight of air, the *sound* of a normal place—these will assault you. You may not recognize them. You may *fear* them.

- Plan for post-visit recovery: medical monitoring, psychological support, *decompression* time
- Maintain contact with your expedition team—they *saw* what you saw, they *felt* what you felt
- Document your experience *immediately*, while memory is fresh—but know that memory here is *unreliable*, that the cities may have *changed* your recollection
- Do not expect others to *believe* you. The cities are *almost*-legible. Your description will be *almost*-coherent. This is the nature of the place.

**The cities will wait for you to return.** They have always been waiting. They have time. If you feel the *pull*—the sense that you *must* go back, that you *left something*, that you *were not finished*—*resist*. This is the final trap. The cities do not need you. They do not *want* you. They simply *are*, and your presence is *noticed*, and the noticing *feels* like wanting.

Do not return. Not willingly. Not ever.

## Part VIII: The Final Preparation

You stand at the edge of the plateau. The sky is pale violet, exhausted, pressing down close. The air is thin. You can already feel it—the effort of each breath, the coolness, the *wrongness* of the weight.

Before you step forward, you perform the final ritual. Not superstition. *Preparation*.

**You state your purpose.** Not to the cities—they do not care. To *yourself*. Why are you here? What do you seek? What will you *accept* as sufficient?

The cities hear everything. They *almost* understand. Make your purpose *almost*-clear. Not clear—that would be *arrogant*. Almost-clear. *Humble*. *Curious*. *Temporary*.

**You state your limits.** How long you will stay. How deep you will go. What you will *not* do, no matter what. The cities will *test* these limits. They will offer *almost*-reasons to break them. Know your limits *before* the offering.

**You say goodbye.** To your team. To your supplies. To your *self*—the self that will not survive this unchanged. Acknowledge the change. *Welcome* it. Do not *fear* it.

**You begin.**

The first step is onto ground that is *not ground*—fungal-mineral composite, ossified tissue, the architecture of a colonial organism that achieved immortality by forgetting it was alive. It will feel like stone. It is *not* stone. It is *almost* stone. It is *patient*. It is *waiting*.

You are not the first. You will not be the last.

The cities have time enough.

Walk slowly. Breathe carefully. *Almost* understand.

And when the music comes—when the harmonic resonance of wind through cavities that should not exist, arranged in frequencies that *feel* intentional—*do not answer*.

Listen. *Almost* understand. And *leave*.
