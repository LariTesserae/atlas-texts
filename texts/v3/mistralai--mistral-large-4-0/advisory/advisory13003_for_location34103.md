# Preparation for the Ringing Plateau

## Before You Climb

You do not prepare for the Ringing Plateau with supplies. Supplies are nearly useless. You prepare with *release*.

Begin by learning to hear what you cannot hear. Spend weeks in ordinary high places—mountain passes, exposed ridges, anywhere the air thins and the wind has room to speak. Train your ears to distinguish frequencies, to feel vibration in your teeth and sternum before it becomes audible sound. The plateau's thrumming will live in your body before your mind can name it. If you cannot yet feel a low tone in your chest when you stand in a cathedral, when you press your palm to a running engine, when you stand near a waterfall—you are not ready.

Practice *not walking*. The paths upward are agreements with wind, routes that shift. You must learn to move without intention, to let your body follow currents it cannot see. Stand in strong wind until you stop fighting it. Let it push you sideways, backward, let it *teach* your muscles what yielding feels like. The plateau will not permit travelers who insist on their own direction.

Practice *thirst without drinking*. The water here is treacherous. You must be able to endure need, to distinguish between bodily want and *attunement*. Drink only when you have listened. This takes discipline you cannot imagine until you need it.

## What You Carry

**Ear protection that does not block sound.** This sounds impossible. It is not. You need *filtering*, not silence—materials that dampen the highest frequencies while letting the subsonic thrumming through. Waxed cloth, layered felt, the densest wool you can find, wrapped and wrapped again. You must hear the plateau *without being destroyed by it*. Test this in ordinary wind first. If you cannot hear speech through your protection in a storm, it is too much. If you can hear the wind clearly, it is too little.

**A listening stone.** A small, smooth piece of quartz or flint, carried against your skin. When you need to know if water is safe, if ground is singing, if a path is true—you press the stone to the surface and *feel*. The stone will transmit vibration to your palm, your arm, your chest. Learn its language. A safe pool hums in a narrow band. A memory-pool sings in *layers*, frequencies that do not resolve, that *ache*. Polished stone that will lift you sings in *rising* tones, a pressure that wants to carry you upward.

**A mirror, small and unbreakable.** Not for vanity. For *seeing sound*. The harmonics become visible as pressure-distortions in the thin air. Your mirror, held at the right angle, will catch these ripples, show you what your eyes cannot parse. Learn to read them. A *settling* pattern means the wind is dying. A *spiraling* pattern means harmonics are building—seek shelter, or *ride* them if you dare. A *flat* pattern, no distortion at all, means you stand on dead stone, safe, but *deaf*—the plateau is not speaking to you here.

**Rope that is not rope.** Something that will not conduct vibration. Silk, perhaps, or braided hair, or a material from somewhere else in the multiverse, something with properties you do not fully understand. Ordinary rope will *sing*, will transmit the plateau's frequencies directly into your body. You will dream the plateau's dreams whether you sleep or not. You will wake *shifted*.

**A tent that is not a tent.** Something that *dampens* rather than blocks. The plateau dreams through its vents, and its dreams are hungry. You need a space where the subsonic pressure is reduced, where your body can rest without being *worked* on. Test this. Sleep inside your shelter in ordinary wind, in low-frequency environments—near heavy machinery, in caves, anywhere the air carries vibration. If you wake *moved*, if you wake with stones in your mouth, if you wake *hollow*—your shelter is not sufficient.

**Food that requires no preparation.** The plateau will not permit fire. The thin air will not sustain flame, and even if it did, the wind would *sing* it into something else, something that consumes rather than warms. Eat dried things, things that need only moisture, things that do not *change* when you eat them. The plateau is *stable*. Your food must be stable too. Anything that ferments, that transforms, that *lives*—will become part of the plateau's metabolism whether you intend it or not.

**Nothing metal.** Metal sings. Metal *resonates*. A belt buckle, a knife, a single coin in your pocket—it will become an instrument, will amplify frequencies until you are *screaming* in a voice you do not have. Leave metal below. Carry stone tools, bone tools, tools of wood that has been *dead* long enough to stop singing.

## What You Wear

Layer upon layer, but not for warmth. For *buffering*.

The outermost layer must be *bright* and *moving*. The singers are curious, sometimes hungry, sometimes *playful*. A membrane-catcher who mistakes you for prey will *surf* your body, and this is not pleasant. Bright colors, flowing fabrics, things that catch light and wind and make you *visible* as a thing that is *not stone*, that is *not plateau*. You want to be *obviously* temporary, obviously *not belonging*, obviously *passing through*.

Beneath this, layers that *absorb*. The auroras pulse with frequencies that make your bones ache. You cannot stop this. You can only *cushion* it, spread the pressure across more of your body, make it *bearable* rather than *transforming*.

Against your skin, something that *grounds* you. A cord of braided hair, worn like a belt or a necklace, touching skin at multiple points. The plateau wants to *lift* you, to *float* you, to make your movements *wrong*. This cord reminds your body where it belongs. It will not stop the buoyancy. It will stop you from *forgetting* yourself in it.

Your feet must be *bare* or nearly so. You need to feel the stone. The polished surfaces that will lift you, the rough surfaces that will hold you—you must distinguish them by touch before your eyes can process what your ears are screaming. Thin soles, worn nearly through, leather that has gone soft with use. You will lose them. Bring duplicates, or accept that you will walk on stone by the end, and prepare your feet for this by walking on stone for weeks before you climb.

## The Climb Itself

The ascent takes days because the paths are *agreements*. This is not metaphor. You are literally negotiating with weather, with wind patterns, with the *mood* of the air itself. You must learn to read the sky as others read faces.

A *good* path day is one where the wind comes from the direction you need to travel, at a velocity that supports rather than fights your movement. You will wait for these days. You will camp in scrub, in lichen, in *nothing*, for days or weeks, watching the wind, feeling its *intention*, learning when it will turn.

On a good day, you walk *with* the wind, and the path reveals itself—a line of disturbed lichen, a depression in the scrub, a *lean* in the stones that points upward. You follow this. You do not *choose* your route. The wind chooses. You *agree*.

As you rise, the air thins, and your body will *complain*. The buoyancy begins here, subtle at first, a lightness that feels like strength. Do not mistake this for fitness. It is the beginning of *wrongness*. Your movements should feel *slightly wrong*, slightly *buoyant*. If they feel *right*, if you feel *strong*, if you feel *capable*—you are not paying attention. The plateau is already *working* on you.

Drink sparingly. The water you carry will run out. You will need to find moisture, to *mine* it from the air, from the stone, from your own breath. Practice this below. Learn to condense water from air, to *squeeze* moisture from lichen, to drink your own sweat collected and filtered. The plateau rewards self-sufficiency. It *punishes* dependence.

Sleep lightly. The wind dreams even at lower elevations, and its dreams are *restless*. You will wake to find yourself moved, to find your camp *arranged* differently, to find *stones* where your gear was. This is normal. Do not fear it yet. But *notice* it. The plateau is learning your patterns. It is *testing* whether you will notice, whether you will *adapt*, whether you are *worthy* of arrival.

## The Final Approach

The last day is the hardest. The air is so thin that each breath is *work*. The sound begins here—a low thrumming, felt more than heard, a *presence* in your chest that will not leave.

You will see the vent-families before you see the plateau proper. Clustered holes in stone, drilled by water and time, singing in the wind. The closer you come, the louder they become, until you are *shouting* to be heard by any companion, until you are *gesturing*, until you are *screaming* in a voice that has no volume.

This is the *threshold*. The plateau is *announcing* you. It is *testing* whether you will turn back. Many do. Many *should*. If you feel *fear* here, *genuine terror*, *the certainty that you will die*—turn back. The plateau will let you descend by the way you came, if you have not yet *arrived*, if you have not yet stood on polished stone, if you have not yet *heard yourself* in the song.

If you feel *awe*, *wonder*, *the certainty that you will be changed but not destroyed*—continue. This is the plateau *choosing* you. It does not choose many. It does not need to.

## Arrival

You will know the moment you arrive. The stone beneath your feet will *change*. One step on ordinary rock, the next on *polished* stone, and the difference is *immediate*, *total*, *irreversible*.

The thrumming *intensifies*. It was in your chest. Now it is in your *bones*, your *skull*, your *teeth*. The auroras appear—sheets of color in the black sky, *pulsing*, *deafening*, *beautiful*.

And you will *see* the singers. The lizard-things with membranes, *surfing* the harmonics, rising and falling in the acoustic currents. They will look at you. They will *know* you. They are *old*, older than your species, older than your world, and they have seen *thousands* of travelers arrive, *thousands* become part of the song.

Stand still. Do not walk. The first rule is *absolute*: do not walk where the stone is polished. The polish is *singing*, and singing is *lift*, and lift is *falling*.

Let your body *adjust*. The buoyancy will feel *natural* soon. You will forget that your movements are *wrong*. This is the danger. You must *remember* it. You must *notice* the wrongness, *catalog* it, *resist* it even as you adapt to it.

Listen. Truly listen. The plateau is *speaking* to you in a language older than words, older than *meaning*. It is telling you where the water is safe, where the paths go sideways, where the walkers are *waiting*. It is telling you *whether you will stay or leave*, though it will not tell you *which* you should choose. It *never* chooses for you. It only *shows*.

## What You Must Learn On-Site

**The geography of sound.** The plateau is not flat. It is *organized* by acoustics. vent-families of similar diameter resonate together—learn to recognize these clusters by their *chords*, their *characteristic frequencies*. Some families sing *low*, subsonic, felt in the marrow. Others sing *high*, audible, *painful*. The walkers hunt near the low families, where the deep singers grow. The singers *surf* above the high families, where the lift is strongest.

Map the plateau *acoustically*. Close your eyes. Listen. The soundscape *is* the map. A *rising* harmonic means polished stone, danger. A *steady* drone means rough stone, safety. A *complex* chord, many frequencies interweaving, means a vent-family—approach with *caution*, with *reverence*, with the *listening stone* pressed to the ground.

**The water-ritual.** Before drinking from any pool, press your listening stone to the surface. *Feel* it. A safe pool hums in a narrow band, a single frequency or a simple chord. A *memory*-pool sings in *layers*, frequencies that do not resolve, that *ache* with accumulated time. If you feel this—*do not drink*. Walk on. The plateau has *infinite* pools, temporary as they are. You do not need this one.

If the pool is safe, drink *slowly*. Sip. Let the water *tell* you what it is. The taste is *mineral and time*, but there is more—if you listen, you may taste *where* the water has been, *what* it has touched, *how long* it has waited. This is *information*. Use it.

**Sleep discipline.** Never sleep where the wind sings loudest. This means: never sleep on polished stone, never sleep near vent-families, never sleep where the auroras are *thickest*, where the sound-made-visible is *brightest*. Find *dead* stone, rough and unpolished, away from the singing. Even here, use your dampening tent. Even here, sleep with your grounding cord against your skin. Set *multiple* alarms—mechanical, not electronic, things that will make *sound* loud enough to wake you if the plateau begins to *move* you.

And *dream carefully*. The plateau's dreams are *hungry*. If you dream of *falling upward*, of *rising through stone that becomes softer*, of *singing without a voice*—wake immediately. Check your position. Check your body. If you find *stones* where you should be flesh, if you find your memories *layered*, overlapping, *wrong*—you have been *touched*. The plateau has begun its work. Resist, or *accept*, but *choose*.

**The walking rules.** You may walk on rough stone. You may walk near, but not *on*, vent-families. You must *never* walk on polished stone—unless you *intend* to be lifted, and know how to *fall*.

Learn the *lift* before you risk it. Stand at the edge of a polished zone, where the stone transitions from rough to smooth. Feel the *pressure* building, the *buoyancy* increasing. Step back. Step back again. The lift is *addictive*. It feels like *freedom*, like *flight*, like *escape* from the weight of ordinary existence. Many travelers *choose* it, step onto the polish, *ride* the harmonics upward, and are *lost*—drowning upward, pulled into the black sky, *changed* or *killed* or *added to the song* in ways no one can predict.

If you must cross polished stone—and sometimes you must—do it *fast*. Do not *linger*. Do not *look up* at the auroras while you walk. Do not *listen* to the beauty. Move. *Survive*. The plateau will try to *keep* you, to make you *part of its equilibrium*. Your only defense is *speed*, *decision*, *the refusal to be beautiful*.

## The Walkers

You will see them. Perhaps not at first—they are *patient*, they are *stone*, they wait for decades in shapes that mimic the plateau's own formations. But eventually, if you stay long enough, you will see one *move*. A shape that was *rock* becomes *limbs*. A formation that was *geology* becomes *intention*. The walker *unfolds*, *extends*, *reaches* into a vent-hole, *consumes* the deep singers.

They will not *attack* you. This is not their nature. They are *part of the plateau's metabolism*, and you are *not yet* part of that metabolism. If you are *lucky*, they will *ignore* you. If you are *unlucky*, they will *notice* you, and their curiosity will be *geological*—slow, *patient*, *hungry*.

Do not *run* from a walker. Running triggers *pursuit*, and though the walker is slow, it is *inevitable*, it is *stone*, it will *flow* toward you across hours or days, and there is nowhere to hide on a plateau that is *all surface*.

Instead, *stand still*. Make yourself *uninteresting*. The walker hunts by *sound*, by *vibration*, by the *disturbance* you make in the plateau's equilibrium. If you stand *perfectly still*, if you *breathe* slowly, if you *think* slowly, you become *almost* stone, *almost* part of the landscape, and the walker may *pass*, may *wait*, may *forget* you in its long patience.

If the walker *approaches*, if it *unfolds* near you, if you feel the *pressure* of its attention—*speak* to it. Not words. *Sound*. Hum a low tone, match your frequency to the vent-family nearest you, *become* part of the song. The walker *understands* sound. It has *eaten* sound for ten thousand years. If you *sing*—even badly, even *wrongly*—you are *not prey*. You are *environment*. You are *weather*.

## The Departure

You do not leave by the path you came. This is *absolute*. The paths that lead up are *one-way*, agreements with the wind that only function in ascension. If you try to descend by your own route, you will *loop*, returning to the same vent-family, the same polished stone, the same *moment*, until you *stop trying*, until you *accept* the plateau's terms.

The paths down are *sideways*. They follow the harmonics, *ride* the sound to somewhere else, somewhere *down* though "down" is not where you expect.

To find them, you must *listen*. Truly listen, with your whole body, until you hear the *subtext* in the plateau's song. The wind sings *many* things at once—melody and warning, beauty and hunger, *welcome* and *farewell*. The farewell is *there*, always, a *current* in the acoustic environment that leads *away*, that *descends*, that *returns* you to the world below.

Follow this current. Walk *with* it, not against it. Let it *carry* you, even when it feels *wrong*, even when it feels like *falling*, even when it feels like *drowning*. The plateau is *releasing* you. It has *used* you, *tested* you, *changed* you, and now it is *letting go*.

The descent is *faster* than the ascent, but it is *harder*. The paths that go down are *narrow*, *precarious*, following harmonics that *shift* without warning. You must *ride* them, *surf* them, *trust* the sound to hold you even when it *drops*, even when it *fails*, even when you are *falling* and the only thing beneath you is *wind* that *sings*.

When you reach the lower zones—scrub, then forest, then *ordinary* ground—you will *stumble*. The weight will return. The *thickness* of ordinary air will feel like *drowning*, like *pressure*, like *stone*. You will *miss* the thinness, the *lightness*, the *wrongness* that felt like *freedom*.

And you will *carry* the thrumming. In your bones. In your *dreams*. In the *silence* between heartbeats, you will hear the plateau's song, *faint*, *persistent*, *calling*.

Some travelers *return*. They cannot *help* it. The plateau *chooses*, and once it has chosen, it *remembers*. The song is in you now. It is *part of you*. You will *hear* it in ordinary wind, in ordinary stone, in the *silence* of ordinary nights.

You will *want* to go back. You will *resist*. This is the *test*. The plateau does not *need* you. It is *complete*, *eternal*, *singing* without you. But it *wants* you, in its way, it *remembers* you, it *waits* for you with the patience of stone, the *hunger* of wind, the *beauty* of sound-made-visible.

If you return, you will find the plateau *changed* and *unchanged*. The harmonics will have *shifted*, the vent-families will have *grown*, the walkers will have *moved*—but the *song* will be the *same*, the *eternal* song of stone and wind and time-that-waits.

And you will *know*, finally, that you were never *visiting*.

You were *becoming*.

Part of the equilibrium. Part of the *now*. Part of the *sound* that makes the stone *live*, that makes the wind *mean*, that makes the *top of the world* a place where *being* is *possible*.
