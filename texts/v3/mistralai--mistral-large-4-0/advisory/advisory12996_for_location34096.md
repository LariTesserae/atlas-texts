# A Traveler's Preparation for the Drownthold of Velmora

## Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before you pack a single item, you must *unlearn*. The Drownthold is not a place you visit. It is a place that visits *you*—that begins its patient work of conversion the moment you cross its threshold. Your preparation must be as much psychological as physical, because Velmora will break the assumptions that made you a functional human being in any other world.

The first truth: **you will never be dry again.** Not merely damp. Not merely humid. You will become *saturated*—a walking sponge in an atmosphere that is itself liquid, that exchanges moisture with your body across every surface, every breath, every pore. Prepare for this. Mourn it, if you must. Then prepare.

---

## The Body: Rebuilding Yourself for Saturation

### Skin and Barrier

Your skin is your first casualty. In Velmora, the distinction between "inside" and "outside" dissolves. The air carries water the way desert air carries dust—visible, particulate, *inhaled*. Your skin will macerate, soften, become permeable. This is not merely uncomfortable; it is dangerous. Macerated skin tears. Tears become infected in an environment riddled with spores, with the digestive chemistry of ten thousand fungal species, with the constant biological activity of a place that is *alive* in its decomposition.

**Preparation:**

- **Train your skin.** Months before departure, begin exposing yourself to prolonged moisture. Long baths. Humid environments. Let your skin learn the texture of wetness without the panic of unfamiliarity. This is not adaptation—you cannot adapt to Velmora's saturation—but it is *acclimation*, the psychological and physiological rehearsal.

- **Study the Velmoran skin treatments.** They do not use oils, which would trap moisture against the body and accelerate maceration. They use *resins*—specific plant resins, seventeen waterproofing formulations known to them, that create a breathable barrier, a membrane that allows gas exchange while resisting liquid penetration. You will need to learn these. You will need to carry the knowledge, if not the materials, to make them.

- **Accept the laceweed.** Eventually, you will wear it. Not as costume, but as *interface*. The Velmorans treat laceweed the way you treat clothing—woven, cut, layered, replaced as it grows and sheds. You will learn to feel naked without it, to sense the wrongness of bare skin exposed to the wet air. Begin now: find plant fibers that can be wetted and dried repeatedly without degrading. Practice wearing them against your skin. Imagine never removing them.

### Breathing: The Delicate Art of Wet Air

At elevation 1.5, the air is thin—thinner than you are accustomed to, if you come from lowlands. Yet it is saturated, each inhalation pulling droplets into your lungs. The temperature (0.7) means this moisture never warms. Your breath mists constantly. You will cough. You will adapt, but never fully.

**Preparation:**

- **Cardiovascular conditioning.** The thin air demands efficiency. Train at altitude, if you can. Train until your breathing is deep and deliberate, a choice rather than a reflex.

- **Learn to breathe wet.** Practice inhaling steam. Practice the sensation of moisture in your airways, the slight resistance, the need to clear. The Velmorans do not fight this; they *inhabit* it, letting the wet air become part of their internal landscape.

- **Carry a respirator?** Only for emergencies. The Velmorans do not use them. To breathe through a filter is to refuse the place, to maintain a boundary that Velmora will erode anyway. Better to learn to breathe *with* the wetness, to let your lungs become as saturated as your skin, to trust the body's capacity to process what it cannot reject.

### Temperature: The Permanent Shiver

Velmora is cold—not arctic, not lethal in itself, but *persistent*. The 0.7 temperature is the temperature of a place that has never known warmth, that has no memory of it. You will shiver. You will shiver for days, for weeks. Your body will burn calories maintaining temperature it cannot maintain. You will be hungry constantly.

**Preparation:**

- **Cold exposure training.** Deliberate, controlled, repeated. Let your body learn the shiver, learn to function within it, learn to find the narrow band of comfort at the edge of hypothermia.

- **Metabolic preparation.** You will need calories—*dense* calories, fats and proteins that burn slowly. The Velmoran diet is rich in bladder-farm products, in peat-worm protein, in the fermented pulps that provide both nutrition and the alcohol that helps maintain morale. Train your digestive system for unfamiliar foods, for the constant fermentation, for eating what grows rather than what is killed.

- **Fire-making under duress.** In Velmora, fire is sacred. A fire that goes out is a catastrophe. You must be able to make fire *wet*—to carry embers through mist and rain, to coax flame from materials that have never known dryness. Practice this obsessively. Your survival may depend on maintaining a coal through days of saturation.

## The Mind: Rewiring Perception

### Vision: Learning to See Green

You will lose the sky. This is not metaphor. In Velmora, the canopy is continuous, multi-layered, *alive*. Light filters through it in shades of green and gold, shifting with humidity, with the mood of the laceweed, with thermal currents you cannot feel. Normal light (1.8) becomes *filtered* light, and the filtering transforms everything.

Depth perception will fail you. The mist distorts distance. The green-gold light flattens contrast. You will misjudge steps, stumble, learn to move with the hesitation of someone navigating by touch rather than sight.

**Preparation:**

- **Train in low, colored light.** Visit forests at dusk. Visit greenhouses. Practice moving through spaces where normal color cues are absent, where you must rely on texture, on sound, on the memory of your body's position.

- **Learn echolocation.** Not literally—though the Velmorans have developed sophisticated auditory navigation—but practice *attending* to sound. Close your eyes in unfamiliar spaces. Let the wet roar of Velmora (2.1) become information rather than noise. The pop of bladder-farms. The rustle of laceweed in specific wind directions. The lowing of bog-elk at depths that indicate water movement.

- **Accept disorientation.** Velmora does not permit the certainty of "I am here." The ground shifts. The mist moves. The light changes. You must learn to hold location loosely, to navigate by relationship rather than by coordinate.

### Sound: The Dense Information Environment

Sound in Velmora is *wet*. It arrives already waterlogged, dense, continuous. The loudness (2.1) is not sharp but *thick*—a roar that never stops, that carries information you must learn to parse. You will strain to hear speech. You will learn to speak louder, to use the structured calls of the Velmoran language of place.

**Preparation:**

- **Auditory training.** Spend time in environments of continuous noise—near waterfalls, in dense forests, in urban centers. Learn to extract signal from roar. Learn to hear the *structure* beneath the texture.

- **Study the Velmoran call system.** They have developed a language of place, calls and responses that carry information about location, safety, peat mood. You cannot learn this fully before arrival, but you can begin. Learn the acoustic signatures of the bladder-farm pop, of laceweed in different wind conditions, of bog-elk at different depths. These will be your map.

- **Prepare for sensory fatigue.** The wet roar will exhaust you. You will need rest—not silence, which does not exist, but *reduction*, spaces where the sound is less dense, where you can recover the capacity to attend.

### The Ground: Trust and Its Absence

In Velmora, the ground is *unreliable*. The peat is saturated, unstable, capable of *bog-surge*—sudden heaving, release of black water and semi-digested matter, swallowing what was standing there. The people build on root-rafts, platforms of living wood that rise with the flood, that shift, that drift.

You will learn to test every step. You will feel the peat breathe beneath you, the slow digestion of ten thousand years of accumulated wet. You will learn the difference between stable ground and ground that will release water, that will heave, that will *remember* your weight and respond.

**Preparation:**

- **Study unstable ground.** Visit bogs, marshes, wetlands. Learn to read the surface—the color, the texture, the way vegetation indicates depth and stability. Learn to move on ground that shifts, that releases, that responds to your presence.

- **Practice floating.** Not swimming—Velmora's water is too dense with organic matter, too shallow in places, too full of life. Practice *being carried*. Rafts, in other worlds. The sensation of yielding to a surface rather than pressing against it. This is how you will move through Velmora: not walking, but *negotiating*.

- **Learn the surge.** You cannot prevent it. You can only prepare for it—to recognize its signs (the bog-elk lowing at depth, the particular quality of mist, the change in the laceweed's movement), to be on a root-raft when it comes, to accept the displacement, the drift, the days of mist-navigation that may follow.

## The Gear: What to Carry, What to Become

### What You Cannot Bring

- **Metal that rusts.** Iron is useless in Velmora. It greens, flakes, becomes part of the peat within weeks. If you bring metal, bring copper—which the laceweed will grow *around*, incorporating into living structure—or bring none.

- **Leather.** It rots. It molds. It becomes part of the wet green dark within days. The Velmorans do not use it. Neither will you.

- **Paper.** All records in Velmora are kept on cured bladder-membrane, on peat tablets carved and resin-sealed. You will learn these materials. You will forget paper.

- **The expectation of dryness.** This is not gear. This is the fundamental reorientation that makes all other preparation possible.

### What You Must Bring/Become

**Fire:**
- Ember-keeping technology. Clay cores, multiple layers of insulation, the knowledge to pass fire from structure to structure like a sacrament. A fire that goes out is a minor catastrophe. You must be *unable* to let it go out.
- The resins that burn clean. Bladder-farm gas, blue in simple clay lamps. You will learn to cultivate this, to tend the colonies, to harvest without destroying.
- The specific resins for smoke. Mosquito-analogues in clouds so dense they darken the light. Only smoke from specific resins controls them. You must know which, must carry the knowledge if not the material.

**Waterproofing:**
- The seventeen formulations. Plant resins that create breathable barriers. You will need to identify local equivalents, to learn the chemistry of the laceweed, the bladder-membranes, the leathery skins of peat-worms.
- The patience to apply them, to maintain them, to accept that they are temporary, that they must be renewed, that dryness is a *process* not a state.

**Navigation:**
- No compass—magnetic fields are unreliable in the peat, and the mist distorts direction anyway. You will navigate by sound, by smell, by the particular quality of mist over different depths of peat.
- A sounding tool. Something to probe the peat, to test its depth, its stability, its willingness to release water. Bone, probably. The Velmorans use bone for everything—it is abundant, it works, it eventually becomes part of the peat too.

**Food:**
- The knowledge to cultivate. You cannot carry enough food for a long stay. You must learn to farm bladder-farms, to harvest laceweed, to gather peat-worms, to ferment the pulps that sustain.
- The digestive adaptation. Your gut must learn this food. Begin now: fermented foods, unfamiliar proteins, the constant presence of moisture in everything you eat. Your body will change. Let it.

**Clothing:**
- Nothing you own will survive. Everything you bring will be *replaced* by laceweed, by bladder-membrane, by the treated fibers of the place. Bring materials to make the transition—tools, knowledge, the willingness to be naked in the wet green dark until you have learned to wear the place itself.

## The Social: Becoming Velmoran

### The People

The people of Velmora are few, and they are *grown* into their place. They do not welcome you. They do not reject you. They *process* you, as they process everything. Your value to them is not your difference but your *incorporation*—your willingness to become wet, to become part of the architecture, to accept that death here is not an ending but a *transformation*, a becoming-soil, a becoming-laceweed, a becoming-walls.

**Preparation:**

- **Learn the language of place.** Not just words, but the structured calls that carry information about location and safety. Practice making sounds that carry in wet air, that can be distinguished from the roar.

- **Prepare to be *used*.** Your labor, your body, your eventual death—all are resources in Velmora. This is not exploitation; it is *participation*, the way a coral reef uses the calcium of dead polyps to build new structure. Accept this, or leave before you arrive.

- **Learn the names.** The buildings have names. The colony trees have names. The hanger colonies have territories that are *negotiated*, generation by generation. You will be given a name, or you will take one, or you will be nameless and therefore *provisional*, not yet incorporated.

### The Hangers

The hangers are not pets. They are not wild. They are *neighbors*, with their own territories in the vertical space, their own negotiations with human-built structures. They will watch you. They will defend their space. Given time, fermented bladder-farm pulp, they will appear at windows, will *attend*.

**Preparation:**

- **Learn to observe without threatening.** The hangers are warm-blooded, numerous, *present*. They will study you as you study them. Do not reach for them. Do not try to tame them. Offer, if you have, and accept refusal without offense.

- **Respect the vertical.** The hangers occupy the laceweed curtains, the space above human height. Do not intrude. Learn to move through the lower space, to accept that the upper space belongs to others, that you are *below* in a world that is fundamentally vertical.

### The Bog-Elk

Larger things move in the deep peat. The bog-elk, or something resembling them—massive, slow, with antlers of compressed peat grown over decades. They are not hunted. They cannot be hunted; the peat will not support pursuit. They are *avoided*, their paths learned, their lowing taken as warning of surges to come.

**Preparation:**

- **Learn to avoid.** This is not cowardice; it is *wisdom*, the recognition that some presences in Velmora are too large, too slow, too *integrated* to confront. Their paths are part of the map. Their lowing is information.

- **Prepare to be *small*.** You will feel small in Velmora. The colony trees are geological. The peat is ancient. The hangers are numerous. The bog-elk are *massive*. Let yourself be small. It is safer. It is more honest.

## The Spiritual: What Velmora Does to You

### The Loss of Dryness

You will mourn dryness. Not as abstract concept, but as *memory*—the memory of skin that dried, of clothes that hung and became light, of the particular quality of air that carries no water, that asks nothing of your body. This mourning is real. Let it happen. Then let it go, because Velmora will not permit you to hold it.

### The Gain of Wet

You will gain something in exchange. The Velmorans have a word for it, though it does not translate—something like *inhabitation*, the sense of being *inside* a place rather than *on* it, of being saturated with meaning the way you are saturated with moisture. You will feel it, eventually. The green-gold light will become *home*. The wet roar will become *silence*. The constant shiver will become *presence*.

### The Compost

You will think about death differently. In Velmora, death is *incorporation*. The Velmorans compost their dead deliberately, in chambers where peat-worms and fungal colonies are concentrated, where the body becomes soil becomes laceweed becomes, eventually, the walls of new construction. To be built into a house after death is the highest honor. To be forgotten, to be left where surges might take you, is the only true tragedy.

**Preparation:**

- **Visit a composting facility.** Not metaphorically—actually. Understand what deliberate decomposition means, the patience it requires, the *gift* of becoming something else.

- **Write your incorporation.** Where do you want to become? What structure? What function? The Velmorans keep records on bladder-membrane, on peat tablets. You will keep yours somewhere, in some form. Prepare this.

- **Accept processing.** Velmora does not welcome you. Velmora does not reject you. It *processes* you, with the patient hunger of a place that has been wet for ten thousand years and expects to remain wet for ten thousand more. Your preparation is the preparation to be processed—to become wet, to become green, to become *part of the architecture*.

## The Final Preparation: The Approach

You will arrive, if arrival is possible, by following a watercourse that narrows until it becomes a stream you can wade, then a channel through peat, then a *path*—not made, but *grown*, a corridor of shorter vegetation maintained by the constant passage of hanger colonies and human feet.

The path is the first negotiation. You are already being *grown into*, the corridor maintained by your passage, your wetness, your slow incorporation. Do not resist. Walk slowly. Feel the peat release water at your steps. Listen to the gas bursts—methane, hydrogen sulfide, the products of endless underground digestion. Breathe the mist. Let it enter you.

The cool (0.7) will strike you first, after the wet. You will shiver, always, in a way that never quite stops. Your breath will mist. Your footsteps will make sucking sounds. This is arrival. This is beginning.

The green-gold light will disorient you. You will lose the sun. You will lose the sky. You will have only the close, dense, breathing world of leaf and hanging filament and mist and the wet sound that never stops.

And then you will notice the *movement*. The turbulence (2.6) is not dramatic—no storms, no catastrophe—but constant. The laceweed sways in currents you cannot feel. The mist drifts in patterns that suggest intelligence. The bladder-farms rise and fall. The hangers watch from above, silent, numerous, *present*. The peat beneath your feet is digesting something, always, somewhere, and will, eventually, digest you too, if you stay, if you let yourself be incorporated into the wet green dark.

## What to Carry: A Final List

**On your body:**
- Nothing that expects dryness
- The willingness to be wet
- The memory of dryness, to be mourned and released

**In your hands:**
- Fire, kept alive, passed like a sacrament
- A sounding tool, bone or copper, to test the peat
- The resins for waterproofing, for smoke, for the seventeen formulations

**In your mind:**
- The language of place, the structured calls
- The acoustic signatures of bladder-farm, laceweed, bog-elk
- The map of surges, of paths, of territories vertical and horizontal

**In your body:**
- The trained skin, the conditioned lungs, the shiver that does not stop
- The adapted digestion, the taste for fermentation, for bladder-farm pulp
- The eventual acceptance of incorporation, of becoming soil, of becoming walls

**In your death:**
- The record of where you want to become
- The understanding that to be built into a house is honor
- The terror of being forgotten, of being left where surges take you

Velmora does not reject you.

Velmora *processes* you.

Prepare to be processed.
