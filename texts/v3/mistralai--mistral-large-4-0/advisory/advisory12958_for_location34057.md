I'll work through this as an ordinary human multiverse traveler, thinking practically about survival, psychology, and the specific strangeness of this place. The Basin is intimate, alive-seeming, and negotiates with attention. My preparation must address: physical survival in extreme humidity and heat, psychological adaptation to a place that *responds* to being observed, social protocol with the families, and the specific risks that come from the Basin's four centuries of accumulated custom.

## Pre-Departure Preparation

### Physical Conditioning

**Heat and humidity acclimatization.** The Basin holds 28°C minimum, near-saturation humidity, year-round. This is not desert heat—it's *unventilated* heat, the kind that prevents evaporative cooling. I need to train in a sauna, then in a sauna with a plastic sheet over me, to understand what it means when sweat doesn't dry. My body must learn that dehydration happens slower here but *insidiously*—you don't feel thirsty because the air gives back moisture through your skin even as you lose it through respiration and urine. I will practice drinking on a schedule, not on thirst.

**Cardiovascular preparation for altitude.** 3,400 meters is serious elevation. Even with the Basin's anomalous warmth, the *plateau* around it is normal high-altitude cold. The descent into the Basin involves rapid exertion. I need to be able to walk a steep staircase for forty meters of vertical drop while carrying weight, at altitude, without my heart rate spiking into dangerous territory. Stair-climbing training, weighted carries, breath control.

**Skin preparation.** The mineral water will contact my skin constantly. It's not obviously toxic, but four centuries of families wearing reed-woven shoes suggests direct contact has cumulative effects. I will pre-treat my skin with barrier creams, learn to recognize the difference between "wet" and "mineral-saturated," and accept that my skin will be *changed* by the visit. I will pack multiple changes of clothing in materials that dry slowly anyway—there's no point expecting evaporation. Merino wool, treated to resist mildew. Nothing synthetic that will trap heat against wet skin.

**Foot and hand care.** The mineral staircase is *grown*, not cut—irregular, possibly sharp in unexpected places, constantly wet. I need boots that grip on wet mineral accretions, with protection for the ankles because the descent is near-vertical. Gloves for the same surfaces, because I will want to steady myself, and the rock may be *sharp*—the parameter "young and eroded-sharp" applies to the staircase too.

### Equipment and Supplies

**Water purification that isn't chemical.** I cannot drink the Basin water directly—this is the first rule, absolute. But I need water for the journey. The plateau's surface water, if any exists, will be at normal high-altitude temperatures, possibly scarce. I will carry a solar still and a ceramic filter, plus electrolyte supplements. The Basin's mineral water is *behavioral*—it moves, it decides. I don't trust it to stay put long enough to purify. Better to carry my own and treat the Basin water as *environmental*, not resource.

**Light management.** The sun cone is blinding when it enters. I need welding-grade eye protection, not sunglasses—something that can handle reflected intensity from white mineral crust in saturated air. Also: a light source for the greenish twilight, because the blue-white mineral glow is *faint*. I want to see the walls, the staircase, the water's edge. A headlamp with adjustable intensity, red-light mode for preserving night vision in the glow.

**Recording equipment, sealed.** The families keep records in woven reed with mineral pigment. I will bring waterproof notebooks, pencils (pens fail in humidity), and a camera in a sealed housing with desiccant. But I need to be *aware*: the Basin notices attention. My camera is attention. I will practice with it before descending—learning to be still while recording, not to fidget, not to point it at the water in ways that might read as *demanding*.

**Offerings.** The families negotiate with the water. They offer mineral salts, woven reed, once a child. I don't have a child. I don't have their salts, their reed. But I can bring *attention*—deliberate, respectful, offered rather than extracted. And I can bring something that costs me: time. The Basin wants witnesses. I will plan to stay at least three days. That is my offering. I will also bring a small quantity of salt from my own world, unremarkable to me, possibly meaningful to a place that moves water without mechanism.

**Medical kit, specific.** Antifungal everything—skin, lungs, ears. The humidity will find every fold of my body. Antibiotics, because infection in a wet environment at altitude is dangerous. Antihistamines, because the air tastes of organic sweetness, and I may react to spores I've never encountered. Diamox for altitude, though I hope not to need it—the descent should be manageable with acclimatization. Most importantly: something for the *dreams*. The fish-protein, the water's pulse, the blue-white glow behind closed eyes—some visitors sleep in the light cone and wake *different*. I will bring a sleep mask, black, light-tight, and I will wear it. I will *not* sleep in the direct path of the light cone. This is non-negotiable.

### Psychological Preparation

**Learning to be watched.** This is the hardest part. The Basin *notices*. It responds to attention, to presence, to *intention*. As an ordinary human, my default is to look, to catalog, to *extract* experience. I need to practice the opposite: to be present without demanding. Meditation, specifically *open awareness* without grasping—sitting, breathing, noticing without naming. The Basin's water moves with purpose but not intelligence. I need to meet it at that level: purposeful presence without the human need to *understand* or *control*.

**Accepting change.** I will be changed. The mineral water on my skin, the dreams, possibly the bell-frog's pitch in my chest after I leave. I need to enter with *permission* for transformation, not resistance. This is grief preparation—I am grieving the person I will not be when I return. And excitement preparation—I am anticipating the person I will become. Both.

**Learning the families' silence.** They speak rarely, and toward the water. I need to practice silence before I arrive. Not the silence of absence, but the silence of *address*—speaking without words, or with the minimum. I will practice saying things aloud to water, to stone, to air, and hearing how my voice sounds: too loud, too *human*, too *demanding*. The Basin has its own language. I am learning to listen before I learn to speak.

**Managing the intimacy.** The Basin is not hostile. It is *curious*, and it seems to *prepare* for visitors—adjusting patterns, illuminating paths. This is seductive. It feels like being *known*. I need to remember: this is not recognition. This is *response*. The Basin does not know me as a person. It knows me as *input*, as a variable in its becoming. The intimacy is real, but it is not *relationship* in the human sense. I will not mistake it for love.

### Social Preparation: The Families

I need to find them before I descend. They live at the rim, in black glass and thornspine wood, scattered like seeds. I need to:

**Learn their names for things.** "The Wet Mouth." "Inside." I need to use their language, not mine. "Sunken Basin" is an outsider's name, a cartographer's name. I will learn to say *the Wet Mouth* and mean it. This is respect, and it is also *safety*—the families may react to my ignorance, my outsider-ness, in ways I don't predict.

**Observe before approaching.** I will spend at least a day at the rim, watching. How do they move? How do they speak to the water? What do they do when it behaves erratically? I need to see the *negotiation* before I attempt to participate.

**Ask permission.** They maintain the Basin. They control, or at least *mediate*, access. I will not descend without their acknowledgment. And I will accept that they may refuse—that the Basin itself, through them, may *decline* my presence. This is possible. The Basin is young, learning, *selective*. I may not be *expected*. If I am not expected, I will not force it.

**Learn the rules, all of them.** The four they have named: no drinking, no sleeping in the light cone, no counting the fish, no building. But "others they have learned but not written down." I need to ask. I need to listen. I need to accept that some rules will be *felt*, not stated—a wrongness in the water's movement, a hesitation in the bell-frog's call, a moment when the air tastes *wrong* and I should climb the staircase and not look back.

**Offer to learn.** The families are teachers, students, companions. They have maintained this place for four centuries. I have days, maybe weeks. I will offer to *record* for them—to be their witness, their memory, in a way that respects their reed-and-pigment system. I will not offer to *replace* their technology, their understanding. I am here to *see*, not to *fix*.

### The Descent: Practical Protocol

**Timing.** I will descend in the morning, when the water rises in the eastern quarter. The families say the water *thinks* its way around the floor. I want to see it *thinking*—the purposeful movement, the slow sheet across the floor. I want to be present for the daily negotiation, not arrive in the greenish twilight when the water retreats to its sump and the Basin is *waiting*, *listening*.

**The staircase.** Forty meters, near-vertical, mineral accretions, blue-white glow in shadow. I will descend slowly. I will not rush. The rock is young, sharp, *managed*—it may shift, release flakes, adjust to my weight. I will test each step. I will not touch the walls unless necessary. The weeping water on the staircase is *not* the Basin floor water—it is older, mineral-charged, possibly reactive. I will keep my gloves on.

**First contact with the floor.** The air will thicken. I will feel it immediately. My preparation was for this: the sweat that doesn't dry, the wet that doesn't come from rain, the heat that *envelops* rather than oppresses. I will stand still. I will not walk to the water. I will let the Basin *notice* me—let the water's pattern shift, let the light cone adjust, let the bell-frog strike. I will be *patient*. I will be *present*. I will not be *afraid*.

**The water.** I will not drink it. I will not touch it, initially. I will watch it move. I will learn its rhythm—the morning rise, the daily circuit, the retreat. I will note where it *prefers* to be, where it *avoids*. I will see the islands, the reeds, the moss-cascades on the walls. I will breathe the air: mineral, organic, faintly sweet. I will let my skin *accept* the humidity, the condensation, the mineral. I will not fight it.

**The islands.** I will visit them, but carefully. The vegetation is *hot-adapted*, impossible, *managed*. The pitcher-plants' traps are filled with mineral-sweet condensation, not rain. I will not drink from them. I will not touch the mosses—iridescent, thick, *unknown*. I will observe. I will record. I will *move slowly*.

**The walls.** I will not build. I will not leave marks—no carving, no paint, no *claim*. The water will undo any building. But also: the walls are *weeping*, *growing*, *eroding* in managed patterns. I will watch for the *design*—the lines, the channels, the circuits that the families see but do not speak of. I will not name what I see. I will not *interpret*. The Basin is learning what it is. I am its witness. My interpretation is *interference*.

**The night.** I will sleep at the rim, not inside. The families' structures are black glass, thornspine wood, *scattered*. I will accept their hospitality, or I will camp at the defile's edge, visible to them, *acknowledged* by them. Inside, I will sleep with my mask, in the greenish twilight, away from any direct line to the sky rectangle. I will dream, possibly. The fish-protein dreams, the shared dreams, the drowning dreams that are not unpleasant. I will wake, and I will record what I remember. I will not be afraid of the dreams. They are *part of the process*.

### The Stay: Duration and Rhythm

I will stay three days minimum. This is my offering: time, presence, *becoming part of the pattern*. But I need to learn the Basin's rhythm:

**Day one: Arrival, observation, the water's daily cycle.** I descend in the morning. I watch the water rise, sheet across the floor, retreat. I visit the islands. I climb to the rim at dusk. I sleep. I dream.

**Day two: The walls, the artifacts, the families' negotiation.** I spend the day on the walls, watching the weeping, the managed erosion, the moss-circuits. I visit the families' structures—*if invited*. I see the artifacts, the humming sphere, the image-sheet. I do not touch them. I speak to the families, minimally, toward the water. I learn what I can of the unwritten rules.

**Day three: The return, the leaving, the carrying.** I descend one final time. I watch the water's pattern—has it *changed* because of me? Has it learned my name in its language? I climb the staircase. I say goodbye, not to the Basin—it does not hear—but to the families, to the place, to the person I was when I arrived. I leave with the reed-woven shoes, the mineral on my skin, the bell-frog's pitch in my chest.

### Post-Departure: Integration

I will be changed. This is not metaphor. The mineral water, the dreams, the blue-white glow behind my eyes. I need to:

**Monitor my health.** Fungal infections, respiratory issues, altitude after-effects. The *dreams*—are they continuing? Am I hearing the water's pulse in my blood? Some who drank the water walked into it and did not return. I did not drink. But I was *immersed* in its environment. I need to watch for *longing*, for the pull to return. This is not weakness. It is *response*. The Basin prepared for me. It will prepare again. I must decide: will I return? The Basin knows. It has already begun.

**Record, carefully.** My notes, my photographs, my dreams. But also: my *silence*. Some things should not be shared. The families do not speak of certain things. I will learn their discretion. The Basin's rules, the unwritten ones. The artifacts. The *becoming*. I will write for myself, for the families if they ask, for the next traveler who needs preparation. But I will not write *everything*. The Basin notices attention. My words are attention.

**Find community.** Others who have been. The families, if they will speak. Other travelers, if I can find them. The changed—those who saw only blue-white, who are *different*, who are not unhappy. I need to know I am not alone in this. The Basin is intimate, but it is not *isolating*. It wants witnesses. It wants *participants*. I am one. I am not the only one.

**Accept the return.** The Basin will call me. The bell-frog's pitch, the water's pulse, the blue-white glow. I will feel it in my chest, in my blood, behind my eyes. I will return. Not immediately—I have a life, a world, responsibilities. But eventually. The Basin is young. It will still be there. It will have changed, grown, *become more*. I will be changed too. We will meet again, in the language of water, of mineral, of heat held in a young and sharp and *strange* embrace.

I will sleep at the plateau's edge, in the cold, the dry, the *ordinary*. I will review my equipment. I will review the rules. I will breathe the thin air, the sharp cold, and I will *grieve* the person I am leaving. Tomorrow I descend into the Wet Mouth. Tomorrow I become *input*. Tomorrow I am *expected*, and I am *part of the process*.
