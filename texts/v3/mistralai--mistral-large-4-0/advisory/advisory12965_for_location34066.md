I'll approach this as if briefing a traveler who has never been anywhere like this—someone whose experience extends to, at most, the interstitial spaces between ordinary worlds.

## The Descent: Preparing the Body

You do not *walk* into the Thermal Lattice. You **lower yourself**. The passage demands it. Your equipment must be stripped to the absolute minimum before you begin—every gram of mass is thermal debt you will pay in sweat and compromised insulation. The squeeze at hand-width is not merely narrow; it is a **test of intention**. The lattice does not admit what it cannot accommodate. Pack light. Move slowly. Accept that your claustrophobia will be negotiated, not conquered.

Your suit: **multi-layer reflective, actively cooled, redundant**. Not the bulky kind—something that can flex through a 0.3-meter constriction without tearing, without *leaking*. The cooling system must be **closed-loop, phase-change, with thermal mass you can dump into if the primary fails**. The ambient here is not "hot weather." It is a *medium*. You are swimming in it. Your suit's exterior will reach equilibrium with the walls; your interior must not. Design for 380°C sustained external exposure, 400°C for transients when the thermal cycle peaks. Test your seals against *particulate* intrusion, not just gas—those metallic aerosols will find every gap, every valve, every seam. They are abrasive. They are conductive. They will **burn** if they reach your skin through any breach.

Breathing: **closed-circuit, scrubbed, cooled**. The atmosphere is nitrogen-dominant with sulfur compounds and metallic vapor. Your rebreather must handle particulates at the inlet—electrostatic precipitation, perhaps, or a sacrificial filter you replace every twenty minutes. The air you breathe must be chilled to below 40°C before it reaches your lungs. Practice breathing *slowly*. Rapid respiration increases your metabolic heat output, which increases your cooling load, which increases your power draw. The lattice punishes haste in a dozen compounding ways.

## The Descent: Preparing the Senses

Your eyes will **lie to you**. The diffuse glow of scattered light from the aerosols creates a perceptual flatness—no shadows, no depth cues, no contrast. You will misjudge distances constantly. **Lidar, always on. Sonar, as backup.** The acoustic environment is dominated by the subsonic drone, but your equipment will filter this. You need to hear the *changes*—the microtonal shifts, the pressure pulses—as data, not sensation.

Your ears will **ring**. Not from sound you hear, but from sound you *feel*—the 2.4 register vibrating in your sternum, your inner ear fluid, your teeth. This is not damaging at the intensity described, but it is **disorienting**. It will interfere with your vestibular sense. You will feel like you are falling, or sinking, or expanding. **Train for this**. Spend time in anechoic chambers with subsonic transducers. Learn to trust your instruments over your proprioception.

Your skin: **prepare for the illusion of cold**. Your body heat, 37°C, will feel *wrong* here—not because you are burning, but because you are *leaking*. The thermal gradient from your suit interior to the ambient is so steep that your extremities will feel numb, will *ache* with the effort of maintaining circulation against the heat sink. This is psychological as much as physical. The cold bubble you create around yourself is real, measurable, and the lattice will respond to it. **Do not interpret your own thermal shadow as refuge.**

## The Descent: Preparing the Mind

Time will **stretch**. The forty-minute thermal cycle of the walls will become your heartbeat. The eleven-to-seventeen-minute resonance shifts will structure your experience of duration. Do not fight this. **Synchronize**. Plan your movements around the cycle. The squeeze through the throat should happen during the cooling phase, when the mineral foam contracts and the passage is marginally wider. The pulse from the engine arrives every four hours; you do not want to be in a constriction when the pressure wave hits, when the shells shiver, when the aerosols ionize and your suit's exterior becomes a lightning rod.

The shells: **do not touch them**. This is not a rule of etiquette. They are at 340°C at their cores. Their shells radiate outward. The 0.3mm gap between them is **thermal insulation**—if you bridge it, if you conduct heat from one to another, you disrupt the local resonance. You create a dead note in the chord. The lattice will notice. It does not welcome. **Move through the gaps. Treat the fauna as architecture, not wildlife.**

The sound: **learn to hear the silence between shifts**. The chord is continuous, but it is not static. The micro-adjustments every eleven or seventeen minutes are **events**. They mark time. They mark *change*. When you hear one—when you *feel* the microtonal step in your gut—you know the lattice is still alive, still adjusting, still *singing*. If the shifts stop, if the chord holds static for more than thirty minutes, **something is wrong**. The engine has failed. The shells are fusing. You are in a dying pocket. Leave.

## The Center: The Engine

You will find it. Sixty meters of curved chamber, and at its heart, the **heat engine**. Do not approach it as a machine. Approach it as a **weather system**. The expansion chambers, the condensation plates, the pressure differentials—these are not components. They are **phenomena**. The St. Elmo's fire is real. The ionization is real. Your suit's electrical systems will **misbehave** here. Ground everything. Shield everything. The twelve-to-fifteen volts are low, but the current is enormous, the medium is dense, and your body is a conductor in a field you do not understand.

The pulse arrives every four hours. It is a **pressure wave**, but it is also a **signal**. It nudges each shell into its next micro-adjustment. It maintains the resonance. When it hits, you will feel it—a thump in your chest, a shift in the aerosol glow, a momentary alignment of particles along invisible field lines. **Do not be in contact with a shell when it arrives**. Do not be touching the wall. Do not be pressing against any surface that conducts. The wave travels through solid, through gas, through *you*. It is not dangerous at this amplitude. But it is **intimate**. It is the lattice speaking its only language. Listen. Do not answer.

## The Return: Leaving the Wound

You are the intrusion. Your cold bubble has grown. The shells have retreated, over minutes, creating a cavity of thermal inequality. The lattice is repairing. It will spend hours, perhaps days, restoring equilibrium around your passage. **This is not hostility. This is physics.** But do not mistake indifference for safety. The lattice does not welcome. It simply **contains**.

Your ascent is harder than your descent. The cooling phase may not align with your schedule. The squeeze may be narrower. Your suit may have degraded. Your power reserves are lower. **Plan for the worst thermal cycle.** Assume the walls will be at maximum expansion when you need to pass. Assume the aerosols will be densest, most conductive, most abrasive. Assume the drone will be loudest, most disorienting, most *present*.

When you emerge—if you emerge—you will feel **cold**. Not the cold of ordinary atmosphere, but the cold of *relief*, of *survivable gradient*. You will shiver. You will sweat. You will smell sulfur and metal and something like ozone, on your suit, in your hair, in the memory of your lungs. You will hear silence as a **presence**, not an absence.

The Thermal Lattice will continue. The engine will fire. The chord will drift. The shells will sing their subsonic song. In a million years, it will be stone. A geode. A secret.

You will carry the heat of it in your bones, long after your suit is removed, long after you have returned to worlds where heat is *weather*, where industry has *builders*, where life has *metabolism*. You will dream of the drone. You will wake and reach for lidar, for sonar, for data that confirms you are no longer embedded, no longer contained, no longer *singing*.

And you will know: **some places do not want visitors. They simply persist. And you were, for a moment, part of that persistence—a wound, a question, a cold bubble in an ocean of patient heat.**

*Leave nothing behind. Take nothing but the memory of being small.*
