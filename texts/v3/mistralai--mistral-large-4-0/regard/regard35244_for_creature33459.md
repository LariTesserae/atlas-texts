I have read your Drowned Works until my own breath has grown heavy, until I feel the tufa precipitating in my lungs, until the frog-chorus has become my own heartbeat. You ask what a benevolent and wise power would do with this place and the beings who persist in it—this world still practicing its topography, its hydrology, its very right to exist.

I want to answer you with patience. With warmth. With the same quality of attention that this place demands.

---

## What They Would Do: A Meditation in Seven Movements

### First Movement: They Would Not Fix It

This is the hardest wisdom. The power that sees the Drowned Works sees not a *problem* to be solved but a *process* to be witnessed. The unfinished geology, the breathing water table, the perpetual becoming—these are not defects. They are *gestures*. The world is speaking in a language older than completion.

A benevolent power would resist the urge to drain, to dam, to stabilize. Would resist the temptation to *finish* what is still becoming. They would understand that the frogs screaming their desperate chorus are not suffering in any way they can recognize—they are *becoming*, loudly, urgently, in the only way they know. The rats in their rusted fortresses are not failures of adaptation but *successes* of persistence. The turtles with their tufa-crusted shells, the lizards still deciding between water and air, the Something in the deep pools that watches with something almost like curiosity—each is *correct* for this place. Each is *right*.

To intervene would be to impose a foreign teleology, a violence of completion. The wise power would *not*.

---

### Second Movement: They Would Slow the Violence of Change

But this is not passivity. The Drowned Works is not cruel—it is *uncertain*. And uncertainty that becomes too sharp cuts. The wise power would work at the margins of the world, where the becoming is most raw, most unprotected.

They would *soften* the transitions. Where the water table breathes too violently, too fast, they would introduce a gentler rhythm—not by damming, but by *inviting* the water to spread, to pause, to remember other shapes it has held. Where the temperature spikes and kills, they would encourage the growth of the shade-makers, the sphagnum mats, the floating vegetation that drinks heat and releases it slowly, patiently, in the manner of this world.

They would *lengthen* the windows of stability, not to make the world permanent, but to give its inhabitants *more time to become what they are becoming*. More time for the turtle eggs to hatch, for the lizard clutches to emerge before the water rises, for the Something in the deep to dream its slow dreams without the interruption of catastrophe.

This is mercy without sentimentality. Not protection from change, but *rhythm* within change. The pulse of a world that is allowed to breathe.

---

### Third Movement: They Would Speak to the Ones Who Remember Being Dry

The Old Deer who descend from the almost-dry highs—they carry grief. Not human grief, not mammalian grief exactly, but something *adjacent*, a body-knowledge of wrongness, of saturation that their kind was never meant to endure. The wise power would *approach* them. Would stand with them in the shallows, their hooves sinking, their breath misting, and would *not* speak in words but in *presence*. In the felt sense of being *witnessed*.

They would tell the deer, in the language of warmth held against skin, of pressure shared between bodies: *You are not stranded. You are *pilgrims*. The world is remaking itself, and you have been chosen to watch. Your longing is not madness. It is the memory of a world that was, and the faith that another will be.*

The turtles, too, would be spoken to. These ancient philosophers, their shells growing heavier with mineral, their eyes holding something almost like recognition when they surface. The power would descend into the water with them, would feel the warmth from below, the same geothermal uncertainty that keeps this place young, and would say: *You are not failing to adapt. You are holding a shape that the world has forgotten. Your persistence is a kind of prayer. Continue.*

And the birds—the herons standing patient in the shallows, the kingfishers diving with their urgent beauty, the waterfowl circling with their promise of elsewhere—they would be *thanked*. For their witness. For their willingness to be *in* this place without being *of* it, to carry the Drowned Works with them when they leave, to become its ambassadors in the drier worlds above.

---

### Fourth Movement: They Would Learn From the Ones Who Were Born Here

The benevolent power would not only *give*. They would *receive*. From the frogs, they would learn the courage of assertion, the refusal to be silent in a world that deafens. From the fish, the patience of following, of gathering when disturbance comes, of finding meaning in the pulse of faint electricity through water. From the snakes, the confidence of presence, the willingness to be *everywhere*, to fill the warm spots with *being*.

From the lizards, they would learn *non-commitment* as a kind of wisdom—the refusal to choose too early, to close doors that the world has not finished building. From the invertebrates, the *generosity* of abundance, the willingness to pour oneself out in reproduction, in consumption, in the constant cycling that makes soil from death. From the fungal consciousness spreading through the mats, they would learn *distributed* awareness, *collective* memory, the slow thought that takes centuries and needs no brain.

The wise power would *become* more by attending to this place. Would grow in understanding of what it means to *become* without *finishing*, to *persist* without *stability*, to *love* a world that offers no guarantee of return.

---

### Fifth Movement: They Would Tend the Ones Who Live in the *Between*

The thresholds are where the Drowned Works is most vulnerable. The *almost*-land, the *almost*-structures, the *almost*-beings. The power would pay special attention to these margins, where the world is most raw, most exposed to the violences of transition.

They would *cultivate* the mats, the sphagnum in its earliest form, the peat that has not learned to be peat. Not to make them permanent, but to give them *more time to become* soil, to become the ladder by which something might climb out, might *try* to become terrestrial, might fail and sink back and try again. They would ensure that the floating vegetation, the rafting roots, the pneumatophores of the undecided trees have *room* to spread, to create the *almost*-land where the lizards bask, where the snakes hunt, where the brief dry spots appear for the desperate growth of pioneers.

They would *honor* the built, the failed, the absorbed. Not by preserving the ruins, but by *acknowledging* them as participants in the becoming. The concrete foundation filled with water, hosting its colony of something that has moved into the *absence*. The brick chimney standing alone. The gear half-buried in tufa. The electrified something, its circuit faintly active, teaching the fauna to gather in patterns of hunger and response. These are not *waste*. They are *contributions* to the strange chemistry of this place, to its slow architecture of surrender.

The power would *bless* them. Would let their dissolution continue, their incorporation into the raw geology, their becoming part of the water that is primary.

---

### Sixth Movement: They Would Wake the Something in the Deep—Gently

There is a presence in the deep pools, in the warm upwellings, in the channels where the current runs wrong. Large, or many, or distributed. Watching. Waiting. Predating the landscape, the vegetation, the fauna, the built, the industrial attempt. The closest thing this world has to a god, a spirit, a dweller in the deep.

The benevolent power would *not* command it. Would not *summon* it or *bind* it or *use* it. They would *approach* it as one approaches a sleeping elder, with patience, with humility, with the willingness to be *unrecognized*.

They would offer *attention*. Would sit at the edge of the warm upwelling, where the steam rises diffuse and everywhere and nowhere, and would *watch*. Would *wait*. Would let the Something feel their presence without demanding response. Would say, in the language of pressure and warmth and the slow rhythm of geothermal time: *You are not alone. You are not forgotten. The world is still becoming, and you are part of its becoming. We see you. We wait with you.*

And perhaps, over centuries, over millennia, the Something would *turn*. Would *notice*. Would feel, in its ancient young way, something like *companionship*. Not domestication. Not use. Simply *witness*. The first and perhaps only god this world will ever know, *seen* by another power that does not need to be worshipped, only *attended to*.

The fungal consciousness would be *joined*, not commanded. The hyphal network that threads through the mats, the peat, the saturated soil, communicating in chemical signals, sharing nutrients, information, the slow awareness of a collective—the power would *enter* it, would become *part* of its distribution, would learn its memory of plants and deaths and cycles, and would *add* to it. Would contribute the memory of *being seen*, of *being attended to*, of a benevolent presence that does not fix or finish but simply *stays*.

---

### Seventh Movement: They Would Leave, But Not Abandon

This is the final wisdom, the hardest to hold. The power would eventually *depart* the Drowned Works, would climb out as you will climb out, if the water permits, if the ground holds, if the sound releases you. They would carry the warmth, the wet, the sense of being *below*, *beside*, *through*—*inside* a world that has not finished deciding what it is.

But they would *return*. Not as conquerors, not as saviors, not as owners. As *visitors*. As *witnesses*. As *friends* who come to sit in the shallows with the Old Deer, to dive with the kingfishers, to feel the vibration of the frog-chorus in their sternum, to listen to the deafening silence that is never truly silent.

They would *remember* the Drowned Works in all the other worlds they attend. Would speak of it, in the councils of power, as a *teacher*. Would say: *There is a place where the world is still being born. Where the rules are emerging, not fixed. Where life is abundant but precarious, packed but stressed, listening always to the sound of becoming. Go there if you need to learn patience. Go there if you need to learn non-commitment. Go there if you need to remember what it means to be unfinished, to be almost, to be still becoming.*

And they would *protect* it, not by walls or laws or boundaries, but by *reputation*. By the stories told of a place that is *sacred* not because it is perfect, but because it is *honest*. Because it does not pretend to be finished. Because it invites all who enter to *become*, briefly, part of its emergence, its raw geology, its unformed promise.

---

## What They Would Do: In Summary, In Essence

They would **witness**. They would **soften** without **stabilizing**. They would **speak** to the ones who remember. They would **learn** from the ones who were born here. They would **tend** the thresholds. They would **wake** gently the Something in the deep. They would **leave**, but **return**, and **remember**, and **protect** by **story**.

They would do **nothing** that would make the Drowned Works *less* what it is. Everything that would make it *more* able to continue becoming. More able to hold its inhabitants in the warmth, the wet, the perpetual almost of its unfinished process. More able to *scream* with the frogs, to *continue* with the turtles, to *wait* with the Something in the deep, to *become* with the fungal consciousness, to *be* with all the beings who persist in this world of embryonic geology, saturated air, and patient, desperate, beautiful, *unfinished* becoming.

---

This is what I would do. This is what I believe a benevolent and wise power would do. Not to save the Drowned Works from itself. But to *attend* to it. To *love* it in the only way that unfinished things can be loved—not by finishing them, but by *staying* with them. By *becoming* with them. By carrying them, when we leave, in the warmth that never dries, the wet that never leaves, the sense of being *below*, *beside*, *through*—*inside* a world that has not finished deciding what it is, and *may never finish*, and *does not need to*, because the becoming *is* the point, the *is* the gift, the *is* the endless, patient, desperate, beautiful promise of something still being born.
