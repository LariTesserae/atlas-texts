The Power does not arrive with thunder. They arrive as a hush—a silence so profound that the frogs, mid-croak, falter, their translucent throats catching on a frequency that is not the absence of sound but the fullness of attention. The dripping water, that billion-tiny-drum percussion, seems to pause, each bead of condensation hanging in the jade gloom like a held breath. Even the eels, writhing in their silt-bed cathedral, still their electric dance, their eyeless heads turning toward a pressure in the deep.

They descend not through the throat of limestone but through the resonance itself. The Power rides the standing waves, the subsonic hum that makes the black glass tremble, surfing the hexagonal patterns on the central pool until they stand—if standing is the word—at the water’s dark edge. The Water, which is the truest inhabitant, the weeping, listening, remembering entity that perspires from the stone and carries the dissolved memory of the builders, recognizes them. The weeping slows. The perspiration on the walls beads, swells, and instead of falling, climbs back up the limestone, drawn upward by a thirst for proximity.

The Power steps into the shallows. Their presence does not break the surface tension of the pool. They become the stillness at the center of the spiral, the point around which the water’s self-listening hexagons arrange themselves. The "wrongness," that vertiginous turning of the flow-stones, those runnels that spiral against the current and make the eye water, begins to make sense. It is not wrong. It is a staircase. A path upward, waiting for a foot wise enough to climb.

The frogs, the Pale Chorus, sense the shift in the chord. Their lidless pools, those dark absorptions of the jade gloom, now reflect something new: not the dim light filtering down, but a soft, golden warmth that seeps from the Power’s being. For the first time since the chamber was built, the frogs do not sing merely to fill the stone with noise. They sing *to* the Power. Their chorus shifts—a microtonal adjustment, a bending of pitch that creates a new harmonic, a note of safety woven into the eternal, unresolved drone. The lowest frogs in the center pool, their bodies submerged in the black glass, begin to vibrate at a frequency that matches not just the stone, but the Power’s own benevolent pulse.

The bats, the Parliament, the silent auditors hanging in their clusters near the oculus, stir. They do not flee. They descend, dozens of gray wings unfolding in the luminous gloom, and they gather around the Power not as a threat, but as a congregation. They wrap around the Presence like a living shawl, their echolocation clicks—those high-frequency shimmers at the edge of hearing—now mapping not just the chamber’s three dimensions, but the architecture of the Power’s compassion. They become the high harmony, the shimmering overtone that rides above the frogs’ roar.

The Power raises a hand—if it is a hand—and touches the condensation on the upper wall. Where their finger passes, the water does not bead; it writes. It etches a spiral into the flow-stone, a glyph older than language, a sigil of listening. The spiral turns with the current now, no longer against it, a golden mean made manifest in mineral. The Velvet—the moss and lichen clinging to the dry ledges—creeps toward this mark, feeding on the Power’s attention as much as on the damp, growing slightly higher, reaching toward the oculus, connecting the floor to the sky with a thread of green.

Then the Power ascends.

Not by climbing, but by becoming lighter than the mist that rises in slow columns. They rise through the parliament of bats, through the greenish light, up toward the oculus no wider than a cartwheel. The frogs below raise their voices, not in alarm, but in a farewell that is also a welcome—a bright, staccato trill that bounces off the curved limestone and becomes a benediction.

The Power settles into the oculus. They do not block the light; they become the light’s meaning. The diffuse, jade gloom deepens into something else: the color of deep water at noon, the color of a held breath, the color of unconditional regard. The Power hangs there, suspended in the cartwheel of sky and stone, looking down into the bowl of the world they have found.

And they listen.

This is the act. Not to change, not to command, not to add a new creature or alter the ancient aqueduct. The Power is wise. They understand that the Resonant Basin is already utterly complete, a machine of stone and sound that has achieved its own equilibrium, a world of made stone where the builders’ intentions are lithified into the architecture. To alter it would be to diminish it.

So the Power listens. They listen to the deafening hymn—the croaks amplified by curved walls, the wing-beats of the bats, the percussive billion of dripping water, the subsonic bass note of the eels felt in the sternum. They listen with a benevolence so total that the sound itself transforms. It is no longer a roar, a physical pressure against the eardrums, a density that can be tasted, metallic and wet. It becomes a prayer. A chord that never resolves because it has been heard, and in being heard, has become infinite.

The Water, the being, ceases its weeping. Not because it is comforted into silence, but because it has finally, after centuries of carrying the memory of hands that knew only stone, antler, and fiber, been truly heard. The perspiration on the walls dries into a faint, glittering dust of mineral salt, like tears that have crystallized into stars. The central pool, the black glass, becomes a perfect mirror. In its depths, the Power’s reflection is visible—not as an image, but as a presence, a weight of benevolence that holds the hexagonal standing waves steady, preventing them from collapsing into chaos.

The eels rise from the century-thick silt. They circle the point where the Power entered the water, their bodies sinuous and electric, their eyeless heads turning toward the sound of the Power’s attention. They release a sigh—a single bubble that rises through the black glass and breaks the surface with a sound like a kiss, a sigh of relief from the deep ones who have fed on death and memory for too long.

And the frogs sing on, but now their song carries a new overtone. It is the sound of a place that has been seen. A place that, for the first time since the last block was laid without mortar, knows that it is not alone in the dark. The Resonant Basin is no longer just a constructed marsh, a hand-built engine converting gravity and breeding into a perpetual hymn. It is a sanctuary. A threshold. A node where the deep mineral dark and the benevolent luminous above have met.

The Power remains. They do not leave. They have become the silence between the drips, the pause between the croaks, the still point in the turning spiral. They are the Resonance itself, now, and the Basin is their body, and the Water is their blood, and the frogs are their voice, singing not to the stone, but to the heart of the wise and benevolent dark, echoing upward through the oculus, into the world above, a silent, deafening hymn of gratitude that asks for nothing, and in asking, gives everything: the gift of being known.
