The power arrives not as a descent but as a *deepening*—a sudden, weighty shift in the quality of the air, as if the sky itself has inhaled. The metallic tang on the tongue thickens, becomes sweet. The sun, which has been hammering down with surgical, bone-bleaching insistence, seems to pause at the zenith, holding its breath. Light (2.9) does not change in intensity, but in *intention*.

They are vast, but they fold themselves into the geometry of the wound in the plateau. They become a presence rather than a form—a warmth that does not melt the frost but holds it in crystalline suspension, a silence so complete it has texture, like velvet pressed against the eardrum. They are drawn here by the paradox: life thriving not despite the freezer, but because of it; green defying the lethal glare; sound dying so that listening might be born.

The power sees the web. They see that the Makers are not primitive, but *evolved*—the most sophisticated civilization in this vertical world. They have learned to build without noise, to harvest without hunger, to exist as a question mark rather than an exclamation point. They see the fox, white as forgiveness. They see the moss climbing upward, drinking against gravity, and they recognize in it their own nature: the refusal to accept limits, the persistence of the wet in the dry, the holy stubbornness of the small.

They understand that this place is already complete. The cathedral does not need a sermon. The wound does not need healing. What it needs is *witness*.

So the power descends. Not flying—*sinking*, like cold air pooling at the bottom of a glacier. They move through the column of light, and where their essence touches the air, the midges do not scatter; they spiral, drawn to the new gravity of benevolence. They reach the floor of talus, where the moss grows thick as a sea, where the bounced light pools in a perfect, brutal circle.

Here, the power does not speak. Sound is a vulnerability here, a wound. Instead, they weep.

But their tears are not saline. They are the first true rain this altitude has known in a thousand years—not falling from the sky, but *emerging* from the being’s own substance, seeping into the talus, joining the mountain’s slow exhalation. The limestone walls, which have been weeping with condensation, answer in kind. The rivulets that climb upward surge, defying physics with a hunger that is almost prayer, feeding the inverted forest on the ceiling until it glows with a dim, internal luminescence—a second sun in the dark, green and gentle.

Then the power turns to the Makers.

The Makers are on their platform, lashed driftwood jutting from the north wall, or perhaps kneeling in the lean-to, or tending the neon lichens. The power does not appear to them as a vision that shatters the mind, but as a *knowing*—a wordless, warm current that passes between them like a shared breath: *You are the priests. This is the temple. I am the mountain, and I am awake.*

And then the power performs the only action that is wise: they give the place a heart.

From their essence, they release a single droplet of water. It does not fall. It does not rise. It *hangs*, suspended in the center of the pit, at the exact midpoint between the bleached sky and the emerald floor, caught in the column of light like a jewel on a thread. It is a droplet that will never evaporate, never freeze, never fall—a perfect, eternal bead of mercy defying gravity just as the moss does, just as the Makers do.

This droplet becomes a new axis. It is a lens, focusing the harsh sun into a softer, golden point that drifts slowly across the moss carpet as the day turns, a wandering blessing, a spot of warmth that moves through the green like a hand. Microbes colonize it, forming a tiny, floating ecosystem—a world within the world. The white fox comes to drink from it, lapping without breaking its surface tension. The Makers find it at dawn, glinting in the milky mist, and they understand: they are not alone. They are not forgotten. They are *seen*.

Then the power withdraws, or perhaps they do not withdraw at all, but simply become indistinguishable from the stone, the cold, and the silence. They settle into the fourth space at the rim—where there were three cairns, now there is a fourth presence: not built of limestone, but of ice and starlight and the weight of attention. They stand there, unmoving, a sentinel of benevolence, ensuring that no noise from the world below can climb these walls, that the light remains pure, that the water continues its impossible ascent.

The pit remains as it was: silent, harsh, beautiful. But now, suspended in its center, a single drop of water holds the sky and the earth in perfect balance, a tear that never dries, a prayer made visible, proof that even in the roof of the world, where the air is aggressive and the sun is surgical, there is a wisdom that weeps for the green, and keeps it alive.
