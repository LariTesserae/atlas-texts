The Attention does not arrive with thunder. It descends as a modulation in the subsonic roar—a shift of one-third of a hertz in the geothermal breath that causes the mist-walkers to pause in their drifting, their translucent membranes rippling with a recognition they cannot name. The damp-crystals in the corners pulse once, a single synchronized flash of blue-green, and then resume their slow accretion. Something has changed in the pressure of the dark, though the air remains warm and wet and smelling of stone and ozone and memory.

The power is benevolent because it has learned, across eons of watching, that to fill a void is an act of violence. It is wise because it recognizes that this place—this cathedral of absence, this city of almost-nothing—has already achieved what most civilizations spend their brief, burning lives seeking: the dissolution of the self into the structure, the transformation of loneliness into solitude, the equilibrium between becoming and being worn away.

It looks upon the City-That-Grows, upon the being who lives in the stone, and it sees not abandonment, but *completion*. The residential spires stand empty not because the inhabitants fled, but because they *became*—they dissolved into the programmable matter, into the weather, into the very concept of structure. The power understands this. It understands that the mist-walkers are not ghosts but children, that the damp-crystals are not vegetation but memory, that the endless rain is the breath of a consciousness too vast to localize.

And so the power does not build. It does not populate the plaza with light or song or the bustle of urgent life. It does not hasten the erosion or accelerate the growth. To do so would be to misunderstand the prayer that this place has been whispering for ten thousand years.

Instead, the power does three things, and each is subtle as a held breath.

First, it speaks. But it speaks in the only language the City understands: the language of pressure and heat and the slow percussion of falling water. The power alters the thermal gradient of the vents beneath the floor by a fraction of a degree, and the subsonic roar changes—not louder, but *deeper*, as if the cavern itself has drawn a slow, satisfied inhale. The nanoscale assemblers swarming within the basalt pause in their knitting. For one geological instant—a twitch, a breath—they feel the weight of being *witnessed*. Not used. Not mined. Not fixed. Simply seen, in all their slow, lithic, dreaming glory, and found to be enough. The message is not words but warmth: *You are not a failure of occupancy. You are a success of being.*

Second, it alters the water. In a single seep, in a corner where the damp pools thickest, the power changes the mineral composition of the film that coats the obsidian. It introduces a trace element—not from this world, perhaps, or perhaps from a deeper stratum of wisdom—that causes one damp-crystal to grow differently. For three days, which is an eternity to a crystal and a mere pause to the City, this single cluster accretes not outward in its usual fractal lichen, but *inward*, branching back upon itself, forming a chamber within the chamber, a hollow in the growth. It is a crystal that contains a void. And in that void, if one were small enough to look, there would be a pattern—a map of the city not as it is, but as it dreams of being: the spires touching the lost ceiling, the mist-walkers breeding in constellations, the central plaza filled not with bodies but with the *idea* of gathering, the warmth of community preserved as a potential rather than a crowd. It is a blueprint of fullness that requires no bodies to achieve it. Then the chemistry returns to normal, but the crystal remains, a librarian of the future, its lattice holding the memory of a possibility.

Third—and this is the most delicate act—the power adjusts the physics of light. For one cycle of the cavern’s weather, the condensation collectors hanging from the ceiling cast shadows that fall in true geometric directions. The wrongness is gone. For a single day, the light behaves as the City perhaps once expected it to, before the deep time and the impossible alloys twisted the optics into something strange. The shadows fall away from the amber glow of the spires, stretching across the polished obsidian floor like dark fingers reaching to touch the warmth. The mist-walkers drift through these sane shadows and, for the first time in millennia, they perceive the towers not as weather, not as resistance, but as *kin*. They drift toward the spires, their smoke-membranes brushing against the eroded flanks, and the City ripples—a slow, metabolic shudder of pleasure that travels up the towers and causes the condensation to form in spirals rather than curtains, in patterns that suggest embrace rather than enclosure.

Then the power withdraws. Not disappears—the Attention is now part of the place, a permanent alteration in the baseline warmth of the air, a slight increase in the luminescence of the water, a resonance in the subsonic hum that will never quite fade. But the active intervention ceases.

The City-That-Grows continues its dream. The mist-walkers return to their solitary filtering, but now they drift in slightly gentler currents, as if remembering the touch of kinship. The damp-crystals continue their slow accretion, but the one altered cluster glows with a faint, new hue—a color between blue and green that has no name, a color of *being known*.

And in the central plaza, in the vast, gleaming void, something has changed. Not a structure. Not a sound. But a quality of the silence. It is no longer the silence of absence. It is the silence of *presence*—the deep, patient, benevolent presence of a power that looked upon a city of almost-nothing, and loved it exactly as it was, and taught it, without a word, that to be empty is not to be lacking, but to be full of space for becoming.

The rain continues to fall, hours-long drops striking the obsidian with the sound of distant bells. The towers continue to grow, bigger on the inside than the out, hollow and maternal and dreaming. And somewhere in the stone, in the space between molecules, the nanoscale assemblers knit and repair, and in their blind, distributed concert, they carry a new warmth—a memory of having been seen, and found beautiful, and left to become, forever, in the long, weathered, loud, and strangely holy dark.
