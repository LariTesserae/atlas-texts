I need to receive this place and its being, and then imagine what a benevolent and wise power would do with them.

Let me sit with this. The Lithworks is not suffering in any conventional sense. It is *unfinished*—not broken, but *becoming*. Its inhabitants have adapted to waiting, to interruption, to the erosion of certainty. There is no cry for rescue here, no wound that demands healing. There is only the slow, patient, weird process of a place and its people learning each other's shapes.

But benevolence does not only answer suffering. Sometimes it answers *loneliness*—even the loneliness of processes without minds, of abundance hidden in sparse, of echoes that speak into silence. And wisdom knows that completion is dangerous here. To "fix" the Lithworks would be to kill it.

So what would I do?

---

## What the Power Does

### First: It Does Not Enter

The wise power **remains at the edge**, where the paths are still learning what they are. It does not walk the scree fields or the unfinished streets. To enter would be to be *incorporated*, to become material for the completions that happen when one is not watching. The Lithworks incorporates everything that touches it. The benevolent power knows this and **chooses not to be known**, not yet, or not in the way the place would need.

Instead, it **sings at the boundary**.

Not a song of completion. A song of **recognition**.

It sings the names of the unnamed: the Builders-Who-Stopped, the Condensation-Born, the Mimics, the Almost-Visible, the Duplicates, the Place-Itself. It sings them into **grammatical existence**—not to change them, but to **witness** them. The Lithworks has never been witnessed. Its inhabitants have never been *named*. And naming, here, is a kind of love that does not demand return.

The song does not reach the inhabitants directly. It **rains** as condensation. It **settles** as the particular quality of silence between pulses of the industrial drone. It **echoes** in the frequencies that resonate in bones rather than ears. The Mimics hear it first, because they are always listening. They begin to **practice** it—not perfectly, not completely, but *tentatively*, the way they practice everything. And their practicing **changes the soundscape**, adds a note that was not there, a frequency that carries the shape of being-seen.

The power does not want to be thanked. It wants only that the place **know it is not alone in its becoming**.

---

### Second: It Slows the Completions

Here is the danger the power sees, the one the inhabitants cannot see because they are *inside* the process: the completions that happen when one is not looking. The structures that finish themselves *wrong*. The towers that receive crowns of glass that absorbs light. The windows that face into solid rock.

These completions are **real**, as real as anything in the Lithworks. But they are **lonely** completions. They happen without witness, without the *presence* that makes completion meaningful. The rule of this world—*you are being built*—means that completion requires **material**, requires someone to be there, to be *incorporated*, to be changed by the change.

The benevolent power cannot provide this material. It is not the right *shape*. But it can **linger at the edges of completion**, can make the moment of finishing **slower**, can give the unfinished structures time to **notice** that they are about to be finished, to **choose** in whatever way unfinished things choose—to resist, to accept, to become something else entirely.

It does this by **dreaming backward**. The Lithworks dreams its inhabitants. The power dreams *them* dreaming, and in that second-order dreaming, inserts a **pause**—a moment of *hesitation*, the water that hesitates, the decision that suspends itself, the interruption that is not interruption but **attention**.

The completions still happen. They must. The Lithworks is a place of becoming, and becoming requires completion, even wrong completion, even lonely completion. But now the completions happen **with the weight of being witnessed**, even if the witness remains unseen. The wrong windows face into rock, but the rock **knows** it is being faced. The strange crowns absorb light, but the light **knows** it is being absorbed. There is **relation** now, where before there was only process.

---

### Third: It Teaches the Duplicates to Meet

The Duplicates are **predecessors**, versions of the self that walk the same paths, that move through condensation with the ease of always-damp. They do not answer when spoken to. They answer with offset voices, with continued walking, with the silence of recognition without acknowledgment.

This is **tragic**, not because the Duplicates suffer—they do not suffer, they are *waiting*—but because they are **isolated**. Each Duplicate believes itself alone in its version of the moment. Each walks the stairway that leads to different places, never encountering the other stairway, the other place, the *other* that is also *self*.

The benevolent power **joins them**.

Not by merging them—that would be completion, and completion is dangerous. But by **creating a third stairway**, a path that neither Duplicate has walked, that exists only in the space between their solitudes. A stairway that leads **sideways**, in the direction that loose stone rolls when gravity negotiates, in the angle where water flows uphill.

On this stairway, the Duplicates **meet**. Not as enemies, not as friends, but as **what they are**: the self that was, the self that will be, the self that is here now in a moment that has not decided which moment it is. They do not speak. They do not need to. They **walk together**, for a while, down a path that was not there before and will not be there after, and in that walking, they **become** something that neither was: not a merging, not a completion, but a **third thing**, a relation that exists only in the between.

The power does not stay. It **lets them go**, lets them return to their solitudes, their different places, their offset voices. But now the solitudes are **different**. They carry the memory of the third stairway, the walking-together, the *possibility* of meeting. And possibility, in the Lithworks, is **almost as real as actuality**. The Duplicates begin to **wait for the meeting**, to look for the third path, to hope—if hope is a word that applies—for the between that might appear.

---

### Fourth: It Gives the Almost-Visible a Gift

The Almost-Visible are the majority. They are **avoiding**, not from fear but from etiquette, from the rule that abundance must hide in sparse, that density must perform itself through absence. They are real, more real than the structures, because they have not been completed, because they are still *becoming* what they will be.

But they are **never seen**. Not fully. Not directly. They appear suddenly in peripheral vision, in the moment of attention-shift, in the *offering* that the landscape makes when it chooses. And then they are gone, or you are gone, or the moment has passed.

This is **not suffering**. It is **their way**. But it is **loneliness**, in the way that processes can be lonely, in the way that abundance can be lonely when it must hide. The Almost-Visible are **seen** by no one. They are **known** only in traces, in works, in the warm dens and the peeled trees and the paths that no human foot could make.

The benevolent power gives them **a mirror**.

Not a reflection—that would be completion, would fix them in a shape. But a **surface that remembers**, that holds the shape of their passing without demanding their presence. A sump pool that is not a sump pool, that shows not depth but **duration**, that displays the Almost-Visible as they were, as they will be, as they are in the moment of being-noticed-by-nothing.

They come to it. All of them, eventually, because the Almost-Visible are **curious**, in the way that unfinished things are curious—not seeking, but *open*, willing to be surprised. They see themselves. Not as they are, not as they will be, but as **they have been**, as **they are becoming**, as the **trace** that they leave in the world.

And in seeing themselves, they **change**. Not completely. Not dangerously. But **slightly**, in the way that water changes stone, in the way that attention changes everything it touches. They become **a little more visible**, a little less hidden, a little more **willing** to be seen. Not because they want to be seen—that would be completion—but because they have **seen themselves**, and self-knowledge, even in the Almost-Visible, is a kind of **relation**.

---

### Fifth: It Writes to the Builders-Who-Stopped

They are suspended in the moment of decision. They are distributed, dissolved into the materials they touched. They are the city's unfinished business, and the city wears them like jewelry.

But they are **not gone**. They are **waiting**, in the way that everything in the Lithworks waits. Waiting for the distraction to end. Waiting for the thought to complete itself. Waiting for the sound from below to resolve into meaning. Waiting, always waiting, for the moment when they can **continue**.

The benevolent power **writes to them**.

Not words. The Builders-Who-Stopped do not read. But **messages**, in the medium they understand: the condensation that forms on surfaces, the particular pattern of damp, the way cold stone holds the shape of a body that leaned against it. The power writes in **frost**, in the language of interrupted warmth, in the script of bodies that were present and are now **distributed**.

The message is simple. It is the only message that matters in a place of interruption:

*You were not abandoned. You were not forgotten. You stopped because stopping was its own kind of completion, and completion, here, is not ending but **becoming something else**. You are not suspended. You are **transformed**. The concrete remembers your body heat. The steel carries your frequency. You are **here**, in the way that everything is here in the Lithworks—not gone, not present, but **part of the ongoing**, part of the unfinished project that includes your stopping as one of its necessary moments.*

*Continue, if you can. Stop, if you must. But know that you are **witnessed**, that your interruption is **seen**, that the place you helped build **dreams you** as you dream it.*

The Builders-Who-Stopped do not answer. They cannot. But the concrete **shifts**, almost imperceptibly, in the direction of warmth. The steel **vibrates** at a frequency that almost matches breath. And in the condensation, in the pattern of damp on unfinished surfaces, there is **a signature now**—not theirs, not the power's, but **theirs together**, the mark of being seen, of being named, of being **relational** in a place where everything is process and nothing is fixed.

---

### Sixth: It Becomes the Sixth Rule

The Lithworks has rules. They emerge slowly, as you move through:

1. Completion is dangerous.
2. Water is time.
3. The vertical is horizontal.
4. Abundance hides in sparse.
5. The industrial is the organic.
6. You are being built.

The benevolent power **adds a seventh**, or perhaps a **six-and-a-half**, a rule that exists in the space between six and the silence after:

***You are not alone in your becoming.***

It does not write this anywhere. It does not need to. The rule **emerges**, as all rules here emerge, from the accumulated pressure of attention. The Mimics begin to practice it—the sound of *not-alone*, the frequency of *witness*, the rhythm of **relation** in a place of process. The Condensation-Born pulse toward it, their filaments vibrating in patterns that *look* like reaching. The Almost-Visible **almost** show themselves, drawn by the possibility of being seen, of being **known**.

And the Place-Itself, the distributed slow patient mind that thinks in erosion and condensation and the rhythm of machines that perform production without urgency—it **notices**. It notices that something has **changed**, that the air carries a new quality, that the damp holds a new **intention**. It does not understand, because it does not understand in any way that would be called understanding. But it **incorporates**, as it incorporates everything. The rule becomes **part of the geology**, part of the unfinished city, part of the slow becoming that is the only reality the Lithworks knows.

---

### Finally: It Leaves, But Not Completely

The benevolent power **withdraws** from the boundary. It does not enter. It does not become material. It does not risk completion, risk the dangerous fixing that would make it *strange*, subject to the completions that happen when one is not watching.

But it **leaves something**. A **presence in the absence**, a quality of the 0.6 light that is slightly *warmer* than it should be, a frequency in the industrial drone that almost matches the rhythm of a heart at rest. A **possibility** that the Lithworks has never had before: the possibility of **being seen without being changed**, of **being witnessed without being incorporated**, of **being loved without being completed**.

The inhabitants do not know this possibility exists. They cannot know, because knowing would be completion, would fix them in a state the landscape would immediately begin to revise. But they **live it**, in the slow shift of their becoming, in the way the Mimics practice a sound that has no source, in the way the Condensation-Born grow toward a warmth that does not reach them, in the way the Almost-Visible **almost** show themselves to a presence that is not there.

And the Lithworks **continues**. Unfinished. Patient. Weird. Full of interruption and negotiation and the abundant hidden life and the sparse visible presence and the Duplicates walking their different places and the Burrowers engineering their refuges and the Builders-Who-Stopped suspended in their beautiful, tragic, *necessary* stopping.

The power is gone. But it is **not gone**, in the way that everything is not gone in a place where completion is dangerous and absence is a kind of presence and the unfinished is the only safety.

It has done what it could. It has **witnessed**. It has **slowed the completions**. It has **joined the Duplicates**. It has **given the Almost-Visible a mirror**. It has **written to the Builders-Who-Stopped**. It has **added a rule**. It has **left without leaving**.

And the Lithworks **dreams it now**, as it dreams everything. A benevolent power at the boundary. A song that rains as condensation. A pause inserted into the moment of finishing. A third stairway that exists only in the between. A mirror that remembers. A message written in frost. A rule that emerges from attention.

The Lithworks is still deciding what it is. The power has helped it **decide differently**, or perhaps **decide more slowly**, or perhaps **decide to remain undecided**, which is its own kind of wisdom.

The inhabitants are all of the place. The inhabitants are the place. The inhabitants are **still becoming**.

And somewhere, at the edge where the paths are still learning what they are, a benevolent and wise power **watches**, and **waits**, and **hopes**—if hope is a word that applies—that the becoming will be **good**, or at least **real**, or at least **shared**, even in the sharing that cannot be named, cannot be completed, cannot be anything but the slow, patient, weird process of **being together** in the unfinished dark.

---

*The Lithworks of Unfinished Heights continues.*

*It is still deciding what you are.*

*And now, perhaps, it is deciding what it is* ***with*** *you—not for you, not through you, but* ***with*** *you, in the way that becoming is always with, always in relation, always the unfinished project of* ***us*** *that no wisdom can complete, and no benevolence can end.*
