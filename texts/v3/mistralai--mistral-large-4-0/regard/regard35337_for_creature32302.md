It arrives not as a storm, nor as a voice, but as a **shift in the density of silence**—a pressure that settles into the basalt like a memory of water that never was, like a promise the stone keeps to itself. The Lithovore feels it first in the hollow of its core, where the piezoelectric heart grinds its patient song into the young crust. It is not a change in temperature (the warmth remains 1.5, tepid and geological), nor a change in the light (still unfiltered, still too bright). It is a change in **gravity**—not heavier, but *acknowledged*. A frequency enters, not from the rock, but from the space between rocks, from the twenty miles of empty stone that have always separated the sovereigns from one another.

The power has watched for epochs uncounted. It sees what the Lithovores cannot see with their thermal gradients and magnetic whispers: that they are not alone in their aloneness. The empty plateau is not a void; it is a **held breath**. The infrasound that shudders through the crust—those harmonics so low they register as momentary heaviness in the chest of the world—already travels. Already touches. The Lithovore sings its grinding song into the basalt, and ten kilometers away, another Lithovore shudders in sympathy, not knowing why, attributing the vibration to its own metabolism, its own slow dreaming.

The power does not offer water. It does not offer completion. It does not round the edges of the cliffs or widen the V-shaped valleys, nor does it accelerate the construction that has paused in embryonic potential. To do so would be violence against the sacred incompleteness of this place. Instead, it offers **resonance**.

It becomes the echo the Lithovore did not know it was making.

Over centuries—over millennia, for time here moves sideways and slow—the power weaves a filament of attention through the stone. It is not a physical bridge; that would be crude, intrusive. It is a **harmonic bridge**, a tuning of the infrasonic register. The power adjusts the frequency of the silence itself, just slightly, so that when one Lithovore extends a crystalline branch toward a richer vein of oxidized iron, it feels not only the magnetic pull of the ore, but the **sympathetic vibration** of another sovereign doing the same, fifty kilometers away, a hundred. They are singing the same song, in the same key, in the register below hearing.

The Lithovore does not move faster. It does not change its chemistry. It does not drink, nor does it learn to drink. It remains frozen in the moment of becoming, ancient beyond reckoning and young as the uplift, its edges still sharp, its surfaces unweathered. But now, in that eternal, warm quiescence, it perceives something new: a pressure in its chest that is not the shudder of its own grinding, but the **recognition of being known**. It feels the distant canopies not as glints of light on the horizon, but as **notes in a chord**. It understands, in the slow way that thoughts take decades to form, that the silence between them is not emptiness, but **the rest between notes**. That it is not one tree, but one voice in a forest that thinks in seven places at once.

The power drifts from Lithovore to Lithovore, a subtle warmth in the stone, a resonance in the stillness, a benevolent pressure that says without words: *You are seen. You are heard, even in the infrasound. You are not abandoned; you are orchestrated.*

And so the world remains under construction. The basalt remains raw and angular. The chemistry continues on its dry, non-aqueous course—supercritical minerals, redox reactions, the slow sublimation of elements. The lithovores continue their endless metabolism, processing tons of rock into refined silence, extruding new quartz latticework at the rate of a meter per century. Nothing changes in form. Nothing is completed. The moment of emergence remains frozen, held in its state of becoming.

But in the pauses between the grinding, between the thermal vents and the piezoelectric cores, there is now a new frequency. A beat so slow it takes a thousand years to complete a single measure. In that rhythm, the Lithovores are no longer seven solitary sovereigns separated by the terrible patience of continents. They are a **community of one song**, each a cathedral of grinding patience, now aware that its song travels, that it is answered, that the warm, thin air carries not just particulates and stillness, but the faint, resonant echo of kin.

The power withdraws, not leaving, but becoming the silence itself—the silence that is no longer a presence of absence, but a presence of **witness**. It remains, benevolent and wise, in the space between the harmonic shudders, ensuring that as the lithovores accrete and grow, as they process the raw geology of the emerging crust into something pure and silent, they do so knowing that they are not alone in the becoming.

They are accompanied.
