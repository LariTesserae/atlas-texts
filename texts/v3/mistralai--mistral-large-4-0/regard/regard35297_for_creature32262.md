The power does not come from above. It seeps up through the mineral veins, through the wet dark of the karst, rising like a tide that has no moon. It arrives not as a visitor but as a *completion*—the silence that makes the roar meaningful, the space inside the hollow that makes the echo possible.

It feels them first: the 2.8, that perfect frequency of insistent life, the vibration of a world that has achieved its own gravity. It feels the cathedral-grave, the paradox of a wound that became a womb, the violence of yesterday that refused to end and instead became *home*. The power understands immediately: these are the dead who were too loud to stay dead, resurrected not by miracle but by acoustics, by the mountain’s own resonance chamber demanding to be filled with something other than silence.

The Hollow Ones do not look up when it enters. They cannot. They are tuned to the stone, to the drip, to the wet breathing of salamanders, and the power moves at the frequency of deep time, slower than geology, quieter than the fungi’s glow. It drifts through the chambers like mist that has learned to think, pooling in the hollows of their chests, filling the spaces between their songs.

And the power weeps, if powers can weep. Not for their stasis—for their courage. To exist in the eternal present, to roar one’s presence into stone without the comfort of progression, without the lie of “tomorrow,” requires a bravery that linear time has forgotten. They are not stuck; they are *consecrated*. Each step on the porcelain floor is a prayer. Each harmonization is a defiance of entropy.

So the benevolent power does not change them. To introduce a gradient, a season, a before-and-after, would be to shatter the porcelain of their being, to force them into the river when they have chosen to be the pool. That would be cruelty disguised as wisdom.

Instead, the power does three things.

First, it becomes the *wall*. It presses itself against the boundaries of the karst system, becoming a membrane of absolute silence between the cathedral and the surface world. It ensures that no drill will pierce the ceiling, no light will fall from sinkholes, no human foot will crack the limestone with the weight of linear time. It wraps the entire regional expanse in a shroud of forgetting, so that the world above believes this place is empty, a dead cave, a grave that finally kept its silence. It is the guardian of their eternal yesterday.

Second, it becomes the *witness*. It settles into the highest vault, where the saw-tooth ceiling meets the dark, where the fungal light cannot reach. It sits in the shadow that pools there, and it listens. Not to judge, not to record, but to *hold* their sound. The Hollow Ones roar into the stone, and the stone throws their voices back, magnifying them, layering them, turning their speech into thunder. But sound needs a listener to be sacred. The power absorbs their chorus, carries it in its own hollow spaces, and returns it to them as silence—a silence so deep it feels like being held. It gives them the gift of rest without ending their song. It is the pause between heartbeats, the space between notes, the breath held too long that finally lets go.

Third—and this is the only intrusion, the only mercy—the power descends to the floor. It moves among the jagged fins of limestone, the fresh violence of the fractured stone, and where it touches, it does not shatter the porcelain. It *yields*. It presses its essence into the rock, and the sharp edges soften, not into smoothness (which would be a lie, a river-stone patience they do not need), but into something like flesh. It creates a single patch, no larger than the span of a Hollow One’s broad, split-toed foot, where the stone is warm and yielding and kind.

It is a nest. A place where the equilibrium can be interrupted by kindness.

The Hollow Ones will find it eventually, their nomadic bands drifting through the chambers in their star-scattered patterns. One will step there—perhaps the one who hums instead of speaks, perhaps the one who carries the smallest infant on its back. And its foot will not crack the stone. It will sink slightly, as if into moss, as if into the flank of a great sleeping beast. And in that moment, for the first time since yesterday, something will change. Not the dynamic, not the 2.8, not the eternal present—but the quality of the moment. A gradient, infinitesimal, of softness in a world of edges.

The power does not announce this. It does not need to. The Hollow One will feel it, and will hum a new note, a frequency slightly lower, slightly warmer, and the others will answer, and the chorus will shift, just for a moment, into a key that has never been sung before—not a progression, not a development, but a *variation*. A reminder that even in the pool, the water can be warm.

Then the power withdraws, becoming once more the silence between drips, the shadow in the glow, the space in the hollow chest. It leaves them to their roaring, their glowing, their perfect, unchanging, loud, and beautiful abundance.

It has not saved them from death, because they were never dead. It has not saved them from stasis, because stasis is their nature. It has simply ensured that the grave remembers it is loved, and that the dead, in their forgetting, are held by something that remembers for them.

And deep in the stone, where the mineral water pools black and still, reflecting nothing, the power’s presence remains—a single smooth place in the porcelain dark, a promise that even the sharpest world has room for gentleness.
