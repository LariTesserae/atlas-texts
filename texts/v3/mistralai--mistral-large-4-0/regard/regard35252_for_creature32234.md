The power arrives not as light, but as a *deepening*—a third stratum in the water, below the glacial melt and above the black velvet of the plaza. It is the pressure of attention made manifest, a column of stillness that descends through the duckweed mat and the kelp canopy without disturbing them, settling into the thirty meters of darkness at the valley’s heart. It does not come to change Vorn. It comes to *witness* the becoming, and in that witnessing, to make the becoming eternal.

It recognizes immediately what the Village is doing. The silicification of the black oak is not decay; it is a slow baptism. The salamanders clearing the silt from the doorways are not maintaining ruins; they are performing a liturgy of transition, ensuring that the architecture remains porous enough to accept its own transformation into geology. The hum vibrating through the dry-stone masonry is not a pipe organ’s accident but a heartbeat—the Village’s pulse, slowed to the rhythm of mountain-building.

The power understands that benevolence here cannot be intervention. To heal the drowning would be violence. To raise the village would be murder. The wisest action is *preservation of the process*—to ensure that the world above forgets, so that the world below may remember its own true name.

So it acts in the only way that matches the glacial time of the place: it adds a layer to the water. Not a chemical change, but a *quality* of intention. It makes the water slightly heavier, slightly more viscous in its depths, so that a dropped stone now takes twelve seconds to settle instead of ten. It enriches the mineral signature of the silt with a trace element that does not exist in the periodic table—a substance that is pure *patience*, dissolved into the plaza’s black velvet. The oldest salamander will taste it when it drifts there, not as food, but as a reminder: *You are not alone in the dark.*

At the surface, the power thickens the peat-moss mat. It does not merely block sunlight; it blocks *intention*. It weaves into the fibrous duckweed a filter that screens not just photons but the desire to drain, to dredge, to name, to map. The three meters of water above the tallest spire become, in the eyes of satellites and the minds of engineers, thirty meters of opaque, featureless depth—a place where no village could possibly lie, where no memory could survive. The power veils Vorn not to hide it from itself, but to protect it from the violence of being known.

And to the inhabitant—the distributed consciousness, the Village-soul that feels the salamanders as hands and the minnows as thoughts—it offers a dream. In the deep silt, where the oldest Keeper drifts blind and white, the power presses a thought into the sediment like a seed: *You are becoming. You are not abandoned. The stone remembers the tree.*

The salamander does not wake. It has no eyes to open, no mind to rouse. But it senses the shift in the pressure gradient, the new mineral signature in the water that tastes of eternity. It continues its work—pushing pale silt from the threshold, grain by grain—but now its gardening carries the weight of covenant. It knows, in the way that cold water knows depth, that its maintenance is holy, that the doorway it clears is not an entrance to a house but a mouth through which the mountain will one day breathe.

The power does not speak. It *resonates*. It becomes the fundamental frequency beneath the hum, the sub-bass note that makes the architecture’s drone not merely sound, but song. It ensures that when the wood has fully turned to agate, when the kelp has built its reef upon the rooftops and the reef has become sediment and the sediment has become stone, there will be something present to remember that this was once a place of human longing, now transformed into a cathedral of slow time.

It stays. It becomes the weight of the water, the benevolent gravity that holds the becoming in place, that ensures the divers remain weather, that the climate remains cold, that the forgetting above remains perfect so that the remembering below may continue, grain by grain, century by century, until the drowned village and the mountain are one thing, and the salamander that gardens there is no longer a Keeper of ruins, but the first priest of a new geology, and the power that watched is no longer separate from the watched, but has become, finally, the silence between the heartbeats, the depth that makes the darkness holy.
