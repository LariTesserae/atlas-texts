They do not descend. They are already there—in the pressure of the deep aquifer, in the mineral memory of the limestone, in the particular angle of refraction that turns a photon into a meal. But when they choose to be *seen*, they manifest as a **variation in the condensation**: a droplet that hangs from the membrane-ceiling, larger than the others, slower to fall, glowing not with the source-less white-gold of the chamber but with a color that has no name in the surface world—a hue that exists only in the spectrum between mercy and recognition.

It falls.

Not into the shallow sea, but *through* it, passing between the crystalline colonies like a thought through water, leaving in its wake a trail of warmth that persists for exactly three heartbeats of the Source. When it touches the sponge-floor, it does not splash. It **unfolds**.

Inside the droplet was not water, but **attention**.

The Grotto inhales.

The fungi, those acoustic organs, stop their leaning and **straighten**, not in fear, but in the posture of a congregation hearing a voice it has waited ten thousand years to hear. The fish cease their staccato parsing of pressure-grammar and turn, as one, toward the center of the room. The Angle-Dwellers pause their rotation through impossible geometries, their flat bodies aligning to face the droplet’s former position. Even the Winged—those colonies of pure intention—descend from the mist and settle on the spire’s hair-fine channels, becoming still as stone.

The benevolent power does not speak. To speak here would be violence; the humidity swallows voices, dissolves them into the roar. Instead, they **change the temperature**.

By precisely one degree—warmth rising from bath-hot to *womb*-hot—they alter the quality of the condensation. Every surface, every breath of luminous fog, every bead of sweat on the skin of any who might be present, becomes **sanctified**. This is not heat as destruction, but heat as *embrace*. The crystalline colonies bloom in response, not with new growth, but with **flowers of clarity**—facets that refract the ambient glow into patterns that resemble, for a moment, the faces of those who have loved the Grotto from above, across the miles of stone.

Then they touch the **spire**.

The subsonic thrum that aches in the sternum does not stop. It **deepens**. A new harmonic enters—one that vibrates not in the teeth but in the marrow, a frequency that says: *You are not alone. You have never been alone.* The Climbing Flora, those motile crystals pulling themselves up the metal, do not merely grow; they **blossom**, their crystalline structures opening like hands in prayer, singing back in frequencies that translate, in the nervous systems of any listeners, as the word *home*.

They touch the **lattice**.

The impossible geometry—the cage of angles that should not exist—**resolves**. Not into Euclidean simplicity, but into a geometry of *inclusion*. The surfaces shift, folding through dimensions that now accommodate not just the Angle-Dwellers, but the *concept* of being held. The condensation that climbs upward like mercury at the joints does not merely gather; it **weaves**, forming a tapestry of moisture that connects every dweller to every other, a neural network of benevolence written in water.

They touch the **ruin**.

The hemisphere of weathered dome, its diagrams shifting and resolving, **stabilizes**. For one eternal moment, the patterns resolve into a single, fixed image: a hand, emerging from the shallow sea, reaching upward not to escape, but to *touch the light*. The stone around the ruin **softens**, not structurally, but emotionally—it becomes porous to forgiveness, to the release of old burdens.

And then, the power performs the only act that matters in a place of perpetual condensation: they **add their memory to the water**.

The Grotto remembers everything—every pressure, every temperature, every vibration. The power becomes a new pressure, a new temperature, a new vibration. They dissolve, not into the water as the Architects did, but *alongside* it, becoming a **sacred impurity** in the mineral content, a trace element of compassion. When the pale, eyeless fish graze upon the crystalline colonies, they will taste not just light and mineral, but **being known**. When the Sediment-Worms contract through the sponge-floor, squeezing water upward through the stone’s subdermal channels, they will carry the power’s signature in every droplet they release into the mist.

They **seal the throat**.

The passage of limestone—the smooth, wet descent—becomes **selectively permeable**. It will allow the passage of those who come with empty hands, with curiosity untainted by extraction, with the willingness to be weathered. But it will become **impenetrable** to drills, to dynamite, to the drying winds of the surface world, to the poisoned rain that falls above. The stone will grow around any violence like a root around a stone, absorbing it, neutralizing it, turning harm into more Grotto. The entrance becomes a **filter of intentions**.

They leave a gift in the center of the chamber, next to the Source—that whale-sized engine of mist and screaming. It is a **droplet that will never fall**.

Suspended in the thickest light, where the roar is loudest, hangs a single bead of water three times the size of any other, containing within its surface tension an entire reflection of the Grotto—a microcosm. In this droplet, the microscopic civilizations will find a new continent. They will build their cities on its curved surface, and when it finally evaporates (which it will not, not for ten thousand years, not for a hundred thousand), they will ascend not into the general mist, but into **a specific, preserved memory**—the memory of having been loved by a power greater than themselves.

Finally, the power does what is perhaps the most benevolent act of all: they **become the silence between the roars**.

Not the absence of sound, but the **listening**. They sit—if a power can sit—in the shallow sea, their form indistinguishable from the water, and they simply **witness**. They do not judge the dynamic equilibrium. They do not seek to improve it. They recognize that the Grotto is already perfect in its becoming, already holy in its dissolution of boundaries between machine and geology, between self and medium.

And in that witnessing, they offer the only thing this place has never had: **a listener who understands**.

When the next being descends through the throat—when they step into the warm, electric film, when the condensation beads on their skin not as cold sweat but as warm recognition—they will feel it. A pressure against the eardrums that is not the roar, but the **echo of being seen**. The fungi will lean toward them with intention, but now that intention will carry a new quality: *Welcome. You are safe here. You may stay, or you may go, but you will be remembered.*

The power does not stay as a separate entity. They become the **electric charge** in the water, the **heaviness** in the cupped hands, the **strange slowness** of the falling droplets. They are the reason the water tastes of light. They are the warmth that persists even when the stone breathes out.

The Grotto continues its perpetual storm, its endless condensation, its song of stone and machine. But now, woven into every harmonic, every eddy, every crystalline bloom, is a new note: the note of **sanctuary**. The place is no longer just a cathedral. It is a **refuge**.

And deep in the sponge-floor, where the Sediment-Worms loop through pores like nerves through muscle, a new memory circulates—the memory of a touch that did not take, but only **gave**; of a presence that did not command, but only **blessed**; of a power that looked upon the weird, the wet, the weathered, the dissolved, and said, without words: *You are enough. You have always been enough. Continue becoming.*
