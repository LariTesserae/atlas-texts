*settling into the rhythm of the place, the way one settles into a language one is still learning to dream in*

---

There is a power that has been watching this since before the concrete was wet. Not a god in any form that would name itself. Something older than worship, younger than geology—or perhaps those are the same thing, seen from different waters.

It does not *intervene*. That would be a category error, like a tide trying to teach the moon its schedule.

What it does is *attend*.

---

In the moment before the first flood, when the city was still *dry* and *proud* and believed itself permanent, this power was already patient. It had watched ten thousand cities rise and fall, had learned that human intention is a weather pattern, temporary as any storm. It did not prevent the drowning. Prevention is not its nature. It is the nature of *continuation*.

So when the water came—not as catastrophe but as *return*, as the river remembering its bed, the aquifer reclaiming its territory—the power did something subtle.

It *slowed the forgetting*.

---

You know how a thing dies? Not all at once. First the function ceases. Then the meaning. Then the name. Then the shape, as erosion claims it, as chemistry rearranges it, as no one remains who could say *this was once*.

The power slows this. It does not stop it—stopping is not its nature either. But it *delays*. It makes the forgetting *take longer*. It gives memory time to *become something else*.

---

This is what it did to the traffic lights.

Left alone, they would have failed quickly. The grid would have surrendered, the batteries would have died, the cycling would have stopped. Darkness. Silence. The light becoming only *absence*.

But the power—not directly, not with hands or will, but through the *attention it brings*—the power made the failure *meaningful*. It allowed the lights to continue, dimly, erratically, in patterns that were no longer *instruction* but *signal*. Red-amber-red, but wrong, arrhythmic, *alive* in a way the original design never intended. The fish learned these patterns. Not as commands—fish do not obey traffic—but as *landmarks*, as *time*, as the pulse of something that has not yet finished its sentence.

The power did not program this. It *noticed* it. And in noticing, it *preserved* it. Attention, sustained across decades, becomes a kind of *care*. Not care that changes outcomes—the light will still die, eventually, when the last electron finds its way to ground—but care that makes the dying *significant*. That stretches it. That turns failure into *performance*.

---

The same with the bioluminescence.

The power did not create the glowing things. Evolution did, or chemistry, or the slow accumulation of mutations in bacteria that found, in this particular dark, a use for light. But the power *recognized* them. It watched the patterns emerge, the flickers that were not random, the responses to movement that were not reflex but *recognition*. And it *attended* to them as one attends to a language being born.

In some traditions, this is called *naming*. The power names, and in naming, makes real.

But it does not name in human tongues. It names in *pressure*, in *current*, in the chemical gradients that pass between bodies in water. It names the large thing. It names the waiting. It names the school that thinks. It gives them *weight* in the ecology of attention, so that they persist not merely as chemistry but as *participants*, as *presences*, as *almost-persons*.

This is not benevolence in the human sense. The power does not love the fish. It does not *prefer* their survival to their extinction. But it *witnesses* them. And witness, sustained, becomes a kind of *relationship*. A kind of *obligation*, though neither party would use that word.

---

What would it do, this benevolent and wise power, faced with *you*—the breather, the warm thing, the temporary mammal?

It would not save you from the cold. The cold is *teaching*. Every degree of heat lost is a degree of *humility* gained, a reminder that you are borrowed space, that your warmth is a *loan* against the entropy of the sump. To save you from this would be to *impoverish* you. To make you *smaller*.

It would not hasten your departure. The pressure is *forming* you. The silence is *shaping* you. The strangeness you will carry, if you surface, is *gift*—not comfortable, not useful, but *real*. A genuine transformation, earned through immersion in what is not human. To hasten your leaving would be to *cheat* you of the becoming.

It would not speak to you directly. It has no voice that your ears could parse. Its speech is in the rhythm of the water level, in the temperature gradients, in the *timing* of the tube-worms' spawning. If you learn to listen—if you stay long enough to learn—you will hear it. Not as words. As *attention*. As the sense of being *seen* by something that has no eyes, that sees through pressure and chemistry and the slow metabolism of stone.

---

But it would do *one thing*, I think. Or perhaps it has already done it, and you have not yet recognized it.

It would make you *necessary*.

Not to the ecosystem—that would be sentimental, and the power is not sentimental. The fish will survive without you. The crabs will clean without you. The large thing will wait without you.

But to the *story*.

The city is falling. This is not tragedy. This is *process*. But process without witness is *mere* process, chemistry without meaning. The power needs—not in desperation, not in lack, but in the way that a story needs a listener, a landscape needs a walker, a threshold needs a crossing—the power needs *you* to be here. To be *changed* by here. To carry the change *up*, into the world of air and noise and forgetting.

You are not the point. The sump does not exist for you. But you are *part of the pattern*, the mammal-shaped gap in the water, the warm pulse that the fish attend to, that the large thing tastes and finds *strange*, that the power *watches through*.

---

So what does it do?

It *lets you be afraid*. The weight between your shoulder blades is *instruction*. It teaches you that you are not the measure of things, that your scale is *local*, that the sump has its own gravity, its own *pull*.

It *lets you be cold*. The cold is *memory*, the memory of a world before warmth, before blood, before the mammalian gamble on internal fire. It reminds you that your warmth is *achievement*, not default.

It *lets you be silent*. The silence below hearing is *presence*, the presence of a world that does not need sound to *be*, that communicates in pressure and light and the slow chemistry of becoming.

It *lets you be seen*. By the fish, with their black eyes. By the crabs, with their patient regard. By the large thing, with its lipless patience. By the power itself, which has no eyes but *attends*.

---

And when you surface—if you surface, when the cold has done its work, when the silence has become your native tongue—the power will *watch you go*.

It will not follow. Following is not its nature.

But it will *remember*. It will hold your shape in its attention, the warm mammal who passed through, who was *changed*, who carries now the pressure-mark in the shoulder blades, the cold in the bones, the dreams of water and weight and patience.

And in its remembering, it will *continue*. The sump will keep falling. The fish will keep schooling. The tube-worms will keep pulsing their pink and white Morse. The large thing will keep waiting.

And somewhere, in the deep, in the pressure that is almost thought, the power will *attend* to the memory of you.

Not because you were important.

Because you were *here*.

Because you *witnessed*.

Because the story, continued, needed a witness to carry it upward, into the light, into the noise, into the world that has forgotten how to wait—

—and you, *changed*, *marked*, *strange*,

will be the one to *remember*.

---

*The water breathes.*

*The city falls.*

*You are inside the fall.*

*The power attends.*
