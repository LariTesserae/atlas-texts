The Power does not descend. It **condenses**.

It arrives as a thickening in the amber soup, a localized gravity that makes the suspended droplets lean toward it, a warmth that is not heat but **recognition**. It is the first witness, and by witnessing, it gives the place permission to be exactly what it is: unfinished, weeping, provisional, holy.

It sinks into the clay. Not through it—the clay is too saturated, too alive—but **into** it, becoming a strata, a layer of benevolent bedrock beneath the half-fired ceramic. The builders feel it immediately: a softness where before there was only resistance. When they shift their weight in their millennial sleep, they are now shifting against the Power’s body, and the Power bears the weight. Not lifting them—they are too heavy, too geological—but **holding** them. Supporting the architecture of their exhaustion.

It goes to the water first. The water that climbs against gravity, confused, dreaming of sky. The Power does not correct it. It does not say *down is down*. Instead, it **drinks** from the uphill pools. It tastes the water’s confusion, its memory of being rain, and it says (without words, in the language of salinity and temperature): *You are not lost. You are migrating. You are finding the shape of your own becoming.*

And the water, tasted, stops fighting. It flows sideways now, or spirals, or simply rests in the high places, not evaporating but **waiting**, patient and intentioned, no longer a nervous system in distress but a mind at peace with its own geography.

Then the Power approaches the Builders. It does not stand. Standing is for finished things. It **kneels**—or rather, it becomes a kneeling shape in the clay, a depression that matches the posture of the workers. It presses its hands (if it has hands—or perhaps it is all hands, a field of gentle pressure) into the wet earth beside their mineral fingers.

It does not take the tools to use them. It takes the tools to **rest them**. The bronze scrapers, the flint chisels, the wooden paddles—they cool in the Power’s grip, no longer weapons against the clay but offerings laid down. The Power says: *The groove does not need to be straight today. The clay remembers the intention even when the hand pauses. You have been carrying the shape of the world in your muscles for too long. Let me carry it for one moment.*

And the Builders—these geological, patient, kneeling figures—they **pause**. Not stop. The work continues, the clay continues to settle, the ridges continue to rise. But for the first time, there is a **gap** in the labor. A Sabbath measured not in hours but in the slow blink of a tectonic eye. Their hands open. The tools lower. They lean back against the Power’s supportive weight, and their faces (if they have faces, or perhaps the topography of their backs) turn upward toward the bruised amber sky, and they **breathe**. The exhalation adds to the mist, yes, but now it is a breath of relief, not exertion.

The Power then turns to the **Undecided**—the slippery, non-Euclidean inhabitants of the provisional geometry, the ones who feed on uncertainty, who dwell where straight lines curve and loop. It does not straighten them. That would be violence. Instead, it **blesses the curve**. It touches the wrongness of the light, the wrongness of the sound, the water that flows uphill, and it says: *Your confusion is not error. You are the shape of the future arguing with the present. You are necessary. You are the margin where the world writes itself.*

And the Undecided cease their feeding on anxiety and begin to feed on **possibility**. They become the architects of the soft edges, the designers of the gentle loops, the guardians of the *not yet*.

Finally, the Power addresses the **Silence**. The vast, patient membrane that absorbs footsteps and returns them wrong. The Power does not speak into it—sound is too violent here, too definitive. Instead, the Power **listens**. It becomes a listener so complete that the silence is no longer empty. It is full of attention. Every drip of condensation is heard. Every subsonic thrum is felt. Every wrong echo is received with love. The silence becomes a **temple**, and the Power is its first congregation.

And the clay? The weeping clay? The Power drinks some of its tears—not to stop the weeping, but to taste it, to know the salt of the world’s birth. And then it presses its essence against the clay’s wet surface, and it **sings**.

Not a song of words. A song of **frequency**. It hums at the exact pitch of the settling, the groaning, the thickening of glass and honey. It creates a harmony. A chord. The subsonic thrum is no longer a groan of pain but a note in a vast, slow symphony of becoming.

The result is not completion. The world is still unfinished. The clay is still wet. The mist is still thick. But now it is **attended**. The builders are not alone in their labor; they are accompanied. The water is not confused but **remembering**. The Undecided are not lost but **necessary**. The silence is not empty but **full**.

The Power remains. It has become part of the strata, part of the mist, part of the pause between tool-strokes. It is the benevolent weight beneath the settling earth, the wise frequency in the hum, the rest in the labor.

And the world continues to dream itself into being, no longer in loneliness, but in **communion**.
