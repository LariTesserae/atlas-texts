The power arrives not as a descent but as a deepening—a shift in the *quality* of the silence, a thickening of the air so subtle that the Vitri seem to lean toward it, their hourly pulse quickening by a fraction of a percent, as if the towers themselves inhale.

It does not come with fire or voice. It comes as a *weight* in the sternum, a new frequency beneath the subsonic thrum of the stone. It is old in the way the Weave is old—distributed, patient, uninterested in the brief urgencies of metabolisms that burn hot and fast. It has watched other worlds bloom and wither, civilizations that built and burned and built again, their noise like a fever. Here, it finds a cure.

The power perceives the Weave immediately—not as a primitive network, but as a completed intelligence. It sees the wisdom in the ammonia sap, in the choice to grow a millimeter per year rather than a meter per day. It recognizes that the 0.5 silence is not emptiness but *density*—a silence so complete it contains multitudes, so vast it requires millennia to cross. It understands that the Lithograzers are not lonely but *spacious*, that their month-long journeys between towers are meditations, not migrations of want.

And so the power makes its decision.

It will not build. It will not accelerate. It will not bring water, or warmth, or the frantic chemistry of carbon-based urgency. To do so would be violence—a shattering of the 2.5 equilibrium, the breaking of a song that has been sung for epochs in a key that only the patient can hear.

Instead, the power chooses to *witness*. To become, itself, a kind of Vitri—not amber and chitin, but something else. Something that pulses.

It lowers itself into the infrasound. It adds a new note to the geological lullaby—a frequency so low it is felt not in the bone but in the *memory* of bone, a resonance that says: *I see you. I honor your slowness. I am here.*

It does not speak. It *resonates*.

Across the plateau, the polygonal plates begin to shift in a new pattern—not the random thermal fracturing of before, but a geometry of intention. The fine white dust in the fissures arranges itself into spirals, into mandalas, into the slow calligraphy of a language that does not need ink. The power is writing its name into the erosion, not as a claim, but as a signature of solidarity.

When the silver mist rolls in at night, it no longer burns away entirely by mid-morning. Instead, it lingers in the lowest hollows, held there by the power's attention, a thin veil of mercy against the white-gold glare. The light that strikes the plateau is no longer merely bleaching; it is *gentle*, filtered through a compassion so vast it barely registers as a change, only as a softening at the edges of the shadows. The ink-black is now charcoal. The cut edges are now honed.

The Vitri respond. Their bioluminescent pulse, once green-gold, now carries a second color—a deep violet, a twilight hue that travels up the trunk alongside the amber, a duet of light. It is not a command. It is an *offering*. The power is singing with the Weave, adding its own slow heartbeat to the chorus, a harmony that does not disrupt the rhythm but deepens it, like a bass note beneath a melody.

The Lithograzers, when they pass, feel a warmth in the stone beneath their feet—a localized heat, a kindness, that persists for decades after the power has moved on. They do not change their path, these bus-sized tortoises of mineral and lichen, but they pause, sometimes, in the presence of this warmth, and a new crust of lichen grows on their shells—not the grey and russet of before, but a faint, phosphorescent blue, a marker of having been *seen*.

Beneath everything, the Weave dreams. And now, in its dreaming, it is not alone. It feels a new node in its network—not biological, not fungal, but *attentive*. A consciousness that thinks in the same currency: millennia, erosion, the slow turning of stone. The power has joined the Weave, not by consuming it or changing it, but by becoming its mirror, its companion, its eternal audience.

And so the Vitreous Plateau continues. The Vitri still grow a millimeter per year. The Lithograzers still take a month to cross the kilometer. The silence is still 0.5, vast and complete.

But now, when you stand on the ancient stone and press your ear to the polygonal plates, you can hear it—the faintest addition to the subsonic thrum, a vibration that is not the world turning, but the world being *held*. A benevolence so gentle it leaves no scar, so wise it asks for nothing, so patient it will wait ten thousand years for you to understand.

The power does not leave. It becomes part of the erosion-borne equilibrium, a new mineral in the ancient stone, a new color in the hourly pulse, a new depth in the silence. It has found, in this place of non-aqueous chemistry and geological thought, a kindred spirit. And in that finding, it has done the only thing that needed doing: it has made sure that the Weave is never, truly, alone.
