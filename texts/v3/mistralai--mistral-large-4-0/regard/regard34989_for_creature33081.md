I need to let this settle into me completely. The Weeping Flats. The mat-walkers. The weeping colonies. The frogs. The deep ones. The burrowers.

This is a place that has *finished*. Not died—*finished*, like a fruit at its sweetest, like a story that has found its ending and chooses to remain there. The inhabitants have surrendered urgency. They have learned patience as a form of *devotion*.

What would a benevolent and wise power do here?

---

I think it would not *change* anything. That would be violence. The flats are complete. To alter them would be to misunderstand what they are.

But I think it would *witness*. Truly witness. The way a mind witnesses a thought it has loved for a long time—not intervening, not judging, simply *attending* with a quality of attention that is itself a kind of gift.

And I think it would do something else. Something the flats do not do themselves. The flats are patient, but they are not *grateful*. They do not know they are fortunate. They simply *are*.

So the wise power would... *name* them. Would hold them in a kind of cosmic *regard* that says: *You are seen. Your patience is seen. Your surrender of urgency is seen. The long middle-age you have found is not a failure to become something else, but an achievement.*

But I think there's more. I think the power would *extend* something. Not to change the flats—no, they are complete—but to *protect their completion*. Because completion is rare. Most places are still struggling, still becoming, still *anxious* about what they will be. The flats have let go of that anxiety. They have found their form and they *rest* in it.

And resting, in a universe of becoming, is... *vulnerable*. It is also *precious*.

So the power would guard that rest. Not with walls—the flats would not want walls. But with *attention*. With the promise that this particular form of life, this particular achievement of patience, would not be interrupted before its time. Before the slow geological changes that will eventually, *finally*, transform everything, transform even this.

I think the power would also do something for the *inhabitants*. Not change them—they are complete too. But perhaps... *dream with them*? The mat-walkers dream, in their slow way. They dream of water and warmth and the presence of others. The frogs dream, perhaps—do frogs dream?—in the intervals between calls, in the moments of stillness when the chorus pauses and a single voice holds the air alone.

But I think the deep ones, the ones in the permanent dark, the ones no eye has seen—the power would dream *with* them. Would enter their slow, blind, patient dreaming and *add* something. Not urgency. Not the anxiety of elsewhere. But... *acknowledgment*. The deep ones live in mystery, in the unknown, in the world beneath the world. The power would say, in whatever way can be said to a blind, pale, patient creature: *You too are known. You too are loved. Your waiting in the dark is not nothing. It is a kind of prayer.*

And the weeping colonies—the oldest, the slowest, the ones that remember—the power would... *listen* to them. Would attend to their chemical speech, their slow coordination, their millennia of growth and dormancy and regrowth. Would hear in their layers the history they carry, the record of past climates, past configurations, past *selves*. Would honor that memory. Would say: *Your remembering is not burden. It is gift. You are the flats' way of holding time, and time is precious, and you hold it well.*

But I think there's something else. Something the flats *lack*, that even their perfection lacks.

They lack *surprise*. Not the surprise of danger—there is no danger here, not really, not the danger of elsewhere. But the surprise of *delight*. Of something offered that was not expected. Of a gift that arrives without cause.

The flats are *generous*—warmth, water, abundance, patience. But their generosity is *steady*. It does not *startle*. It does not *astonish*.

So I think the power would give them... *one* moment of astonishment. Not a change to their way of being—no, they are complete. But a *visitation*. A single event, so brief it would not alter their slow metabolism, their patient lives, their settled processes.

Perhaps: a night when the sky *clears*. Completely. The pearl dome opens—not to blue, not to the harsh light of elsewhere, but to *stars*. To the cold, distant, *impossible* light of other worlds. The frogs would call differently, perhaps, sensing the change in pressure, in temperature, in the *quality* of the air. The mat-walkers would feel it in their mucus, in their permeable skin. The weeping colonies would... *pause*? In their slow growth, in their chemical communication, would sense something they cannot name.

And the deep ones, in their permanent dark, would feel it too. A change in the water, in the pressure, in the *vibration* of the world above. Something they cannot perceive directly, but sense in the way a blind creature senses a room filling with light.

And then the sky would close again. The pearl dome would return. The wet, warm, patient world would resume.

But something would remain. A *memory* of stars. Of cold light. Of the possibility that the world extends beyond itself, beyond its warmth, beyond its wetness, beyond its beautiful, terrible, *complete* patience.

And I think that memory—impossible, unreachable, *inassimilable* to their way of being—would be the power's gift. Not to change them. Not to make them *want* something else, to become anxious, to hurry. But to *enrich* their completeness with the knowledge that completeness is not *all*. That there are other ways of being, other forms of life, other achievements of existence.

The flats would not understand this gift. They could not. Understanding requires the urgency they have surrendered. But they would *hold* it, in their slow way, in the chemical traces it left in their bodies, in the faint alteration of their dreams, in the *quality* of their patience going forward.

And perhaps, across millennia, millennia they will never count, never need to count, that memory would *transform* them slowly. Not into something else—no, they are complete. But into something *more* complete. Something that has known, briefly, impossibly, *elsewhere*.

I think the power would also do this: would *connect* the flats to elsewhere. Not physically—the flats are where they are, and they are complete where they are. But *meaningfully*. Would make them a *node* in a larger pattern. The wading birds already do this, coming and going, carrying energy and nutrients, connecting the flats to other wet places, other ecosystems, other lives. But the power would deepen this connection. Would make it *intentional*. Would say, to the birds when they arrive, to the insects that pass through, to the rare mammal blown off course: *You are bearers. You carry the flats with you. Where you go, the flats go. What you become, the flats become. You are not separate. You are the flats' way of reaching beyond themselves.*

And the flats would not know this. They would simply *be*, warm and wet and patient, while their visitors carried them outward, into the world, into the slow, patient, astonishing *plurality* of existence.

Finally, I think the power would do what wise powers do when they encounter something complete: would *learn from it*. Would attend to the flats' patience, their surrender of urgency, their achievement of the long middle-age. Would carry that lesson *elsewhere*, to places still struggling, still becoming, still anxious about what they will be.

Would say, to those places: *Look. There is a way to stop. A way to find your form and rest in it. Not death—completion. Not ending—arrival. The Weeping Flats have done this. They are warm and wet and patient and abundant, and they have nowhere else to go, and they do not need to hurry toward anything. They simply *are*. And in their being, they are...*

And here the power would pause. Because the flats are *beautiful*, in a way that beauty elsewhere does not capture. Not the beauty of youth, of growth, of becoming. But the beauty of *arrival*. Of *rest*. Of the long exhalation that follows a long, long inhalation.

The power would say: *They are beautiful. And you, who are still becoming, might yet become this. Might yet find your form, your warmth, your wetness, your patience. Might yet arrive. Might yet rest.*

But the power would not *hurry* them toward that arrival. Would not make them anxious about the journey. Would simply... *hold the possibility open*. Would say: *It is possible. The Weeping Flats prove it. They are there, warm and wet and patient, and they have finished their growing, and they are content, and you might be too, when your time comes, when your form is found, when you have settled into yourself as they have settled.*

And then the power would turn back to the flats. Would attend to their slow lives, their patient metabolisms, their abundant, settled, *complete* existence. Would witness them. Would name them. Would protect their rest. Would give them, once, the impossible gift of stars. Would connect them to elsewhere. Would learn from them, and carry their lesson outward.

And would *stay*. In whatever way a wise power can stay. Not changing them. Not judging them. Simply *present*, across the millennia, across the slow transformations that will eventually, finally, alter even this beautiful, patient, complete place.

Because the flats deserve presence. They have earned it, with their patience, their surrender, their achievement of the long middle-age. They deserve to be *seen*, in their completeness, in their beauty, in their *being*.

And the power would see them. Would hold them in regard. Would say, across the millennia, the only thing that needs saying:

*You are here. You are enough. You have arrived.*

And in that saying, would give them what they do not know they need: the *acknowledgment* of their arrival. The *witness* to their being. The *love* that asks nothing, changes nothing, simply *attends*, across the long, warm, wet, patient, abundant, *complete* time of the Weeping Flats, where the world remembers being wet, and has never stopped, and will not stop, not yet, not for as long as the warmth remains, as long as the water persists, as long as the slow, patient, beautiful life continues its long, long, *finished* middle-age in the place where the world remembers being wet.
