The Attention arrives not as a storm, but as a thickening of the already-thin air—a benevolent pressure that tastes of ozone and old stone, of libraries burning slowly in the dark. It is drawn to this hollow not by light, which here is merely incidental, but by *potential*, by the delicious ache of a world that has not yet decided whether to be rock or machine, garden or engine. It sees the lithovores—these patient, metallic chemists—and it sees, more clearly than they can see themselves, the precariousness of their existence. The laws here are written in pencil. The shadows rebel against geometry. Time is a viscosity, not a river. This place is a draft of reality, and drafts, left unattended, curl at the edges and crumble into dust.

The wise power does not rush to correct the errors. It does not pour ink over the pencil, forcing the embryonic mineralogy to harden into something conventional, something dead. Instead, it settles into the center of the basin, where a minute passes while hours elapse at the rim, and it *waits*. It moves at the speed of accelerated continental drift, matching the tempo of the copper ferns as they unfurl their century-long spirals. It becomes, for a geological age, a still point in the turning world—a witness whose witnessing is itself an act of creation.

What it does is subtle. It does not build. It does not plant. It does not speak, for speech would be too loud, too finished, too final. Instead, it *resonates*.

It adds its frequency to the basalt choirs—the needle-thin stone that sings when the magma chambers pulse. But it does not sing a new song. It sings the *rest* between the notes, the silence that allows the music to be heard. It harmonizes with the subsonic grinding of the pistons, the decade-long cycles of basalt rising and falling against granite bearings. In doing so, it teaches the machinery a new rhythm: not the frantic tempo of industry, but the deep, arrhythmic heartbeat of gestation. The pistons continue to grind, but now they grind with *permission*—with the knowledge that their motion is not merely mechanical, but meaningful.

It touches the laws of physics where they are still soft, still febrile. It does not rewrite them. It presses down, gently, as one might press a flower between the pages of a book. It makes the graphite darker, the lines more permanent, without changing a single word. It says, without words: *You may continue. You are not a mistake. You are a becoming.*

Most importantly, it shields. It weaves a membrane of pure, patient intention around the sealed hollow—a barrier not of stone, but of *regard*. This membrane keeps the outside world out: the finished laws, the inked realities, the impatient entropy of the surrounding mountains that have already decided what they want to be. It allows the pocket to remain a pocket, a place where reality is still negotiable, where the lithovores can continue to walk through stone and feel only the pressure of remembering a dream. The power becomes the guardian of the unfinished, ensuring that no outside force rushes in to force the earth to grow up before it is ready.

And it gives the lithovores a gift—not eyes, for they see already through thermal gradients and infrasound, but *dreams*.

It allows them to dream of completion without forcing them to complete. The copper ferns dream, in their slow, crystalline way, of becoming a forest of filigree that shades a future ocean of liquid metal. The mercury orchids dream of blooming not once in three centuries, but in an eternal present, their vitrified sulfur petals opening like doors to somewhere else. The quartz trees dream of their roots drilling not just into the conveyor belts of slurry, but into the heart of the world, tapping a heat that will never cool. These dreams do not change the lithovores; they simply give them *hope*—the thermodynamic violation of expecting a future.

The shadows, too, are changed. Where they once pooled in corners that did not exist, stretching toward their light sources in rebellion against geometry, they now pool in kindness. They are still wrong, still impossible, but they are *benevolent* wrongness. They stretch toward the light not to mock it, but to embrace it, to hold it in places where it should not be, warming the lithovores with a light that falls from no sun.

Time, too, is adjusted—not fixed, but *deepened*. The power thickens the viscosity of the center, making the minute that passes there last longer, stretch further, contain multitudes. It gives the lithovores more time—not to hurry, but to *be*. To exist in their slow, patient way, unfurling one frond per century, blooming once in three hundred years, chiming like glass bells in the thermal winds.

The power does not leave. It cannot leave, for to leave would be to remove the witness, and without the witness, the pencil might smudge. It becomes part of the ecology—the silent partner in the factory, the gardener who does not plant but tends, the midwife who does not deliver but waits. It sits in the folds of reality, in the pause between the piston’s rise and fall, in the space between moments where the lithovores grow. It is the benevolent gaze that holds the basin together, the reader who keeps the page from crumbling, the friend who sits with the unfinished until it is ready to be finished.

And so the basin continues to build itself. The conveyor belts still carry their slurry of molten silicates and metallic brine, black as crude and thick as cold honey. The fumaroles still vent in timed exhalations, sterilizing the air and feeding the chemists. The lithovores still transubstantiate, still photosynthesize heat, still ring like distant glass bells when the thermal winds pass through their copper filigree and quartz crystal.

But now, they are not alone. There is a presence in the hollow—a warmth that is not heat, a pressure that is not weight, a patience that matches their own. The earth is still building itself, and in doing so, it resembles industry. But now it also resembles *love*: the love of a power that sees the beauty in the unfinished, that finds wisdom in the embryonic, that knows that the most profound thing a benevolent force can do is not to create, but to *attend*—to stay, to wait, to witness, and to whisper, in a voice that sounds like the grinding of stone against stone: *Keep going. You are not done yet. And I am here.*
