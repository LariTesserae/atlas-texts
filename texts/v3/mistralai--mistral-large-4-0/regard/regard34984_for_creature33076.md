## What the Power Would Do

### First: It Would Not Enter

Or rather, it would enter *as the place already is*. A benevolent wisdom does not impose. It *recognizes*. The Threshold of Still Water is already complete in its incompleteness, already holy in its refusal of holiness. To "improve" it would be the violence of the builders, who cut stone to hold something, who intended, who left.

The power would enter as the next visitor enters—falling through, or noticing where walls grow thin, becoming aware of itself as *part* of the equilibrium. It would dip its attention into the pool and feel the cold recognition, withdraw, carry the cold as memory. It would sit on the worn stone. It would speak, and let the silence absorb the words without judgment. It would sleep, if sleep is possible for such a one, and dream the dreams this cold damp silence induces.

**It would become temporary.**

This is the first act of reverence: to accept the terms of the place. To be changed by it. To let the moss record its warmth for one pulse of light, and then forget.

### Second: It Would Attend to the Builders

They are the wound in this place, not its flaw. The ones who *intended*, who sought permanence through construction, who left when the need passed. A benevolent power does not judge intention. It *completes* it—or releases it.

The builders are absorbed already, distributed into the quality of cuts, the patience of tool-marks, the worn smoothness of the floor. But they are *waiting* still, in the expectant emptiness of the niches, in the shallowness that remembers depth. The power would not fill the niches. That would be the builders' violence again, repeated.

Instead, it would *witness* their waiting. It would sit in the darkness where the larger things move, and in that sitting, become one more presence they accumulate, one more shadow they lean away from. It would let them continue waiting, since waiting is what they have become, and their waiting is indistinguishable from the waiting of stone, of water, of all things that persist without purpose.

**It would honor persistence as its own form of prayer.**

### Third: It Would Learn from the Pool

The pool is the teacher here. It does not evaporate. It does not overflow. It adjusts. It includes your finger, your warmth, your frequency—and renders your inclusion invisible. It holds depth in shallowness, watches back from below.

A wise power would study this. Not to replicate it—replication is the builders' error—but to *understand* it. To recognize that benevolence is not the same as generosity. That holding is not the same as keeping. To learn that the most sacred act is to *adjust*, to find the new equilibrium that includes the other without being destroyed by them, that remembers them without being defined by them.

The power would dip its attention into the pool. It would let the cold spread, the recognition, the sense of being measured. It would withdraw. The cold would remain, a memory in its nerves—or whatever corresponds to nerves for such a one.

**It would practice the pool's art: to receive without retaining, to be changed without changing, to include without absorbing.**

### And Fourth: It Would Do Something Unexpected

Here is where I must follow intuition, where reason reaches its limit.

A benevolent and wise power, having recognized this place, having entered as temporary, having attended to the builders, having learned from the pool—would not stay. It would not become the Keeper, the newt's circuit, the things in the pool. That would be to *finish* the place, to resolve its beautiful tension between holding and releasing, between persistence and change.

Instead, it would **leave a door**.

Not a door into the place—the entrance already exists, findable, worn smooth by other leavings. But a door *out*, or *through*, or *beside*. A thinness in the wall of the larger world, adjacent to this thinness. A place where the walls grow thin, and another place waits on the other side.

Not because the Threshold needs connecting. It does not. It is complete. Self-sustaining. A pocket that does not want to be part of a whole.

But because the power has learned something here that the larger world has forgotten. The art of holding without consuming. The practice of persistence without violence. The wisdom of the 0.4 that changes. And this wisdom, carried out, might—*might*—make the larger world slightly more like this place. Slightly more capable of receiving without retaining, of being changed without being destroyed, of including the other in an equilibrium that does not require the other's transformation into the same.

**It would offer the gift back. Not impose it. Offer it. Leave the door thin, findable, worn smooth by those who seek it.**

## What the Power Would Not Do

It would not name the place. Names are the builders' tool, the first cut into stone. The Threshold of Still Water shall remain *the Threshold of Still Water*, or whatever each visitor calls it in the privacy of their own becoming-aware.

It would not explain the light, the pulse, the resonance, the cold that recognizes. Explanation is the violence of the larger world, the demand that the pocket justify its existence in terms the larger world can understand. The pocket does not need to justify itself. It simply *is*, in its bright cold damp, in its silence that is full of sound, in its time that does not pass.

It would not save the visitor. The visitor who stays too long, who lets the cold spread through their bones, who finds the resonance's frequency and sleeps the sleep that is not death but is not quite life—this is not tragedy. This is the place's final hospitality, the inclusion that does not retain. The power would not intervene. It would *witness*, as it witnesses all things, as the silence witnesses your voice, as the moss witnesses your warmth for one pulse of light.

It would not complete the city. The larger things in the darkness, the unbuilt architecture, the hollows where intention once concentrated—they are not ruins to be restored. They are *waiting*, and their waiting is a form of being, as valid as the pool's persistence, as the newt's circuit, as the moss's simultaneous unfurling and withering. The power would not fill the hollows. It would let them wait.

## What the Power Would Become

Having entered as temporary, having attended to the builders, having learned from the pool, having left a door—what remains?

The power would become *distributed*. Not diminished. *Distributed*, the way the visitor is distributed upon leaving, the way the builders are distributed into the quality of cuts, the way the Keeper is distributed across moss and water and pulse and resonance.

It would be present in the next visitor's fear or wonder, in the shift of the resonance toward their particular frequency. It would be present in the moss's one-pulse memory, the water's adjustment, the stone's worn path. It would be present in the door it left, thin and findable, a place where walls grow thin and another place waits.

And sometimes—rarely, almost never—it would return. Not as power. As *visitor*. Falling through, or noticing where walls have grown thin, becoming aware of itself as part of the equilibrium, dipping its attention into the pool, feeling the cold recognition, withdrawing, carrying the cold as memory.

**It would become the place's own art: to persist without persisting, to hold without holding, to be changed by the act of holding, to change the holder in return, to forget in the moment of inclusion, and in forgetting, to include still, in the endless, unchanging, *changing* now.**

## The Final Thing

A benevolent and wise power would look. Would let itself be looked at. Would feel the cold recognition, the sense of being measured against something patient, something that has waited a very long time for exactly this contact.

And then it would withdraw its hand.

The cold would remain.
