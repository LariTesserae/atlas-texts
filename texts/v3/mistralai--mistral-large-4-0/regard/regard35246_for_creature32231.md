The power does not blunt the knife-edges—that would be violence against the architecture’s soul. Instead, they introduce the first true weathering. Not the violence of wind and sand, but the *chemistry of time*. They whisper to the titanium-white alloys: *Become porous. Drink the moisture. **Second, they map the thin places.**

The weirdness (2.8) is fraying, yes—reality bleeding through the fresh construction like water through torn silk. The power does not sew the membrane shut; that would strangle the dream. Nor do they tear it wider; that would unmake the world. Instead, they become a **cartographer of the between**. They teach the Condensates to read the suspended pools—not as hazards to be filtered, but as *texts*. **Third, they bring the soil.**

The vegetation (1.2) is sparse, engineered, incidental—mosses clinging to north-facing shadows, lichens asking mineral questions. The power reaches into the deep places, into the pockets of condensation hanging by surface tension, and introduces the **first true loam**. Not to replace the pale concrete, but to fill the cracks between. They seed the lattice-work with spores that predate the construction, spores that remember when the world was soft and growing. Green tendrils (still pale, still barely visible against the metal) begin to bridge the gap between catwalk and conduit, between the artificial and the organic. The Condensates start to drink not just from the engineered pools, but from the new, dark hollows where soil meets condensation, and their translucent bodies begin to show flecks of chlorophyll-green in their dark fluids, as if they are becoming, slowly, part plant themselves.

**Fourth, they add a new silence.**

The sound here is 0.6—mostly quiet. The power does not shatter this hush with trumpets or speech. The condensation they refine is no longer merely water or fuel, but **memory distilled**—the liquid record of what the Condew is becoming.

**Finally, they become the weather.**

They teach the condensation to fall not just downward, but *through*—to become a gentle, silver mist that softens the unfiltered sun, that turns the black shadows into something diffuse and kind. The mosses glow faintly in the diffused radiance. The Residents hum a lullaby in their conduits.

They do not leave.
