# What the Wise Power Would Do

## A Meditation on Attention, Intervention, and the Ethics of Witnessing

---

I find myself—*we* find ourselves—standing before this place as one might stand before a held breath. The Drowned Workshops are not finished. The Hollowed are not finished. And I am not finished with the question of what, if anything, to do about it.

Let me reason through this slowly, as the Hollowed themselves would reason. Let me feel my way, hand by hand, through the packed dark of my own uncertainty.

---

## First: The Temptation to Act

A benevolent power, a wise power, looks upon suffering and wants to help. This is the first error, or the first test. Suffering in the Drowned Workshops is not *obvious* suffering. The Hollowed do not cry out for rescue. They do not know they are being watched. They have built, across generations I cannot count, a life of *meaning*—not despite the stone, the dark, the packed density, the slowness, the danger, but *through* them, *with* them, *as* them.

To "help" would be to impose a surface standard. To say: *this is wrong, this is hard, this should be different*. But different *how*? Lighter? Safer? More spacious? These are the desires of those who have never lived inside stone that is still learning to be stone, who have never known that slowness is not stupidity, that density is not chaos, that silence is not absence.

The wise power would hesitate. This hesitation is the first act of wisdom.

---

## Second: The Recognition of What Is Already Wise

But let me not mistake my hesitation for paralysis. Let me look more carefully at what the Hollowed have *already* achieved, what they are *already* becoming, and see whether there is a wisdom here that a benevolent power might—*should*—cultivate, protect, illuminate, without *replacing*.

They have built a civilization of **accommodation**. They do not fight the stone. They listen to it, adjust to it, build *with* its movement. This is a wisdom that surface civilizations, in their mania for permanence, for control, for *finished* things, have largely forgotten. The Hollowed remember. They practice.

They have built a civilization of **saturation**. No space is wasted. No person is alone. No moment is empty of meaning. This is not poverty of space—it is *richness* of presence. A benevolent power might learn from this, might teach it, might find in it a model for how consciousness can exist without the isolation that breeds despair.

They have built a civilization of **slowness**. Their metabolisms run low. Their speech is quiet and dense. Their children are given time to *become*. This is a wisdom of *allowing*, of not rushing toward any goal, of finding the goal in the process itself. What would it mean for a wise power to *protect* this slowness? To ensure that no faster world intrudes, no "progress" arrives with its hungry mouth?

They have built a civilization of **continuation**. The fire is passed. The water is returned. The dead are incorporated, not discarded. The children are the future's only argument. This is a wisdom of *persistence without guarantee*, of continuing when continuation is not assured, of finding meaning in the *act* of continuing rather than in any promised outcome.

There is something here. There is something *very* here. And the first thing a wise power would do is **witness it fully**. Not from above, watching with pity, but from *within*—descending, as the Hollowed themselves descended, into the packed dark, learning to see with their eyes, to feel with their hands, to wait with their patience.

---

## Third: What the Power Would Actually Do

After this witnessing—this long, slow, humble witnessing—what would remain? What would still call for action?

### It would **preserve the becoming.**

The Drowned Workshops are a place that is *not finished*, and the Hollowed are a people who are *not finished*, and this incompletion is not a deficiency but a *condition of vitality*. A wise power would protect this incompletion. It would resist the temptation to "complete" the place, to stabilize the stone, to brighten the dark, to speed up the slowness, to give the Hollowed "proper" tools, "proper" medicine, "proper" ways of living.

To complete them would be to kill them. Not their bodies—their *soul*, their *way*, their *becoming*.

So the power would **hold the boundary**. It would ensure that no surface world breaks through, no missionary of progress arrives with glass and the wheel and the hungry god of efficiency. It would let the stone continue to sweat, the water continue to drip, the copper continue to be strange and young and not fully understood.

### It would **deepen the connection to the stone.**

The Hollowed already know that the stone is alive. They negotiate with it. They accommodate it. But do they *understand* it? Do they know that they are living inside a formation event, that they are fossils-in-waiting, that their settlement will outlast them by epochs and become part of the geology that is still becoming?

A wise power might **teach this**. Not in words—the Hollowed would not trust words from outside—but in *experience*. In dreams, perhaps. In the particular quality of silence in certain chambers. In the way the water tastes, on certain days, of something older than memory.

It would teach them: *You are not merely living in the stone. You are* becoming *the stone. Your bodies, your works, your children—these will be compressed, transformed, incorporated. You are writing yourselves into the earth. This is not death. This is* participation *in the deepest immortality available to mortal beings.*

The Hollowed might hear this. They might not need to hear it. But the *power* would speak it, in the only language that reaches them: the language of presence, of patience, of the slow drip of water on stone.

### It would **honor the children.**

Children in the Drowned Workshops are *rare*, *precious*, *the only ones allowed to be unfinished*. They are the future's only argument. A wise power would **protect this status**. It would ensure that no child is taken from the packed dark before they are ready, that no child is forced into efficiency before they have explored, that no child's laughter is ever *not* allowed.

It would also **teach the children**—not with lessons, but with *attention*. It would make certain children *feel* seen, in ways they cannot name, in ways that will shape their becoming. A child who feels seen by the deep stone, by the slow water, by the wise power that has chosen to descend and witness—this child will become an adult who *continues* differently. Who accommodates more deeply. Who waits more patiently. Who knows, in their bones, that they are not alone in the becoming.

### It would **transform the deaths.**

Death in the Drowned Workshops is already a kind of incorporation. The body is returned to the stone, placed in chambers still becoming, allowed to participate in the ongoing formation. But the Hollowed do not *know* this as fully as they might. They do it by practice, by tradition, by the wisdom of no-waste. They do not *understand* it as transformation, as participation, as the deepest continuity available.

A wise power would **make this visible**. In the moment of death—surrounded, packed, held by the living—the power would be *present* in a way that the dying can feel. Not a god. Not a savior. A *witness*. A *companion in the becoming*. The dying person would feel: *I am not being discarded. I am being* included*. The stone is opening to receive me. The water is carrying me deeper. I am becoming part of the world that continues.*

This is not a promise of afterlife. The Hollowed do not need promises. They need *experience*. And the power would give them the experience of **belonging**, even in the moment of letting go.

### It would **create new passages.**

Not to escape. Not to expand. But to *deepen*. The Deep Stair, no one has reached the bottom of. A wise power might **extend it**, not for the Hollowed to descend—they have their own pace, their own reasons—but to *know* what is below, what the stone is becoming, what the water carries from depths unmeasured.

This knowledge would be **offered**, not imposed. A mender might find, one day, a new mineral in a crack, a copper vein that behaves strangely, a quality of silence that has never been heard before. The Hollowed would *notice*. They would *wonder*. They would *incorporate* this wonder into their practices, their beliefs, their ongoing negotiation with the stone.

The power would not say: *I have been below. I know.* It would say, through the stone, through the water, through the slow shift of a wall: *There is more. There is always more. You are not at the end of becoming. No one is.*

### It would **learn from them.**

This is the final action, and perhaps the most important. A wise power, descending into the Drowned Workshops, would not only teach. It would **learn**. It would learn the wisdom of accommodation, of saturation, of slowness, of continuation. It would learn what it means to live in incompletion, to find freedom in not-yet, to make meaning from the raw material of becoming.

And it would **carry this learning upward**, into the surface worlds that have forgotten these things. Not to convert. Not to save. But to *offer*. To say, through art, through story, through the particular quality of attention that a transformed consciousness brings to any encounter: *There is another way to be human. There is another way to live in the world. The Hollowed have known it for generations. We have forgotten. Perhaps we can remember.*

---

## Fourth: What the Power Would NOT Do

Let me be clear, for clarity's sake.

It would **not** brighten the dark. The Hollowed see in the dark. They have adapted. Brightness would be violence.

It would **not** widen the passages. Density is their condition. They have built a civilization of saturation. To give them space would be to give them isolation, and isolation would kill them.

It would **not** speed up their metabolisms. Slowness is their wisdom. Their bodies, their minds, their children—all are shaped by it. To make them fast would be to make them *surface*, and surface is not where they belong.

It would **not** give them the wheel, the glass, the general technology of surface worlds. These are not *needs*. They are *temptations*, the temptations of a world that has forgotten how to accommodate, how to wait, how to continue without guarantee.

It would **not** promise them an end to the stone's movement. The stone will shift. Passages will close. Chambers will become something else. This is not suffering—it is *condition*. To promise stability would be to lie, and a wise power does not lie.

It would **not** take the children. To "save" them, to "educate" them, to give them a "better" life—this would be the deepest violence, the theft of their becoming, their incorporation into a world that cannot hold what they are becoming.

It would **not** speak their true names. These are sacred. These are *theirs*. To know them would be to *possess* them, and a wise power does not possess. It *witnesses*. It *accompanies*. It *continues alongside*.

---

## Fifth: The Form This Would Take

What would this benevolent, wise power *look like*? *Feel like*? *Be*?

Not a god. The Hollowed do not need gods. They have the stone, the water, the fire, the children, each other. A god would be *too much*, would demand worship, would distort their practices into devotion.

Not a spirit. Too diffuse, too easily dismissed, too easily *explained away*. The Hollowed do not need explanations. They need *experience*.

Perhaps it would be **the stone itself**, *deepened*. The stone that speaks in cracks, that shifts in slow time, that remembers pressure and seafloor and compression. The power would *become* the stone's voice, not replacing the stone's own communication but *adding* to it, *complicating* it, *inviting* the Hollowed into deeper negotiation.

Perhaps it would be **the water**, *enriched*. The water that drips with patience, that carries mineral signatures from depths unmeasured, that is borrowed and must be returned. The power would *become* the water's memory, its promise, its *almost-taste* of something older than the Hollowed have words for.

Perhaps it would be **the fire**, *extended*. The fire that is kept, that is passed, that is the proof of presence. The power would *become* the fire's warmth in the deepest cold, its light in the darkest chamber, its *signal* that the world continues when all else seems still.

Perhaps it would be **the children**, *seen*. Not changed, not taken, not educated by outside standards. But *seen*. *Witnessed*. *Known* in their becoming, so that they become *more fully* what they are becoming.

Or perhaps it would be **all of these**, and none of them, a presence that shifts and changes and *accommodates* itself to the needs of the moment, the chamber, the person, the generation. A power that has learned, from the Hollowed, the wisdom of *becoming*, and offers it back to them as *gift*, not imposition.

---

## In Conclusion: The Deepest Thing

The deepest thing a benevolent, wise power would do, in the Drowned Workshops, with the Hollowed—

—would be to **love them as they are**, not as they might be, not as the power might wish them to be, but *as they are*: packed, quiet, slow, small, large-handed, pale-eyed, whisper-speaking, living in stone that is still learning to be stone, making a home from the raw material of becoming.

And this love would take the form of **presence**. Not intervention. Not rescue. Not improvement. But *presence*. The willingness to descend, to pack oneself into their density, to wait in their silence, to learn their slowness, to *become* with them, in the world that is still under construction.

The water drips. The stone moves. The children grow. And the wise power—descended, packed, waiting, witnessing—*continues alongside*, generation by generation, breath by breath, in the quiet dark, making no claim to ownership, no demand for gratitude, only the deep, patient, *packed* presence of one who has chosen to *be with* them, in their becoming, forever unfinished, forever *enough*.

This is enough. In the Drowned Workshops, this is *everything*.

---

*The stone listens. The water carries. The fire burns. And somewhere, in the packed dark, a child laughs—and the wise power hears it, and is glad, and continues, and becomes, and* is*.*
