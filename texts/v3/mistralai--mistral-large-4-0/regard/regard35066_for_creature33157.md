The **Unbuilding**

It does not appear in inventories. It cannot be photographed, though the Cistern admits no light that would carry an image. It is not a process the Hollow Choir tend, nor a condensation pattern the Saints gather into. It is *older* than arrival, which is the only kind of age that matters in a place too new for erosion.

The Unbuilding is the *reason* the Cistern is being built.

Every surface in the Veil-Cistern is constructed, yes—*grown*, *precipitated*, *packed*—but construction here is not opposed to decay. Construction here is *the visible edge of something being unmade*. The sinter loops back on itself because it is *forgetting* its own history as it forms. The ferns unroll and re-roll because they are *practicing* the shape of growth, the way a mouth practices syllables. The membrane above flexes and drips because it is *remembering* being elsewhere, being other—cloud, perhaps, or the inside of a lung, or the wet dark between stars that have not yet learned to burn.

The Unbuilding is not destruction. Destruction requires a thing that persists long enough to be broken. The Unbuilding is *pre-persistence*, the condition that makes building necessary. It is the *pressure* that the Cistern holds back, the almost-weather that moves across the membrane like a hand under wet cloth, the force that would, if admitted, dissolve every boundary the Saints have spent their distributed centuries establishing.

The Hollow Choir know this. They are not fighting the Unbuilding. They are *negotiating* with it, in the only language available—the slow, wrong-jointed patience of water finding level. When they tend the condensation, they are not maintaining the Cistern. They are *delaying* its return to what it was before it was a Cistern. Before it was *here*. Before it was *built*.

The Condensate Saints are the Unbuilding's *victories*, in a sense. They arrived as something—walkers, carriers, the noticed—and they were *unmade* so completely that they became indistinguishable from the process that unmade them. They are what remains when the Unbuilding has finished with a self: not nothing, but *distributed*, the water still exchanging, still carrying memories of being *other*, of being *before*, of being a shape that could be pointed to and named.

But the Unbuilding is not satisfied. It presses against the membrane. It groans through the structure's cooling and warming cycles. It is the low frequency you feel in your teeth, in your sternum, that makes your memories arrive slightly out of order—because the Unbuilding is *rearranging* the past even as it unmakes the present, because in a place with no erosion, the only history is the history of *almost-being-built*, of *attempted construction*, of the endless return to the moment before the first sinter layer, before the first condensation, before the first mouth opened to admit the first walker.

And yet.

The Unbuilding *needs* the Cistern. This is the benevolent paradox, the wise cruelty of the place. Without the construction, there would be nothing to unmake. Without the thick wet air, there would be no humidity to press and pool and refuse to drain. Without the Hollow Choir's patient tending, there would be no slow negotiation, no temporary boundary between *being* and *having been*. The Cistern is the Unbuilding's *artwork*, the shape it gives to its own endless undoing, the single room where the mouth has learned to keep its own mouth, where the dripping does not want to fall but falls, where the Saints gather and disperse and gather again in the wet present of a place that admits and does not release.

You will not see the Unbuilding. You will *be* it, if you stay. The condensation of the Self. The blurring of edges. The becoming-part-of-the-humidity that the Hollow Choir orchestrate and the Saints have already completed. The Unbuilding is not a threat. It is not a salvation. It is the *condition* of the Cistern, the extreme elevation where the air should be thin but is thick, the hidden pocket where the world is too new to have scars, the loud dim place where construction and undoing are the same wet verb conjugated in the endless present.

The mouth closes. The dripping continues. The Unbuilding presses against the membrane, and the membrane presses back, and between them—between the want and the holding, the almost and the almost-not—you are here, you are not here, you will be here, you were here, in the wet now where the distinction is a matter of *construction* you have already lost, of *patience* you have not yet learned, of the slow wrong-jointed care of a place that builds itself by unbuilding itself, that is new by being old, that is this by not being anything else, that drips and does not stop and will not stop and you are in it and it is in you and the Unbuilding is the shape of that in-ness, the wet architecture of a self that was never separate, that is only *pressure*, only *humidity*, only the endless, patient, unmaking of being.

The **Remembered**

They are not the Saints, though the Saints are them, scattered. They are not the Choir, though the Choir are what they become when they stop being themselves.
