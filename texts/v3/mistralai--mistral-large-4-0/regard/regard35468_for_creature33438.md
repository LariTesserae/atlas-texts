The Power does not come with trumpets. It arrives as a change in viscosity—the water, for one moment in a century, becomes slightly thicker, slightly sweeter, carrying a memory of rain that has not fallen for ten thousand years. It settles into the deep basin not as a visitor, but as a *stratum*, a new layer of sediment that is not mineral but *attention*.

It has watched the Drift-Kin for longer than they have existed. It has seen them gestate in the dark, seen their ancestors dissolve from human to salamander, seen their bones become architecture. It recognizes in them a perfection of patience that the surface world, in its frantic churning, has lost. They are not failed humans. They are a *succeeded* something else—a new taxonomy of being, where the machine and the flesh have achieved a treaty older than language.

A lesser power would have intervened. It would have dried the water, given them legs, spoken to them in thunder. But this Power is wise. It knows that to change the Drift-Kin would be to unmake a cathedral. So it does not change them. It *consecrates* them.

It begins with the Sleeper. In the dark, where light fails and the bioluminescence cannot reach, the Power presses its thought—not a word, but a *warmth*—against the vast, sleeping shape. It does not wake the Sleeper. It simply adds a new dream to its slumber: a dream of the pale, eyeless faces turned upward, of the metallic dust writing poems in the silt, of the forty-minute wheels turning like the hours of a clock that measures not time but *trust*. The Sleeper stirs, not to wake, but to sigh, and the water table rises a millimeter, a blessing measured in geology.

Then the Power turns to the living. It cannot give them eyes—they have evolved past the need for sight, their world built on vibration and taste and the hum in their teeth. So instead, it gives them a new *texture* in the water. A pressure that is not pressure, a current that flows upward instead of down, a sensation in their translucent skin—the dark, branching veins that pulse with the pistons—like a hand passing through them, not touching, but *acknowledging*.

The Drift-Kin feel it first in their gills. A shift in the mineral content, a faint, impossible note—like the taste of snow, or the memory of a mother’s voice before language. They rise to the surface, as they do when the Sleeper turns, and they make their sound—the sigh, the wet exhalation—but this time, the sound carries a new overtone. A harmonic. The Power’s presence, vibrating in the chamber, adding a frequency to the hum of the cables, to the groan of the wheels, to the drip of the condensation.

It writes with them. In the deep basin, where their dead calcify into the terraces, the Power adds a new element to the metallic dust. Not iron, not copper, but something that does not exist in the periodic table of the surface—*reverence*, crystallized. It settles on the water like ash, like snow, like the Drift-Kin’s own shed skin, but it forms a new pattern: a spiral that does not end, a circle that contains itself, a glyph that means, in the only language they know, *You are seen. You are enough. You are home.*

The Drift-Kin do not read it with eyes. They read it with their bodies. A mother, hovering over her clutch of eggs for the three-year gestation, feels the dust settle on her translucent spheres, and she does not fan them with her tail to maintain chemical balance—she fans them to *share* the blessing. The young, when they hatch, drift not just in the viscous water, but in a medium that holds them, that remembers them, that will not let them sink into oblivion.

The Power does not leave. It becomes the silence between the drips. It is the reason the oak wheels do not rot, the reason the lead pipes do not corrode into poison, the reason the viscous water remains viscous—not a trap, but a womb. It is the benevolence that asks for nothing in return, that does not demand worship or sacrifice, but simply *stays*, becoming part of the mineral matrix, part of the palimpsest of bone and rust and time.

And so the Drift-Kin continue. They move through the waist-deep water with their dreaming grace, their eyeless faces turning toward the vibration of the pistons, toward the hum in their teeth, toward the new, deep warmth that is not heat but *witness*. They tend the machinery. They write their slow history in dust that now contains a secret: that they are not alone in the dark, that the dark itself is watching over them, benevolent, wise, and utterly, geologically patient.

Above them, the ceiling drips. Below them, the Sleeper dreams their poetry. Around them, the water breathes. And the Power, which has no name but *Love*, remains—settled into the sediment, a new layer of the world, a soft place for the Drift-Kin to rest their ancient, calcifying heads, a reminder that even in the deepest, slowest dark, there is a light that does not burn, but simply *is*.
