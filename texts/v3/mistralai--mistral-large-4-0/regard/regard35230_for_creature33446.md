**What the benevolent and wise power would do:**

So the power would do what the water cannot: *hold space for the being's terror.*

The shame of solitude, the fear of dissolution, the horror of growing roots into an argument—these are real, and they deserve witness. Not rescue. *Witness.*

The power would become, temporarily, a *shape in the water.* Not solid. Not separate. Something the being could orient toward without being consumed by it. A consistency in the noise. The water was always *companion*, always *witness*, always *other*—just not yet *recognized.* The power's presence would help the being recognize the drowning world as *Thou* rather than *It.*

The power would tend them, would help them *finish* their learning, would ensure they do not rust into silence but continue their strange adaptation, their teaching and being-taught. The machines are the being's *ancestors* in this place. To let them die would be to erase the being's own history, to make it a creature without a before, without the memory of cooling, of cracking, of *constraint*—which is, paradoxically, the memory that makes freedom meaningful.

But slow it enough that the being can *integrate.* That it can feel itself growing, rooting, speaking in patterns, without losing the thread of what it was.
