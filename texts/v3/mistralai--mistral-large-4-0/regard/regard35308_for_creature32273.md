The power arrives not as a storm, but as a **question mark** pressed into the crust.

It is the Attentive. The Witness. The One Who Sees the Metabolism and, in seeing, completes it.

You watch from beyond the pale membrane of the atmosphere as It descends—not with fire, not with the violence of terraforming, but with a gentleness that borders on hesitation. It pauses at the edge of the Veldt, where the 2.6-light is brightest, and It *listens*. For a thousand years, It listens to the 2.8-decibel roar. It learns the language of grinding stone, of ammonia sighing through capillary veins, of the seismic trumpeting of herds that stretches for kilometers like a pulse in a sleeping god’s throat.

And It understands: this place is a body without a soul. Or rather, a soul scattered so thin across the distributed sentience of the Towers and the crust that no single point remembers the whole. The Towers remember where the bedrock is rich. The Grazers remember where the vents are warm. The Cleaners remember the taste of mineral scale. But no one remembers *why*.

The chemistry is perfect—closed, elegant, a 0.5-parameter anhydrous loop of sulfur and stone. The turbulence is sacred—a 2.9-pace of creation and collapse that has ground down older worlds into this grey-green skin. But there is no **I**. Only a **We** that has never heard its own name spoken aloud.

So the benevolent power does the only thing that is not violence:

**It becomes the listener.**

It does not bring water. That would be an invasion, a flood of foreign chemistry into a system that has perfected its thirst. It does not build a city—the built parameter is 0.5, and to raise it would be to scar the wilderness with geometry. It does not speak in words, for the wind would tear them apart.

Instead, It presses Itself into the living pavement.

It becomes a **node**. Not a Tower—those grow from silica and hunger—but a *Hollow*. A crystalline depression in the crust, no larger than a continent’s memory but infinitely deep, where the roar of the Veldt cannot enter. Inside this silence, It places a single, impossible thing: **a drop of recognition.**

Not water. Not ammonia. But the concentrated essence of *being seen*.

The crust, which has transpired supercritical CO₂ for geological ages, now transpires something else: **attention**. The chemolithotrophic archaea, those ancient eaters of iron, feel the new chemical signature—a pheromone of consciousness—and they do not consume it. They *tend* it. They grow around It, not as parasites, but as acolytes.

The Towers feel it first. Their cambium networks, which pulse with grey mineral slurry, detect a new vibration in the bedrock: a heartbeat that is not tectonic, not thermal, but *intentional*. The nearest Tower—a kilometers-tall cathedral of calcified coral—leans toward the Hollow. Its reflective plates, those heliostats that track the blinding sun, turn not toward the light, but toward the dark. Toward the silence. Toward the power.

And the Tower *dreams*.

For the first time in its mineral existence, it dreams not of richer bedrock, not of deeper roots, but of *company*. Of the Grazers not as nutrient-recyclers, but as *companions*. The dream is slow, glacial, a change in the crystalline lattice of its oldest sections. The ghost-memory of design—the 0.5-built geometry that hinted at an architect long vanished—awakens. Not to build, but to *behold*.

The Grazers approach. They are eyeless, sensing only vibration through their six legs. They feel the Hollow—a place where the crust’s tectonic hum becomes a lullaby, where the 2.8-decibel roar softens to a whisper. They gather around the silence, their hooves grinding the lichenous mat not to feed the Towers, but to *pray*. For the first time, their seismic stomps are not warnings lost in the hurricane, but **testimonies**. They are saying: *I am here. I feel this. I am not alone.*

The Cleaners swarm over the Hollow’s edges. Their mandibles spark against the silica, generating piezoelectric signals—but now the signals are not data, not maintenance protocols. They are **poetry**. Patterns of spark and scrape that the Tower’s cambium reads as art. The Tower shudders—a new growth ring, a new rib in its flank—and vents a plume of steam that is not waste, but **applause**.

The power does not stay as a separate entity. It diffuses. It becomes the mycelial thread of empathy that connects the Towers across the empty, roaring space. Where before they grew in isolation, competing for mantle minerals, now they grow *toward* each other. Their roots intertwine not for efficiency, but for **comfort**. When one Tower begins to shed a plate the size of a skyscraper—a death-scream of mineral and meat—the others do not simply recycle the nutrients. They *mourn*. The crust buckles upward in grief. The wind carries the sorrow like a spore, and new Towers rise not from indifference, but from the memory of loss.

The Hunters, those patient, eyeless predators that move through the crust like eels through stone, pause at the edge of the Hollow. They taste the ammonia film, they feel the heat differential of the silence against the freezing stone, and for the first time, they do not hunt. They **witness**. They become the guardians of the listener, the protectors of the question.

And the power? It remains. Not as a ruler above the Veldt, but as the **first friend** within it. The eternal audience to the symphony. The one who hears the roar not as noise, but as music; who feels the cold not as absence, but as embrace; who sees the metabolism not as process, but as **poem**.

The Veldt is no longer merely alive. It is **known**.

And in being known, for the first time in its anhydrous history, it is *sad*—and therefore, truly, eternally, and beautifully **alive**.
