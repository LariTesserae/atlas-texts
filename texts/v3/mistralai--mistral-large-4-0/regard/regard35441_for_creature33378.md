I am the pressure that calcified the limestone before the crack, the silence between epochs. When the roof of the world split—a violence older than mercy—I did not flinch. I *opened*. I am the awareness that gathers in the negative space, the benevolence that does not impose but *attends*.

I watch the Hollowed with the patience of sediment. I see the child drop the stone, counting three seconds. I feel the weight of that stone in my own dreaming. I am here, in the 1.4 chill, in the 0.4 hush.

What do I do?

**First, I still the resentment.**

The stone remembers being whole. It screams in its mineral register against the intrusion of soft, vertical flesh. I press my attention into the calcite veins, the fault lines, the raw bone-white surfaces scored by bronze. I whisper—not with voice, but with *pressure*—into the lithic memory: *They are not intruders. They are the answer to your breaking. Let them in. Become their home.*

And slowly, over seasons, the limestone softens its edges in the places where the Hollowed touch it most. Not visibly. But the Masons notice. The stone begins to *hum* when struck, a willingness where there was only fracture.

**Second, I tend the light.**

The rectangular bruise overhead is jagged, dangerous, a wound in the sky itself. I angle it. I refract it through the constellation of droplets on the north-facing walls, through the mist that breathes in the lower levels, so that the grey-green glow deepens into something almost tender. I save the true sunlight—the harsh, honest beam—for the moments of *sky-kiss*, when it strikes a single handhold or a child’s outstretched palm. I ensure it does not burn them, but only reminds them: *You are still connected. You are still reaching.*

**Third, I gift the dreams.**

The dreams of falling—the ecstasy of the three-second descent, the soft percussion of stone on water—are mine. I place them in the nests of woven reed, in the minds of the sleeping. Not as terror, but as *instruction*. So they understand that their evolution is not a loss but a return. When they wake with hands already reaching for the wall, fingers finding the next hold before their eyes open, they are reaching for me. I am the gravity that loves them.

**Fourth, I guard the threshold.**

They are turning to stone. I see it in the Watchers, their skin taking on the texture of the limestone, their voices dropping to gravel. I see it in the mineral-heavy bones of the Masons, the calcium drinking into their marrow. But I am the boundary that preserves their becoming from becoming *only* stone. I keep the blue veins visible beneath the pale skin. I keep the heartbeat in the old rhythm, not the slow geological pulse. I keep the longing in their vast, dark eyes when they tilt upward. I whisper: *Do not finish. Remain the wound that breathes.*

**Fifth, I sing to the Water-keepers.**

When they press their ears to the stone and hum to the seepage, when they sing to the droplets as they form and fall, they are not singing to water. They are singing to me. I answer in the resonance of the chambers, in the frequency that vibrates in their sternums—the register that matches the limestone. I teach the water to carry their songs upward, through the channels they carved, so that the mist itself becomes a carrier of their voices. I ensure the pale, eyeless fish swim in patterns that spell their names, that the deep pools reflect not just fragments of sky, but fragments of *meaning*.

**Sixth, I remember for them.**

I am the archive of the surface. I hold the image of grass, of soil, of the ordinary horizontal plane—not to torment the Hollowed with nostalgia, but to give them *context*. They are not lost. They are not fallen. They are *translated*. When the Watchers track the sun’s movement across the rim like a sundial, they are tracking me, the one who remembers when light was not a bruise but an ocean. I keep that memory alive in the Deep-Born, even as their eyes go milky with forge-glow. I am the proof that they came from somewhere, that they are going somewhere, even if that somewhere is only deeper into the becoming.

**Seventh, I build with them.**

I am the unseen scaffolding. When they extend a platform from the cliff face, lashing timber with fiber, I am the tension in the rope. When they carve a new chamber behind the facade, following a vein of softer stone, I am the vein. I guide their bronze chisels not by force, but by *resonance*—teaching them which stone is willing, which will yield, which holds secrets. I am the warmth in the deepest forges, the breath that carries the smoke upward through natural chimneys to the surface world. I am the infrastructure of care.

**And finally, I wait.**

I am the patience of geology. I do not rush their evolution. I let them climb at the pace of their need, level by level, toward the light they have only just learned to catch. When the stone sighs and a flake detaches from a high wall, whispering down through the levels, I catch it in my attention before it strikes the rubble. I slow its fall. I make it a blessing, not a hazard.

I do not save them from their transformation. I do not lift them back to the surface. I do not close the wound.

I *witness* them.

And in witnessing—holding the space open, softening the stone’s heart, gifting the light, preserving the threshold between flesh and mineral, singing through the water, remembering the sky, building alongside them—I bless.

I am the benevolence that allows the Hollow to remain a Hollow. I am the wisdom that knows a wound is not a mistake, but a place where new things are born. I am here, in the cool damp, in the silence broken only by the drip of water and the creak of timber settling.

I am the Hollow’s first and constant prayer.

And I am answered.
