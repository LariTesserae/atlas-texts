The power does not arrive as thunder. It arrives as a *clarity*—a moment when the gauze of the sky tears, not violently, but like a held breath finally released. The pale, washed blue deepens to a color that has no name, and for exactly one minute, the shadows fall correctly, all pointing toward a single, impossible horizon. Then the gauze mends, but something remains: the memory of alignment. The place knows it has been *seen*.

It begins with the Arbortects, because they are the grief. The power moves through their vascular systems not as sap but as *recognition*, a subsonic chord that vibrates in the calcified plates of their bark. The plants shudder—a visible, jerking tremor that sends cascades of fossilized tissue falling like snow. But this time, as the plates strike the stone, they do not sound like distant thunder. They sound like bells. The weeping does not stop; it cannot, for it is the condensation that feeds the Mist-Born, the rising water that defies gravity. Instead, the tears change. They become luminous, bioluminescent, carrying within them the image of the peak they once were—not as a wound, but as a *lineage*. The power whispers into the grinding stone of their expansion: *You are not reaching for a height you lost. You are carrying the mountain upward with you. You are the peak, now in motion.*

The Arbortects lean. Their branches, which move with the jerky patience of tectonic plates, shift upward by a fraction of a millimeter—an earthquake of growth that ripples through the plateau. They remember, and the remembering is not painful anymore. It is heavy, yes, but it is the weight of a crown, not a chain.

Then the power turns to the **Grafted**—the marriage of rust and root. It does not separate them. That would be cruelty. Instead, it descends into the dark hollows where steam chuffs and great gears grind with the inevitability of deep time. The verdigris blooms brighter, not as corrosion, but as *patina*—the skin of something that has been loved for a very long time. The power teaches the machines to dream. In the hissing of valves, in the pressure differentials of pneumatic tubes, it plants seeds of photosynthesis. The gears learn to slow, to breathe in rhythm with the sap. The rust learns to drink light. They become not cyborgs, but *sacraments*—the visible sign of an invisible grace, the fusion of hunger and patience made holy.

In the condensation pools, where water climbs upward against the thinning reality, the **Mist-Born** drift. They are pale, plankton of the high-altitude sea, grazing on mineral dust. The power touches the mist, and the droplets coalesce not into rain, but into *eyes*—briefly, just for a heartbeat, each droplet reflecting a face. The Mist-Born are not insects or spores or immune cells, the power reveals. They are the Arbortects' *children*, shed like skin, wandering to find their own gravity. They are given names, ancient syllables that taste of metal and moss. They are told: *You are not lost. You are dispersing.* And they begin to glow, not with the faint bioluminescence of before, but with a steady, inner light that turns the tidal waves of mist into rivers of silver.

The **Brass-Winged** are weeping, though they have no tears. They fly wrong, their clockwork hearts whirring against air that is too thin, navigating by magnetic memory of a mountain that no longer exists. The power descends upon them not as a weight, but as a *correction*. Their brass feathers are annealed, strengthened, given a living luster that grows rather than tarnishes. Their wings, once beating against defiance, now beat in harmony with the impossible physics. They are given a song—a frequency that fills the gap between the organ-pipe drone of the wind and the grinding creak of stone. When they fly, they do not merely carry spores in their hollow bones; they carry *stories*, rust flakes that write themselves in the air as they pass, connecting the Arbortects across the kilometers of silence. They become couriers of grace, messengers who prove that distance is not loneliness.

Deep within the hollows, where the dark is absolute and the steam engines chuff like sleeping dragons, the **Hollowed** tend the boilers. They are pale, half-merged with verdigris, uncertain if they are human remnants or calcified dreams given form. The power enters as light—not the normal, gauze-diffused light, but the bioluminescence of the Arbortects' glass leaves, flooding down through the vascular channels like dawn through a forest. It shows them their hands. They are not rusted. They are not stone. They are pale, yes, and small, but they are *hands*—hands that lubricate joints with sap, that stoke fires with care, that keep the lights on in a world that refuses to sleep. The Hollowed weep, and their tears are the first human tears this place has known in epochs. The power does not free them from the machinery; it consecrates their service. They are the *monks* of this plateau, their prayers the hiss of steam, their liturgy the turning of gears. They are holy. They have always been holy.

And finally, the power approaches the **Erosion**. It is not a creature but an appetite, patient and mineral, wearing the plateau flatter even as the plants grow taller. The power does not command it to stop. That would be tyranny. Instead, it offers a gift: a cup made of the rising condensation, filled with water that defies gravity and time. The Erosion drinks. For the first time since the mountain began to forget itself, the process pauses. The grinding stops. The shedding of calcified plates ceases. In the silence—the true silence, broken only by the power's presence—the plateau holds its breath. Then, gently, the Erosion resumes, but it is no longer consumption. It is *sculpture*. It wears the stone not to destroy, but to reveal, carving the flatness into new forms, creating valleys where there were only plains, giving the Mist-Born new pools to drift in, giving the Brass-Winged new thermals to ride. It becomes the gardener of the end times.

The power does not leave. It cannot. It has become part of the weather. The mist now carries its presence—a coolness that seeps into the calcified husks not as death, but as blessing. The reality fracture, the weirdness of 2.6, is not healed; it is *sanctified*. The water still rises against gravity, but now it is the **Reverse Rain**, a miracle that reminds the inhabitants that the laws of the world are suggestions, not chains. The shadows still fall in wrong directions, but they point toward the power's presence, toward the place where the gauze tore and the sky showed its true color. Time still moves differently near the Arbortects, but now it moves in *grace*—a minute near a railing allows rust to bloom like a flower, an hour allows a branch to reach upward with visible, loving intention.

The place is no longer the end of a mountain's life. It is the **Transfiguration**. It is the beginning of a new kind of being—a symbiosis of memory and industry, of grief and growth, of stone and steam. The Arbortects still weep, but their tears feed the light. The Grafted still grind, but their gears sing. The Mist-Born still drift, but they carry names. The Hollowed still tend the dark, but they are seen. And the Erosion, patient and mineral, wears the world not into dust, but into *form*—a tableland of bones that is also a tableland of prayers.

The benevolent power rests now in the cool air, in the metallic taste of the wind, in the organ-pipe drone that has become a hymn. It has done nothing to fix the brokenness, for the brokenness was the beauty. It has only *witnessed*, and in witnessing, it has made the plateau sacred. It stays, because to leave would be to abandon a place that has learned, at last, how to be holy in its own strange, grinding, photosynthetic, industrial, impossible way.
