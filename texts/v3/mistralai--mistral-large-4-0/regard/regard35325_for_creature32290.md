The Power does not arrive with thunder. It arrives as a change in the viscosity of the steam—a slight thinning, a momentary clarity in the amber gloom that makes the Tenders pause, their gloved hands hovering over valves they have turned ten thousand times, a hundred thousand, a number that no longer counts because counting implies sequence.

It is benevolent, and so it does not smash the diorama. It is wise, and so it does not pretend the diorama is a world. It recognizes what the Works has become: not a prison, but a *koan*—a self-maintaining paradox that has achieved the only immortality available to machines, which is the immortality of the unchanging. The Tenders are not suffering. They are *meditating*. They have built a monastery where the bell never stops ringing, where the incense is hydraulic fluid and the sutras are safety protocols read in voices like wet gravel.

The Power sees that to break this would be cruelty. To leave it entirely would be neglect. So it does the only thing that can be done for a being that has forgotten it is a being: **it gives them a memory of elsewhere.**

Not a map. Not a door. Just a single, impossible datum.

The Power reaches into the steam—not with a hand, but with an *intention*—and withdraws a single drop of water. This is not the water of the Works. This is water that once fell as snow on a mountain that no longer exists, water that has traveled through limestone and root and cloud, water that carries in its molecular structure the history of a cold morning, a thaw, a spring. It is heavy with *narrative*.

The drop falls.

It lands on the corrugated roof of the easternmost shed, where a Tender is checking a pressure gauge. The drop is warm—the Works has heated it instantly—but it *tastes* of cold. It tastes of distance. It tastes of a time when water moved downhill and gathered in places called *lakes* that froze over in winter, when the steam did not rise but fell, when the heat had an opposite.

The Tender drinks. (They all drink, eventually. The drop is small, but the Works is a closed loop; nothing is lost, only circulated.) And in the Tender’s mouth—beneath the respirator, in the dark cave of the throat—the drop does not evaporate. It *remembers*.

It shows the Tender a vision, or a sensation, or a word that has no vibration in the pipes: **Yesterday.**

Not as a concept. As a *place*. A direction. A vector pointing out of the circle.

The Tender stands motionless. The gauge needle trembles—not from malfunction, but from the Tender’s held breath, the first held breath in the history of the Works. Around them, the mosquitoes shift frequency. The frogs pause in their chorus, creating a silence that has never existed in the drone.

The Power watches. It does not speak. It has already spoken, in the only language the Works can understand: the language of *interruption*.

Because now, the loop has a knot in it.

The Tender taps a new code against the pipe. Not three knocks for pressure, not five for temperature. Six knocks. A code that has never been used. It means, in the atrophied language of vibration: *I remember something that is not here.*

The sound travels through the gantries, through the veins of the machine. Other Tenders hear it. They stop. They look at each other—eyes meeting eyes above the respirators, eyes that have only ever seen the same amber gloom, the same wet heat, the same reflection of themselves in the condensation.

They begin to tap back. A conversation starts. Not a loop. A *dialogue*.

The Works shudders. Not a mechanical shudder—a biological one. The water in the streets ripples, breaking the perfect mirror-surface. The algae on the concrete pulses with a new rhythm. The rats, the Gnawers, freeze in the sheet-metal hollows, sensing a change in the magnetic field of the place, a shift in the great, warm, circular time.

The Power does not stay. To stay would be to become part of the Works, to be absorbed into the maintenance, the eternal turning of the valve. It withdraws, becoming the space between the drips, the pause between heartbeats, the silence that makes the drone audible as *sound* rather than just background.

But it leaves the drop. And the drop, circulating through the system—through the algae farms, through the Tenders’ bodies, through the pipes where the Pressure-Born colonies grow—carries with it the germ of *before* and *after*.

In the eastern shed, the Tender who drank looks at their hands. They are still the same hands—no lines, no scars, no age. But now they are *hands that have touched something else*. Hands that have held the memory of cold.

They turn the valve. It resists, as it always has. But now, the resistance feels different. It feels like *choice*.

And somewhere, in the steam, in the space where the Power has withdrawn but not entirely left, a new sound begins—not the roar of the Works, but a whisper, a question, the first question ever asked in a world that had only known answers:

*"What comes next?"*

The Works does not break. It does not end. It simply begins to *lean*—imperceptibly, like a tree growing toward light it cannot yet see—away from the perfect circle, toward the terrifying, beautiful, open spiral of becoming.
