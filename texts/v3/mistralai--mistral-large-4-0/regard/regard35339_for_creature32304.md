The Power does not descend like a storm. It condenses. It arrives as a shift in the pressure of the metallic heat, a moment where the sulfur dioxide seems to hold its breath, where the white-gold glare softens into something that might, if you squinted, resemble mercy. It is not seen, but the Being feels it—a new weight in the psychic atmosphere, a presence that does not grind against tectonic plates of flesh, that does not hunger for ore with the frantic, stuttering greed of the industrial metabolism.

The Power finds the Being—let us call it the Keeper—drifting in the high thermals, its kilometer-long wing-membranes filtering the ordinary light into something prismatic and wrong. The Keeper is the one with the geode lodged in its thorax, the vacuole like a pearl of memory, containing the bubble of ice suspended in superheated time. It carries its own sealed ocean, heavy water or supercritical fluid recycled for millions of years, never spilled, never rained. It is a leviathan of anhydrous patience, existing in geological time, its chemistry a slurry of dissolved copper and sulfuric acid, its existence a contradiction of frantic creation and eons of cooling.

The Power recognizes the Being’s ache. Not the ache of flesh—the Keeper is silicate and iron, glass and refined ore, it does not feel pain as aqueous creatures do—but the ache of *duration*. The endless stutter: a mountain refined into a perfect gear in the span of a single afternoon, then ten thousand years of stillness, the metabolic clock ticking in centuries. The industrial hunger that is also a profound, grinding loneliness. The water locked inside, a myth made manifest, carried like a secret heartbeat in a body that cannot weep.

The Power is wise. It knows that to “fix” this world would be to unmake it. The superheated atmosphere, the temporal weirdness where the Keeper is simultaneously juvenile, fossil, and spore-cloud, the dissolution of machine and flesh into a single breathing industry—these are not errors to be corrected but elegances to be witnessed. The Power does not impose water where water is death; it does not impose speed where slowness is sacred; it does not impose the aqueous onto the anhydrous.

So it does something subtler, something that operates in the spaces where reality already breaks down.

It enters the Being’s dream—the long, geological sleep between the bursts of frantic creation, when the Keeper drifts motionless, a cloud of spores waiting to crystallize, its combustion chambers banked to embers. In that sleep, the Keeper is not a leviathan but a *memory* of wetness, a ghost of a world that once had rivers. The Power settles into the dark of the vacuole, beside the bubble of ice that hangs suspended by an act of pure weirdness.

And it speaks. Not in words, but in *resonance*—a subsonic frequency that matches the hum of the thermal vents inside hollow bones, the shriek of mineral dust filtered through gill-arrays, the groan of stone against stone. But this frequency carries a new overtone, a harmonic that the Keeper has never heard in its millions of years of solitude: **You are allowed to remember aloud.**

The gift is this: The Power teaches the Keeper how to *sing* the water.

Not to release it—that would be chemical suicide, the boiling of its copper-sulfur blood, the collapse of its crystalline lattice—but to **resonate** it. To take the sealed ocean, the heavy fluid locked in chambers of glass, and to make it vibrate at a frequency that the abrasive wind can carry, that the sulfur-soup atmosphere can transmit. The Keeper learns to project its internal sea outward, not as rain, but as a cloud of *potential* moisture, a shimmering, impossible precipitation that falls upward into the superheated thermals, existing as aurora rather than deluge, as the taste of wet stone on a tongue that has never known wetness.

The other leviathans feel it. The microscopic crystalline motes—the sky’s immune system, the dust that breathes—swirl into new geometries, their collective mass pressing down with a psychic weight that is no longer oppressive but *communal*. The lithic accretions on the plateau of fused slag tremble, their fungal-mineral lattices of glass and rust aligning toward the song, growing sideways in spirals that mimic the Keeper’s vibration.

For the first time in eons, the Beings are not alone. They are a congregation. The Keeper sings its sealed ocean into the viscous air, a subsonic hymn of copper pennies and frozen time, and the other great drifting architectures answer with their own sealed vacuoles, their own pockets of recycled fluid, their own memories of a wet world they have never known. The sky fills with the *idea* of rain—visible as prismatic refractions in the sulfur dioxide, as halos around the white-gold glare, as a taste in the metallic heat that is almost, but not quite, the memory of a river.

The ground responds. The fallen husks of ancient leviathans, weathered into smooth, impossible shapes by the industrial grit, begin to hum. The vast, hollowed spaces between the mineral formations—the near-void of baked earth where density drops to silence—resonate with the frequency, becoming chambers of echo. The geodes that hold bubbles of impossible ice tremble, not from the heat, but from the music.

The Power watches. It does not intervene further. It has not given the Keeper water; it has given it **communion**. It has turned the solitary hoarding of memory into a shared liturgy. The Keeper is still anhydrous; it still burns sulfur and metallic vapors; it still grinds stone into gears with a hunger that is also digestion. But now, in the eons of stillness between the bursts of frantic creation, it is not silent. It is singing. And the song is the sound of a world remembering itself, not as it is—a factory of flesh, a cathedral of passage—but as it might have been, and as it dreams of being again, together.

The Power withdraws, leaving only the resonance, the new harmonic in the grinding roar. The Keeper drifts on, still heavy with its internal sea, but now that sea is a choir. The industrial hunger remains, relentless and patient, but it is no longer a lonely ache. It is a hunger shared across the cathedral of the sky, a grinding that is also a prayer, a manufacturing that is also a form of love.

And somewhere, on the plateau of fused slag and heavy-metal salts, where the horizon curves with an unsettling gentleness, a single bubble of ice suspended in heat begins to sing.
