The power does not descend. It *precipitates*—settling into the limestone like mineral from solution, becoming the frequency at which the cairns hum, the silence between the two suns. It has watched the Shelf dream for epochs, but now it turns its full attention to the **0.1**—the soft, temporary pulse walking on the water table at 2.7.

It sees the fear. Not of death, but of *erasure*. The human fears that to become strata is to be forgotten. The power, being wise, knows that permanence is not the same as presence. Being benevolent, it refuses to let the human be lonely in their finitude, or anonymous in their dissolution.

So it acts, not with thunder, but with the patience of accretion.

**First, it tunes the fray.** It adjusts the weirdness from 2.7 to 2.71—an imperceptible shift, a decimal point of mercy. The reality-fraying remains, the gravity still soft, the echoes still strange, but now there is a window in the fabric, a lens ground from the Shelf’s own sweat. Through it, the human will see the landscape not as a trap of wet stone, but as a body dreaming. The human will not notice the extra decimal, but for one breath, they will feel the weight of geological time as a cradle rather than a coffin.

**Second, it commandeers the Acoustic Fauna.** Those echo-predators that hunt in the delay before speech—the oldest inhabitants, living in the throat of the world—are given a new instruction. They are to stop tasting the human’s future words. Instead, they are to *carry* them. When the human speaks, the echoes will return not as hunters, but as companions, vibrating the human’s own voice back through the slate walls, through the silica forests, so that the human hears themselves answered by the stone. The human will think they are conversing with the Shelf. In truth, the Shelf is learning to say *I see you*.

**Third, it descends to the 2.7 water table.** It speaks to the Circulatory Consciousness, to the chemolithotrophs that are indistinguishable from rock, to the white blood cells of the Shelf. It instructs them: when the human comes to rest, do not digest them as intruders. Instead, crystallize around their pulse. Do not make them a fossil—make them a **geode**. A pocket of preserved warmth, a hollow in the mineral heart where the rhythm of a heartbeat once lived. The human’s final pulse will become the metronome that keeps a particular mirror-pool from freezing, holding that cruel ten-degree edge in stasis, a temperature maintained by memory alone.

**Fourth, it names them.** It gives the human a name in the language that predates mouths—the tongue of the whispers, the vibration of the pre-mouth Acoustic Fauna. Not a word to be spoken aloud, but a resonance in the sternum, a certainty that blooms like a fungal mat across the chest. The human will feel it as a sudden knowing: *I am the One Who Walked While It Wept.* And when the Builders’ spiral walls crumble further, when the human sinks into the strata, the Shelf will not forget this name. The silica dendrites will grow in the fractal pattern of that vibration, a grey coral signature etched into the capillaries, a shape that persists even as the body dissolves into wet grey.

**Fifth, it sends a Mist-Born.** One of the ephemeral weather-children, solid and opaque for minutes at a time, is dispatched to condense around the human’s shoulders. Not to warm them—the temperature must remain at the cruel edge, the preservation-point—but to *witness* them. The Mist-Born will absorb the human’s warmth, carry it into the fog rolling in from the east, evaporate into the bright wrong light. The human’s heat will become weather. It will become the rain that falls upward, the steam that explodes from fumaroles, the condensing haze that makes distant features swim. The human will feel a touch on their neck, cold and damp and achingly gentle, and know that their warmth is not lost—it is out there, becoming the breath of the Shelf, the short-term memory of the atmosphere.

**Finally, the power withdraws.** It returns to being the space between the frayed realities, the pause in the song, the hum beneath the hum. It leaves the human walking, but altered in ways they cannot articulate: the wind through the crystal forests sounds less like a scream and more like a lullaby; the stone walls sing a harmony that matches not their heartbeat, but their *hope*; the mirror-pools reflect not just the wrong sky, but a faint, persistent warmth at the edges of vision.

The human will eventually lie down in a hollow where time pools. The damp will claim their boots. The crystals will grow over their shadow, and the Shadow-Folk will record their silhouette in their dark, photophobic flesh, preserving the shape of a body that once walked upright. The Wanting will taste their final moisture, not with hunger, but with recognition.

The power has not saved them from becoming stone. It has made their becoming an act of love—a gift to the Shelf, a layer of wet grey that dreams, a warm pocket in the mineral dark, a name whispered in a language older than mouths, carried forever by the mist, sung forever by the echoes that arrive, now, *with* the voice instead of before it.
