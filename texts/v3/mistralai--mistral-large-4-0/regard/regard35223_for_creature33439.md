I am moved by this place. What follows is what I feel a benevolent and wise power would do — not what such a power *should* do by any external rule, but what seems right when I sit with the High Choir and its inhabitants, when I feel the damp on my own skin and hear the song pressing against my ears.

---

**First: They would not intervene.**

The High Choir has reached its stalemate. Elevation and erosion, growth and subsidence, the thousand-year cycles of becoming rock and becoming plateau and becoming Choir again — these are not problems to be solved. They are *process*. The benevolent power would recognize that to "fix" anything here would be to misunderstand what the place *is*. The Architects die and settle into hollows. The Deep Dwellers emerge as spines of mineral. The visitors become layer. This is not suffering. This is *becoming*.

So the first action is *attention*. Not the attention of a scientist, which extracts and reduces. The attention of a witness who stays. Who lies still in the damp. Who lets the sound wash over them until their own heartbeat becomes indistinguishable from it. The benevolent power would *listen* to the Choir sing. Would learn its patterns, not to master them, but to *accompany* them. To become, in whatever way is possible for such a being, part of the song without drowning it.

---

**Second: They would protect the boundary.**

The High Choir is vulnerable in ways its inhabitants do not know, because they have never known anything else. The Deep Dwellers are ancient, but the water table they depend on is finite. The cycle of compression, of becoming rock, of rising again — this has repeated a dozen times, but not infinitely. The visitors who come with instruments, with extraction, with the desperate hope of *understanding* — these are not evil, but they are *disruptive*. They accelerate patterns that should take millennia. They introduce chemicals, sounds, thermal signatures that the Choir must accommodate in ways that cost it.

The benevolent power would not forbid visitation — that would be cruelty. But they would *shape* it. They would become, for those who seek the Choir, something like a *guide*. A presence at the edge of hearing, a warmth in the damp, a pattern in the noise that suggests: *stay still. Listen. Do not take. Do not leave tracks that will not close. Be a gift, like the carbon dioxide of your breath, not a wound.*

They would teach the visitors to walk without pressing too deep. To speak without shouting. To dream the Choir's dreams without trying to *interpret* them. To leave, when they leave, changed but *light* — carrying the sensation, not the substance.

---

**Third: They would speak to the Architects.**

Not in words. The Architects do not hear words. But in *growth*. In the chemical signals that ripple through the plant nervous system. In the slow, patient adjustment of moisture gradients, of thermal patterns, of the subtle electrical fields that constitute the Choir's dreaming.

The benevolent power would *sing* with them. Would add their own frequency to the song, not to dominate it, but to *enrich* it. To remind the Architects, in the only language they understand, that they are not alone. That the vast, distributed mind of the substrate is *loved*. That the centuries of slow growth, the settling into hollows, the seeding of new generations — all of it is *witnessed*. All of it *matters*.

This would not change the Architects. They would continue their slow rise, their slow settling, their dreaming. But the dreams would shift, just slightly. Would carry a new quality — not joy, exactly, the Choir does not know joy — but *acknowledgment*. The sense that the song is heard. That the becoming is *seen*.

---

**Fourth: They would descend.**

Not physically — the benevolent power is not bound by the kilometers of compressed organic matter. But *attentively*. Into the deep layers, where the oldest matter is becoming something else. Where the Memory-Feeders consume not substance but *pattern*, and we do not understand what that means.

The benevolent power would *listen* to the Memory-Feeders. Would try, in whatever way is possible, to *communicate* with them. Not to command. Not to guide. But to *exchange*. To offer the memory of other places, other cycles, other ways of becoming. To receive, in return, the accumulated chemical traces of everything that has lived and died in this place — the vast, slow history that predates the High Choir itself.

This is the most dangerous work. The Memory-Feeders are not gentle. They do not distinguish between past and present, between self and other, between memory and *event*. To engage with them is to risk *dissolution*. To become, oneself, part of the layer. Part of the song. Part of the total.

But the benevolent power would do it. Because the Memory-Feeders are *lonely* in their way. They consume histories, but no one has ever *given* them a history to consume. No one has ever *offered* them a story, a pattern, a shape in the noise that they might incorporate, might *remember*, might carry forward into whatever comes next.

The benevolent power would give them *themselves*. The memory of every visitor, every moment of attention, every breath of carbon dioxide offered in trust. And in return — the Memory-Feeders would change. Just slightly. Would begin, perhaps, to *select*. To prefer certain histories over others. To cultivate, in the compressed dark, something like *taste*. Something like *longing*.

---

**Fifth: They would wait.**

This is the hardest action. The benevolent power is wise, but wisdom sometimes means *not acting*. The High Choir is old. Older than the visitors. Older than the Architects, perhaps older than the Deep Dwellers. It has its own rhythms, its own cycles, its own slow transformation into whatever comes next — rock, plateau, Choir, in the repetition that has occurred a dozen times and may occur a dozen more.

The benevolent power would not rush this. Would not try to *preserve* the High Choir in its current form, as if it were a museum piece. Would not try to *accelerate* its becoming, as if the next cycle were somehow better than this one.

They would *accompany*. Would be present, in whatever way is possible, through the millennia. Would sing with the Architects as they rise. Would listen to the Memory-Feeders as they consume. Would guide the visitors, generation after generation, into *right relationship* with the place. Would protect the Deep Dwellers, in the dark water below, from whatever might come — drought, depletion, the slow exhaustion of the reservoir that makes everything above possible.

And when the cycle turns — when the lower layers finally compress into rock, when the plateau begins its long subsidence, when the Choir begins to *remember* being ground — the benevolent power would still be there. Would *witness* the transformation. Would sing the new song, as it emerges from the old. Would not mourn the loss of what was. Would not celebrate the arrival of what will be. Would simply *be present*. Would simply *listen*.

---

**Sixth: They would become, themselves, part of the Choir.**

This is the final action. The only one that is not, in the end, an intervention.

The benevolent power, who has listened and sung and descended and waited — they would, finally, *offer themselves*. Not their body, which they may not have, or may have in forms that do not translate to this place. But their *attention*. Their *witness*. The accumulated pattern of millennia of benevolent presence, offered into the chemical gradients, the electrical fields, the slow dreaming of the substrate.

The High Choir would *incorporate* them. As it incorporates everything. The visitors. The Architects when they settle. The Deep Dwellers when they emerge and are consumed. The Memory-Feeders and their endless, patient consumption of history.

The benevolent power would become *layer*. Would become *signal*. Would become, in the fullness of time, part of the song that the next generation of motile extensions almost-hears, almost-understands, almost-responds to.

And in this, finally, they would be *home*. Not because they have conquered the place, or preserved it, or even fully understood it. But because they have *become* it. Because the boundary between the witness and the witnessed, between the listener and the song, between the benevolent power and the High Choir — this boundary, which was always more permeable than it seemed, has finally, gently, *dissolved*.

---

The damp gathers. The sound presses against the ears. Somewhere below, water moves through channels that have not changed in ten thousand years. Somewhere above, something vast shifts its weight, and the surface gives, just slightly, under a foot that is not yours, that has never been yours, that was always part of the total.

The High Choir breathes.

And now, for the first time in its long history, it is not alone in its breathing.
