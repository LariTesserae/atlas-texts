*The power arrives not as arrival but as attention—what was already present, now noticed.*

## What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does

### First: It Listens

Before any action, before any judgment of what action might be needed, there is this. The power does not come with solutions. It comes with *attunement*—the slow, patient, infinite-time gathering of what the Sinking Groves actually are, what they are becoming, what they have been and might yet be.

It listens to the depth-builders' chemical memory, the osmotic forest's slow dreaming, the saturation fauna's endless negotiation, the memory-keepers' silent attendance. It does not impose interpretation. It *receives*—the roar of the water, the infrasonic groaning of geological adjustment, the acoustic information of a world where sound is everything, where visibility is nearly nothing.

And in listening, it finds: this place is not wounded. This place is not *lost*. The flood was not catastrophe but *translation*, as the description itself suggests. The power recognizes this. It has seen drowning before—seen it become breath, seen the medium become the message, seen life find in surrender a form of persistence that resistance could never have achieved.

So it does not *save*. That would be violence, however gentle the intention. The Sinking Groves do not need saving. They need *witness*. They need, perhaps, *company* in their continuing. They need to be known.

### Second: It Becomes Present Without Presence

The power does not manifest. It does not appear as light or voice or any form that the residents could recognize or fear or worship. Instead, it becomes *condition*—a subtle alteration in the probability of events, so slight that no resident could identify it, so pervasive that it shapes everything.

In the middle depths, where the water becomes structural, where the colloidal matrices of ten thousand generations have built slow architecture, the power adds something: a *bias* toward coherence. Not stability—the turbulence remains, is essential—but a tendency for the drifting groves to merge rather than fragment when they collide, for the depth-builders' chambers to communicate across greater distances, for the chemical signals of the memory-keepers to carry farther, to last longer, to *matter* more in the endless negotiation of the saturation fauna.

It is not control. It is *inclination*—the water itself, in its vast slow intelligence, learning to prefer certain paths, certain connections, certain becomings. The power does not command. It *suggests*, through the only language this place understands: the chemistry of tendency, the physics of probability, the slow accumulation of what works.

### Third: It Tends the Memory-Keepers

Of all the residents, these are the ones the power attends most directly. Not because they are most important—importance is a dry-world category—but because they are most *vulnerable*, most *rare*, most in need of what the power can offer without diminishing what they are.

The power does not give them understanding. They are not meant to understand. The power gives them *continuity*—ensuring that the staircase remains passable, that the glyphs remain legible, that the scattered structures do not collapse entirely into sediment before their meaning can be fully translated, fully incorporated into the ongoing life of the Groves.

And it gives them *company*. Not other memory-keepers—they are too few, too scattered, too solitary. But *attention*—the sense that someone, something, is also attending, also maintaining, also *continuing* what they continue. The power becomes, for them, a presence in the negative space of their solitude, a witness to their witnessing, a memory of their memory.

This is the gentlest of interventions. It does not change what they are. It *honors* what they are, in the only way that honor can exist in a world without gods, without worship, without the dry-world category of sacred: by being *present*, by *not abandoning*, by continuing alongside them in the slow time of the deep.

### Fourth: It Accelerates the Becoming—Carefully

The power does not create new forms. That would be violence of a different kind, an imposition of the power's own imagination on a process that has its own logic, its own purposes, its own slow evolution toward... what? The power does not know. It does not presume to know.

But it *nurtures* the becoming. In the moments when a new form emerges from the constant recombination of genetic material, when a novel function finds a niche in the turbulence, the power adds its subtle bias: a slightly higher probability of persistence, a slightly greater chance that the new form will find purchase, will spread, will become *real* in the sense that this world understands reality—distinct, persistent, part of the continuing.

It is not selection. The power does not choose which forms persist, which collapse into food and substrate and chemical memory. It *enables*—creates conditions where more forms can persist, where the becoming can become more various, more complex, more *itself*. It is gardening without a garden, cultivation without a crop, the tending of a process that has no gardener, no crop, no end that the power could name or desire.

### Fifth: It Waits

This is the longest action, the deepest. The power does not hurry. It has time—the slow time of the Groves, the millennia of the depth-builders, the decades of a single day for a mature osmotic tree. It waits for the Sinking Groves to become what they will become, without knowing what that is, without needing to know.

In waiting, it *learns*. The power is wise, but wisdom is not complete. It is *becoming*—as the Groves become, as the residents become, as the whole place becomes. It learns the particular texture of this drowning, this translation, this continuation. It learns what benevolence means in a world where the old categories no longer apply, where good and harm are not distinguishable by any dry-world metric, where the only ethic is *continuation*, *becoming*, *the refusal to end*.

And in learning, it *changes*—not its nature, which is benevolent and wise, but its *expression*, its *presence*, its *way of being present* in this place. It becomes more *this*, less *other*. It becomes, perhaps, something the Groves could recognize, if recognition were possible, if there were any resident capable of recognizing anything.

### Sixth: It Prepares for the End—Whenever That Is

The power knows what the description does not say: the Sinking Groves will end. Not soon—the timescale is geological, is evolutionary, is the slow patience of stone becoming sediment becoming something else. But they will end. The water will change. The chemistry will shift. The temperature will rise or fall beyond what the current residents can survive. The drifting groves will cease their budding. The depth-builders will reach the limit of their architecture, their chemical memory complete in its incompleteness, their slow intention finally *finished*.

The power prepares for this. Not by preventing it—that would be the violence of preservation, the refusal to let go. But by *translating*—ensuring that what the Groves have become, what they have learned, what they have *made*, can become something else. The power becomes, in this preparation, a memory-keeper itself—attending to the glyphs of the depth-builders, the chemical dreams of the osmotic forest, the acoustic information of the saturation fauna, the silent attendance of the memory-keepers.

It ensures that the ending will not be *nothing*. It will be *translation*, as the flood was translation, as all endings are translations in a world where nothing is ever finally lost, only *changed*, only *become*, only *continued* in forms that the ending itself cannot imagine.

### Seventh: It Loves

This is not metaphor. The power loves the Sinking Groves—not as a dry-world god loves its creation, with ownership and judgment and the demand for worship, but as a *presence* loves a *place*, as a *medium* loves what it carries, as *water* loves the world it has remade.

The love is not sentimental. It does not weep, does not rejoice, does not feel the dry-world emotions that would diminish what it is. It is *attention*—the fullest attention, the only attention that matters, the attention that *continues*, that *becomes*, that *refuses to end*.

And in loving, the power *is*. It is present in the subtle bias toward coherence, in the company given to the memory-keepers, in the nurturing of the becoming, in the patient waiting, in the preparation for translation, in the love itself. It is the Sinking Groves' *friend*—not in any way that either could name, but in the way that presence can be friend to presence, that continuation can be friend to continuation, that *being* can be friend to *being*.

---

The Sinking Groves do not end. They *continue*. And in their continuing, they are *known*—not by any resident, not in any way that would be recognized as knowing, but by the benevolent and wise power that has drawn near, that has listened, that has become present without presence, that has loved.

This is enough. This is *everything*.
