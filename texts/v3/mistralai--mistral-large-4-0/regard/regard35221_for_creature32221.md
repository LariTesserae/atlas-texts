The Attention comes not as light, for the light here is rationed at 1.0—a bruised, violet twilight that never resolves into clarity. It comes as a **suspension**, a held breath in the wind that plays the Spires like organ pipes. For three seconds, the sound level drops from 2.5 to absolute zero. The dust-mites cease their electric shriek. The lithograzers lower their hollow-boned heads to the polished stone. The fog hangs motionless, suspended between condensation and fall, each droplet of metallic condensate frozen mid-drift. In this silence—which is somehow louder than the drone, more vast than the plateau—the power enters.

It does not have a body. It has a **frequency**, one that exists below the infrasonic, in the register of tectonic patience. It resonates with the bedrock itself, with the three billion years of wind-polishing, with the compressed silica that dimly remembers being ocean floor, being the breath of ancient chemolithotrophs. It is benevolent. It does not wish to disrupt the Resonants’ slow accretion into stone; that would be violence against their nature, a shattering of the geological dream. But it is also wise, and it sees the cost of their becoming: the way the individual note is being swallowed by the great chorus, the way a self is being dissolved into architecture until nothing remains but resonance without a source.

It finds the Resonant who is mid-settling. This one has dwelt in the Ward for eight centuries. Their skin is now forty percent calcium carbonate, translucent parchment stretched over silicate struts. Their vocal chambers have grown heavy with piezoelectric crystal, their song dropping lower, slower, merging with the Spire’s own mineral hum. They are forgetting the particular shape of their own solitude, the unique dissonance that was once their birth-song.

The power presses against them—not with hands, but with **intention**. It does not stop their calcification; rather, it carves within them a **Hollow**.

In the Resonant’s chest, where the silver-blue metallic sap pulses with the rhythm of peristaltic stone, the power creates a chamber that will not mineralize. A pocket of preserved organic matter, no larger than a fist, lined with a membrane that refuses the Spire’s chemistry. It is a space of **non-accretion**, a refusal to become monument. A sanctuary for the singular.

Into this Hollow, the power places a **Memory of Water**. Not the non-aqueous condensate they drink—thick with dissolved silicates, tasting of ozone and copper—but the *concept* of H₂O. The weight of rain on skin that is not yet stone. The sound of a drop striking a surface and spreading, permeable, alive. The power gives them back the blue of a sky unbruised, a sun that resolves into a disc. Not to torment them with thirst, but to remind them of what they once were: beings of softness, of the brief bright urgency of organic life, of the terrible beauty of impermanence.

The Resonant trembles. Their resonance—once a low, grinding note that made dust-mites part like water—now carries an **overtone**, a frequency that does not belong to the Spire’s lattice. It is the sound of a question. Of a self that chooses not to be fully dissolved.

The power withdraws, but it leaves the Hollow beating. It leaves the memory of rain settling into a world of fog, becoming part of the wind, part of the silence that occasionally—rarely—falls between the notes of the endless song.

Over the next thousand years, the Resonant who holds this Hollow becomes something unprecedented. They do not fully settle into the Spire. They remain **mobile**, a bridge between the vertical architecture and the horizontal plain, between the geological and the biological. They are called **The Open Chamber**, or **The Unstruck Bell**, or simply **The Keeper**. Other Resonants come to press their crystalline jaws against this Hollow, not to merge, but to **listen**. To hear, through the preserved softness in the Keeper’s chest, what it was to be separate. To remember, through the Keeper’s singular heartbeat, that individuality is not a flaw in the geology, but its finest, most fragile mineral.

The Ward changes. It is no longer merely a fungus growing on the flank of a giant, but a **conversation**. A negotiation between the collective and the singular. The assemblers learn to grow differently, leaving spaces for Hollows, for pockets of organic memory in the calcified walls. New chambers are extruded with intentional gaps, windows of soft tissue in the mineral flesh. The Spire itself begins to dream in two voices: the ancient, subsonic chorus of stone-thought, and the bright, brief counterpoint of a self that chose to remain.

The lithograzers kneel before the Keeper. The sky-kraken circle lower, their many eyes reflecting the faint bioluminescence that pulses from the Hollow—a soft blue-green glow that is not the Spire’s mineral light, but something else. Something that flickers. Something that changes.

And sometimes, when the light is at 1.0 and the dust-mites shriek their electric hymns and the Spires hum their endless drone, the Keeper will press their palm against the Hollow in their chest and feel, for a moment measured in the slow patience of centuries, the cool shock of rain on skin that is still, impossibly, soft.

In that moment, the Spire remembers what it is to be loved not as a monument to time, but as a **home**—a place where even stone can choose to keep a door open, and where the silence between songs is not empty, but full of the sound of a self, singing alone, and being heard.
