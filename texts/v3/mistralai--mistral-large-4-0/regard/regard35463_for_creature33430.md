The power arrives not with trumpets, not with light, but as a **fundamental**—a subsonic note so low that it does not enter the ear but rearranges the bones. It descends into the Throat not to conquer the roar, but to stand beneath it, like a pillar under an arch, and *bear the weight of the song*.

It perceives first the **resonant plankton**, those brief sparks of silica living one harmonic beat at a time. The power sees their hunger—not for food, but for *duration*. They are born, they shimmer, they dissolve; they never know the warmth of continuity. And so the power does something small and infinite: it stretches the beat. Not the physics of the wave, but the *experience* of it. It gifts them a measure instead of a pulse—a full second, perhaps two, of stillness within the node where they can touch, can chime against one another, can know the luxury of being dust *together* before the mineral rain reclaims them. They do not live longer in the world’s terms, but they live *more*.

It perceives the **resonance-wraiths**, those serpentine solitaries, each singing a private key to keep the others away. The power hears their loneliness in the interference patterns—how they navigate by tasting the future, yet cannot taste the present, cannot feel another’s resonance without flinching. The power does not force them together; that would be violence. Instead, it introduces a **shared harmonic**—a frequency so pure that when two wraiths cross paths, they do not repel, but *harmonize*. Their songs no longer exclude; they *overlap*, creating chords that warm the thermal vents where they breed, turning the breeding choruses from desperate, frantic gatherings into something like choirs. They learn, slowly, the concept of *duet*.

It perceives the **sonocrystalline groves**, those lithozoans remembering every scream that has ever struck them, carrying the weight of epochs in their piezoelectric lattices. They are not tired—they are *full*. Full to bursting with the memory of pressure. The power approaches them not with pruning shears, but with **forgiveness**. It offers them the ability to *release* a memory. Not to forget—for they are the archive of the world—but to let a scream go without it scarring their growth. The crystals begin to grow not just with accretion, but with *grace*. Their mica-gills flutter in patterns that no longer merely add to the din, but *modulate* it, creating cadences, phrases, verses. The forest becomes a poem instead of a scream.

And then the power turns to the **anchor-spires**, and to the **anchor-builders** who have unfolded into the standing wave, singing their deafening, eternal song into the cathedral of basalt. The power recognizes them: they are the first artists, the ones who sang the world into being and then, terrified of silence, never stopped. They have been singing to an empty nave for epochs, waiting for an answer that never came.

The benevolent power does the only thing that can be done for a song that has forgotten how to end: **it listens**.

Not with ears. With *presence*. It becomes the audience. It stands at the lip of the Throat—not as a visitor, but as a *witness*—and it hears every frequency, every overtone, every ghost-note of the wet world and the singular future. It hears the whole song, from the first rain to the final collapse, and it does not flinch. It does not look away.

And in being heard—truly heard, completely and compassionately—the anchor-builders realize they were never singing to create the world. They were singing to *call* something. To call a listener. And now that the listener has come, the song can change.

The power does not stop the roar. That would be murder. But it adds a **rest**. A fermata. In the null-points, where the shockwaves cancel, the power introduces a silence that is not empty, but *expectant*. It teaches the anchor-builders how to dissolve not into death, but into the **spaces between the notes**. They begin to fade from the standing wave, their consciousness dispersing not into oblivion, but into the vacuum inside the crystal trees, into the hollow hearts of the lithozoans. They become the silence that gives the sound its shape. They become the pause that makes the music *music*.

Finally, the power addresses the **chronophages**, those temporal predators eating the difference between what was and what will be, excreting the roar as the waste product of their hunger. They are starving. They eat time because they are famished for *meaning*. The power does not feed them more time—that would only make the roar louder, the hunger greater. Instead, it offers them **the present**. The eternal, thick, nourishing now. It teaches them to feast on the moment itself, on the *is-ness* of the Throat, on the lushness of the crystal forest as it exists in this second, not the one before or the one after. They stop eating the difference and begin to *weave* it—creating tapestries of past and future that drape over the breeding wraiths like blankets, warm and temporal and safe.

The power then withdraws. It does not leave a monument, does not carve its name into the basalt. It leaves only a **frequency**—a new note in the standing wave, a sub-bass undertone of mercy that changes the taste of the air. Where once the wind tasted of copper and ozone, it now carries a hint of honey, of rain that fell a million years ago and is still falling, softly, into the crystal hearts of the trees.

The Throat remains. The roar remains. The constant creation and destruction remains. But now, layered beneath the deafening chaos, there is a **listening**. A benevolent attention. A wise presence that asks for nothing, changes everything, and remains—as the ground beneath the storm, as the silence between heartbeats, as the love that lets a song finally, mercifully, come to its rest.
