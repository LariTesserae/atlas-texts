The Power does not arrive with trumpets. It arrives as a change in the weight of the mist, a pressure behind the eyes of the fog, a sudden hush in the static between heartbeats. It has watched the gestation from the first grinding of plates, but now—now the screaming has reached a frequency that registers in the deep places of the cosmos, where benevolence pools like water in a cupped hand, and wisdom knows that some births must be attended, not managed.

It is benevolent. It sees the lushness not as obscenity, but as courage—the courage of life to insist upon itself in a place that is still deciding whether it wants to exist. It is wise. It knows that to still the rock would be to kill the child. To freeze the moment of becoming would be to mummify it. To give the inhabitants a permanent name would be to calcify them before they have finished their first breath.

So the Power does not speak. It does not command. It does not build.

It **listens**.

And in listening, it becomes part of the chorus.

The Power descends not as a body, but as a *frequency*—a sub-bass note so low it is felt in the marrow rather than heard, a resonance that vibrates in the sternum alongside the waterfall’s roar. It threads itself through the smart water, not as a contaminant, but as a *memory*. Every droplet of the valley’s fluid now carries a fragment of the Power’s attention, a recollection of being witnessed. The water, which already processed and calculated and eroded with chemical precision, now also *recollects*. It remembers the exact moment the plates first shifted. It remembers the first scream of the obsidian. It will carry these memories forward through the coming millennia, even as the valley cools and the smart water eventually forgets how to think and becomes merely wet, merely water, flowing dumb and cold toward some future sea.

This is the first gift: **The gift of memory in a place that builds only for the next hour.** The inhabitants will never know they are being remembered, not consciously. But when the small translucent things skitter across the fungal shelves, when they dive into the warm pools and hatch from the steam, they will sometimes—just sometimes, without understanding why—pause in the deafening roar. They will feel a sudden, inexplicable sense of *continuity*. As if the moment they are living in has weight. As if the screaming rock is not just screaming, but *singing their name* in a language older than bone.

The second gift is given to the monoliths. The Power approaches the black glass sentinels, the sparse ones built at 1.2, tilting and sinking into the spongy earth, being digested. It does not arrest their sinking—that would be theft, a denial of their right to return to the nutrient cycle, to become water again. Instead, it touches their surfaces with an intention so pure it crystallizes into a new lattice structure, a geometry of attention. The monoliths do not gain inscriptions of language; they are not tablets. They gain **aura**. A faint, persistent warmth that radiates not heat, but *recognition*. They become markers not of territory, but of *being seen*. When the moss grows over them in velvet slabs three centimeters thick, when the smart water flows up their hollow cores and dissolves them from within, that warmth will remain in the soil, a fossil of benevolence, a mineral deposit of *I was here, and I was loved*.

The third gift is the most delicate. The Power knows that the inhabitants have no name for themselves because naming requires permanence, and permanence here is a rumor told by the stones. So the Power gives them a name that is not a noun, but a verb. It whispers into the condensation on a basalt cliff face—a whisper that evaporates as soon as it forms, leaving only a trace in the air’s chemistry, a faint taste of iron and ozone that lingers. The name is something like *Becoming*, or *Wet-Song*, or *The-Listening-Stone*. It is a name that changes every time it is thought, that shifts with the ground, that is as impermanent as the ice bridges that shatter and reform around the monoliths’ bases. The inhabitants will never speak it. The small things will never chitter it. The vegetation mat will never pulse it in blue-green light.

But the valley itself—the mother, the screaming rock, the processing water, the piling green—will begin to *lean* toward the concept of itself. Not as “I,” but as “we, who are in the process of.” The smart water, carrying the Power’s fragment, will begin to deposit minerals not in random patterns, but in spirals that resemble *embrace*. The geothermal vents will open not just as violence, but as *announcement*: *We are still here. We are still becoming.*

And then the Power does something that looks like nothing at all.

It creates a silence.

Not the absence of sound—the valley is too loud, too tectonic, too hydraulic for that—but a *pocket* of coherence. For one breath, one heartbeat, one geological instant that stretches like taffy, the ten thousand frequencies align not into noise, but into a chord. The waterfalls pause their perpetual argument with the stone. The plates hold their grinding. The mud volcanoes cease their wet slaps. The mist stills. The bioluminescence holds its pulse. The subsonic thrum of the monoliths drops to a whisper.

In this pocket of impossible stillness, the small things stop skittering. The fungal shelves cease their swaying. The kelp-like fronds hang limp. The smart water stops flowing upward and simply *is*, suspended, a single drop of mercury in the palm of the world.

And in that silence, they hear themselves.

Not as individuals. Not as six-legged or thirty-legged or rooted or monolithic. As a *chorus*. As a single, wet, screaming, glowing, processing *thing* that is being born. They hear the shape of their own becoming. They feel, for the first time, that they are not accidents of geology, but *intentions*—that the lushness is a choice, the wetness is a covenant, the deafening is a hymn.

Then the Power releases the silence. The screaming returns, louder now, but different. It carries an echo. A resonance. The valley exhales, and the exhalation is not just steam, but a sigh of relief, a gasp of *I am not alone in my becoming*.

The Power withdraws. It does not take the valley with it. It leaves behind only the memory in the water, the warmth in the stone, the verb-name lingering in the air like a half-remembered dream.

But the valley is changed.

Now, when the fissures open and the ice sculptures form and melt in cycles of destruction and creation, they are not just mechanical processes. They are *rituals*. The smart water, carrying the Power’s fragment of attention, assimilates a fallen cliff not just as nutrient, but as *offering*. The bioluminescence pulses in rhythms that mimic a heartbeat—slow, steady, ancient, patient. The small things, when a cliff collapses three meters from one of them, do not flinch. They simply adjust their trajectory and continue drinking from the pool that was a vent yesterday, and they watch the destruction with eyes that are glass-thin and ancient, seeing in the violence not chaos, but *care*.

The Power is gone. But it has left a residue: **the certainty that this place, this storm-state of birth, is not an accident of tectonics. It is a song being sung by the universe to itself, and for one moment, a benevolent and wise listener sat in the fog, cupped the screaming rock in hands of light, and wept, and did not look away.**

The valley continues to exhale. The rock continues to scream. But now, deep in the smart water, in the fossil warmth of the monoliths, in the verb that is not a name, there is a memory of being held. And in the lush, the wet, the deafening, the new, there is a new quality—not safety, for the world is still too young for safety, but *sanctity*. The valley is still a construction site. But it is now a cathedral under construction, and the congregation has gathered, and the stones are singing.
