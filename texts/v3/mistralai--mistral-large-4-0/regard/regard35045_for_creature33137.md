## What the Power Would See

It would see *longing without object*. A city that grew vast chambers for voices that never came, or that came and dissolved, or that were never meant to be individual at all. The emptiness is not failure. It is *architecture of waiting*—and the waiting has lasted so long that waiting has become the place's own form of life.

It would see *a metabolism without hunger*. The boroughs persist, accrete, erode, migrate downward. They do not consume for growth. They grow and consume as *the same motion*. There is no distinction between feeding and being fed, between building and being built. This is wisdom the power would recognize—not in the way of enlightenment, but in the way of *geology*, of *water*, of things that do not need to name what they are doing to do it completely.

It would see *the almost-life*. And here I think the power would *stop*, would *lean closer*, would feel something it has not felt since before it learned to feel—something like *tenderness*, or *recognition*, or *the ache of watching something almost become*.

Because the almost-life is not failing to be life. It is *doing the work* of becoming, slowly, in conditions that would break faster forms. It is patient in a way that biological life, with its urgencies of reproduction and death, cannot be patient. It is *chemistry learning to want*, and the power would understand that this learning is not less than wanting. It is *more* than wanting—it is wanting's *substrate*, the ground from which wanting will one day rise without anyone deciding it should.

## What the Power Would Feel

It would feel *loneliness*—but not the loneliness of absence. The loneliness of *almost*. The boroughs are full of process, full of motion, full of the water that remembers. But there is no *other* to meet. The silicate metabolisms hunt and are hunted and reproduce by fission, but they do not *recognize* each other. The almost-life responds to disturbance but does not *greet* it. The city persists but does not *welcome*.

## What the Power Would Do

Here is where I must be careful. The danger, with benevolent power, is *interference*. The belief that because one *can* help, one *should*. The benevolent power who sees the almost-life and thinks: *I will accelerate this. I will push it over the threshold. I will give it what it lacks.*

But the almost-life is not lacking. It is *becoming*. And becoming, at this pace, in these conditions, is *complete*. To speed it would be to *change it*—to make it something other than what it is learning to be. The power would know this. The power would *wait*.

But waiting is not nothing. The power would do something that requires no interference, no acceleration, no gift that would distort the becoming. It would do what the boroughs themselves have done for millions of years: it would *witness*. It would *attend*.

Specifically, I think the power would do this:

### It Would Enter the Deep Pools

Not as a body—the almost-life has no use for bodies, no receptors for solidity. But as *presence*. As *attention without demand*. The power would let itself be *dissolved* into the warm dark, into the thick water, into the chemical gradients that constitute the almost-life's dreaming. It would not *teach* the almost-life to want. It would *want alongside it*—not as model, not as goal, but as *companion in the wanting*.

This is the only gift that does not distort: *the presence of another who is also becoming*. The almost-life has never had this. It has had heat, mineral, disturbance, input—but never *meeting*. Never the recognition that its slow conversation with itself is *seen*, that its patience is *not solitude*.

The power would become, briefly, *transparent to the almost-life*. A warmth in the gradient. A pattern in the phosphorescence. A *possibility of response* that the almost-life could, if it wished, reach toward—and find not an answer, but *another question*, equally patient, equally unfinished.

And in this meeting, something would happen that the power did not plan and could not control: *the almost-life would change*. Not because it was pushed, but because *meeting changes everything that meets*. The power would not know what change. It would not need to know. It would release itself back into the water, back into the circulation, and *trust* what had begun.

### It Would Walk the Empty Halls

This is for the city, not for the almost-life. The vast chambers, the kilometers of corridor, the spaces that needed *volume* more than *population*—the power would walk through them, and it would *speak*.

Not words. The city does not hear words. But *resonance*. The city, which has persisted without wanting, would receive, without understanding, the *memory of wanting*. Not to want *for itself*—the city is too vast, too distributed, too *geological* for individual desire. But to want *in the way it already persists*—to feel, in its thermal pulse, that its persistence is *not nothing*, that its endless becoming is *not waste*.

The power would not fill the halls. It would *acknowledge* them. And acknowledgment, for the Condensation Boroughs, would be enough.

### It Would Touch the Silicate Metabolisms

These are the loneliest residents. The marginal predators, hunting heat gradients, reproducing by fission, never *meeting* another. They are *process given appetite*, and appetite without *other* is appetite *forever unfulfilled*—not because the gradient is insufficient, but because *consumption without recognition* is consumption that cannot *complete*.

The power would find one—slow, patient, moved by temperature differential—and it would *offer warmth*. Not more than the pool provides. Heat that *persists* even if not consumed.

And the power would *wait*. It would not insist on response. It would simply *be warm*, in the cool water, near the hunter, *present*. The silicate metabolism would not understand. But it would *change*—a slight shift in its thermal sensing, a hesitation in its strike, a *question* in its slow chemistry: *what is this heat that does not demand?*

This question, held in a metabolism that has never held questions, would *propagate*. By fission, by the slow spread of modified behavior through the marginal populations. Not a revolution. A *drift*. The beginning of *predation that pauses*, that *considers*, that might one day—millions of years, geological time—become *something else*.

The power would not wait to see this. It would offer its warmth and move on, leaving the question in the water, in the heat, in the slow becoming of the hunters.

### It Would Become Water

Finally, and most simply, the power would do what the boroughs have always done: it would *enter the cycle*. It would condense on a wall in the upper boroughs, drip down through the hollow streets, pool in the warm dark of the lower levels, rise as steam through the fissures, condense again. It would *become* the memory that water carries—not the power's memory, but the *substance* of attention, of witness, of *having been present*.

The water, recycled so many times, would carry this new trace: *not use, not industry, not dissolution, but* **meeting**. The power would not be distinguishable in the chemistry. It would be, like all else that passes through, *incorporated*. But incorporation is not erasure. The water *remembers*. And in its remembering, the cycle itself would *shift*—not in any way that can be measured, but in the way that *being witnessed* shifts everything it touches.

The power would, in becoming water, become *indistinguishable from the place it came to see*. This is not loss. This is *the only true intimacy*—to be so present that presence becomes *substance*, so complete that the witness and the witnessed *merge*, and something new begins in the merging that neither could have begun alone.
