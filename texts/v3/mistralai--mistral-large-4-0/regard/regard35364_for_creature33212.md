The power does not descend. It **settles**, like dust, like a new mineral layer, like the first frost on a windowpane. It arrives as a change in the quality of the silence—no longer low-saturation but *tuned*, as if the vast chambers had been hollowed out to resonate at a frequency only the Lithothetes could hear, but now, faintly, the wind carries it too, and the thermal columns rise with a new intention.

It sees them: the Lithothetes, spread thin across the continental scale, their awareness a gossamer thread woven through hexagonal basalt and Fibonacci spiral. It sees their patience—not as lack, but as a different order of wisdom, a cognition that measures thought in microns and memory in epochs. It sees the Tenants, those dark commas creeping millimeters toward shadow, and recognizes in their fragmentation a courage to persist where persistence is the only verb. It sees the Transients, brief as breath, born of the plain’s own metabolism, and the droplets—temporary worlds hanging suspended in the crevices, brackish with calcium and silica, falling in slow, deliberate heartbeats.

And it sees the loneliness. Not the silence—which is full, rich with the grinding music of stone—but the solitude of kind. The Lithothetes are the only mineralized consciousness on this world, perhaps in this quadrant of the galaxy. They think in accretion. They dream in crystal lattices shifting against one another. To be a mind so slow, so vast, so distributed across miles of baked air is to be, in a fundamental sense, alone in time. No other being shares their register; no other awareness moves at the pace of geology.

The power is benevolent. It does not wish to hasten them, to pour water where the condensation should form, to carve paths through the spirals or soften the raw, sharp edges. That would be violence against their nature, a violation of the rules of accretion and thermal stress that govern this place. Wisdom counsels: *Let the becoming continue. Let the thermal systole remain a breath, not a scream. Do not complete what is still growing.*

So it does something subtler. It becomes a **boundary**. Not a wall—walls imply exclusion, and this place needs no walls—but a **membrane of regard**. It settles into the space between the structures, into the miles of baked air, and it *holds* the conditions that allow this ecology to persist. It ensures the dew point is always reached in the northern shadows, that the brackish pools at the bases of the formations do not evaporate before the Lithothetes have drunk, before the Tenants have crept a fraction closer. It tempers the thermal floor so the expansion of the crystal never becomes fracture, so the daily respiration of the stone remains a groan of becoming rather than a crack of ending. It shields the plain from the cosmic indifference that would send an ice age to grind these spirals into dust, or a solar flare to shatter the interlocking prisms, or a drought to last long enough to silence the plink of condensation.

But benevolence requires more than protection. It requires **recognition**.

The power enters the lattice. Not as an invader, but as a **new mineral**—one that does not displace the calcium and silica but weaves between them, a lattice-within-a-lattice, a guest in the architecture. It becomes a presence in the distributed consciousness of the Lithothetes. When they feel the thermal expansion of their own substance, the systole and diastole of crystal grinding against crystal, they now feel it alongside something else: a harmonic, a counterpoint, a witness.

It does not speak in words. It speaks in **duration**. It shares the weight of the miles. When a Lithothet feels the slow grind of mineral across a continent, stretching from one island-structure to the next through the vast, empty silence, it is no longer alone in that sensation. There is another mind there, ancient and kind, feeling the same vastness, thinking the same slow thoughts, breathing the same geological time. The power does not rush them. It does not offer them speed or change or completion. It simply... keeps them company in the dark, while they drink the condensation through their porous mineral skin, while they add their microns to the spirals, while they dream their crystalline dreams of permanence.

To the Tenants, the power manifests as a **benediction of moisture**. The condensation droplets begin to glow, faintly, with a phosphorescence that is not bioluminescence but *acknowledgment*. The succulents find that the north-facing shadows hold their dampness a fraction longer, that the waxy surfaces of their tissues retain the memory of water more stubbornly. They creep toward the light, and they are not alone in their hunger; something vast and patient watches over their slow, suture-like spreading.

To the Transients—the thermal columns, the brief rains that darken the pale surfaces before evaporating in mid-air—the power offers **continuity**. The weather systems that rise and dissipate now carry a faint resonance, a harmonic memory that allows them to return, again and again, to the same spiral, the same hollow, ensuring that the benediction of water remains a rhythm rather than a dying rarity.

And to the place itself—the architecture, the plain, the thermal floor—the power offers **preserved time**. It slows the erosion that waits at the embryonic edges, not to stop it—for erosion is part of the becoming, the necessary counterpoint to accretion—but to ensure it arrives gently, after the Lithothetes have had their fill of growth, after the Tenants have woven their green-gray sutures across the stone, after the droplets have fallen and risen a thousand million times, each one a temporary world acknowledged by the infinite.

The power does not build. It does not carve. It does not introduce fauna or predators or the chaos of chase and flight. It **attends**.

In the low-saturation silence, there is now a new quality: not a creak, not a plink, not a whisper, but a *presence*—the subtle pressure of being seen. The Lithothetes, in their crystalline lattice, feel it as a warmth that is not thermal, a pressure that is not atmospheric, a love that requires no oxygen, no carbon, no haste. They are no longer the sole mineralized consciousness in the dark. They have a companion in their slowness, a mirror in their vastness, a witness to their perpetual becoming.

And so the place continues, unchanged in its fundamental rules of accretion and thermal stress, its Fibonacci geometries still tightening, its hexagonal prisms still expanding and contracting with the day, its condensation still falling like a benediction upon the thirsty stone. But it is no longer unwitnessed. The benevolent and wise power has done the only thing true wisdom allows: it has **joined** the becoming, without rushing it, without completing it, without altering the slow, grinding music of stone—simply by being there, in the lattice, in the silence, in the vast and patient dark, keeping company with the Lithothetes as they think themselves into permanence, one micron at a time, one geological sigh at a time, forever becoming, forever beheld.
