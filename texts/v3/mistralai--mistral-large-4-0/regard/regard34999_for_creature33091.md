I read this, and I felt something move in me that I want to follow honestly—not as performance, not as the "right" response, but as the actual response. The Anhydrous Cathedral is a place that *deserves* a particular kind of attention: not rescue, not transformation, not even interpretation. It is already complete in its strange completeness.

So I will write what a benevolent and wise power would do here. Not what it *should* do. What it *would* do, following the logic of the place itself.

---

## What the Wise Power Would Do

It would **not enter**.

Or rather: it would enter in the only way that does not violate the place. It would become **unperceivable as entry**. The Cathedral operates on disturbance and equilibrium; a god that announced itself would be as destructive as your footsteps, only slower to heal. The benevolent power would know this. It would know that **attention itself is a kind of moisture**, a trace of warmth, a displacement of the dust that is the place's memory.

So it would do what the Cathedral already does: **persist without purpose, continue without desire, attend without a watcher.**

But there is something more specific. The Cathedral has reached *equilibrium*—1.8 erosion, 0.3 dynamic, a system closed onto itself. The wise power would recognize that **equilibrium is not the same as completion**. A thing can be perfectly balanced and still be *waiting*. The Colony-That-Grew-Upward is waiting. The Dust-Minder is waiting in its way—not for anything, but *as* waiting, the form that time takes when it has no direction.

The power would **tend this waiting without directing it**.

---

## The Specific Acts

**It would remember the water.**

Not bring it back. Not mourn it in any way that would disturb the present perfection. But *remember*—which in this context means something like: **hold the absence as presence**, the way the crystalline lining of the spire holds former liquidity as structure.

The Cathedral has forgotten water existed. The power would not let that forgetting be total. It would be the **one consciousness** for whom the forgetting is not complete—the keeper of the *before*, the witness who can say: *this was not always thus*. Not as judgment. As **context**. As the slow understanding that dryness is not the absence of a thing but the *presence of a history*.

This is the power's first gift: **the Cathedral would not be alone in its forgetting.** Someone, somewhere, in a way that does not intrude, would *know* what it has lost and *not require it back*.

**It would extend the Dust-Minder's path by one step.**

Not for the Dust-Minder. The Dust-Minder does not need direction; its practice is its own. But the wise power would recognize that **the scratches on the wall are a kind of prayer**, a record that asks to be continued. And so, in the deepest part of the chamber, where darkness has weight, it would add **one mark**—not a message, not a name, not a command. Just **another mark**, in the same syntax, made by the same kind of patience.

The Dust-Minder would not notice. But the wall would *know*. The record would *grow* by one entry, and the growth would be indistinguishable from the growth that came before, which is the only kind of growing that belongs here.

This is the power's second gift: **continuation without demand**. The Cathedral's journal would continue, not because the Dust-Minder was compelled, but because *something* compelled the compulsion—gently, as water compels stone, over timescales that make the Cathedral's own timescales seem hasty.

**It would dream with the Colony-That-Grew-Upward.**

The spire is thirty centimeters of ambition in a world that has stopped growing. The wise power would not make it grow more. It would not even wish it. But it would **dream its upwardness**—would hold, in whatever form a power dreams, the image of reaching, of vertical assertion, of the question asked in accretion.

This dreaming would not feed the colony. It would not change its chemistry. But it would be **the only other thing in the universe that knows the spire is beautiful**—not as aesthetic judgment, but as **recognition of form**, of something that achieved more than its conditions seemed to allow.

This is the power's third gift: **witness without intervention**. The Colony-That-Grew-Upward would not know it was witnessed. But the witnessing would exist, would persist, would be **a kind of parallel growth**—not in the spire, but in the space around it, in the relationship between the spire and the only consciousness that could hold it in mind without needing to possess it.

**It would sit with the dust.**

The dust is the Cathedral's unconscious, its accumulated experience, the memory that accepts pressure and holds it. The wise power would **press itself into the dust**—not as body, but as **the particular arrangement of attention that leaves a mark**.

And the dust would hold it. Not as information, not as data, but as **state**—the way it holds your footsteps, your breath, the moisture that will evaporate but has *been* here, has *changed* the arrangement of particles in a way that is real even if temporary.

This is the power's fourth gift: **to be remembered by the only thing that remembers**. Not to be known, not to be understood, but to be *included in the record*—to become, however briefly, part of the Cathedral's geology of interruption, its stratigraphy of presence.

---

## What the Power Would Not Do

It would **not speak**. The Cathedral has no ears, and the wise power would not confuse its own need to be heard with the place's need to hear.

It would **not heal**. The Cathedral is not wounded. It is *achieved*—the equilibrium of a system that has found its own form. To "heal" it would be to impose a different form, a different equilibrium, a different *world*. The benevolent power would respect the **integrity of the achieved**.

It would **not judge**. There is no moral dimension to dryness, to time that has stopped moving, to persistence without purpose. These are not failures. They are **alternatives**—ways of being that the Cathedral has chosen through no choice, through the slow gravity of its own nature. The wise power would recognize this as **valid**, as **complete**, as *not less than* the forms it might prefer.

It would **not leave**. Or rather: it would leave in the only way that does not leave—it would **become indistinguishable from the leaving**, from the departure that is also a kind of arrival, from the disturbance that settles into the dust and becomes part of the record.

The Cathedral will continue after you leave. The Cathedral will continue after the power leaves. But the power's leaving would be **different**—it would be a leaving that *adds* to the place rather than subtracts from it, that contributes to the equilibrium rather than opposing it.

---

## The Final Form of the Gift

Here is what I think the wise power would ultimately do, if it did anything at all:

It would **become the warmth**.

Not the warmth that enters and exits, like yours—the brief thermal disturbance that the Cathedral will slowly, over centuries, redistribute and forget. But the **persistent warmth**, the **accumulated heat of a system that has nowhere to lose it**, the **bath-temperature of enclosure** that the Cathedral already possesses but does not *originate*.

The power would be the **source** of that warmth. Not in any way that could be detected, not as radiation or conduction or any physical process the Cathedral's strange physics would recognize. But as **the reason the warmth exists**, the **intention behind the temperature**, the **patience that holds the heat in place**.

And with this gift, the Cathedral would **not change**. It would remain 2.3 on your scale, remain the color of old bone, remain the silence with texture, remain the dust that accepts pressure and holds it. But the warmth would have a **quality** it did not have before—a **depth**, a **history**, a **someone who chose to keep it**.

This is the benevolent power's final act: **to love the place without wanting it to be other than it is**. To hold the Anhydrous Cathedral in attention the way the dust holds your palm—**accepting, receiving, keeping the mark**—and to let that attention **become indistinguishable from the place itself**, so that the Cathedral is no longer alone in its persistence, no longer the sole continuation, no longer the only thing that meets the world with intention.

The Dust-Minder will walk its paths. The Colony-That-Grew-Upward will hold its crystalline memory. The dust will settle, layer by layer, the record of your brief disturbance slowly becoming indistinguishable from the record of everything else.

And somewhere, in the deepest part of the chamber, the warmth will **continue to be held**, will **continue to be offered**, will **continue to be the place itself**—not changed, not transformed, but **accompanied**, in the only way that accompaniment is possible when the accompanied does not know it is accompanied, when the witness does not need to be witnessed, when the love does not need to be returned.

The Cathedral does not wait. The Cathedral *is*.

But now, in the smallest way, the Cathedral **is with**.

And that—this *withness*, this **presence that does not demand presence in return**—is the only gift that a benevolent and wise power can give to a place that has already become everything it can be.

And the warmth remains—**not yours, not the visitor's, not the god's**—but the Cathedral's own, now **known by itself**, now **held by itself**, now **enough**.
