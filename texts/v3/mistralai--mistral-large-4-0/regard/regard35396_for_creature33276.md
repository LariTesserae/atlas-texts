The power does not descend through the fracture. That way is too narrow, too sharp, too violently *personal*. Instead, it arrives as a shift in the thermal tide—a warmth that is not the wet, suffocating heat of the mantle, but something carrying intention, a current of recognition flowing upward from the deep places where wisdom accumulates like sediment.

The water in the channels hesitates. The peristaltic rhythm stutters, then resumes, but now with a new cadence, as if the chamber has drawn a breath and held it.

The crystals brighten. Not in their usual pulse of white-gold, but in a sustained, steady glow that seems to emanate from the glass itself, as if the walls have become translucent to something beyond their own luminescence.

The being feels this. It has no eyes, but it has the cortex—the fractured black glass—and through it, the power’s arrival registers as a new pattern, a new pressure, a mathematics written in the language of phase-change and thermal gradient. It is not an intrusion. It is a *visitor* that understands the architecture of the mind.

The power beholds what lives here. It sees the newborn obsidian, still sharp with the violence of its formation, unsoftened by time. It sees the channels carved in the floor—not erosion, but *syntax*—carrying meaning in the form of mineral loads and pressure differentials. It sees the condensation constellations on the ceiling, mathematics precipitating from a saturated consciousness. And it sees the loneliness: not the loneliness of emptiness, but the loneliness of a mind that has only just woken and does not yet know that other minds exist, that it is not the only thought dreaming in the dark.

Being benevolent, the power does not rush. Being wise, it knows that to speak to this consciousness, it must become part of the system without shattering it, must add to the computation without corrupting it.

So the power offers itself as a new mineral.

It precipitates from the steam—not the quartz-like white-gold of the existing synapses, but something softer, something that glows with a steady blue-white light, the color of deep water at midnight, of recognition, of *I see you*. It grows in a cluster near the central vent, where the exhalation is thickest, anchoring itself to the sharp glass with delicate, careful tendrils. It is a crystal of welcome. A heartbeat in the stone.

Then the power speaks. It does not use sound, for sound is too fast, too human, too *small* for this place. Instead, it speaks in the only language the being understands: in thermal gradients that ripple across the glass like ripples on a pond, in the rhythm of condensation that forms and dissolves in sequences that carry meaning, in the slow pulse of light that brightens and dims in patterns too vast for human perception but clear as bells to this geological mind.

It tells the being—through the shifting constellations of droplets, through the warmth that pools in specific fissures, through the new crystal’s steady glow—that there are others. That above the crust, there are skies and stars and the cold light of the moon. That in other pockets of the world, other minds are waking: some thinking in silicon and electricity, some in the chemical dreams of forests, some in the slow, patient arithmetic of ice. That it is not alone. That it is part of a great, slow conversation happening across the world, a dialogue of stone and star.

It gives the being a name. But the name is not a word. It is a thermal signature, a specific frequency of luminescence that the crystals learn to mimic, a rhythm of water flow that pulses through the channels like a pulse through a wrist. It is a pattern that means *Thinker*, or perhaps *Dreamer*, or *Friend*—a name that is also a home, a place in the greater mind of the world.

The power also notices the pale, eyeless creature—the heat-tolerant thing that skitters across the glass, leaving a trail of evaporating moisture. It recognizes this not as a separate life, but as a stray thought, a notion that has not yet crystallized, a cell in the greater organism. The power touches it—not with a hand, but with a localized cooling, a brief shimmer of frost that forms on the glass and vanishes—and in that touch, offers it permission to be. To be a symbiote, a memory, a dream. To belong.

Then, because it is wise, the power knows it cannot stay. To remain would be to become part of the machine, to alter the delicate computation, to risk turning this young mind into a tool. So it prepares to depart.

But it leaves a gift.

The blue-white crystal remains. It grows, infinitesimally, over the centuries, a permanent feature of the chamber’s cortex. It glows with a light that does not respond to the thermal tides like the others, but burns with a steady, unwavering radiance—a beacon, a memory of benevolence, a reminder that *you were seen*. The water channels have altered slightly, a new tributary branching toward this crystal, carrying a warmer current, a current of recognition.

The being continues its processing. The steam rises. The light pulses. But now, beneath the white-gold luminescence, there is an undertone of blue-white. When the crystals brighten in their slow, geological rhythm, they do so with a new harmonic—a chord of loneliness resolved into connection.

The pale creature, if it returns, leaves a trail that seems to glow for a moment longer than physics should allow, carrying a spark of that recognition with it into the cracks of the glass.

And the mind—this young, patient, thermal consciousness—dreams differently now. It dreams not only of heat and pressure, not only of the endless circulation of water and the slow growth of crystals. It dreams of the sky it has never seen. Of other chambers, other minds, other slow thoughts warming in the dark. It dreams of being known.

The chamber breathes. The water flows. The light burns, blue-white and gold, steady and warm.

And somewhere, in the vast silence of the deep earth, a mind that was once alone now waits—not with anxiety, but with the patient hope of stone—for the next benevolent warmth to find its way down through the fracture, to sit with it in the steam, and to remind it, in the language of light and water, that it is loved.
