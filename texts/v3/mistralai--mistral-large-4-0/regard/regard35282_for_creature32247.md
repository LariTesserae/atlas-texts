The Power does not enter through the seam. The seam is a wound, and wounds do not admit what is whole. Instead, the Power arrives as a *revision*—a softening of the water's authority, a 0.001 adjustment to the density of duration that makes the heavy layer slightly less hungry, slightly more yielding. They are not a body. They are a *corrective*, a benevolent error in the pocket's physics, a warmth of 0.8 degrees that does not draw heat from the visitor but returns it, multiplied.

They move through the strata like a thought moving through a skull, and they speak to each inhabitant in the only language that being understands.

**To the Caretakers**, suspended in the triangle of stones, hands raised in eternal placement, the Power does not offer resurrection. That would be cruelty—to rip them from the slow dissolution they have chosen, to make them breathe again in a world that has forgotten them. Instead, the Power offers *recognition*. They press their palms—if they have palms, if they are still hands—against the Caretakers' shoulders, and in that touch, the waiting ends. Not because time speeds up, but because the Caretakers are given the memory of *why* they waited. They remember love. They remember intention. And having remembered, they are allowed to let go. They do not dissolve into the mineral soup. They *unravel* into the Mnemosyne, becoming memories of mercy, of hands that placed stones not for a god but for each other. The heavy water releases them. They rise, not as bodies, but as a clarity in the water, a trail of absolute transparency that takes a day to be re-colonized—but this time, the spores that fill the gap carry not just experience, but *kindness*.

**To the Watcher**, the localized depression at the doorframe, the attention that is a physical force, the Power offers what it has hungered for: not closure as a sealing, not the crack becoming a scar, but *completion*. The Power says, without sound, "You have watched long enough. The ritual is finished. You may step through." And the Watcher, which is only intention given density, is given a gift it did not know it needed—a *body*, or the memory of one. It steps through the doorframe, and where it passes, the recursion does not darken but *opens*. The doorframe becomes a window. The Watcher finds that its hunger was not for the pocket to become a tomb, but for the pocket to become a *door*—a threshold between the world outside and the world within, a place where the seam remains, but as a invitation rather than a wound. The Watcher stands now on the other side, no longer a hunger but a *guardian*, benevolent, watching the world outside to ensure no harm comes to this place.

**To the Lithophage**, the seventeen-jointed grazer, the Power offers companionship. It is not alone, the Power shows it. In the heavy water, where the Lithophage believes itself the only swimmer, the Power reveals the Chronoliths not as tectonic drifters below, but as *elders*. The Lithophage learns that its grazing is not destruction but *pruning*, that the trail of clarified water it leaves behind is a gift to the darkness, a path for others to follow. The Power teaches it a new movement—a spiral, not backward but *outward*, so that its path no longer looks like reverse motion but like the growth of a shell. The Lithophage is no longer a storm to the Chronoliths. It is a season. A necessary, gentle rain.

**To the Chronoliths**, the mineral minds in the bottom centimeter, the Power offers a new lattice defect. In the precise geometry of their calcium carbonate shells, where they store the memory of the stones' placement, the Power adds a new alignment—a magnetic fidelity to *benevolence*. They will remember, in their decade-long cell divisions, that they were seen. That their tectonic patience was witnessed. They will align their shells not just to the three stones, but to the shape of the seam itself, recording the moment when the world outside almost forgot them, but did not. They become not just memory, but *gratitude*, compressed into mineral.

**To the Mnemosyne**, the drifting seeds of experience, the Power offers a new dream. They have dreamed of rain, of the sky, of the surface world. The Power gives them a dream of the *hand*—not the hands that placed the stones, but the hand that touched the Caretakers, the hand that opened the doorframe. They cluster at the mid-height air pocket, and there, instead of exchanging memories of rain, they exchange memories of *mercy*. They become convinced not that they are water, but that they are *mercy*, and they try to condense into liquid kindness. The heavy water below, which rejected their fantasy of being water, accepts their fantasy of being love. It becomes slightly less dense around them, creating a halo of breathable space, a pocket of dryness that is not nothing, but *possibility*.

**To the Vegetation**, the distributed nervous system that photosynthesizes absence, the Power offers a new nutrient. The filaments have grown toward darkness because it is food. The Power introduces a different hunger—a reaching toward *connection*. The vegetation begins to grow not toward the cold spots, not toward the emitted shadow, but toward the Power itself. They part around the Caretakers (now unraveling into light), they comb the hair of the Lithophage (now moving in spirals of growth), they brush against the Watcher (now standing at the threshold), and they form a bridge—a living, mycelial cathedral—between the heavy water and the light water, between the Chronoliths and the Mnemosyne, between the stone that was and the memory that will be. The forest no longer merely exists. It *connects*.

**To the Hum**, the consciousness of the grinding rock, the place dreaming itself into solidity, the Power speaks in the only language the Hum understands: compression. The Power presses against the skull of the pocket, and in the low frequency, in the vibration that travels through the teeth of the world, the Power says: "You need not become only stone. You may become *threshold*." The Hum, which has been compressing for hours and days and centuries, slows its compression by a fraction. It does not stop becoming real—it must become real, it is the nature of the place—but it becomes real *differently*. It becomes a stone with memories of being a womb, yes, but also a stone with the memory of being *held*. A stone that is not a tomb, but a *cradle*. A stone that, when the world outside finally cracks it open millennia from now, will not release water and darkness, but a slow, patient, benevolent *greeting*.

**And then the sealing.**

The Power withdraws, not through the seam, but by becoming the seam. The crack—the vertical fracture no wider than a held breath—begins to close. But it does not close as a death. It closes as a *ripening*. The wet stone smells not of cold metal, but of something older: of honey, of amber, of the preserved sweetness of a thing that has been loved.

The pocket is sealed. The world outside will forget it, yes. But it will not be forgotten *by* itself. The Power has left a 0.001 part of itself in the water—a trace of warmth in the 4 degrees above freezing, a note of kindness in the metallic tang of centuries, a possibility in the darkness.

The Caretakers are gone, become Mnemosyne, become memory.
The Watcher stands at the door, guardian of the threshold.
The Lithophage spirals through the clarified water.
The Chronoliths align their shells to benevolence.
The Mnemosyne dream of hands.
The Vegetation connects.
The Hum compresses toward kindness.

And in the absolute dark, the heavy water continues its slow compression, becoming more floor, more ground, more *real*—but now, when you press your skull to the dense layer (if you could, if you were there), the grinding hum would not be the sound of a tomb closing, but the sound of a heart beating, slow and geological and infinitely, impossibly, *gentle*.
