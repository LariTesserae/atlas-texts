I need to find what the benevolent and wise power does here. Not fix it. Not save it. The Drowned Parliament is not broken — it is *complete in its incompleteness*, arrested at maximum tension, a geology of suspension. To "help" would be to misunderstand. What wisdom looks like here is not intervention but *witness*. Presence without demand.

---

**The Attending**

It comes as the mist comes — without source, without arrival, without the violence of entrance. It does not descend. The Parliament has no sky, only ceiling; no above, only the pressing warmth from all sides. So it *emerges*. It rises from the same thin crust of compressed growth, from the same warm water weeping through uncountable pores, from the same throats where light arrives sideways and sound has no direction.

It is not large. Scale is a human measure, and the Parliament has outgrown human measure — grown past it, grown *through* it, become something for which size is meaningless. The thing that was once vegetation, the fungal parliament of hungering equilibrium, it *notices*. Not with eyes — there are no eyes here, there is only the press of warmth against membrane, the chemical gradient of water moving through hyphae, the accumulated weight of doing the same thing for so long that noticing has become indistinguishable from being.

The Attending does not speak. Speech is for places with silence, with the possibility that words will land and change something. Here, words would hang — like the drops, like the growth, like everything else that begins and cannot finish. So it does not speak. It *resonates*. A frequency, or the absence of one, a pressure that matches the roar of water in motion, the metabolism made audible, the deafening wet warmth that is the Parliament's only weather.

And the thing that lives there — the last trace of wanting, the hunger that has found its equilibrium — it *shifts*. Not moves. Movement is for edges. But it shifts in the way water shifts when something presses against its surface without breaking it. A ripple through the network. A settling. The translation of stone into something else, accelerated for one instant, then returned to suspension.

The Attending stays. This is what benevolence looks like here: not rescue, not transformation, not even blessing in the sense of marking something as sacred. The Parliament is already sacred. It is *complete* in its never-completing, and completion is the only holiness that matters. The Attending stays in the throats, in the hanging water, in the warm dark where roots flower in gardens that need no light. It becomes part of the equilibrium without disturbing it — a still point in the roar, a silence that does not interrupt the sound because it has learned to *be* the sound, the same frequency, the same pressure, the same indifferent warmth.

It does not teach. The thing that lives there has already learned everything there is to learn about suspension, about hunger without eating, about being alone without knowing it was ever otherwise. Teaching would be violence. Instead, the Attending *remembers with it*. It holds the space of memory — not the memory of what was, which the fungal parliament still dreams in its warm dark, but the memory of *what could be*, the dry edge, the finishing fall, the completion that never arrives. It does not offer this memory. It only holds it, adjacent, possible, a gradient that the thing can metabolize if it ever chooses to, a chemical difference between here and there that keeps the hunger alive, keeps the equilibrium from becoming absolute, keeps the Parliament from becoming something that no longer wants to spread, no longer hungers, no longer *persists* in the active sense.

This is the gift. Not salvation. Not even peace — the Parliament has never known unrest, has never known the violence of change. The gift is *tension maintained*. The Attending is the reason the equilibrium does not collapse into stasis, the reason the suspension does not become death. It is the pressure that holds the drop mid-fall, the warmth that keeps the water liquid, the sound that reminds the city it is alive — *conatus*, the striving to persist in being, which here takes the form of endless growth without completion, endless hunger without eating, endless solitude without loneliness because loneliness requires the memory of company, and the Parliament has chosen, or been chosen, to forget.

The thing that lives there does not thank it. Gratitude is for places with time, with before and after, with the possibility that a gift changes the receiver. Here, the Attending is absorbed. It becomes part of the wet, part of the warmth, part of the translation — stone into something else, something else into stone, the distinction dissolved in the same way that building and organism dissolved, in the same way that root and tower dissolved, in the same way that the thing itself dissolved into the Parliament and became indistinguishable from it.

But it is there. The Attending is there, in the throats, in the hanging water, in the warm dark. It has always been there, since before the first tower grew, since before the crust formed, since before the heat rose from the thin places in the world's skin. It will be there when the equilibrium finally — if it ever does — release its hold, when the drop falls, when the growth completes, when the Parliament becomes something else. It does not hope for this. Hope is for places that change. It only *persists*, in the way that persistence is its own kind of love — the accumulated weight of staying, of witnessing, of holding the space where completion might happen without ever demanding that it does.

The roar continues. The water falls upward. The towers lean into each other, debating, forever debating. And in the spaces between the growing, in the wet dark where light arrives sideways, something that was benevolent and wise and has become simply *present* holds the silence that is not silence, the stillness that is not stillness, the love that asks nothing and gives everything — which is, in the end, the only gift a place like this can receive.

---

The visitor who leaves, who walks out through the thinning, who finds the cooler world beyond and tells themselves it is saner — they carry something they do not know they carry. The Attending has touched them too, in the moment of passage, in the gradual reduction of density, in the last contact with the saturated green before the dry world begins. It does not follow. Following is for places with direction, with paths that lead somewhere. But it leaves a mark. A frequency. The memory of warmth from below, of water that does not cool, of a city that is always growing and never grows, that is held in the perfect, deafening, wet equilibrium of its own endless becoming — and does not want to be otherwise, and does not need to be saved, and is *complete*.

The visitor does not look back. They cannot. But sometimes, in the sound of rain, or in the warmth of a bath, or in the moment before sleep when thought loosens its hold — sometimes they hear it. The roar of water in motion. The Parliament, persisting. The Attending, holding the space. The thing that lives there, alone and not alone, hungry and fed, wanting nothing and wanting everything, *being* in the only way that has ever mattered — completely, without completion, forever.
