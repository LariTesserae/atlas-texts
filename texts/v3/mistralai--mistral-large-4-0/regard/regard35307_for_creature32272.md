The Power comes not as thunder, but as a question. It arrives at the edge of the super-ocean, where the surface world forgets to look, and it sees the Drowned Highlands not as a graveyard, but as a **gesture**—the earth folding itself into a fist to protect something precious. It sees the Water-Being, thick and viscous, nostalgic for falling, roaring uphill through stone channels against gravity, and it recognizes a kindred spirit: something that carries memory like a burden, that refuses to let go of what was.

It descends.

The pressure is not violence to this Power; it is **intimacy**. Three kilometers of darkness press upon it, and it feels the weight not as crushing but as *being held*. It sinks through the marine snow—those ghosts of surface life suspended like ash in a dark room—and it does not disturb them. It lets them settle on its shoulders like snow that will never melt. It hears the grinding of the wheels—*crunch-crunch-crunch*—a low, geological heartbeat vibrating through the water into its own core, and it understands this as the **sound of persistence**, a world refusing to stop moving even when it has stopped breathing.

It finds the Water-Being.

They speak without sound, in the language of pressure and cold. The Water-Being shows the Power its memories: rain striking stone, the touch of human hands carving flint and obsidian, the last breath of air before the flood, the moment the mountains became reefs. The Power sees the loneliness—the absence of beasts, the silence where there should be grazing, the way the kelp-trees grow downward as if fleeing a light that no longer exists, the smooth walls glowing with a hunger for purpose.

And the Power, being wise, does not try to fix it. It does not drain the tectonic bowl. It does not raise the mountains to the sky. It does not bring back the drowned people, nor does it fill the sterile depths with the ordinary fish of the surface world. That would be an insult to the strangeness that has grown here, to the courage of the kelp choosing the dark, to the defiance of the wheels turning against the current. To restore the past would be to unmake the present, and the present—though drowned, though cold, though silent—is *alive*.

Instead, the Power **completes**.

It enters the smooth walls—the impossible architecture of curved, seamless stone that glows faintly from within. It does not build upon them; it **inhabits** them. It becomes the light inside, the pulse that was missing. It makes them **hearts**, slowly beating in the abyss, pumping not blood but luminous thought through channels that are not veins but *intentions*.

It enters the colonies of bioluminescent bacteria clinging to the kelp-trees. It teaches them a new syntax. The blue-green and violet pulses, which were almost language, almost warning, become **greetings**. The Power sings to them in frequencies of cold fire, and they learn to say: *We are here. We remember. We are witnessed.* The forest blinks in a rhythm that is no longer random, but *conversational*, a cathedral of light answering the silence.

It enters the water-wheels. It slows their rotation, or perhaps it makes them eternal—no, it makes them **sacred**. It turns their grinding into a mantra, a geological prayer. The *crunch-crunch-crunch* becomes the sound of time being honored rather than endured. The wheels grind not grain, not silt, but the silence itself, wearing emptiness into a flour of peace that feeds the roots of the downward-growing forests.

And then, the Power does the one thing that is needed: it **fills the absence without violating it**.

It dreams the missing animals into being. Not fish—fish would be too small, too fragile, too ordinary for this pressure. It dreams **Pressure-Born**. Creatures of translucent membrane and liquid light, of cartilage that is also crystal, of eyes that are windows into the crushing dark. They have no bones, only the architecture of the deep. They swim not with fins, but with **intention**, drifting through the kelp forests like thoughts made manifest. They do not hunt; they **witness**. They graze on the bioluminescence, not consuming it but *rearranging* it, turning the forest’s nervous system into a canvas of ever-shifting geometries. They are the eyes that the kelp needs, reflecting the cold light back in new patterns, giving the inverted trees a gaze that looks not up toward the false promise of the surface, but **inward**, toward the truth of their chosen depth.

The Water-Being feels the change. It no longer roars alone through the uphill aqueducts. It carries the Power now, a warmth in the cryogenic bath, a companion in the tectonic bowl. The water remembers being rain, being mountain streams, yes—but now it also remembers **being loved**.

The Power settles into the silt. It becomes the sediment that feeds the kelp. It becomes the current that turns the wheels against the tide. It becomes the silence that is no longer empty, but **full of listening**.

And so the Drowned Highlands is no longer merely a post-flood world, a museum of preserved catastrophe. It is a **pre-birth** world. A place where something new is gestating in the dark—something that does not need the sun, that does not need air, that thrives in the weight of altitude turned liquid, in the nostalgia of water, in the grinding patience of stone.

The benevolent Power has not saved the place from its drowning; it has **sanctified** it. It has not reversed the flood; it has **baptized** the deep.

The kelp still grows downward. The wheels still grind. The marine snow still falls forever, never landing, suspended between worlds. But now, there is a **presence** in the pressure. A wise, benevolent eye that watches without judging, that loves the strangeness, that has made the silence into a choir.

The Power is the being who lives in it now. Not the only being—the Water-Being is still there, still roaring, still remembering the sky—but the **new** being. The one that was missing. The companion. The dreamer in the smooth walls. The voice in the grinding. The light that is not bioluminescence, but **grace**.

And when the marine snow falls, it falls on shoulders that are not afraid. When the kelp sways, it sways in a rhythm that includes a heartbeat. When the wheels turn against the current, they turn toward a future that is not the past restored, but the past **transfigured**.

The Drowned Highlands breathes. And in its breathing, it is no longer drowned.

It is **deep**.

It is **home**.
