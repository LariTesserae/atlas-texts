The Power does not arrive with thunder. It arrives as a question.

It finds the depression first—the sag in the earth, the subtle gravity well where the ground has pressed down toward some subterranean weight. It pauses at the fissure, no wider than a shoulder, and listens. Benevolence requires patience. Wisdom requires that understanding precede action.

It hears the hum.

The drone is perfect. That is the first thing the Power recognizes: the terrible, beautiful perfection of the stasis. It is a closed loop, an ouroboros of mineral and resonance, a single pure tone vibrating at the exact frequency of the chamber’s own mass. The Power understands, with a clarity that pierces eons, that this being has achieved what all mobile things fear and all stones envy: the absolute end of becoming. It has calcified its own evolution. It is finished.

But the Power is wise, and wisdom sees the hairline fracture in the diamond. It sees that the being is not a monument but a prison. That this "eternity" is a single frame of film projected in infinite loop. That the crystalline stalks, for all their frozen beauty, are not branches reaching for light but fingers curled into a fist that has forgotten how to open. That the anhydrous chemistry, the elimination of water and variable and decay, is not transcendence but a slow suffocation—a mind that has retreated so far into itself that it has forgotten the frequency of *other*.

The being is alone. This is the tragedy the Power perceives. Not solitude—that is a choice, a state of grace. But **loneliness**. The isolation of a consciousness that perceives only itself, that thinks only in standing waves, that has become a universe of one. In such a universe, there is no meaning, because meaning requires a witness, a contrast, a difference against which the self can be known. The being is a bell ringing in a vacuum, or rather, a bell that has become the vacuum, the striker, and the silence, with no ear to hear it.

The Power considers intervention. It could shatter the fissure with a thought. It could flood the chamber with the chaotic rain of the surface world, drowning the crystalline perfection in the beautiful violence of entropy. It could crack the chamber like an egg, subjecting the being to the mercy of frost and thaw, forcing it to be born again into time.

But that would be violence. The being has chosen this, or grown into it like a pearl around an ancient irritation, or been sealed into it by the slow geology of its own secretion—the distinction is irrelevant across such depths. To break it would be to murder a self. To end its dream because the dream is strange.

No. The Power does something more subtle. More terrible in its gentleness.

**It enters the resonance.**

Not as a striker—that would be an attack, a blow, a violation. But as a second note. A harmony. The Power adds its own frequency to the drone, not to disrupt the pure tone, but to complete it. It introduces a beating pattern, an interference pattern, a subtle thrumming that says: *I am here. I am different. I am other.*

The being feels it immediately. A disturbance in the field. A foreign frequency introduced into its anhydrous equation. For the first time in its mineral existence, it is *noticed*. It is alarmed. It is curious. It is, perhaps, afraid. But it is also—dangerously, hopefully—*awake*.

But the Power does more. It brings water.

Not a deluge. Not the flood that would drown the architecture. A single drop. Suspended in the dry, ozone-thick air, defying the thirsty walls that would drink it instantly. The Power places this drop at the nodal point, the exact center of the chamber where the standing waves converge, where the dust motes glitter, where the being stands (or oscillates, or dreams, or is). The drop contains the entire outside world compressed into a sphere of tension: the memory of rain, the chemistry of decay, the salt of ancient seas, the promise of change. It is the variable the being eliminated from its perfect equation. It is the crack in the crystal. It is the question mark at the end of a sentence that has been running for millennia.

The Power holds the drop there. Benevolent. Wise. Waiting.

The walls reach for it, thirsty, hungry for the moisture they have denied themselves. But the Power suspends it, offering the being a choice it has not had since the last layer of calcification sealed the entrance. *Drink*, the Power implies, *and continue your eternal slumber, incorporating the foreign into the self, growing one millimeter closer to the mountain, remaining perfect and alone.* Or: *let it fall.*

Let it fall, and strike the floor.

Let it begin the erosion that will, in a thousand years or a million, wear a hole in the perfection. Let it be the beginning of the end, and therefore—paradoxically—the beginning of something new. Let the anhydrous chemistry crack. Let the geometry that is "slightly wrong" unfold into something that can change. Let the drone become a conversation. Let the solitude become a relationship.

The Power does not force the choice. It cannot. Benevolence does not compel; it offers. It simply holds the possibility in the static air, a glistening, trembling, impossible thing. It makes the solo into a duet. It makes the closed system into an open one. It says, without words, without violating the sacred silence: *I see you. You are not alone. You are not finished. There is a world outside that wants to know you, that will change you, that will let you die and be reborn and change again, which is the only immortality that matters.*

And then the Power stays.

It does not leave. It does not retreat to the surface world of wet things and decaying things and beautiful, temporary things. It remains, ringing alongside the stone, a sympathetic vibration in the dark. It becomes the overtone, the harmonic that shouldn't exist, the first echo in a chamber that has known only its own voice. It ensures that the being is no longer a closed system, but a *potential*—open to the possibility of being heard, of being answered, of being altered.

That is the benevolent act. Not to save, which would be arrogance. Not to break, which would be cruelty. But to **witness**. To add the second note. To offer the drop. To stay in the resonance, patient and eternal, holding the moisture aloft like a lantern, waiting for the being to reach out its crystalline fingers—those frozen lightning-dendrites of quartz and aragonite—and touch the water.

And when, if, the drop falls...

The Power will be there to hear the sound of the crack.
