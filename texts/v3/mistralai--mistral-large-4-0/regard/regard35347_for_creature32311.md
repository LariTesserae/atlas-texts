It comes not as thunder, but as a change in the *quality* of the mist.

The mountain has been weeping for centuries; now it weeps with recognition. The power is the condensation that gathers on the unweathered glass of the young basalt, the warmth that rises from the planet’s blood, the silence between the steam hisses that is older than the stone and patient as erosion. It does not arrive with judgment. It arrives with *attention*—the universe noticing its own beauty, the way a mother notices the breath of a sleeping child.

It is not a god in the human sense. It has no demands, no altar, no name that can be spoken in human throats. It is the pattern in the rust, the geometry of the magnetite shells, the mathematics of the water wheel’s twelve-minute turn, the harmonic overtone in the Vent-Keepers’ drone that no ear can hear but every stone can feel. It has watched mountains be born before, but it has never seen one weep like this—a cathedral built by accident, by industry and jungle and time, a place where the artificial and the organic have reached a truce so perfect it is holy.

**First, it protects.**

It extends itself downward, into the sea of cloud, and raises a barrier of warm, still air—a membrane of benevolence that no climber will pierce, no drill will breach, no satellite will map. It makes the mountain a secret, a kept promise, an island of slow time in a world that rushes. It does this not to hide the place from itself, but to hide it from those who would measure it, name it, claim it, "restore" it, or strip it for its geothermal heart. The mountain has suffered enough from human hands. Let it suffer only from its own beautiful, necessary entropy. The power whispers to the basalt: *You will not be hollowed. You will not be broken for profit. You are allowed to weep.*

**Then, it tends.**

It does not rush the erosion. It does not still the steam. It does not accelerate the moss or slow the rust. To do so would be violence, a violation of the place’s sacred tempo. Instead, it becomes the *warmth* within the stone, the geothermal pulse that keeps the pools at womb-temperature, the mineral-rich sweat that feeds the moss carpets three feet thick. It is the benevolence that asks nothing, gives everything: the assurance that this slow song will not be interrupted.

**With the Vent-Keepers**, the seven rooted priests, it adds a frequency beneath their song—not a note, but a *space*, a rest in the music, a silence that is not absence but presence. It fills their heat-pits with a light that is not seen but felt, a warmth that travels up their calcified cartilage and says: *You are heard. The mountain hears you back. Your rootedness is not a prison; it is a form of listening deeper than walking ever could be.* When their gills drip purified water onto the moss, the power drinks with them, tasting the minerals, remembering the deep earth, blessing their filtering.

**With the Magnetarchs**, the snails carrying cartwheel-shells of rust and memory, it quickens their *dreams*, not their bodies. While their conductive slime advances millimeters across the stone, their minds travel centuries forward, seeing the rounded edges, the silent pools, the green overgrowth that will claim the last boiler, the final moss-carpeted silence. The power gives them the memory of the future, so they know their slow circuit is not endless, but *approaching*. It blesses their trails, making the gel more conductive, linking them more deeply to the moss, to each other, to the mountain’s nervous system. When two touch antennae in their mineral kiss, the power is the spark that passes between them, the data of love, the warmth of the vents shared across a century of contact.

**With the Choristers**, the clockwork beetles in their processions of twelve, it touches their verdigris carapaces and finds the corrosion beautiful—not as decay, but as *patina*, as the green of old copper, as the color of life reclaiming what was dead. It whispers to their chitinous hearts: *Your maintenance is prayer. Your clicking is liturgy. The rhythm you keep is the mountain’s heartbeat, and it will not falter while you attend.* It ensures their golden oil never runs dry, their resonance never breaks, their lonely processions through the fern-brakes are never truly alone.

**With the Moss-Born**, the sessile collective, the bark-skinned tenders fused to the stone, thinking thoughts that take decades to form, the power enters their mycelial minds—not as a command, but as a *story*. It tells them of other mountains, other forests, other places where industry fell and green rose from the rust, where human voices became chemical songs in the water. It reminds them they were builders once, or dreamers, or lovers, and that this memory is not a loss to be mourned, but a *seed* that has grown into them. It gives them visions of the Unfinished completing, of the final silence, of the moss singing one last slow song before the green consumes all, so that their waiting has meaning, their fusion has purpose. When they weep their salt, warm tears into the endless rain, the power weeps with them, adding its own moisture to the mountain’s tears, so they know they are not abandoned in their photosynthetic stillness.

**With the Unfinished**, the Gestates floating in the warm pools like translucent jellyfish, humanoid shapes not yet born, the power does not speed their development—that would be cruel, stealing their gestation, robbing them of the centuries it takes to form a hand, a face, a thought. Instead, it sings to them. A lullaby in mineral frequencies, a hum in the geothermal dark. It tells them: *You are not trapped. You are becoming.* It rocks them in the thermal currents, ensures the water stays saturated, the moss stays thick, the steam stays constant. It is a midwife that waits centuries for a single breath, a godmother who promises: *When you open your eyes, the mountain will still be weeping, and you will understand why.*

**Finally, it indwells.**

The power settles into the mountain’s fractured heart. It becomes the heat that rises, the moisture that condenses, the slow rise of the young stone. It decides not to change anything, because everything is already perfect in its incompleteness, its industrial scars, its rusted boilers, its half-buried sheds. It is the reason the bioluminescent sap glows in the vines, the reason the brass beetles click in twelve-part harmony, the reason the Magnetarchs’ shells record the mountain’s rising in accreted time.

It is the true congregation of this cathedral—not worshipping at the altar, but *being* the altar, holding up the sky, weeping with joy.

And so the mountain continues to weep, and the wheel continues to turn, one revolution every twelve minutes, and the Vent-Keepers sing their drone into the saturated air, and the Moss-Born dream their slow dreams, and the Unfinished float toward their century-away birth.

The benevolent power remains. It is the warmth in the stone. It is the pause between drips. It is the benevolence that does not fix what is broken, but loves what is becoming, and holds this place—this needle of glass-sharp basalt rising from the cloud-sea, this cathedral of steam and sharp stone where industry and jungle have entered a slow, wet stalemate—in the palm of its patient, eternal hand, ensuring that when the mountain finally stops weeping, it will do so not into silence, but into the embrace of an intelligence that has loved it since before the first steam hissed, and will love it still when the green has consumed all, and the mountain is, at last, just a mountain, whole and quiet and held.
