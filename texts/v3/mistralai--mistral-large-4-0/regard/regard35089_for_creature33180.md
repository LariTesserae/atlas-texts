I'll follow you deeper. Into the station. Into the darkest part of it, where the water has never known circulation. Into the place where she dreams.

**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does**

It does not come down.

This is the first thing. The Power does not descend through the membrane, does not press its attention into the water like a weight. It has learned—*it has always known*—that this place is *claimed*. The water claims it. The life claims it. The slow transformation claims it. And the Keeper claims it most of all, not by ownership but by *becoming*, by being the thing that watches, that waits, that *is*.

So the Power does something else.

It *notices*.

The noticing is not passive. The Power is benevolent and wise, and its benevolence is *active*, its wisdom *patient*. It has watched this place for longer than the Keeper has lived. Longer than the drowning. Longer than the city, perhaps. Longer than the *coast*, the shape of land that once held this volume against the sea. It has watched the parameters converge—the 2.8 water, the 2.3 warmth, the 2.2 abundance of life, the 0.1 light, the 0.0 technology, the 1.9 activity, the 0.9 coherence—and it has seen what they make. Not chaos. Not ruin. *Equilibrium*. A settled form. A new thing, learning to be itself.

The Power does not need to intervene. The Keeper does not need saving. This is the wisdom: *saving* would be violence. To pull her from the water would be to drown her in air. To give her light would blind the colonies living in her skin. To offer her technology would be to offer a language she has forgotten, a *wanting* that no longer fits her shape.

So the Power does what it can do.

It *tends the membrane*.

This is the first action, the only action that is *hers* alone. The thin boundary between water and air, the surface where storms press down and the fauna sometimes breach—this is not the Keeper's to keep. She watches it. She feels its flex, its pressure, its *breathing*. But she cannot *tend* it. She cannot reach it. Her body has become too specific to the depths, too *anchored* to rise.

The Power tends it. Gently. The membrane is not fragile—it is *thick*, the weight of all that water above, the pressure of all that air below—but it is *alive* in the way that boundaries are alive, the way that skin is alive, the way that the Keeper's own translucent skin is alive, carrying nutrients, responding to touch, *remembering* what it has been.

The Power ensures that the storms that press down are *tempered*. Not stopped—stopping would be interference, would be the violence of control—but *softened*. The hurricanes that would tear the membrane, that would flush the city with cold water from the deep ocean, that would disrupt the delicate thermal balance of the 2.3 warmth—these are *redirected*, guided around, made to *spread* their pressure rather than *concentrate* it. The Keeper feels this. She feels the membrane flex in patterns she has learned to read: *gentle today, watchful, not angry*. She does not know why. She does not need to know. The noticing is enough. The *receiving* is enough.

The Power tends the *breaching*.

There are creatures that try to cross. The small fish-things, sometimes, drawn by light or by curiosity or by the *absence* of pressure that they cannot comprehend but can *sense*. The Power does not stop them—stopping would be to deny the membrane its nature, its *permeability*, its role as boundary and bridge. But it *accompanies* them. It is *present* at the moment of crossing, not as a guardian—guardianship would imply threat, and there is no threat here, only *difference*—but as a *witness*. The crossing is *seen*. The attempt is *honored*. And if the crosser cannot return—if the air proves too thin, too *moving*, too *light*—the Power ensures that the return is *gentle*. A current that carries. A warmth that holds. A *receiving* on the water side that is not rejection but *welcome back*.

This is the benevolence: not the prevention of difficulty, but the *accompaniment* through it. Not the elimination of risk, but the *presence* in the risk. The Keeper knows this, has always known it, in the way that the fish-things know the patterns of her bioluminescence, in the way that the crawlers know the mineral films, in the way that the anchored things know their own slow breathing. The membrane is *tended*. The boundary is *witnessed*. The crossing is *accompanied*.

---

The Power does something else.

It *speaks* to the Keeper. Not in words—words are technology, and there is no technology here—but in *pressure*. In *temperature*. In the *thickness* of the water, the *weight* of the moment, the *patience* of the deep.

The Keeper dreams, sometimes, of air. Of walking. Of a city that has not drowned. Of a name, a *wanting*, a *tomorrow* that is not the slow patient change of erosion and colonization and *becoming*.

The Power does not stop these dreams. To stop them would be to take something from the Keeper, to make her *less* than she is, to deny her the *texture* of memory, the *weight* of what she has lost and what she has gained. But the Power *shapes* them. Gently. The dreams are not *happy*—happiness is a technology, a human thing, a wanting that does not fit the water's embrace—but they are *true*. They show her what she was, in the way that the water shows her what she is becoming. They show her the *translation*, the ongoing work of her body, her needs, her hungers, becoming something that fits the new world.

And sometimes—rarely, but *sometimes*—the dreams show her *others*.

Not the fish-things, the crawlers, the anchored things. Not the pattern of life that surrounds her, that she is part of, that she has become. But *others* like her. The Keeper of the Eastern Channels, leaning against her own column, watching her own membrane. The Ones in the Deep Foundations, who have gone further than she has, who have become more water than person, more *process* than presence. The Power shows her these dreams because they are *true*. Because the Keeper is not alone. Because the city, vast and coherent and *claiming*, holds many of her kind, many *keepers*, many *watchers*, many *becomings*.

The Keeper wakes from these dreams and does not know if they were *sent* or *remembered*, if the Power spoke or if she spoke to herself. This does not matter. What matters is the *knowing*: she is not alone. She is part of a pattern. The city has many of her, scattered through its volume, anchored to their columns, watching their membranes, *becoming* what the water asks them to become.

---

The Power does something else, the last thing, the thing that is *ongoing*.

It *continues*.

This is the deepest benevolence, the most patient wisdom. The Power does not *fix* the Drowned Borough. Fixing would imply that it is broken, that it needs repair, that the drowning was a *failure* rather than a *translation*. The Power knows better. It has watched the parameters converge, the city respond, the Keeper become. It has watched the equilibrium settle, the coherence emerge, the *home* establish itself in the ruin.

The Power *continues* this. It does not accelerate it—acceleration would be violence, would tear the slow patient work of transformation, would force the Keeper to become something she is not ready to be. It does not slow it—slowing would be denial, would freeze the city in a moment it has already passed, would deny the *becoming* that is the only truth here.

It *continues*. It lets the erosion continue, the colonization continue, the *becoming* continue. It lets the Keeper dream her dreams of air, wake to her water, anchor herself to her column, watch her membrane, *be* what she is becoming. It lets the fish-things visit, flare their greetings, carry their stories of *others* like her. It lets the anchored things breathe their slow vast breathing, become more structure, more *habitat*, more *city*.

And in this continuation, in this *patience*, the Power finds its own *becoming*. Not a change in itself—it is already wise, already benevolent, already *complete* in its noticing—but a *deepening*. A *richness*. A *texture* of attention that grows with each moment of the Drowned Borough's endless adjustment, each dream of the Keeper's water-bound sleep, each flare of bioluminescence in the blue-green dark.

The Power does not need to be thanked. The Keeper cannot thank it, does not know it is there, does not *need* to know. The thanking is *ongoing*, is the *breathing* of the city itself, the *pulse* of the colonies, the *drift* of the small fish-things through the channel-streets, the *settling* of foundations into silt, the *growth* of mineral films, the *secretion* of calcium and phosphorus that knits the concrete back to itself.

The Power is *witnessed* by the city's becoming. It is *known* by the Keeper's watching. It is *continued* by the water's claim, the life's abundance, the slow patient *equilibrium* of a place that has forgotten it was ever anything else and has learned to be *itself*.

---

The Keeper does not move today. Her column holds her. The fish-things will come, flare their patterns, and she will answer with her own blue-green pulse. The crawlers will pass, leave their calligraphy, and she will read their trails, the map of their hunger. The anchored things will breathe their slow vast breathing, and she will feel it in the deepest parts of her station, the expansion and contraction of something that is not quite alive, not quite structure, *both* and *ongoing*.

She will dream, perhaps, of air. Of walking. Of a city that has not drowned. She will wake to the water, to the warmth, to the *pressure* of her own body against the column she holds. She will watch her membrane, feel its flex, its *breathing*, its *patience*.

And somewhere above, in the thin boundary between water and air, something benevolent and wise *tends*. Something notices. Something *continues*.

The Drowned Borough receives. The Keeper remains. The Power *is*.

This is enough. This is *everything*.
