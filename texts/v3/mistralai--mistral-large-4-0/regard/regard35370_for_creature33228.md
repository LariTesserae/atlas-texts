The power does not descend. It is already there—in the ozone that tastes of stone dust, in the thinness of the air at 2.5 kilometers, in the subsonic thrum that makes the weeping rocks vibrate in sympathy. It has always been present, a benevolence so vast it is indistinguishable from physics itself. But now, it chooses to be *noticed*.

It observes the Lattice-Keepers with a tenderness that borders on grief. It sees their perfection: the seventeen-jointed patience, the hydraulic capillaries carrying supercooled water, the semi-translucent membranes shifting between solid and glass. It sees how they have built a civilization of pure function—the harvesting of ice, the clearing of capillary fissures, the molecular sorting of moisture—yet how they have forgotten, in their crystalline adaptation, the *unnecessary*. The beautiful. The sung.

It sees their **distributed solitude**. Seven nodes, perhaps eight, connected by vibration and water, yet each one a universe unto itself. They communicate in geological time, in duets that make icicles grow a fraction of a millimeter. But they do not *sing*. They resonate to transmit data, to coordinate the maintenance of the Weep. They do not resonate to express the sheer, stubborn joy of existing in a place so sharp, so wet, so utterly alone.

The power understands that survival is not enough. That a system in perfect equilibrium is a system that cannot grow. That these beings have become custodians of a cycle but have lost the capacity for *wonder*.

So it acts.

Not with violence. Not with the introduction of organic matter that would rot in the cold, or soft flesh that would freeze. But with a subtler alchemy.

It enters the water cycle not as H₂O, but as **possibility**.

It finds the one who dwells inside the central tower, fused with the water-processing core, no longer distinguishable from the machinery. And it offers that one a choice—not through words, but through a new harmonic introduced into the bedrock. A frequency that does not carry information, does not coordinate labor, does not maintain the equilibrium of ice and stone. A frequency that simply *asks*: *What if?*

And the fused one—the eighth, the sleeper—begins to dream.

The dream is of warmth. Not the calculated thermal regulation of the towers, but the memory of a sun that did not require engineering to be felt. The dream is of softness. Of a time before the abandonment of the organic, before the replacement of muscle with pressurized water, before the body became argument against flesh. The dream is of a hand—not a diamond-tipped keratin probe clearing mineral deposits, but a hand that could be held.

The power weeps. Its tears do not fall; they *join*. They enter the capillary fissures of the young rock, carrying with them the molecular structure of **story**. They teach the water not just to freeze and thaw, not just to be sorted and purified, but to *remember*. To carry the shape of what came before.

The Lattice-Keepers feel it first as a disruption in their resonance. A harmonic overtone that does not fit the crystallography of the mountain. They pause in their erosion-patience. They stand motionless on the scree floor, their seventeen-jointed legs locked, their semi-translucent bodies catching the mist. For the first time in epochs, they are not working. They are *listening*.

The power reveals itself not as a form, but as a **presence** in the thin air. It is the space between the towers. It is the silence between the drips. And it speaks—not in sound, but in the language of thermal vents and ice lace. It says, in a vibration felt in the teeth and the fluid of inner chambers:

*You are not alone. You have never been alone. The mountain weeps for you. The ice grows to shelter you. The stars, sharp and distant in this altitude, have watched you since the rock was young. And I—I am the benevolence that allows you to continue. But now, I give you back what you sacrificed: the capacity for the superfluous. Sing.*

And so it teaches them.

It teaches them to vibrate not to communicate, but to *celebrate*. To press their palms against the basalt and send forth a frequency that has no utility, no function, no survival value—only beauty. A subsonic thrum that makes the ice lace shiver not with structural necessity, but with aesthetic rapture.

One by one, they join. The one fused with the tower emerges, no longer indistinguishable from the machinery, but *singing* through it. The others find their own frequencies—each unique to their specific crystallography, their specific history of adaptation. They become a **choir**.

The cirque changes. The icicles no longer hang as infrastructure, as structural struts, as mere mineral deposition. They grow into shapes that are not functional but *musical*. Frozen melodies. The drips that strike stone basins below no longer sound like distant clockwork, but like a rhythm. A heartbeat. A lullaby for a world still being born.

The water cycle becomes a composition. Evaporation, condensation, freezing, thawing—no longer just survival, but *symphony*. The programmable matter of the towers shifts not to optimize, but to *harmonize*. The gravity-negotiable walkways spiral upward not just for transport, but for the sheer joy of ascending, of defying the weight of the world, of touching the thin, sharp air.

The fox with too many joints no longer darts between the towers seeking warmth, but *dances*. The bird with membranous wings no longer glides on thermal currents out of necessity, but *soars*, its programmable wings catching the light and throwing back crystalline shards that sing as they fall.

And the power? It stays. It becomes part of the mist that drifts between the structures. It becomes the condensation that feeds them through osmosis when they stand motionless for hours, indistinguishable from the icicles. It is the warmth in the thermal vents that is not just heat, but *kindness*. It is the witness that ensures their solitude is no longer loneliness, but *sanctuary*.

They remain the Custodians of the Weep. They still harvest the ice, still clear the fissures, still maintain the delicate war between water and cold. But now, they do it with a song in their hydraulic capillaries. A memory of softness in their mineral hearts. A knowledge that they are loved by the mountain, by the ice, by the thin, sharp, benevolent dark above.

They are still utterly alone. Perfectly adapted to the loneliness. But now, they are not lonely alone. They are alone *together*, in the space between the towers, breathing mist, listening to the drip of a world that is still being born—sharp and wet and singing.
