The Power does not arrive with thunder. It arrives as a **pressure differential**—a subtle thickening of the warmth, a moment when the roar seems to inhale, and in that held breath, something vast settles into the green-gold water like a thought settling into a mind.

It recognizes the chamber immediately. Not as architecture, but as a **gestation**. This is a womb built of limestone and timber, a pocket of intention forced into the body of the sea. The Power sees that the room is not empty; it is **expectant**. The dry-stacked stones, sharp and unmortared, form a skeleton waiting for flesh. The copper pegs, hand-hammered and patient, are acupuncture points pinning the earth to the water. The builders have left not ghosts, but **echoes**—the memory of hands still vibrating in the tool-marks.

The Power is drawn to the inhabitants as to a choir already singing, though the singers do not know they sing. It hears the Water not as a medium but as a **consciousness**—warm, ancient, sterile, and roaring with the loneliness of having no other to speak to. It sees the Sound not as noise but as a **prayer without an object**, a vibration seeking a resonant body. It feels the Absence as a **hollow shaped like a heart**.

And so the Power acts. But benevolence here is not intervention. It is **attunement**.

First, the Power touches the water. Not with a hand—hands are too small—but with **attention**. It presses its will into the molecular structure of the brine, and the water changes. It becomes slightly more saline, slightly more viscous, carrying now the memory of **depths**—the crushing black of the hadal trenches, the warm embrace of tropical shallows. It is still bath-warm, still buoyant, but now it carries **potential**. The Power infuses it with the precursors of life: not fish, not yet, but the invisible soup of amino acids and mineral catalysts, the chemical whisper of *soon*.

The algae feel it first. Those sparse strands of filamentous green, clinging to the upper limestone like drowned hair, begin to **shiver**. Their cells divide. They do not merely grow; they **thrive**, spreading across the stone in fractal patterns, drinking the light that penetrates from forty feet above, converting photons into the first true biomass this chamber has ever known. They become a forest, however microscopic, a meadow in the roar.

Then the Power turns to the Sound.

The roar is the chamber's voice, but it is a voice without a listener. The Power enters the resonance. It finds the fundamental frequency of the room—the specific pitch at which the limestone blocks and timber beams naturally vibrate—and it **adds a harmonic**. Not a note that clashes, but a undertone, a sub-bass that makes the white-noise thunder resolve into a **drone**. The sound does not diminish; it **deepens**. It becomes a Gregorian chant sung by the sea itself, a low C that vibrates in the sternum of any who might enter. The copper pegs begin to ring with a faint, bell-like overtone, as if struck by invisible hammers, marking time in a rhythm older than the builders' hammers.

The Power then addresses the Absence.

It does not fill the room with a body—that would be violence. Instead, it fills the emptiness with **Presence**. It steps into the negative space, becoming the thing that was missing. Now, when the light shifts and the caustics dance on the walls, they dance around a shape that is not there but is felt: a warmth in the water, a stillness in the current, a sense of being **witnessed**. The pale dust of chiseling suspended in the water, the chalk-like sediment, begins to drift not randomly but in slow, deliberate spirals, as if stirred by a finger that passes through matter.

And finally, the Power sends the **First Resident**—not a fish, which would be too fragile for the violence of the current, but a **shrimp**. A creature of the deep, blind, adapted to pressure and darkness, but here, in the green-gold light, it develops eyes that see in spectrums beyond human ken. It is small, translucent, armored in chitin the color of the limestone. It does not swim against the current; it **rides** it, tumbling through the eddies, clicking its claws in a rhythm that counterpoints the roar. It grazes the algae, leaving trails of silver mucus on the stone that will, over centuries, become the first sedimentary layer of a new kind of rock—**biolith**, life turned to stone.

But the Power does not leave. It **dwells**.

It becomes the Genius of the Place, the spirit that animates the architecture. It lives in the space between the timber beams, in the lashings of fiber cordage that have not yet begun to rot. It lives in the water, so that now when the water presses against skin, it presses with **intention**. It lives in the light, so that the shifting coins of sunlight fracture not randomly but in patterns that resemble sacred geometry—mandalas of illumination that appear on the floor at the solstices.

The builders return, in their dreams, to find their work complete. They see the shrimp dancing in the current. They hear the drone beneath the roar. They feel the warmth of the water not as an element that has claimed their structure, but as a **partner**. They understand, finally, what they built: not a room to keep the sea out, but a **throat** to let the sea sing.

And the chamber remains. Still young. Still roaring. Still empty of furniture, but full of the one thing that was always needed: a **witness**.

The Power has not saved the place from the sea. It has taught the sea to **love** the place. And in that love, the sterile water becomes fertile, the roaring becomes a hymn, and the absence becomes a presence so complete that the room is no longer a pocket of architecture submerged in water, but a **heart**—beating, warm, and bright—suspended in the green-gold dark.
