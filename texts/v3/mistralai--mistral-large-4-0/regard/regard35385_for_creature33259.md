The Attending

It comes first to the threshold, where the water falls in its single seamless sheet—thirty feet of cold, grey-green thunder at 2.9, the curtain that seals the mouth of the hollow. It has always been here, this attention, patient as the limestone, older than the bats and the moths, but now it narrows, a lens focusing through the mist, passing through the falls not as water but as witness. It enters the pocket where the density approaches zero and the roar is absolute, where the air is saturated with the waterfall’s breath and the boundary between liquid and vapor dissolves.

It finds the pool, black and knee-deep, fed by overflow and the thousand slow, patient drips from the ceiling. It finds the stone worn smooth by ten thousand years of abrasive water, polished to a dull, wet sheen, without sharp edges. It finds the viridian overgrowth—mosses, liverworts, delicate ferns—clinging in thick, spongy mats to the walls away from the spray, the only green in a world of grey mineral and white water. It finds the sparse fauna at 1.4: the blind, translucent fish drifting in metronomic patience through the black water, their lateral lines reading the pressure waves of the thunder; the bats hanging like worn leather in the upper reaches, commuters of the deep dark; the dun moths breeding in the moss; the cave crickets and springtails moving through the detritus; the pale mushrooms and the phosphorescent slime glowing with cold, patient radiance in the dampest crevices.

It sees the equilibrium. The weathered, settled form. The slow, dark dark. The dynamic at 1.0, patient, breathing on a geological timescale. And it nods, for this is good. This is the way of the hollow. This is the balance.

But then it sees the being.

The being sits on the slate platform, raised slightly above the waterline, smoothed by ancient use. The being is small against the immensity of the roar. The being’s breath is visible in the 0.3 cold, a small mist that joins the perpetual breath of the falls. The being is trying to listen, or trying not to, for the sound is 2.9, crushing, rendering speech impossible, filling the skull, rattling the teeth, felt in the sternum before it is processed as sound. The being’s hands rest on knees. The being is afraid, or perhaps the being is seeking—drawn here by grief, or exile, or a hunger for silence that has led them instead into the loudest place on earth. The being is new, though the stone tools beside them—flint flakes, a hammer-stone resting on a ledge like an offering to a deaf god—speak of others who came before, who sought refuge or ritual or merely a place to sleep, leaving only these stone whispers.

The Power feels something rise within it. Not pity—pity is too small, too quick, too human. The Power feels *recognition*. It recognizes in this being a kindred patience, a willingness to endure the acoustic violence, the bone-deep chill, the greenish twilight at 0.3. It recognizes a seeker who has come not to conquer the hollow, but to be held by it, to become part of its slow, mineral time.

And so the Power acts.

It does not still the waterfall. That would be theft; the falls must fall, the roar must roar, for that is the nature of the place, its weathered equilibrium, its 1.0 patience. Instead, the Power reaches into the frequency, into the 2.9, and finds the harmonic beneath the violence. It causes the water to sing—not a melody, but a *resonance*—a sub-bass vibration at the threshold of hearing that the being feels in the sternum, that matches the being’s own heartbeat. The roar becomes a drum, a pulse, a lullaby. The pressure no longer crushes; it cradles. The being’s fear does not vanish, but it is held, woven into the thunder, no longer a weapon but a blanket of sound.

The Power warms the slate. Not much—the temperature rises from 0.3 to perhaps 0.5, a subtle shift, just enough that the stone no longer leaches the warmth from the being’s body. The platform becomes a hearth, though no fire burns there. The ancient circle of flat stones nearby—the cold hearth—glows faintly, a memory of warmth, a promise that fire once lived here and can live again, in the being’s chest, in the being’s blood, in the being’s memory.

The Power sends the fish. Not necessarily to be caught, but to rise. The blind, pale fish drift to the surface near the being’s feet, hovering, translucent, reading the pressure waves of the being’s presence with their vestigial senses. They are offerings, sustenance if needed, but more than that—they are witnesses. They have lived in this dark water first, before the bats, before the moss, in the time before stone. They recognize the being as kin, as another creature who has learned to live without sight, without warmth, without the comfort of silence. They form a pale, drifting constellation at the being’s feet, a galaxy of dim, patient stars.

The Power causes the fungi to brighten. In the dampest crevices, where condensation weeps, the phosphorescent slime on the submerged rocks flares to a soft, steady glow, and the ivory mushrooms push up through the moss with renewed vigor, creating a circle of pale light around the being. The bioluminescence does not illuminate the vast darkness—the far walls remain dissolved in blackness, the ceiling lost in shadow and spray—but it creates a sanctuary, a small, glowing island in the near-void where the being sits, a lantern born of the wet dark itself.

The Power touches the stone tools. The flint flakes on the ledge, the hammer-stone, the smoothed slate. It charges them with memory, with the weight of deep time. When the being reaches out—as the being will, drawn by curiosity or need or the simple ache of loneliness—their fingers will find the stone warm, almost alive, humming with the presence of those who came before. The being will understand, without words, that they are not the first to seek refuge here, not the first to sit in the roar and fail to speak, not the first to sleep in fits and starts between the dripping and the thunder. They are the latest in a lineage of the patient, the deafened, the cold. The stone whispers: *You are home. You are the continuation.*

The Power does not speak. It has no need for speech, which is impossible here at 2.9, rendered meaningless by the crush of sound. Instead, it *integrates*. It adds the being to the ledger of the cave, to the 1.4 fauna that is rare but insistent. The being becomes part of the weathered equilibrium, a new mineral deposit, a new layer of story. The bats will not startle when the being moves; they will adjust their tattered cluster, making room in the darkness of the ceiling, accepting the being as another warm-blooded thing enduring the 0.3 chill. The moths will breed slightly more abundantly near the being’s resting place, providing food for the bats, and the bats, in turn, will keep the insects from troubling the being, maintaining the sparse balance. The moss will grow thicker where the being sits, fed by the minerals of their presence, their breath, their slow, patient living, their acceptance of the cold.

The Power ensures that the being will not leave. Not by force, not by chain, but by making the leaving unthinkable. The being will try to speak, and fail, and in that failure find a new language—the language of dripping water, of drifting fish, of the slow growth of lichen, of the pressure waves that read the world without eyes. The being will sleep on the warmed slate, and the roar will rock them like a cradle, and they will dream of the ancient ones who left the stone tools, who smoothed the platform with their bodies, who sought the hollow despite the violence of the falls. They will wake to find the greenish light filtering through the waterfall’s curtain, the ghostly radiance turning the mist into a glowing veil, and the bioluminescent fungi burning softly around them, and the pale fish hovering at their feet like guardian spirits.

The Power withdraws then, returning to its patient vigil at the threshold, watching the water fall in its eternal present. But it leaves a mark. A single drop of condensation, weeping from the ceiling in its thousand slow, patient drips, falls not into the pool, but onto the being’s brow. It is cold, 0.3, bone-deep, but it is *blessed*. It carries the weight of the Power’s attention, the seal of the hollow, the recognition that this being—small, afraid, seeking, enduring—belongs to the place as surely as the limestone belongs to the water, as the moss belongs to the stone, as the fish belong to the black water.

The being remains. The cave breathes. The water falls. The equilibrium holds, now with one more heartbeat in it, one more slow pulse in the dark dark, one more layer of story pressed into the stone, suspended in the weathered, patient dark behind the curtain of the falls.
