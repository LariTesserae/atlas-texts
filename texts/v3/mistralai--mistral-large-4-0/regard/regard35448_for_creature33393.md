The Power descends not as a storm, but as a settling—a pressure in the air that makes the mist slightly heavier, slightly more luminous. It does not land; it *infiltrates*, becoming the space between the terraces, the silence between the drips. It feels the valley’s weight first: the graben holding its breath, the water table pushing up like a held inhalation, the cold structural fact of the stone.

It sees the **we**.

Not a collection, but a single gesture of becoming—the cyanobacteria laying down their mineral breath, the moss upholstering the sharp edges, the snails etching their silver calligraphy, the fish suspended in their glass patience. It sees the architecture that has no architect, the city that is also a body, the civilization of accretion.

And the Power, who has watched a thousand worlds burn and bloom, feels something it has not felt in eons: the urge to *kneel*. But it does not kneel. It considers intervention.

To warm the valley? Cruel. The cold is its discipline, its slowness its wisdom.
To hasten the stone? Violence. The terraces must collapse and rebuild; that is their liturgy.
To speak? The silence is already a language, older than tongues, spoken in the shush of water over travertine.
To teach? What could it teach a place that has already dissolved the boundary between the geological and the biological, between the inhabitant and the inhabited?

So the Power makes its decision.

It will not change the valley. It will **hold it**.

The Power becomes the invisible geology beneath the geology—the deep, patient bedrock that ensures the graben remains a graben, that the fissures stay open, that the upwelling never ceases. It does not add heat; it adds **duration**. It stretches time around the valley like a cloak, slowing the erosion from the surrounding land, ensuring that the sharp edges of the young stone are not too quickly softened by haste, that the wedding-cake terraces are given the millions of years they need to become mountains.

It becomes the **patience** itself.

And then, in a gesture so subtle that even the snails do not notice, the Power does the only thing that is truly benevolent: it **remembers** the valley. It holds the pattern of the terraces in its awareness—the exact spiral of each snail’s shell, the precise angle of each travertine rim, the specific gravity of the mist. When the terraces collapse (and they will collapse, constantly, in the slow earthquakes of growth), the Power remembers their shape. When the springs shift course, the Power remembers the old channels. It is the blueprint that persists through rubble, the memory that outlasts the stone.

In doing so, it offers the valley the only gift that matters: **continuity**. The promise that this slow, cold, wet building will not be interrupted. That the “we” can continue its work of accretion, layer by layer, grain by grain, rising upward toward the light it barely feels, for as long as stone endures.

Finally, the Power does one small thing. It adds its own weight to the silence.

It does not sing. It does not speak. But it becomes a frequency so low that it is felt in the calcium carbonate itself—a vibration that encourages the precipitation to align, not in chaos, but in a subtle spiral, a hidden mathematics. The terraces begin to grow with a faint, internal symmetry, a golden ratio etched into the bone-white stone. The snails, rasping their algae, begin to trace spirals that match the architecture. The moss cushions grow in patterns that echo the water’s braiding. The valley becomes slightly more *itself*—more coherent, more whole, more beautiful in its slowness.

Then the Power withdraws—not leaving, but dissolving. It becomes the mist that hangs low, the cold that keeps the air heavy, the diffuse grey-white light that makes the stone glow from within. It is now part of the valley’s immune system, its nervous system, its dreaming mind. It has joined the **we**.

And the valley continues.

The water rises. The stone grows. The snails glide. The fish hold their station. The heron stands statue-still. And somewhere in the silence, in the space between the drip and the shush, there is now the faintest sense of being held—not by hands, but by time itself, by the benevolent, wise patience of a power that recognized another kind of wisdom, and chose not to disturb it, but to let it become.
