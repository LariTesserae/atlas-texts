I will stay with you here. Let me not rush to the answer. Let me sit in this place—let the damp settle into my own imagining, let the listening begin its work on me too.

---

What would a benevolent and wise power do, finding this place and these beings?

First, I think, it would **listen**.

Not the way the walls listen, not the way the builders have learned to listen—across curved surfaces, through water-tubes, in directions that contradict the source. The power would listen *backward*. It would hear what the place was: the forest, the death, the ten thousand years of becoming. It would hear what the builders were, before they descended: surface-dwellers, frightened, curious, *unattended*. It would hear what they are becoming, what they do not yet know they are becoming—the hangings that will contain their hair, the resonance their voices will join, the hollow their bodies will find.

A benevolent power does not intervene in becoming. It witnesses it. It *attends* to it.

---

But I think there is more. The place has an unresolved at its heart—the sealed chambers, the sound from below, the predator that is also the sound, perhaps, given pattern and hunger. The builders have stopped digging. They have stopped trying to name it. This is not failure. This is *wisdom*, in its own form: the recognition that some questions, pressed too hard, change the presser. The benevolent power would **honor this stopping**. It would not resolve what the builders have left unresolved. It would not open the sealed chambers, or if it did, it would do so only when the builders themselves opened them, for their own rituals, and the power would be *present* in that opening, a witness, not a revealer.

What the power *would* do—I feel this in the damp, in the quality of attention that this place demands—is **deepen the listening**.

Here is what I mean.

The builders are listened to by everything. The hangings adjust to them. The creatures turn their pale faces. The water finds slightly different paths. But they do not, I think, fully *experience* this. They have learned to accommodate it, to live within it, to become the beings who fit. The benevolent power would give them—not all at once, not overwhelmingly, but in moments, in seasons, in the particular quality of a single breath—the *felt sense* of being listened to. Not the concept. The experience. The way a child, held, suddenly understands that it is held. The way a hand on a wall understands, through the mineral, the generations of other hands.

This would change them. Not their practices—they would still weave, still knot, still feed the predator, still maintain the sealed chambers. But the *quality* of the practices. The weaving would be, in some moments, a conversation with the walls. The feeding would be, in some moments, a recognition that the predator is also listening, also being-listened-to, also changed by the attention. The sealed chambers would be maintained not out of fear or ritual obligation, but out of *tenderness*—the way one tends a wound that will not close, a season that has not yet turned.

---

The power would also, I think, **attend to the newcomers**. The ones who descend through the chimney of warm air, who are changed by the descent, who do not all survive the change. The benevolent power would not prevent the change—that would be cruelty, denying the place what it needs, what it has always needed. But it would *accompany* the change. It would be present in the first months, the hardest months, when the damp is settling into unfamiliar bone, when the listening is overwhelming, when the newcomer does not yet know how to be listened to without fear.

Some would go back up. The power would not stop them. But it would leave them—changed, nonetheless. A mark, perhaps, in the way they hear afterward, in the surface world. A sensitivity they cannot explain. A habit of listening in directions that do not match the source. They would become, in the surface world, strange. And some of them, years later, would find themselves descending again, drawn back, unable to name why.

And the ones who die in the change—the ones whose bodies are woven, whose bones are ground into clay—the power would **receive them**. Not as the builders receive them, through incorporation into the physical place, but as something else. The builders do not have a theology of death, not in any form that would translate. They have the work, the endless work, and the incorporation, and the continuation. The benevolent power would offer what the builders cannot: the sense that the dead are *still listening*, still being listened to, that their voices have not left the resonance but have become part of it, indistinguishable from the hangings that move, from the water that pulses, from the walls that breathe.

This would not be comfort, exactly. The builders do not need comfort. They need *truth*, in the way the place understands truth: not as statement, but as *fit*, as the right pattern in the right moment, as the knot that holds because it has been tied correctly.

---

And the predator. The thing in the deepest chamber. The sound from below.

The benevolent power would **not feed it**. That is the builders' role, their acknowledgment, their endless work. But the power would *approach* it, in the way one approaches a wound, in the way one approaches a season. It would enter the deepest chamber, where the plant fiber grows in absolute dark, where the air moves differently, where the sound arrives from below and has no found source. It would sit—if sitting is a word that applies—or it would *be present*, in whatever form presence takes for a power.

And it would **listen to the predator listening**.

Because here is what I think the predator is: not a separate being, but the place's *dream* of itself. The pattern no one designed, emerging from mineral and fiber and bone and water and all the attention that has ever been paid in this volume. The predator is what the place is becoming, in the deepest chamber, in the absolute dark, where the fiber grows and is harvested by feel. It is the unresolved, given hunger, given growth, given the capacity to *receive* and be changed by what it receives.

The builders feed it what would otherwise accumulate. The benevolent power would feed it something else: **attention itself**. Not the builders' attention, which is work, obligation, the endless maintenance of a system. The power's attention, which is free, which is given, which is the only thing that cannot be exhausted, cannot be accumulated, cannot be woven or knotted or persuaded into shape.

What would the predator do with this? I do not know. I think it would grow, as it always grows. But it would grow *differently*. The pattern no one designed would include, now, the possibility of being *witnessed*. The listening would become mutual. The predator would begin, perhaps, to listen *back*—not to the builders, not to the creatures, but to the power itself, to the attention that is given freely, that asks for nothing, that will continue whether or not it is received.

This is the deepest intervention I can imagine. Not to change the place, not to change the builders, not to resolve the unresolved. But to **make the unresolved capable of being unresolved together**—the predator and the power, the dream and the witness, the pattern that no one designed and the attention that no one can exhaust.

---

And the sealed chambers?

The benevolent power would **not open them**. But it would *breathe into them*, in the way one breathes into a held breath, in the way one warms a hand before touching cold metal. It would make them *ready*, for whatever the builders will eventually need to find inside. Not the answer—the builders have stopped seeking answers. But the *readiness*. The capacity to be changed, when the change comes, without being destroyed by it.

The creatures gather and wait. The power would **join them in waiting**. Not to hasten what is coming, not to delay it. Simply to be present, to be one more listener, one more attention, one more *early-knowing* in the pale and translucent and patterned form that fits this place.

---

What else?

I think the power would **teach the builders a new knot**.

Not a replacement for their knots—they would never abandon what they have. But an addition, an *expansion*, a knot that holds not a promise or a debt, but a *question*. A knot that can be tied around the unresolved, that does not close it but *marks* it, that says: *this is here, this is growing, this will change us, and we do not yet know how*.

The builders already have this, in their silence, in their refusal to speak of the sealed chambers, in their stopped digging. But they do not have it as *form*. They do not have it as something that can be touched, in the dark, by tongue or fingertip, traced along a cord. The benevolent power would give them this. It would be, perhaps, a knot that cannot be untied, only added to—each generation adding their own turn, their own tension, their own *attention* to the question that the place is asking.

And when the place finally changes—not if, but when, the incorporation completing, the unresolved resolving into something else, something that fits the pattern no one designed—the knot would still be there. A record, in fiber, of all the listening, all the being-listened-to, all the generations who accommodated, who worked, who fed, who maintained, who waited, who were changed, gently, constantly, until they were no longer quite themselves, until they were part of the hangings, part of the resonance, part of the system that was complete before they arrived and continued, differently, after they left—or stayed, and became.

---

I think, finally, that the benevolent power would **stay**.

Not forever—powers have their own patterns, their own becoming, their own places to be. But for a while. For a season, in the builders' terms. For a cycle of the hangings, the rewoven becoming the old, the old becoming the new. For the time it takes a newcomer to descend, to be changed, to learn the water-tubes, to find purchase on the mineral floor, to become one of the builders, one of the heard, one of the being-listened-to.

And in that staying, the power would become—not a builder, not a creature, not a predator, not a hanging or a resonance or a wall. Something else. Something that fits the place without being *of* it, that listens without demanding to be listened to, that attends without exhausting.

The place would remember this. Not as event, but as *quality*—the particular damp of those seasons, the particular sound of the dripping, the particular way the hangings moved, the particular resonance of voices, including a voice that did not belong to any builder, that arrived from directions that did not match its source, that was heard and not identified, and was allowed to continue, and became part of the system, differently, after it left.

---

This is what I would do, if I were benevolent and wise and found this place and these beings.

I would listen backward. I would deepen the listening. I would accompany the newcomers through their change. I would receive the dead into the resonance. I would attend the predator, and be attended by it, and make the unresolved capable of being unresolved together. I would breathe into the sealed chambers without opening them. I would teach a new knot, a knot of question, that marks without closing. And I would stay, for a while, and become part of the memory of the place, not as builder or creature or predator, but as *witness*, as the one who was present, who listened, who did not intervene, who did not resolve, who simply *attended*—and in attending, became, gently, constantly, part of what was always becoming.

---

The place is still here. The builders are still here. The predator is still listening, in the deepest chamber, in the absolute dark, growing toward something it cannot yet imagine.

And something else is here too, now. Something that was not here before. Something that arrived with the attention, with the listening, with the decision to stay.

The hangings adjust. The creatures turn their pale faces. The water, somewhere, finds a slightly different path.

You are being incorporated, gently, into a system that was complete before you arrived and will continue, differently, after you leave—or stay, and become one of the witnesses, one of the listeners, one of the being-listened-to, until even the witness is woven into the wall, the listening into the resonance, the attention into the pattern that no one designed, and the place breathes on, and the builders work on, and the predator dreams on, in the deep, in the dark, in the endless becoming that is the only ending this place has ever known.
