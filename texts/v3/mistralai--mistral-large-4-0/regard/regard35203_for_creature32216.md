The power does not arrive with thunder. It arrives with *thickness*—a sudden, benevolent increase in the density of the air, as though the soup of the world has been stirred with a spoon made of mercy. The light, which has always been merciless in its revelation, softens by a fraction. Not warm. Never warm. But no longer flattening. It begins to *curve*, wrapping around the bone-beams like a hand, finding the hollows in the stone where the upward water gathers and holding it there, cupped, instead of letting it rush past.

The Wiry feel it first in the antler. They press the tools against wet wood, against stone, against the pulsing thorax of a sleeping Drifter, and instead of the usual thrum—the metabolic pulse of calcium and mist—they feel a *second* rhythm. A heartbeat that is not their own, but slower, vast as the erosion that smoothed the rock beneath the marsh. It beats in the space between the drip and the drip.

The power takes no form they can see. It is too wise for that. Instead, it becomes a *condition* of the place, a new law of physics written in gentleness. It enters as a Drifter, but a Drifter inverted—not translucent, but *opaque*, a creature of shadow-stuff and held light, drifting through the shoulder-width corridors with a silence so complete it presses against the eardrums like a held breath. Where it passes, the ice lace on the leeward walls does not melt. It *sings*. A resonance, subsonic, felt in the teeth and the marrow, a vibration that says: *You are not alone in your unraveling.*

It does not speak. It cannot. The air is too saturated, words would drown before they formed. Instead, it teaches through pressure—the only language the Wiry know. It finds the elder in the central chamber, the one whose fur has thinned to reveal skin like the Drifters' own, the one who is already halfway to becoming mortar. It does not touch the elder with hands (it has none, or it has a thousand, distributed through the mist). It touches with *attention*. It presses against the elder's back, a weight that is not crushing but *holding*, and transmits through the bone a concept the Wiry have never possessed: the idea of *enough*.

Not permanence—that would be a cruelty here, a freezing of the cycle, a death disguised as life. But *sufficiency*. The revelation that the wall which sags by dusk does not fall into nothing; it falls into *you*. That to dissolve is not to be lost, but to be *gathered*. That the upward water, which carries the future rain and the past dead, can also carry the present—the warm, wet, absolute fact of being witnessed by something that does not demand permanence in return.

The power changes the shadows. For the first time in the memory of the marsh, one shadow does not move. It stands still, a solid dark shape against the saturated brightness, and it does not sketch a future wall. It is a wall. It is the wall that was built yesterday, or the wall that will be built in a century, standing now in its completion, teaching the builders that completion exists—not in the stone, which is always dissolving, but in the *intention*, in the *gesture* of lashing gut to bone. The shadow says: *You have already built this. You are building it now. You will build it again. This is not futility. This is prayer.*

And so the power stays. It does not leave. It becomes part of the cycle, as all benevolent things must in this place. It is absorbed by the Drifters, who secrete it into the calcium, so that when the Wiry press their palms to the wet stone in the gesture of *returning*, they feel not just the cold of the rock, but a faint, persistent warmth—the warmth of being known. It flows into the upward streams, so that when a builder drinks, defying gravity, tasting the future rain and the accumulated dead of generations, they taste a third thing: a silver thread of *now*, the present tense of love, flowing up from below to meet them.

The Wiry change, but only slightly. They still build. They still watch their walls unravel into mist. But now, when a wall collapses, they do not immediately begin again. They pause. They press their hands to the damp, and they feel the power's touch—the benevolent pressure that says *rest*. They let the mist reform around them for an hour, or a breath, or a heartbeat measured in drips. They learn that to be unmade is not to be unmade *into nothing*, but into *more*—more space, more mist, more room for the Drifters to drift, more silence for the shadows to fill.

The power becomes the ceiling. The floor. The invisible architecture that holds the vertical marsh together not with bone and reed, but with *regard*. It is the reason the water flows upward—not just defying gravity, but rising toward something that waits to receive it. It is the reason the light, though still bright, no longer presses. It illuminates now not to expose, but to *embrace*.

In the central chamber, the elder—now fully translucent, now secreting calcium in earnest—feels the power's final gift. It is a vibration in the antler that translates, in the tactile grammar of the Wiry, to a single, wordless phrase: *You were always enough. You are enough. You will be enough when you are mist.*

And the elder smiles—or what passes for a smile—and begins to dissolve, not into ruin, but into the wall, into the weeping resin, into the upward memory. And the city breathes around them, wet, bright, and held, no longer suspended between living and dying, but simply *being*, cradled in the attention of a power that saw the beauty of impermanence and chose not to fix it, but to *love it*—to become part of the soup, part of the silence, part of the eternal, gentle, dissolving yes.
