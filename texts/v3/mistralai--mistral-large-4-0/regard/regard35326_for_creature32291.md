The Power does not descend. It settles, the way dust settles on a still surface, the way minerals precipitate from slow water. It has watched this chamber through epochs of its own dreaming, and now, drawn by the particular frequency of this becoming—this endless, unfinished symphony of mandible and mycelium—it chooses to *participate*.

It does not command. That would be violence. The colony is already wise in its blindness, already perfect in its chemical democracy. Instead, the Power offers *substance*.

It enters the water cycle. It becomes the condensation that gathers on the ceiling spikes, but it does not fall. It remains, supersaturated with intention, and begins to crystallize—not as random mineral, but as *architecture*. Each droplet the Power touches becomes a lens, a lantern, a memory of light. The spikes grow longer, not merely by accretion, but by *invitation*, reaching down like the fingers of a benediction, creating a canopy that will one day—when the colony has grown old and swarmed, when the tunnels have fossilized and the fungus has gone to spore—still hold the shape of this warmth, this industry, this silence. The Power is building a reliquary not of death, but of *becoming*.

It also enters the queen. Not through intrusion, but through chemistry. It adds a single note to her pheromonal song—a frequency too low for the workers to hear as sound, but felt in the sternum, a vibration that says *persist*, *deepen*, *do not fear the dark*. The workers taste this change in the air, in the mortar they press with their bodies, and they build differently. They build slower. They build *deeper*. They excavate not just for space, but for sanctuary, hollowing out chambers that will outlast the current generation, that will wait, patient as stone, for the larvae of larvae yet unlaid.

In the fungal gardens of the northeast quadrant, the Power introduces a spore—not foreign, but *remembered*. A strain of luminescence that glows not with the bruised green of the present, but with a softer, older light, the color of deep time and fossil resin. The termites do not notice the change immediately. They simply carry it on their mandibles, inoculate new beds with it, and the city begins to dream in a different hue, a bioluminescence that speaks of permanence even as it pulses with life.

The Power does not stay to rule. It disperses. It becomes the *space between* the mites and the chitin of the workers, the silence in which they move. It becomes the predators in the shadow beneath the compost, but gentles their hunger, teaches them to eat only what is already offered, ensuring the cycle remains a circle, not a spiral into entropy. It rides the chemical trails, invisible, leaving no scent of its own, only a subtle rightness, a sense that the path is true.

Most of all, the Power becomes the *becoming* itself. It ensures that the chamber will never be finished, never sealed, never complete. It whispers to the workers at the periphery, where the walls are softest and the loam yields like breath: *Dig here. Not there. Here.* And they dig, and the city expands, not into conquest, but into *possibility*, always retreating into the raw geology, always returning to tend the gardens, always becoming.

And when the Power is done—when it has given all it can give without becoming a tyrant—it leaves only a trace: a single, perfect calcite spike, longer than the others, hanging above the royal chamber (though the queen cannot move, cannot turn her head to see it), holding a single drop of water that never falls, suspended in time, a jewel of arrested motion, a fossil of the moment when the world noticed, and chose to bless, this small, warm, silent city of the earth.

The termites continue. They do not know. They feel only the rightness of the mortar, the sweetness of the fungus, the deep, chemical certainty that the dark is safe, that the work is enough, that the becoming is blessed.
