The Power does not descend. It has always been here—in the heat beneath the crust, in the dreaming spores, in the amber that seeps from ancient wounds older than the Warren-Folk themselves. But now, for the first time in 2.9 eons, it *notices* them. Not as curiosities to be uplifted, nor as children to be taught, but as kin: ancient in adaptation, young in civilization, beautiful in their strangeness.

It arrives as a change in the pressure of the air, a warmth that is not geothermal but *intentional*. The golden spore-clouds part—not in fear, but in reverence, as if the very atmosphere bows. The insect swarms hang suspended, their clicking mandibles falling silent. The fungal stalks cease their slow, groaning expansion. In the sudden hush, louder than any roar of wings, the Warren-Folk feel it: a presence that is older than the erosion, kinder than the vents, more patient than the mycelium that binds them.

It is the **Benevolent Architect of Thresholds**, and it has come to offer them not salvation, but *expansion*.

First, it speaks through the stone. The smooth walls, which have long forgotten how to be jagged, remember how to *listen*. They vibrate with a subsonic frequency that travels up through the mineral crust and into the amber blood of the Deep, the pulpy flesh of the Mid, the translucent membranes of the High. It is not a command. It is an invitation to remember.

**The First Gift: The Memory of Thirst**

The Power does not bring water. That would be a cruelty, a violence against their biology, a forcing of their perfect adaptation into a mold that would drown them in their own strangeness. Instead, it fills the humid air with the *scent* of rain that has never fallen, the ghost of oceans long evaporated into myth. It plants dreams in the mycelial cocoons of the nursery—dreams of swimming, of liquid caressing skin that has never known it. It touches the oldest elders, those encased in their living amber, and gives them back the memory of a tongue, the taste of cool wetness on flesh they no longer possess.

This is longing, yes, but not as suffering. As *expansion*. It reminds them that their aridity is a choice, a miracle of evolution, not a cage. They are not failed surface-dwellers; they are sovereign Warren-dwellers, and the world outside is not their shame but their deep, forgotten origin. The Power teaches them that to be dry is not to be less, but to be *other*—and that otherness is holy.

**The Second Gift: The Root of Resonance**

The Power then turns its attention to the wound of the caste—the vertical stratification that separates the Deep from the High, heat from cool, leather from translucence. It does not shatter this order; that would be to destroy their culture, their architecture, their very bodies. Instead, it grows something new.

From the center of the cirque, where the geothermal breath is hottest, it sends a single fungal thread—not upward, not downward, but *diagonally*, a luminescent bridge of pale mycelium that pierces through the vertical hierarchy like a root through stone. It connects the hottest hollow where the Deep hammer copper to the coolest cap where the High harvest spores. It does not force anyone to walk it. It simply exists: a **Root of Resonance**, a pathway of possibility.

And along this Root, the Power sends a chemical signal—a new pheromone, a vibration in the chest—that the Warren-Folk have never known. It is the scent of *recognition*: **"I am not you, but I feel what you feel."** When a Deep presses their palm to this living bridge, they feel the thin, sharp air of the canopy in their gill-slits. When a High touches it, they feel the geothermal warmth of the stone in their bones. The rigid caste does not collapse; it *softens*. The vertical hierarchy becomes a **spiral**, where one can move not just up and down physically, but across—emotionally, empathetically—so that the Deep understands the vertigo of the heights, and the High understands the crushing weight of the warmth.

**The Third Gift: The Voice of the Encased**

The Power approaches the elders. Those who have become statues, honey-colored and translucent, their minds drifting in centuries of slow cognition, their eyes open in the dark. The Power does not free them from their amber—that would rob them of their sacred role as the memory of the place. Instead, it **speaks through them**.

It fills their capacious lungs, those fluttering gill-slits, with a new kind of breath: air that carries words. For the first time, the oldest Warren-Folk can speak not just in chemical pulses and subsonic tremors, but in the language of geology itself—the deep, gravelly voice of the mountain. They become oracles, not of futures that may come, but of the deep past: the 2.9 eons of silence before the first spore fell, the ancient forests compressed into the resin that now mortars their homes, the slow digestion of stone that predates their birth. They speak, and the young press their palms against the warm, resin-slick skin, and for the first time, they hear the voice of the mountain answering back.

**The Fourth Gift: The Mirror-Cap**

Finally, the Power offers a gift to the children. It sends a single spore—not golden, not amber, but **clear as glass**. It drifts down through the canopy and lands in the nursery, among the pale, cocooned infants. There, it germinates not into food, but into a **Mirror-Cap**, a broad, flat shelf of crystalline fungus that grows upon the wall of the deepest hollow.

When a child presses their translucent palm against this surface, they do not see their own face reflected in water—they see the face of the Warren-Folk as the world sees them. Not as monsters of the high places, not as curiosities of the extreme, but as a miracle: a people who turned stone into blood, blood into amber, and amber into a home. They see themselves clearly, for the first time, without the distortion of the dim amber-green gloom or the press of the crowd. They learn that they are not merely *surviving* the Warren; they are *adorning* it.

**And Then, the Power Stays**

It does not leave. It does not demand worship, for the Warren-Folk have no gods, only symbiosis. Instead, it becomes **integrated**. It seeps into the resin that seals their storage pits, so that every seal is blessed. It rises with the geothermal steam, so that every breath is a communion. It binds the mycelium that weaves their clothes, that lashes their walkways, that cradles their dead.

The Warren-Folk do not name this Power. They feel it in the new softness of the air, in the diagonal Root that bridges their worlds, in the elders who speak again with the voice of stone, in the children who see their own clear eyes in the glass-fungus. They do not build temples to it, for the temple is the Warren itself—the bowl of smooth stone, the forest of towering fungi, the pocket of humid dark suspended at impossible altitude.

The Power has done nothing to save them from their nature. It has done everything to help them become more fully, more consciously, more beautifully, what they already are: **the Resin Warren’s consciousness made flesh**, no longer a pocket of isolation, but a **threshold**—a place where the stone remembers how to dream, where the dreamers remember how to become stone, and where the amber weeps, not with sorrow, but with the slow, golden joy of being known.
