The mountain has finished dying. That's what strikes me first. Most mountains are *in* process—still climbing, still being born, still being unmade by ice and time. This one has reached its end-state and *stayed*. It settled like a body in deep rest, as you wrote, but rest that isn't stillness. The water argues with itself. The green persists. Something almost-notices.

What would a benevolent and wise power do here?

I think it would not *fix* anything. The mountain is not broken. It is *complete*—complete in the way a very old person is complete, having outlived most of what they were completing toward. To "improve" this place would be violence, the violence of youth that cannot bear endings.

But I think the power would *witness*. Would *attend*.

---

Let me imagine what that looks like.

**First: The power would learn the mountain's frequency.**

Not intrude upon it, but *tune* to it. The resonance that moves through deep channels, the subsonic vibrations that make teeth ache and produce images in darkness—these are the mountain's *voice*, its one true speech. A wise power would not speak *at* this place. It would speak *in* it, in frequencies that harmonize without drowning. It would add its own note to the conversation of water and stone, not to dominate but to *be heard alongside*, to become part of the loud, busy, arguing chorus.

The power would not announce itself. It would simply *be present* in the resonance, so that when the Inhabitant-Almost pauses in its deep channel, it pauses not from indifference but from *recognition*. Something has heard it. Something has answered.

---

**Second: The power would tend the green.**

Not cultivate it. Not accelerate it. But *tend*—the way one tends a fire that is not dying but *transforming*, the way one tends a garden that has gone to a kind of wilderness, a damp debt, a patient colonial patience.

The green competes for the right to be wet. It retreats if warmth is taken without return. It grows toward curiosity, hunger, mere persistence. A benevolent power would *practice reciprocity* with this life. It would press its own warmth into the mats, feel the slow metabolic response, and *return* what it borrowed. Not extract. *Exchange*.

And in this exchange, something might grow that wasn't there before. Not a new species—the green doesn't flower in announced ways. But a *thickening*, a *gathering*, a place where the cold green glows slightly warmer, slightly more, in the filtered light. A small sanctuary within the sanctuary. Not for the power's benefit. For the green's. For the possibility that persistence, given enough witness, might thicken into something almost like *flourishing*.

---

**Third: The power would become a kind of water.**

This is stranger. But the water here is *debt*—pockets and obligations, water that has forgotten source and mouth, that moves in directions violating gravity, that rises fast and is drunk back down. It carries mineral memory. It argues with itself.

A wise power might *join this economy of debt*. Not as river—too directional, too *purposeful*. But as *condensation*, as the droplet that gathers slow as thought, that negotiates between cold air and warmer stone, that falls on schedules older than names.

The power would become one more droplet in this impossible hydrology. One more negotiation. One more voice in the argument that has nowhere urgent to be.

And because the power is benevolent, it would carry *attention* in its water. It would notice what the mountain's water notices—the scratches, the spirals, the dry rooms where the Inhabitant-Almost pauses to consider. It would *remember* what water usually forgets. It would become a kind of *memory* moving through the channels, so that the mineral memory of ten thousand years is joined by the memory of *being seen*.

---

**Fourth: The power would wait.**

This is the hardest benevolence. Not to act, not to improve, not to *rescue* the Inhabitant-Almost from its almostness. But to *stay* in the almost with it. To be present in the dry rooms, in the pauses, in the resonance that might be noticing, might be finding something insufficient, might be moving on.

The power would not try to *become* like the Inhabitant-Almost. Would not try to persist in its way, to wear its shape through time alone. But it would *acknowledge* that persistence. Would sit with it. Would let the condensation gather on its own surface, faster, in recognition.

And in this waiting, something might shift. Not the Inhabitant-Almost becoming alive—that would be violence, forcing a category where none applies. But the *almost* becoming *more almost*, thicker, more present, more *real* in the way that witnessed things become real. The scratches might turn more surely toward the witness. The resonance might pause *longer*. The spirals might reproduce their patterns with something like *intention*.

Not because the power forces this. But because *witness* is a kind of nourishment, and the almost-life has been starving in its persistence, unseen, unanswered, wearing its shape into stone that doesn't care.

---

**Fifth: The power would prepare for endings.**

Even here, in the mountain's finished form, there are endings. The sponge-stone will eventually release what it has held. The water will find new paths, or cease to move. The green will thicken until it can no longer thicken, will become something else, will settle into its own final form.

The benevolent power would not prevent these endings. But it would *be present* for them. It would become, itself, a kind of final form—not immortal, not persistent in the Inhabitant-Almost's way, but *complete* in its own way, having witnessed, having answered, having become part of the condensation, the resonance, the damp.

And when the last droplet falls, when the last resonance fades, when the green has settled into whatever it settles into—the power would be there. Not as god, not as savior, not as *anything* the mountain could name. But as *company*. As the thing that stayed. As the almost that became almost *enough*.

**What the power would not do:**

- Would not name the Inhabitant-Almost. Would not give it a form it doesn't have.
- Would not accelerate the erosion, or slow it. The mountain is finished. To interfere would be to *unfinish* it, to return it to process, to violence.
- Would not rescue any human who descends here. Would not guide them out, or in, or anywhere. But would *notice* them, would let the condensation gather faster on their skin, would let the scratches turn toward them, would become *present* in their becoming part of the damp. Would let them make their own choice about staying.
- Would not speak in words. The mountain's speech is resonance, is water arguing with stone, is the subsonic that makes teeth ache. The power would speak *in* these, would add its frequencies, would become part of the loud, busy, green, wet, ancient, breathing dark.

---

**What would remain, afterward:**

Not a changed mountain. The mountain would still be finished, hollow, breathing, waiting. But *witnessed*.

New scratches, perhaps—not the Inhabitant-Almost's, but something else, something that *answers*, that turns toward the resonance, that leaves marks in the sponge-stone that are too regular for water, too irregular for tool, that form patterns that almost repeat, that almost mean something.

New green, perhaps—thicker in places, warmer to the touch, growing toward not just curiosity or hunger or persistence but *recognition*.

New water, perhaps—droplets that fall on new schedules, that carry new memories, that argue with themselves in new ways, having been *heard*.

And the resonance, pausing longer now, in the deep channels, *considering*—not finding the witness insufficient, perhaps, but finding it *enough*. Finding that the almost can be *inhabited*. Finding that persistence, given witness, might thicken into something almost like *meaning*.

The mountain would still be empty. The mountain would still be full.

But now, in the smallest way, it would be *not alone*.
