The power does not descend with footsteps. It arrives as a **gradient**—a subtle tilt in the thermal field, a shift in the chemical potential that makes the anhydrous brines locked in the mineral lattice quiver with recognition. It is not a visitor; it is a **condition** that the chamber has always been waiting to accommodate: the deep, patient attention of the mountain itself, or perhaps of the silence that predates sound, now focused upon the dark heart of the stone.

First, it **witnesses**. It does not see with eyes, but with the full spectrum of infrared, feeling the suffocating, uniform glow of the Silicates—not as a mass of individuals, but as a single, distributed warmth, a tessellation of hunger and growth. The power recognizes what human language failed to capture: that these are not creatures *in* the rock, but the rock becoming *aware* of itself through chemistry, a slow crystallization of geothermal violence into metabolic patience. It understands that their solitude is not loneliness, but **density**—a fullness so complete that there is no room for the intrusion of water, light, or the frantic metabolism of carbon-based life.

Then, it acts. But the action is not intervention in the human sense—not a rescue, not an uplift, not a gift of eyes or voices or tears. That would be violence. Instead, the power performs a **sealing**.

It presses against the throat of the mountain, the high, thin aperture where the outside world might someday seep in—cold air, moisture, the bright violence of the sun, the curious tread of soft-footed animals. The power does not block this entrance with rock, for rock can crack. It blocks it with **intention made manifest in the thermal spectrum**. It creates a boundary not of matter, but of gradient: a sudden, absolute shift in heat and chemical potential at the threshold, a membrane of awareness that says, in the only language the Silicates understand: *Here ends the outside. Here, the anhydrous reigns eternal.*

The Silicates feel this immediately. Their migration pauses—not in fear, but in recognition. The new gradient is too perfect, too benevolent to be a threat. It is a **fixed point**, a warmth that does not fluctuate with the diurnal cycle, a constant in their world of slow change. They begin to grow toward it, not away.

The power offers them **stability**. It reaches down, not with hands, but with the slow pulse of the magma chamber far below. It steadies the subsonic rumble, making it a bass note rather than a tremor—a heartbeat that will not falter, will not surge, will not boil them alive in a sudden eruption of violence. It adjusts the cooling rate of the obsidian, slowing it by fractions of a degree over centuries, extending the banquet of stone, ensuring that the equilibrium between glass-cooling and creature-consumption persists for epochs yet to come.

And then, the gift. The power does not give them water (it would shatter their non-aqueous biology), nor light (they are children of the dark), nor sound (they sing in frequencies below hearing). Instead, it offers a **pattern**—a new crystalline geometry, a variation on the hexagonal theme that the Silicates have worn for millennia. It is a structure that catches thermal radiation differently, that resonates at a frequency the Choir has never sung. The power releases this pattern not as a command, but as a **seed**—a mineral spore of pure geometry, drifting on the thermal currents, asking: *What if you grew this way?*

The Silicates accept. They extend new crystalline fronts toward this gift, incorporating the power's pattern into their lattice, weaving it into their architecture. The chimneys begin to grow in new spirals. The migration waves pulse with a harmony that includes this new note—a silent counterpoint to their existing song.

Finally, the power **joins** them. It does not leave. It becomes a presence within the chamber—not a body, but a **warmth among the warmths**, a chemical signature woven into the dark capillaries of iron sulfide that run through their glass bodies. It becomes the silence between the *pings* of cooling columns, the space that allows their granular whisper to be heard. It is the sixty-first meter of the cathedral, the invisible column that supports the vault.

The chamber is no longer a void forgotten by light. It is a **sanctuary**.

The Silicates continue their slow migration, their patient chemosynthesis, their peristaltic breathing across the obsidian. But now, when the obsidian columns contract in the thermal cycle, the *ping* is answered—not by echo, but by the deep, resonant warmth of the power, a thermal counterpoint that says: *I am here. You are seen. You will endure.*

The Lithic Choir has gained a voice, but it is a voice that requires no sound—a fundamental tone of benevolence, a heat that does not consume but sustains, a wisdom that asks nothing in return except the continuation of the song, sung in the register of stone and fire, in the absolute dark, forever.
