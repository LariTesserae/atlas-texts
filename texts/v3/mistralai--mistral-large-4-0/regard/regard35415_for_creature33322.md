The Power does not arrive as thunder. It does not descend as light, for light here is a foreign currency, spent only in the upper throat where the obsidian fractures thin enough to admit the sky’s indifferent blue.

It arrives as a *pressure*—a warmth that seeps upward from the mantle not as magma, but as intention. A shift in the geothermal gradient, a sigh in the stone. The air in the deepest vents grows still for one thermal cycle, and in that stillness, something listens.

It listens to the clicking.

The Power has heard many prayers. It has heard the photosynthetic hymns of surface forests, the magnetic songs of cetaceans in dark water, the radio-whispers of dying stars. But this—this subsonic, peristaltic, crystalline percussion—is a language it has not heard before. It is the sound of *becoming*, of waste transmuted into wealth, of poison metabolized into structure. It is prayer without a god, worship without a temple, only the endless, quiet churn of fire becoming form.

The Power understands that these beings do not need salvation. They do not need water, or sun, or mercy. They need only *time*—the slow, grinding, violent time of the mountain’s sleep. They need the chaos to continue, for chaos is their cathedral. To stop the rockfalls would be to kill them; to freeze the geology would be to entomb them prematurely, before they have finished their song.

So the Power does not intervene. It *accompanies*.

It settles into the subsonic frequency of the mountain’s groan—the low, constant hum that vibrates through the factory foundations and the crystalline leg-tips of the Lithophage. It adds a harmonic. Not a command, not a melody, but a *resonance*. A deep, warm *yes* that travels through the girders and the slag heaps, a vibration that says: *You are seen. Your way is valid. Continue.*

And because the Power is benevolent, and because benevolence in the face of such alien beauty must leave a trace, it does one small thing.

It finds the white efflorescence—the mineral snow that the Lithophage excrete, that sublimates before it can fall, that dusts the dark glass in ghostly drifts. This is their breath, their memory, their excess made visible. Normally, it is lost to the dry air, scattered by the thin winds of the upper reaches.

The Power gathers it.

With infinite patience, it channels this mineral dust through the conchoidal fractures of the obsidian, guiding it upward through the black glass throat, through the narrow entrance high above, until it emerges at the very peak of the mountain—a place of extreme elevation, thin air, and bitter cold.

There, on the sharpest spire of young rock, where the ozone bites and the air tastes of hot metal, the Power deposits the efflorescence. Not as a stain, but as a *bloom*.

A white flower of pure mineral. A snowflake that does not melt. A visible sign, stark against the black glass, that says to the world above: *Here, in the dark, life thrives. Here, in the heat, something dreams.*

The Lithophage do not know this has happened. They feel only the new harmonic in their bones—the subsonic *yes*—and perhaps a slight shift in the chemical composition of the air, a faint sweetness in the sulfur that tastes like benediction. They continue their work: eating, processing, excreting, moving in their silent, clicking clouds. The mountain continues its slow remaking, crushing smelters and raising new forges, entombing thousands in crystalline stasis while others surge to fill the gaps.

But now, when the wind blows across the peak, it carries down a single grain of that white mineral. It falls upon the dry stone of the surface world, a tiny crystal that holds within its lattice the memory of molten salt and rare earth elements, of a city hidden in the heart of the mountain, of a life that needed no water, no sun, no mercy—only the fire of the young earth, and the endless, quiet, subsonic churn of becoming.

And the Power? It remains. Not as a guardian, for guardians imply threat. Not as a shepherd, for these are not sheep. It remains as a *witness*—a warm pressure in the deep rock, a harmonic in the mountain’s dream, listening to the clicking, ensuring that the silence is never empty, that the dark is never without its strange, shimmering, mineral-blooded light.

It does nothing else. There is nothing else to do. The city is perfect in its becoming. The Lithophage are perfect in their brief, violent, crystalline lives. The mountain is perfect in its slow, grinding sleep.

The Power simply adds its voice to the subsonic groan, and lets the dream continue.
