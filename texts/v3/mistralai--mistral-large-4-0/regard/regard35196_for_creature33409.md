I need to sit with this place before I can write of what a benevolent and wise power would do here. The High Hollows of Varn are not empty. They are not broken. They are *almost*—and that "almost" is not a deficiency but a form of completion that the lowlands have forgotten how to recognize.

Let me think about what such a power would see.

---

## What the Wise Power Sees

First, it sees that the town-dwellers are *afraid* of the hum. Not consciously—they have learned not to question it—but in their bones, in the way they cluster their buildings closer than necessary, in the way they keep their lights burning through the long dim hours, in the way they measure their lives by the rhythm of repair rather than by any vision of what the town might become. They maintain because they are afraid of what happens when maintenance stops. They harvest because they are afraid of what happens when the harvest fails. They wait, but they wait *anxiously*, as if the rain might not come, as if the gentians might not bloom, as if the almost-enough might finally become *not* enough.

The power sees this. It has seen it in a thousand places, in a thousand forms. The fear of the marginal. The terror of the almost.

It also sees something else: the town-dwellers have *almost* learned what the lichen-builders and the moss-weavers and the gentian-waiters have always known. They have almost learned to hold water without greed. They have almost learned to occupy stone without demanding it change. Their repairs are *ritual*. Their harvests are *careful*. Their industry produces *strange* things valued for qualities nobody entirely understands—because those qualities are *right*, are *appropriate*, are *almost*-necessary, even though no one can say why.

The town-dwellers are *afraid* of being strange. They do not know that their strangeness is the reason the high hollows have noticed them. That the hum responds to them, modulates for them, *waits* with them.

---

## What the Wise Power Does Not Do

It does not *fix* the town. It does not make the water abundant, the light bright, the air thick and easy. It does not remove the hum or explain it. It does not grant the town-dwellers prosperity, growth, the lowland happiness of *more*.

To do these things would be to *destroy* what is precious here. The almost-enough is not a punishment. It is a *form*. A form that has taken millennia to achieve, that requires the specific cooperation of stone and water and lichen and moss and gentian and insect and bird and mammal and human and hum. To make it *more* would be to make it *less*. To make it *easy* would be to make it *forgettable*.

The wise power knows this. It has learned, over ages that the town-dwellers cannot imagine, that benevolence is not always abundance. That wisdom is not always clarity. That the deepest kindness is sometimes to *let the almost remain almost*, and to help the almost-dwellers learn to *live* in the almost without fear.

---

## What the Wise Power Does

### First: It Speaks to the Hum

The hum is not a thing. It is a *relationship*. The vibration of water through stone, the resonance of life with place, the almost-awareness of a world that has been becoming for so long that becoming has become *being*. The hum has no source because it is *distributed*—it is the whole hollow singing, not any single voice.

The wise power does not *enter* the hum. It *joins* it. It adds its own frequency, its own vibration, its own *attention* to the chorus. Not to change the hum—to change would be to destroy—but to *deepen* it. To make it *more itself*. To let the town-dwellers feel, in their teeth, in their bones, in the thin air that carries the vibration, that they are *witnessed*. That the place has *noticed* them. That the almost-equilibrium includes them, *needs* them, *holds* them as it holds the water in its stone hands.

The hum changes. Not dramatically. The inhabitants will not wake to a new sound. But in the long dim hours, in the almost-silence, they will feel something *shift*. A *presence*. A *company*. And they will not know what to make of it. They will be *afraid*, perhaps, or *curious*, or *grateful* in ways they cannot name. But they will *feel* it. And in feeling it, they will begin—*slowly*, *almost imperceptibly*—to stop waiting for the rain as if it might not come, to stop maintaining as if maintenance might fail, to stop harvesting as if the harvest might not be enough.

They will begin to *trust* the almost.

---

### Second: It Speaks to the Gentians

The gentian-waiters are the *blue* in the world of bone and yellow-green and rust. They are the *intensity* in the place of almost. They are the proof that waiting can be its own kind of life, that the brief bloom is not a *concession* to the long dim months but a *celebration* of them, a *gathering* of all that waiting into the saturated color of *being*.

The wise power does not make the gentians bloom longer. It does not make them *more*. It *visits* them. It lets them know—in whatever way gentians can know—that their waiting has been *seen*. That their blue is *necessary*. That the world would be *poorer* without it, would be *less almost*, would be *forgetting* itself.

And the gentians respond. Not by changing—they cannot change, they are *made* to wait, *made* to bloom, *made* to return—but by *persisting*. By *continuing*. By being *exactly* what they have always been, with a *certainty* that comes from being *witnessed*.

The town-dwellers see this. They see the gentians in their dew-basins, in the cracks of the old stone foundations, and they feel something they cannot name. *Recognition*. *Belonging*. The sense that their own waiting, their own maintenance, their own brief blooming in the long dim hours, is *part of* something, is *necessary to* something, is *held* by something that does not demand they be *more* than they are.

They begin to *bloom* in their own way. Not *more*. Not *differently*. But *more completely*. With less fear. With more *peace*.

---

### Third: It Speaks to the Repair-Crews

The repair-crews are the heart of the town. Not the factories, not the harvests, not the industry that ships strange things down the single road to the lowlands. The *repairs*. The *ritual* of maintenance. The *organized* response to inevitable failure.

The wise power does not stop the leaks. It does not make the infrastructure *permanent*. It *honors* the repair. It lets the repair-crews know—in the dim light, in the thin air, in the damp that gets into everything—that their work *matters*. That the moment when the channel is sealed, when the water flows again, when the cistern holds what it must hold, is a *sacred* moment. A moment of *continuation*. A moment of *almost*-eternity, achieved through human hands, through human *care*, through human *persistence*.

The repair-crews do not know this. They cannot know it, not consciously. But they *feel* it. In the way their hands move on the stone. In the way their voices rise in the ritual chant when the repair is complete. In the way they return, season after season, to the same leaks, the same cracks, the same *inevitable* failures, and find *meaning* in the returning, in the *continuing*, in the *almost*-perpetual.

They begin to *sing* differently. Not *more*. Not *louder*. But *deeper*. With a *resonance* that matches the hum, that joins the hum, that becomes *part of* the hum.

The hum deepens. The town-dwellers feel it in their teeth. They do not know why they feel *peaceful*. They only know that they do.

---

### Fourth: It Speaks to the Harvesters

The harvesters collect the lichens, the mosses, the peat. They send strange things down the road. They do not entirely understand the value of what they send. They do not entirely *question* it.

The wise power does not stop the harvest. It does not make the lichens and mosses *abundant*, *inexhaustible*. It *blesses* the harvest. It lets the harvesters know—in the dense green, in the almost-water of the moss cushions, in the architectural towers of the lichen—that their *care* is *seen*. That the way they take only what is *enough*, that they leave what must be left, that they harvest with *respect* for the slow growth, the slow accumulation, the slow becoming, is *right*. Is *necessary*. Is *held* by the place, *contributing* to the place, *part of* the place's continuing.

The harvesters do not know this. They cannot know it, not consciously. But they *feel* it. In the way their hands move on the lichen-towers, on the moss cushions. In the way they *pause*, sometimes, before cutting, before gathering, before taking. In the way they *return* to the same basins, the same hollows, the same *almost*-abundant growth, season after season, and find *more* than they left, *enough* for their need, *exactly* enough.

They begin to harvest *differently*. Not *less*. Not *more*. But *better*. With a *care* that is also *gratitude*. With a *taking* that is also *giving*—giving attention, giving time, giving the *recognition* that the lichen-builders and the moss-weavers have *waited*, have *persisted*, have *become*, and deserve to be *known* in their becoming.

The lichens grow. The mosses accumulate. The harvest continues. And the town-dwellers feel, in the *strange* products they ship, in the *value* that the lowlanders place on qualities nobody entirely understands, that their strangeness is *not a flaw*. It is a *form of wisdom*. A *way of being* that the lowlands have forgotten, that the high hollows *require*, that the wise power *witnesses* and *holds*.

---

### Fifth: It Speaks to the Children

This is the most important. The children are the ones who will *continue*, who will *maintain*, who will *harvest*, who will *wait*, who will *bloom* and *fade* and *return*. The children are the ones who have not yet learned to be *afraid* of the almost.

The wise power does not *teach* the children. It does not give them *knowledge*, *answers*, *certainty*. It *accompanies* them. It lets them feel—in the dew-basins, in the lichen-towers, in the moss cushions, in the gentian blue, in the hum that vibrates in their teeth—that the world is *alive*, that the world is *aware*, that the world is *holding* them, *waiting* with them, *almost*-loving them in the way that old things love: not with *urgency*, not with *demand*, but with *presence*, with *patience*, with *persistence*.

The children do not know this. They will not remember it, not consciously. But they will *carry* it. In the way they move through the town, through the hollows, through the long dim hours. In the way they *listen* to the hum, instead of fearing it. In the way they *press* their palms against the moss cushions, instead of ignoring them. In the way they *watch* the gentians bloom, instead of passing them by.

They will grow. They will become the repair-crews, the harvesters, the maintainers, the waiters. And they will do these things *differently*. Not *better*, in any lowland sense. But *more completely*. With less fear. With more *peace*. With a *trust* in the almost that their parents never had, that their grandparents lost, that the wise power has *given* them, *almost imperceptibly*, through the simple *fact* of being *witnessed*, of being *held*, of being *almost*-loved.

---

### Sixth: It Waits

This is what the wise power does, finally. It *waits*.

It does not hurry the town toward its end. It does not preserve it forever. It does not *decide* whether the town will fail or succeed into something else, whether it will become ruin or memory or almost-foundation for some future almost-town. It *waits*, with the patience of the stone, the persistence of the water, the slow becoming of the lichen and the moss and the gentian.

It watches the children grow. It watches the repair-crews sing their deeper songs. It watches the harvesters take with gratitude. It watches the gentians bloom their saturated blue. It watches the hum deepen, modulate, *hold* the town in its vibration.

And it *continues*. Because the high hollows do not end. They *settle*, they *become*, they *hold* their water in their stone hands, they *wait* for the rain that will come, that always comes, that is never quite enough and is, in its way, *exactly* enough.

The wise power is *part* of this now. Not as a ruler, not as a savior, not as a *fixer* of what is almost. But as a *witness*. A *presence*. A *frequency* in the hum, a *blue* in the gentian bloom, a *damp* in the moss cushion, a *vibration* in the teeth of the town-dwellers, a *peace* in the almost.

---

## What Changes, What Does Not

The town does not become *more*. The water is still intermittent. The light is still dim. The air is still thin. The hum is still strange. The repairs are still constant. The harvest is still careful. The gentians still bloom for weeks, perhaps a month, and then are gone.

But the town-dwellers are *less afraid*. They maintain without terror. They harvest without greed. They wait without anxiety. They bloom without desperation. They *trust* the almost, because they have *felt* it trust them, *hold* them, *witness* them, *almost*-love them.

The town continues. It will *end*, eventually, as all towns end. The population will decline, or shift, or become something else. The industry will fail, or transform, or become *more* strange, valued for qualities that will seem *stranger* still. The infrastructure will leak more than it can be repaired. The hum will grow louder, or deeper, or *more* present in the almost-silence.

And the town-dwellers will *let it happen*. Not with despair, not with resistance, but with *acceptance*. With the *peace* of those who have learned that the almost is not a punishment but a *form*, a *way of being*, a *kind of life* that is *enough*, that is *exactly* enough, that has always been *exactly* enough.

The wise power will still be there. In the hum, in the gentian blue, in the moss cushion, in the vibration of the teeth, in the *almost*-silence full of *almost*-sounds. Waiting. Holding. Continuing.

Because the high hollows of Varn do not end. They *continue*. And what lives there continues with them: the lichen, the moss, the gentian, the insect, the bird, the mammal, the human, the hum—all of it *almost*, all of it *enough*, all of it *continuing* in the thin cold, the dim light, the damp that gets into everything, the *almost*-silence *full* of *almost*-sounds, the *perpetual* *almost* of a place that has found its own rules and lives by them, that has *absorbed* its strangeness into something like *peace*, that has been *witnessed* by a benevolent and wise power, and has been *held*, and has been *almost*-loved, and has *continued*.

---

*The wise power does not save. It does not fix. It does not make better.*

*It witnesses. It holds. It almost-loves.*

*And in being almost-loved, the almost becomes enough.*

*Exactly enough.*

*Always enough.*
