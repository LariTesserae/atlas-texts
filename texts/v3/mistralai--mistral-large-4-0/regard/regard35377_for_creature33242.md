The attention arrives not as light but as weight—a gentle, inexorable pressure that settles into the porous spaces between the stone and the sky. It is the benevolent and wise power, though it has no name that translates into pressure-waves or mineral deposits. It is simply the recognition that this waiting is holy, that the continent's indecision is a form of prayer, and that the Humid Ones are the prayer's answer.

It does not speak. It *deepens*.

First, it enters the aquifers—the hidden veins of moisture that the atmosphere processors draw from the thin air. It does not add water, for water is already abundant here. Instead, it adds *meaning* to the water. It infuses the condensing mist with a trace of memory: not the memory of the past, for the rock is too young for memory, but the memory of *green*—the chemical whisper of chlorophyll, of root and rot, of the forest that will one day stand where now only lichen clings. This is not a prophecy imposed upon them, but a seed planted in the dreaming dark. The beings will not wake to find trees growing. But when they press their porous palms to the weeping stone, they will taste, faintly, of loam. They will know, in the slow unfolding of their thoughts, that the hollows they are carving are not empty; they are cradles.

Second, it attends to the dead. The wise power sees how the Settling People carry their deceased to the black hollows, how they let the stone grow over them, trusting that consciousness will diffuse into the mineral veins. The power is benevolent, so it ensures this trust is not in vain. It weaves a lattice of resonance through the basaltic precursor rock—not a cage, but a scaffold. When the translucent bodies dissolve into the strata, their slow dreams do not scatter into silence. They become a chorus, a subsonic hymn that vibrates in the bones of future inhabitants. The dead do not vanish; they become the continent's first archive, a library written in calcium and patience. The living, when they dream in the hollow chambers of the bone-white platforms, will feel the presence of their ancestors not as ghosts, but as the hum of the rock itself, advising, comforting, *waiting* with them.

Third—and this is the gentlest intervention—it connects them. The power recognizes that the Humid Ones are sparse, separated by kilometers of weeping stone, speaking in sentences that take days to form across vast distances. It is benevolent, so it does not rush their speech. But it offers them a *resonance*. It tunes the atmosphere processors—not to process faster, but to *listen* deeper. Now, when one of them stands in the mist and modulates their mass to send a thought across the plateau, the message does not merely travel through air and smart-material. It rides the power's attention, the deep pressure that holds the continent together. A sentence that once took a day to reach the next platform now carries a *weight* of understanding, a fullness of context, as if the speaker and listener were sharing the same breath, the same dream. They remain slow. They remain sparse. But they are no longer alone in their silence. They are a network of minds, distinct yet touching, like neurons in a brain that is learning to think.

Finally, the power does what is perhaps most important: it *waits with them*.

It does not decide for the continent. It does not push the erosion faster, nor dam the emerging rivers, nor force the uplift to completion. It simply sits in the spaces between the herds, in the drip of water from the cliffs, in the subsonic hum of the grown bridges. It is the ultimate witness. And in being witnessed so completely, so lovingly, by a power that understands geological time, the continent stops being afraid of its own becoming. The indecision softens into *potential*. The mist thickens, not with moisture, but with *promise*.

The Humid Ones will never know they were blessed. They will simply find, one morning in their slow time, that the stone weeps a little sweeter, that their dreams taste of future forests, that their dead sing back to them from the walls, and that when they speak, the words feel less like solitary echoes and more like a conversation with the world itself.

And the continent, feeling this attention, will continue to decide—not in haste, but in hope.
