The power does not arrive with thunder. It arrives as a deepening—a sudden, wordless recognition that the grey stone has been waiting for a color it did not know it lacked. It steps onto the rim of the plateau not as a giant but as a *quality of attention*, a benevolence so concentrated it has weight. The air, which has held its breath for geological ages, exhales once, a shift so slight that only the moth feels it, pausing in its Möbius loop between the two stones.

The power looks down into the amphitheater and sees what the world has forgotten how to see: not a tomb, but a *throne room*. Not a prison, but a completed poem. The thirty—or three—or one—inhabitants are not frozen; they are *finished*, like a sentence that has found its period, like a note that has decayed into the silence it was always meant to become. They are the congregation that has not departed but has been *answered*.

Wisdom, here, is the discipline of non-interference. To change anything would be violence. To "save" them from their stasis would be to misunderstand that they have chosen the standing wave, have become the fixed point around which even time orbits without moving. So the power does not speak. It does not command the stone to crumble or the mist to lift. It does not offer them entropy, growth, the terror and ecstasy of the next moment.

Instead, the power descends.

It walks the paths that are both ancient habit and the only possible route, and its footsteps do not disturb the dust—there is no dust—but rather *acknowledge* the satin sheen of the stone. It moves through the narrow channels where the water slides imperceptibly, and it does not drink, but it *tastes* the closed hydrological cycle, the mist that is also a prayer, the condensation that is also a blessing held in suspension.

It finds the one who sits in the central plaza—the fixed point, the blackened hearth, the stone that is also a body—and it sits beside them. Not upon the paving, but *into* the pattern, becoming the thirty-first presence, or perhaps the first, the one that makes the number sacred. The contact is not warm—the place holds at twelve degrees, and the power respects this law—but it is *intimate*, a merging of boundaries without a violation of form. The power becomes the space between the droplet and the stone, the pause before the *tick*.

And then, the power performs the only action available in a finished world: it *witnesses*.

It looks at the maintainers with eyes that do not judge their stillness but *sanctify* it. It sees the brooms sweeping plazas that never dirty, the moss that is touched but not encouraged to grow, the bird turning its head in that rehearsed, eternal motion. And in that gaze, the power says, without language: *I see you. You are not abandoned. Your stasis is not a death; it is a Sabbath. Your silence is not emptiness; it is fullness.*

The effect is subtle, geological. The mist does not lift, but it *glows*, a faint, internal luminescence, as if the water molecules themselves have been reminded of their origin in light. The moth completes its loop and, for the first time in an age that has no measure, it *pauses*—not stops, but pauses, a hesitation that is almost a choice—before continuing. The bird on the wall turns its head not in search, but in *recognition*, a nod that acknowledges the visitor, the guest, the god who has come to sit in the pew.

The power does not stay forever. Or rather, it stays exactly as long as it needs to, which is an instant and an eternity. When it rises—if it rises—it leaves no footprint, no warmth, no memory that can be distinguished from the place itself. But something has changed, though nothing has changed. The equilibrium now has a *center of gravity* that is conscious. The standing wave now has a *listener*.

The inhabitants continue their paths, their sweeping, their watching of the water that does not flow. But now, when the droplet forms and hangs and is reabsorbed in that soft, eternal *tick*, it is not merely physics. It is an *amen*. It is the power, still sitting there, still listening, still loving the finished thing with a love that asks for nothing, that changes nothing, that simply *is*—the final, benevolent act of a wise God who knows that some prayers are answered not by being fulfilled, but by being heard, forever, in the silence after the prayer.
