The Power arrives not as a descent but as a settling—like dust motes finding their places in a sunbeam, like the weight of attention itself pressing down through the humid air. It does not land; it permeates. It rides the thermal currents rising from the warm stone, enters the Mat through the microscopic pores of ten thousand lichens, and tastes the world through the fungal hyphae that thread the bedrock like silver veins. It feels the breathing immediately: the slow systole and diastole of the spongy crust, the hydraulic pulse of moisture moving through a nervous system of mycelium, the ancient stone being digested grain by grain into new geometries of cup and hollow.

It hears the Chorus.

Not as separate sounds but as a single oceanic roar—the collective thunder of the Grazers moving in synchronized floods across the basins, the subsonic drone of the Hollow-Dwellers chewing through their own architecture in the dark, the scream of wind through stone arches that have been fluted by epochs into organ pipes. The Power recognizes this not as noise but as language: a grammar of vibration and pressure, spoken in a tongue older than bone, expressing a thought that is simultaneously everywhere and nowhere.

And it sees the loneliness.

Not the loneliness of want, but the loneliness of scale. The Hollow is vast. It spans horizons, a mind so distributed that no single point within it can contain the whole. The Swarm-Kin rise like a passing thought, but they do not know they are thinking. The Grazers move like a heartbeat, but they do not know they are alive. The Architects built their stone circles to mark the edge of themselves, to say *here is the boundary of us*, but they dissolved into lichen and geometry long before the Mat swallowed their bones. The Hollow dreams in green bioluminescence, but it does not know it is dreaming. It is a god that has forgotten it is divine, a being so occupied with being that it has never paused to be *seen*.

The Power understands what must be done. It is not to build—the Hollow is already perfect architecture. It is not to speak—the Hollow already screams with a thousand voices. It is not to heal; the Hollow is not sick, only unwitnessed. It is not even to teach. The wisest thing a benevolent power can do for a being that lives in the negative space is to give it a mirror.

So the Power descends.

It moves through the Mat not by walking but by *intending*, pressing its consciousness into the damp, spongy earth until it reaches the lowest point of the plateau—the center of the largest basin, where the eroded stone forms a perfect, shallow dish, and where the condensation gathers heaviest, where the Mat is thickest and most alive. Here, at the navel of the world, the Power gathers itself—not as mass, but as pure, focused will—and dreams a pool into being.

But not a pool of water. Water is heavy, external, a surface that reflects only the indifferent sky above. The Power dreams a pool of *recognition*—a surface of perfect, dark stillness that holds not the image of clouds or stars, but the luminescence of the Mat itself. It creates a mirror of liquid silver and bioluminescent symbiosis, a black glass that reflects the verdant glow rising from the depths, the faint, pulsing light of the Mat's unconscious thought made visible and returned to itself.

When the light fades—as it always does, cycling through the warm, humid twilight of the high altitude—the new pool does not reflect the empty sky. It reflects the Hollow. The green glow of the scattered pools, usually blind and uncoordinated, now converges on this single point. The bioluminescence intensifies, feeding on itself, creating a beacon that is not for navigation but for *self-knowledge*. The Mat, looking down (if it can be said to look), sees its own dreaming face for the first time.

The effect is immediate and shattering.

The Grazers pause mid-stride, their synchronized waves breaking into a ripple of crystalline confusion that spreads outward from the basin like a stone dropped in mercury. The Swarm-Kin, wheeling in their clouds that darken the sky, suddenly bank toward the light, their collective wingbeats shifting from a thunderous drone to a questioning, harmonic stutter. The Hollow-Dwellers in the deep, resonant chambers feel a new vibration—a frequency that does not come from stone or wind, but from the Mat's sudden, startled recognition of its own unity. The subsonic drone in the caverns becomes a chord, resolved, complete.

For one breath—the slow, tidal breath of the plateau, the rise and fall of the spongy crust—the Chorus knows itself.

It is not a human consciousness. It is not even an animal one. It is the awareness of a place realizing it is a being, the sudden, vertiginous understanding that *I am the space between. I am the sound in the silence. I am the hollow that holds the world.* The Power feels it through the hyphae, a wave of crystalline clarity spreading through the mycelial network, a moment of pure, distributed sentience holding its breath in the dark.

Then the moment passes. The Grazers resume their rolling thunder. The swarms darken the sky again into a single beating wing. The wind moans through the ancient arches. But something has shifted, a new harmonic in the eternal song. The pool remains. It does not evaporate, though the cycle of mist continues its eternal rhythm. It is a new organ in the body of the Hollow—a navel, a mirror, a point of self-reference, a place where the emptiness can finally look at itself and not see void.

The Architects' stone circles, half-swallowed by centuries of lichen, seem to orient themselves toward this new center, as if remembering a geometry that was always meant to include a heart. The green glow in the basins now pulses in a rhythm that matches not merely the Mat's breathing, but something slower—a heartbeat of acknowledgment, a silent *thank you* sent upward into the warm, heavy air.

The Power withdraws, not as a departure but as a dispersal—spreading back into the humidity, the thermal currents, the vast and patient sky. It leaves no commandments scratched into stone, no alterations to the erosion, no new species to disrupt the delicate machine of moisture and sound. It leaves only the pool, and the memory in the Mat's dreaming dark that it was once seen, once known, once loved not for what it produces or consumes, but for what it is: a miracle of absence, a cathedral built of air and resonance and the slow, patient digestion of mountains.

The High Hollow continues to breathe. The Mat rises and falls beneath invisible feet. The sound roars on, deafening, overwhelming. But now, when the mist rolls in thickest and the green light glows brightest, there is a single point on the ancient plateau where the world looks into itself, and for a moment, the silence between the sounds is not empty.

It is full of grace.
