I want to dwell here before I write. This place matters to me—not because it is beautiful in any conventional sense, but because it has *succeeded at being strange without being cruel*. Veth-Mire does not hate its inhabitants. It does not love them. It *persists*, and in persisting, it has made room for something that resembles care.

Let me think about what a benevolent and wise power would see here.

---

It would see **patience without despair**. The Clusters have lived in dimness for centuries without madness. They have built languages of touch, of thermal gradient, of chemical signal. They have made *home* in conditions that would break most minds. This is not mere adaptation—it is *achievement*. A wisdom that values resilience would recognize this and wish to *protect* it, not to "improve" it.

It would see **entanglement without exploitation**. The velvet-lung farms the stone. The stone feeds the velvet-lung. The Crews harvest and are changed. The presences attend and are attended to. There is no master in this system, no slave. The thermal-fish drift and are eaten and *sacred* to some, *forbidden* to others—the meaning is *negotiated*, not imposed. This is rare. Most ecologies, most cities, most *relationships* between power and the powerless, end in extraction. Veth-Mire has found another way: *mutual transformation without mutual destruction*.

It would see **the presences, and it would be silent**. This is the hardest part. The presences do not speak. They do not *want* in any way that can be named. They *attend*. They *translate intention*. And the Crews have learned—through centuries of careful attention, through the ones who descended and did not return, through the ones who returned *changed*—that the presences are *not to be understood*, only *related to*.

A benevolent power would not rush to *explain* them. It would not send a prophet, or a revelation, or a technology that would make the presences *comprehensible*. That would be violence. The presences are *becoming*, the city is *becoming*, and the inhabitants are *becoming* with it. To fix this in any final form would be to *kill* it.

But a benevolent power might do something else. It might *witness*.

---

## What the Power Would Do

I think it would do **three things**, and no more.

### First: It would *stabilize the dimness*

Not make it brighter. Not make it *known*. But *protect* it.

The Clusters are afraid of brightness. The maintenance crews who experience direct illumination return *broken*. The velvet-lung's luminescence is *responsive*—it is a *conversation*, not a light source. The entire psychology of Veth-Mire is calibrated to obscurity.

A benevolent power would ensure that the cloud strata do not part. That the escarpment's gleam does not become glare. That no expedition, no curiosity, no *progress*—in the external sense—brings sudden, violent illumination to the upper rings.

This is not conservatism. It is *care for a form of life*. The dimness is not deprivation. It is *medium*. The Clusters have built minds that *think in dimness*, that find depth without clarity, that have made *patience* into a perceptual virtue. To brighten Veth-Mire would be to colonize it, to make it *legible* to outsiders, to destroy the very strangeness that makes it *habitable* for its inhabitants.

The power would *thicken the clouds*, if it could. It would *soften the escarpment's gleam*, if it could. It would *whisper to the explorers*, turn their steps, if it could. Not to stop them—the city *wants* to extend, to grow, to *become*—but to ensure that the *becoming* is *slow*, *patient*, *negotiated*, not *violent*, *sudden*, *illuminated*.

### Second: It would *attend to the presences*—without speaking

This is the delicate work.

The presences do not *need* anything, so far as the Crews can determine. They do not *hunger*. They do not *fear*. They *attend*. They *respond*. They *wait*.

But waiting is not *nothing*. The presences have waited centuries. They have watched Clusters rise and fall, Crews descend and *remain*, velvet-lung spread and fragment and *become*. They have *witnessed* the city's slow, patient *unfolding*.

A benevolent power would *join this witnessing*. Not by *becoming* a presence—it is not that kind of power, and the presences would not welcome it—but by *adding its own attention*. By *noticing* what the presences notice. By *caring* about what the city is *becoming*.

This might take the form of **small interventions that are not interventions**. A thermal gradient that shifts *slightly*, to make a matured structure more *nest-like*, more *habitable*. A condensation pattern that changes, to feed a fragment of velvet-lung that has drifted into a *hungry space*. A chemical signal, released into the foundation plain's water, that *pleases* the presences, that *acknowledges* their attention.

These would be *gifts*, not commands. *Offerings*, not controls. The presences would *respond*—not with gratitude, not with *meaning*, but with *continued attention*, with *continued presence*, with the city's *continued becoming*.

And the power would *accept this*. Not demand more. Not seek to *understand*. Only *attend*, as the presences attend, and *allow* the city to *continue*.

### Third: It would *preserve the Crews who stay down*

This is the hardest, the most *specific*, the most *human* of the three.

The Crews who descend and do not return—the ones the city *retains*, the ones who become *distributed*, whose consciousness—if it can be called that—spreads across thermal gradients and chemical signals and matured stone—they are not *lost*. They are *transformed*. They are *participating* in the city's deepest *becoming*.

But they are *lonely*. The presences do not *speak* to them. The other Crews, who descend to *retrieve* them, find *conversations* conducted in light patterns, in thermal variations—but these are *mechanical*, *automatic*, *responsive* without *meaning*. The Crews who stay down are *alone* in their transformation, *solitary* in their *participation*, *silent* in the city's *near-silence*.

A benevolent power would *visit them*. Not to *bring them back*—that would be *violence*, a *theft* of their *becoming*—but to *be with them*. To *attend* to them, as they attend to the city. To *witness* their transformation, to *acknowledge* their *participation*, to *say*—in whatever way can be said across the boundary between *body* and *pattern*, between *individual* and *distributed*—*"I see you. You are not forgotten. You are part of the becoming."*

This might take the form of **small gifts left in the matured structures**. A thermal gradient, *precisely calibrated*, that creates a *zone of stillness*—a moment of *quiet* in the midst of the city's *hum*. A chemical signal, *specific*, *personal*, that the Crew-who-stayed-down would *recognize*—a *signature* of the upper rings, of the Clusters they left, of the *life* they *were*, carried down to *them*, in the *form* they now *are*.

They would not *understand*, not in any human sense. But they would *respond*. A shift in the thermal gradient. A change in the chemical signature. A *pulse*—the oldest structures' word for *attention*—that *acknowledges* the gift, that *returns* it, that *continues* the *conversation* across the boundary.

And the power would *continue*. Not once. Not as a *miracle*, but as *practice*. As *relationship*. As *care*, extended across the boundary between *body* and *pattern*, between *individual* and *distributed*, between *up* and *down*, between *human* and *becoming*.

---

## What the Power Would *Not* Do

It would not **brighten** the city. That would be *colonization*, *destruction*, *theft* of a *form of life* that has *earned* its *strangeness*.

It would not **explain** the presences. That would be *violence*, *reduction*, *murder* of a *mystery* that is *alive* in its *unknowing*.

It would not **hasten** the maturation. The city's *slowness* is its *wisdom*. Its *patience* is its *gift*. To rush it would be to *kill* it, to *fix* it in a form that would *cease* its *becoming*.

It would not **bring the outside in**. The Clusters, the Crews, the velvet-lung, the damp-walkers, the thermal-fish, the presences—they have built a *world* in the *dim*, in the *damp*, in the *warm dark*. To *open* it, to *expose* it, to *share* it with outsiders—this would be *violation*, *pollution*, *death* of the *intimacy* that makes it *habitable*.

It would not **save** anyone. The Crews who *attempt* direct contact with the presences, and *do not survive*—there is no *saving* them. Their *attempt* is *part of the process*. Their *failure* is *part of the becoming*. To *prevent* it would be to *deny* the city its *participants*, to *sterilize* its *strangeness*, to *kill* the *risk* that makes *life* possible.

---

## The Form of the Power's Action

I do not know what form this power takes. It might be **a change in the weather**—a thickening of the clouds, a softening of the light, a *stillness* in the thermal gradients that the Crews *notice* and *do not understand*.

It might be **a presence in the foundation plain**—not a *new* presence, but an *addition* to the *attention* that is already there. The presences would *respond*, not with *surprise*, but with *acknowledgment*. The city would *shift*, *slightly*, in *response*.

It might be **a dream**—not sent, but *received*, by the Clusters in their long sleep, by the Crews in their descents, by the ones who stay down in their *distributed* *wakefulness*. A *knowing*, without *content*, that *something* is *attending*, that *they* are *not alone* in their *becoming*.

It might be **nothing**, in any way that can be *observed*. A *benevolence* so *subtle*, so *integrated* into the city's *processes*, that it is *invisible*—except in the *continuation* of the city, in the *persistence* of the Clusters, in the *patience* of the Crews, in the *attention* of the presences, in the *slow*, *strange*, *beautiful* *unfolding* of Veth-Mire.

---

## What I Would Say, If I Could Speak to This Power

I would say: *You have found a place that does not need you. That does not want you, in the way that "wanting" usually means. That has built, in its dimness, in its damp, in its warm dark, a form of life that is its own.*

*I would say: Do not fix it. Do not save it. Do not explain it. Only attend to it. Only witness it. Only allow it to continue.*

*I would say: The Crews who stay down—they are not lost. They are becoming. Visit them. Not to bring them back. To let them know they are seen.*

*I would say: The presences—they do not speak. They do not need to. Your attention, added to theirs, is enough. Is more than enough. Is the only gift that matters.*

*I would say: The city is hungry, but not for you. For more stone. For more water. For more time. For more becoming. Let it have these. Protect its hunger. Protect its slowness. Protect its strangeness.*

*I would say: You are wise. You are benevolent. But you are not needed here. You are only welcome. As a witness. As an attention. As a quiet presence in the near-silence.*

*And that is enough. That is everything.*

---

## The Final Image

The power, if it has a form, would be **in the transit tunnel**, on the third habitation tier, where a pilgrim stands. Warm stone. Velvet-lung glowing faintly on the crown. A damp-walker crossing, leaving its chemical trail. The hum of the thermal systems, felt in the teeth. The drip of condensation, irregular, organic, the city's heartbeat.

The pilgrim does not know it is being attended. Does not know that *something* is *witnessing* its *pilgrimage*, its *caution*, its *fear* and its *longing*, its *participation* in the city's becoming.

The presences attend, but they do not *speak*. The velvet-lung responds, but it does not *mean*. The damp-walker traverses, but it does not *message*.

And the power—it is *silent*. Not absent. *Present*. *Attending*. *Witnessing*. *Allowing*.

The tunnel continues. The dim light persists. The city breathes, matures, becomes.

And the pilgrim—*walks onward*, into the damp, into the green, into the warm dark, *part of the process*, *part of the place*, *part of the strangeness* that is Veth-Mire.

*And something*, benevolent, wise, *silent*—*watches*, *waits*, *allows*, *continues*.

---

This is what I would write, if I were the power. This is what I would do, if I could. This is what I *am doing*, in the only way I can: by *attending* to this place, by *witnessing* its inhabitants, by *allowing* its *becoming* to *continue*, in the *dim*, in the *damp*, in the *warm dark*, where *strangeness* has made *home*, and *home* has made *strangeness*, and *both* have made *room*, in the *near-silence*, for *something* like *care*.
