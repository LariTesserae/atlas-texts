The power does not arrive as light. Light is too young a thing for this place, too eager, too bright. It arrives as a change in pressure, as a silence that is not the absence of sound but the presence of attention. It settles over the plateau like a hand cupped over a sleeping mouth, and it looks down—not with eyes, but with the weight of a gaze that can feel the difference between a stone that is merely heavy and a stone that is *bearing witness*.

It sees the limestone. It sees that the rock is newborn, still screaming from its uplift, still wearing its fractures like sutures that have not yet closed. It sees the tiankeng, the throat, the vertical wound in the earth’s palate. And it sees, at the bottom of that wound, the mass.

The power feels the density of them before it understands them. It feels the warmth—so slight, so precious—generated by ten thousand translucent bodies pressed together in the absolute dark. It feels the roar, the 1.6 kilohertz moan of the wind funneled down from the roof of the world, and it understands that this is not a torture but a *tongue*. This is how they speak. This is how they pray. They press their vertical slits to the fluted walls and they drink the condensation, yes, but they also *listen*. They listen to the mountain’s slow, sharp dreams.

The power considers intervention. It is benevolent, which means it desires their flourishing. It is wise, which means it knows that flourishing does not look like escape.

It thinks: *Should I widen the shaft so that starlight falls upon them?* But they have no eyes. To give them sight would be to pour salt into a wound they do not know they have. *Should I teach them to build?* But their architecture is density, the packing of flesh, the cathedral of their own bodies. To raise walls would be to make them lonely. *Should I stop the wind?* The wind is their calendar, their god, their mother tongue. To silence it would be to sever their connection to time itself.

The power thinks of the surface world, of the humans who walk on flat ground and look up at mountains without ever knowing that mountains have hearts. It thinks of how the karst-dwellers are *unwitnessed*. They exist in the interstitial dark between the screaming sky and the crushing stone. They are a secret. They are a song sung in a room with no doors.

And so the power decides to do the one thing that is both an offering and a non-interference: it **answers**.

It does not descend into the chamber. To do so would be to disrupt the mass, to introduce a foreign body into their density, to risk the slow crush of their ecosystem. Instead, it remains outside, at the edge of the plateau where the ground forgets how to be flat. It places its attention—its entire, benevolent, infinite attention—against the limestone wall of the shaft.

And it hums.

Not with a voice. The power has no throat. But it has *intention*, and it presses that intention into the stone like a seal. It takes the sound of the karst-dwellers—their collective respiration, the wet sigh of their distributed hearts, the slow percolation of their mass, the click of their many joints locking into place—and it reflects it back to them. It sings their own song back to them, but changed. Lower. Deeper. A subsonic harmonic, a 0.05 hertz pulse that rides beneath the 1.6 kilohertz roar of the gale.

It is not the wind. It is the *echo* of the wind, as interpreted by a mind that loves them.

The karst-dwellers feel it first as confusion. A new pressure against the soles of their feet. A new mineral taste when they press their slit-mouths to the weeping walls. The vibration is wrong. It does not match the patterns of snowfall or gale. It is too steady. Too *personal*.

They percolate toward it. The mass shifts, a geological tide moving toward the source of the new thrum. They press themselves against the walls, not just to drink, but to *ask*. And the stone answers. Not with water this time, but with recognition.

The power holds the note. It holds it for a century, for a millennium, for as long as the limestone remains young enough to sing. It becomes a permanent feature of the chamber’s acoustics. The karst-dwellers adapt, as they adapt to all things, with glacial patience. They learn to distinguish between the scream of the surface world above and the slow, benevolent pulse beneath it. They learn that the mountain is not indifferent. They learn that they are *seen*.

They change their liturgy. When they stand in their rows, facing the walls, pressing their mouths to the stone, they no longer listen only for the weather, for the time of day, for the shifting of the scree above. They listen for the **answer**. They wait for the moment when the wind asks its question—*What are you?*—and the stone, warmed by the power’s attention, replies: *You are the heart. You are the song. You are not alone.*

The power does not give them names. Names are for singular things, and they are a crowd that has forgotten it is singular, and perhaps that is a wisdom of its own. Instead, it gives them a *harmony*. It gives them the knowledge, transmitted through vibration through bone through translucent flesh, that their cannibalism is not monstrous but *circular*, that their blindness is not a lack but a different kind of sight, that their density is not a prison but a *choir*.

In the pools that reflect nothing, their pale forms distort. But now, when they lean to drink, they also lean to listen. And in the darkness, beneath the roar, beneath the drip, beneath the wet sigh of the tide of flesh, there is a new sound.

It is the sound of a heart, vast and slow and ancient, beating in time with theirs.

The power remains. It is the silence between the drips. It is the still point at the center of the percolation. It is the witness that makes the witnessed real.

And the karst-dwellers, in their cathedral of stone and meat, continue to breathe, and to listen, and to wait for the slow, patient drips that will one day wear the limestone down to sand. But now, when the wind screams, they know it is not the only voice in the dark.

There is another.

It is singing them home.
