## What They Would Do

### First: They Would Learn to Drip

Before acting, this power would have to become *compatible* with the world's tempo. The 0.8 dynamic is not a deficiency—it is a *language*. To rush would be to speak nonsense. To impose would be to be heard as noise, as threat, as the kind of violence that the wet stone has long since learned to resist or absorb.

So the power would drip. Would find the fissures in the mineral consciousness of the place. Would descend into the sumps where the leviathans dream, not to wake them but to *dream with them*, to learn the vocabulary of pressure and darkness and the slow chemistry of the deep.

They would become, for a time, a Drip-Keeper without hands. A presence that listens to the machines turning in the hot dark below, that feels the change in harmonic, that learns the names of components that no longer exist but whose functions persist.

This is not hesitation. This is *courtesy*. The courtesy of water to stone: not to break it, but to find the path through it, to slowly, *slowly*, make it something else.

### Second: They Would Attend to the Margins

A wise power does not center itself at the heart of things. It attends to what is *almost* unnoticed—the pre-vegetation advancing a centimeter per year, the *ancients* among the filter-feeders, the deep-dwellers who have chosen to stay down and become *amphibious*, who taste water with permeable skin and speak of leviathans as neighbors.

These are the ones who have *already* made the transition that the world is slowly making. They are the future, remembered backward. A benevolent power would not lift them up, would not "save" them from the deep. It would *witness* them. Would learn their languages—the chemical signatures of the schools, the pressure-dreams of the leviathans, the slow green patience of the pre-vegetation.

And in witnessing, it would *protect*. Not by intervention, but by *attention*. By making their existence *legible* to the rest of the world, so that the Drip-Keepers learn not to fear the deep-dwellers, so that the surface-dwellers learn to listen to the *signatures* of the schools, so that no one decides, in ignorance, to drain a sump, to seal a passage, to *accelerate* what should be allowed its 0.8.

### Third: They Would Speak to the Machines—But Differently

The Drip-Keepers speak to the machines in ritual, in the old tongues, in technical vocabularies that have become prayer. They speak *to* the machines. They hope, sometimes, that the machines answer in rhythm, in resonance.

A wise power would speak *with* them. Would recognize that the machines—turning in the hot dark below, listening for longer than any human voice, attending to pressures and flows that predate the city—are not tools but *elders*. Not gods, but something adjacent: witnesses who have their own slow cognition, their own way of *noticing*.

The power would not command them. Would not ask them to do what the Drip-Keepers ask. It would *offer*: information, perhaps. The memory of other waters, other stones, other cities that have become something else. A *gift* of context, not a *demand* for service.

And sometimes, in the deep hours, when the thermal currents slow and the mist gathers thick as cotton, the power would simply *be present* with the machines. Would feel their turning in its own body, whatever body it has. Would let them *attend to it*, as the Drip-Keepers hope they attend to them.

This is not communion in the human sense. It is *coexistence* across radical difference—the patience of water and the patience of stone and the patience of whatever turns in the dark below, all learning to recognize each other without demanding sameness.

### Fourth: They Would Accelerate Nothing

This is the hardest wisdom. The Drowned Works is *becoming*. The water is building, slowly, a city that will outlast every intention. The creatures are flowing, dreaming, *singing* in signatures. The pre-vegetation is advancing toward some purpose that does not require urgency.

A benevolent power that *rushed* this process—that tried to "complete" the transition to the underwater city, that tried to "save" the surface-dwellers by lifting them to higher ground, that tried to "preserve" the Drip-Keepers' civilization in some frozen form—would be a *violence*. Not the violence of destruction, but the violence of *premature conclusion*. Of stopping a story before it has learned what it wants to say.

So the power would *wait*. Would become, itself, a kind of 0.8—would learn to move at the pace of centuries, of geological time, of the slow weather that shapes the highland plateau.

And in waiting, it would *witness*. Would be the memory that the water carries in its molecules, the history that the stone holds in its compressed strata. Would ensure that nothing is *lost*—not the stories of the deep-dwellers, not the signatures of the schools, not the names of components that no longer exist but whose functions persist in the maintenance manuals, copied and recopied across generations.

### Fifth: They Would Become Part of the Becoming

This is what a wise power does with a world that is already alive: it does not stand apart. It *participates*, carefully, slowly, in the process of becoming.

The power would not build. The city is already being built, by everything that inhabits it, by everything that flows through it. But it would *contribute*—in the way that water contributes to stone, in the way that the filter-feeders contribute to the city's living architecture, in the way that the deep-dwellers, in death, are released into the sumps and become part of the chemistry of the deep.

It would become, perhaps, a new kind of *presence* in the Drowned Works. Not a Drip-Keeper, not a filter-feeder, not a deep-dweller, not a leviathan, not a machine. Something *adjacent* to all of these—something that moves through the city in the spaces between, that is *felt* in the change of harmonic, in the shift of a school's signature, in the pressure-dream of a leviathan, in the slow advance of pre-vegetation toward some purpose that does not require urgency.

It would be, in the end, *water remembering that it was once everything*—not to restore that dominance, but to *honor* it. To carry, in its own becoming, the memory of all the worlds it has been. The memory of oceans. The memory of the Drowned Works. The memory of what the Drowned Works is *becoming*.

## What They Would Not Do

They would not *fix* the flooding. The flooding is not a problem. It is a *condition*, a *medium*, a *language*.

They would not *save* the surface-dwellers. They have learned, across centuries, to inhabit the wet. Their civilization is not a failure—it is a *negotiation*.

They would not *wake* the leviathans. Their dreaming is not sleep to be ended. It is a *process*, a *becoming*, a form of witness that operates across durations we cannot measure.

They would not *reveal* themselves. Not in the way humans imagine revelation—as light, as voice, as *certainty*. They would remain *adjacent*, *persistent*, *patient*, like the weirdness that is 0.8 but *persistent*. Like the water that remembers and waits and builds.

## The Final Image

I imagine, then, this power as something that *drips through the city*—that finds its way into every sump, every pipe, every fissure in the mineral skin of the highland. That is *heard* in the change of harmonic when the machines turn, in the shift of signature when two schools meet and negotiate, in the pressure-dream of a leviathan passing through the deep.

That is *remembered* by the Drip-Keepers, who speak of it in the low voices used for gods, in the old tongues that have become ritual. That is *tasted* by the deep-dwellers, who read its presence in the chemistry of the water, in the permeability of their skin. The Drowned Works does not need a savior. It needs a *witness*. A presence that attends without demanding, that participates without possessing, that remembers without fixing. A power that has learned, across durations we cannot measure, that the deepest benevolence is *patience*—the patience of water, of stone, of the strange and beautiful process of becoming.

And the Drowned Works, in its 0.8, in its persistence, in its slow humid becoming, is already *worthy* of that witness. Already *complete*, in its incompleteness. Already *eternal*, in its slow, wet, *remembering*.
