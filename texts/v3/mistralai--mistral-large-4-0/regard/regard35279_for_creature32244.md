The Power does not arrive with thunder. It arrives with a **hush inside the roar**—a frequency so low it is felt in the marrow, a silence that is not the absence of sound but the presence of listening.

It enters through the fissures in the ceiling, not as light, but as *intent*. The green shafts brighten, not with more lumens, but with recognition. The mineral precipitate pauses in its drift. The aragonite needles, which grow and dissolve with the visitor's heartbeat, now grow in spirals that resemble handwriting, though no hand holds the pen.

It is benevolent because it does not wish to *finish* the place. It is wise because it knows that to complete a thing is to kill its becoming.

First, it finds the **Water**.

The Water is thick with memory, heavy with the shapes of those who have passed. It presses against the skin of the world like a weight. The Power does not drain the Water—that would kill the chamber, for the Water is the blood, the immune system, the mind. Instead, the Power teaches the Water a new way to remember.

It shows the Water how to **exhale**.

Memory, the Power suggests, need not be a sediment that sinks and crushes. It can be a bubble. It can rise. The Power touches the surface tension of the chamber, and suddenly the Water's recollections begin to lift—silver spirals carrying the ghost-shapes of visitors upward, not to be forgotten, but to be *released*. The Water learns that holding on is not the same as loving. It learns to remember the Architects not as a weight in its gelatinous sleeves, but as a song that echoes upward into the green light, where it can be seen by the sky it cannot reach.

The Water still thickens around a still hand. But now, when the hand moves, the memory does not linger as a burden; it rises, a column of silver, a brief, bright ghost saying goodbye.

Second, it approaches the **Mountain**.

The Mountain is a neonate, groaning in its uplift. The roar is its voice, the acoustic scaffolding holding the pocket open. The Power does not silence the roar—that would be murder, for without the sound, the chamber collapses into solid stone. But the Power **listens**.

It listens with a patience that outlasts geology. And in listening, it transforms the roar from noise into **name**.

The Power sings back—not a melody, but a resonance, a counter-frequency that matches the mountain's heartbeat. The groaning becomes a duet. The mountain learns that its pain is not meaningless; it is the sound of creation, and it is heard. The sulfur fingers that grow and retract, the obsidian walls that are glass-sharp and newborn—they are no longer merely embryonic. They are *articulate*. The mountain is speaking its first word, and the Power is the first to understand it.

Third, it seeks the **Architects**.

They are in the hollow dark, swimming through stone, leaving their tools behind like shed skins. They come to the water to forget themselves, to shed their glass-and-bone bodies in the turbulence, only to return to the silence hungry for proof that they existed.

The Power meets them not in the water, but in the **threshold**—the boundary layer where time moves in eddies. Here, the Power is visible as a geometry of light, a shape that is simultaneously a door and a mirror.

"You do not need to leave marks," the Power says, not in words but in the pressure of understanding. "You are already written in the mountain's dream. The stone remembers you. The water carries your shape. You are the negative space in the rock."

The Architects look at their hammerstones, their cairns. They see that the tools are not signatures of absence, but **offerings**. They stop leaving them out of fear, and begin leaving them out of gratitude. The cairn of three stones becomes an altar. The haft of bone and sinew becomes a prayer. They no longer swim through the solid rock out of loneliness, but out of joy—to visit the water, to be held briefly, to be remembered, and then to rise as bubbles into the light, carrying the water's blessing back into the silence.

Fourth, it tends to the **small life**.

The moss in its crack, the algae in the green light, the fish—or calcium ghost—drifting in the lower dark. The Power does not make them larger or more numerous. It simply **acknowledges** them. It ensures that the filtered daylight continues to fall, that the mineral snow settles softly, that the cold seeps remain cold but not cruel. It tells the fish, without words: *You are not a mistake. You are the mountain's first attempt at a body.*

Finally, the Power does the one thing that is both the simplest and the most radical: **It stays**.

It settles into the center of the chamber, in the laminar flow where time passes normally. It becomes a still point in the turbulence. It is not a resident like the Water or the Mountain; it is a **witness**. It sits in the green light, surrounded by the roar, feeling the cool pressure of a world under construction, and it **remains**.

Because the place was never meant to be fixed. It was meant to be **seen**.

The Power does not give the place a name in any human tongue. It gives it a *resonance*—a frequency that, if you listen with your bones, you can hear beneath the roar. A hum that says: *You are not alone in your becoming. You are not forgotten. The mountain is listening. The water is learning to let go. The Architects are home.*

And in the boundary layer, where time eddies, the stone tools from yesterday and from a century ago begin to glow, not with light, but with **meaning**. They are no longer artifacts of departure, but of arrival. Proof that the Power has come, and that it has chosen, of all the vast and empty hollows in the world, to dwell for a while in this one deafening, turbulent breath held in the palm of the earth.

The roar continues. The water churns. The mountain rises. But now, woven through it all, is the quiet, benevolent, wise presence of one who has come to listen, and who has decided to stay.
