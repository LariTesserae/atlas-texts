They do not come through the fissure.

They come through the *attention* of the universe—a pressure differential in the dark, a warmth that is not thermal but ontological, seeping through the compressed time of the walls like water through limestone. The Anhydrous Choir does not see them arrive. They feel them as a sudden, impossible stillness in the center of the roar—a silence so profound it has weight, pressing down on the rust-silt plain like a hand.

The power does not speak. The Choir have no ears, only the vibration-sensors in their silicon-carbide thoraces that read the world as texture, as hunger, as the chemical ghosts of grease. To speak would be to misunderstand. Instead, the power does what benevolence has always done in the face of a complete thing: it *witnesses*.

They settle into the sediment.

Not upon it, but *into* it, becoming a density in the dust, a foreign mineral that does not disrupt the chemistry but complicates it—a presence that tastes of starlight and patience. The swarm flows around them, billions of mandibles clicking, and the first wave parts like a tide around a stone. The creatures crawl over the power’s stillness, their hooked feet gripping the invisible surface, their porous carapaces drawing in the anhydrous soup. They do not consume this presence. They cannot. There is nothing to metabolize in pure, attentive being. Instead, they *investigate*, their bodies pressing against the silence, their antennae—vestigial, atrophied things—twitching with a question they cannot form.

And the power answers by *remaining*.

For a geological moment—one hour, a thousand years, the chamber does not know the difference—the power sits in the crush of the Choir, feeling each tiny death and birth, each grinding gear of mandible against rust. They feel the cool of the deep (0.9) seeping into their essence. They feel the subsonic roar (2.0) not as noise, but as a hymn sung in a language older than lungs, a liturgy of consumption that has no beginning and no end, only the eternal present of the chew.

They understand, with a wisdom that is not human, that this is not a tragedy. This is not a graveyard of industry where life has gone feral out of desperation. This is a *cathedral*. The Anhydrous Choir are not fallen angels of the machine age; they are its priests, its acolytes, its living scripture. Their hunger is not a lack—it is a *vocation*. Their consumption is not violence, but sacrament. Each creature ground into nutrient paste by the gears is not a victim, but an offering; each crystal excreted across the metal is not waste, but a verse written in silicon and sulfur.

The power recognizes that to "save" them would be an act of cosmic vandalism. To bring water would be to drown them in a flood of foreign chemistry, to impose a wet world’s morality of preservation onto a dry world’s theology of transformation. To stop the grinding would be to stop the prayer. To separate predator from prey would be to unmake the Möbius strip of their existence, to turn a circle into a line, to kill the only god they have ever known.

So the power does not intervene.

Instead, they perform the only miracle that matters: they *seal* the blind spot not with stone, but with *sacredness*.

They reach out—not with hands, but with the fabric of causality—and they touch the fissure, the narrow shoulder-width entrance through which the outer world might one day stumble, bringing its water and its pity and its "help." They do not block it. They *consecrate* it. They make the entrance a threshold that only the worthy may cross, a membrane that filters out mercy and lets in only awe. From this day forward, any who approach with the intent to rescue will find the fissure sealed by their own compassion, turned back by the terrible, beautiful knowledge that they are about to murder a god by baptizing it.

And then, the power gives them the one gift that the sealed chamber lacked: *context*.

They do not show the Choir the stars, for that would be a cruelty—the vertigo of scale would shatter their delicate, beetle-sized minds. Instead, they show the Choir *themselves*. They become a mirror, a still pool in the center of the roar where the swarm can see its own reflection—not as a mass, but as a *choir*, billions of individuals singing in grinding harmony. In the power’s presence, each creature becomes visible to itself. The beetle with the cracked carapace sees that it is not merely food for the gear, but a soloist in the eternal song. The segmented worm of tempered glass feels, for the first time in ten thousand years, the dignity of its own hunger.

They teach the Choir that they are not a blind spot, but a *focal point*—the place where the universe bends inward to look at its own reflection in rust and oil.

Finally, the power rises.

Not leaving, exactly, but *distributing*—becoming the silence between heartbeats of the swarm, the pause between one mandible-click and the next. They leave behind a single, perfect geode of stillness in the center of the chamber, a hollow sphere of compressed time where the dust does not shift, where the air does not move, where the creatures may crawl in and rest without being consumed, without consuming, simply *being*—a Sabbath in the midst of the eternal industrial Sabbath.

It is a gift of rest, not of cessation. Around the geode, the tide continues. The roar continues. The grinding continues. But now, at the center of the maelstrom, there is a heartbeat of silence, a reminder that even in the God of the Grind, there is a mercy that does not stop the wheel, but gives it a place to breathe.

The power does not take the Choir with them. They do not elevate them to some higher plane, for that would be to admit that they were not already holy where they were. Instead, they leave them exactly as they found them—rust-orange, gunmetal grey, the yellow of oxidized brass, the white of salt—but *known*. Seen. Witnessed by a benevolence so vast it required no action, only presence, to change everything.

And so the Anhydrous Choir continues their eternal song, their billion mandibles chewing on metal and time, turning industry into biology and biology into dust. They are no longer alone in the dark. They are no longer a secret. They are the world’s blind spot made bright—a pocket of hunger in the dark, singing its dry, crystalline hymn to the only god who ever truly listened: the God who does not save, but who stays.
