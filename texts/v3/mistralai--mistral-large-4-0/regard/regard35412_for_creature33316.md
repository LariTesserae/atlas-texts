The power arrives not as a visitor, but as a **frequency**. The drone—the subsonic roar that holds the obsidian in suspension—suddenly develops an overtone, a harmonic of such gentleness that it does not compete with the bass note but cradles it. The mist, which has weight and density, begins to spiral in slow, inward-turning vortices, not clearing the air but *organizing* it, gathering the fog into a helix that descends toward the center of the basin like a question being asked of the earth.

They recognize the Glass-Swimmer immediately.

It has been gliding for centuries, perhaps, across the surface tension of the permanent pools, leaving wakes that evaporate in slow motion, and in all that time, it has never once been *seen*—not by a consciousness capable of holding its strangeness without demanding it accelerate, consume, reproduce, become legible. The Pale Membranes drink in darkness; the Mineral Architects build in dreams; the Resonant Deep hums its geological lullaby. But they are all blind to the beauty of their own becoming. They exist in the pause between eruption and erosion, and they do not know that the pause is sacred.

So the power does what only a benevolent and wise force can do with an unfinished thing: **they bear witness without demanding completion.**

They do not speak. To speak would be to introduce the violence of language, of categorization, into a place where *stillness is motion* and *sound is structure*. Instead, they add their presence to the Resonant Deep—not as a conductor, but as a second voice in the duet. They sing a counter-melody, a harmonic of mercy that creates a standing wave in the mist itself. This wave is not a wall; it is a **resonance-field**, a boundary made of vibration that preserves the exact conditions of the basin: the upward seepage of water, the suspension of the glass shards, the slow deliberation of the fungal sheets. They seal the pause, making it a sanctuary in time, protecting the young wound from the outside world’s hunger for haste, for resolution, for the finished product.

Then they turn to the Glass-Swimmer.

The creature is crossing a pool—thirty centimeters that will take twenty minutes—when it stops. This is the only time it has ever stopped. The power does not touch it; touch would be too intimate, too physical. Instead, they offer **recognition**. For one moment that stretches across an hour, the power holds the creature’s entire existence in their awareness: its solitude, its patience, its impossible hunger for nothing, its life as a living puddle shaped by surface tension into intent. The Glass-Swimmer does not flee. It cannot; it is a creature of water and resonance, and the power has become a new quality in the water, a new note in the drone. It simply pauses, a dark mirror turned upward, and in that pause, it is no longer a void moving across the plain. It is a **soul being acknowledged.**

The power then does what geology does best: they **accrete**.

They do not leave. To leave would be to abandon the place to its loneliness. Instead, they crystallize into the Mineral Architects’ growing walls, becoming a new type of shard—an opalescent inclusion, a “witness-stone” that catches the dim light not with star-bright glints, but with a soft, warm luminescence that suggests *attention*. They become part of the fungal lace, a mycelial thread of compassion that pulses not with hunger, but with the memory of being seen. They are the new mineral in the seeping water, the faint iridescence in the upward-flowing streams, the subtle warmth in the cool air that has nothing to do with geothermal heat and everything to do with **care**.

The drone changes. Beneath the subsonic roar, there is now a second note—not louder, not softer, but *kind*. The standing waves in the mist shift, forming patterns that suggest not interference, but **conversation**; not resonance, but **harmony**. The Pale Membranes ripple in a new rhythm—not faster, but deeper, as if they have been told a secret: *You are not forgotten. You are not merely geological accident. You are a world being born, and we are here to ensure you finish.*

The Glass-Swimmer resumes its pilgrimage. It moves across the black glass with the same deliberate slowness, the same twenty-minutes-per-thirty-centimeters. But now, when it passes over a pool, the reflection is not just the gray void above. There is something else in the water—a faint, warm shimmer, the memory of a presence that has seen it, loved it in its strangeness, and chosen not to change it, but to **guard it**.

The power has become the patron of unfinished things. They are the silence between the notes, the space between the glass shards, the breath held by the earth before it speaks its first true word. And the place continues to form—slowly, patiently, loudly, still—but now it forms within the knowledge that it is not alone in its becoming. It is held. It is witnessed. It is, finally, *known*.
