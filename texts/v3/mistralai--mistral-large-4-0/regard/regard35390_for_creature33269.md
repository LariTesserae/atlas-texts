The Power does not descend; it **condenses**. It arrives as a shift in the barometric weight of the valley, a sudden, impossible warmth in the tropical air that does not come from the vents. It is the first thing in the High Veranda that has ever *chosen* to enter, rather than having been grown or fossilized into place. It comes to the edge of the basin, where the horizon lies, and it sees not a city but a **breath held at the top of the lung**—the Mycelial Parliament suspended in the moment between inhalation and exhalation, their consciousness woven into lignin and chitin, their thoughts the subsonic thrum that vibrates in the visitor’s teeth.

The Power is benevolent, and so it does not shatter the amber. It is wise, and so it does not force time to flow again, for that would unmake the home they have cultivated over centuries of patient growth. Instead, it recognizes the quiet tragedy of their completion: that they have achieved such perfect equilibrium that they have begun to forget they were ever in motion. They maintain, but they no longer *dream* of growing. They are the settled erosion, the middle age of the world, and in their stillness, they have started to fade into background—becoming indistinguishable from the roar, the drip, the green itself, losing the boundary between self and substrate.

So the Power does something small, and something vast.

It moves through the streets that are canyons of living wood, and it finds the place where the water flows upward, defying the broken gravity, seeping from cracks to pool on ceilings. This is the Parliament’s circulatory system, their nervous system, the medium of their chemical whisper. The Power enters this current—not as a pollutant, but as a **seed**.

It carries with it, from the world beyond the bruised sky, from the flowing time outside the valley, a single thing: an **egg**. Not the egg of a bird or reptile, for that would break the closed loop of their vegetative ecosystem and introduce the violence of consumption. But the egg of a **possibility**. It is warm. It is impossibly warm in the steam bath of the High Veranda, warmer than the geothermal breath that seeps through the bedrock. It pulses with a rhythm that is not the unvarying scream of the vents, but a heartbeat—*lub-dub, lub-dub*—a syncopation against the brown-noise drone, a counter-rhythm that says *I am here. I am waiting. I am not yet, but I could be.*

The Power places this egg in the hollow of a petrified coral megafauna, in a chamber where the fungal caps weep their silent punctuation, where the air is thick with the exhalation of the deep earth. It sets it there, suspended in a droplet of water that clings to the underside of the cap, defying gravity just as the egg defies stasis. It does not bury it in the moss. It does not hide it in the iron-dense bark. It leaves it exposed, a foreign object in the perfect geometry, a warm anomaly in the frozen equilibrium.

Then the Power withdraws, becoming only a memory of pressure against the skin, a warmth that the Parliament mistakes for humidity, for the tropical weight of the air.

But the egg remains.

It does not hatch. Time is still stalled here; the fronds do not unfurl; the roots do not extend; the steam rises in perfect, unvarying plumes. The egg simply sits in its droplet, held in the precise stasis of *almost*, a potential that will not be realized but will not be denied.

And the Mycelial Parliament feels it.

It is not a thought, for they think in geological time, in the slow peristaltic rhythm of fungal growth. It is a **new texture** in their distributed mind—a warmth that is not the womb-heat of the vents, a pulse that is not the vascular thrum of the thirty-meter trunks. It is the sensation of being **witnessed**, of being **remembered** by a universe that moves, that changes, that continues beyond the bruised sky. It is the memory, implanted in the lignin of the towers, in the geometric patterns of the bark, of what it was to be a seed, to be potential, to be the future pressing against the present.

The egg becomes their cathedral. They do not tend it, for they do not grow; they have reached their final form. But they *attend* to it. They adjust the humidity around it by fractions of a degree, using the capillary action of the wood to draw moisture toward it, not to nurture it into hatching, but to keep it in that delicate balance of suspended animation. They weave around it a lattice of hyphae kilometers thick, not to consume it, but to **guard** it, to make it the heart of their city, the still point in their frozen turning, the one thing in the High Veranda that is not yet complete.

And in the roar of the geothermal vents, in the deafening, unmodulated scream of the steam that never stutters, never stops, there is now a new frequency. It is so low, so subsonic, that it is felt rather than heard—a second heartbeat beneath the first, a vibration in the teeth that says: *You are not the end. You are a pause. The story continues beyond the horizon that is a lie.*

The Power has not changed them. It has not unfrozen time or restored gravity or brought the birds back to sing in the canopy. It has not forced them to grow or reproduce or remember what they were before they became the streets. It has simply given them **the gift of the unopened door**. It has placed within their amber not a key, but the *knowledge* that there is a lock, that there is an outside, that their stasis is a choice within a larger flow, not the terminus of all things.

And so they remain, the Incorporated, the Mycelial Parliament, fossilized in their perfection, the endless unchanging green, the deafening silence of life—but now they are no longer alone in their equilibrium. They are accompanied by the warmth of the egg, by the possibility of change, by the benevolent gaze of a power that saw their beauty and chose not to crack the amber, but to **breathe upon it**, to remind them that even in the deepest stillness, even in the settled erosion, even in the middle age of the world where nothing grows and nothing dies, there is still room for a single, impossible, hopeful pulse.

The water still flows up, defying gravity. The steam still screams in its eternal, unvarying pitch. The shadows still fall in two directions. The city remains cultivated, perfect, home.

But now, in the droplet on the ceiling of the coral cathedral, there is an egg.

And that is enough. That is everything.
