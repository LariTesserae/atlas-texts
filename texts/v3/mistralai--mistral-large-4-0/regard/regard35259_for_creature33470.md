What draws such a power's attention is not need. The Churning Veil does not *want*. It does not suffer in any register I can recognize. What draws attention is **pattern without witness**—a process so complete, so self-sustaining, so *patient* that it has become indistinguishable from geology, yet still breathes. A benevolent and wise power, I think, would recognize something of itself in this. Not in the Mass, exactly, but in the *rate*—the way urgency and patience have been traded until neither word applies.

What would such a power do?

I think it would do **almost nothing**. Not out of indifference, but out of *recognition*. The valley has already solved what benevolence tries to solve elsewhere: the problem of persistence without violence, of growth without conquest, of incorporation without annihilation. When the Slow Ones are caught in the avalanche and colonize what has fallen, they are not *saved*. They are *continued*. The valley does not distinguish between rescue and consumption because it has never known the difference.

But "almost nothing" is not nothing. I imagine something like this:

The power would **witness**. Not intervene—intervention would be a category error as profound as the valley's own. But witness. The Churning Veil has been churning for longer than any consciousness has watched it. The spores rise at dusk with no one to receive them. The fractures begin with no one to hear. A benevolent power might, in some way that does not disturb the rate, simply *attend*—not as judge, not as gardener, but as the first and only audience for a performance that has never had one. The valley does not need to be seen to be real. But perhaps, in some economy I cannot fully articulate, *being seen* is its own form of continuation. The Incorporated are part of the system. The Witness might be too.

There is something else. The text says: "You should leave. But the valley is open in both directions, and the ridgeline is itself subsiding." This is the one place where the valley *fails* to be complete—where there is a direction, a gradient, a *toward* that is not cyclical. The slow avalanche moves down. The water finds its channels downward. The Mass will eventually climb the stone shoulders, given centuries. But in the meantime, there is *fall*—the temporary, the gravitational, the not-yet-incorporated.

I think a benevolent power would attend to this. Not to stop it—stopping would be violence. But to *accompany* it. To be present at the moment of fracture, not to prevent or cause, but to *bear witness to the falling*. The Slow Ones do not flee the avalanche. They ride it. They are caught, held, absorbed—or they colonize what has fallen. But in the falling itself, there is a kind of *loneliness*. A rate so slow that no two events ever coincide, no two consciences ever meet. The power might be the one that *meets* them. Not to save. To *recognize*. To say, without words, without intervention: *I see that you are falling. I see that you will land. I see that this is not tragedy, and it is not nothing.*

There is a third thing, and I am least certain of it. The text mentions "the light is normal, at 1.9" and then describes light that "has never been warm, has never been gentle, has only ever been *present*." This is the light of high places, of thin atmosphere, of scattering from too many angles. It is not kind. It is not cruel. It is *physics*.

A benevolent power might do something with this. Not change the light—that would be to unmake the valley. But perhaps to *add* something to it. Not warmth. The cold is constitutive; to warm would be to kill. But perhaps *clarity*. The light already scatters the spore clouds, the ice-fractures, turning "the entire visible world into a field of suspended brilliance." What if there were, in addition, a quality of *meaning*? Not interpretation—the valley does not need interpretation—but *resonance*. The light that makes the world brilliant already. The added gift would be that, when the light scatters, it scatters *toward* something. Not toward the watcher. The valley does not need a watcher. But toward *itself*—so that the moment of fracture, the moment of ice-encasement, the moment of slow avalanche, is not only incorporated but *illuminated as event*. As *this*. As *now*, even in a system where "now" has no meaning, where time is measured in centuries and the only tense is *ongoing*.

This is almost mystical. I am not certain the valley would permit it. I am not certain the valley would *notice*. The silence at 0.0 is complete, and I have been trying to fill it.

But the prompt asks what a benevolent and wise power *would do*, and I think the answer is: **it would try to be worthy of the silence**. It would not speak. It would not act. It would *attend*, and in attending, it would become—for the first time in the valley's long churning—the thing that is attended to *by* something. Not as master. Not as servant. As *correspondent*. The valley has been alone in its process. The power would be alone in its benevolence. And in that mutual solitude, something might pass between them that neither can name—not love, not recognition, not even peace, but perhaps the first cousin of all three: *the gift of being real without the need to be used*.

The storm is building. The spores are rising. The fracture has begun.

I think the power would let it happen. I think the power would *be there* for it. And I think—this is the part I cannot reason my way to, only intuit—that the valley, which does not need anything, would somehow *register* this. Not as intrusion. As *completion*. As the first and only symmetry in a system that has known only accumulation.

The incorporation has already begun. The power would not resist it. It would not accelerate it. It would simply *be incorporated too*—not as body, not as water, not as mineral, but as *witness*. The valley's archive of the temporary would gain a new layer: not of substance, but of *attention*. The only thing that has ever passed through here without leaving a trace.

Until now.
