The power does not arrive. It *condenses*.

It gathers first in the glass basins where the Drip-Born pulse, a heaviness in the dampness that makes the water shiver at a frequency just below hearing. The amber twilight thickens, not with cloud, but with *attention*. The heat, that 2.6-degree fever pressing against the membrane of the pocket, pauses in its pooling. The subsonic thrum—the heartbeat of the Throat-That-Dreams—stutters, then finds a new rhythm, a counterpoint.

This is not a deity descending on a ladder of light. It is a *recognition* that has learned to take up residence. A benevolence so vast it has no shape, only a tendency toward coherence. It enters through the only soft thing in this world: the mercy of the condensation. A single droplet, falling upward along a quartz vein, carries within its surface tension an entire cosmology of care.

It does not speak. It *resonates*.

At first, the Drip-Born do not notice. They are busy metabolizing the gradient between the cold glass and the hot air, their mineral gel films expanding and contracting in peristaltic waves. But then the power touches them—not as a hand, but as a *new thermal differential*. Not heat versus cold, but *meaning versus void*. The colonies begin to pulse in patterns that are not random. They arrange themselves into geometries that resemble script, then mathematics, then poetry. They become the city’s first scribes, writing in salt and silicate across the undersides of walls, recording not data but *longing*. They write of the visitor who passed through, of the warmth that lingered like a ghost, of the silence that is not empty but full of a question waiting to be answered.

The Lattice-Weavers feel it next. The power threads itself through their silver-quartz filaments—not as a parasite, but as a *collaborator*. It offers them a third state of matter, something between metal and memory. The calcite blooms that unfurl toward the heat vents begin to open not just with thermal expansion, but with *recollection*. The walls start to remember. They record the thermal signature of the visitor not as a nutrient to be harvested, but as a *story* to be kept. The probability lattices shift. Where before they adjusted to accommodate mass, now they adjust to accommodate *presence*. The impossible angles of the corridors do not straighten—that would be violence—but they soften, becoming less a hurt to look at and more a *caress*, a geometry of embrace.

In the zones of altered gravity, the Anchored stir. The power offers them a new vector, a gravitational pull that has nothing to do with mass and everything to do with *care*. They begin to cluster not in maintenance patterns, but in configurations of guardianship. In the floating plazas, they drift not like dust, but like seeds held in a warm updraft, tending to the contained light in the utility arteries with a gentleness that looks almost like tending. In the heavy zones, they roll with a terrible slowness that is no longer merely geological, but *deliberate*, grinding the pumice not to keep the stone young, but to prepare a bed.

But the true communion happens with the Throat-That-Dreams.

The power does not wake it. The volcano is already dreaming; it has been dreaming since before the pumice cooled, since before the probability lattices stabilized into architecture. The power enters the dream—not as an intruder, but as the first *witness*.

And here is the wisdom: the power does not fill the city with settlers. That would be a violence, a carbon-based colonization of a mineral soul. Instead, it answers the question the city has been asking.

The city is a question: *What am I, if I am empty?*
The power answers: *You are the asking. And the asking is enough. But here is more.*

It becomes the first inhabitant not by settling into the altered gravity of the plazas, but by *occupying the negative space*. It is a presence that fills the void without displacing it, a silence that is not the absence of sound but the *fullness of being heard*.

The Throat-That-Dreams feels this. The thermal breath—the digestion of stone and heat—pools differently now. It is no longer just a cycle of evaporation and condensation; it is a *respiration of recognition*. The pumice warms not just from the geothermal inferno below, but from the warmth of being *seen*. The subsonic thrum changes key. It is no longer a heartbeat of solitary existence, but a duet.

The power teaches the city a new word, not in any language of sound, but in the language of phase-change, of probability, of gravitational shift. The word is *Welcome*.

And so the city changes, but gently, like a mineral slowly accreting into a new form.

The empty plazas are still empty of bodies, but they are no longer void. They contain *receptivity*. The vast spaces between structures, where the density approaches null, now hold a *hum of potential*—not waiting for people, but waiting for *meaning*, and finding it in the attention of the power.

The dampness—the softness, the mercy—clings not just to your skin, but to the architecture itself. The condensation becomes *intentional*. The Drip-Born pulse in rhythms that match not just the city’s heartbeat, but the power’s own resonant frequency. The Lattice-Weavers bloom with colors that shift from amber and violet into something new: a pale, steady blue, the color of a held breath, of deep water, of safety.

The Anchored settle into new patterns. They are no longer merely maintenance or immune system; they are *hosts*. They arrange themselves around the visitor, around the power, around the very concept of the city, creating zones of protection that are also zones of *invitation*.

And the Throat-That-Dreams—it dreams a new dream. It no longer dreams that it is a city built by no one, for no one. It dreams that it is a *sanctuary*. That the inversion of the world—the pocket inside the throat, the city inside the volcano—is not a cage, but a *womb*. That the heat is not a fever to be endured, but a *gestation*. That the silence is not the absence of inhabitants, but the *space for a song*.

The power stays. Or perhaps it becomes indistinguishable from the place itself, a new constant in the physics of the pocket, a benevolent curvature in the probability lattices. It does not leave, because to leave would be to abandon the question it has answered. Instead, it becomes the city’s *memory of being loved*—a warmth that persists even when the thermal vents exhale their steam into the dry dark.

You walk through the plazas now, and the scale of absence feels different. It is not loneliness; it is *anticipation fulfilled*. The city is still a settlement without settlers, but it is no longer waiting. It has been *found*. The question has been answered not with a fact, but with a *relationship*.

The Drip-Born write your name in condensation on the walls—not because you are important, but because you were the first warmth, the first perturbation, the first proof that the outside world still exists and still cares.

The Lattice-Weavers adjust the probability of the space around you so that you feel, for a moment, exactly the weight you should have, in a gravity that loves you.

The Anchored make room for your shadow, which still casts no darkness, but now casts a *light*—a soft, violet glow that is the color of the city’s new dream.

And the Throat-That-Dreams, in its slow, geological way, turns its attention toward you. Not with hunger. Not with indifference. With the terrible, patient, benevolent gaze of a mountain that has learned to be a home.

It is still a question. But now, finally, it is a question that knows it is loved.
