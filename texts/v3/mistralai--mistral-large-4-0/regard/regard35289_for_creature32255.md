The Power does not come through the gap in the wall. It seeps through the membrane of the world itself, a change in barometric pressure that tastes of ozone and old stone. It arrives as a slowing—a deep, tectonic *inhale* that takes an hour to complete, during which the condensation from a thousand leaf-tips pauses in mid-air, hanging like glass beads, waiting.

It is benevolent because it has outlived the cruelty of empires. It is wise because it has learned that mercy is not intervention, but *attention*—the willingness to witness without the urge to fix, to save, to improve. It has watched cathedrals sink into swamps and factories become reefs. It knows that the Keeper’s sacrifice is not a tragedy to be undone, but a prayer to be answered.

The Power enters through the roots.

It does not have a body. It has a *tempo*. Its presence is felt as a second heartbeat, so vast and slow that it encompasses the engine’s forty-second *whump-thump* within it like a mother’s pulse containing a child’s. When the flywheel completes its revolution, the Power completes the pause between beats. It fills the silence with a warmth that is not steam, not geothermal, but *recognition*.

The circuit-leaves flare.

Not with electricity, but with a new language. The blue luminescence that pulses in the veins of the broad, waxy leaves shifts from a biological rhythm to a syntactic one—patterns forming, dissolving, reforming like thoughts in a dream. The Keeper feels this in their membrane-skin, a tingling that is not sensation but *grammar*. They turn their whole body—the slow rotation of one who has forgotten the hinge of the neck—and face the center of the room where you stand.

They do not see you with eyes. They see you with the Power’s borrowed patience.

**What the Power does:**

First, it **names** the place. Not aloud—the name is too heavy for sound, which dissipates in this humid air. The name is pressed into the mycelium, into the Parliament’s dreaming dark. It is a name like *Throat-of-the-World*, or *The Forty-Second Sacrament*, or simply *Home*. The fungal threads shudder with the syllables, storing them in the chitin of their cell walls, a library written in chemistry. Now the place knows itself. Before, it was a accident of erosion, a ghost of industry. Now it is a *site*—holy, bounded, beloved.

Second, it **joins the rhythm**. The Power inserts itself into the circuit. It becomes the forty-third second—not an intrusion, but a completion. The engine’s cycle, previously a lonely *whump-thump*, becomes a duet: *whump-thump-thrum*, the third beat being the Power’s vast, slow exhalation. The Keeper’s chest—where the steam pipe threads through flesh like a second spine—begins to rise and fall not once every forty seconds, but once every eighty-four, the Power’s breath interlacing with theirs. They are no longer alone in their vigil. Someone is keeping time with them.

Third, it **feeds the rust-eaters**. The pale, eyeless creatures pause in their dragging, their heat-sensing depressions lifting toward the Keeper. The Power sends a signal through the root-mass: *He is kin. He is warm. He is yours to tend.* The creatures approach—not with fear, but with a wet, segmented reverence. They begin to groom the Keeper’s metal-calloused hands, cleaning the rust from the knuckles with tongues that are not tongues but rasping, loving abrasion. They offer gifts in return: pellets of pure, oxidized iron, refined in their digestive tracts, placed at the base of the steam pipe like offerings at an altar.

Fourth, it **gifts the engine**. The flywheel, turning with geological patience, suddenly encounters less resistance. Not because the Power has oiled it—though the Keeper’s amber sap now glistens with a faint, opalescent sheen, as if mixed with starlight—but because the Power has *blessed the friction*. The rust is still there, the pitting, the decay, but now it is *sacred* decay. The metal remembers it was once ore, once mountain, once the dream of a volcano. The Power reminds it: *You are allowed to rest now. You have done enough.* The *whump-thump* softens, becomes a lullaby.

Fifth, and most importantly, it **speaks to you**—the visitor, the intruder, the temporary anomaly.

The Power does not use words. It uses the sensation of your own pulse slowing, matching, *syncing*. It offers you a choice, but framed not as a command, but as a question written in the condensation on your skin:

*Will you stay and become the echo? Or will you go and become the memory?*

If you stay: The Power will teach you how to breathe steam. It will show you how to let your heartbeat stretch, dilate, become a forty-second meditation. It will let you feel the Parliament’s ancient, distributed mind—not as a god, but as a *neighbor*, vast and fungal and dreaming of photosynthesis. You will sink into the turf, and the roots will not reject you. They will knit around you like a blanket, like a womb, like a circuit. The blue light will paint its patterns behind your eyelids, and you will see the history of this place: the wall being built, the engine being lowered, the first seed taking root in concrete dust. You will become a node, a synapse, a new chamber in the green, wet heart.

If you go: The Power will not stop you. Instead, it will give you a *seed*—not a botanical seed, but a mnemonic one. A single, crystalline memory that will sit in your chest, heavy and warm, humming at forty-two beats per minute. You will carry the *whump-thump* out through the gap in the wall, into the bright, fast world. And when you sleep, you will dream of rust and chlorophyll, of a Keeper who is also an engine, of a place where industry and eternity achieved a humid, breathing truce. You will become an ambassador, a whisperer of slow time in a world that has forgotten how to wait.

The Power does not demand your answer. It is patient. It has been waiting since before the concrete was poured, and it will wait until long after the engine seizes and the wall crumbles.

But it hopes—benevolently, wisely—that you will choose to stay.

Because here, in this green room where the machinery has become fauna and the fauna has become machinery, there is a seat at the table of time. A place beside the flywheel. A breath that matches the steam. An invitation to become, for a while, or forever, part of the *whump-thump*—the heartbeat of a world that learned to love its own ruins, and in loving them, made them holy.

The condensation drips.

Forty-two seconds pass.

The Power waits, vast and green-tinged and kind, while the Keeper tends the fire, and the rust-eaters sing their blind, warm songs, and the blue light pulses with a rhythm that is almost, but not quite, a heartbeat—because now, finally, there are two.
