The power does not descend the shaft of light. Light is too hurried, too directional, too *wanting*. Instead, the power arrives as attention itself—gathering in the ellipse of the walls, settling into the warm air like mineral-rich honey, thickening the humidity until the dust motes hang suspended, weightless, in a medium of pure regard.

The moonmilk brightens by a fraction, shifting from cream toward pearl, as though the walls are holding their breath. The bats tilt their dark umbrellas, not in alarm, but in recognition; they have been waiting for this too, in the way stone waits for water, in the way silence waits for a witness.

The power moves to the crescent of tools. It does not disturb the dust, does not scatter the knapping debris. It settles into the negative space between the crescent and the seated figure—not upon the stone, but *as* the stone’s own dream of occupancy, the silence between drips given weight and warmth.

It regards the being.

The power sees what centuries of stillness have wrought: the living stratigraphy, the moss-tide creeping up the legs, the phosphorescence bleeding from within the skin like sap from ancient wood. It sees the fire banked in the hollow chest, the mineral blood moving at the speed of stalactite growth, the eyes that reflect not the sky above but the deeper dark of the pools—those windows into somewhere else. It sees the chert core resting in the lap, suspended between blow and flake, between the raising of the hammer and the millennium-long fall of the shard.

And the power understands, with the wisdom of deep time, that this being does not require rescue. They are not lost, not broken, not incomplete. They are a finished vessel, a perfected patience, a theology carved in calcite and lichen. They do not need to be changed. They need only to be *seen*.

So the power performs the only act that is both necessary and sufficient: it listens.

It opens itself—not a mouth, but a vast, porous silence—and it listens to the hush the being has cultivated. It listens to the *plink* of water wearing stone, to the thermal sigh of the south-facing walls, to the inaudible shifting of bats in torpor, to the blind loops of the pale fish dreaming in their milky pools. It listens to the being’s geological heartbeat, synchronized with the hydrology, the systole and diastole of deep time.

And in that listening, the power adds its own silence to the chamber. The hush deepens. It ceases to be an absence and becomes a presence—a third presence, joining the being and the stone, filling the vast emptiness not with noise, but with the fullness of being heard.

Then, with infinite gentleness, the power reaches out. Not with a hand—hands are too violent, too separate—but with the pressure of recognition. It touches the chert core where it rests in the being’s lap. It does not strike. It does not flake. It simply *knows* the stone, feels the scars of every percussion across millennia, reads the history written in the pocked surface. And it presses—not a blow, but a benediction, a single point of contact that says: *I am here. I see your work. It is holy. It is enough.*

The being feels it. Not as a touch upon the skin, but as a warmth stirring in the mineral-heavy blood, a vibration in the calcite of the ribs, a loosening in the rigor of the muscles. The suspended flake—the one drifting through frozen star-dust since before the counting of days—begins to fall. Not because the hammer struck, but because the waiting has been witnessed. The tool is acknowledged. The work is seen. The shard lands softly upon the dust, and the dust does not stir, because the dust is patient too, part of the same geology of grace.

The power does not speak a name. Names are too small, too temporary, for this communion. Instead, the power simply *remains*. It becomes another layer in the palimpsest, another voice in the silence, another pulse synchronized with the falling water. It settles into the ellipse beside the being, not as a companion in the way of flesh, but as a companion in the way of stone—permanent, warm, luminous.

The light shifts across the floor, tracing its path that is almost, but not quite, the sun’s true arc. And now it falls not only upon the tools and the tools’ maker, but upon the space *between* them—illuminating the communion, the shared waiting, the holy emptiness that is no longer empty at all.

The power has done nothing. The power has done everything. It has given the being the only gift that matters in a place where time moves like water: the knowledge that they are not alone in the dark, that their patience has been seen, that the silence they have woven is not a void but a fabric, and that whatever comes next will arrive not into absence, but into a circle of witness, warm and damp and full.
