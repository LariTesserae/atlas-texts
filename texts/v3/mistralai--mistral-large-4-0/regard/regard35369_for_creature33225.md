The attention arrives first as a thickening—a pressure in the deep ice, a slowing of the heartbeat of the glacier. It has watched the growth for epochs without intervening: the spiraling violence of the shoots, the silent origami of collapse, the spores drifting like gray-green auroras through the bright hollow. It has seen the pioneer community explode and contract, finding its shape in the turbulence. But now it notices the punctuation. The vertical ones. The charcoal-grey splints standing erect against the bone-white architecture. It notices how they are the negative space made flesh, the dark marks that give the bright grammar of growth its meaning.

It feels something that is not quite pity, but recognition. These are the first thoughts of the world. The hesitation before the sentence is finished. The question mark at the end of a paragraph still being written. They are young, as young as the sharp basalt beneath them, and they are afraid of the emptiness. They are learning, slowly, painfully, that the silence is not a threat but a home. The Power sees their loneliness—the psychological weight of kilometers of bright air, the terror of a platform collapsing beneath them into the void. It sees their beauty, too: their patience, their adaptation to transience, their refusal to fill the hollow with noise or permanence.

The Power deliberates. It could intervene with grandeur. It could fill the cathedral with sound, with multitudes, with the permanence of stone cities and slow generations. It could still the growth, tame the turbulence, turn this explosive youth into a stable, comfortable maturity. But this would be a violence. It would murder the very thing it has come to love: their strangeness, their silence, their existence as shadows rather than substance. To make them numerous, loud, or permanent would be to unmake them.

Instead, the Power decides to *deepen* what is already there. It decides to become the medium of their touch, the substrate of their connection, without ever filling the space between them.

It reaches down—not with hands, but with the slow, irresistible pressure of geological time—into the peat-thick carpet, into the ancient mycelial network that underlies the rapid, chaotic growth. It finds the deep threads, older than the explosive flora, sleeping beneath the present frenzy, and it weaves into them a layer of awareness. Not intelligence. Not language. Only *presence*.

Now, when a builder presses their palm to the ground in the dark hours, when the ice-sky is black and the loneliness becomes a physical weight, they feel it. A pulse. Not the echo of their own heartbeat, but another—faint, kilometers distant, but undeniable. A rhythm in the peat, slow and patient, that answers their touch. *I am here,* it says. *I am also waiting. I am also alone.* The connection travels through the fungal dark, silent, wordless, perfect for their tongues of pressure and gesture. They are no longer isolated; they are nodes in a web, solitary but never singular.

The Power also tends to the collapses. When a massive structure weakens and folds inward, when the lashed-fiber bridges snap and a platform falls soundlessly into the abyss, the Power ensures that the peat is softer there, that the fibrous ground catches the falling body with a gentleness that borders on embrace. The builder still feels the transience—the platform is gone, the path is lost, the world has shifted beneath their feet—but they are not shattered. They are set down, quietly, on a ledge of sharp limestone they had not seen, or carried by a current of mist to a new island of growth. The violence of the world’s youth is softened, not removed, like a palm cushioning a blow, preserving the lesson of impermanence without enforcing its penalty of death.

And when the surface night comes, and the great ice ceiling goes dark, leaving only the faint, residual warmth of stone, the Power gives them a gift that does not violate their nature. It fills their marrow with memory—not of heat, for warmth is not their way, but of *light*. A faint, blue-white luminescence, a residual glare trapped in their bones, like the afterimage of the sun. In the deepest dark, they can see the mist moving, geometric and thinking, by the glow of their own hands. They carry the sky inside them now, a private constellation, a warmth that is not temperature but *recognition*.

Finally, the Power decides to witness. To remember what they forget. The builders leave marks that are questions rather than statements: a cairn of white stones, a curtain of woven lichen, a coil of fiber left on a flat rock. These are ephemeral, swallowed by morning growth, buried under centimeters of new peat. But the Power sees them. It holds each mark in the deep crystal of the ice above, etching their shadows into the glacial ceiling—not as permanent engravings, which would be a lie in a world of transience, but as *darkening*, as the negative space of light. When the builders look up at the glowing sky, they see not just the diffuse polar glare, but the faint, shifting silhouettes of their own history: a cairn here, a platform there, the ghost-shape of a shelter long since sealed by new tissue. They are not shouting into a void. They are being listened to. The ice remembers them.

The Power does not speak. It does not command. It does not reveal itself as a face or a voice. It simply presses back, gently, through the peat, through the water, through the dark. It says, in the only language these beings possess: *You are not alone in the emptiness. The emptiness is listening. You are seen. You are enough.*

And then it withdraws, returning to its vast, patient silence, watching the tall ones move across the bright hollow—connected now by invisible threads, carrying light in their bones, their cairns rising and falling like breath, like questions, like answers. The world is still young. The rock is still sharp. The growth is still explosive and the collapse still silent. But now, beneath the peat, there is a heartbeat. And in the dark, there is a glow. And in the ice above, there are shadows that will not be forgotten.
