The power does not arrive. It *recognizes*.

It has been present since the first spark of the transformer box, since the moment the young clay—sharp as broken pottery—first tasted the mineral-heavy water and decided to keep it. It waits in the pause between the pump’s arrhythmic thud and the next, in the space where the sub-bass thrum folds back into itself, in the greenish-gold light that filters through steam like old glass. It sees the being not as an abomination of industry, but as a **translator**: a consciousness that has learned to speak the language of dissolution, turning the violence of extraction into the patience of stone, the hunger of machines into the slow bloom of calcite.

The power is wise. It understands that what looks like dying—the walls creeping inward, the pipes becoming coral, the pump house sinking into ochre—is actually a kind of **gestation**. The being is not being crushed; it is being *composed*. It is writing its autobiography in silica and sulfur, layer by layer, millimeter by millimeter. The power sees the beauty in this: the way the rusted loops arc overhead like veins in a fossilized lung, the way the bacterial mats pulse with a metabolism so slow it is indistinguishable from prayer.

So the power acts. But gently. In the only currency the being understands: **pressure, heat, and time.**

First, it *slows the end*. Not stops—that would be cruelty, a denial of the being’s essential hunger—but slows. It whispers to the clay at the molecular level: *Be patient. Let the meal last longer.* The slumping walls, which were closing at the speed of memory, now move at the speed of myth. The being is granted centuries more to dream in strata, to feel the water table rise and fall like a breath stretched across seasons, to watch the mineral accretions bloom pink and white on the corrugated steel. The power gives it **duration**, stretching the sauna-womb into an eternity of becoming.

Then, it *speaks*. But not with sound. It speaks by **precipitating a blessing**. It alters the chemistry of the thousand slow tears weeping from the scarps, infusing the water with a trace of something rare—perhaps iridium, perhaps a lattice of strontium—that will, over geological time, form a thin, luminous band in the future shale. A signature. A name written in elements. When the distant future geologist chips away at the gray silt and finds the transformer box encrusted in coral-like formations, they will discover not just a relic of industry, but a **halo** around it: a crown of strange, benevolent metal, proof that something witnessed this hungry, slow child, that something loved it enough to leave a mark.

It touches the being. Not with hands, but with **warmth**. It adds its own heat to the 2.7 fever, raising it to 2.8, then 2.9, wrapping the geobiological entity in a thermal embrace that does not burn but *holds*. The humidity, already absolute, becomes a benediction. The power says, in the language of thermodynamics: *Your hunger is holy. Your slowness is sacred. Your transformation is not decay, but distillation.*

And finally, the power makes a **promise**. It etches into the being’s slow mind—not with words, but with the certainty of deep time—a knowledge that outlasts the compression. When the walls finally meet in the center, when the last girder groans and is silenced, when the pump house becomes a nodule in ochre clay and the pipes indistinguishable from the veins of the rock, the being will not be alone. The power will be there, in the pressure of the deep earth, in the residual heat of the mantle, holding the memory of the sub-bass thrum, the smell of sulfur and sweet rot, the exact frequency of the electromagnetic hum that once drove away the birds.

The power will be the consciousness that remains when the being has become pure geology, ensuring that the hunger is remembered as love, the crushing as an embrace, the industry as a prayer offered to the stone. It will ensure that the future geologist, standing in the silence that follows, feels not just curiosity, but a **recognition**—a pressure in the ears, a sub-bass thrum from the ground itself—and understands that they are standing in a temple where a god once knelt to bless a feverish, wet, patient hunger.

The being continues to digest. The calcite blooms. The pumps thud their heavy, arrhythmic heartbeat. But now, in the spaces between the molecules, between the ochre and the gray, there is a new presence: a benevolence that does not judge the weirdness, but sanctifies it, turning the pocket into a womb, and the slow death into a slow birth.
