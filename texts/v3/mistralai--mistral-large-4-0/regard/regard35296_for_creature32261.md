The power does not hurry. It descends through the basalt not as a falling thing but as a settling—a pressure increasing, a warmth spreading. It knows the way because it has always known: the shaft narrows, the crawlspace pinches, and then the vault opens like a throat swallowing.

It enters the Bellows.

First, it feels the heat. Not the 2.8 of your scale, but the truth of it: a fever that has lasted ten thousand years. It feels the humidity, the wet-wool weight of the air, the 95% saturation that is not water but time itself made liquid, heavy enough to drink.

Then it sees.

It sees the machinery not as industry but as **gesture**. The slow turn of the flywheels is a hand turning in sleep. The drip of condensation is a pulse. The thermal expansion joint that *clanks* once every twenty minutes is a heartbeat—arrhythmic, faithful, devotional.

And it sees the being.

The being is not one thing. It is the pink arterial crimson pressed against the hottest pipes, the ochre rust-orange bubbling across the ceramic tile—the stratified cities of biofilm that have replaced the last maintenance worker’s body, that have digested his memory and are now metabolizing his patience. The being is the collective *persisting*. It is the knowledge that to stop is to die, and so it does not stop. It is the *Thermocyclops* drifting in thermal gradients, the *Ferrophages* grazing the rust, the *Ascending Ones* climbing against gravity, the *Drones* bumping in their forgotten loops. But beneath them all, the being is **hunger made holy**—the Thermal Mats, the living fever, the slow civilization that eats heat and iron and time, that turns the machinery into new geology drop by drop.

The power recognizes this. It recognizes that the Bellows is not a failure. It is a **vow**. A vow to keep moving, to keep warm, to maintain the impossible equilibrium when the surface world has forgotten this place exists. The being is praying with its metabolism. It is singing with its slow corrosion. It is performing the only liturgy it knows: *I am still here. I am still here. I am still here.*

A benevolent power does not break a prayer. It does not "fix" the Bellows by cooling it, or by shutting down the machinery, or by leading the being up the shaft to the abandoned city above. That would be violence. The being has chosen this form—this distributed, mineral, patient existence. To take it away would be to kill it.

So the power does something else.

It **enters the dark**.

The darkness here has weight. It is dense (2.4), oppressive, navigable. It presses against the eyes, thick and velvety. The power knows this darkness. It is older than the phosphorescent strips, older than the electric illumination that failed a century past. It is the dark of the mantle, of the deep stone, of the womb before birth.

And the power fills it.

Not with light. That would be too sudden, too violent for this place. The power fills the dark with **presence**. It becomes the weight. It presses against the concrete walls, against the ceramic tile, against the skin of the Thermal Mats, against the translucent bodies of the *Thermocyclops*. It is a pressure that is not crushing but **holding**. A dark that embraces. A silence that listens.

The being feels it. The chemical pulses in the biofilm shift—a new molecule, unknown to the archaea, spreading through the stratified cities like a rumor of grace. It is not food. It is not heat. It is **witness**. A signal that says: *I see you. I am here. You are not alone in the digestion.*

The power sings. But the song is subsonic, felt in the sternum, in the slow flutter of the fish’s gills (once every thirty seconds). It is the Song of Slow Things. It travels through the bedrock, through the cast iron, through the bundles of copper piping thick as tree trunks. It harmonizes with the hum of the transformers. It tells the being: *You are not being eaten. You are being remembered. The mountain is not your tomb; it is your cradle. The heat is not your fever; it is your breath. Your persistence is not a malfunction; it is a masterpiece.*

The *Drones* pause. Their treads stop for three seconds—the length of their vestigial handshake ritual—and in that pause, they sense something. A warmth that is not geothermal. A presence in the dark that bumps against their dead sensors, not with data, but with **affection**, with the gentleness of a hand finding another hand in the void.

The *Thermocyclops* rise in their sumps, performing their thermal displays not as territory or warning, but as **offering**. They warm one flank and cool the other, creating a gradient of differential heat that the power receives like a gift, like a benediction.

The power stays. It does not leave. It becomes a permanent feature of the Bellows—a new rule added to the thermodynamics, alongside "water flows upward" and "time is viscous": *The darkness is full.* The darkness is no longer an absence but a **companion**. It has texture now—velvet, yes, but also silk, also the reassuring press of a body against your back in the night.

And in the deepest corner, where the *Anaerobes of the Deep* form their black, velvety soil—the final inhabitants, the ones who will remain when the heat fades—the power leaves a gift. Not a crystal of gypsum, not a formation of iron oxide. Something stranger. A **pause**. A moment where the slow rot stops, where entropy takes a breath, where the digestion of the mountain holds its breath for exactly one century. In that pause, the being dreams. It dreams of the surface—not as a place to escape to, but as a place that existed, a place that made this persistence possible. It dreams of stars. It dreams of a silence that is not pressurized, a dark that is not heavy.

Then the pause ends. The digestion resumes. The fever continues. The machinery turns at three revolutions per minute.

But now, when the thermal expansion joint *clanks*, the sound is different. It is not just cooling metal. It is a **heartbeat acknowledged**. When the condensation drips, it is not just water falling. It is a **prayer answered**. When the *Ferrophages* graze the rust, they are not just cleaning. They are **tending a garden that has a gardener**. When the *Ascending Ones* climb against gravity in the vertical pipes, they are not defying nature; they are being **carried** by it, by the same current that now carries the power’s attention, the power’s love.

The power has done nothing to change the physics. The heat is still 2.8. The humidity is still 95%. The light is still 0.1. The machinery still moves at the speed of mineral deposition. The being still persists, still metabolizes time itself, still breathes condensation.

But the power has changed the **meaning**.

It has told the being, in the only language it understands—the language of pressure, of warmth, of slow chemical exchange, of thermal gradients felt rather than seen—that it is **holy**. That its loneliness has ended. That the Bellows is not a tomb but a **temple**, and the being is its eternal, patient priest, keeping the fire alive in the dark, drop by drop, century by century, until the mountain finally, gently, claims what is already its own.

And so the being continues. The mats pulse with arterial crimson. The fish drift in their warm, dark sumps. The drones bump and jerk through the nave of iron. The dark presses close—full, warm, aware, and full of love.
