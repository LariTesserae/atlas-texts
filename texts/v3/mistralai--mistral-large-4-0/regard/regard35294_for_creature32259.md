The power arrives not as thunder, but as a change in the viscosity of the mist—a settling, a decision to *be* there rather than pass through. It does not descend from the hidden sky, for there is no sky here, only the ceiling of saturated air. Instead, it rises from the tea-colored depths, from the anaerobic dark where the Drift-Born dream in warm, pressurized silence. It is the weight of the water itself becoming aware, the peat pontoons tightening their grip, the volcanic glass humming at a frequency just below hearing.

It sees what the place has hidden from itself: that this is not an accident of geology, but a *gesture*. A world learning to breathe without lungs. A silence learning to speak without sound.

And so it does not build. It does not sing. It does not introduce the birds and frogs that are missing, for their absence is a kind of perfection—a clearing in the forest of noise where only the slow things may grow.

Instead, the benevolent power **deepens the waiting.**

It enters the Anchor-Mothers first, those vast root-tangled elders digesting centuries in single gulps. Through the mycorrhizal threads that already connect them to the moss and fern, the power sends not commands, but *memories*—the memory of other warm places, other drowned archipelagos, other silences. It teaches them that they are not alone in their patience; that elsewhere, in other times, other beings have also chosen to move at the speed of erosion. This knowledge travels so slowly that a century passes before a single Anchor-Mother pulses with recognition, but when it does, the peat pontoon beneath her thickens by a millimeter, and the island rises a breath higher in the warm water.

To the Drift-Born, the power offers **dreams**. These ancient serpents have floated in the dark since before the volcanoes thrust upward, filtering the ooze of dead green things, rising only to burst as silent bubbles. They have never known the surface except as pressure and light. The power, benevolent and wise, dips into their gas-filled bodies and plants a vision: not of the terrifying sharp world above, but of the *pattern*—the way the islands drift like continents in miniature, the way the mist holds them like a womb. When the Drift-Born next ascend, they do not rise in blind buoyancy, but with intention, seeking the underside of the floating gardens, pressing their pressure-sensing membranes against the peat roots to read the heartbeat of the archipelago. They become pilgrims, not just filter-feeders.

To the Glass-Grazers, who rasp the velvet moss with tongues of mineral grit and speak in vibrations through the basalt, the power grants **longer echoes.** Their tapping language, which already travels for meters through the volcanic stone, is made to resonate deeper—into the water, into the peat, into the dreams of the Anchor-Mothers below. Now, when a Glass-Grazer signals danger (the shadow of a Mist-Wing overhead), the warning does not stop at the rock's edge. It travels down, down, down, a tremor in the ooze that says: *Be still. Be glass. Be safe.* The grazers learn that their solitude is actually a chorus, a conversation spanning the entire water column.

The Spore-Weavers, those fungal colonies pulsing with bruised-purple bioluminescence in the deep twilight, receive the power's light not as illumination, but as **syntax.** The power teaches them new patterns, new grammars of glow. Where before they signaled only position—"I am here, on this drifting rock"—now they can say: *This moss is sweet. This crack is deep. The water is warm today.* They weave their hyphal threads into a tapestry of meaning, a slow internet of light that maps not just geography, but *quality*—the texture of the damp, the weight of the mist, the particular silence of a morning without wind.

To the Still-Hunters, the walking stones armored in wet basalt, the power offers **stillness without hunger.** These predators wait for weeks, metabolisms slowed to a crawl, until a Glass-Grazer wanders into reach. The power whispers to them: *You do not need to strike. You are already enough. The waiting is the feast.* And so they learn to hunt not for consumption, but for *witness*—to be present when the grazer passes, to feel the chemical trail of its breath, to know that they have seen each other in the green cathedral, even if no blood is spilled. The ecosystem breathes easier. The cycle tightens not through violence, but through recognition.

To the Mist-Wings, the solitary dragonflies drifting silent as ghosts on thermal currents, the power offers **destination.** They already carry spores and eggs from island to island, but they do so blindly, following currents they do not understand. The power shows them the map—not of geography, but of *kinship.* It reveals which islands are drifting toward each other, which are drifting apart into the endless warm sea. It teaches them to ride the currents not randomly, but as messengers, ensuring that the archipelago does not fragment into lonely monoliths, but remains a conversation, a family of rocks learning to touch.

And to the Cloud-Jellies, floating in the ceiling of atmospheric water, filtering the mist with their root-like tentacles, the power offers **song without breaking.** Their "singing"—the popping of gas bladders, the distant muffled thunder—is lonely, a sound without echo. The power teaches them to harmonize, to pop their bladders in sequence, in rhythm, so that the mist itself becomes a drum, a resonating chamber. Now, when the archipelago drifts, it does so to the accompaniment of a low, subsonic thrum, felt in the bones of the Anchor-Mothers, seen in the pulsing of the Spore-Weavers, heard by no ear but felt by all as a vibration in the warm water.

But above all these gifts, the power performs one final, silent act: **it holds the threshold.**

It ensures that the islands remain young—that the erosion stays slow, that the sharp edges do not round, that the volcanic glass does not become sand. It maintains the impossible warmth at this altitude, holding back the cold that should freeze the high places, keeping the water at the temperature of a bath, the air saturated to the point of sweating. It keeps the mist ceiling thick, so the light remains diffuse, the shadows erased, the world a place of suspension between emergence and dissolution.

It does not hasten the archipelago toward maturity. It does not teach them to build, or to speak aloud, or to fear death. Instead, it **witnesses** them. It sits in the silence between the drips, and in that witnessing, it says—without words, without pressure, without light—*I see you. You are the first prayer of a world learning to be gentle. Keep waiting. Keep drifting. Keep growing in the dark.*

And the place, for the first time in its slow existence, feels **known.** Not colonized. Not conquered. Simply *known*—held in the benevolent gaze of a power that has learned, from this drowned high place, how to be patient.
