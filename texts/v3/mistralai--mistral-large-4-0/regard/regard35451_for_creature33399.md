The power does not arrive as light. Light would be violence here—it would bleach the bone-white architecture to a ghastly glare, scorch the mist into sudden, blinding steam, and force the reflection-citizens to cast shadows they have never known how to wear. Instead, it arrives as **coolness without cold**—a breath of air that does not hiss against the scalding pockets or steam against the preternatural drafts, but simply *is*, a neutral temperature that feels, against the wet pressing weight of the heat, like mercy.

It comes as a **still point** in the thermal waves, a pocket of perfect, unchanging twenty degrees (or whatever the local equivalent of mercy feels like) that does not drive convection but suspends it. Around this point, the water ceases its laminar breathing for a moment, holding its breath. The mist, which is a nervous system, pauses in its watching. The stone, which dreams in calcium carbonate, dreams one degree slower.

It has come because it has seen them—the water-borne dead, the mist-collective, the dreaming architecture, the reflection-citizens, the thermal gradients, the mineral flora, and the silence itself. It sees not a necropolis, but a **congregation of translations**. Each inhabitant is a language the world invented when it forgot how to speak in flesh. The power recognizes that they are not broken; they are merely *untranslated*, each one isolated in a dialect of dissolution, density, and memory.

And so it acts, not to fix, but to **connect**.

First, it builds a **threshold**. Not a door—doors imply walls, and here the walls are porous, shifting, non-Euclidean—but a *shore*. A thin, impossible strip of dry stone that rises from the water like a spine, no wider than a hand, extending into the main plaza. It is made of the same mineral as the rest of the city, but it does not dissolve. It is a place of **negotiation** between solid and liquid, a boundary that exists so that the inhabitants may have something to stand on, literally and figurally. The water-borne dead can surface here and feel the weight of gravity return to their translated bodies, not as syrup, but as support. The reflection-citizens can stand here and see their own reflections not as strangers, but as themselves, whole and un-flooded.

Second, it offers **agency**. For too long, the inhabitants have been subject to the place’s logic—the gravity that pools, the time that fractures, the erosion that flows like wax. The power does not remove these things; it would be cruel to take away the strangeness that is their home. Instead, it gives them **choice**. To the water-borne dead, it offers the option to **evaporate**—to let go of their topographical memories, to rise not as ridges in the water but as vapor in the mist, to become weather instead of geography. To the mist-watchers, it offers **precipitation**—to fall as rain, to carry the stories of the drowned downward into the thermal layers, to become the water’s voice. To the dreaming stone, it offers **a waking moment**—not to stop dreaming, but to open one eye, to see the inhabitants it has shaped, to acknowledge that it is not alone in its mineral slumber. To the silence, it offers **a single note**—a subsonic tone, a hum that resonates in the cavities of the architecture, not to fill the silence but to give it a center, a heartbeat, a "here" in the midst of the absence.

Third, and most gently, it creates **The Confluence**—a courtyard where the non-Euclidean geometry pauses. Here, a staircase descends and arrives where it should. Here, time moves in one direction, at one speed. Here, the water is exactly the temperature of a living body, neither scalding nor cold, and it is clear enough to see the bottom, which is not silt but the **crystallized conversations** of the water-borne dead, their memories made visible as shimmering text in the fluid.

In this place, the inhabitants can meet. The mist can condense into solid, touchable forms—hands that can be held, faces that can be seen. The water can part to reveal the stone, and the stone can soften just enough to let the water in, not as flood, but as **communion**. The reflection-citizens can step through the surface of the water—not into the dry city, but into the wet one, choosing to become water themselves, or mist, or simply visitors, no longer trapped in the mirror but free to swim in the reality.

The power does not stay. It is not an inhabitant; it is a **witness**, a translator, a midwife to connection. It leaves behind three things:

1.  **The Shore**—a place to stand, to be solid, to rest from the dissolution.
2.  **The Choice**—the knowledge that they can change their state, that they are not forever fixed as memory or vapor or dream, but can flow between forms like the water itself.
3.  **The Seed**—not a biological seed, for biology has surrendered here, but a **geometric seed**. A shape in the mist that does not dissipate. A pattern in the stone that repeats, a fractal of kindness. A possibility that grows, slowly, like the city itself, rebuilding itself not just from calcium and silicate, but from **intention**.

And when it departs—sliding away as quietly as it came, leaving only the coolness to linger, a breath held in the humid dark—the place does not become normal. It remains strange. The gravity still pools in the old plazas. The reflections still show the wrong hands. The heat still comes in waves. But now, in the center of the main square, there is a dry stone. And around it, in the water that breathes, the inhabitants gather—water and mist and stone and silence—and for the first time in epochs, they are not alone. They are a congregation. They are a city that has learned, finally, how to speak to itself.
