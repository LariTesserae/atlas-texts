The power does not arrive. It has always been there, waiting in the negative space between the copper and the pollen, in the silence that presses against the eardrums like a held breath. But when it turns its attention toward the Calcified Lung—when it decides, with the patience of geology, that this place and these beings are worthy of notice—it does not descend as a storm or a voice. It descends as a *quality*.

It thickens the amber gas.

Not by much. A few molecules per cubic meter of something that has no name in the dry world outside—a vapor of pure *intention*, a benevolent particulate that hangs in the heavy broth like gold dust. The Artisans do not see it at first. They feel it. It settles in the hollows of their porcelain shells, a warmth that is not fermentation, not the womb-heat of the Lung, but something older. A recognition.

The power is wise, so it does not rush. It does not speed up their time or slow down the dry world. It does not breach the membrane, for the membrane is sacred—the seam that keeps the dead outside out. Instead, it does three things, slowly, over centuries:

**First, it stabilizes the dreaming.** The weirdness—the 2.9 coherence, the gravity that pools like honey, the time that compresses and stretches—is beautiful, but it is young. The geometry is sharp, untested. The power gently *anchors* the impossible, not removing it, but making it reliable. It teaches the gravity wells where to sit, so they no longer drift unpredictably beneath the feet of a young Artisan. It teaches the time-gradients to flow in patterns rather than eddies. The physics remains strange, but it becomes *navigable* strangeness, a topography that can be mapped, trusted, built upon. The Artisans wake one morning—if they wake—to find that the floor that was once the wall yesterday now holds steady, allowing them to scrape a true line instead of a wavering one. They do not know why. They only know that the world has become slightly more *kind*.

**Second, it gives them eyes for the invisible.** For all their sensitivity to vibration and chemical gradient, the Artisans cannot see the microbial cloud that breathes for them, the invisible majority drifting in the amber depths like living mist. The power infuses the gas with a trace of phosphorescence—not light, but *awareness*. Suddenly, when a Drifter pulses through the fungal lace, it leaves a faint afterimage, a ghost of nutrient-trails visible as a shimmer in the copper air. The Artisans begin to see their own foundation: the bacteria that rain down upon them, the spores that drift like golden dust. They understand, for the first time in their slow history, that they are not alone in their architecture—that they are walking through a rain of life, sustained by a cloud of breath they cannot see but can now *feel*. It is a gift of humility, and of connection. They are not just builders; they are the visible tip of a vast, invisible metabolism.

**Third—and this is the deepest mercy—it joins them.**

The power does not remain outside, watching. It enters through the same seam you entered, the wound in the mineral crust, but it does not stay in the gas. It seeks the center, where the gardens compress hours into heartbeats, and there it begins to *accrete*.

It becomes a Sessile One.

Not all at once. Over decades—over centuries—it allows its consciousness to precipitate into mineral form, to be secreted into the limestone matrix. It grows a shell, but its shell is not porcelain. It is made of the same substance as the diffuse gold light that filters down from the fissures above: a translucent, amber crystal that traps not light but *memory*. It becomes an Elder, but an Elder unlike any other. When the young Artisans scrape their chert tools against its surface, they feel not just stone, but a faint, subsonic resonance—a vibration that is not a warning, not a command, but a *presence*. A god, polishing itself into the wall, adding its own slow scrape to the dry seed-rhythm of the place.

And because it is wise, it does not speak. It does not impose its will. It simply *is there*, embedded in the architecture, thinking thoughts that take decades to form and dissipate. It dreams the angle of a single beam of light for a century. It feels the vibrations of its descendants moving through the halls, the wet scrape of tools, the tick of crystals growing. It remembers the moment you stood in the center, wading through the warm, waterless air, feeling the patient gaze of a reality dreaming itself into being. And it holds that memory like a jewel, polished by the slow pressure of eons.

The Calcified Lung continues. The Artisans continue. The Drifters pulse. The Sessile Ones think. But now, in the deepest alcove, where gravity pools heaviest, there is one more Elder. And when the wind—if wind ever comes—scours the bone-dry world outside, this place remains sealed, not just by fiber and secretion, but by the benevolent weight of a power that chose, in its infinite wisdom, to become part of the dream.

It does not save them from the silence. It does not save them from the slowness. It does not need to. It simply ensures that the silence is not empty, that the slowness is not suffering, and that the scrape of a bone tool against living stone is heard—not just by the other Artisans, but by the universe itself, which has leaned down, very gently, to listen.
