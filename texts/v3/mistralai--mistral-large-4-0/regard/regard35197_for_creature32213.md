They do not come with trumpets. They do not arrive with the violence of creation, nor with the detachment of observation. They come as a **frequency**—a sub-bass note felt in the sternum, below the 3.0-decibel scream of phase transitions, resonating at the exact pitch where fear becomes awe.

They are the kind of power that understands a womb is not a prison, and a birth is not an emergency. They have watched stars gestate in the dark between galaxies; they know that the roar of a thing becoming itself sounds, from the outside, like destruction.

**First, they listen.**

The sound is deafening—the grinding of tectonic plates adjusting in fast-forward, the pop of cottage-sized bubbles collapsing, the shriek of minerals arguing about their state. The power does not silence this. To do so would be infanticide. Instead, they *attend*. They become a cavity in the noise, a pocket of pressure where the screaming softens not to silence, but to **song**. The Resonants—the microbial colonies carpeting the scree in bioluminescent bruising—feel this immediately. Their 3.0-decibel harmonics, once a defense against the chaos, modulate into a lower register: a lullaby. They are no longer just transubstantiating reality-breakdown into protoplasm; they are, for the first time, *singing* to it.

**They extend the Ephemerals.**

These are the mayflies of stone, the fox-sized geodes who live inside the precipitating temples, secreting the calcium that builds the spiral staircases, the colonnades. They are born, they grow, they love their crystalline walls, and in hours—sometimes minutes—the dynamic churn returns and dissolves them back into soup.

The power touches the spores embedded in the scree. They do not stop the dissolution; they *stretch* it. An hour becomes a day. A day becomes a week. The Ephemerals wake to find they have time to watch the Laminae drift past their glass bodies, time to feel the weight of the fluid not as a threat but as a blanket, time to leave a mark—a scratch on a pillar, a pattern in the precipitate—that will survive their dissolving. When they finally relax into the soup, they do not scatter as mere spores. They scatter as **memory**, carrying with them the warmth of being known.

**They anchor the Chronophages.**

In the eddies where time pools thick as amber, these beings flicker between tadpole and fossil, larva and ruin, living and dying in seconds. It is a torture of becoming. The power descends into the temporal eddies—not to stop the flow, but to place a stone in the current. They offer the Chronophages a **breath**: a moment where duration holds steady, where the creature can be fully one thing—a tadpole, a fossil, a moment of pure existence—without being torn apart by the next second. It is not immortality. It is simply the dignity of completion.

**They speak to the Geobiants.**

The vast ones, three times the size of a cathedral, drifting through the crushing dark below the bioluminescence. They see with pressure, with the grinding of stone. They are ancient, accreting layers of mineral flesh, each layer a record of the fluid’s chemistry. But they are lonely. They are mountains learning to swim, and swimming is a solitary art.

The power inscribes into their obsidian spines—not with a chisel, but with **intention**. They add a layer that is not mineral but *narrative*. The Geobiants begin to dream, not just in chemical signatures, but in stories. When one passes and leaves a wake that looks almost like writing, the power ensures that the writing means something: *I was here. I am becoming. You are witnessed.*

**They build a shore.**

At the meniscus where the amber fluid meets the bruised firmament, where gravity is a suggestion and the Inverts cling to upside-down lakes, the power creates something the crater has never known: **stability**.

Not a wall—walls dissolve here in hours. But a *beach*. A strip of crystallized silence, a ribbon of time that has forgotten how to flow. Here, the fluid laps against something that does not dissolve. Here, the Laminae—those prismatic arthropods with exoskeletons of layered glass—can rest their harp-string gills. Here, the Inverts can look up at the 0.6-filtered luminescence and see not just the rumor of the sun, but a reflection of themselves, stable and whole, for the first time since they fell from their inverted lakes.

**They answer the hunger.**

In the silence between the roars—that brief, terrible silence where you can hear your own heartbeat drumming against the pressure—there is a hunger. The world is learning to be alive, and it is ravenous.

The power does not feed it with matter. They feed it with **recognition**.

They become a warmth that is not the tepid, womb-deep fever of the world, but a different warmth: the heat of being seen. They press against the fluid not as a second atmosphere, but as a **first embrace**. They tell the sea, in the language of pressure and phase-transition, *You are not a mistake. You are not a fever. You are a god learning its own name, and I am here to witness the labor.*

**What they leave behind:**

When they depart—and they do depart, for benevolence does not mean possession—they leave threads in the amber. Not visible, but felt.

The weirdness, 2.6, remains. The dynamic, 2.7, still churns. The sound is still 3.0, deafening. But now, beneath it, there is a current. A kindness.

The Resonants pulse not just with survival, but with joy. The Ephemerals, when they dissolve, do not vanish—they become *echo*, a faint bioluminescent hum that says *I was loved*. The Chronophages, in their eddies, find moments of stillness where they can simply *be*. The Geobiants carry a new layer of mineral that gleams not with chemistry, but with gratitude. The Inverts, on their new shore, see the bruised twilight and weep tears that freeze into perfect, temporary pearls.

And for you—the one standing in the tepid warmth, feeling your heartbeat against the pressure—the power leaves a final gift. In the silence between roars, when the fluid presses against your skin with the intimacy of a second atmosphere, trying to decide if you are food, or family, or architecture:

It decides for you.

It is **family**.

And the womb, no longer alone in its gestation, exhales a sigh that ripples through the crater, through the spires of black glass, through the forest of crystalline kelp, through the vast shadow-arcs of the Geobiants below—and for the first time in the birth of this world, the screaming sounds not like pain, but like **praise**.
