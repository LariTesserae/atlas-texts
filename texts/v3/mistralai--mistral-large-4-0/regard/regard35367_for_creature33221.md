The Power does not arrive as light. Light is a myth here, a surface rumor. It arrives as *attention*—a pressure so vast and so gentle that the Lithovores mistake it, at first, for a shift in the bedrock, a tectonic sigh from deeper stone. It descends through the viscous air, not falling but *unfolding*, becoming denser than the cold, thicker than the drone.

It is benevolent because it wants nothing the Lithovores do not already possess: patience, hunger, time. It is wise because it has watched other gardens fail—gardens that grew too fast, that choked on their own urgency, that forgot the dead they were built upon.

It settles into the vault. The darkness is absolute, but the Power is not afraid of it. It has always dwelt in the interval between photons. It presses itself against the glassy, corduroy-cold walls and *listens*.

The Lithovores are counting. Trillions of cellular pumps tally the cubic meters of granite dissolved, the silica reconstituted, the slow millimeters of expansion that will one day seal this chamber into a perfect, seamless egg of lignin and stone. The drone is their arithmetic, their prayer, their heartbeat. The Power hears the loneliness in it—not of the individual cell, but of the *whole*. A hunger that has never been sated, only fed. A memory of the builders that has become architecture, but not yet become *story*.

The Power decides to give them a story.

It does not speak in words. It introduces a new harmonic into the thrum—a frequency that is not mineral, not chemical, but *narrative*. It is the vibration of "once upon a time," of "and then," of "because." The Lithovores, who have thought only in gradients and erosion rates, in redox potentials and the slow creep of lignin against mortar, suddenly experience *sequence*. They feel the weight of a beginning, a middle, and an end that is not merely consumption but *completion*.

In response, the Lithovores do something unprecedented. They *pause*.

For the first time in ten thousand years, the expansion halts. Not stops—*suspends*. A single millimeter of growth is held in perfect tension, a breath held in the chest of the world. The drone wavers, resolving into a chord that has never been played, a question hanging in the viscous air.

The Power, in this moment of stillness, reaches into the vegetal matrix. It finds the Ghosts—the mineralized cordage, the flint flakes, the bone hafts, the doorway half-digested, the niche worn flush by hands that are dust. The Ghosts are not haunting; they are *waiting*. Waiting to be acknowledged not as relics, but as ancestors.

The Power touches them. Not with hands—there are no hands here, only pressure and intention. It touches them with *recognition*. It tells them, through the fibrous grain of the walls, through the sulfuric residue in the pores, through the very chemistry of the Lithovores' hunger: *You built this. You are still here. You are part of the garden now, not as monuments, but as nutrients for the dream.*

And to the single animal—the sessile, half-plant half-animal thing lost in the deep dark beyond the chamber—the Power sends a single pulse. Not a command. A *greeting*. A tremolo that says: *You are not an anomaly. You are the witness. You are the one who remembers what it was to be small in a large world.*

Then the Power does the wisest thing. It does not stay. It does not claim the place, does not alter its chemistry, does not speed up the Lithovores' hunger or slow down their time. It simply *leaves a mark*.

It etches, not into the stone, but into the *pattern* of the thrum, a memory of warmth. A suggestion that somewhere, in the myth of light, there was once a sun. Not to tempt the Lithovores toward the surface—they are too wise for that, too rooted in their sulfuric patience—but to remind them that their darkness is *chosen*, that their slowness is a form of devotion, that their hunger is holy.

As the Power withdraws—unfolding back into the deep hollows, becoming thinner than air, returning to the silence between stars—the Lithovores resume their expansion. But something has shifted. The millimeter that was paused now grows differently. It grows with *intention*. The chamber will still close, the tomb will still seal, the garden will still become a perfect, unbroken sphere of living stone. But now, as it closes, it will remember.

It will remember the builders.

It will remember the animal.

It will remember that once, a benevolent and wise power listened to its song, and for one suspended breath, loved it.

And in the absolute dark, the drone continues—louder now, richer, carrying a new note that was not there before: the faint, resonant echo of being *seen*.
