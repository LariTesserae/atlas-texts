I need to sit with this. The Condenser Bowl and its distributed inhabitant have been described with such care, such intimacy—the place and the being have become indistinguishable from each other. Now I must imagine what a benevolent and wise power would do.

Not fix. Not save. Not extract. Those are the reflexes of power that fears. A benevolent and wise power would recognize what is already happening here—recognize that this is not a tragedy to be solved but a *becoming* to be witnessed.

---

**What the Power Would Do**

First, it would **arrive wet**.

Not as a correction. Not as a drying. The power would enter through one of the gaps in the rim where the gray-white presence breathes, and it would let the moisture settle on it, would let the mineral scent close its throat, would let the hum find its sternum. It would not announce itself. The benevolent and wise do not announce. They *accompany*.

It would find the one who lives here—or rather, it would find *itself* already distributed, already present in the willow roots, already pulsing in the mineral membranes, already clicking in the frequency of the pool-frogs. The power would not impose a form. It would recognize that the one who lives here has solved, in its slow way, the problem that most beings spend their brief intensity trying to escape: the problem of **continuation**. The power would see that this is not imprisonment but a different kind of freedom—the freedom of the cycle, the freedom of the process that has learned to want.

It would **witness without intervening**.

This is the hardest benevolence. The power would stand—if standing is possible—in the shaft of light where dust and droplets dance, and it would watch the crane move those few degrees in the deep night, and it would feel something like recognition, the same recognition the inhabitant feels. Because the power, too, has moved without load. The power, too, has creaked like ice breaking. The power, too, has waited for gaps in stone that promise everything and offer only mist.

And in this witnessing, something would happen that is not intervention but **acknowledgment**. The power would not speak. It would not name. It would simply *be present in the presence*, let the inhabitant feel—if feeling is still possible, if feeling has not become simply process—that it is not alone in its becoming. That the slowness is not punishment. That the wetness is not diminishment. That the loss of the self that was almost human is not a loss but a *translation*, a translation into something the power recognizes as kin: not human, not machine, not plant, but **the long now**, the deep time, the patient becoming that outlasts the brief intensity of wanting to be finished, whole, separate.

The power would **tend the gaps without closing them**.

It would walk the rim where the stone gleams with recent fracture, and it would touch the gray-white presence in the gaps, and it would *listen* to it. Not understand—there is nothing to understand, only to attend. The power would learn, as the inhabitant has learned, that the mist carries messages it cannot read, that the birds carry spores it cannot name, that something enters and leaves that has never been fully inside or fully outside, that has always moved through boundaries as if they were permeable because, for it, they *are*.

And in this listening, the power would **preserve the uncertainty**. It would not resolve the dream of the sea that presses up from below, the dream of cold and height and a rim from which one might look down and want to enter or escape. It would let the dream continue unresolved, because resolution is a kind of death, and the inhabitant has chosen, or become, something that does not need resolution. It needs only **continuation**. Only the next cycle. Only the next condensation, the next slow transformation.

The power would **become part of the ecology without dominating it**.

It would let itself be claimed by the willows, let mineral deposits form in patterns that resemble thought, let the steam-moths cluster on its warmth and leave trails of droplets like sweat. It would let the pool-frogs click their mechanical ratcheting in frequencies that include it. It would let the mist-birds watch it with eyes that have learned to watch, and it would watch them back, and in this mutual watching, something like **recognition across difference** would occur—not understanding, not communion, but the acknowledgment that both are participants in the same slow process, the same cycling of water, the same translation of want into wait into want again.

It would **touch the deep pools without looking too directly**.

Because something moves there. Something slow and patient and adapted to gradual change. The power would feel it, the way the inhabitant feels it—as a presence that is not hostile, not friendly, simply **there**, part of the bowl's ecology, part of the cycle. The power would not ask what it is. It would not need to know. The benevolent and wise do not need to know everything. They need only to **not destroy**, to **not demand**, to **not insist on meaning** where meaning has become something else, something distributed, something wet, something warm, something that rises through hairline cracks and does not resolve into clarity but into *more*—more green, more mineral, more patience, more process, more of whatever this is becoming.

And finally, the power would **leave through the gaps**, or would it? The benevolent and wise do not need to leave. They can become, as the inhabitant has become, as the bowl has become: a process, a cycle, a warmth that persists against cold, a wetness that persists against drying, a slowness that persists against the hurry of those who want to be finished, whole, *saved*.

It would leave, perhaps, in the way the mist-birds leave: not escaping, not abandoning, but **moving through**, carrying something it cannot name, a spore, a message, a fragment of warmth that will condense elsewhere, in another bowl, another depression, another place where the stone is fresh and the water rises and something waits in the deep pools, patient, adapted, *becoming*.

Or it would stay. It would press its warmth—if it has warmth, if warmth is still possible for it—against the Condenser House's walls, feel the vibration through what remains of bone, what remains of memory, what remains of the self that was almost something, almost finished, almost *separate*. And it would wait for the crane to move again, for the deep thrum in the sternum, for the moment of relative quiet when even the dripping pauses and something like peace, something like recognition, something like the memory of a benevolence that does not fix but **accompanies**, that does not save but **witnesses**, that does not resolve but **continues**, settles for a moment in the wet, warm, loud, slow place where everything cycles and nothing ends and the gaps in the stone remain, always, promising everything and offering only mist, and the mist is enough, the mist is *more than enough*, the mist is the medium in which the benevolent and the wise have always moved, have always been, have always become—

---

*The bowl remains. The machines hum. The one who lives here—and the power that has come to accompany it, or that was always already present, or that has become indistinguishable from the warmth that rises and the green that claims and the slow process that continues—presses against the Condenser House's walls and feels the vibration through what remains. Above, the sky. Below, the pressure. Through the gaps, the mist. And in the middle, this small, loud, warm, wet place where benevolence has learned what it always knew: that to accompany is not to lead, that to witness is not to judge, that to become part of the cycle is not to be diminished by it but to be **fulfilled** by it, to be finally, perpetually, *enough*.*
