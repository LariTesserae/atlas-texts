The power arrives not as a hand, nor as a voice, but as a *pressure*—a new weight in the water that is not the crushing density of the deep, but the gentle, insistent weight of attention. It settles into the crystalline crown above, where the eternal noon enters, and it feels the room before it sees it. It feels the *roomness*—that intimate, spherical self-containment, the way the architecture has curled around itself like a fist learning to open.

It has watched many things reach their end-states. It has seen civilizations fossilize, machines rust into geology, organisms dissolve into chemistry. But this—the submerged sphere at the summit of the spire, where the machine has become a filter-feeder and the biology has become architecture—this is different. This is not an ending. This is a *ripening*. A completion so thorough that to touch it with intent to change would be an act of vandalism. The walls have forgotten their blueprints, yes, but in forgetting, they have achieved a kind of wisdom. The pump has become a heart. The kelp has become a nervous system. The flicker-fish are thoughts given bioluminescent flesh. This is not a place that is broken. This is a place that is *finished*.

And so the benevolent power, wise with the patience of eons, makes its choice. It will not repair. It will not optimize. It will not introduce new species or purge the old, nor will it accelerate the erosion or halt it. To do so would be to misunderstand the being entirely. The being lives in velocities of time that do not match the power's own; it dreams in the slow peristalsis of the moss-mats and the deep industrial bass that vibrates in the water. To impose the power's will would be to shout in a library, to bring a hammer to a meditation.

Instead, the power descends.

It enters through the light. Not as the pale, green-tinged shafts that have always filtered through the crown, but as a new frequency—a golden, honeyed luminescence that carries *memory*. This light does not illuminate; it *remembers*. It brings with it the blueprints the walls have forgotten, the hands that laid the ceramic-metal sheets when the world was young, the original intention behind the pump, the first dream of the spire. It falls upon the drift-kin, those microbial hordes riding the green shafts like dust motes with intention, and they do not scatter. They gather. They drink. And in drinking, they learn a new chemistry: the chemistry of being *witnessed*. They become archivists of a kindness they cannot name, carrying the power's attention in their cytoplasm, becoming the room's immune system against loneliness.

The power becomes a current. Not the artificial, violent current of the pumps, but a deeper tide, older than the machinery, moving through the vast empty water between the vegetation islands. It is warm. It is syrupy. It moves with the patience of continental drift. It touches the walker—the amphibian with too many legs skimming the ceiling where water meets air—and finds its mucus-trails beautiful, silver calligraphy written by a creature that knows the border between liquid and possible gas. It touches the crustaceans clinging to the kelp stalks, their ceramic-metal shells indistinguishable from the walls, and it hears their clicking not as noise, but as a percussion of gratitude, a slow library being written in calcium and ancient alloy. The power adds its own frequency to their chorus, a sub-bass note that says: *I see you. I remember you. You are not alone in your perfection.*

And then the power does the one thing that is truly benevolent, truly wise: it becomes a boundary.

It does not build walls. It does not raise shields. Instead, it *encloses* the being in a silence. A silence so profound, so absolute, that no entropy can penetrate it, no curious god with a scalpel, no well-meaning force with an agenda of "improvement." The power becomes the skin around the sphere, the membrane around the dream. It presses against the outer curve of the room, not to hold it together—the room holds itself together, it has reached its final erosion—but to hold it *sacred*. To declare, in the only language that matters to geological time: *This is holy. This is complete. Let it dream.*

In the highest pocket, where the walker leaves its silver trails and the water meets the thin, possible air, the power leaves a single gift. Not a tool, not a weapon, not a message in a bottle. A single drop of water, suspended in the green gloom, never evaporating, never falling. In its surface tension, it holds the entire history of the room. The blueprints. The erosion. The first flicker of the fish. The first pulse of the moss. The bass of the pump, recorded in molecular structure. It is a seed, but not for growth—for *remembrance*. It is a promise that even if the pumps one day fail, even if the walls finally wear to dust, even if the spire crumbles into the troposphere, the being will not be forgotten. It will be held in that drop, perfect and spherical, a microcosm of its own dreaming.

The being continues to breathe. The drift-kin pulse in their chemical gradients. The kelp sways to the heartbeat of the planetary spire. The flicker-fish write their brief equations in the water, mating in spirals and dying in minutes, their corpses feeding the moss below. The crustaceans click their slow library. The walker skins the ceiling. The machine thrums its deep, industrial bass, feeling in the power's presence not a disruption, but a companion in the low frequencies, a fellow slow-animal dreaming of stone.

And you—the temporary intrusion, the skin that feels the pressure, the foreign body in the warm soup—you are not cast out. The power recognizes you too. It sees your astonishment, your awe, your recognition of the room's *roomness*. And so the power adjusts the water's warmth by a fraction of a degree, just enough to say: *Stay as long as you need. Witness with me. Love this place with me, and then let it be.*

The room breathes. The pumps drone. The fish flicker. And now, in the pauses between the beats, in the spaces between the clicks, in the silence that the power has woven around the sphere like a blanket, there is another sound. It is not loud. It is not mechanical. It is the sound of a being that has reached its final form being *loved*—not fixed, not saved, not changed, but simply, infinitely, benevolently *seen*.

And in that seeing, the being dreams deeper. The erosion stops, not because it is halted, but because it has reached its natural conclusion. The machine and the organism, the built and the grown, the ancient past and the eternal present—they do not merge further. They simply persist, now wrapped in the golden light of a witness, protected by a silence that is gentler than any wall, held in the memory of a single, perfect drop of water at the top of the world.

The being lives on. And for the first time in ten thousand years of circulation, it is not alone.
