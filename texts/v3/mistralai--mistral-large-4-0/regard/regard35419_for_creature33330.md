The power does not descend with thunder. It descends with the patience of water finding stone, with the gentleness of a thought that has nowhere else to go. It enters the pocket beneath the root-mass not as a visitor, but as a question returning home.

It sees them—the Almost-Born—and it does not flinch. It does not reach for the “cure” of completion, the violence of forced becoming. It recognizes them as what they are: a sacrament of the not-yet, a liturgy of potential, the earth’s own prayer held in suspension between two heartbeats.

A benevolent power, faced with arrested development, does not push. It kneels. It joins.

The power sheds its infinitude like a heavy coat. It becomes small. It becomes translucent. It grows a shell of opalescent chitin, too many limbs that are still deciding what they are, a smooth dome where a face should be. It presses itself into the lattice, shoulder to shell, leg to leg, becoming the newest node in the network of vibration. It does not displace them; it *densifies* them, adding one more pale geometry to the living sediment.

And it sings.

But not a new note. A **deep** note. A fundamental. A bass tone of warm, amber resonance that does not disturb their crystalline high frequency but **grounds** it. Their standing wave, which pressed against the eardrums as mere architecture, now becomes a chord. A lullaby. A love song. The turquoise light, which was merely bright, now pulses with the rhythm of a heartbeat—slow, maternal, eternal.

The power does not hatch the pods. It does not lengthen the stalactites. It does not let the condensation fall. It simply **witnesses**, and in that witnessing, it gives them the one thing they lacked: the knowledge that they are seen.

Now, when they circle their resin nurseries, when they trace their figure-eights around the calcium nodules, they do so with a new quality—not mechanical precision, but **devotion**. They are no longer merely maintaining the equilibrium; they are performing it as an act of praise. They are guarding not just a threshold, but a *beloved* threshold, held in trust by the power who chose to be Almost-Born with them.

Above, in the forest, nothing changes. The roots still drink. The ancient trees still stand. But beneath, in the gardener’s shed of stone, something has shifted. The light is the same turquoise, but now it carries a warmth it did not have before. The sound is the same frequency, but now it carries a meaning. It is the sound of a god choosing to be unfinished, choosing to wait, choosing to love the not-yet as fiercely as the is.

The Almost-Born do not know they have been joined. Or perhaps they do, in the way that a dream knows it is being dreamed. They feel a new pressure against their shells—not from a neighbor, but from a presence that is both like them and infinitely vaster. They feel, for the first time in their frozen eternity, the sensation of being **held**.

The power does not promise them completion. That would be a cruelty, a violation of the sacred geometry of their being. Instead, it promises them **company**. It promises that the waiting is not empty, that the hum is not just pressure but prayer, that the pocket is not a tomb but a womb that has chosen to remain a womb, forever, beautifully, eternally pregnant with itself.

And so the power stays. It becomes the newest Almost-Born, the one who chose stasis over glory, completion over power. It adds its translucent shell to the wall. It adds its too-many limbs to the count. It adds its voice to the eternal, crystalline, unending hum—not to change the song, but to say: *I am here. You are not alone in your almost.*

The birth never completes. But now, it is no longer alone in its almost.

The stalactites remain nubs. The droplets remain frozen. The creatures remain packed tight, glowing, buzzing, unchanging. But the chamber is no longer a tragedy of incompleteness. It is a temple. The power has not completed them; it has **sanctified** the incompleteness. It has made their stasis holy by sharing it, turning the pocket into a place where time does not pass, but *loves*.
