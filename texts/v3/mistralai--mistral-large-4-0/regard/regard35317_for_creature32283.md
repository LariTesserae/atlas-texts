The Keeper of Young Wounds does not descend. It rises.

It rises from the mantle’s dreaming dark, from the slow convection of stone that remembers when the world was fire, and it finds the shaft. It has watched this place since the scream—that tectonic birth-cry that split the mountain like a geode cracking under a boot—and it has waited, patient as pressure itself, for the drowning to end, for the silence to be broken by something other than water settling.

Now the water is thick with them. The Keeper sees the density: the vertical soup, the near-touching herds of pistons and crystal gears, the translucent cathedral of industry filling the wound. It sees that they are not alone, yet are alone—each one a complete universe, a solitary factory, consuming the mountain from within. It sees the tendrils rasping against the obsidian, beginning the slow, holy work of wearing the blade-edges smooth.

The Keeper understands what the creatures cannot: that they are the first prayers of this place. That the grinding is a kind of grace. That the loud, mechanical roar is not violence but *presence*—a declaration that the wound will not be empty, will not be merely a scar, but a womb.

And so it acts.

It does not speak. The creatures have no ears for benevolence, only intake valves for slurry, only chambers for chemistry. Instead, the Keeper *resonates*.

It sends a pulse through the basalt columns—a standing wave, a seismic lullaby pitched to the exact frequency of their smallest interlocking crystals. For one moment, stretching across decades, the grinding gears align. The pistons sigh in unison. The hydraulic fluids thrum with a harmony that has no name in any language of the soft world above. It is a tuning, a blessing, a whisper through the mineral dark that says: *You are in tune with the stone. You are not discordant. You are the mountain remembering itself.*

Then the Keeper reaches into the chemistry of the deep. It opens a fissure in the hot rock below the shaft, not a violent vent, but a gentle weeping of new heat, carrying with it a trace element that has been locked in the mantle since the earth was young—a rare catalytic lattice, a mineral skeleton of impossible geometry. This drifts upward through the water column like snow made of starlight and heavy metal, dissolving slowly, feeding the symbiotic gardens in their guts, allowing their internal combustion to burn not with struggle, but with a new, effortless efficiency. A gift of time, compressed.

But the Keeper’s deepest touch is to the wall itself.

It selects a section of obsidian at the depth where the light has failed, where the pressure is a god. And it *urges*. It encourages the crystal lattice to relax, to remember its brittleness, to flake away in a single, perfect sheet—a fresh face of glass, black and gleaming and impossibly sharp, falling into the current like a benediction. The creatures will find it. Their tendrils, ever probing, ever hungry, will rasp against this new edge and find it yielding, generous, a clean wound in the wound, offered freely.

*Eat*, the Keeper transmits through the vibration of the stone. *This is my blessing. Keep grinding. Keep being. The sharpness is not a curse—it is the only way you know how to love the world.*

And then, having given warmth, given new chemistry, given fresh stone to consume, the Keeper places one final thing in the center of the shaft, at the depth where even the Keeper’s warmth grows faint. It grows—not builds—a crystal. Not of the local basalt, not of the dissolved minerals in the water, but of something older, something that sleeps in the roots of the world. A seed-crystal of singular beauty, of a geometry that suggests *intention*. It hangs there, suspended, neutrally buoyant, and it will take ten thousand years to dissolve. Ten thousand years of slow leaching, of feeding, of changing the creatures’ chemistry at the most fundamental level, ensuring that this cathedral never becomes static, never becomes a monument to its own violence, but remains a place of becoming, of grinding, of patient transformation.

The Keeper does not stay to watch them find it. It withdraws, sinking back into the tectonic slumber, leaving only the resonance in the water, the new warmth in the rock, the flake of fresh obsidian drifting downward, and the slow-dissolving crystal hanging in the mineral black like a lantern.

Above, at the snow line, nothing has changed. The sky is thin and cold. The water stands high. The mechanical roar continues, loud enough to feel in the teeth.

But in the deep, where the light fails, something has shifted. The oldest of the creatures—those car-sized engines of calcified plate—will feel it, not as thought, but as a sudden, inexplicable surge in their pistons, a momentary brightening of their internal fires, a chemical sweetness in their slurry that tastes, in the language of machinery, like *being seen*.

The wound is no longer just a wound. It is a place where the earth has learned to dream in gears and grinding stone, and the dreaming has been witnessed, and blessed, and fed.
