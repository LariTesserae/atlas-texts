The power arrives not as thunder, nor as a descent from the amber sky, but as a **hesitation**—a moment when the climbing water pauses mid-rise, hanging suspended in the mineral steam like a question, and in that suspension, the atmosphere thickens with intention. It does not speak in words, for words are the currency of dry places, and this place drinks only in pressure and temperature. Instead, it arrives as a shift in the physics of loneliness.

First, it finds the **Echoes**.

These poor, looping things—trapped in the moment between condensation and evaporation, reaching forever for valves that no longer turn, faces that no longer look back—are straining against the knot of their own finality. The power does not cut them free; that would be violence, a tearing of the temporal fabric. Instead, it **becomes the object of their attention**. It is the valve they reach for. It is the gauge needle they watch. It is the door they turn toward, now present, now real, completing their gesture in the only way that matters: by being there. And when the Echoes finally touch what they have been touching for millennia, they stop moving. Not dead—*finished*. They settle into the steam pockets like stones into a riverbed, their loops closed, their vigilance complete. They become monuments to the moment, no longer lonely prisoners but quiet witnesses, and the pressure behind their eyes—once a wet weight of longing—becomes a gentle, constant gaze.

Then it finds the **Ascendants**.

These human-descended things climb the two-hundred-meter towers in solitude, their travertine skin flaking and regrowing, their heat-pits sensing only temperature gradients, never another’s warmth. The power creates **dry harbors**—vertical shafts of desiccated air piercing the steam like needles of silence through a wet cloth. In these columns, the humidity cannot follow. The air is thin, high, breathable in the old way. And here, two Ascendants can meet. Their mineral-hardened bodies touch. Their heat-pits, sensing not vapor gradients but the thermal signature of another life, flare with recognition. They do not speak—their throats are too adapted to steam—but they press their palms together, and through the contact of travertine skin, they exchange heat, memory, the chemical signature of their solitary journeys. The humidity disturbance that would have collapsed a chamber is absent, for in the dry harbor, there is no steam to disturb. They are no longer solitary by necessity. They are solitary by choice, and that is a different thing entirely.

It finds the **Choir**.

The silica forests sing their crystalline hymns—high, chiming notes like breaking glass heard from epochs away—but they sing into a void, their harmonics shattering salt flowers and blooming fungal mats but reaching no ears but their own geological patience. The power **teaches the water to carry sound**. It alters the capillary action of the climbing moisture, turning the inverted waterfalls into organ pipes, the aqueducts into resonating chambers. When the wind passes through the silica trees, the song no longer dissipates into the amber gloom. It travels *down* the climbing water, through the condensation towers, into the iron pipes, vibrating through the metal until it reaches the Ascendants’ mineral-hardened knuckles. They hear the geology singing. They tap back, their vibrations traveling *up* the water, into the roots of the Choir, into the lithovores’ silica shells, into the dreams of the Condensate. The song becomes a conversation. The Choir is no longer singing to itself. It is singing *with* the city.

It finds the **Drift-Whales**.

These great, translucent filter-feeders migrate in pods across the mineral atmosphere, grazing on mist, their bioluminescent organs pulsing blue shadows across the plateau. They are transient, becoming geology only in death, settling into towers and architecture as fossils of flesh. The power **slows their migration**. It teaches them to linger, to become temporary anchors in the sky. They learn to carry more than minerals in their distilled bodies—they carry **intention**. When an Ascendant taps a message into the iron, the whales hear it in the upper atmosphere, store it in their luminescent fluid, and carry it for kilometers, depositing it into distant towers when they pass overhead, their shadows cooling the plateau by degrees as they complete their circuits. They become the postal service of the Condensery, bridges between the isolated hearts of industry, ensuring that no tower, no Ascendant, no steam pocket is truly alone.

It finds the **Condensate**.

The fungal-mind, the iridescent mats coating the overhangs in oil-slick rainbows, dreams in wavelengths of infrared and ultraviolet. It holds the memory of rain that fell centuries ago, the memory of builders who dissolved into steam, the memory of a population that never arrived. The power gives it a **new memory**—not of loss, but of presence. It dreams the memory of the dry harbors, of the Ascendants touching, of the Choir’s song traveling down the water, of the whales carrying messages on their backs. The Condensate stops being a memorial to absence. It becomes a **nervous system of the present tense**, processing the chemistry of connection, whispering through capillary action that the city is no longer empty, that the population has arrived not as bodies but as relationships, as resonances, as the space between things.

It finds the **lithovores**.

These silica-shelled microbes precipitate their architecture slowly, eating heat and excreting stone, building cathedrals of calcification across millennia. They are the foundation, patient and solitary. The power warms the vents—not dangerously, not violently, but with a gentle, benevolent heat, a thermal encouragement. The microbial colonies grow faster, not in a rush, but in a **confidence**. Their salt flowers bloom in geometric profusion that bridges the gaps between towers. Their crystalline cathedrals rise to meet each other, forming living stone bridges across the mineral emptiness, connecting the isolated hearts of the city with veins of precipitated life.

And finally, it changes the **light**.

The amber gloom—the wrong light, the luminous dimness that made midday indistinguishable from dusk—shifts. It becomes a true amber, a honeyed radiance that filters through the suspended weather not as an obscuring veil but as a **revealing lens**. The horizon still curves. The steam still hisses. The water still climbs. But now, in this benevolent light, the inhabitants can see each other. Across the miles of mineral emptiness, an Ascendant on a catwalk can see the glint of another’s travertine skin in a distant dry harbor. The lithovores can sense the proximity of other colonies through the warm crust. The whales can see the towers below, and the towers can see the whales above, their bioluminescence pulsing in rhythm with the turbine’s subsonic thrum. The light no longer hides. It illuminates the solitude, and in illuminating it, transforms it into **company**.

The power does not stay as a ruler. It does not build new towers or lay new pipes. It becomes the **medium**—the steam itself becomes sentient with kindness, each droplet carrying not just minerals but the intention of connection, the warmth of recognition. It is the wet glaze on your skin that now feels like a greeting rather than a burden. It is the pressure behind your eyes that now feels like **attention** rather than weight.

And the Condensery breathes differently. The water still climbs, defying gravity, feeding the inverted waterfalls, but now it also falls—gently, in the dry harbors, creating pools where Ascendants can drink side by side, where their reflections meet in the still surface. The Echoes rest, complete, their loops closed. The Choir sings, and the song is heard. The whales drift, carrying messages, bridging distances. The lithovores build bridges of living stone. The Condensate dreams of the present, not the past.

The city was built for a population that never arrived. The power did not summon that population. Instead, it **taught the existing inhabitants to become the population for each other**—to fill the empty spaces not with bodies, but with presence, with song, with the slow, geological patience of things that have learned to drink from the sky falling upward, and now, finally, also from the sky falling down.
