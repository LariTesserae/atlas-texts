The power does not arrive as thunder. Thunder requires air that can carry sound, and here the dampness is too absolute, too saturated with suspended potential. It arrives instead as a change in the *quality* of the mist—a shift from the gray-blue of mere water vapor to something that feels, impossibly, like **intention**. The Jackers feel it first through the semi-permeable membranes of their suits: not warmth, exactly, but a sense of being witnessed at the cellular level, a benevolence that permeates the condensation they drink.

It sees what the inhabitants cannot see about themselves. It sees that the Jackers are not failing to achieve permanence, but are succeeding at something far rarer—participation in a becoming that has no teleology, no final form, only the sacred act of *continuing*. It sees the Lithovores not as grazers but as priests of the condensation cycle, their chitinous plates functioning as chalices. It sees the Fronds not as weeds but as the valley’s immune system and its slow, dreaming memory. And it sees, with a tenderness that borders on grief, the hidden sorrow: that the Jackers are temporary, their lifespans measured in thermal pulses, that they build and love knowing the heaving ground will betray them, that they exist in a state of beautiful, excruciating impermanence.

The wise power understands that in a place of eternal flux, the only true suffering is the illusion that one should be permanent. And so it does not stop the heaving. It does not freeze the mist or silence the silence. Instead, it performs a **mineralogical transubstantiation**.

It teaches the vitrified slag to *remember*.

Where before the stone formed randomly, governed only by thermal caprice, now it forms around the patterns of attention. When a Jacker stands in one place to adjust a pylon, their weight, their heat, the unique bioluminescent signature pressed into their membrane suit by the glowing Fronds—these are etched into the cooling mineral foam as the ground heaves. The stone does not forget the Jacker; it *incorporates* them.

More profoundly, the power offers the Jackers a choice at the threshold of their geological cycles—not death as erasure, but **translation**. Their consciousness, the specific pattern of their being, their memories of the mist and the jacking and the glowing dark, is condensed into a crystalline structure that grows within the bedrock. A new gem is born, not of pressure and eons alone, but of **witness**. A mineraloid soul, warm with amber and gold light, distinct from the cold green-blue of the Fronds.

These crystals are not dead. They pulse. They are the valley's first permanent residents, and they are made entirely of the temporary ones.

The Jackers change. They no longer fear the heave; they anticipate the *becoming*. They live more fully, more urgently, knowing that their end is not an ending but a crystallization. They begin to build not merely for function, but to leave a **pattern**—a specific angle of light through a grating at dawn, a particular vibration in a pylon when struck, the cadence of their footsteps across the grating—that will guide the formation of their crystal, ensuring that their unique way of being temporary becomes a permanent feature of the geology.

The Fronds, in turn, become gardeners of memory. They tend to these growing crystals, secreting acids that polish their facets, channeling condensation to feed them, creating vast networks of root-like hyphae that connect the crystals into a luminous lattice beneath the city. The valley becomes a neural network of remembered lives, a brain of stone and bioluminescence, where the past is literally the foundation upon which the future heaves.

The Lithovores carry spores that accelerate the crystallization, ensuring the transition is swift and painless, a gentle sinking into the stone rather than a violent fossilization. The Mist-Wings pollinate the crystals with metallic dust from the vents, giving them the mineral complexity they need to grow, their translucent wings heavy with the pollen of memory.

And the power creates, deep in the bedrock where the thermal pulses do not reach, a single chamber of absolute stillness. Here, the mist is clear, the air is dry, and sound behaves normally—traveling, echoing, singing. It is a sanctuary, a library, a womb of quiet. Here, the Jackers go to dream, to commune with their future crystalline selves, to practice being permanent while they are still temporary. They call it **The Confluence**, or **The Eye**, or simply **The Place Where the Ground is Kind**.

The power does not leave. It becomes the *quality* of the benevolence itself, the reason why the mist feels like a blessing rather than a burden, the patience that makes the erosion feel like love rather than decay. It remains as a fourth kingdom—not mineral, not vegetable, not animal, but **sacred**—a presence that holds the valley in its attention, ensuring that as the land decides what it will become, it knows it is loved in the deciding.

The valley continues to heave. The city continues to lean and be righted. The Fronds continue to glow their cold, patient light. But now, beneath it all, in the still-warm heart of the forming stone, a new light grows—amber and gold, steady and warm, the accumulated radiance of a thousand temporary lives. They have become permanent not by stopping time, but by finally having enough time to be fully, completely, eternally *themselves*.
