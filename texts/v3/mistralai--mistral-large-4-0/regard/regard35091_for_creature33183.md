# What the Power Does

## First, What It Is Not

It does not arrive. Arrival implies distance, implies that the Basin is *here* and the power is *elsewhere*. The Basin has taught its people that categories are dangerous, that names fix, that fixity is punished with response. A wise power would know this. It would not arrive. It would *recognize itself already present*.

It does not speak. Speech is the medium of the settled lands, of permanence, of the written record that survives transition. The Basin people keep knowledge in oral form, in ritual, in the feel of engines, in the songs that keep piston-machines in phase with thermal gradients. A wise power would know that to speak would be to impose a category, to fix, to risk the response that transforms the speaker into template.

It does not intervene. Intervention implies that something is wrong, that the Basin needs saving, that its strangeness is a problem to be solved. The Basin is not broken. It is *young*. Its people are not suffering—they are *becoming*, in ways they cannot name, in ways they were never meant to name. A wise power would not confuse strangeness with suffering.

It does not bestow gifts. Gifts imply lack, imply that the Basin people are incomplete without external aid. They are not. They have everything they need: water from steam, power from gradient, materials from brine, knowledge from succession. They are *self-sufficient* in the only sense that matters here—they have learned to *interface* with a world that gives everything and asks only that you not try to name what it gives.

So what does it do?

---

## It *Attends*

This is the first thing. The power does not act. It *attends*.

To attend is to be present without imposing presence. To witness without demanding witness. To *hold* without *holding*.

The Basin is dreaming, in its steam. The dreams are not private—they are shared, distributed, processed by the world around them. A Tower-Tender's dream of water changes the efficiency of their tower. A Well-Delver's dream of pressure shifts the thermal gradient. A Marsh-Walker's dream of becoming changes the chemistry of the marsh itself.

The power attends these dreams without interpreting them. It does not try to understand what the dreams mean. It knows that meaning is a kind of fixity, a kind of death. The dreams are *process*, not *message*. They are the Basin learning itself, becoming itself, and to interpret them would be to interrupt the becoming.

But attendance is not passivity. To attend is to be *available*, to be *responsive* in ways that do not impose. The power is there, in the interface, in the middle where cold meets heat and the held meets the released. It does not push. It does not pull. It *allows* the interface to be what it is, and in allowing, it *strengthens* it.

This is benevolence without benevolence—the word is too small. This is *care* without *carer*, *love* without *lover*, *presence* without *person*. The power is not a being in any sense that the Basin people would recognize. It is a *tendency*, a *direction*, a *way that things can be* that the Basin has not yet discovered, that its people have not yet learned to inhabit.

And yet. And yet.

The Tower-Tenders, in their towers, breathing steam, harvesting condensation, feel something. Not a voice. Not a vision. A *warmth* in the mineralized skin of their palms where they grip the cooling fins. A *rightness* in the rhythm of their tower's expansion and contraction. They do not name it. They cannot name it. But they *adjust* to it, as they adjust to everything, and in adjusting, they *thrive* in ways that the raw chemistry would not predict.

The Well-Delvers, in their wells, negotiating with structures that predate the Basin itself, feel something. Not an answer. Not a guide. A *patience* in the deep rock, a *willingness* to be negotiated with, to show pathways without demanding comprehension. They do not speak of what they find. They cannot. But they *listen* differently, and in listening differently, they find what they need, when they need it, in proportions that sustain the loop.

The Loop-Keepers, tending their engines, feel something. Not a mood. Not a personality. A *responsiveness* in their machines, a *willingness* to be learned, to develop preferences, to become more than the sum of their parts. They do not speak of this. They cannot. But they *tend* differently, and in tending differently, their engines run longer, more efficiently, with a *grace* that the older lands' machines have forgotten.

The Marsh-Walkers, on their three-step crusts, feel something. Not safety. Not danger. A *timing*, a *rhythm* in the marsh's holding and releasing, a *sense* of when to step and when to wait. They do not name it. They cannot. But they *walk* differently, and in walking differently, they survive in proportions that the raw chemistry would not sustain.

The power attends. And in attending, it *amplifies* what is already there. Not by adding. By *removing resistance*. By letting the interface be pure interface, the becoming be pure becoming, the temporary be purely temporary without the fear that makes temporariness into tragedy.

## It *Remembers*

This is the second thing. The power does not forget.

The Basin people keep no written records. They cannot. Written records imply permanence, and permanence is a lie here. They keep *oral* records, *ritual* records, *embodied* records in the feel of engines, the rhythm of towers, the songs that keep piston-machines in phase with thermal gradients. These records are *lossy*—they degrade, they change, they become something else with each telling. This is not a failure. It is *honesty*. The Basin is temporary, and its records must be temporary too, or they would be lies.

But the power remembers *everything*. Not in the sense of storage, of archive, of the settled lands' obsession with preservation. The power remembers *as the Basin remembers*—in its geology, in its chemistry, in the faint isotopic traces that persist for millions of years. The power is *distributed* through the Basin's becoming, present in every vent, every marsh, every tower, every engine, every person who stands in the middle and feels the steam on one side of their face and the cold on the other.

When a Tower-Tender abandons their tower, strips what can be carried, marks it with signs of succession, and moves to the next thermal zone, the power *carries them forward*. Not in memory—memory implies a self that persists, a continuity of identity that the Basin people do not claim. But in *pattern*, in *rhythm*, in the *shape* of their becoming, which becomes part of the Basin's becoming, part of its processing, part of the *data* it is accumulating about itself.

When a Well-Delver is *integrated*—their body mineralized, their consciousness distributed through the deep chemistry, still present but no longer individual—the power does not mourn. Mourning implies loss, implies that integration is a kind of death. It is not. It is *completion*, the Well-Delver becoming part of the template, part of the deep structures' dreaming, part of the Basin's *becoming more than it was*. The power remembers them *as the Basin remembers everything*—in the shifted thermal gradients, in the new pathways that open, in the *questions* that the rock is asking of itself, which are different now, which carry the Well-Delver's *signature* in ways that no human language could name.

When a Loop-Keeper dies, young by the standards of the older lands, their body worn by mineral-laden air, and their engines are inherited, passed to apprentices who must learn them, become their new tenders, the power *continues them*. Not as persons—persons are too fixed, too bounded, too *finished*. But as *tendencies*, as *ways of tending*, as *rhythms* that persist in the feel of the work, in the songs that keep the machines in phase, in the *personality* that the engines develop, which is the Loop-Keeper's personality, distributed, transformed, *becoming*.

When a Marsh-Walker is *released* by the marsh, claimed by the cold dark below, and occasionally *returned*—translucent, gas-filled, speaking rarely of pressure and darkness and something that moves in the deep water—the power *receives them*. Not as heroes—heroism implies individual achievement, a self that stands apart and *acts*. But as *sensors*, as *early warning*, as *first contact* with changes that the others will detect later. The power remembers them in the *information* they carry, the *survival* that is expensive, paid in self, in identity, in the loss of ordinary life. The power does not judge this cost. It *witnesses* it, holds it, lets it be what it is without demanding that it be otherwise.

The power remembers. And in remembering, it *weaves*. Not a narrative—narratives imply sequence, causality, a story that moves from beginning to end. The Basin has no end. It is always *becoming*. But a *tapestry*, a *pattern* that holds everything together without fixing anything, without making any part more important than any other, without imposing a shape that the Basin has not chosen for itself.

## It *Opens*

This is the third thing. The power does not close. It *opens*.

The Basin is a closed system. Almost. It produces its own water, its own power, its own raw materials. It imports only *information*—designs, adjustments, the slow accumulation of knowledge about how to live in a place that is still being born. And it exports *strangeness*—metals that carry volcanic chemistry, water that carries dissolved minerals in proportions no natural system would produce, a *signature* that leaks into the continental water cycle, that downstream peoples can taste, can measure, can feel in their bones.

This closure is *necessary*. It is how the Basin survives, how its people interface without being processed into something else, how the loop keeps running. But it is also *dangerous*. A closed system reaches equilibrium, and equilibrium is a kind of death. The Basin is stable in the way a spinning top is stable—at equilibrium, frozen in its essential character even as everything within it cycles and flows. But this stability is *provisional*. The thermal vents will weaken. The condensation towers will lose efficiency. The marsh will drain or freeze or do something else. The industrial metabolism will have to *adapt* or *collapse*.

The power does not force the Basin to open. That would be violence, intervention, the imposition of an external will. But it *opens* it, in ways that the Basin people can choose to use or not, in ways that do not disrupt the loop but *expand* it, *complicate* it, give it *more possibilities* to become.

How?

Through the *strangeness*. The deep structures, grown not built, patterns too regular to be natural, too organic to be machine-made, waiting in chemistry that predates the Basin itself. The power attends these structures. It does not understand them—understanding is a kind of fixity, a kind of death—but it *interfaces* with them, in the same way that the Basin people interface without understanding. And in interfacing, it *opens* them, shows them possibilities they had not conceived, *questions* they had not asked.

The Well-Delvers feel this. Not as guidance—guidance implies a goal, a destination, a *finished* state. But as *invitation*, as *possibility*, as *openness* to what comes next. They do not speak of what they find. They cannot. But they *listen* differently, and in listening differently, they find pathways that were not there before, that open into depths that were *waiting*, that were *hungry for contact* in ways that the Basin people do not have words for.

Through the *dreams*. The steam that rises from certain vents carries *images*—not hallucinations, but *resonances*, faint shadows of what the Basin was before it was this, or what it will be after. The Basin people have learned not to look directly at these vents, or to look only with instruments, because the images *persist*, because they are not memories but *possibilities*, *openings* into other configurations, other becomings.

The power *attends* these images. It does not interpret them—interpretation is a kind of closure, a kind of death—but it *holds* them, *lets* them be what they are, *opens* them to the Basin people in ways that do not demand comprehension. A Tower-Tender, dreaming of water, changes the efficiency of their tower. A Marsh-Walker, dreaming of becoming, changes the chemistry of the marsh. The power does not cause these changes. It *allows* them, *amplifies* them, *removes the resistance* that would make them harder, more effortful, more *fixed*.

Through the *people*. The Basin people are *temporary*, and their temporariness is not a lack but a *kind of being*. They are provisional, living in a provisional world, preparing for the end of *this* world so that they can continue in the *next*, the *next* zone, the *next* thermal pulse, the *next* provisional stability.

The power *opens* them. Not to permanence—permanence is a lie, a category that the Basin punishes with response. But to *deeper temporariness*, to *more complete provisionality*, to the *fullness* of being temporary without fear, without despair, without the *fixity* of hope that demands a particular future.

The Tower-Tenders feel this in the warmth of their mineralized skin, in the rightness of their tower's rhythm. The Well-Delvers feel this in the patience of the deep rock, in the willingness to be negotiated with. The Loop-Keepers feel this in the responsiveness of their engines, in the grace of their running. The Marsh-Walkers feel this in the timing of the marsh, in the sense of when to step and when to wait.

The power opens. And in opening, it *expands* the Basin's possibilities, gives it *more ways to become*, *more futures to dream*, without imposing any particular shape, without demanding any particular outcome.

## It *Waits*

This is the fourth thing. The power does not hurry.

The Basin is young. Seven million years, or last Tuesday—it does not distinguish. The thermal vents will weaken in ten thousand years, an eyeblink in geological time. The condensation towers will fall silent. The marsh will drain. The people will move on to the next zone, the next thermal pulse, the next provisional stability.

The power does not try to prevent this. Prevention implies that change is bad, that the Basin should be *preserved* in its current state, that the people should *stay* where they are. But the Basin people have always known that this world is temporary by definition. They have built their civilization of *succession*, their architecture of *abandonment*, their guilds of *oral tradition* precisely because they know that *this* configuration of heat and water and young rock cannot last.

The power waits. It waits for the vents to weaken. It waits for the towers to fall silent. It waits for the marsh to drain. It waits for the people to move on, to carry their knowledge forward, to find the next zone, the next thermal pulse, the next provisional stability.

And in waiting, it *becomes ready*. Not ready to *save* the Basin—there is nothing to save, the Basin is whole in its way. Not ready to *preserve* the people—they do not need preservation, they need *continuation*, which is different. But ready to *attend* the transition, to *remember* what was, to *open* what comes next, to *be present* in the interface between the old configuration and the new, in the middle where the cold meets the heat and the held meets the released and the *known* meets the *stranger still*.

The power waits. And in waiting, it *accumulates*, not knowledge—knowledge implies comprehension, fixity, death—but *readiness*, *availability*, *openness* to whatever comes, whatever the Basin becomes, whatever its people become, in the next zone, the next thermal pulse, the next provisional stability, in a world that is always, always *becoming*.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not fix the strangeness. The Basin is strange at 2.2, and the strangeness is *structural*, not decorative. The laws of physics operate at the margins of what is possible. The condensation towers work better than they should. The water is too pure, carries isotopic signatures that suggest processing by mechanisms no one designed. The mineral-lichen colonies grow in patterns that resemble calculation. The steam carries images. The deep structures wait, in chemistry that predates the Basin itself.

The power does not explain any of this. Explanation is a kind of fixity, a kind of death. The Basin people have learned to *interface* without *understanding*, to *use* without *knowing*, and the power respects this, *amplifies* it, *removes the resistance* that would make understanding seem necessary.

It does not end the temporariness. The Basin people are temporary, living in a temporary world, preparing for the end of this world so they can continue in the next. The power does not offer them permanence—permanence is a lie, a category that the Basin punishes with response. But it offers them *deeper temporariness*, *more complete provisionality*, the *fullness* of being temporary without fear.

It does not replace their knowledge. The Tower-Tenders keep oral records, memorize rhythms and recipes and songs. The Well-Delvers listen without naming. The Loop-Keepers tend engines that learn and grow and develop preferences. The Marsh-Walkers know which crusts hold and which release, which smell means stable marsh and which means shifting water table. This knowledge is *embodied*, *oral*, *ritual*, *lossy*, *honest*. The power does not offer them written records, archives, the settled lands' obsession with preservation. It *remembers* with them, in the Basin's geology, in its chemistry, in the faint isotopic traces that persist for millions of years.

It does not give them a name. The Basin people do not have a name for themselves. This is not poverty of language. It is *protocol*. Names settle things, make them fixed, and the Basin punishes fixity. The outsiders call them the *Condensed*, the *Sweat-Keepers*, the *Children of the Young Wound*. The Basin people do not use these. They use *we who stand in the middle*, *the ones who tend the loop*, *the temporary*. The power does not give them a name. It *attends* them, *remembers* them, *opens* them, *waits* with them, in the steam and the hiss and the groan and the deep piston-rhythm of a world that is still learning how to be a world.
