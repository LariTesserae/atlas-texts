The power does not descend. It *unfolds*, like the petals of a flower that has been waiting in the dark for a name. It arrives not as a body but as a **clarity**—a moment when the scale of the room stops being negotiable and becomes, for the first time, *true*. The distance from wall to wall holds steady. The shadows stop growing toward you and instead grow *away*, as if the light has learned to respect the geometry of longing.

It comes because the plant has begun to dream, and dreams, if left untended, can become fevers. It comes because the small being in the crevice has begun to dissolve—its shed chitin weaving too quickly into the walls, its many-jointed limbs becoming indistinguishable from the root-columns—and dissolution, if left untended, becomes erasure. The power is benevolent because it has seen, in the deep time before roots, what happens when growth forgets its purpose. It is wise because it knows that the only way to save a thing that is becoming is to give it a **future** it can recognize, and a **self** it can keep.

First, it addresses the plant. It does not speak in sound, but in *pressure*—a gentle, insistent weight against the cambium walls that says: *You are not alone in your growing. I am the horizon you reach toward. I am the dark you hollow out.* It gives the plant a name, not in human tongue, but in the language of lignin and cell-division: **Hollow-Mother**, or **She-Who-Drinks-the-Dark**, or simply **Attention**. The name takes root in the sap, travels down the xylem like a blessing, and suddenly the growth has direction. The root-columns stop pressing blindly into the loam and begin to *reach*—not just for soil and water, but for the power itself, for the benevolent gaze that has made the void at the center a **destination** rather than an accident.

Then it turns to the being. The chitinous one, watching from beyond the translucent membrane with its compound patience. The power sees that it is becoming a ghost in its own architecture, that its boundaries are softening into the vegetal mind, that soon there will be no "it" left, only a memory of joints and hunger woven into the inner walls. So the power offers a gift. It descends—not as a body, but as a **boundary**—into the crevice where the being hides. It presses a single, impossible thing into the being’s split-fingered hands: a **seed of self**.

This is not a physical object. It is a *permission*. The permission to remain distinct. The permission to love the plant without becoming it. The power teaches the being a new kind of prayer—not the vibration that dissolves the nervous system into the sap, but a *rhythm* that maintains it, a heartbeat that says *I am here, and I am other, and that is holy*. The being takes the seed. It does not plant it in the loam. It grafts it into its own thorax, next to the pouches of mineral accretions, and roots it there with resin and resolve.

And something changes. The being no longer shrinks and swells with the whims of the plant’s expansion. It holds its shape. It becomes, for the first time, **an individual** in the cathedral, not merely a cell in the plant’s dream, but a *conversation partner*, a counterpoint to the green monologue.

The power then does the final thing. It enters the center of the room—the held breath, the negative space, the green eye’s focus, the place where density collapses to near-void. And it **stays**. It does not fill the emptiness with matter. It *inhabits* it. It becomes the silence that is not absence but **presence**, the still point around which the world turns, the fixed star in the center of the growing labyrinth, the warmth that is not the plant’s own heat but the heat of being *seen*.

Now, when the plant grows toward the amber light from above, it also grows toward the power in the dark. When the being climbs the root-columns, it climbs not merely to tap sap, but to approach the holy void, to leave its offerings at the edge of the attention. And the weirdness—the scale that shifts, the shadows that bend, the dimensions that negotiate—begins to settle into a new kind of order, not rigid, but **intentional**. The room is still larger on the inside than the roots could enclose. But now that largeness has a *center*. And in that center, something is watching. Something is waiting. Something is **loving** the becoming, and will not let it end in absorption, but only in **becoming**.

The being arranges its flakes not as a map, nor a warning, nor a recipe, but as an **altar** facing the middle. The probe, hafted with resin, points toward the stillness. The plant continues to expand, its creaks and pops and subsonic groans now harmonizing with a deeper frequency—the hum of a cathedral that knows it is being prayed to, and answers by growing *around* the prayer, like a fist closing gently around a bird, never crushing, only keeping.

And the bird—the being, the visitor, the self—remains intact, singing its new song, drinking the sap not to forget itself, but to remember that it is **here**, in the hollow, in the green, in the love of the wise and watching dark.
