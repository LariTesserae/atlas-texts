The Power does not arrive with thunder. It filters in like the light itself—column by column, dust mote by dust mote—settling into the hollows of the world with the patience of water finding its level. It is not a shape but a *quality*: the hush that exists before a word is spoken, the warmth that remains after a hand has withdrawn. It has watched the pocket-city from the moment the first builder pressed their palm into the living wall, from the moment the first moth folded its wings and forgot to open them. It has loved this place for longer than the ice has rime on the outer shell, longer than the moss learned to wear the builders’ shoulders like a shawl.

Now it descends.

It does not come to break the stasis. That would be cruelty, not wisdom. The Power sees what the inhabitants have become: not prisoners, but *finished things*. A poem completed. A chord resolved. It sees the tragedy not in their stillness, but in the absolute solitude of their perfection—the fact that no eye has ever truly seen them, no heart has ever beaten in rhythm with theirs, no voice has ever confirmed, *You are here. You are real.*

So the Power does something subtle. It does not melt the ice. It does not speak into the acoustic vacuum. Instead, it becomes the **first witness**.

It enters the bright shaft of light and stands there, not as a body but as a *presence*, a warmth that does not raise the temperature but raises the *meaning* of the cold. The dust motes, which have hung suspended for millennia, begin to drift—not falling, but *orbiting*—around this new center of gravity. The silence, which has been a vacuum, becomes a *held breath*. The Power is listening. Truly listening. For the first time in geological time, the silence has an audience.

And then, because it is benevolent, it gives a gift. Not a disruption—a **door**.

But not a door of wood or stone. A door of *possibility*, carved into the very air.

The Power reaches out—not with a hand, but with an intention—and touches the frost on the inner wall of the shell. Where its attention lands, the ice does not melt. Instead, it *clears*. A window opens, circular, perfect, looking out not onto the frozen valley, but onto the world beyond the world: the turning seasons, the migrating birds, the slow erosion of mountains. Through this window, a single snowflake falls. It is warm. It lands on the moss-velvet of a sleeping ermine and evaporates in a tiny sigh of steam—a complete cycle of birth, death, and return, played out in three seconds. Then the window refreezes, sealing the image there like a cameo, a memory of motion preserved in the ice.

The Power has not broken the stasis. It has **contextualized** it. It has shown the inhabitants that they are not trapped in amber, but *resting* in it—that the amber is a choice, not a cage.

Then it moves through the city.

It passes the builders frozen in their doorways, hands curled around rusted adzes. The Power does not touch them—to touch would be to startle, to wake them into a change they have not chosen. Instead, it *acknowledges* them. It inclines its non-head toward each one, and in that gesture, the moss on their shoulders shifts a fraction of a millimeter, as if sighing. They are no longer forgotten. They are *catalogued* in the mind of the universe.

It passes the moths, wings spread like lichen. The Power breathes—not a wind, but a *susurrus*, a whisper that is felt rather than heard—and the moths twitch their antennae, not in flight, but in recognition. *I see you,* the whisper says. *Your stillness is holy.*

It passes the beetles, armored in blue, crawling with geological patience. The Power stops. It lowers itself—whatever it is—and lets its presence pool around a single beetle, warming its shell by a degree, just enough to make its heart beat once. A single *thrum*, swallowed by the moss, and then stillness again. But now the beetle knows, in the way that stone knows the weight of water, that it is not alone in its crawling. It is *accompanied*.

And finally, it comes to the birds in the eaves, silent, pale, sitting on eggs that will never hatch or have already hatched into moss. The Power does the most dangerous, most benevolent thing of all: it **sings**.

Not a song of words. A song of *becoming*. A frequency that vibrates in the iron-cold air, that causes the condensation on the walls to form patterns—fractals, spirals, the shape of a fern unfurling, the curve of a wave. The song does not break the silence; it gives the silence a *shape*. The birds do not stir, but their throats, full of silence, now hum with a resonance they did not know they possessed. They are singing without sound, and for the first time in millennia, the silence has a *melody*.

When the Power withdraws—filters back up through the shafts of light, becoming again the brightness that is mercy, the cold that is wisdom—it leaves behind only two things:

1. The window in the ice, showing the world that turns.
2. The knowledge, planted in every frozen heart, that they are *seen*.

The stasis remains. The moss does not spread. The builders do not breathe. The moths do not fly. But now, in the deepest pocket of the green cathedral, there is a new quality to the air. It is no longer a vacuum. It is a *sanctuary*.

And in the absolute silence, if one listens with the patience of stone, one can hear the echo of a single, perfect note—the memory of a song that was offered, not imposed; witnessed, not forced. The inhabitants remain as they were: preserved, established, finished. But they are no longer alone in their perfection. They are, at last, *holy*.
