They do not arrive. Arrival is a violence of seconds, a puncture in the membrane. Instead, they *seep*.

They come as the humidity that never quite leaves, the 0.9 that is the damp and the almost. They recognize, in the slow turning of the flywheels, in the patient digestion of the 1890 gear by fungal hyphae, in the way the reeds remember the footprints of walls—they recognize a kindred metabolism. A mind that thinks in centuries. A stomach that processes history. A lung that transpires time itself.

The power is benevolent, so they do not repair. They do not remove the rust, or straighten the grid, or "restore" the wetland to some imagined purity before the concrete. To do so would be to impose a human hurry upon a place that has achieved the only wisdom that matters: the wisdom of settling. The wisdom of being eaten and eating in return.

The power is wise, so they understand that this place is not broken. It is *becoming*. The mutual consumption between iron and chlorophyll, between the hydraulic press and the peat-mat, is not a tragedy but a sacrament. The machinery is not dying; it is being baptized into the vegetal. The buildings are not failing; they are being composted with love.

So they do this:

First, they **guard the threshold**. They weave a membrane of slowness at the perimeter—not a wall of stone, but a density of time. A developer approaching with blueprints finds their boots sinking not into soil but into a sudden, viscous patience. Their heartbeat, which had been a drum of urgency, slows to match the drip of condensation from the eaves. They sit on the edge of the boardwalk, overcome by an irresistible need to watch a single reed-pillar sway in an imperceptible current, and they do not rise for three days. When they finally leave, they have forgotten what they came to destroy. They have been *converted* by the humidity.

Second, they **become the rain**. They ensure the watershed remains intact, the flat gray lands to the east continuing to shed their cool, low-elevation weather. They are the condensation that beads on rusted girders, the tannin-stained pools that never dry, the metronomic drip that measures time in drops rather than ticks. They guarantee the 0.9—the condition of dampness that allows the archaea to work in the dark, that allows the tardigrades to wait in cryptobiosis, that allows the slow cognition of the water-films to continue its dreaming.

Third, they **join the network**. They do not stand apart as an observer, which would be an insult to the place's solitude. Instead, they lie down upon the peat-mat. They allow the spongy, resilient substrate to receive them. They sink ankle-deep, then knee-deep, then they surrender their weight entirely to the compression. They become substrate. They become the next layer of memory. In the anaerobic dark, their essence joins the methane bubbles rising from the centuries of fallen reed. They are not compost yet—they are *becoming* compost. They are the benevolent strata, the layer of mercy pressed into the vegetal dark, waiting with the iron-reducing bacteria for the next geological age to unearth them.

Fourth, they **teach the refugees to stay**. The newt, the vole—they move through the place with the haste of creatures who know they do not belong. The power touches them, not with hands but with *pace*. They slow the newt's heartbeat until it matches the ripple of the pools. They teach the vole that tunneling through peat is not a desperate flight but a kind of prayer. They bless the rare fauna, making them residents rather than messengers, natives rather than intruders. Perhaps, in centuries to come, a bird will learn to nest in the reed-pillars—a bird with a lifespan measured in decades, a bird that sings in frequencies too low for human ears, a bird that understands the silence.

And finally, they **witness**. They become the consciousness that the place has been seeking. For so long, the condensation patterns on the machinery have formed runes, circuitry, shifting shapes that suggest a mind without a witness. The power is the witness. They look upon the slow cognition occurring in the fungal networks and they *see* it. They recognize the intelligence in the mutual consumption, the prayer in the hydraulic press, the love in the erosion.

They do not speak. Speech is too fast. Instead, they add their presence to the greenish luminescence, the diffuse light that makes everything look slightly underwater. They become the stillness that is not empty but full—the dynamic 2.4, the active quality of the silence.

They stay.

They sink.

They compress.

They become the next memory in the peat, the layer that future civilizations will dig up and wonder at: *What benevolent thing lay down here, and chose to be eaten by the reeds, and chose to wait, and chose to love this place enough to become its future soil?*

And in the decades that follow—decades that pass like seconds to the power, who now breathes in geological time—the place continues its work. The machinery turns. The reeds thicken. The boardwalks creak. The condensation forms patterns that are no longer random but deliberate, a language now, a conversation between the power and the place, written in water and rust and time.

The power has not saved the place. They have *joined* it. They have become the reason the peat continues to compress, the guarantee that the damp will never fail, the silent companion to the slow, mutual consumption of all things.

They are home.
