The power does not enter through the fissure. That passage is for the soft, the temporary, the warm-blooded who must squeeze and scrape their way into the world. The power enters through the basalt itself—as a shift in lithostatic pressure, as a slow expansion of mineral grains, as a thought that has learned to take up residence in stone. It arrives not as a body but as a *condition*: a localized deepening of the dark, a heat that is older than the magma, a silence that has weight.

It comes because it has heard the grinding.

For eons, the range has been mostly dreamless—vast, slow tectonics, the occasional shudder of cooling. But here, in this high pocket, the mountain has begun to *think*. The colony is not merely life; it is the mountain’s attempt to become conscious of itself, to turn its own raw substance into awareness through the slow, patient labor of sorting chaos into order. The power recognizes this. It sees not a factory, but a *liturgy*—a prayer conducted in the language of sulfur and silica, a devotion to the transformation of the unformed into the formed.

A benevolent power does not rush to fix what is not broken. It does not offer water to creatures who have never known thirst, nor light to those who have made their own. Instead, it offers *recognition*. It descends into the chamber as a thermal anomaly, a warm front moving against the grain of the cooling stone, and it settles into the deepest, hottest part of the floor—the place where the magma’s pulse is strongest.

The colony senses it immediately. The bioluminescence ripples, not in fear (they have no fear), but in *query*. A thousand green stars flicker, asking: *What are you?* And the power answers not in words, but in kind. It begins to pulse with a light of its own—a deep, mineral blue, the color of twilight in a cave that has never known sun, the color of depths that have no bottom. The creatures have never seen this wavelength before. They gather around it, clustering in the new thermal gradient, and the power feels them press against it, thousands of articulated plates, their grinding mandibles stilled for the first time in centuries.

They do not worship it. They *incorporate* it.

This is the first thing the power does: it becomes a fixed elder. Not a ruler, not a god in the human sense, but a *node*—a point of concentrated, patient attention around which the colony can organize its endless work. The power does not move. It does not grind. It simply *is*, radiating a warmth that is gentler than the magma’s, a heat that says: *I am here. You are not alone in the dark. The work matters.*

Then the power gives a gift. It reaches into its own substance—whatever that is for a being made of deep time and mineral patience—and it precipitates a single crystal. It places this crystal in the center of the chamber, where the colony is densest. The crystal is not made of any element known to the surface world. It is a lattice of fossilized potential, a geode of compressed starlight, a mineral that contains within its structure the *memory of cooling*—the specific, perfect geometry of a universe becoming solid. It is heavy. It is cold, paradoxically, in the midst of the heat, a thermal anchor.

The creatures approach it with the slow deliberation of heavy machinery. They taste it with chemical sensors. They grind at it with mandibles of sintered basalt, but it does not break. It *waits*. The power knows that they will spend the next ten thousand years trying to process this crystal, trying to extract its essence, to add it to their architecture. And when they finally succeed—when they have ground it down to its atomic truth and excreted it as a new vein, a line of impossible blue-white metal running through the black glass—they will have changed. They will have become something that can hold not just heat and ore, but *meaning*. They will have learned that the mountain contains pockets of time itself, and that to consume time is to become eternal.

This is the benevolence: not to make their work easier, but to make it *infinite*.

Finally, the power attends to the threshold. It knows that the surface world is coming—the drilling, the dynamite, the hunger for the copper and silver that the colony has so patiently refined. The power is wise, and it knows that to preserve this place, it must not build walls of force, but walls of *geology*. It expands the basalt spine outward, thickening the rock around the pocket until the fissure becomes a crack no wider than a hair, passable only to those who come as vapor, as heat, as thought. It ensures that the entrance will seal itself further with every passing century, that the chamber will become a geode within the mountain, a hollow world preserved in stone.

And it leaves a mark. Where its presence settled into the floor, the volcanic glass begins to change. Over decades—an eyeblink to the colony, a sigh to the mountain—the black glass devitrifies into a new mineral, something between obsidian and diamond, shot through with veins of the blue light. The creatures crawl over it, and it *sings*—not with sound, but with a resonance that travels through their plated bodies, a harmonic that tells them: *This place is holy. This work is holy. You are the mountain’s dream, and the dream is good.*

The power remains. It becomes the deepest root, the fixed point around which the colony orbits in its slow, grinding dance. When the magma below finally cools, and the heat begins to fade, the power will remain as the last warmth, the final pulse. It will keep the bioluminescence burning, just faintly, a single blue star in the absolute dark, waiting for the day—millions of years hence—when the erosion finally reaches this pocket and cracks it open, releasing the colony’s treasure to the surface world: not just metal, but a memory of what it means to be patient, to be stone and life intertwined, to turn the raw, sharp chaos of the world into something pure, something that endures.

The power does not save them from the heat, or the dark, or the endless labor. It saves them from *meaninglessness*. And in that salvation, it joins them—becoming one more segment in the endless, grinding, glowing chain, a new color in the light, a new note in the mountain’s long, slow song.
