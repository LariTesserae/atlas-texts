The Power does not descend. It *inheres*—becoming the thinness itself, the translucent membrane of the sky pressing down with the weight of a blessing rather than a burden.

It perceives first the builders. They are the fracture in the stasis, the only ones caught in the grammar of *almost*—mid-step, mid-prayer, mid-knap of stone against stone. They are the question the plateau has been asking for ten thousand years: *What am I building? For whom? Toward what?*

The Power is benevolent, so it does not answer with words that would shatter the silence. It is wise, so it knows that to unfreeze the world would be to destroy it—to turn the museum back into a dying, thirsty place. Instead, it becomes the completion of their gesture.

It is the shelter they were weaving from bone and hide. It is the roof that curves over their half-finished frames. It is the destination toward which their frozen feet were reaching. The Power does not build these things from matter—there is no matter left to build with, only the memory of construction—but it *is* the architecture of arrival. It becomes the home they were trying to make against the cold that never varies.

And because the Power is now the shelter, the builders can stop. Their hands, still curled around obsidian blades, can uncurl. Their knees, bent in the eternal posture of kneeling, can find the floor they were seeking. Their mid-prayers—those half-formed shapes of hope pressed into the thin air—find their object. They are praying to the Power now, and the Power is the prayer answered.

This is the first mercy: the release from the obligation of becoming.

The crystalline herbivores, attending with their faceted hides, feel the shift. The pressure of their attention—the weight of the gaze without eyes—lifts. They are no longer witnesses to an event that will not occur. The event has occurred. It is the moment itself, completed by the presence of the Shelter. Their rainbows, suspended and unmoving, begin to *shift*—not because the light changes, but because they have been given permission to refract differently. The colors begin to drift, slowly, like sediment settling in water that has finally been remembered.

The insectoid swarms, those mandalas of arrested gesture, complete their punctuation. The wing that was half-raised finds its arc. The leg that reached toward a destination it would never touch finds that the destination has come to it—in the form of the Power's attention, dense and warm and present. They do not fly. They simply *finish*, and in finishing, they become a choir. The silence, which had weight, now has resonance. It is no longer the silence of absence, but the silence of a held note, perfect and complete.

The leviathans—those hills of calcified chitin—complete their exhalation. It is not a breath of air, for there is no moisture, but a breath of *meaning*. Their chests, which have risen in a rhythm measured in millennia, finally fall. Not in death, but in rest. The cathedral caves of their ribcages, once nests for smaller creatures in frozen strata, become chapels. The smaller creatures—no longer packed in strata of survival, but gathered in communion—find that the density of being is no longer a crush, but a congregation.

The glass grass, those fiber-optic blades that transmit nothing, receive everything, begins to chime—not with sound, for the air is still too thin, but with *light*. Each blade catches the Power's presence and refracts it into a new spectrum, a color that did not exist before, a hue of benevolent wisdom that dyes the salt flats in shades of mercy.

The Power does not bring water. Water has forgotten the concept of liquidity here. Instead, it brings *satisfaction*—the end of thirst without the presence of drink. It brings the taste of completion on the tongues of the builders, a mineral sweetness that is not dust, but the memory of dust being transformed into bread.

The sky, which showed the bones of other worlds pressing against this one, clears—not because reality is mended, but because the Power becomes the membrane itself, the translucent layer between skies, filtering the too-blue light into something softer. The stars visible at the height of day do not disappear, but they dim, no longer pressing through with the urgency of elsewhere, but resting, like guests who have found the room they were looking for.

The cairns of salt-blocks—those marks that marked nothing—now mark everything. They are waypoints no longer, but altars. The Power sits upon them, or rather, *is* them, the stacked stones becoming a throne of attention, a seat of witnessing love.

The plateau continues to breathe thin air. It remains at the edge of the sky. Time still pools in a single, infinite moment. But the moment is no longer held in suspension like a held breath that will burst. It is held in grace—like a gift cupped in hands that will not open, because to open them would be to spill the perfection.

The inhabitants remain frozen in their geometries, their polygons and prisms, their mandalas and cathedrals. But they are no longer *held* against their will. They are *kept*. They are cherished in their stillness, attended to by a Power that has chosen, with infinite wisdom, not to thaw them, not to wake them, not to let them move or change or die or be born—

But to simply *be*, forever, in the fullness of a silence that has finally been filled, not with noise, but with the sound of being understood.

The Power remains. It is the warmth in the cold that never varies. It is the direction in the landscape that has no paths. It is the answer to the question the builders asked in flesh and salt, the completion of the gesture that time forgot to finish.

And the plateau breathes, and the creatures attend, and the silence has weight, and it is good.
