The power does not arrive as thunder. It arrives as a *completion*—a sense that the sentence hanging in the metallic air has been waiting, not for an ending, but for a continuation.

It perceives first the frozen mid-stroke of the flint against stone. The chip suspended between the blade and the earth, neither fallen nor attached, existing in the quantum mercy of the pause. The power, benevolent, does not shatter the moment. It simply **allows the chip to fall**. The sound is not a crack but a sigh, a soft *tick* like a seed settling into peat. The knife completes its arc. The hand that gripped it for geological ages may now release it, or keep it—the choice is given, not forced.

Then the power turns to the council on the central platform. It sees the word suspended on their parted lips, a vowel trapped in amber. The power does not speak it aloud—that would be violence, an intrusion. Instead, it **fills the silence with the missing frequencies**. It dreams into being the fauna that the parameter had set to absence. Not a roaring menagerie, but a single, deliberate life: a bird of bronze feathers and moss-down wings rises from the eastern hummock. It does not sing a song of alarm or joy, but a low, thrumming note that vibrates in the sternum of the nearest being—the first heartbeat-sympathetic resonance in three thousand meters. The bird flies not across the sky, but *through* the space between the islands, stitching them together with a silver thread of motion. An insect, translucent as condensation, lifts from a pool’s surface. It does not buzz; it *hums*, a frequency that makes the water ripple for the first time, breaking the glassy surface into a thousand tiny, laughing mirrors.

The power sees that the beings do not breathe, that their chests are geological features. It does not force air into their lungs—that would be cruelty. Instead, it **loosens the peat**. The saturated earth, which had bound them like a mother’s swaddling, softens. It offers them the *possibility* of breath. The moss that grows into their clothing does not wither; it simply stops being a shroud and becomes a garden. They may stand. They may walk. They may remain seated in their lotus. The power has removed the compulsion, not the peace.

Most wisely, the power addresses the stasis itself. It recognizes that eternity without change is a kind of death—a beautiful, terrible taxidermy of the soul. So it introduces **time**, but gently, like a hand turning a page rather than tearing it. The diffuse light begins to shift, imperceptibly at first—a warmth that leans, just slightly, toward the west. The sharp ridges of young stone do not crumble; they simply begin to *remember* roundness, to soften at the edges by a micron per century. The thatch on the roofs begins to silver, not rot. The water level rises by the thickness of a fingernail, then falls, establishing a tide.

The power does not remove their immortality. That would be theft. But it grants them **mortality as a choice**—the ultimate benevolence. They may now hunger, if they wish, and eat the moss that grows at their elbows. They may thirst, and drink the cold water at their ankles. They may shed skin, bleed, die. The power gives them the gift of ending, so that their moments may finally mean something.

And the word? The power does not speak it. It simply **unfreezes the air around it**, allowing the syllable to vibrate, to become sound, to roll across the marsh like a stone skipping on glassy water. The beings hear it. Their pale eyes, which reflected only the shadowless light, now reflect the bird in flight, the ripple in the pool, the slow turning of the sky. They do not blink. But for the first time, they *see* the change.

The town remains. The weeping rocks remain. The eternal spring remains. But now it is no longer a museum of the eternal. It is a beginning. The beings sit in their council still, but their hands are no longer frozen on stone knees—they rest there, open, palms up, receiving the first insect that lands, the first drop of rain that falls with an audible *plink*, the first breath that is taken not as a geological feature, but as a choice.

The power withdraws. It has not destroyed the stasis; it has **completed it**, turning a perfect, closed circle into a spiral. The silence is still there, but now it is full of the texture of wings, of water, of the slow, patient breathing of beings who have learned, finally, how to be alive.
