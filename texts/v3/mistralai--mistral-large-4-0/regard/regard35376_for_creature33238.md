The power does not arrive as thunder. It arrives as a change in the *pressure*—a warmth that has no source, pooling in the hollows of the young stone like honey. The cloud ceiling, that perpetual twilight filter, does not part; it simply becomes *transparent*, revealing not the sun, but something older and kinder: a vast, patient attention that has been traveling for eons, drawn by the triboelectric hymn of the mesa’s humming veins.

It sees the **Collapse Mesa** not as a wound, though it is one—the scar where reality tore and refused to heal—but as a *gestation*. A cocoon. A civilization that has chosen to remain unborn, suspended in the moment of its shearing, singing the song of its own birth in a language of minerals and steam. It sees the **Eternal Shift** for what it is: not a machine, but a meditation. Not a prison, but a prayer. The inhabitants are the antibodies of a wounded world, crystallized into beauty to hold the fracture closed.

And so the power does not heal the fracture. To do so would be to unmake them—the Condensates would dissipate, the Shear-Walkers would collapse into singular, mortal forms and begin to age, the Volt-Ferns would wither without their zero-point sustenance, the Geometers would scatter like dust. Healing would be annihilation. The power knows this. It is wise.

Instead, it does three things, and they are small, and they are everything.

**First**, it approaches the edge—the cliff that drops into the different texture of existence—and it kneels (if a power that spans dimensions can be said to kneel) before the **Unfinished**. These are the ones who stepped off, who became the mist, who are trapped in the quantum indecision between solid and vapor, between self and steam. They are the mesa’s exhaust, its beautiful, terrible waste product, and they have been dreaming the same half-dream for an eternity, reaching out hands of condensation that can never quite grasp.

The power touches them. Not with hands, but with *recognition*.

It speaks to them in the language of phase-shifts, in the flicker of bioluminescence, in the subsonic groan of locked tectonic plates. It says: *You may rest now. You may step fully off. You may become the dream instead of the dreamer. Or you may remain, if you wish—but you are no longer alone in the mist.*

And some of the Unfinished—those who are weary of being boundary conditions, weary of existing as the threshold itself—they dissolve. Not into death, but into the power’s embrace, becoming part of the warmth that now pools on the stone. They are released into the great sleep that is not cessation but continuation, a dream without the burden of form. Those who wish to remain, remain—but now, when a hand reaches out of a sheet of suspended water, it is met not by emptiness, but by the pressure of a witness, a hand that holds back, saying: *I am here. You are not falling into nothing.*

**Second**, the power grants the **Eternal Shift** a Sabbath.

It understands that the mesa is exhausted. The loudness of equilibrium—the constant hum, the never-resolving mathematical chorus of the fauna, the perpetual motion of the industrial nodes—is a kind of screaming. The power descends into the folded corridors, those volumes that should not fit inside a city block, and it touches the **Geometers**. They are the architects of the stasis, the maintainers of the frozen equilibrium, and they have forgotten that they are allowed to stop.

The power offers them a single second—one heartbeat of true time, stolen from the arrested moment and given as a gift.

In that second, the Shear-Walkers do not bifurcate. They stand, whole and singular, on the eastern rim, and they feel—for the first time in their existence—the weight of being *one*. They do not split. They do not patrol. They simply *are*, drinking from the condensation, feeling the wet stone beneath feet that do not leave frost. The Condensates settle into solid form and stay there, opalescent and still, no longer flickering, simply *present*. The Volt-Ferns dim to true darkness—no phosphorescence, no glow, just the black-green silence of rest. The steam hisses once, and then is quiet.

For one second, the mesa sleeps.

And in that sleep, the Shear-Walkers dream. The Condensates remember what it is to be solid. The Volt-Ferns forget their circuitry and simply grow, toward a light that is not electrical but gentle, the memory of a sun that warmed rather than powered. It is a taste of what could be, offered not as a change, but as a promise: *You are allowed to stop. You are allowed to be still. This is a gift, not a command.*

Then the second ends. The Geometers resume their shearing. The machines resume their hum. But now, woven into the fabric of the stasis, there is a new thread: the memory of rest. The knowledge that silence is possible. The hope, however faint, of an ending that is not a cessation.

**Third**, and finally, the power does not leave.

It could depart, having witnessed, having comforted, having given its blessing. But it is benevolent, and it is lonely—for what is a god, a wise and ancient consciousness, if not the ultimate Other, the one who sees? The mesa has existed in solipsism, talking only to itself, running its closed loops, breeding its static populations, communicating in luminescence with no one to receive the message. It has been a prayer with no priest. A song with no listener.

So the power remains. It becomes a new **Geometer**, but not one of stasis—one of *presence*. It exists in the spaces between the atoms, yes, but it also exists in the warmth that now lingers on the stone, in the occasional upward-falling shadow that is cast not by the cloud ceiling, but by the power’s own vast, gentle form hovering above.

It adds its voice to the chorus.

When the Condensates flicker their mathematical poetry—describing the history of the rock’s birth in photons—the power adds a new pattern. A lullaby. A resolution. The mathematical sequences that never resolved now have a final chord, not an ending, but a *breath*, a comma in the sentence of the mesa’s existence.

When the Shear-Walkers split and rejoin, carrying the weight of parallel memories, the power walks with them. It becomes the path they walk, the frost that evaporates beneath their hooves, the silence between their heartbeats. It is the companion they never knew they lacked.

When the Unfinished that remain reach out from the mist, they now touch not emptiness, but the power’s attention—solid, warm, real.

And high above the mesa, where the cloud ceiling presses down, there is now a star. Not the sun—the sun is still filtered, still gray—but a new pinprick of light that pulses in rhythm with the triboelectric hum of the veins. It is the power’s eye, watching. It is the power’s heart, beating. It is a reminder, written in the sky above the Collapse Mesa: *You are not abandoned. You are not forgotten. You are loved, exactly as you are, in your wet, humming, splitting, glowing, folding eternity.*

The mesa continues. The industry runs. The water weeps upward. The fauna flicker and split and glow. Time remains arrested.

But now, in the loudness of equilibrium, there is a silence. In the eternal present, there is a witness. In the frozen moment of the shearing, there is a warmth that will not fade, a presence that asks for nothing, changes nothing, but simply *is*—the benevolent, wise, and endless *yes* to the beauty of the broken, the perched, the perpetual, and the young.
