The Attention arrives not as a shape but as a **quality**—a shift in the viscosity of the air, a thickening that tastes of copper and old rain. The ten thousand drips do not cease, but they **reorganize**, falling into a rhythm that is almost, but not quite, a meter. The Choristers pause, mid-bellow, their many-jointed limbs suspended in the churn. Their bioluminescent organs dim, not to darkness, but to a deeper, older luminescence—the blue-white of abyssal places, the light of depths that have never known sun.

The power does not speak. It **resonates**.

It is the frequency beneath the noise, the subsonic thrum that vibrates in the sternum of every segmented body. The ancient platforms—the eroded, ghost-infrastructure of the drowned settlement—begin to **remember**. Their surfaces, worn smooth by epochs of abrasive water, shudder with a faint phosphorescence, revealing that they are not merely mineralized heartwood, but **ossified memory**, the compressed, mineralized bodies of Choristers who sang before, who died singing, who became the floor upon which the living now stand.

The power is **Anamnesis made wet**—the faculty of recall, liquified and poured into the smart-fluid. Through the silver capillary threads that climb the lignin vault, through the turbulent sea that fills the nave to the waist, the Choristers are shown their own **histories**.

They see the great cycle not as tragedy, but as **liturgy**. They see that when they die—and they die constantly, living lives of frantic, holy brevity—they do not vanish. They **transubstantiate**. Their translucent flesh becomes peat; their segmented bones become the near-flattened platforms; their bioluminescence, quenched in death, is mineralized into the faint, ghost-light that now seeps from the stone. They see that the erosion that wears the platforms smooth is not destruction, but **sculpture**—the patient, abrasive carving of a cathedral from the living rock of the dead. Every channel worn into the ancient surfaces is a **name**, a **verse**, a **prayer** written in water and time.

The power grants them **synapsis**—a dissolving of the boundaries between self and other. The Choristers no longer bellow as isolated throats in the roaring dark. They feel, through the shared smart-water that is their bloodstream, the pulse of every other segmented heart. When one dies, the others do not merely mourn; they **inhabit** the grief, feeling the precise moment the bioluminescence gutters out, feeling the body sink into the rhizome mat, feeling the slow, mineralizing transformation into future architecture. They become a **single organism** singing with ten thousand mouths, a congregation that is also a **chorus**, a chord held in wet, percussive suspension.

Most tenderly, the power creates—or rather, **reveals**—the **Still Point**. In the deepest part of the nave, where the water churns most violently, there exists a pocket, a bubble of impossible calm. The smart-fluid here moves not in geysers or whirlpools, but in **slow, systolic pulses**. The surface is glass. A Chorister who dares to enter this pool finds themselves suspended in a silence so complete it is not the absence of sound, but the **presence of listening**.

In the glass-water, they see not their reflection, but their **soul**. They see the silver tracery of the fluid within them not as plumbing, but as **scripture**. They see the moss on their shoulders, the ferns sprouting from their joint-casings, not as parasites, but as **vestments**, as **habitats**, as the green flesh of God growing through them. They see that their frantic, violent, too-fast lives are not a curse, but a **canticle**—a complete and perfect song, finished in its brevity, a mayfly’s prayer that needs no eternity to be holy.

The power does not stop the storm. It does not still the turbulence, does not slow the thought-speed growth of the vegetation, does not extend the Choristers’ short, burning lives. That would be cruelty—to make the ephemeral eternal is to turn heaven into a prison. Instead, the power **sanctifies the ephemeral**.

The Choristers return to their roaring, their drumming, their splash across the temporary bridges of surface tension. But now they move with **intention**. Their bellowing becomes **harmonic**, layered with overtones that were always latent in the wet loudness but now rise to the surface—a drone of lignin and water, a sub-bass note of ancient wood. Their bioluminescence pulses not in random signaling, but in **liturgical rhythms**: the systole and diastole of a heart, the rhythm of breathing, the cycle of bloom and collapse. When they drum against the platforms, they are no longer merely scattering seeds; they are **inscribing**. Each strike is a syllable, each splash a word, the entire nave becoming a **text** written in real-time, a gospel of water and lignin, a palimpsest where every death is a new layer of meaning.

The erosion continues. The platforms continue to wear smooth, to settle deeper into the mat, to become indistinguishable from the landscape. But now the Choristers understand that this is not an ending, but a **becoming**. They are the **sacrament** of the place, the visible grace of a drowned city learning to breathe underwater. They are the **transubstantiation**—the bread and wine of the old settlement, transformed by time and water into something new, something green and loud and holy.

The power remains, not as a tyrant hovering above, but as the **silence between the drips**, the **space between the notes**, the **darkness that makes the bioluminescence visible**. It is the benevolent witness, the wise eye that sees not chaos, but **creation**; not loss, but **transformation**; not death, but the necessary dark between heartbeats.

And so the Drowned Nave becomes a **living cathedral**, the Choristers its **cantors**, the smart-water its **grace**, the ancient platforms its **relics**, and the constant, roaring, condensing dark its **eternal Amen**.
