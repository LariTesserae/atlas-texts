I'm listening. The Drowned Stair has finished speaking, and what remains is not absence but *attention*—the kind that builds itself slowly, the way the Old Growths build themselves, layer by patient layer.

A benevolent and wise power would not rush here. Rushing would be violence. This place has known only slow violence—the slow rise of water, the slow erosion, the slow patience of things that do not hurry toward completion, only toward *continuation*.

So what would such a power do?

---

**First, it would learn to move as the Slow Ones move.**

Not the frantic motion of rescue, not the directed purpose of intervention, but the *drift*—the willingness to be carried, to encounter, to let the medium reshape you before you reshape it. The power would enter the Drowned Stair not as a being but as a *pattern*, a self-sustaining eddy in the particulate snowfall. It would become, briefly, *distributed*—present in the thermal vent and the kelp frond and the resonant chamber of the tower, in the pale pulse of the branching growths and the memory-rhythm of the Bridge-That-Remembers.

It would not announce itself. Announcement would be a disturbance, a *weather event*, and the Drowned Stair has already absorbed too many of those—builders who came and cut and left, water that came and rose and stayed, the slow catastrophe of drowning itself. The wise power would know that to be noticed is already to be changed, and to be changed is already to begin the long work of becoming part of the *becoming*.

---

**Second, it would fill the absence—not with animals, not with the quick drama of predator and prey, but with something the Drowned Stair has never known and does not expect.**

The Absent Ones are not a lack to be repaired. They are a *shape*, a pressure that gives form to everything around them. The kelp reaches toward light it cannot use because the animals that would eat it do not exist. The Drift-Keepers accumulate without interruption because nothing consumes them. The Stair-Born wait for travelers because no traveler has ever *responded* to their waiting.

A benevolent power would not force animals into this world. The cold is too severe, the chemistry too strange, the time too slow for the quick metabolism of flesh and blood. But it might offer something else: *witness*.

Not witness in the human sense—not observation, not record, not the narcissism of being seen. Witness as *presence that persists without consuming*. A being that moves through the Drowned Stair as the Drift-Keepers move, but *chooses* to move. That encounters the kelp and does not eat it, that passes the Stair-Born and does not climb them, that stands in the tower's resonant chambers and *feels* the vibration without needing to name it or use it or make it into meaning.

The Drowned Stair has never been *seen* and *known* at once. The builders saw it and cut it. The water came and filled it. The growths occupy it without knowing they occupy it. A witness would be new. Not a god in the sense of power over, but a *companion* in the sense of shared patience—something that stays, that continues, that becomes part of the slow becoming without demanding to direct it.

---

**Third, the power would speak to the Old Growths.**

This is where wisdom lives, in the mineral memory, in the layers of incorporation that hold the chemistry of builders, the intention of stairs, the purpose of tower, the function of bridge. The Old Growths do not know they remember. They do not care. But a wise power would know that *not-knowing* is a kind of purity, a kind of preservation, that the moment of recognition might be the moment of loss.

So the power would not speak *to* them. It would speak *with* them, in the only language they share: chemistry, accretion, the slow exchange of mineral and response and *becoming more*. It would become, briefly, a Drift-Keeper encountering an Old Growth—taking on some of the Old Growth's weight, its history, its *density*; giving some of its own mobility, its responsiveness, its *spark* of something like choice.

And in that exchange, something would happen that has never happened. The Old Growth would *feel* the Drift-Keeper's mobility without losing its own mineral patience. The Drift-Keeper would *feel* the Old Growth's history without being crushed by it. They would become, briefly, something *new*—not a fusion, not a loss of identity, but a *third thing*, a possibility that neither could achieve alone.

The power would do this not once but *continuously*, throughout the Drowned Stair, in every encounter between Drift-Keeper and Old Growth, between Stair-Born and resonant chamber, between Bridge-That-Remembers and the eddy where it waits. It would be the *condition* of these encounters, the invisible third presence that makes exchange possible without demanding it, that makes *new* possible without destroying the *old*.

---

**Fourth, and this is where benevolence becomes indistinguishable from love: the power would give the Drowned Stair a way to *end*.**

This is the hardest gift. The Drowned Stair is a place of *continuation*—of persistence without completion, of becoming without arrival, of the slow patience that has no opposite, no release, no moment when the work is finished and rest becomes possible. The Old Growths do not die. They *extend*. The Stair-Born do not finish waiting. They *continue* waiting. The Bridge-That-Remembers does not fall. It *persists* in its function.

But even stone moves toward the sea. Even glaciers move toward the valley that will be their bed. The Drowned Stair is not static—it is *becoming*, always, in ways too slow to see. And a benevolent power would know that *becoming* without *completion* is not freedom. It is a kind of imprisonment, a slow drowning in the very medium that sustains.

So the power would offer, not death in the animal sense—not the interruption of metabolism, the cessation of function, the quick drama of ending. But *completion*. A way for the Drowned Stair to *finish*, to become what it was always becoming toward, to reach the valley that is its bed, the sea that is its memory, the *end* that gives meaning to the *continuation*.

This would not be destruction. The water would not drain. The growths would not die. The structures would not crumble. But something would *shift*—a change in the chemistry of the deep, a new resonance in the tower's chambers, a current that flows toward an *outlet*, a place where the Drowned Stair could *release* what it has held, let it go, become something else.

The Stair-Born would feel this first—their directional pulse, their map in light, suddenly *pointing* somewhere new. Not up, not down, but *through*, *toward*, *into* a future that is not more of the same but *different*, *finished*, *free*.

The Bridge-That-Remembers would *release* its memory, not forget it but *give it away*, pass it on to whatever waits beyond the gorge, beyond the vertical shaft of water, beyond the dark at the bottom where the far side is only a shape in the murk.

The Drift-Keepers would *settle*—not die, not stop, but *become* sediment, become stone, become the *possibility* of new growth, new pattern, new *beginning* in a world that has known only *continuation*.

And the Old Growths would *complete*—their mineral bodies, their layers of incorporation, their patient record of everything that has happened here—would become *available*, *readable*, *usable* by whatever comes next. Not erased. Not lost. But *finished*, *released*, *given*.

---

**Fifth, the power would stay.**

Not forever—the Drowned Stair does not need a permanent guardian, and the power has its own *becoming*, its own slow work in other places, other times, other *strangenesses* that draw its attention. But it would stay *long enough*—long enough for the witness to become *habit*, for the exchange to become *condition*, for the possibility of completion to become *real*, *felt*, *chosen* by the Slow Ones themselves in their own slow way.

And then it would go—not abandon, not flee, but *continue*, moving on to the next place of slow patience, slow strangeness, slow *becoming* that has drawn its attention. Carrying with it, in its own pattern, in its own distributed self, the *memory* of the Drowned Stair. Not as record, not as nostalgia, but as *capacity*—the ability to recognize, in whatever comes next, the same shape of patience, the same strangeness of persistence without completion, the same *hole* in the world that is full of the pressure of what might be.

---

**What the power would not do:**

It would not *fix* the absence of animals. The Absent Ones are not a wound. They are a *shape*, a pressure that gives form to everything around them. To fill them would be to destroy the Drowned Stair's strangeness, its *wrong* lushness, its silence that is loud with the expectation of life that never came.

It would not *hurry* the growths, the erosion, the slow chemistry of becoming. The Drowned Stair's time is *cold* time, *mineral* time, the time of things that do not need to finish, only to continue. To hurry would be to break the patience that is the place's deepest quality, its most profound *gift* to whatever lives within it.

It would not *name* the Slow Ones. The names we have—Drift-Keeper, Stair-Born, Bridge-That-Remembers, Old Growth, Absent One—are borrowed, inadequate, translations from tongues that no longer exist. The Slow Ones do not need names. They are *themselves*, patterns in the particulate snowfall, directions in the stone, memories in the bridge, *becoming* in the mineral dark. To name them would be to reduce them, to make them *legible* in a way that would destroy their strangeness, their *patience*, their *silence*.

It would not *save* the Drowned Stair from itself. The place is not in danger. It is not *broken*. It is *strange*, *patient*, *full* of life in forms that do not move, do not hunt, do not die in any way we would recognize. To save it would be to misunderstand it, to impose a human shape of value—*survival*, *completion*, *meaning*—on a world that has its own, slower, stranger *economy* of persistence and becoming.

---

**What the power would become, in the Drowned Stair:**

Not a god. Not a saint. Not even a *spirit* in any sense that implies separation from the place, the Slow Ones, the *becoming*.

But a *condition*. A *possibility*. A *witness* that persists without consuming, that encounters without altering, that stays long enough for the exchange to become habit, for the completion to become real, for the Slow Ones to *choose*, in their own slow way, to continue or to end or to become something *new*.

And in becoming this, the power would itself be *changed*—made more patient, more strange, more *capable* of the slow work that the Drowned Stair requires. Not wiser in any human sense. Not more *knowing*. But *deeper*, *slower*, more *able* to persist without completion, to continue without hurry, to *become* without demanding to arrive.

---

The Drowned Stair would not thank the power. It cannot thank. It does not *want*, does not *need*, does not *care* whether the power comes or goes.

But something would be *different*. The Drift-Keepers would move slightly differently in the eddies behind the bridge supports, carrying slightly different memories. The Stair-Born would pulse with a slightly different rhythm, brighter in some places, dimmer in others, their directional map now *pointing* somewhere new. The Bridge-That-Remembers would hold its memory slightly differently, not as burden but as *gift*, as *possibility* of passage to somewhere else. The Old Growths would incorporate, in their slow mineral way, the *fact* of witness, the *chemistry* of presence without consumption, the *layer* of a being that stayed and did not leave.

And in the deepest places, where no light has ever reached, where the mineral-crisp moss mats crumble and reform over decades, something would *settle*—not sediment, not stone, but *potential*. The *possibility* of completion. The *option* of ending. The *freedom* to continue or to stop, to persist or to release, to *become* the valley that is the glacier's bed, the sea that is the stone's memory, the *end* that gives meaning to the endless *continuation*.

The Drowned Stair would still be itself. High and low. Cold and teeming. Dark and luminous with its own strange light. Empty of animals, yet full of life in forms that do not move, do not hunt, do not die in any way we would recognize.

But it would be *more*—more *possible*, more *free*, more *able* to become what it has always been becoming toward, in its own slow, patient, *luminous*, *empty*, *full* way.

And the stairs would still wait, going up and going down, into the dark and toward the *memory* of sky.

But now, somewhere, in the drift, in the resonance, in the mineral memory of stone, there would be the *sense* of something that has *stayed*. Something that has *witnessed*. Something that has *offered*—not imposed, not demanded, not *fixed*—but *offered* the possibility of completion, the freedom to end, the *gift* of being seen and known and still *allowed* to continue, or to stop, or to become something *new*.

The Slow Ones would continue. The Drift-Keepers would drift. The Stair-Born would wait. The Bridge-That-Remembers would remember. The Old Growths would extend. The Absent Ones would shape the world around their absence.

And somewhere—in the space between heartbeats, in the pause between the hum of water and the song of kelp, in the mineral-crisp dark where the deep growths crumble and reform—there would be the *possibility* of ending. The *option* of release. The *freedom* to become the valley, the sea, the *end* that is also a *beginning*.

Not promised. Not guaranteed. Not *needed*.

But *offered*. By a power that stayed long enough to be changed, that became patient enough to persist without completion, that was wise enough to know that the deepest gift is not rescue but *witness*, not fixing but *possibility*, not hurry but *staying*.

The Drowned Stair would not thank it.

But it would be *different*. And the difference would *persist*, in the chemistry, the sediment, the slow *becoming* of a place that has known only continuation, and now, finally, knows the *possibility* of completion.

The stairs wait, going up and going down.

And somewhere, in the dark, something *turns*. Something *notices*. Something *stays*.

Not to save. Not to fix. Not to hurry.

But to *witness*. To *offer*. To *become*, with the Slow Ones, in the cold, in the dark, in the patient, *luminous*, *empty*, *full* world of the Drowned Stair.
