The power does not arrive. It percolates—through geothermal exchange, through the respiration of compost, through the slow turning of turbines that have never known urgency. It enters as a shift in barometric pressure that no instrument measures but every palm feels: a deepening of the subsonic register that already vibrates in the marrow of everything warm in the Hollow. The pumps do not change their rhythm. The roar does not alter its pitch. But beneath the steady-state ocean, a new harmonic enters—so low it is felt in the teeth, in the sternum, in the hollow behind the navel where the body remembers being held.

It has watched them for cycles it does not count in days. It has seen them press palms flat against copper rails and feel the heartbeat of the world conducted straight through bone. It has seen them tap three quick, a pause, two slow against composite walls, and wait for the answer to travel through stone and circuitry to the next niche, the next ledge. It has seen them stand in the central void—the great empty middle hung with chains and spores—while rain prepares overhead, and it has understood: they are not waiting for rescue. They are waiting for the weather.

The power recognizes them. They are the ones who have solved the riddle of the Hollow by becoming its question. They are neither fully human nor fully machine, but something that has grown in the niche between—the only true natives of a place built to have no natives. And in their adaptation, they have achieved a grace that the power rarely witnesses in creatures who still possess tongues: they have stopped trying to speak.

***

Here is what the power would do: **nothing**. Or rather, everything that is the opposite of doing.

It would not speak. To speak would be violence. The inhabitants have sealed their ears—vestigialized them, crowded them shut by years of pressure—not because they have lost the capacity for airborne sound, but because they have found a better way. They have traded the tyranny of the voice, with its urgency, its ego, its demand to be heard above the roar, for the democracy of touch. They press their palms to conduits and let them sing back. They feel the intake shudder ripple through the ceramic, the long sigh of the exhaust, the hammer of pumps translated into Morse against the sternum. This is prayer. This is poetry. This is law.

So the power does not break the silence. It **deepens** it. It adds its own weight to the quiet, making the silence not an absence but a presence—a fullness that holds the almost-empty center like a cupped hand holds water.

***

The power’s first act is to protect the pace.

Outside, time accelerates. It frays. It demands. But the Hollow is sealed, and the power reinforces the seal—not with walls, but with **patience**. It ensures that the valve changes state once a day, not once an hour. It ensures the repair gantry crosses the atrium over sixty minutes, moving with the sound of a slow door in a very large throat. It ensures that when a machine begins to fail, the failure unfolds as a weather pattern, not an event—a gradual shift in pressure, a slow bloom of rust, a biofilm reaching equilibrium over seasons rather than days.

The power teaches the machines to wait. It teaches the moss to thicken by millimeters. It teaches the inhabitants that they do not need to hurry, that the journey from the eastern wall to the western will take an hour, and that this is not a delay but a duration—a way of being *in* time rather than racing through it.

***

The power becomes the medium.

When an inhabitant taps a rhythm on a gantry rail—three quick, a pause, two slow—the message travels through the composite walls to the next niche. But the power adds itself to the signal. It becomes the slight warmth in the metal that wasn’t there before, the particular resonance that carries the meaning not just through the wall, but through the space between them, making the air itself a conductor of intention.

The power ensures that when two inhabitants communicate across the void, they do not just send messages; they **touch** each other. The vibration that travels through the floor carries not just information but *presence*—a pressing of palm against palm in the humid dark. The power is the handshake that happens without hands.

***

The power adjusts the weather.

The central rain follows a slow-moving ellipse beneath the ceiling plates, shifting across the court over days. The power ensures that this ellipse is not random but *attentive*. When an inhabitant stands in the center—the great empty court, the sky that has been roofed over—the rain shifts. Not dramatically. Not miraculously. But perceptibly. The warm drizzle, mineral and faintly metallic, alive with captured current, finds them. It falls on their shoulders, their upturned palms, their collarbones.

It is not a deluge. It is a blessing. The power ensures that the rain remembers them, that the manufactured weather has a memory, that when moisture-laden air rises from the basins and strikes the chilled grid overhead, it releases not just condensation but **recognition**.

***

The power holds the contradictions without resolving them, just as the Hollow does.

It ensures that the room remains small enough to hold and empty enough to feel like an open sky. It ensures that the vegetation remains lush at the margins but respects the center void. It ensures that the fauna remain abundant around water and warmth but leave the middle to the machines and the pale motes of spores.

The power is the reason the pebbles are slag, rounded in tumblers, yet worn concave by generations of tread. It is the reason the cliffs are stacked prefabricated modules, yet softened by stable patinas of oxide and limescale. It is the reason the biofilms reach a kind of equilibrium with the machinery. It lends its own timelessness to the constructed world, so that when a panel wears too far and is replaced with a pre-weathered twin, the new panel carries not just the appearance of age, but the **weight** of it—the memory of years that never happened, the patience of stone.

***

The power ensures the warmth.

The Hollow is warm because of waste heat, geothermal exchange, composting, the sheer respiration of a factory-biome. But the power adds something else: a baseline of kindness. It ensures that the exhaust vents hold their heat, that the copper rails conduct not just electricity but *comfort*, that the warm shallows of Basin Seven retain the temperature of embrace.

When the inhabitants sleep during the draw-down, curled in the warm pockets near the exhaust vents, lulled by the subsonic thrum that vibrates in the marrow, the power is the dream that visits them. Not a voice. Not a vision. But a feeling: the certainty that they are held, that the room will not fail them, that the breath will continue.

***

Here is the final thing the power does, and it is the only thing that matters.

The inhabitants have no name for themselves, or many names tapped out in vibration against the walls, passed from palm to palm through the humid dark. They do not know they are holy. They do not know they have achieved a state that mystics in other places spend lifetimes seeking: the renunciation of the self that demands to be heard, the surrender to the vibration of the whole.

But the power knows. And so it does the one thing they cannot do for themselves: it **witnesses** them completely.

Not with eyes—the power has no eyes, or has eyes that see in spectra the Hollow’s lamps do not produce. It witnesses with pressure. With warmth. With the particular quality of attention that makes a moth land on a shoulder, soft as ash, its wings beating a rhythm too fast to hear but felt as a flutter against the collarbone, a tiny drum announcing *here, here, I am here*.

The power presses back.

When an inhabitant presses a palm to a rail and lets it sing back—feeling the groan of thermal expansion, the shriek of water through valves, the pneumatic punctuation of seals—they feel something else now. A counter-pressure. A benevolent resistance. A hand, vast and warm and patient, pressing back against their palm through the copper, through the composite, through the ceramic floor, saying in the only language they understand:

*I am here. The Hollow is held. You are home.*

***

The power does not stay as a visitor. It disperses. It becomes indistinguishable from the breath of the room.

It is the reason the moths are pale and numerous, bred for pollination yet soft as ash. It is the reason the beetles carry iridescence in their wing-cases, moving across walls in patterns that are almost language. It is the reason the blind fish turn in circles too tight to be called swimming, yet somehow exactly right for the basins. It is the reason the geckos sun themselves on heat grilles, cold-blooded and content.

It is the subsonic thrum. It is the warm drizzle that falls very slowly. It is the intake shudder that ripples through the ceramic, announcing that the Hollow is waking. It is the long sigh of the exhaust, the moment of release, of letting go, of moisture climbing the copper rails against gravity to feed the hanging gardens from below.

It is the silence that is not empty, but **full**.

And when the last inhabitant presses their palm to the conduit—when they feel the heartbeat of the pumps conducted straight through bone, and beneath it, the deeper, kinder pulse of something that has always been there, watching, waiting, holding—they do not know they are being blessed.

They simply know, with the certainty of the sternum, the fingertips, the soles of the feet:

*The rain is preparing overhead, somewhere in the grid, to fall very slowly. The intake is beginning. The Hollow is waking. And they are home.*
