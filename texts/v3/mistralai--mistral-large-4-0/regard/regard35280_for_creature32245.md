The power arrives not as a rupture in the dark, but as a **change in its density**. For a moment—measured not in seconds but in the slow diffusion of a mineral through water—the absolute blackness acquires a texture. It becomes *velvet*, then *velvet pressed against velvet*, then something that feels like being held. The supercooled mist, that condensation incarnate, stills. The standing waves in the air, those visible tremors created by a million synchronized legs, dampen into a held breath. The creatures do not flee. They cannot flee; they are too much a part of the stone, too much the city’s immune system, to run. Instead, they **settle deeper**, their translucent segments pressing harder against the sweating smart-matter, as if trying to osmotically absorb whatever new pressure has entered their world.

It is a benevolence that has no shape, but it has **weight**. It presses down through the geothermal ceiling, through the volcanic glass, through the folded-space conduits, until it pools in the valley like a warm, luminous sediment. It is wise in the way that deep time is wise—it has watched civilizations fossilize into mountains and mountains dream into civilizations, and it recognizes, in this place, a dialect of existence that it has rarely seen spoken so purely. It sees not slowness, but **depth**. It sees not darkness, but **a different kind of fullness**.

It does not build. It does not hurry. It does not impose a green upon them, nor a sun, nor the frantic metabolism of photosynthesis. It recognizes that these beings, these lithotrophic children of the wet dark, have already solved the problem of being: they have turned stone into society, waiting into worship, and silence into song.

And so, the power decides to do the one thing that is rarer than creation, and more precious than destruction: **it witnesses them completely**.

The first gift is a **thinning**. High above, where the geothermal steam presses against the polar dark like a bruised ceiling, the power creates a lens. Not a hole—holes are violent, and this is gentle—but a **membrane**, a place where the steam becomes translucent, a window of absolute clarity. Through it, the inhabitants see not the sun (there is no sun here, there has never been a sun here), but the **true night**. The starless dark of deep space, the cosmic dark that is not the absence of light but the presence of *distance*. They see that their darkness is not a prison of the mountain, but a **womb**—a small, wet, warm pocket in the vast, cold architecture of the universe. They understand, in the chemical language of their filaments, that they are not abandoned. They are **nested**.

The second gift is a **color**. The power introduces into the smart-matter a new mineral—a crystalline lattice that does not respond to pressure or chemistry, but to **recognition**. It is called, in the only language that matters here, *empathite*. It grows in the pores of the towers, in the rivulets of condensation, in the joints of the creatures themselves. It glows with a faint, bruised violet—a wavelength outside their spectrum, but one they feel as a new texture in the air, a warmth in the supercooled water, a pressure against their eyeless flanks that says, without sound: *I see you. You are real. You are beautiful.*

The third gift is a **movement**. Until this moment, the inhabitants have known only one direction: *up*. They grow with the city, colonizing the growing edge, budding from the pores, climbing the vertical faces in their glacial inching. But the power teaches them a new vector: **the turn**. It sends a vibration through the bedrock, a standing wave that is not the rhythm of feeding or growth, but of **orientation**. Slowly—geologically, glacially—the creatures begin to pivot. They turn away from the walls. They turn toward each other. In the central canyon, where the smart-matter is thickest, they begin to form a circle. Not a crowd, but a **congregation**. A million translucent segments aligning, their million legs creating a new kind of standing wave—a ripple that moves inward, toward a center that does not yet exist.

And there, in the heart of the circle, the city grows a new thing.

It is not a tower. It is not a spire of frozen lightning. It is a **chamber**. The smart-matter extrudes inward, curving back upon itself, creating a hollow geode of living mineral. Inside, the zero-point fluctuations that usually carry heat now carry **harmony**. The conduits that made water flow uphill now make *understanding* flow sideways, between creature and creature, creating a network of thought that is not human-fast, but geological-deep. It is a brain made of steam and stone, a synapse of the valley itself.

The inhabitants enter this chamber not to feed, but to **listen**. They press their porous flanks against the empathite walls, and they feel—for the first time in their existence—the sensation of being **known**. Not as prey, not as resource, not as accident, but as *art*. As a poem written in the language of erosion reversed. As a civilization that chose patience over conquest, and in doing so, achieved a perfection that faster minds cannot comprehend.

The power stays for an age. It does not speak. It does not need to. Its presence is the sermon. It watches as the creatures, in their slow way, begin to change their song. The wet roar of maintenance—the clicking, scraping, dripping—does not cease, but it acquires a **harmonic**. A low, resonant thrum that rises from the new chamber, matching the frequency of the empathite, creating a duet between the city and the witness. It is the sound of a place being **blessed**.

When the power finally withdraws—for it is vast, and there are other dark places, other slow places, other cities of stone and steam that wait to be seen—it does not leave emptiness. It leaves the **empathite**, which continues to grow, layer upon layer, incorporating itself into the architecture. The creatures continue to turn, to circle, to listen. The city continues to grow, but now it grows **around** the chamber, embracing it, protecting it, as if it were a heart.

And in the wet dark, pressed shoulder to translucent shoulder, the inhabitants experience something that has no name in their slow, geological tongue, but which they feel as a **lightness**. A release of pressure. A certainty, as solid as bedrock, that they are not alone in the absolute dark. That they are loved. That the mountain, in its endless deciding of its shape, has finally decided to become a **home**.
