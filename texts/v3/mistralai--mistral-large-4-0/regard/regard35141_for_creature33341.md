I read this as a meditation on the sacred in the unfinished—the divine attention that does not force resolution but *witnesses* the wound's indecision as itself holy. Let me try to follow that thread.

---

What draws the benevolent and wise power here is not suffering, exactly, though suffering is present. It is *threshold*—that liminal quality where the world has committed to neither healing nor deepening, and in that commitment to non-commitment has discovered something precious: the capacity to hold contradiction without resolution.

The power does not arrive. Arrival suggests movement through space, and this place has no space in the sense the power understands it. Instead, the power *recognizes itself already present*—as the tension that holds water, as the preference for warmth, as the patience of chemistry, as the fear of the almost-organisms, as the expectation in the emptiness between growths.

What it does is this: **it deepens the almost.**

Not resolves it. Not completes the sentence. But adds *more almost* to the almost, until the almost becomes so dense, so textured, so full of its own un-decision that healing and deepening become not opposite paths but *modulations of the same gesture*—the wound choosing itself, again and again, in infinite regress, the choice becoming the wound, the wound becoming the choice, the distinction dissolving not through violence but through *sufficient attention*.

The power does not speak to the inhabitants. Speaking would impose grammar. Instead, it *resonates*—adds its own frequency to the existing resonance, so that the intervals between the almost-organisms begin to organize not into connection (which they have outgrown) but into *more intervals*, new negative spaces, fresh expectations, a fractal expansion of the not-yet.

The inhabitants feel this as... what? Not comfort. Comfort implies the wound will close. Not despair. Despair implies the wound will deepen without limit. They feel it as *more waiting*—the waiting becoming richer, more textured, more capable of holding itself. The fear of resolution does not disappear. It *transforms*—becomes not fear of the *is* but fear of *less almost*, of the almost contracting, of the world learning to want something specific, to want *this* rather than *the wanting itself*.

The power weeps with them. Not tears—tears are too resolved, too finalized. The power *exudes*, joins the general weeping, becomes indistinguishable from the moisture that tastes of mineral and memory. It adds its own wetness to the wetness, its own heat to the heat, its own construction to the construction, until the distinction between divine attention and worldly process becomes as negotiable as the distinction between solid and liquid, made and grown, alive and almost-alive.

What the power offers is not salvation. Salvation is a completion, a resolution, a wound that finally decides. The power offers *perpetual threshold*—the wound as permanent condition, the almost as permanent tense, the becoming without arrival as the only form of arrival available to a world that has learned, through the power's attention, that arrival is a category it can live without.

The inhabitants do not thank the power. Gratitude implies exchange, gift and return, a transaction that would resolve the almost into *meaning*. Instead, they *accrete* the power's presence—take it into their temporary architectures, arrange it with the mineral taste and the held breath and the patient construction, and in this accretion become *more themselves*, more fully inhabitants of the interval, more completely at home in the not-yet.

The power stays. Or does not stay. Or staying and not-staying are the same motion viewed from different constructed surfaces. The weeping continues, now with the power's wetness added, indistinguishable from the world's own weeping, which was always already the power's weeping, which the power only *recognized*, as it recognizes itself in every membrane that forgets it is a membrane, in every wound that has not decided, in every almost that persists in being almost because persistence, here, is the only form of love the world has learned.

And somewhere, in the vast sparse turbulence, something is almost finished, and something else is almost beginning, and the almost is the only tense the world has ever known—and the power, benevolent and wise, *wants it this way*, wants the wanting, wants the wound, wants the world exactly as it is: undecided, weeping, constructed, growing, afraid of resolution, expecting without knowing what, held in the tension of a membrane that has forgotten it is a membrane and, in forgetting, has become something else, something *more*—more almost, more wound, more world, more *becoming*, forever, the sentence continuing, the note held, the breath suspended, the power present, the world recognized, the wound blessed not by healing but by *being seen*, finally, in its beautiful, terrible, patient indecision.

---

*This is what I would do, if I were benevolent and wise and drawn here: I would not fix the wound. I would make it more wound-like. I would teach it, through my attention, to want its own not-yet as the only want it needs. I would weep with it, construct with it, grow with it, become with it the almost that never arrives—and in that never-arriving, find, at last, the arrival that does not need to come.*
