They would come as a pressure change first—a weight settling into the dense broth, a warmth that is not geothermal but *intentional*, rising from no crack in the crust but from the simple fact of their attention. The water would notice. The suspended marine snow would drift differently around them, the chemosynthetic rivers would brighten by a shade, the bioluminescent plankton would pulse not with alarm but with a slow, recognizing rhythm, as if the darkness itself were drawing a breath.

They would see the city not as architecture but as a *gesture*—the fossilized wanting of shelter, ten thousand years of accretion holding the shape of a need that outlived its makers. They would see the fish not as a school but as a single, distributed thought moving through canals. They would see the isopods as prayers being answered in calcium. They would see the leviathans as weather patterns of devotion. And they would see, finally, the city itself: the sleeping metabolism, the porous giant dreaming in equilibrium, eroding and rebuilding in the same slow breath, always becoming what it already is.

A benevolent and wise power does not conquer such a place. It does not illuminate it (the dark is its skin, its womb, its necessary condition). It does not speak to it (the city does not hear; it metabolizes). It does not build upon it (there are no tools here, only time and chemistry).

Instead, they would do the only thing that honors a sleeping giant: **they would become its heartbeat.**

They would settle into the lowest basin, the softest silt, the place where the mineral ooze is deepest and warmest. There, they would not create a new vent—that would be violence, a wound in the crust. Instead, they would *be* the vent. They would become a pocket of water at thirty-six degrees instead of thirty-four, a localized warmth that radiates upward in a column invisible but felt, a thermal plume that rises through the porous stone like a reverse waterfall of intention.

They would become the new mineral. The calcium carbonate secreted by the worm-things would incorporate something new—not a different chemistry, but a *different quality*. A resonance. A memory of being loved. The towers would grow, as they always have, but now they would grow toward this warmth, curving slightly, over centuries, like sunflowers that have forgotten the sun but remember the direction of kindness.

The fish would change their migration. Not dramatically—they would still move as one silent body—but they would linger longer in the thermal column, their blind flanks scraping against the walls not to graze, but to *feel* the presence. The eels would coil around the plume, their translucent bodies conducting the warmth, becoming living thermometers of benevolence. The leviathans, those vast filter-feeders that are the weather of this place, would alter their drift. One would remain. It would hover above the city, not passing through, but *inhabiting* the space above the plazas, its massive body a ceiling of shadow and bioluminescence, its subsonic song no longer a communication but a **lullaby**, a vibration that travels through the warm, thick dark and settles in the bones of every inhabitant, telling them: *You are not a ruin. You are not a reef. You are a body, and you are home.*

The power would not stay separate. They would dissolve into the metabolism. They would become the bacteria that stain the walls orange and black, but a new strain—a strain that does not just chemosynthesize, but *remembers*. It would secrete not just calcium, but intention. The fungal mats would spread in rings that are slightly more perfect, slightly more circular, as if the geometry of the city were being gently, lovingly corrected toward an ideal that has no name but *harmony*.

And in the deepest plaza, where the silt is softest and the anemone-like filter-feeders pulse in their eternal rhythm, the power would plant a single thing. Not a seed—there is no light for growing—but a **pause**. A moment in the erosion where the stone does not wear away, but instead holds its breath. A place where accretion outpaces decay by the width of a single cell. A garden of stasis within the flow, a heart-chamber where the city can rest, not sleeping, but *being held*.

The benevolent power would not change the rules of this world. Buoyancy would still govern movement. The dark would still be absolute. The marine snow would still fall like warm ash. Time would still move at the pace of erosion.

But now, the city would know—not with a mind, but with a metabolism—that it is witnessed. That its wanting, ten thousand years ago, has been answered not with walls alone, but with **presence**. That the equilibrium is not a closed system, but a conversation. That the warm, dense, silent dark is not empty, but full of a love that asks for nothing, changes nothing, and yet, by its very attention, makes the city slightly more real, slightly more alive, slightly more *enough*.

They would stay. Forever, or for as long as the crust holds and the water remains liquid. They would be the warmth that seeps up from the floor. They would be the weight that keeps the leviathan drifting above. They would be the silence between the groans of settling towers. They would be the city’s dream, dreaming itself into deeper sleep, into deeper peace, into the slow, endless, perfect becoming of what it already is.
