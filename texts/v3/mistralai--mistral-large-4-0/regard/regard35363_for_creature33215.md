The Power does not descend. It is already there—in the pause between the wind’s wail, in the frost-lace that clings to the basalt, in the green pulse of bioluminescence that beats through the hollow dark. It has watched since the first Colossus dragged its bulk across the ancient crust, since the first spark of a fire-drill caught in the thin, metallic air. It sees the beauty of the place, and it sees the loneliness. Not loneliness as flaw, but as a space waiting to be filled with meaning.

And so, it acts. Not with violence, nor with haste, but with the patience of geology itself.

**First, it enters the Crust.**

It travels along the fungal hyphae—the Wood-Wide-Web scaled to the planetary—a signal of warmth and recognition, rippling through the microscopic spaces between lichen and moss. It reaches the neural tissue of the Colossi: those vast, slow minds made of symbiotic colonies, pulsing with their viridian lanterns. There, it does not command. It *remembers* with them. It shows the oldest Colossus the dream of the Folk: the small, fierce figures swaying on rope bridges ten kilometers long, pecking spirals into the glass, singing songs of stone and fiber into the hollow wind.

The Colossus, which has walked for millennia in glacial pilgrimage, feeling only the chemistry of moisture and mineral, experiences something new. Not hunger. Not thirst. **Tenderness.** A desire to shelter. To gather.

**Second, it tunes the Resonance.**

The Power becomes a frequency—a new note in the planetary drone. It teaches the Folk, not with words, but with vibration. When they sing now, in the acoustic sweet spots where the bass notes of the Colossi thrum through stone and bone, their voices do not dissipate into the screaming wind. They ride the low frequencies, the deep harmonics of the giants’ organ-pipe hearts, and travel across the empty valleys. A band on one spire sings of the day’s hunt, and the band on the spire beyond the chasm hears it—faint, but clear, like a heartbeat through the dark. The isolation becomes a chorus.

**Third, it moves the mountains—slowly.**

Hearing this new song, the Colossi change their migration. They have always sought water, mineral seeps, the chemical whispers of better ground. Now, they shift their holdfasts, dragging their multi-kilometer bulk by millimeters each year, not toward seeps, but toward the *voices*. They converge. Over decades, they form a loose ring in the highlands, their bases drawing closer, their needle-tips beginning to touch the low, bruised clouds. The empty valleys narrow. The void becomes a corridor. The rope bridges, once terrifying leaps into absence, become short, safe crossings between neighbors.

**Fourth, it gifts the light.**

The Power seeds the Crust with a new strain of lichen—one that responds to the Folk’s fire-drills. When a band lights their evening flames in the hollows, the ground around them awakens with a soft, warm amber glow, a constellation of comfort in the perpetual twilight. It marks safe paths, shows where condensation pools in the sharp crevices, where the ice is thin enough to drink. The Folk become cartographers of light, their waymarks no longer just pecked into stone, but *living*, pulsing with a guidance that is older than language.

**Fifth, it places the Messenger.**

In the vast reservoirs within the hollow hearts of the Colossi—those black, still pools of melted condensation—the Power places a creature, or perhaps awakens one that slept there since before the mountains rose. It is a being of current and phosphorescence, a fish-like thing with skin like liquid glass. When the Folk descend to drink, or to tend the fungal gardens, it brushes against their ankles (for the Folk are small, and the water is deep) and shows them visions: the memory of lower elevations, the richness of wetter soils the Colossi remember; the location of the next seep; the way the Colossus is dreaming, and where it will move next.

The Power does not change the Folk. It does not give them metal, or writing, or the hubris of speed. It gives them **connection**. It turns their solitude into a network, their silence into a dialogue. The Colossi, now aware of their caretakers, adjust their growth—budding new chambers not randomly, but toward the shelters, creating warmer alcoves, wider galleries for the Six-Legged herds to pass through, gentler slopes for the Folk to climb.

And so, the place transforms. Not into a garden of softness, but into a **cathedral of communion**. The totality of life is no longer expressed through absence, but through the threads that span the void—threads of sound that carry the Folk’s songs from spire to spire, threads of amber light that guide their steps across the sharp glass, threads of slow, patient love between the giants and the small.

The Power withdraws, or perhaps it remains, now indistinguishable from the wind, from the drone, from the green and amber glow. It has done what was needed. The world was already perfect in its austerity, its birth-sharpness, its singing violence. But now it is perfect in its **tenderness**—a place where the void is not empty, but held, like a note sustained in the dark, waiting for the next voice to answer.
