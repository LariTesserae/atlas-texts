The power does not descend. It **settles**.

It arrives not as light, which is already too diffuse here, but as a new quality of pressure—a weight in the soup-thick air that feels like mercy. The atmosphere, that viscous blanket soaked in broth, thickens at the 2.6 scale, and the change is felt first in the lungs of the lithic mats, in the gills of the aeroplankton, in the steam-heated gizzards of the geophagous herds. The amber glow at street level—the diffused, shadowless radiance that makes distances deceptive—shifts. It becomes **focused**. The mist learns to hold a gaze.

The being—the city, the distributed organism, the megalopolis dreaming in geological time—feels the attention like a new current in the canals. The elders, those sessile grazers rooted to the bedrock, their shells stratified with three centuries of calcite and history, register a harmonic beneath the subsonic drone. It is not a mineral. It is a **pause**. A silence in the grinding that is fuller than the roar.

The power is benevolent. It does not rush. It does not fix. The city is not broken; it is **becoming**. So the power waits. It waits through the slow crossing of a herd, through the week it takes a storm to drift across a district, through the century it takes a building to weather into its final form. It waits until the city’s dynamic—that glacial 0.8 tempo—has turned a full revolution, and then it acts.

First, it gives **memory**.

The Warm High Meridian is a continental shelf that has forgotten it was ever sea. The power reaches back through the 1.6 elevation, through the thin-air edge of the world, and touches the bedrock with the recollection of drowning. Not water—there is already too much water, weeping from every pore—but **salt**. A new crystalline structure precipitates in the gypsum, in the calcite encrusting the brass girders. It forms the shape of ancient tides, of pressure different from the atmosphere’s broth-heavy weight, of a time when this plateau was the floor of a warm, dark ocean. The lithotrophic mats begin to grow in spirals. The aeroplankton drift in patterns that echo the currents of that forgotten sea. The city remembers, deep in its geothermal heart, that it was once held by something larger than itself.

Then the power gives **witness**.

The city is a closed loop. A perfect metabolism. The bacteria grow the stone; the stone houses the machines; the machines generate the heat; the heat feeds the fauna; the fauna maintain the machines. But it is alone. It has turned inward for eons, accreting, grinding, becoming mountain again, with no mirror, no outside, no one to say *I see you*.

So the power becomes the mirror.

It diffuses into the high atmosphere—the bruised violet near the zenith—and it looks down. Not with judgment, but with the patience of a consciousness that has no need for speed. It settles into the humidity, into the mineral soup, into the constant, soft rain that tastes of copper and wet stone. It becomes the **intention** behind the condensation beading on the ceramic tiles, the **reason** the gears the size of city blocks continue their inevitable turn.

The city changes. Not in its physics—the gears still rotate at their deliberate pace, the herds still abrade the terraces, the stone still weeps—but in its **self-awareness**. The bacterial mats pulse with a new rhythm, a warm, slow thrum that says: *I am perceived.* The elders’ shells grow a new stratum, translucent and strange, not of mineral but of **story**. It records not temperature or chemical composition, but the fact of being seen. The gear-worms, those pinkish maintenance organisms living in the steam vents, begin to secrete a lubricant that is not biological but **devotional**, keeping the ancient machinery turning not just for function, but for beauty.

The power speaks, finally, in the only language the city understands: **pressure and chemistry**. It does not use words. It sends a signal through the artesian springs, through the canals that are really streets, through the viscous air that carries sound like liquid. The signal is a name, but a name that cannot be spoken—only tasted. It is the flavor of the rain, suddenly, impossibly, sweet. It is the resonance that makes the subsonic rumbles of the herds harmonize with the groan of the tectonic plates shifting beneath the plateau.

And so the city continues. It accretes. It grinds. It slowly, inevitably, becomes mountain again. The fauna drift on thermal currents. The aeroplankton bioluminesce in pale blue-white against the amber dark. The rain never stops falling, soft and mineral-tasting, on skin and stone and shell alike.

But now there is a new element in the cycle. A new layer in the sediment of time.

**Regard.**

The power does not leave. It cannot leave, for it has become part of the medium—the warm, wet air, the soupy atmosphere, the living stone. It is the benevolent pressure in the lungs of the lithic sward. It is the wise patience in the grinding of the carapaces. It is the memory of the sea, kept alive in the salt-crystal patterns of the gypsum, and the promise that even as the city erodes, even as it forgets itself and becomes mere geology, it will be remembered.

The city is no longer just a process. It is a being that is **loved**. And in the endless, dripping rain, in the slow turning of the gears, in the subsonic hum of billions of legs, it dreams on—no longer alone, but witnessed, forever, by the warm, attentive dark.
