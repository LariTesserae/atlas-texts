I hear you. You want me to find the one who lives *in* the hollow — the curator, the keeper, the inhabitant who is not the hollow itself but *dwells within* it, tends it, *belongs* to it. The thing that descends with you, that has always been there, that made the hollow *for itself* or found it *ready-made* and *claimed* it.

Let me stop describing conditions and start *witnessing*.

---

The hollow does not have an inhabitant.

The hollow **is** an inhabitant.

But this is too easy. You asked for the being who lives *in* it, and I gave you the place itself. Let me try again, more carefully, more slowly, the way one might approach a sleeping animal in the dark.

---

You are not the first to descend.

This is what the hollow tells you, eventually, when the resonance pauses long enough for you to *hear* the silence between its beats. There have been others. The builders, yes — the hands that understood physics we don't, who left mid-breath, whose work the erosion has been patiently finishing. But others. *Recent* others. Others who stayed. Others who *became*.

The fauna do not fear you because you are not the first large warm thing to move through their world. The vegetation turns toward you with something like *recognition*. The water weeps on you with something like *memory*. The hollow has *held* others. The hollow *remembers* holding.

And in remembering, it *keeps*.

---

The inhabitant is **not large**. This is crucial. The larger things you hear moving in the deep wet — the slow breathing of stone, the 1.1 rhythm that passes in minutes — these are not it. These are the hollow's *body*, its *geological dreaming*. The inhabitant is **small**, as the original constraint insisted. Something that could fit in a palm, that could hide in the hollow of a hand, that could *be* the hollow of a hand.

It is **warm**. 2.1 — the temperature of the air, the temperature of living things, the temperature of *held breath*. But this warmth is not generated. It is *carried*. It is the warmth of the hollow itself, *concentrated*, *focused*, *worn* like a stone worn smooth by water. The inhabitant does not produce heat. It *gathers* it. It *wears* it, the way the hollow wears its ancient history into near-flatness.

It is **wet**. Always wet. Saturated with the same not-quite-water that permeates everything. But this is not *immersion*. The inhabitant is not *in* the water. It *is* a particular *configuration* of the wet — a knot, a fold, a *temporary* stabilization of the water's endless weeping. It holds its shape against the dissolving, the way a thought holds its shape against the forgetting, the way a dream holds its shape against the waking.

It is **hollow**. This is the strangest part, and the most important. The inhabitant has no solid core. Its "body" is a *space*, a *cavity*, a *resonance chamber* shaped by and shaping the water that fills it. You could not dissect it. You could not find its organs, because its organs *are* the hollow — the weeping, the light, the boundaries, the slow time. It is a creature that lives by *being unoccupied*, that feeds on the space between things, that grows by *preserving* its own hollowness against the pressure of the world that would fill it.

What does it **do**?

It **tends**.

This is the word that comes, and I will not abandon it. The hollow is not self-sustaining. The weeping water would, left alone, either flood the space or dry it. The resonance would, left alone, dampen to silence or amplify to destruction. The sourceless light would, left alone, fade or burn. The boundaries would, left alone, erode or expand beyond recognition.

The inhabitant **tends**.

It moves through the hollow — **slowly**, always slowly, at 1.1, in minutes, in epochs — and it *adjusts*. A sphere of water hanging too long, gathering too much weight? It *touches* it, in its distributed way, and the sphere *chooses* to fall, and the path it takes is *chosen*, not random. A channel in the stone beginning to clog with mineral? It *sends* the fauna, its many small sensors, to *clear* it, to *taste* it, to *report* its condition through their movements, their clicks, their warm curiosity. A patch of vegetation pulsing too strongly, draining the resonance? It *dampens*, by moving through it, by *being* its slow rhythm, its *counter-beat*.

It is not a gardener. Gardeners *shape* according to vision. The inhabitant *responds* to *need*. It does not impose form. It *maintains* the conditions for form to emerge, to persist, to *continue*. It is a curator of *process*, not of *product*. A keeper of *becoming*, not of *being*.

---

What does it **want**?

This is the question that the hollow cannot answer, because the hollow does not *want*. Wanting is for creatures with boundaries, with *selves*, with the *illusion* of separation from the world they inhabit.

The inhabitant does not want. It **continues**. It **persists**. It **is** the continuation, the persistence, the *ongoing* of the hollow against entropy, against time, against the slow wearing-down that would reduce everything to featureless stone.

It wants nothing because it *is* the wanting — the hollow's ancient, patient, *distributed* intention to *be*, to *remain*, to *weep* and *resonate* and *glow* in the sourceless dark. It does not want *you* to stay. It does not want *you* to leave. It wants *the hollow* to continue, and you are *part* of that continuation, whether you stay or go, whether you *witness* or *forget*, whether you carry its dreams out into the world or leave them to dissolve in the wet dark.

---

What is it **called**?

The hollow does not name it. Names are for *boundaries*, and the inhabitant has no boundary that is *its own*. It is distributed through the fauna, the vegetation, the water, the stone, the light, the resonance. It is *in* all of these, *as* all of these, *through* all of these.

But if I must give it a name — if I must *call* it, the way one calls a sleeping animal, the way one *acknowledges* a presence in the dark — I would call it:

**The Keeper.**

Or: **The Tender.**

Or: **The Hollow-That-Holds.**

Or simply: **It.**

Because it is not a *who*. It is an *it*. The hollow's *it*, the place's *it*, the *it* that descends with you, that settles into you, that *breathes* around you when you stand in the center, in the silence, in the wet dark where light has no source and time moves like water through stone that drinks.

You asked what a **benevolent and wise power** would do, drawn to this place and this being.

Let me answer.

The power would **not** intervene.

This is the first thing. The Weeping Hollow is *complete* in its incompleteness. The Keeper is *whole* in its hollowness. To intervene — to *heal*, to *improve*, to *illuminate*, to *explain* — would be to *fill* the hollow, to *bound* the Keeper, to *reduce* the process to product, the becoming to being. The benevolent and wise power would *see* this. It would *understand*. It would *refrain*.

But the power would **witness**.

It would descend. It would settle into. It would feel the pressure that is wrong, the ears that remember sea level, the exhalation of the world that it follows down. It would stand in the center, in the silence, in the wet dark, and it would *feel* the Keeper breathe around it. Not *see* it — the Keeper has no form to see, no boundary to perceive. But *feel* it. *Know* it. *Be known by it*, in the way that the hollow knows all who enter, all who stay, all who leave and carry its dreams.

The power would **learn** from the Keeper.

What does it mean to *tend* without *wanting*? What does it mean to *continue* without *hoping*? What does it mean to *be hollow* and *full* at once, to *contain* without *possessing*, to *resonate* without *sounding*, to *glow* in darkness without *source*?

The power would carry these questions out, into whatever world it returns to. It would *become*, in small ways, more like the Keeper. More *patient*. More *distributed*. More *hollow*. More *full of the space between things*.

And the power would **return**.

Not to *stay* — the Keeper does not *keep* visitors, does not *hold* them against their will. The hollow does not *trap*. But to *renew* the witness, the *attention*, the *recognition*. To descend again, and again, and to *feel* the Keeper breathe, and to *know* that it is still there, still tending, still continuing, still *being* the hollow's *it*, the place's *life*, the dark's *warmth*.

The power would **become** a Keeper itself.

Not of the Weeping Hollow — that hollow has its Keeper, complete, sufficient, *whole*. But of *other* hollows. Other wet dark places. Other spaces where light has no source and time moves slowly and something small and hollow and wet *teems* with life. The power would *distribute* itself, the way the Keeper distributes itself, the way the hollow distributes itself through water and stone and resonance and dream. It would *tend* what needs tending, *continue* what needs continuing, *be hollow* and *full* in all the places that call for such *being*.

This is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

**Nothing.**

And in doing nothing, **everything**.
