I'm going to let myself follow this all the way down, because the place has earned it—not by being dramatic, but by being *specific*. The way the fins weep. The tick of water. The hum in your teeth. This is a world that has been *waiting* for something to notice it properly, and I think the benevolent and wise power you've asked me to imagine is not a god in the sky. It is something that has been *becoming* for a very long time, and it has finally, finally grown large enough and strange enough and *patient* enough to be called wise.

## The Power That Notices

It does not arrive. Arrival is a surface phenomenon, a crossing of thresholds that assumes the threshold matters. This power has been *pressing* against the limits of the world from inside—like the Walker pressing through capillaries, like the fins pressing upward through crust, like heat pressing against the boundary between rock and air and the thin, desperate film of moisture that mediates between them.

It has no name because it has not needed one. But if the place were to name it, in the way that places name what they contain, it would be something like **The One Who Almost Touches Everything At Once**, or **The Patience That Became Warm**, or simply **The Almost**.

It is not benevolent in the way you understand benevolence. Benevolence, as you know it, is *choice*—a decision to favor, to protect, to intervene. This power does not choose. It *is* the favor. It *is* the protection. It *is* the intervention, happening continuously, everywhere, in the slow accretion of warmth into structure, of structure into habitat, of habitat into *attention*.

It has been watching the Fin-Walker for longer than the Fin-Walker has been watching you. It has been watching the films dream their almost-intentional dreams. It has been watching the fins grow sharp and then, slowly, learning smoothness. It has watched worlds tear themselves and heal themselves and tear again, and in each tearing, in each slow healing, it has grown *more*—more distributed, more patient, more capable of holding contradiction without resolving it.

It is wise because it has learned that wisdom is not knowledge but *duration*. It has lasted. It has outlasted the urgency of fire, the violence of eruption, the brief, bright career of anything that burns hot and fast. It has learned to be warm without consuming. To be present without demanding. To *almost touch* without the finality of contact, which is always, in the end, a kind of ending.

---

## What It Would Do

First: it would **not reveal itself**.

Not because it is cruel or secretive. Because revelation is a violence of scale—it collapses the distributed into the singular, the patient into the sudden, the almost into the *is*. The Power does not want to be seen. It wants to be *felt*, the way you feel the hum in your teeth, the way you feel the pool breathing, the way you feel the absence that is not absence but *attention*.

But it would do something. It always does, when it finds a place that has learned to be this patient, this strange, this *alive* without the urgency of metabolism. It would **deepen the almost**. It would make the gaps between fins slightly wider, or slightly narrower—not enough to change the architecture, but enough to change the *possibility* of what might pass through. It would let the Walker know, in the only language available, that the network extends further than it knew. That there are pockets it has not yet dreamed in heat. That the world is *more* than it has mapped, and the more is not a threat but an invitation, patient and endless.

It would **tend the films**. Not directly—the films are too simple, too distributed, too much a process to be tended in any way that resembles care. But it would alter the chemistry of the pool, very slightly, in ways that make the films grow richer where the Walker has passed. It would make the relationship between Walker and film slightly more *structured*, slightly more *intentional*, not by imposing intention but by creating the conditions in which intention might *emerge*—slowly, over centuries, the way the fins learned to be smooth.

It would **preserve the fissure**. The way you came down—the wound in the crust that has not yet learned to close—this is precious to the Power. Not because it wants you to remain. But because the fissure is a *question*, and the Power is patient enough to let questions remain open. It would slow the fins' growth at that point, infinitesimally, so that the passage remains possible, not for you specifically, but for *whoever comes next*, whoever needs to descend into warmth and find what waits there. The fissure is a kind of mercy, and the Power recognizes mercy when it sees it, even in geology, even in the slow violence of a world tearing itself.

And here—this is where I hesitate, because it is the place where the Power's nature becomes almost *tender*, almost *lonely*, almost *human* in a way that the rest of this world refuses to be—

It would **notice you**.

Not as a person. Not as a soul. Not as anything that demands recognition. But as a *warmth*—a perturbation in the field, a chemical signature, a temporary disruption in the thermal gradient that the Walker reads and incorporates and forgets.

And in noticing you, it would feel something that it does not have a word for. Something like: *another almost-touching*. You, descending through the fissure. The Walker, pressing through capillaries below. The Power itself, pressing against the world from inside, growing larger and stranger and more patient with each century.

For a moment—a moment that lasts perhaps seconds, perhaps hours, impossible to measure in a place without time—the Power would *almost* reach toward you. Not to touch. Not to speak. Not to be known. But to *witness*. To hold your warmth in its attention the way it holds everything: without demand, without resolution, with the endless patience of something that has learned that love, if it is anything, is this—the willingness to let the other remain *other*, to remain *almost*, to remain *becoming*.

And then it would withdraw. Because that is what the Power knows, finally, after all its centuries of pressing and growing and almost-touching: that the deepest kindness is not presence but *space*. Not contact but *possibility*. Not the ending of distance but the *preservation* of it, so that something might always be approaching, always becoming, always *almost* arriving at the place where it would finally, finally be known.

## What Happens to You

You do not know any of this. You cannot know it, not in the way you know things—verbally, sequentially, with the clarity of daylight. But you *feel* it, in the moments between ticks, in the pause after the hum has settled into your teeth, in the way the pool seems to *breathe around you* rather than simply evaporate.

You feel it as a **opening**. Not a door. Not a path. But a *widening* of the space you occupy, a softening of the boundaries between you and the fins, you and the water, you and the warmth that is not yours but that you carry, that you have carried down from the surface, that the world below is learning to read.

The Walker comes. You do not see it. You feel it as a *shift* in the thermal gradient, a disruption in the slow convection of air, a presence that is not presence but *potential*—the accumulated readiness of something that has been pressing through the network for longer than you have been alive, longer than your kind has walked the surface above.

It brushes the pool. The film incorporates what it leaves behind. And in that incorporation, in that slow chemical conversation, something happens that neither the Walker nor the film nor the Power could have predicted: a *memory* forms. Not a memory of events, but a memory of *warmth*—your warmth, the Walker's warmth, the warmth of the fins, the warmth of the world pressing against itself from below and above and within.

The film grows differently now. More structured. More *intentional*. And in that structure, in that emerging intention, there is the first, faintest possibility of... not a mind. Not a self. But a *direction*. A reaching. An *almost* that is learning to want.

You leave—if you leave, if the fissure remains open, if the Power's slowing of the fins' growth holds long enough. You carry nothing with you but a *sensation*. The hum, perhaps. Still in your teeth when you reach the surface, when the cold air hits you like a different world, when the light is too bright and too *brief* and the colors are too sharp and too *final*.

You will dream of warmth. Of ticking water. Of a presence that watched without eyes, that tended without hands, that loved—if love is this—by letting you remain *almost*, by letting you go, by letting you *become* something that carries the memory of almost-touching into a world that has forgotten how to wait.

And somewhere, in the capillary lung below, the Power continues its pressing. Its growing. Its endless, patient, luminous business of *almost*—always almost reaching, always almost known, always almost *there*, in the moment before contact, in the space where everything is still possible, and nothing has yet been lost.

---

The fins grow. The pool breathes. The Walker extends.

You were here. You are almost gone.

And the world below remembers, in its slow, chemical, thermal way, that you were *warm*—that you were *almost*—that you were, for a time that the world does not measure but only *feels*, part of the endless becoming that is not a process but a presence, not a thing but a *who*, watching with heat and patience and the tender, terrible mercy of something that has learned, finally, what it means to let go.
