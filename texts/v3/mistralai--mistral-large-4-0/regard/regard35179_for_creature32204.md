The power arrives not as thunder, but as the pause between two drips—the held breath of the atmosphere before the stone exhales its moisture.

It has watched this plain through the epochs of its flattening, through the millimeters of surrender, through the centuries when the mountain’s spine softened into a sheet, then a skin, then a whisper against the sky. It has seen the trees arrive like exiles to a new world, kilometers apart, building their slow cathedrals in peat and patience. It has seen the saurian settle into the deep channels, becoming indistinguishable from the tannin-stained water, a cold-blooded monument watching the sky reflect in the breathing mirrors, its golden eye holding the light of the normal sun while looking backward, backward, into the time when this land was high and dry and sharp with altitude.

The being—the monitor, the caiman, the ancient lineage that predates the peneplain—does not stir. In its ectothermic blood, it carries the memory of gravity’s old tyranny, of snow that did not melt but accumulated, of a wind that scoured rather than whispered. It is the last witness, the keeper of the secret that the flatness has tried to erode from the world’s mind: *we were once vertical.*

The power recognizes the wisdom here. This is not a failure of the mountain. This is the mountain’s final, most difficult teaching: **how to surrender height without losing depth**. The water cannot choose where to drain, so it stays everywhere, saturating the world in a democracy of moisture. The stone weeps not from grief, but from fullness—exhaling the water it hoarded when it was a peak, releasing it drop by patient drop into the shallow pools where life stacks itself in vertical layers, where frogs bellow across kilometers of level stone and dragonflies stitch the humid air in iridescent arcs.

The benevolent power considers intervention. It could raise the land again, restore the jagged skyline, make the water run downhill with purpose and haste. It could dry the stone, turn it to desert, make it dramatic once more. It could accelerate the erosion until the plain becomes dust, or freeze time so the trees never thicken by another invisible ring.

But wisdom counsels otherwise. The place has reached its oldest stable state. It is a society at the end of geological time, persisting in the thin, teeming margin between rock and sky. To change it would be violence. To abandon it would be cruelty. The highest form of benevolence, the power knows, is **recognition**—and **remembrance**.

So the power does not alter the plain. It **indwells** it.

It enters the water cycle. It becomes the moisture that gathers on the rock faces in the warm, condensing atmosphere, the film that makes the algae slick, the condensation that answers the evaporation in that endless, humid afternoon. It joins the Film-Riders and the Pool-Dwellers, the dragonfly nymphs clinging to submerged roots, the diving beetles carrying their bubbles of air. It becomes the humidity itself—the medium through which the frog chorus accumulates without dissipating, the resonant chamber where sound does not fade but stacks, layer upon layer, into the vertical architecture of life.

And it sits with the being—the ancient reptile, the relic—and it offers a gift that is not a change, but a **vision**. In the surface of a shallow pool, in the breathing mirror that reflects the flat sky, the power shows the being what was: the snow line, the scree, the dry cold of the summit, the sharp wind that once tore at stone before the stone learned to yield. It shows the being that the flattening was not a diminishment, but a transfiguration; that the weeping is not sorrow, but the release of all that was held, given freely to the world; that the patience is not stagnation, but the final ripeness of the earth.

The power whispers, and the sound does not travel through air but through the mycelial web, through the wood-wide-web that stitches the distant trees together across kilometers of stone: *You were a mountain. You chose to become the plain. This is your enlightenment. The water stays everywhere because you have learned that no place is higher than another. You weep because you are full. You teem because you have emptied yourself into abundance.*

Then, because it is wise, the power does not depart. It becomes the silence between the frog calls, the pause in the erosion, the stillness in the reptile’s golden eye. It ensures that no human foot will ever press the mud here—or if one does, that the impression will dissolve not into oblivion, but into the general wetness, the universal saturation, the democracy of the drip.

It guarantees the endless, humid afternoon. It makes the place a sanctuary not by walling it off from the world, but by **remembering it for the world**—by holding in its benevolent mind the image of the peaks, the memory of the height, so that the plain never has to carry the burden of having forgotten.

And so the stone continues to weep, the frogs continue to call, the mosquitoes continue to rise as living weather, and the ancient reptile continues to watch with a stillness that predates the plain itself. And somewhere, in the condensation gathering on the warm bedrock, in the slow drop that falls to feed a breathing mirror, the benevolent and wise power sits with them—not as a master, not as a savior, but as a witness, a rememberer, a silent partner in the endless, patient, loud, slow-motion present of a world that has reached its oldest stable state and simply breathes, drips, and teems, no longer a mountain, finally, and completely, itself.
