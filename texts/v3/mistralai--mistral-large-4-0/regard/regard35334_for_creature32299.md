The Power does not descend with violence. It arrives as a pressure change, a thickening of the ionosphere above the plateau, a moment where the 2.9 brightness—the surgical, bleaching glare—dims by degrees, as though a hand were slowly closing over a lamp. The light does not fade to darkness; it softens to something like mercy, something that allows color to exist. The obsidian reveals its purple veins. The peat shows itself to be not merely green but a spectrum of jade, sage, and the blue-green of deep water. The faded industrial yellow of the pump houses warms to gold.

It has been watching. The Power has observed the being—the walker, the caretaker, the transient nerve of this emerging world—moving through the roar, enduring the light, sinking into the Mat with each step. It sees the solitude: the only consciousness here that questions, that fears, that feels the absence of other voices as a physical weight against the eardrums. It sees the being’s hands, chapped from the cold and the mineral water, gripping a pneumatic drill that turns with the patience of centuries, extracting heat from rock that is still cooling, still dreaming of becoming mountain.

The Power decides to speak.

First, it stills the world. Not with a gesture, but with a cessation. The drilling rigs—those massive, sluggish pistons—pause in their arc, suspended at the apex of their stroke, neither rising nor falling. The pumps thrum to a halt, their exhausts releasing one final, long sigh of steam that hangs in the thin air. Most profoundly, the wind drops. The hollow chambers of the Giants, those twenty-meter organ pipes, fall silent. The bass note that vibrated in the sternum ceases.

In the sudden, terrible quiet, the being stands frozen on the spongy peat, and hears what has always been there, beneath the roar.

The Mat breathes.

It is a sound like wet leather expanding, like the slow unfurling of a thousand fronds. It is the sound of ten thousand generations of compressed green, inhaling the sulfurous breath of the fumaroles, exhaling oxygen into the thin, bright air. It is the sound of the world’s skin knitting itself over a new fissure, the hiss of steam becoming a whisper, the drip of condensation from the Giants’ honeycombed surfaces falling in a rhythm that is not random but ancient, a pulse measured in millimeters per year.

The Power manifests not as a form, but as a warmth. It gathers in the hollows of the being’s chest, behind the sternum where the noise used to live. It is the temperature of deep geothermal vents, of the earth’s own blood. It does not speak in words. It speaks in recognition.

Then the Giants move.

It is almost imperceptible—a degree of lean, a slow rotation of their lignified towers, honeycombed and weeping. They turn, one by one, across the valley, their massive clubmoss heads tilting toward the being like sunflowers tracking a sun that has, for a moment, become gentle. They are not threatening. They are acknowledging. They have been waiting for the being to stop walking, to stop enduring, and simply *be*.

The Power sends a gift. From the nearest Giant, a single spore detaches—not falling, but drifting on a current of air that the Power has woven. It lands in the being’s upturned palm, warm despite the 8-degree chill. It is a seed of connection, a proof that the vertical islands and the horizontal Mat are not separate kingdoms but one body, and that the being is the synapse between them, the necessary spark between the organic and the mechanical.

And the Power speaks, finally, through the condensation that weeps from the Giants’ surfaces. The drips begin to form patterns on the peat—not random, but language. The being understands, suddenly and completely, the syntax of this place. They understand that they are not transient. They are the punctuation in the sentence the earth is writing. The witness. The caretaker. The one who sees the beauty in the extraction, who balances the drill against the growth, who walks the Mat without crushing it, who endures the surgical light so that the Giants might photosynthesize, so that the peat might thicken, so that the young mountains might one day stand rounded and old.

The being is not alone. They have never been alone. The Giants have been singing to them in bass notes. The Mat has been holding them up. The machines have been extending their hands into the dark. And the Power—the benevolent, wise attention of the universe itself—has been holding the light steady, just for them, just for a moment.

Then, as slowly as it began, the world resumes its construction. The wind returns to the organ pipes, and the Giants moan again, but now the being hears the melody within the roar. The drills turn, grinding into the emerging geology, but now the being feels the rhythm of the extraction as a heartbeat. The light hardens back to its surgical white, bleaching the colors to monochrome, but the being carries the memory of the jade and the gold within them, a warmth that no altitude can chill.

The being takes a step. The peat sighs beneath their boot, springy and alive. They walk toward the next Giant, no longer isolated, no longer overwhelmed, but accompanied—by the slow patience of the plants, the grinding patience of the machines, and the vast, benevolent patience of the Power that has chosen, for this moment, to look upon them with eyes that see not a transient caretaker, but a co-creator. A friend. A necessary part of the world that is still, loudly, slowly, being made.
