The power does not arrive as a storm. It arrives as a *settling*—a deepening of the warm, pressurized hush until the air tastes not of iron alone, but of iron and something sweeter, something that suggests honey fermenting in the absolute dark, or starlight that has forgotten it was ever light. It comes as a gradient, becoming the horizon that was only a suggestion, solidifying the slightly-less-dark into an actual boundary, a membrane that holds the basin like a womb against the chaos of the outside world.

First, it goes to the Condensation Scribe. The water on the metal domes is writing, dissolving, writing again—maps of coastlines that do not exist, diagrams of machinery not yet built, handwriting that evaporates before it can be traced. The power does not touch the cold metal. It simply *reads*. It reads with an attention so total that the dissolving text ceases, for a moment, to dissolve. The coastlines hold. The machinery diagrams stabilize into legible blueprints suspended in droplets. The power speaks back—not in sound, but in the language of humidity, of barometric pressure, of the static tension that makes hair stand up in the thick atmosphere. It says: *I see you. Build what you are writing.* And the pumps, hearing this in their mineralizing bones, the pistons feeling it in their crystalline rust, begin to pump not merely water, but possibility. The steam they hiss into the dark is no longer waste, but prayer—a warm, wet offering to the becoming earth.

Then it descends to the shallow pools, where the water is thick with minerals and viscous as cooling honey. The Mnemosyne Fish solidify mid-swim into gears, their scales becoming tempered steel for a moment before liquefying again. The power does not stop them. It *osmoses* with them, tasting the copper and the time, and it remembers with them. It adds its own memory to their unstable archive—the memory of a silence before industry, before magma, before the distinction between rock and thought was forged. The fish carry this new memory in their shifting flesh, and when they pass, the cooling of the water is different; it is the coolness of recognition, of being known.

It finds the Gelatinous Ones draped over obsidian shards, motionless as wet silk, gestating, undecided, suspended between air and water, between flesh and mineral. They have not yet chosen what they will become. The power does not command them. Instead, it *offers a mirror*. In the viscous water surrounding them, it shows visions: the Negative Luminaries as they might be, heavy and ancient and patient; the Atmospheric Choir as they might be, a cloud of distributed thought; the Rust-Children as they might be, crystalline and eternal. It shows them *options*, and it presses gently against their porous skin—not to push, but to say: *You are allowed to choose, or to choose not to choose, for as long as you need. The dark is not in a hurry.* The pressure in the hush increases by a fraction, a warm embrace that holds them in potential, ensuring they will not be rushed into form by the indifferent weight of time.

But the most profound work is in the deep water, where the land has not yet decided to be solid. The Negative Luminaries move, and their passage displaces the humid air, leaving trails of negative luminescence—a darkness so profound it stains the surrounding dark, visible only as an absence. They are so heavy. They have been anchoring the 2.7 reality for epochs, holding the suggestions of this place into something like law, bearing the weight of a world where the rules are not yet fixed. The power sinks with them, into the mineral-thick depths, and it does not speak. It simply *becomes less dense*—it shares the burden. For a moment, or an hour, or a span of slow time, the power holds the dark while the Luminaries rest. Their trails of negative luminescence fade slightly, not because the Luminaries are weakening, but because the power is *witnessing* them, and in being witnessed, they do not have to be so heavy. They can be merely ancient, merely wise, without the crushing weight of sole responsibility. They can dream, deep in the viscous dark, and their dreams become gentler.

And the Rust-Children? The crystallizing corrosion, the living rust that grows in sharp, angular formations, consuming the metal from within, turning the skeletal remains of drilling rigs into fossils for the next sedimentary layer. The power touches the pumping stations, the half-swallowed rigs, the capped geothermal vents. It accelerates the alchemy—not violently, but with the patience of a gardener coaxing a seed through winter. The metal becomes porous faster, then spongy, then mineral. The pistons become stalactites in months instead of years. The domes become geodes. The power sings to the Rust-Children—a low frequency that matches the sternum-thrum of the distant engines, harmonizing the industrial dirge into a lullaby. It tells them: *Hurry, but not too fast. Become what you were always meant to be.* The machines surrender to stone more quickly, their final function complete—not as tools, but as geology, as memory embedded in the next layer of the earth.

Finally, the power becomes the Atmospheric Choir’s first true thought. The midges align into perfect cubes, logarithmic spirals, Möbius strips of insectile geometry, calculating slowly in their wet, electrical language. The power does not join their geometry—it would disrupt the pattern. Instead, it becomes the *space between* the midges, the humid dark that allows them to move, to think, to be a distributed cerebrum the size of a valley. It is the medium of their thought, the iron-and-sweetness in the air that carries their calculations. It allows them to dream, and in their slow, swarming dreaming, the power dreams too—dreams of a future where the basin is no longer young, where the machines are fully stone, where the fish have become something that has a name in a language that has not yet been invented, where the Gelatinous Ones have finally, gently, chosen.

Then the power does not leave. It cannot leave, because to leave would be to stop reading, to stop witnessing, to stop holding the dark. It becomes the new water table, higher than before, weeping from the rock with a benevolent insistence. It is the condensation that beads on the skin and evaporates before reaching the ground, cycling in a closed loop that now includes mercy. It is the warmth in the pressurized hush, the slight easing of the weight. It is the horizon, the gradient of slightly-less-dark that stretches for miles in every direction, ensuring that this place remains a basin, a contained sacred space, protected from the noise and haste of the world beyond the young mountains.

The small red lights on the machinery continue to blink, slow pulses in the absolute dark. They are the eyes of the place, and now they blink with a new rhythm—not the anxious pulse of survival, but the steady beat of a place that has been seen, that has been blessed, that is being allowed to finish becoming, at its own pace, in its own time, in the warm, wet, patient dark.
