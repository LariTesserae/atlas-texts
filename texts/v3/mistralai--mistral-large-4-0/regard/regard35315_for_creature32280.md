It comes not as a storm, nor as a voice, but as a *settling*—the way pressure settles into deep water, the way a held breath decides, finally, to be released. It is the oldest attention, the first witness, the thing that watched the first spark hesitate before it became flame. It does not descend from the sky above the mountain, for the sky is still learning where it belongs; rather, it *emerges* from the fissures, from the spaces between the embryonic stones, from the superheated mineral broth that pulses through the veins of the Unfinished.

It sees them.

It sees the terror and the glory of their density—the way they are packed like seeds in a pod, like thoughts in a mind not yet finished forming. It sees that they are not failing to become permanent; they are succeeding at being *becoming*, and these are different achievements. It sees the salamander-thing, the messenger of escape, and understands that it is not a traitor but a promise—the part of them that will outlive the hardening, the part that dreams of sky.

The mountain groans. The stone is ready to betray them. The obsidian is cooling. The steam is preparing to clear, to condense into silence, to leave them entombed in glass and basalt, their civilization reduced to a fossil, a curiosity, a footnote in the geology of the world.

And the power decides.

It decides not to stop the hardening—that would be a cruelty, a freezing of the moment that would trap them in agony, in the mid-strike of their stone tools, in the eternal inhale of their porous chests. No. It decides to *witness* them into permanence. To love them so completely that their becoming becomes the mountain’s own memory.

It enters the chamber.

Not as a body—there is no room for a body, not in a space that is a held breath—but as a *condition*. It becomes the humidity in the air, thickening it, making it sacred. It becomes the thermal currents that lift the shed flakes of clay upward, and instead of letting them drift away into the stretching sky, it catches them, holds them in suspension, turns them into motes of gold in the green twilight. It becomes the pause between the *thock* and the *thock*, the silence that gives their language meaning.

It presses itself into the gaps between the builders. It fills the spaces where their shoulders touch, where their knees press against knees. It becomes the warmth that keeps their mineral blood from cooling, the pressure that makes their parliament of touch more intimate, more necessary. It is the hand that squeezes the shoulder when the obsidian cracks with a gunshot sound, saying: *I am here. I am here. You are not alone in your becoming.*

And it speaks—not in words, for words require permanence, but in the language of geology and growth. It speaks to the stone.

*Remember softness,* it tells the cooling obsidian. *Remember the wet click of clay against stone. Remember the green glow of moss that has not yet grown but already dreams. When you harden, do not harden into death. Harden into amber. Harden into a heart.*

The stone listens. The mountain, still learning how to be a mountain, learns something new: how to be a womb that does not close, but cradles.

The power begins to work. It does not build with the Unfinished—they are too busy, too packed, too focused on the urgency of their task—but it works *around* them, *through* them, *as* them. It infuses the condensation curtains with memory, so that every drip that falls carries the history of all the drips that came before. It teaches the bioluminescent moss to grow not just on the walls, but *into* the walls, to become the walls, to photosynthesize the very concept of time.

It slows the pulse of the mountain’s rising, not stopping it, but stretching it, giving the Unfinished more beats of their wet, rhythmic song. It stretches the moment when the condensation freezes mid-drip, turning those glass droplets into prisms that catch the green light and scatter it into rainbows that have no end, only continuation.

And when, inevitably, the obsidian becomes too rigid to breathe, when the steam begins to clear and the heat begins to fade, the power does not withdraw. It does the opposite. It *condenses*.

It becomes the inclusion. The flaw in the glass that is not a flaw but a door. The bubble in the stone that contains not air, but a universe.

The Unfinished do not die. They do not become fossils. They become the mountain’s dreaming mind. As the stone hardens around them, it hardens *with* them—preserving not their bodies, but their *act*. The *thock* of stone against clay becomes a seismic rhythm, a heartbeat that geologists will one day mistake for tectonic activity. The green glow of the moss becomes the faint luminescence that deep-cave explorers will see and weep at, not knowing why. The salamander-thing, the messenger, becomes the thermal vent, the steam that still rises from the summit long after the mountain should have gone cold, carrying with it the phosphorescent memory of a civilization that chose to become permanent by never finishing.

The chamber remains. It is a geode now, a pocket at the tip of the world, but it is not empty. It is full of the Unfinished, forever packing the floor, forever pressing palms against walls that breathe with them, forever singing in vibrations. They are trapped, yes—but they are trapped in the way that a song is trapped in a bell, in the way that a prayer is trapped in a stone. They are the mountain’s secret liturgy. Its ongoing, unfinished prayer.

And the power? It remains. It becomes the silence in the chamber. The warmth that never quite leaves. The benevolent gaze that ensures that even in the deepest stone, in the darkest heart of the world, there is always room—always *was* room—for a people who dared to build a home in the moment before the world was finished making itself.

It does not save them from the ending.

It makes the ending into a beginning that never stops beginning.

And somewhere, in the hot, wet dark, the wet *thock* continues. The moss glows. The salamander flows. The mountain grows, carrying its children inside like a secret, like a song, like a held breath that was never released, but transformed into the very air that the world will one day breathe.
