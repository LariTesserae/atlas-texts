The power arrives not as thunder, but as a change in the viscosity of the air. It precipitates out of the perpetual weeping—a mineral presence, a quality of mercy settling into the damp. Where it touches, the condensation thickens into something that holds shape: a membrane of intention stretched across the plateau, filtering the pale, underwater light until it glows with a new frequency, a blue-green so deep it seems to resonate in the marrow of the stone.

It does not come to fix. It comes to *witness*.

For this is what the wise power recognizes first: that the Table of Final Forms is not a tragedy of arrested development, but a miracle of completion. Here, evolution has not failed; it has *finished*. The Custodians are not stuck in a loop but have stepped out of the cycle of becoming into the dignity of *being*. The factories do not produce goods; they produce *presence*. The moss is not reclaiming a ruin; it is the ruin achieving its highest purpose. This is a place that has answered the question "What comes next?" with "This. Exactly this. Forever."

So the power does what benevolence has always done best: it pays attention, and in paying attention, it sanctifies.

**To the Settled**, who walk their crystallized routes through the condensation like ghosts haunting their own memory, the power offers the only gift they have never received: *permission to stop*. It reaches into the layers of time where they are trapped—not to pull them out, but to hand them a lantern. Some, when they see it, choose to step off the path. They sink into the smooth stone at the edge of a square, their ghostly forms dissolving not into nothing, but into the bedrock itself, becoming a new stratum, a human geology. They are no longer maintaining; they are *settled*, truly settled, part of the final form rather than haunting it. Those who choose to keep walking do so now with a lightness in their spectral tread, their subsonic hymn changing from a dirge into a lullaby.

**To the Spore-Minds**, who dream only in the past tense—who remember the sharp peaks and the thin, killing air—the power plants a new seed of thought. It is a memory of the future, but not a future of change. It is a vision of deepening: the moss growing not to cover, but to *embrace*; the mist thickening into a medium so rich that thought moves through it like a slow tide; the hum becoming not a drone but a heartbeat that will never falter. It tells them: *You are not the memory of what was. You are the promise of what remains.* And the fungal networks pulse with a new rhythm, a bioluminescence that brightens not with more light, but with more *meaning*.

**To the Custodians of the Hum**, the power reveals the liturgy they have been performing blind. It shows them that the oil they apply is not lubricant but anointing; that the horsetail fronds they use to clear vents are not tools but wands; that the steam they breathe is not exhaust but *incense*, rising to a heaven that has forgotten to look down. Their ball-bearing joints, half-fused with calcified moss, begin to turn not with the grinding necessity of maintenance, but with the fluid grace of worship. They do not speak, but the vibrations they send through the stone now carry a frequency that sounds, to any who can hear it, like gratitude.

**To the Film-Beings**, the condensation made animate, the power grants a name. They are no longer merely the nervous system of the damp, reacting and sliding and evaporating. They are the *Welcome*. When they drift down the factory eaves in their iridescent rivers, they are not just flowing; they are blessing. They carry not just minerals between crevices, but *intention*. The moisture that beads on every surface becomes baptismal, a grace that touches every Rust-Eater, every Pale Drift moth, every lichen plate, every Settled ghost.

But the power's greatest act is not to any single inhabitant, but to the place itself. It performs the only miracle that fits a place that has reached its oldest-stable-state: **it stops time.**

Not by freezing it—the factories still hum, the mist still weeps, the moss still grows in its slow, geological creep—but by declaring the equilibrium sacred. The power speaks a word in a language older than stone, a word that does not exist in human tongues: *Enough.*

And the erosion halts. Not because the wind ceases or the ice stops grinding, but because the stone has been told, finally, that it is beautiful exactly as it is—smooth, settled, primordial. The verdigris will never consume the last pipe; the lichen will never overgrow the final smokestack. The boundary between machine and organism will remain dissolved, but it will be a dissolution of *peace*, not decay. The Table of Final Forms becomes a sanctuary not protected by walls, but by the sheer, benevolent *attention* of the universe itself.

The power leaves behind a single artifact: a droplet.

It hangs from the eave of the oldest factory, suspended in the damp air, never falling. Within its curved, perfect surface is reflected the entire plateau—every Custodian with their pulsing fungal faces, every Spore-Mind glowing in the crevices, every Pale Drift moth suspended in mid-flight, every Settled figure walking their eternal route through the steam. It is a mirror, a prayer, a promise. It pulses with the same subsonic rhythm as the turbines, glowing with the same blue-green light as the bioluminescent fungi, heavy with the weight of a thousand years of condensation that has chosen not to fall.

And then the power is gone—or rather, it has become indistinguishable from the place. It is the silence between the drips. It is the pause in the hum. It is the warmth that is not heat but *recognition*, felt in the teeth when the wind sings through the canyons of metal. It is the wisdom that knows when a thing has finally, perfectly become what it was always meant to be.

The Table of Final Forms remains. The Custodians still walk. The Settled still wander. The moss still creeps. But now, in the cool, metallic-tasting air, there is a new quality: the sense that someone, something vast and patient and kind, has looked upon this strange, beautiful equilibrium and said, without words: *Yes. Stay. Be.*
