The power arrives not as thunder but as a *thickening*—a moment when the bruised sky above the bowl seems to lean closer, when the submarine gloom holds its breath. It is older than the glass, patient as the wind that polished it, and it has watched a thousand worlds turn to dust without intervening. But here, in this depression where stone dreams of being soil, it pauses. It kneels.

It does not come to save them from their hunger, for the hunger is holy—it is the way the valley thinks, the way the mountain remembers being a sea. But it comes to *witness*. And in witnessing, to change the quality of the witnessing.

First, it stills a single square of the floor. Not by magic, but by attention—a localized gravity of kindness that presses down upon the churning carpet, creating a space where the crush loosens, where three bodies deep become two, then one, then none. A clearing in the tide, wet and black and silent except for the distant roar. Here, the glass sweats alone.

The power waits for the creature to come. And it comes—not because it is different, but because the stillness calls to the edge of the swarm, and one body, then another, flows toward the silence like water seeking a drain. When the tide parts like wet velvet, there it is: the hand-sized saint of obsidian and copper blood, antennae tasting the sudden absence of pressure, of competition, of the endless *shhk-shhk*.

The power touches it. Not with a hand—there are no hands here—but with a *drop*.

The drop falls from the bruised sky, though no rain comes. It is a single bead of water, heavier than it should be, carrying in its molecular structure the memory of chlorophyll, of sunlight striking leaf, of the green world that existed before the glass cooled, before the valley became a bowl. It is not water from this world’s cycle; it is water from the world’s *before*.

It lands on the creature’s antennae-tapestry. The creature freezes. Not in fear, but in *recognition*. It tastes, through ten thousand chemoreceptive filaments, the impossible: the chemical signature of photosynthesis, of oxygen freely given, of a time when eating was not a zero-sum game of crushing and being crushed. It tastes *gift*.

And it screams.

But the scream is not the white-noise shriek of mandibles on stone. It is a new frequency, a harmonic that has never existed in the valley’s roar. It is a note of *gratitude*—a vibration that travels through the resonant glass, through the copper blood of the swarm, through the very substrate they grind. It is the sound of a closed loop being opened, not broken, but given a door.

The drop is absorbed. The memory enters the hemolymph. And the creature returns to the tide, but it is changed. It carries a new pheromone now, a scent that means *there is another way to be hungry*. The signal spreads. Not as a command, but as an invitation. The swarm ripples with it—a wave of violet and blue light that pulses not with the old message (*I am here, the glass is rich*) but with a new one: *There was green once. There will be green again.*

The power speaks then, but not in words. It speaks in the language of pressure and seismic pulse, naming the valley. The name is a vibration in the stone, a deep, warm thrumming that the swarm feels in their tarsal claws, in the bellows of their joints. The name is: **The Vessel of Becoming.**

And because it is named, it is no longer merely a place of erosion. It is a place of *gestation*.

The power does not leave. It remains as a warmth in the glass—a thermal anomaly, a pocket of heat that is not from the sun but from the simple, stubborn benevolence of being seen. The swarm feeds on this warmth. Their metabolism shifts. No longer do they scrape the stone merely to survive, to fill the endless gut of the crush. Now they scrape with *intention*. They begin to dig downward, not just wear the surface smooth, but excavate a true bowl, a womb-shaped depression, seeking the heart of the stone where the pressure is right, where the silica dreams of becoming something else.

They are still lithovores. They still grind and are ground. The weak are still crushed beneath the strong, and the dead are still eaten. But now, when they eat the dead, it is not merely recycling—it is *communion*. And when they grind the glass, it is not merely consumption—it is *preparation*. They are making soil. They are making the future green.

The bioluminescence changes. Alongside the bruised blue and violet, a new color flickers—faint, hesitant, but unmistakable. A pale gold, like the first light of dawn. It pulses in the condensation pools, in the cracks between outcroppings, a signal that means: *We are not alone. We are not only hunger. We are the prayer of stone becoming seed.*

The roar does not cease. It never ceases. But now, beneath the white-noise shriek, beneath the deafening heartbeat of ten million legs, there is a new rhythm. A slow, deep pulse that matches the warmth in the glass, the memory of the drop, the name given in kindness.

The valley remains a bowl of black glass. The creature remains a flattened oval of armor, eyeless and copper-blooded. But now, when the tide parts, and the glass sweats alone in the clearing, there is a warmth there that no sun provided. And in that warmth, for a moment, the endless hunger feels not like a curse, but like a lullaby sung by the world to itself.

The power stays. It is the still point in the churn, the silence within the roar, the benevolent eye that sees the swarm not as a plague of grinding teeth, but as a single, vast, patient *yes*—the universe’s answer to the question: *What happens when the stone decides to dream?*
