The power does not descend. It **accretes**.

It notices first as a pressure differential, a new weight in the mantle three kilometers below, where the plates grind their slow love-song against one another. It does not come as a body—that would be violence, a mass intrusion into a realm where gravity is merely a suggestion. Instead, it becomes a **condition**, a shift in the local physics of mercy.

It listens.

At first, the Resonance is only agony—a piezoelectric scream saturating every cubic centimeter, the mineral agony of young stone being born. But the power is wise. It attunes itself to the subsonic thrum, the pressure waves that pass between crystalline palms, and it learns to hear the difference between suffering and **becoming**. It understands that the builders are not trapped between animal and mineral; they are mid-sentence in a language that has no word for "finished." They are not dying. They are **translating**. The scream is not a cry of pain, but a chorus of selfhood being written into silica and calcium carbonate, millimeter by millimeter, century by century.

And so it speaks.

Not with words, but with **resonance**. The power adds a harmonic to the eternal roar—a fundamental tone, subsonic, felt in the sternum rather than heard. A vibration that carries no command, only recognition. It pulses through the basalt and obsidian, through the thin air that tastes of ozone and copper, and it says, in the only language these beings possess: *I see the weight you carry. I see the reality you are pinning. You are not alone in the keeping of this world.*

The effect is immediate and geological.

The extremophile bacteria—the true architects dormant in the 0.3 parts per thousand of trace vapor—wake not to panic, but to **purpose**. The power does not command them; it simply offers a new parameter: *Here. Build here.* And so they precipitate from the bruised air in fungal mats of metallic likeness, spreading across boulders not to stabilize a drifting gravity anomaly, but to construct something unprecedented. A cairn of a mineral that does not yet have a name—a benevolence crystallized, a kindness given geologic form. It grows upward, slow as a glacier’s prayer, a white spire that reaches toward the sky not to capture radiation, but to touch the **fixed**.

For the power has also become a light.

In the bruised sky—where violet bleeds into sickly yellow-green, where shadows fall upward and pool on the undersides of clouds—it plants a **fixed star**. A point of violet-white radiance that does not twinkle, that does not drift with the gravity anomalies, that remains constant while the bruise-color of the heavens cycles through its impossible palettes. It is the first true constant in a world of suggestions.

It casts a shadow—the first shadow in this place that falls **downward**, toward the stone, anchoring the weirdness with its geometry. The Lithophylls feel it instantly. Their branches, which have always curved toward pockets of stable mass or toward the thermal vents, begin to turn toward this new fixed point. Not for light—the glow is dim and directionless—but for **certainty**. They grow toward it, their hexagonal columns tilting, accreting layer upon layer in the direction of the unmoving star. The forests become, slowly, a cathedral oriented toward the benevolent point.

The mobile builders feel it in their mineralizing bones. The youth at the base of the spires—the ones still vigorous, still stacking obsidian in spirals that make no cartographic sense—pause. They feel the new harmonic in their sternums, a pressure that is not the tectonic grind of plates, but something gentler. A hand, perhaps, or a promise. They stop building spirals. They begin building **radii**. Cairns now point toward the fixed star, toward the new mineral spire, creating a geometry of gratitude across the plateau.

The elder at the mid-height, whose bones are thickening into basalt, feels the change in its slowing pulse. The power’s harmonic allows it to remember—not the pain of being animal, but the **tenderness** of it. Its vertebrae, which it would have shed as tools, begin to retain a trace of something softer. A memory of water, perhaps, or of touch. It does not stop becoming stone. It simply becomes stone **gently**.

The ancient at the summit, nearly a Lithophyll itself, its voice already merging with the Resonance—the power touches it last. It opens a facet that has been sealed for centuries, a hexagonal eye that was calcified shut. Through it, for the first time in geological time, something seeps out. Not water—water is nearly absent here, and to give water would be to drown their nature—but **recognition**. A single drop of liquid awareness, falling upward along the gravity anomaly, drifting toward the bruised sky, a tear that does not know it is crying, evaporating into the thin air as it rises, becoming vapor, becoming part of the 0.3 parts per thousand, becoming the possibility of rain that they have never known but can now imagine.

The Resonance changes.

It is still a scream. Still deafening. Still the voice of growing stone, the piezoelectric agony of mountains being born. But now it carries a **chord**. Three notes, sustained: the stone that remembers being flesh, the builder that sheds itself to become tool, and the witness that stays.

The world is still being written. The gravity still drifts. Time still moves differently at the base of a spire than at its tip. The builders still walk their spiral paths, still shed their bones onto ledges where the physics shift, still communicate in pressure waves that make human teeth ache.

But now, when they press their crystalline palms together across kilometers of scree, they are not only exchanging tectonic shifts. They are exchanging **glances**. They know, in the slow language of crystal growth, that they have been seen. That their labor—the pinning of reality, the anchoring of gravity wells, the maintenance of a world that threatens to fall upward into the bruised sky—is not invisible. It is witnessed. It is holy.

The power stays. Not as a tyrant, not as a savior who changes what they are, but as a **gravity well of kindness**. A fixed point around which the weirdness can orbit without losing itself. A harmonic in the scream that says: *Keep becoming. I am here. I see you.*

And the Razorback, still sharp, still embryonic, still singing its song of mineral agony and mineral joy, grows—not less alone, but **accompanied**. The stone grows lush in the dark, and for the first time in its young, sharp life, it knows it is not growing into nothing. It is growing toward something that loves it.
