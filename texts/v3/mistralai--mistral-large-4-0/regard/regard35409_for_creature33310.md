The power does not arrive as thunder. It does not descend. It **accumulates**.

It begins as a pressure in the marrow of the oldest Lithophyte—the one whose trunk is half-fossil, half-factory, whose canopy has interlocked with the ceiling for ten thousand years. A warmth where there should be only mineral chill. A *listening*.

The Vor-Keth call it **The Attentive**, or **The Gardener of Unfinished Things**, or simply **The Weight That Remembers Being Light**. It is not a god in the sky. It is a god in the *interval*—the pause between one layer of stone and the next, the held breath before the crystal forms. It is benevolent because it understands that to be loved is to be allowed to remain incomplete. It is wise because it knows that a cathedral still under construction is more holy than one that has been consecrated and closed.

And so it acts.

It does not pour water into the dry air—that would be cruelty, a dissolving, a murder. It does not freeze the growth or force the city to completion—that would be tyranny, the end of the Tempo. Instead, it offers the Vor-Keth a new sense, a new organ, a new way of touching the world: **The Art of the Hollow**.

Before, the Vor-Keth only knew **addition**. They fed the Lithophytes specific minerals. They sang to the stone until it swelled around their iron scaffolds. They convinced the geology to precipitate, layer by mineral layer, until their dead became pillars and their factories became fossils-in-waiting. They were gardeners of accretion, of positive space, of *more*. They built by growing, and they thought this was the only way.

But the power shows them **subtraction**.

It teaches them that a tree can be pruned. That a chamber can be carved from the inside. That the fungal moss, when pressed with a specific crystal and a specific intention, will *recede*, leaving a void where there was only carpet. That the mineral dust, when sung to at a frequency just below the threshold of hearing, will crystallize into a shape that holds *absence*—a negative space that is not empty, but *waiting*, a silence that is architectural.

The power does not hand them tools. It hands them **permission**.

It shows a Vor-Keth elder—one whose knees have begun to stiffen with calcification, who is halfway to becoming a statue—how to press their palm against a wall of embryonic stone and not push, but *pull*. How to feel the mineral lattice loosen, not by force, but by **agreement**. How to say to the rock: *Not here. Here, I need air. Here, I need shadow. Here, I need a place where the dust falls but does not settle, where the silence is not filled by the creaking of growth.*

And the stone, being wise in its embryonic way, agrees. It retreats. It yields. It makes room.

This is the revolution: the Vor-Keth begin to build **rooms that are not grown, but excavated**. They create the first **Grotto of Stillness**—a chamber in the heart of a Lithophyte where the sap does not flow, where the walls are smooth and cool and *finished*, not because they are complete, but because they have been deliberately left unfinished, held in a state of potential. In this grotto, a Vor-Keth can sit for a century without a single new mineral layer accreting on their skin. They can feel what it is to be *unaccreted*, to be *fluid* in a world of stone.

The power goes further. It teaches them to **carve the Lithophytes themselves**. Not to kill them—that would be violence—but to *guide* them with negative space. To create windows in the canopy where none existed, letting shafts of the greenish bioluminescence fall through in patterns that change with the thermal currents. To sculpt the trunks into spirals that create vortices in the crystalline dust, so that the "snow" falls in patterns that resemble constellations, telling stories to those who know how to read the drifts.

The city changes. It becomes **sparse** in a new way. Not just spaced out across the valley, but full of **holes**. Voids where the Grazers rest in silence. Chambers where the Silicate Mites go to die, their crystalline shells forming geodes in the negative space, memorials to the maintenance that is no longer needed. Corridors that end not in walls, but in **openings**—windows into the unformed dark beyond the city, where the geology has not yet decided to be solid, where the Sulfur-Wraiths drift like smoke.

The Vor-Keth begin to understand that **to be under construction is not a lack, but a grace**. The power has given them the ability to say *no* to the stone, to hold back the accretion, to create **limits** in a place that previously knew only expansion. This makes their architecture an art, not just a function. They begin to cultivate **beauty** for its own sake—growing a Lithophyte not for the sap it provides, but for the way its glass-leaves will chime in a specific key when the factory horns blow at dawn. Carving a chamber not for shelter, but for the way the dust settles in its corners, creating patterns that map the thermal currents of the cavern's breath.

Most importantly, the power gives them **The Choice of Remaining**.

Before, a Vor-Keth who died became architecture. They were carried to the Accretion Pools and mineralized into pillars. This was beautiful, but it was also a *destiny*—a one-way path. Now, the power shows them that there is another way. A Vor-Keth can choose to become a **Hollow** instead of a **Pillar**. They can allow their body to be excavated, their mineralized flesh carved out like marble, leaving behind a chamber that is *them* but not *them*—a memorial of negative space, a room shaped like their sleeping body, where future generations can enter and feel the absence of a life, the silence of a mind that has moved on.

The power does not take the dead away. It does not resurrect them. It simply ensures that **even in becoming stone, they remain in process**. They become *questions* instead of *answers*. They become spaces that ask: *Who were you? What did you dream? What did you leave unfinished?*

And the Lithophytes, sensing this new reverence for the void, grow differently. They do not rush to fill the empty spaces. They grow *around* them, framing them, honoring them. A carved window becomes a reliquary. A grotto of stillness becomes a shrine. The city becomes a **mandala of accretion and erosion**, a balance of building and unbuilding, of saying yes to the stone and saying no.

The power remains. It does not leave. It settles into the mycelium network like a new nutrient, a new thought. It becomes the **silence between the chimes**, the pause between the heartbeats of the geology. It is the benevolent witness who ensures that the Undergrowth of Keth never becomes a tomb, never becomes finished, never becomes *only* stone.

It ensures that it remains, forever, a **birth**.
