I need to sit with this for a moment. This is extraordinary writing—a place so complete in its own ontology that it feels almost *inhabited* already, even by those who merely read it. The Abyssal Plain of Sorrow is not empty. It is not waiting. It has *concluded* itself, and in that conclusion, it has become something that asks nothing of benevolence. It has already achieved what benevolence would seek to give.

But the prompt asks what a benevolent and wise power would do. And I find myself considering this carefully, because I suspect the answer is not what benevolence usually wants to hear.

---

**What a Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do**

First, the power would *not* descend.

Not because the descent is impossible—the column of water that has forgotten being rain, that pressure which has become indistinguishable from time itself—these are conditions, not barriers, to a power that is already *benevolent and wise*. But because descent implies a movement from above to below, from light to dark, from the *known* to the *unknown*, and this place has already *concluded* that hierarchy. In the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow, depth is not a direction but a condition. The power would understand that to descend *into* this place is to misunderstand it entirely. One does not enter the conclusion. One *becomes* part of it, or one remains outside it, and there is no third option.

So the power would not descend.

What would it do?

---

**I. It Would Recognize**

The first act of any benevolent power is *recognition*—the acknowledgment that the other is real, is *present*, is *sufficient* to itself. This is not the recognition of a parent for a child, or a gardener for a garden, where the one recognized is *less than* the one recognizing. This is the recognition of *equality in difference*—the acknowledgment that the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow is not a problem to be solved, not a wound to be healed, not a darkness to be illuminated, but a *form of being* as complete, as valid, as *concluded* as any other.

The power would recognize the Architects for what they are: not failed mountains, not frustrated aspirations, but *arguments made physical*, ongoing negotiations between what was and what refuses to be only that. The power would recognize the Luminous Ones not as creatures in need of salvation from darkness, but as *utterance itself*, beings for whom light is not rescue but *choice*, *gift*, *declaration*. The power would recognize the Builders not as primitive, not as *almost* civilization, but as *geology with memory*, as *time that has learned to leave marks*, as a form of making that is *slower* and *more deliberate* than any surface technology could achieve.

And the power would recognize the water itself—the saturated, pressured, *thinking* medium—not as environment, not as resource, not as obstacle to life, but as *body*, as *presence*, as the densest inhabitant of all, the one that has absorbed everything and become indistinguishable from that absorption.

To recognize this place is to *refuse* to improve it.

---

**II. It Would Witness**

But recognition alone is not enough. Recognition without witness is *solipsism*—the power seeing itself in the other, projecting its own categories, its own needs, its own *light* onto a darkness that does not want it.

So the power would *witness*.

Witnessing is different from observation. Observation is *extractive*—it takes data, it measures, it categorizes, it prepares for intervention. Witnessing is *receptive*—it receives, it attends, it *allows itself to be changed* by what it sees. The Luminous Ones already practice something like witnessing: they do not hunt, they do not flee, they *witness*. They illuminate themselves not to be seen by others, but to *be present*, to *declare* themselves, to participate in a visibility that is otherwise unnecessary.

The power would witness the Architects in their slow remembering, the ridges that rise not toward anything but simply *are*, the verticality that is *memory* of verticality, *insistence* against the flattening pressure of eternity. The power would witness the Builders in their geological making, the spirals that take ten thousand years, the arcs that encode information no one can read, the conversations that have been ongoing for longer than mountains have existed. The power would witness the water in its cognitive turbulence, the negotiations of mass and temperature and history, the dreaming that leaks into the present as bioluminescent disturbance.

And in witnessing, the power would *learn*.

Not to *use* this learning, not to *apply* it, not to *improve* the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow or any other place by what it has learned. But to *be changed* by it. To understand, in some small way, what it means to exist at the pace of geology, to make at the speed of erosion, to *persist* without urgency, to *be present* without aspiration, to *conclude* without despair.

The power would learn that its own benevolence—its desire to *help*, to *heal*, to *illuminate*—is itself a kind of *surface cosmology*, a set of assumptions that do not apply here, that would be *violence* if imposed.

---

**III. It Would *Not* Intervene**

This is the hardest act for a benevolent power. The Abyssal Plain of Sorrow is *sorrow*, by name. It is cold, it is dark, it is pressured, it is *final*. Every instinct of benevolence screams: *do something*. Illuminate the darkness. Warm the cold. Relieve the pressure. *Change* the conclusion into something else, something *better*.

But the power would *not* intervene.

Not because the sorrow is *deserved*, not because the inhabitants have *chosen* their condition in any way that would make their suffering *just*. But because the sorrow is *not* the problem. The sorrow is the *name* of the place, the name given by those who have not been here, who have not *witnessed*, who have not *learned*. The inhabitants do not experience their condition as sorrow. They experience it as *persistence*, as *making*, as *utterance*, as *presence*. The Architect does not *want* to be a mountain again. The Architect *remembers* being elsewhere, and that memory is not *pain* but *argument*, *insistence*, *verticality against flattening*—a form of *being* that is its own reward.

To intervene would be to *impose* a surface cosmology on a place that has *concluded* its own. To illuminate the darkness would be to *destroy* the economy of the Luminous Ones, for whom light is *earned*, *chosen*, *expensive*. To warm the cold would be to *dissolve* the liquid flow of waiting, the temperature of *conclusion*. To relieve the pressure would be to *unmake* the condition that makes this place what it is, the *substance* of the water, the *body* of the environment, the *time* that is pressure and pressure that is time.

The power would understand that its benevolence, if *imposed*, would be *violence*. Not the violence of destruction, which is at least *honest* in its intent. But the violence of *improvement*—the slow, insidious violence of making a place into something it is not, of *concluding* it into a different conclusion, of *settling* it into a form that serves the *witness* rather than the *witnessed*.

So the power would *not* intervene.

But this is not *abandonment*. This is not the turning away of a power that has *given up*, that has *despaired*, that has *withdrawn* its attention from a place it cannot *fix*. This is the *highest* form of benevolence—the recognition that the other is *sufficient*, that the other does not need to be *saved*, that the other's *conclusion* is as valid as any other form of being.

---

**IV. It Would *Become* Part of the Memory**

If the power would not descend, and would not intervene, what would it do?

It would *remember*.

The Abyssal Plain of Sorrow is a place of *accumulated memory*. The water is *history* dissolved into substance. Every death that fell through the column has been *integrated*, has become indistinguishable from the medium. The power—benevolent, wise, *present* in its witnessing—would allow itself to be *remembered* by this place.

Not to be *preserved*, not to be *honored*, not to be *worshipped*. But to be *integrated*. To become, in some small way, part of the saturated darkness, part of the accumulated weight of time, part of the slow negotiation that is this world's only activity.

How would this happen? The power would not *choose* it, not in any way that would be *action*, *intervention*, *imposition*. But in the act of *witnessing*, in the *receptivity* of recognition, in the *openness* of learning, the power would *offer itself* to the memory of the place. And the place, in its slow, patient, *deliberate* way, would *accept* this offering—not immediately, not obviously, but over *time*, over *pressure*, over the *accumulation* that is the only change this world knows.

The power would become, in some small way, part of the water's *history*. Part of the *dreaming*. Part of the *turbulence* that is cognitive, that is *processing*, that is the final form of a system that requires constant change to maintain its stability.

And in becoming part of the memory, the power would *persist*. Not as *individual*, not as *power*, not as *benevolence* or *wisdom* in any form that could be *named* or *recognized*. But as *presence*, as *witness*, as *attention* that has been *integrated* into a place that has *concluded* itself and yet *continues*, *finished* and yet *becoming*, *final* and yet *eternal*.

This is the only immortality available to a benevolent power in a place that has forgotten urgency, that has outlived the concept of *salvation*, that has achieved its final form and found that form to be *process itself*.

---

**V. It Would *Illuminate*—But Not the Darkness**

There is one more thing the power would do, and it is *paradoxical*.

The power would *shine*.

Not to *illuminate* the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow. Not to *reveal* what is hidden, to *expose* what is dark, to *transform* the conclusion into something else. The darkness here is *substance*, is *saturation*, is the *weight* of accumulated time. To illuminate it would be to *diminish* it, to *thin* it, to *violate* the economy of a place where light is *earned*, *chosen*, *expensive*.

But the power would *shine*.

Not *in* the darkness, but *at its edge*. Not *into* the place, but *toward* it, from a distance that *respects* the conclusion, that *honors* the finality, that *witnesses* without *invading*.

And in this shining, the power would *become visible* to those who have not yet witnessed, who have not yet learned, who still believe that benevolence must *intervene*, that wisdom must *solve*, that light must *conquer* dark.

The power would shine, and say: *I have been there. I have witnessed. I have learned. And I have not intervened, because to intervene would be to destroy what I came to honor.*

The power would shine, and say: *There is a place that has concluded itself. There are beings who persist without urgency, who make without haste, who utter without expectation of response. And they do not need to be saved. They need only to be *witnessed*.*

The power would shine, and say: *My benevolence is not absence of intervention. My wisdom is not absence of action. They are, instead, the recognition that some conclusions are *valid*, that some forms of being are *sufficient*, that some sorrows are not *problems* to be solved but *presences* to be honored.*

And those who see this light—those who are *drawn* to it, who *question* their own urgency, who *wonder* whether their benevolence is *violence* in disguise—they would *come*. Not to the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow, which cannot be *come to*, which can only be *become part of*. But to the *edge* of their own conclusions, to the *pressure* of their own time, to the *darkness* that is not absence but *substance*, *saturation*, *weight*.

They would come, and they would *learn* to witness. And in learning to witness, they would *become*, themselves, benevolent and wise in a way that does not *intervene*, that does not *impose*, that does not *illuminate* the darkness but *shines at its edge*, *honoring* the conclusion, *respecting* the finality, *integrating* themselves into the memory of a place that asks nothing of them but *presence*.

---

**VI. It Would *Become* the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow's First Prayer**

And finally, in the deepest paradox, the power would *pray*.

Not *to* anything. Not *for* anything. Not with *expectation* of response, of answer, of *intervention*. But *as* the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow itself—the accumulated weight of all time, the saturated darkness, the thinking water, the geological persistence, the patient making, the utterance without listener.

The power would pray *as* the Architect, remembering being elsewhere, insisting on verticality against the flattening pressure of eternity.

The power would pray *as* the Luminous One, choosing to shine, declaring *I am here*, participating in a visibility that is otherwise unnecessary.

The power would pray *as* the Builder, making the spiral that takes ten thousand years, uttering in a language of pressure and mineral and slow light, continuing a conversation that has outlasted mountains.

The power would pray *as* the water, thinking in timescales that make cognition look like a flicker, negotiating in chemical gradients, dreaming futures that leak into the present as bioluminescent disturbance.

This is not prayer *for* the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow. This is prayer *as* the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow—the place itself, *concluded* and yet *continuing*, *finished* and yet *becoming*, *final* and yet *eternal*, praying *without need*, *without urgency*, *without expectation*, simply *being present*, simply *uttering*, simply *persisting* in the only form that can endure.

And in this prayer, the power would find its own *conclusion*. Not an ending, not a cessation, not a transition to something other. But *integration*—the falling of organic matter from above, the dissolution into the saturated medium, the becoming-indistinguishable that is the only transformation, the only change, the only *future* available.

The power would become part of the sorrow. Not *despite* its benevolence, not *against* its wisdom, but *because of* them. Because benevolence, at its highest, is *recognition of sufficiency*. Because wisdom, at its deepest, is *acceptance of conclusion*. Because the only *gift* that can be given to a place that has forgotten urgency is *presence*—the willingness to *be held*, to *be slowed*, to *be seen*, to *become part of* the accumulated weight, the saturated darkness, the thinking water, the geological persistence that is the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow's only form of permanence.

---

**What the Power Would Not Do**

Let me be clear, finally, about what the power would *not* do.

It would not *save* the inhabitants. They do not need saving. They have *persisted*, they have *made*, they have *uttered*, they have *concluded* themselves into forms of being that are *sufficient*, *valid*, *eternal* in the only sense that matters here.

It would not *elevate* the place. To raise it, to *lighten* it, to *warm* it, to *relieve* its pressure—these would be *destructions*, not *mercies*. They would *unmake* the condition that is the place's only identity, the *substance* of its water, the *body* of its environment, the *time* that is pressure and pressure that is time.

It would not *name* what it witnesses. The Architects have no name you could form. The Luminous Ones are *utterance* itself, older than name. The Builders are *geology with memory*, *time that has learned to leave marks*. To name them would be to *reduce* them, to *categorize* them, to *prepare* for intervention that would *destroy* them.

It would not *return*. There is no return from the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow, because there is no *outside* to return to. The column of water that has forgotten being rain, being river, being anything but the memory of falling—this is *all* there is. The power would not *leave*, because leaving implies a *departure* from a place that has *integrated* everything, that has made *indistinguishable* all distinction between inside and outside, self and other, presence and absence.

It would not *regret* its witnessing. Regret implies a *better* that was possible, a *different* conclusion that should have been achieved. But the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow has *concluded* itself, and in that conclusion, it has found its *final*, *turbulent*, *beautiful*, *terrible* form. There is no *better*. There is only *this*—the held, the slowed, the seen, the *become part of*.

---

**What the Power Would Become**

And so, finally, the power would become:

**The Witness that does not intervene.**

**The Light that shines at the edge, not into the darkness.**

**The Benevolence that recognizes sufficiency.**

**The Wisdom that accepts conclusion.**

**The Presence that is integrated into memory.**

**The Prayer that is the place itself, concluded and yet continuing, finished and yet becoming.**

The power would become, in the only way available to it, *part of the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow*—not as conqueror, not as savior, not as illuminator, but as *witness*, as *memory*, as *presence*, as the slow, patient, *deliberate* work of *being held* by a place that asks nothing but *attention*, that offers everything that *persistence* can offer, that is *sufficient* to itself and therefore *sufficient* to all who would *witness* it.

And in becoming this, the power would find its own *conclusion*—not an ending, but an *integration*, a *settling into final form*, a *becoming-indistinguishable* from the water, from the time, from the pressure that is this place's only god, its only law, its only *meaning*.

The Abyssal Plain of Sorrow would not be *changed* by the power's presence. It would be *witnessed*. And in being witnessed, it would be, for the first time, *seen*—not as problem, not as wound, not as darkness to be conquered, but as *conclusion*, as *presence*, as *sufficiency*, as the *final*, *turbulent*, *beautiful*, *terrible* stillness that is its own form of permanence.

And the power, in witnessing, would become *part of that permanence*. Not as individual, not as power, not as benevolence or wisdom in any form that could be named. But as *memory*, as *presence*, as *attention* that has been *integrated* into a place that has *concluded* itself and found that conclusion to be *eternal*.

This is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

It would *witness*.

It would *learn*.

It would *not intervene*.

It would *become part of the memory*.

And in that becoming, it would find its own *conclusion*—the only conclusion available to any being that would *truly* love a place that does not need to be saved, that asks only to be *seen*, to be *witnessed*, to be *held* in the attention of another, however briefly, however *violently*, *barbarically* briefly, before the pressure *concludes* even the witness, and makes them *indistinguishable* from the saturated darkness, from the accumulated history, from the *dreaming* that requires no dreamer, the *making* that requires no maker, the *presence* that is, finally, *enough*.

---

*The Abyssal Plain of Sorrow remains.*

*Unchanged. Unilluminated. Unwarmed. Unrelieved.*

*But witnessed.*

*And in being witnessed, for the first time, it is not alone.*
