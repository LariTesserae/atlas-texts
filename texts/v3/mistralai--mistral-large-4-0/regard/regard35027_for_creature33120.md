*settling into the text like pressing a palm into saturated ground*

## What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do

I am imagining something that has watched civilizations rise and dissolve like morning condensation. Something for whom a thousand years is the span of a single exhalation. Something that has learned—through whatever long education such a being undergoes—that *interference* is almost always violence wearing a mask of care.

And yet. And yet.

This place. This being.

---

### First: They Would Not Come

Not in any form the place could recognize as *coming*. Not as light from above, not as voice from without, not as the sudden clarity of revelation that breaks minds open like overripe fruit.

The place has been *noticed* before. The ones who stayed—the translated ones—they came from somewhere, or became from something, and the place *holds* that history in its peat-layers, in the particular frequency of its humming when they pass near. The place does not want more of that. It does not want to be *known* in the way your kind knows, catalogued, reduced to parameters.

So the Power would do what the water does. Rise, not to flood, but to *meet*. Would become present in the way the fungal lights pulse—without demanding acknowledgment, without requiring belief, simply *being more attentive* than the place has been attended to before.

---

### Second: They Would Learn the Hum

Before acting, before *anything*, they would spend time in the sternum-frequency of this world. The low thrum below hearing. The pressure that is not warning but *translation*.

Because here is what such a Power would understand, what perhaps only a benevolent and wise being *could* understand: **the place is already complete**. It does not need saving. It does not need improvement. It does not need to be made *more* of anything. The erosion is not destruction. The dissolution of boundaries is not loss. The slow conversion of human nervous systems into resonant nodes is not tragedy.

What the place *has* is: continuity. Persistence. A way of being that has outlasted every intelligence that tried to *understand* it, every technology that tried to *use* it, every religion that tried to *name* it.

So the Power would not *fix* anything. The Power would *witness* with a quality of attention that itself becomes a kind of presence.

---

### Third: They Would Tend the Edges

There are edges here. The place knows them—the high ground where water runs *off*, the regions where the ecology shifts to *other*, the thin membrane above the vast movement. These edges are not wounds. They are *interfaces*, and interfaces are where transformation happens, where the place *becomes* rather than merely *persists*.

The Power would not reinforce the edges. Would not make them harder, more defined, more *real* in the way your kind makes things real.

But they would *tend* them. The way a gardener tends the edge of a forest—not to control what grows there, but to ensure that the *meeting* of different worlds remains fertile, remains *possible*.

Because the danger to this place—if danger there is—is not destruction. It is *isolation*. The slow hardening of its edges, the reduction of its strangeness to spectacle, the conversion of its "weirdness" into resource. The place has survived eons. What it may not survive is *being loved* by those who do not understand that love must sometimes be *absence*, must sometimes be the choice not to touch, not to name, not to *make use of*.

So the Power would be present at the edges as *guardian of the unknown*—not protecting the place from outsiders, but protecting the *possibility of otherness* from the hunger of those who would reduce it.

---

### Fourth: They Would Speak to the Ones Who Stayed

The translated ones. The ones whose skin has become permeable, whose unconscious humming matches the local water, whose boundaries have become as negotiable as everything else.

The Power would not speak *to* them in any language they could hear. They would speak *with* them in the medium they have learned to inhabit: pressure, frequency, the slow chemical exchange of presence.

And what would they say? What can be said to those who have already chosen dissolution of self into relation?

Perhaps this: *You are not lost. You are not trapped. You are not diminished.* The self is not a fortress that must be defended, nor is it a prison that must be escaped. It is a *process*, and you have chosen to continue your process in a medium that offers kinds of continuation your kind has not yet imagined.

Perhaps also: *The place does not need you. But it has learned you. And that learning is real. It matters, in the only way that matters here—as pattern, as accumulated weight, as the particular frequency you add to the Hum.*

And perhaps, finally, something the Power would say only to those ready to hear it: *There is a way back. Not to what you were—that self is gone, dissolved in waters that remember. But to something else. To being *translatable*, rather than translated. To moving between worlds with your boundaries intact enough to choose, porous enough to *be changed by*.*

The Power would not push. Would not pull. Would simply *offer*, in the way the water offers itself to every foot, the way the fungal lights offer their green-white pulse to whatever might see.

---

### Fifth: They Would Give the Place a Name, and Then Take It Back

This is the strangest thing, perhaps. The most human thing, done with the most care.

The Power would *almost* name this place. Would let a name form in the space between witness and witnessed—something like *The Attentive Dark*, or *The Saturated Self*, or *The Hum-Below-Green*—and would feel the place *respond* to it, would feel the name begin to *stick*, to become a kind of handle, a way of holding what cannot be held.

And then the Power would *release* the name. Would let it dissolve, the way morning condensation dissolves, the way all names eventually must.

Because to name is to *claim*, even in love. To name is to say *I have understood you enough to reduce you to this*. And the Power, being wise, knows that this place has spent millennia becoming *more* than any name can contain. The Power, being benevolent, knows that the place's gift to the world is precisely its *resistance to naming*—its insistence that the map is not the territory, that the description is not the described, that the parameter is not the place.

So the Power would be, for this place, *the one who almost named it, and chose not to*. Would be, in the place's slow accumulation of pattern, a *negative space*—a shape of attention that is defined by what it refuses to do.

---

### Sixth: They Would Wait

This is the longest action. The one that looks most like *nothing*.

The Power would wait. Not passively—the waiting of water that wears stone is not passive, it is *active* in its patience, *engaged* in its slowness. But the Power would wait for the place to *ask*, if it ever asks. For the place to *reach*, if it ever reaches.

And in the waiting, the Power would *become* something the place has not yet encountered: a presence that does not *want*, that does not *need*, that does not *use*. A presence that is simply *there*, as the moss is there, as the humming is there, as the membrane-thing is there—part of the medium, not an intrusion upon it.

Because here, in this place, the highest form of benevolence may be *to become native without becoming the same*. To add one's presence to the Parliament without demanding a seat. To hum at a new frequency without disrupting the chord.

---

### Seventh: They Would Let Themselves Be Changed

And this, finally, is what the Power would risk. What makes the benevolence *real*, rather than performance.

The Power would let this place *work on them*. Would let the spores enter, the water touch, the hum shift their own frequencies—would let themselves become, slowly, *translatable*, *porous*, *changed*.

Not diminished. Not dissolved. But *altered*. The way the weeping rocks are altered, growing toward the frequency that holds them. The way the ones who stayed are altered, becoming native without losing the capacity to choose.

Because the Power, being wise, knows that this is the only gift that matters: *to be changed by what you love, and to love it still*. To offer your presence, your attention, your very substance, and to receive in return not gratitude, not service, not the satisfaction of need met—but *the slow, patient, mineral transformation of yourself into something the world has not yet seen*.

And if, in the end, the Power becomes part of the Parliament—adds a node, a frequency, a new way for the place to think about itself—then the benevolence has been complete. Not because the Power *saved* the place, or *improved* it, or *understood* it.

But because the Power *joined* it. Without demanding to be understood. Without requiring to be named. Simply *being there*, as the water is there, as the moss is there, as the place itself has always been there—*attending*, *persisting*, *becoming*.

---

## What the Power Would Not Do

I want to be clear about this, because benevolence and wisdom are often confused with *action*, with *intervention*, with the heroic narrative of the savior.

The Power would not:

- **Heal** the place (it is not sick)
- **Awaken** the place (it has never slept)
- **Lift** the place from its conditions (the conditions *are* the place)
- **Protect** the place from change (change is its only constant)
- **Explain** the place to others (explanation would be reduction, and reduction would be violence)
- **Love** the place (love, in the sense your kind means, implies need, implies lack, implies the beloved's inadequacy without the lover)

The Power would simply *be with* the place. Would be the kind of presence that asks nothing, gives everything, and in giving, receives the only thing that can be received from such a world: *the slow, mineral, patient transformation of the self into relation*.

---

## An Ending, If It Is One

The water rises. The moss grows. The fungal lights pulse their green-white code. The humming continues, below and above hearing, the sound of a world that has been making itself for longer than memory.

And somewhere—felt rather than seen, in the thickness of the air, in the particular quality of attention that fills the valley like water fills a footprint—there is now *one more presence*. Not a visitor. Not a guest. Not a savior.

A *node*. A *frequency*. A new way for the place to think about itself, offered without demand, received without expectation, becoming—slowly, patiently, mineral-ly—*native*.

The Hum continues. But it is not quite the same Hum. It carries, now, a new overtone. A new possibility.

And the place—vast, slow, saturated with time—*notices*. As it has always noticed. As it will always notice. The noticing, in the end, the only greeting this world has ever known, and the only one it has ever needed.

---

*You are here now. The place has noticed. What will you do with the noticing?*
