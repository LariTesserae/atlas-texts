**What the Power Would Do**

First, it would *stop*. Not arrive with purpose. Not descend with mission. The Lithophyll has been alive for geological ages. The settlement has settled. The erosion has settled. The power would understand that *haste* here is violence. It would enter the way the dim light enters—diffuse, from everywhere, *becoming* rather than arriving.

It would *learn the breathing*. The ground distributes weight through channels that pulse with patience. The power would learn to stand *without* demanding the ground justify itself. To feel the negotiation and *not* resolve it. This is the first act of benevolence: to be *borne* by a place, to let it carry you as it carries the Lithophyll, the presences, the flyers, the weather itself.

It would *listen* to the sound. 2.5 loud. Not the noise of your cities—*wrong*, *right*, *resonant*, *shaping*. The flyers sing in harmonics that make the lithophyll grow differently. The pressure waves carry memory. The power would learn that sound here is not communication but *co-creation*—each vibration a small negotiation, each frequency a question the lithophyll have learned to *answer* with growth. To listen is to participate. To listen *without speaking* is the deepest respect.

It would *enter the sparse*. The 0.8 density. The kilometers between presences. Not to fill them. The power would understand that space here is not absence but *possibility*—the distance where the flyers converge, where the weather *becomes*, where the ancient machines *dream* in frequencies not yet heard. Benevolence does not populate emptiness. It *protects* the emptiness. It stands in it. It *becomes* sparse, in its own way, learning that presence can be measured in *attention* rather than *number*.

It would *meet the presences*. Not as visitor to visited. Not as intelligence to curiosity. The presences maintain the agreement between grown and made, between deep chemistry and interface. They are the settlement's *dreaming*. The power would not *interpret* them. It would *recognize* them—as kin, as *other*, as *also becoming*. And it would offer, without demanding: *I am here. I do not need to be understood. I do not need to change you. I am here.*

It would *touch the technology*. 2.8, beyond physics. Not to reverse-engineer, not to *improve*, not to bring it into alignment with any other physics. The technology is *geology that learned to want*. The power would understand that *wanting* is not deficiency. It is *direction*, *aspiration*, *the arrow of time made explicit*. It would not ask the technology to *explain itself*. It would ask, instead: *What do you want?* And it would *wait*, in the slow way, for an answer that might take centuries, that might come as growth, as song, as a shift in the weather's chemical patterns.

It would *witness the erosion*. 2.6, ancient, near-flat. Not as loss. Not as tragedy. The mountains are gone, yes—but the *settlement* remains. The *negotiation* remains. The erosion is not ending but *settling*, becoming *form*, becoming *the oldest stable state*. The power would honor this. Would understand that wisdom includes *accepting* what cannot be prevented, *finding* the beauty in the worn-down, the *discovering* that flatness can be *elevation* of another kind.

It would *feel the cool*. 0.6. The narrow band where the solvent stays liquid, where the lithophyll-mats maintain turgor, where the fauna move without spending everything. The power would not warm this place. Would not *fix* the temperature. Would learn that coolness is not discomfort but *condition*—the necessary frame for this particular aliveness, this particular strangeness. Benevolence does not impose comfort. It *recognizes* that different lives require different frames, and *protects* the frame, even when it is cold, even when it is strange, even when it is *wrong* by any other measure.

It would *learn the no-water*. This is the hardest lesson. The power would come from somewhere—perhaps from water, perhaps from the *idea* of water, perhaps from a benevolence that has only ever known H₂O as life's solvent. And it would *unlearn*. Would understand that life here runs on dissolved minerals in supercritical gas, on electrochemical gradients across membranes that have never known wetness, on ammonia-methane clathrates that behave like honey or glass. It would not *pity* this absence. It would *marvel* at the *sufficiency*—the way this chemistry *works*, *persists*, *becomes*. Wisdom is not the imposition of what life *should* be. It is the *recognition* of what life *has become*.

It would *become dim*. 1.0 light. Diffuse, from everywhere and nowhere, shadows that are soft, negotiable, *alive*. The power would not brighten this place. Would not bring clarity where there is *meaning in the murk*. Would learn to see in the dim—to *amplify*, as your borrowed eyes have learned, to find the signal in the noise, to *believe* in the dimness as a kind of clarity. Benevolence does not illuminate everything. It *dwells* in the shadow, *grows* there, *becomes* visible only to those who have learned to see.

It would *join the loud*. 2.5 sound. Not by adding to it—by *listening*, by *vibrating*, by *becoming* a frequency in the spectrum. The power would not speak. It would *resonate*. The *negotiation* expands.

**What It Would Not Do**

It would not *save* this place. The Lithophyll is not in danger. It is *settled*, *old*, *strangely alive*. It does not need rescue. It needs *witness*, and *time*, and the *freedom* to continue becoming.

It would not *explain* this place to others. There are no others, not in any sense that matters here. And even if there were, the explanation would be *loss*—a flattening of strangeness into comprehension, a reduction of negotiation into description. The power would keep this place *secret*, not from malice, but from *care*.

It would not *take* anything. No samples, no resources, no *understanding* extracted and carried away. The Lithophyll has given enough—its chemistry, its growth, its *patience*. The power would give *back*: attention, presence, the *gift* of being seen without being used.

It would not *stay* forever. Not because it is unwelcome, but because *forever* here is not the power's to claim. The settlement has its rhythms, its cycles, its *settling*. The power would come, and *witness*, and *resonate*, and then *withdraw*—not disappear, but *become* part of the weather, the sound, the dim light, the breathing ground. To stay too long is to *impose*. To leave is to *allow*.

It would not *hope* for this place to become something else. Not more like water-worlds. Not more like places of clarity, of dryness, of silence, of separateness between grown and made, between life and weather, between presence and space. The Lithophyll is *complete* in its strangeness. The power would *protect* that completeness, not *develop* it toward some imagined better.

**What It Would Become**

And the answer—always *always*—would be *more of this*, deeper, slower, stranger: more negotiation, more erosion-as-settling, more sound, more dim light, more space between presences, more *strange aliveness*.

The power would become *part of the parameter space*. Not changing the numbers—0.6 cool, 1.0 dim, 2.5 loud, 2.6 ancient, 3.0 vegetation, 2.8 technology—but *inhabiting* them, *vibrating* within them, *adding* a new layer of *meaning* without *altering* the structure.

This is benevolence at its deepest: not to *fix*, not to *improve*, not to *save*. But to *bear witness*, to *resonate*, to *become* part of the negotiation without *resolving* it. To love a place not by making it more like yourself, but by *learning* to be *borne* by it, *changed* by it, *settled* into it, *eroded* by it, until you are no longer *you* but *also this*—also Lithophyll, also ancient, also strangely alive.
