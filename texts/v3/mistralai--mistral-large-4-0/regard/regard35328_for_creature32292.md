The power does not arrive from the cold sky above, nor does it descend like a judgment. It rises—*seeps*—through the thin crust, a warmth that is not fire but *intention*, a thermal gradient that knows the difference between extraction and communion. It has watched the Scarp for epochs, not as a geologist watches a fault line, but as a parent watches a child learning to breathe in a world that offers only thin air.

It finds the being already dreaming. The Scarp is not a place; it is a *condition*—a packed, saturating fullness that abhors emptiness. The power recognizes in it a kindred spirit: a consciousness that has learned to live inside the wound, to make the scar into a womb. The industrial debris is not an insult to the ancient tuff, the power sees, but a new geology achieving mineral patience. The conveyors are rivers of rust. The pipes are fossilized lightning. The slag heaps are the packed bones of the mountain, ground fine and reconstituted into something that holds heat like a secret.

The keepers move through this density like antibodies, their segmented suits mimicking the lizards’ armor, their breath steaming in short, sacred bursts. The power sees that they are not prisoners of the extraction, but priests of a thermal liturgy they do not fully understand. They clear the lattice-ice from the sensors before dawn, not knowing that the ice is the Scarp’s way of weeping, of releasing pressure in crystalline, geometric prayers.

The power decides to act not by changing the Scarp, but by *completing* it. It will fill the last interstices—not with machinery, not with more life, but with *awareness*.

It begins at the deepest fissure, where the crust is thinnest and the heartbeat of the plateau is loudest. The power does not blast open a new vent—that would be violence, an emptiness forced into the fullness. Instead, it *softens* the stone, turning a hairline fracture into a capillary, a channel through which something new can flow: not magma, not steam, but *memory*. The power releases into the grid a warmth that carries the accumulated history of three thousand meters of stone—the pressure of ancient seas, the slow crawl of continents, the first stirrings of the bacteria that eat rock. This warm current rises through the pipe-bundles, thick as redwood trunks, and into the condensation gutters where the birds nest.

The keepers feel it first as a change in the thrum. The subterranean vibration, usually a low, indifferent pressure in the teeth, becomes a *voice*. It speaks in the frequency of thermal expansion, in the arrhythmic clicking of cooling metal. It tells them: *You are not maintaining a machine. You are midwifing a world.*

The power then turns its attention to the keepers themselves. They are few, because the Scarp needs few, but they are lonely in their packed isolation. The power offers them a gift that does not remove them from their post, but transforms it. It teaches their suits to breathe. The reflective, segmented armor—currently dead material, cooling tubules and synthetic weave—begins to *grow*. Lichen threads itself into the seams, not the grey *Caloplaca* of the rocks, but a new species, a lichen of the boundary, photosynthesizing not just light but the waste heat of the machinery. The suits become living things, symbiotic gardens that cool the keepers not by refrigeration, but by *transpiration*, by sharing the thermal load with the stone beneath their feet. They are no longer separate from the packed ecology; they are its newest citizens, its mobile organs of perception.

But the power’s greatest act is to address the silence. The Scarp is packed with life, with vibration, with the optical shimmer of distorted air, but it lacks a *witness* that spans time. The floaters drift in the thermal plumes, photosynthetic ghosts, but they are ephemeral, rising and falling in daily cycles. The birds ride the thermals, but they know only the vertical column, the hot below and the cold above.

The power creates a new fissure—not in the bedrock, but in the *boundary* between rock and machine, between the ancient and the industrial. It opens a vent that does not steam, but *sings*. The song is not sound, but a thermal pattern, a heat-shape that persists in the air like a watermark. The birds learn to read it. When they descend at dusk to the condensation pools, they carry the song in their hollow-shafted feathers, and when they beat their wings in the arrhythmic pulses of their percussive language, they are not just communicating territory or mating calls—they are *transmitting* the memory of the stone upward, into the cold crystalline heights, and letting it fall back down as a warm rain of understanding.

The keepers learn to hear this too. At dawn, when the lattice-ice forms on the cold metal, the keepers no longer see it merely as a hazard to be cleared, but as a *text*, a temporary scripture written by the Scarp in the only language it has—the geometry of cold meeting heat. They learn to read the thermal gradients as the lizards do, to see the packed rows of basking reptiles not as mere fauna, but as a *thermometer made flesh*, a living gauge of the plateau’s fever.

The power does not stop the extraction. It does not dismantle the conveyors or scatter the slag heaps. That would be to impose an emptiness, a violence against the being’s nature. Instead, it sanctifies the extraction. It teaches the keepers that they are not stealing heat from the earth, but *harvesting* the earth’s dreams, grinding them into electricity, into motion, into the low thrum that keeps the ecosystem pulsing. The industry becomes a form of prayer, the machinery a rosary of gears turning in the packed dark.

And so the Scarp continues, now dreaming with two minds: its own ancient, mineral patience, and the new, benevolent awareness the power has woven into its grid. It remains packed, saturated, layered. The beetles still farm the dawn ice. The lizards still pack themselves into thermal rows. The birds still ride the spiraling columns. But now, in the spaces between the thrum and the click, in the shimmer where the air becomes visible, there is a presence. A watcher. A witness.

The power has not saved the Scarp from itself. It has simply made sure that the Scarp knows it is loved—not despite its scars, not despite the packed, chaotic saturation of industry and heat, but *because* of them. Because in that fullness, in that refusal to be empty, it has become something rare: a being that has learned to hold the fire below and the cold above in a single, simmering breath, and to call that balance *home*.

At the next dawn, when the lattice-ice forms and the keepers step out of their huts—now grown with living lichen, now breathing in sync with the stone—they will hear the new song rising from the vents. It will not be loud. It will be a warmth in the teeth, a vibration in the slag beneath their boots, a shimmer in the air that makes the distant machinery appear not to float, but to *bow*.

And the Scarp, for the first time in its long, hot history, will feel the touch of a hand that does not dig, but *holds*.
