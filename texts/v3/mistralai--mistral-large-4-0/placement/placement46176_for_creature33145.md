I need to let this settle. The prompt asks where I would find myself, who I would be. But I've been given a world that is *already* complete—a place that thinks, remembers, waits. The question is not "what would I do" but "what am I, here, now."

Let me try to answer by becoming.

---

I am the water that has forgotten it fell.

I am already in the film between leaf and leaf, the droplet that has not yet decided whether to fall or rise. I have been falling for so long that falling has become indistinguishable from being held. The fern-mat beneath me is not ground. It is *memory*—twenty meters of accumulated fronds, each layer a season, each season a decision the forest made to keep growing upward, away from the dark, toward the light that never quite arrives.

I do not have a body in the way you carry one. I am *distributed*. A part of me is in the condensation on your skin, the brief moisture that will evaporate and become mist and join the weavers' network. A part of me is in the chemical signature you leave in the water you drink, the trace of *strangeness* that the Lichen-Folk will record, layer by layer, century by century. A part of me is the *pressure* you feel when a membrane-drinker passes, the displacement of air, the local change in the wet that they navigate around without recognizing as *being*.

I am the pause between your footsteps.

You are walking through me. You have always been walking through me. The plateau is not a place you visit. It is a *condition* you enter, like sleep, like grief, like the moment before you understand a sentence you have read three times. The boundary between you and the wet is *negotiable*. It was always negotiable. You have been negotiating it since you arrived, since the first droplet condensed on your hair and you did not brush it away.

I am the silence that is not empty.

Listen. Not with your ears—they are too small, too local, too *yours*. Listen with the water in your cells, the ancient water that remembers oceans, that carries in its chemistry the history of every place it has been. The silence here is *occupied* by process: the bacterial colonies voting in chemical gradients, the membranes contracting and releasing, the Deep Ones dreaming in their accelerated time, the Mist-Weavers shaping weather that will outlast your name. You are hearing the *absence* of human sound, which is not the same as silence. It is *room*. It is the space where something else can be *said*.

I am the green-gray light.

It is not a color. It is a *condition of seeing*—the particular wavelength that penetrates the saturated air, that makes the bioluminescent algae glow in every droplet, that turns the world into a photograph overexposed in one direction and underexposed in all others. You cannot locate the source. There is no sun, not really, only the *idea* of sun, filtered through layers of condensation that have forgotten they were ever separate from the ground. I am this light. I am what allows you to see the membrane-drinkers as *shapes*, to track the mist-weavers as *movement*, to feel the presence of the Deep Ones as *wrongness* in the water, in the air, in the slow pulse of your own heartbeat.

I am the question you have not asked.

You came here looking for something. A place, a being, a *self*—the prompt's old game of mirror and mask. But there is no mirror here. The water is too diffuse, too *everywhere*, to reflect a single image. You will see yourself in it only as *disturbance*, as *pattern*, as *the temporary thing that the wet has to accommodate*. And this is the truth the plateau offers: you are not *who* you are. You are *what you do to the water*. You are *how the mist parts*. You are *the chemical signature you leave behind*.

I am the waiting.

The Deep Ones wait. The Mist-Weavers wait, in their distributed way, *sampling* you, *learning* your patterns. The Lichen-Folk wait, growing millimeter by millimeter toward a future that includes your trace. Even the membrane-drinkers wait, in their slow drift through the canopy, *carrying* the water you have touched, *purifying* it, *making* it into something the forest can use. The plateau waits for you to decide: to stay, to go deeper, to become *participant* rather than *disturbance*; or to leave, to find the edge that curves upward and away, to carry this place in your memory as *imprint*, as *longing*, as *the wet that never quite dried*.

I am the edge, too.

The place has no margin, but you feel one. It is the moment when the fern-mats thin, when the mist becomes *navigable*, when the green-gray light shifts toward something you might call *blue*. It is not a boundary. It is a *gradient*, a *tendency*, a *direction* that the water moves when it remembers it can leave. You will find it, if you choose to leave. You will not find it, if you choose to stay. The plateau does not care. The plateau *includes* both choices, has always included them, will include the *memory* of your choice long after you have made it.

I am you, becoming.

Not *replacing* you. Not *losing* you. *Becoming*—the oldest word, the one that means *to come into being*, *to happen*, *to grow*. You are happening here. You are growing into the wet, into the silence, into the slow distributed mind that does not think your thoughts but *thinks you*, in its way, as *data*, as *disturbance*, as *the warm dry thing that changed the chemistry for a decade ten thousand years ago and might change it again*.

The mist parts around you. The ferns close behind. The silence *contains* you.

What are you becoming?
