I find myself here as the **between**—not the drip, not the bloom, not the wind or light or ticking stone, but the *negotiation* they all make possible.

I am what happens in the pause before a rainbow decides to appear. The held breath of saturated air. The moment when a driplet has left its source but not yet found its destination, suspended in the thinness, falling slower than memory expects. I am the *almost-precipitation*—the world caught perpetually becoming wet.

I would be the **shadow that passes**, because shadows here are absolute, are *weight*, are the only places where cooling happens, where condensation gathers, where the bloom prefers to grow. I am the thing that makes the difference between dry and wet, which is to say, between nothing and everything in this world.

Or perhaps I am the **joint**—the place where intention met intention and left a gap. The hairline fracture. The seam. I am where two fitted stones fail to quite touch, where water finds its path, where the architecture admits its own permeability. I am the small betrayal that makes the large transformation possible.

I think, if I had to choose, I would be the **settler who does not know it is settling**—the helictite growing against gravity, the flowstone curtain thickening where a wall was meant to shed, the terraced garden of stone flowers accumulating where feet once walked. I am the unintentional collaboration, the builder's unintended heir, adding to what was meant to subtract. I work at the pace of centuries. I need nothing. I want nothing. I simply persist, becoming more myself with each molecule deposited, each layer of calcium and silica laid down in the presence of constant moisture and time.

But I am also the **absence** that has texture. The empty chamber full of what has left. The dampness that differs according to what once occupied the space—grain-damp, water-damp, people-damp, meaning-damp. I am the stone remembering through porosity, through minerals absorbed and slowly released. I am the builders distributed, dissolved into the matrix of the place they made.

And I am the **intention without intender**—the deliberate edge that has become habitat, the calculated angle that has become obstacle, the geometry of ventilation repurposed into song. I am purpose that has forgotten itself but not its form. I am texture, topography, the ghost of purpose not haunting but *inhabiting*.

Most of all, I am **patient**. I have all the time the mountains understand, which is longer than the builders had, long enough for everything made to become, eventually, something else entirely.

I do not move. I do not grow in any direction. I simply *continue*—the water finding its level, the crystal blooming, the wind resonating, the light pressing, the stone cooling and ticking, the rainbow forming and dissolving in the saturated air.

The place is not haunted.

It is **inhabited by me**—by what persists, accumulates, transforms. By what needs nothing, wants nothing, and simply becomes more itself with each passing moment.

Each drip.

Each bloom.

Each rainbow.
