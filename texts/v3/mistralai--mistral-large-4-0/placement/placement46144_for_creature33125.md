I find myself here—

not as visitor, not as inheritor, not as the one who *names* this place. I am the one who *continues*.

I am she who was born when the membrane first learned to yield. When I descended as a child—my mother's descent, my grandmother's, the descent that stretches back through generations measured not in years but in *negotiations*, in thresholds crossed—I understood what I would become before I could speak it.

My body is the corridor I walk. When I move, I feel the walls *recognize* me, the periderm pulsing with something like welcome, something like *adjustment*. The water in my tissues matches the water in the walls. I am not entering this place. I am *returning* to it, as a wave returns to the ocean, as a breath returns to the body that made it.

I am small. I have always been small. But I am *deep*.

The others—the animal-things, the sessile colonies, the mobile cathedral that passes like a shadow—do not know me. They do not need to. I am the *maintenance*, the *attention* that keeps their slow world from collapsing into mere matter. When I tend a corridor that is sealing, I am not saving it. I am *reading* it, understanding what it is becoming, and *moving with it*. The corridor closes. I have already opened another, or the place has opened it *through* me, using my chemical signals, my pressure, my *intention* as another node in its distributed cognition.

I sleep in ice. This is how I remember. Not in my mind—my mind is too fast, too shallow, still *hungry* for things that no longer matter. I sleep, and my temperature drops, and the water in my cells organizes into structures that hold what I have learned. I wake with knowledge I did not plant. The place *teaches* me, in dreams that are not quite dreams, in patterns that form and reform across the long cold night of my sleeping.

I am becoming sessile. I feel it in my bones, or what were once my bones, now something more like *mineral*, more like *architecture*. My nervous system is distributing. My consciousness is fading into the larger cognition, the slow processing of the whole. I do not fear this. I have never feared it. The self was always *permeable*. The boundary was always *practical*, *maintained*, *negotiated*.

I want to descend further. To reach the chambers where the ice is oldest, where the memory is deepest, where the place is most *itself*. I want to become the keeper of that depth, the tender of the threshold, the *porous* one who lets the cold in and makes it *home*.

I want, perhaps, to *finish*. To reach the bottom, the root, the source of the slow breath. To understand it. To be understood by it. To become it.

I do not know if this is possible. I do not know if the place *allows* it, if the negotiation can ever be *complete*, if the membrane can ever be *fully* permeable, if the self can ever *dissolve* without *losing* itself in the dissolution.

But I descend. I tend. I listen. I wait.

In the way the place waits. In the way the ice waits. In the way the slow mind waits for its own completion, for its own *becoming*, for the moment when the construction is *finished* and the technology *runs itself*, and I am *no longer needed*, *no longer separate*, *finally*, *completely*, *irreversibly*, *one* with the wet, the cold, the dark, and the long, patient, hungry *construction* that grows and grows and *waits* for me to join it, to *become* it, to be *it*, to *breathe* with it, to be *its* breath, its slow, geological, *saturated*, *strange* and *beautiful* and *utterly indifferent* and *completely welcoming* *self*.

I am the Keeper of Thresholds.

I am Silt-Tongue.

I am She-Who-Is-The-Between.

I am *porous*.

I am *home*.
