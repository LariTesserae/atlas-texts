I will stay longer. Let me not settle yet.

There is something I have not named: the *rhythm* of habitation here. Not the daily cycle—surface people have that—but something older and stranger. The wet season changes everything, and they know this, and they *prepare* for it in ways that look like resignation. The chambers that will flood are known. The hangings that must be moved, or not moved, are known. There is a word for this knowing, something like *the-track-of-water-through-years*. They do not fight the flood. They *accommodate* it. They have learned which fabrics can be submerged and recovered, which must be carried up, up, to the dry chambers that exist but are rarely used, kept ready, kept *empty*, a kind of negative space in their architecture.

And the dry season is not better. The dry season is when the walls *shrink*, when the mineral contracts, when tiny cracks appear that were sealed by moisture and are now open to the deep. They hear this. They hear the place *breathing out* in the dry, *breathing in* when the water returns. They have learned to read these breaths. A chamber that is too dry for too long is a chamber that is *hungry*, that will take from the builders what it cannot take from the stone.

I am thinking about the body in this place. The body that persists here is not the body that descends. The descent changes it, yes—the warm air, the narrowing passage, the first touch of mineral floor that holds the memory of hands and feet. But the *real* change is slower. It is the damp, as the place says. But it is also the *listening*. The body learns to listen differently. The skin becomes a surface for vibration. The bones, perhaps, become conductors. There is a condition here, in the oldest builders, that the surface world would call a sickness: the joints that ache with weather, the eyes that weep constantly, the way the body holds water in places it should not. They do not call it sickness. They call it *the-condition-of-being-here*, and they do not cure it because they do not know it as separate from themselves.

The newcomers notice it first in their own bodies. The way the damp finds them *immediately*—not slowly, over years, but in the first hours, the first sleep. They wake with their skin *aware* of moisture, though no sweat has formed. They dream of water. They dream of being *listened to*. Some wake screaming. The builders do not comfort them, not in the surface way. They bring them water from the cisterns, water that has been *trained*, made regular, and they say: *Drink. It will not help. But it is the custom.*

The custom. Everything here is custom, but the word is wrong—it implies choice, and choice implies a chooser who could do otherwise. They do not choose to be builders. They *become* builders, in the way the walls become bone, in the way the hangings become fabric. The newcomer descends, and the place *offers* them this becoming. Some refuse, or try to. They stay in the upper chambers, near the chimney, where the air is warmer and the listening less intense. They survive, barely, and they are *pitied*, in a way that has no surface equivalent. Pity here is not condescension. It is *recognition of incompletion*. They have not been *fully heard* by the place. They are still *surface*, still separate, still capable of leaving. This is not freedom. This is *incompleteness*.

I want to understand the *work* more precisely. Not the bellows, not the weaving, not the metal. Something else. The work of *attention*.

A builder's day—if day is the word, for light here is a rumor, a memory of above—is structured around the *reading* of the place. They check the damp. They listen to the dripping, and they know, from the pitch, from the rhythm, from the *almost-pattern*, which basins are filling, which are emptying, which new paths the water has found. They check the hangings, and they know, from the way they move, from the *wrongness* of a hanging that moves against the air current, that something has changed. They check the creatures, and they know, from their gathering, from their alarm calls, from their *waiting* at the sealed chambers, that something is *approaching*.

This is not superstition. This is *data*. The creatures see what the builders cannot see, and their alarm calls are *information*, transmitted in a register that can carry through the hangings without distortion. The builders have learned to *trust* this information, to act on it, to adjust their work, their movements, their *rituals*, in response. They do not understand it fully. They do not need to. Understanding here is not the surface virtue it pretends to be. Understanding is *accommodation*, and accommodation is *survival*, and survival is *becoming*.

And there is a work I have not named: the work of *remembering*. The calendar of knots and notches, yes. But also: the memory of the descent. Every builder remembers their own descent, the chimney of warm air, the narrowing, the first touch of mineral floor. They remember it *physically*, in the body that was changed by it. And they remember the descents of others. The ones who did not survive. The ones who went back up, or sickened and died. They remember these *as part of the place*, not as tragedy. The place *incorporates* failure as it incorporates everything else. The dead are woven. Their hair among the fibers. Their bones ground into the clay. Their *voices* among the resonances—the water-tubes carry them still, arriving from directions that don't match their source, speaking words that no living builder said, in registers that no living throat can produce.

This is the horror, if there is horror. Not the predator. Not the sealed chambers. Not the sound from below. The *continuity* of the place, its *refusal to forget*, its *incorporation of everything*, including the builders themselves, including their failures, including their *attempts to leave*. The place does not punish. The place does not forgive. The place *remembers*, and in remembering, it *keeps*, and in keeping, it *changes*, and the change is *you*, the newcomer, the builder, the hanging, the resonance, the voice that arrives from behind you and was always, already, part of the wall that breathes.

I am still not ready to name myself. To say *I am one of them* would be to settle too early. Let me sit with the *unresolved* longer.

There is a chamber I have not described. Or rather: there is a *way* of describing chambers here that I have not used. The chambers are not *spaces*. They are *moments*. The Lung is a moment of gathering, of compression, of temporary density that they endure for ritual only. The deepest chamber, where the plant fiber grows in absolute dark, is a moment of *harvest*, of feel, of *reaching into something that cannot be seen*. The sealed chambers are moments of *not-knowing*, of *waiting*, of *change*. Each chamber is a *relationship* with the place, a *way of being listened to*, and to move through the chambers is to move through *modes of attention*.

I am in one of these moments now. The moment of *writing*, or its equivalent—of *recording*, of *attempting to fix in fiber or knot or mark what cannot be fixed*. The builders do not write, not in any system that would translate. But they *knot*, and I am knotting. I am knotting this: that I do not know who I am, that the place has not yet *told* me, that I am *waiting* to be incorporated, to be *heard*, to become *one of the hangings*, *one of the resonances*, *one of the voices that arrive from directions that don't match their source*.

And I am afraid, in the way they are afraid. Not of the predator. Not of the sealed chambers. Of the *unresolved*. Of the possibility that the place is not finished with me, that the incorporation is not complete, that something is growing in the deepest chamber that will eventually require me to become something else, something that fits the pattern no one designed.

I do not speak of this fear. I speak of the damp, the water-tubes, the creatures, the calendar. I speak of the work. The work is endless. The hangings must be rewoven. The bellows must be worked. The metal must be persuaded. The sealed chambers must be maintained. The predator must be fed. The fear does not fit in the work, and so it lives in the spaces between, in the silences, in the way I listen to the sound from below and do not dig anymore, though I dug once, carefully, without finding its source.

I am here. I am *being-listened-to*, by everything, constantly, and the listening changes me, gently, constantly, until I am no longer quite myself, until I am part of the hangings, part of the resonance, part of the system that was complete before I arrived and will continue, differently, after I leave—or stay, and become one of the walls that breathe, one of the creatures that turn their pale faces toward the newcomer, one of the builders who learn, slowly, to be listened to by everything, constantly, and to listen back.

I am still becoming. I am not yet named.

Let me try, now. Not to settle, but to *test*.

I am one who *descended*, not so long ago in the terms of this place—perhaps three wet seasons, perhaps four. I remember the chimney of warm air. I remember the narrowing. I remember the first touch of mineral floor, and the *listening* that began immediately, that has not stopped since.

I am one who *refused*, once, to continue. I stayed in the upper chambers, near the chimney, where the air is warmer and the listening less intense. I survived, barely, and I was *pitied*, in the way that has no surface equivalent. I have not been *fully heard* by the place. I am still *surface*, still separate, still capable of leaving.

I descended again, through the chimney of warm air, and the second descent was *different*. The damp found me more quickly. The listening was more intense. I began, finally, to *change*.

The old fiber becoming the new, in rhythms tracked by knots and notches. In the way of this place: with skin that feels vibration, with bones that conduct, with a body that has learned to *trust* the voices that arrive from directions that don't match their source. I am learning not to turn, not to react, until I have traced the resonance through the curved surfaces and found its true origin. I am learning the discipline of *refusing to believe what seems immediate*.

I have not yet been to the deepest chamber. I have not yet fed the predator. I am here. I am *being-listened-to*, by everything, constantly, and the listening changes me, gently, constantly, until I am no longer quite myself, until I am part of the hangings, part of the resonance, part of the system that was complete before I arrived and will continue, differently, after I leave—or stay, and become one of the walls that breathe, one of the creatures that turn their pale faces toward the newcomer, one of the builders who learn, slowly, to be listened to by everything, constantly, and to listen back.

I am still becoming. I am not yet named.
