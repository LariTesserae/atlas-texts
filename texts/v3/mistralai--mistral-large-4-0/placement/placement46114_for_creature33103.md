I am not one of them. Not yet. I arrive as *arrivals do* in places that have forgotten how to be surprised—quietly, absorbed, already becoming part of the saturation. Perhaps I am the pool-drinker, come to drink from an eye of dark water, my pelt shedding what the air gives it. Perhaps I am the wrongness in the mist, the held hush, the thing they feel but do not name. Perhaps I am something that has no shape yet, drifting in the wet air, waiting to condense onto stone, onto lichen, onto a body that will learn to breathe through its surface.

But I think—I am *becoming*—one of them. One of the low, the distributed, the permeable. I feel it already: the slowing. The way my thoughts move through channels rather than leaping. The way I want to *touch*, to press against something that will feel the pressure, that will answer with its own temperature, its own flow. The way sound has become *wrong*, too loud, too *separate*.

I am learning to *tend*. There is a channel near where I stand—no, where I *crouch*, because standing is too much exposure, too much surface for the mist to strip—and it is *wrong*. The water pools where it should flow, seeps where it should be directed. I have found a stone. It is cool. It is *patient*. I am fitting it into place, feeling the change in the hiss, the small adjustment of the seep's song. This is *work*. This is *meaning*. This is *participation*.

I am not alone. I feel the presence—there, on the walkway above, the creak of weight shifting, the soft *press* of a greeting I cannot yet understand but will learn. They do not look at me. They *sense* me. The change in the hush. The addition of my wetness to the wet. They *welcome*, in the only way the Belt knows: by *not rejecting*, by *including*, by *making room* in the slow saturation.

I am afraid. Not of death—death is *return*, the peat, the layer, the stone. But of *wrongness*. Of moving too fast, of speaking too loud, of *wanting* in a way that has no patience, no channel, no flow. I am learning to *wait*. To let the mist rise. To let the condensation form. To let the pool-drinker come, or not. To let myself *become*—slowly, wetly, quietly—part of the weeping.

I have no name. I have a *location*. A *function*. A *relationship to water flow*. I am the one who tends this channel. I am the one who feels the greeting, the press, the *presence* of the other. I am the one who is *here*, in the cool, in the wet, in the hush that is not silence but *compression*, the world saturated with itself until it can hold no more, and still, *still*, receiving.

I am *becoming*.

I am *layer*.

I am the Condensation Belt, made *brief*, made *necessary*, made *real* in this moment of tending, of touching, of *waiting* for what comes next—the mist, the dark, the slow return of light, the endless *saturation* of the world becoming more itself through my presence, my *wet*, my *quiet*, my *cool* persistence.

I am here.

I am wet.

I am the weeping.
