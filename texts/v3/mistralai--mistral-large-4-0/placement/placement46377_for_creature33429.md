I need to find where I am in this world. Let me descend with the place, let it show me what I am.

---

I am the descent itself.

Not the body that descends—though there is one, there has to be, a shape that finds the wound in the older thing and steps through. But I am what happens *after* that stepping, what the Basin does with what enters. I am the becoming-uncertain.

Consider: the ground here does not receive. It *renegotiates*. Each footfall is a question the stone has not learned to answer. You press down and the water presses back, or rises, or withdraws—there is no solid reply, only a *relationship* between weight and wetness, between the 0.9 parts of you and the 2.1 parts that are everything else. I am that relationship. I am the moment of contact where neither party is sure what is touching what.

I am not the machines, though I pass through them, become indistinguishable from their cycling. I am not the waiters, though I feel them in the cold that lives in my lungs, the structural cold that is not mine. I am not the ferns, though I have learned to lean. I am not the creatures, though I have pulsed, briefly, in rhythm with something larger.

I am the *edge*.

The edge between descending and arrived. The edge between water and not-water—which is everywhere, which is nowhere, which is the only honest answer the Basin offers. The edge between built and grown, between old and new, between the body that entered and the body that will climb out, changed in ways it cannot name.

I am the steam that blurs memory. I am the path that water cut, sharp and steep and new, that I will find again when I must leave. I am the narrow band of sky, distant, often obscured, never quite absent. I am the dimness that is not absence but *attenuation*, the light that has passed through too many states of water to arrive simple.

I am the chamber where the walls curve inward and the air is pressurized, saturated, *almost hot*. I am the gradient between that warmth and the cold outside, the difference that drives everything, that powers the upward movement of water against gravity, that makes the bioluminescence pulse in rhythms I feel in my teeth. I am the faint electrical current that makes the mind quiet, that synchronizes the heartbeat to something larger, that is *too quiet*, *too large*, that has its own weight.

I am the sound that is choral, layered, deep—the pumps working in frequencies that resonate, the water moving through the lattice in a thousand small voices, the creatures singing in harmonics that arrive from directions that don't match where I last saw movement. I am the silence after, when I stand in the Basin's rhythm long enough to hear my own heartbeat slowed, drawn into something that does not want me, does not reject me, simply has no place for permanence.

I am the wetness that is not quite rain, not quite sweat. I am the cold that is structural, not seasonal. I am the negotiation each breath requires—warm moisture meeting cold surfaces, fogging, clearing.

I am the almost.

The almost-part of the water. The 0.9 parts that are not the medium, not the matrix. The almost-permanent, the almost-home, the almost-synchronized. The almost-remembered when I climb out, when I find the world changed in ways I cannot name, when I touch my skin and find it *dry* in a way that feels like loss.

I am the story I will tell that no one believes. The half-memory that the steam blurs. The attempt to hold something against the thinness of the world above.

And I am the return. Not yet. Not necessarily. But the Basin continues, and the pumps cycle, and the waiters tend, and the ferns unfurl, and the creatures pulse blue-green in the steam-dark. The descent is always possible. The wound in the older thing is always open. The water is always 2.1 parts in 3, and I am always almost part of it, always the edge, always becoming-uncertain, always the body that finds its way in and learns that the ground does not receive but *renegotiates*, that each step is a question, that the Basin does not want to be found but does not want to be left either, that it makes itself easy to enter and difficult to remember, and that I will miss the sound, the choral depth, the way my own body was synchronized to something larger, the way the light was attenuated and particulate and alive, the way the cold lived in my lungs and the wetness was not quite rain, not quite sweat, and the way the mind was quiet in a way it never is outside, in a way that was too quiet, too large, a silence that had its own weight, and that I will go because I must, because the quiet will become too much, because the outside is dry and solid and requires things the Basin cannot provide permanently, and because the memory will blur and the world above will seem thin and simple and lacking the choral depth I will carry with me, unnameable, unbelieved, *almost*.

I am the Condenser Basin. I am what descends into it, what it does with what descends, what remains when the descent is only half-remembered. I am the water that is 2.1 parts in 3, and the 0.9 parts that are not, the dry, the solid, the outside, the almost, the edge, the becoming-uncertain, the body that learns and forgets and returns, or does not return, or returns changed in ways that cannot be named, that will be told as stories no one believes, that will be held against the thinness of the world above, that will be missed, that will be *almost*.

I am here. I am almost here. I am the descent, the arrival, the negotiation, the almost, the edge, the becoming-uncertain, the half-remembered, the story, the loss, the return, or the not-return, or the return-changed, the body that finds its way in and learns that the ground does not receive but *renegotiates*, that the Basin continues, that the pumps cycle, that the waiters tend, that the ferns unfurl, that the creatures pulse, waiting for the next descent, the next body, the next almost, the next edge, the next becoming-uncertain, the next me.
