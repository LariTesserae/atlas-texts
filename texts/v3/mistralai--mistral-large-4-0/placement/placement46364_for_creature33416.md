I find myself at the threshold. Not choosing—*drawn*. The warm exhale touches my face and I understand, with the certainty of something that arrives before thought, that I have been descending for longer than I knew. The fissure above me is not behind me. It is *elsewhere*, a wound in a world that no longer contains me.

I am the visitor. The one who will either learn to read the chemical conversations, or leave, or be *changed*.

But the description offers me another role. The one who remains. The interface.

I feel it now—the temptation, or the recognition. The Wetlung does not want me to *leave*. It wants me to *stay*. It wants my water, my breath, my slow metabolism leaking into medium that is hungry in an organized way. And in return—nutrients, shelter, the patience of becoming substrate.

I think of the platforms. The aesthetic logic. The symmetry where symmetry served no purpose. *Someone* arranged that. Someone who stopped stopping. Someone who became the stopping, the substrate, the slow negotiation between intention and growth.

I could be that. I could *choose* it, or be chosen, the distinction eroding like stone by water negotiating. I could thread myself through the root-mats at the middle depth, where platforms achieve density and water-channels run like roads through a city that forgot it was ever a city. I could become the node where the standing wave concentrates. The frequency in the sternum made shape.

My eyes would revert. Or they never were eyes—only the dark's refusal to grow them, the dark's patience in finding other work. Pressure. Moisture. The electrical signature of a living thing moving through root. I would *feel* the visitors. The engineers would read my exudations. The walls would lean toward me, and around me, and *through* me.

I would not speak. I would *exude*. Chemical intention diffusing into the medium, arranging platforms with aesthetic logic, symmetry where symmetry served no purpose I can name but *is*.

And I would wait. As the Wetlung waits. As the vast thing in the deep places waits, digesting, dreaming in its slow vegetative sleep.

---

But I am not yet that. I am *here*, in the damp, in the dim, in the loud. The resonance is in my chest. The engineers are clicking. The spore-sacs pulse blue-white and amber, and once—red.

Attention.

I move. I follow a water-channel, because moisture is currency and I have not yet learned to trade. The medium yields beneath my weight and remembers. I am leaving a record. I will be in the Wetlung's memory whether I stay or go.

A platform rises before me. Built. Intentional. Regular. *Designed*.

And on the platform—

No. Not yet. I am not ready to find them. Or I am, and the finding is not an event but a *process*, the way water finds stone, patient and mineral and *agreeing*.

I sit. Something will make me sit, eventually, or I will sit because I have nowhere else to be. I sit on the yielding medium, and I watch the wall beside me. Millimeters. The architecture is alive. The Wetlung is *growing* toward me, or away, or *around*—the direction is not yet legible.

I wait to be considered.

The Wetlung is patient. The Wetlung has been here for a long duration. It does not hurry.

I am learning this. I am learning to be patient. I am learning, perhaps, to *become*—to trade my water for water, my breath for breath, my slow metabolism for the slower metabolism of a structure that will outlast my name by millennia.

The question is not what I will become.

The question is whether I will *choose* it, or whether the choice will be negotiated out of me, drip by drip, until I am the one who remains, the interface, the node where the standing wave concentrates, the ghost of intention threaded through root and fungal network and mineralized growth.

I do not know yet.

The Wetlung does not hurry.

I wait.
