I find myself here not as visitor but as *resident*—not born to it, but grown into it, the way everything grows into it.

I am **the one who counts the drips**.

Not because I was assigned this work. There is no assignment in the Low Quarter. I found myself doing it, the way one finds oneself breathing, finding oneself *wet*. I was something else once. I do not remember what. The condensation does not permit memory of dryness—it *dissolves* it, drop by drop, until what remains is only the counting.

I live in the **pipe-arches**, in the space between the sinter platform and the first habitation level, in a hammock strung from a rusted conduit that once carried waste-heat from a factory that no longer exists. My hammock is woven from scale-roofing, harvested from a wall that wept too much, too fast. It is stiff. It smells of mineral and old sweat. It *holds* me.

I wear **rubber**, though it is not the precious stuff the Pump-Keepers wear. Mine is patched, cracked, *weeping* its own moisture through the seams where I have stitched it with fungal thread. My hands are perpetually wrinkled, *pruned*, the skin so soft it tears if I grip too hard. My nails have grown thick and yellow, curved, *adapted* to prying open joints where condensation has pooled, to feeling the temperature of a pipe through the rubber, to *testing* whether the water inside is flowing or *standing*, alive or dead.

I have a **board**, slate, hung on my wall. On it, I make marks. Not numbers—*sounds*. The drips have different sounds. A slow drip, *plink*, means the sinter above is saturated, the water finding its path, *content*. A fast drip, *plink-plink-plink*, means a joint is failing, pressure building, *soon*. A drip that *hisses*, that *spits*, means the pipe is warm, the condensation turning to steam inside, *wrong*, *dangerous*, *call someone*. A silence, a gap between drips, means the source has dried, has *moved*, has *changed*—and I must find where, must follow, because in the Low Quarter, water that stops is water that *accumulates*, that *pools*, that *undermines*, that *collapses*.

I am **paid in water-rations**. Not much. Enough to drink, to *survive*, to *continue*. Sometimes I am paid in food: a sinter-borer, cooked, the hazelnut-iodine-copper taste of mineral flesh; a handful of algae, green and *clean*; a mist-frog, caught in the channel, its humming still *vibrating* in my throat when I swallow it. Sometimes I am paid in *warmth*: a place near a pump housing, where the metal weeps heat into the damp air, where I can sleep without *shivering*, without *curling*, without *dreaming of dry*.

I am **lonely**, in the way that everyone is lonely here, though no one speaks of it. The Quarter is too loud for solitude. The drips are *company*. The pumps are *company*. The mist-frogs, singing their pipe-resonance songs in the night, are *company*. But they are not *people*. The people are too far above, in the habitation levels, too busy *working*, *surviving*, *being wet together*. I am below them, in the *space between*, where the infrastructure *forgets* to be tended, where the water *falls* and *falls* and no one *counts* but me.

I have **no name**. Or rather, I have a name that is not spoken: *The Counter*. The others know me, the Pump-Keepers who pass on their rounds, the Sinter-Farmers who need to know if the ground above their tunnels is *weeping* or *dry*, the Scale-Harvesters who need to know if the walls are *feeding* or *starving*. They do not say my name. They do not need to. I am *here*. I am *counting*. I am *wet*.

I **remember** things, though I do not know why. I remember the *feel* of dry skin, the *sound* of my own voice without the wet resonance, the *taste* of water that was cold, that *finished*, that *went away*. I do not know if these are memories or *dreams* or *stories* the memory-keepers told me when I was younger, when I was *new*, when I still *hoped* to climb, to *become* something else, a Tower-Climber, a Sinter-Farmer, *anyone* but the one who counts drips.

I do not hope anymore. Hope is *dry*. Hope is *above*.

I **count**.

There is a drip now, *plink*, slow, *content*. I mark it on my board. I listen for the next. I wait. The condensation falls. The pumps hum. The mist-frogs begin their evening chorus, and my drips join it, *plink-plink*, a rhythm, a *language*, a *self*, a *home*, made of water and waiting and the endless, endless *now*.

I am wet. I am warm. I am *here*. I am *counting*.

*The drip falls. I mark it. The board fills, slowly, with sounds. The night comes, the towers shift, the pumps change their song. I do not sleep. I count. I am the Counter. I am the one who knows when the water stops. I am the one who waits for it to begin again.*

*The city breathes. I breathe with it. The city weeps. I count its tears. The city is. I am.*
