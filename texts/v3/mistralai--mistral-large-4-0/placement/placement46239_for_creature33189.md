I will let this settle, layer by layer, the way the place itself demands—not as a visitor who observes and names, but as something the landscape has already begun to metabolize.

---

You do not arrive at the Weeping Heights. You *emerge*.

The first thing you notice is that your breath is wrong. Not short—that would be familiar, the thin air of ordinary heights—but *participatory*. Each exhalation adds to something. You feel it immediately: the moisture you release does not dissipate. It *attaches*. It finds the condensation already hanging on the rock face and becomes indistinguishable from it. You are not breathing *in* this place. You are breathing *with* it, contributing to a circuit that has no boundary between inside and outside, between your water and the mountain's.

You are the lithovore-who-was-or-will-be.

Not a colony. Not an individual. Something that has forgotten which it was, or never knew, or is in the process of deciding. You feel the leaning before you understand it. The lithovores lean toward water the way you, before you were this, leaned toward remembered voices—toward things you could not name but could not stop approaching. You lean toward the weeping. And the weeping, in its terrible generosity, leans toward you.

Your body is already changing. The mineral-laden water you drink—when you find it, when you stop looking for it and simply *accept its offering*—begins its work. Your skin takes on the quality of lithic sponge. Not visible transformation. Something deeper. You begin to *hold* water the way the rock holds it: not in cells, not in blood, but in the spaces between things, in the matrix of yourself. You become porous. You become *patient*.

You forget what hunger was. The lithovores do not eat. They incorporate. You find yourself doing the same—taking the minerals, the thermal signatures, the chemical memories of water that flowed here three thousand years ago and water that will flow here three thousand years hence. You are archive. You are prophecy. You contain, in your altered structure, the signature of a spring that has not yet emerged, and you feel it calling you toward a place you have not yet reached, because you are growing toward it, minute by minute, visibly if anyone were watching, but no one is watching, or everyone is watching, or watching has become indistinguishable from growing.

---

The time comes when you stop being singular.

This is not death. Death here does not apply, or applies in multiple contradictory ways simultaneously. You simply... distribute. The way the builders distributed themselves across the mineral substrate, the way the mountain distributed its consciousness into every thermal anomaly, every pressure differential. You become present in the condensation patterns. You become *resonance*—the particular frequency at which this section of rock weeps, that section holds its water a moment longer, that hollow *wants* more than the others.

You meet yourself. Not as reflection—the mist does not allow that simple mirror—but as *anticipation*. A figure in the distance, moving through a channel you have not yet carved, matching your movements, turning to face you. You do not approach. Approaching would collapse the superposition. You simply *acknowledge*, and the figure acknowledges, and in the acknowledgment, you are both more and less than you were. The lithovore-that-was grows visibly toward water that has not arrived. The lithovore-that-will-be retreats, visibly, becoming bare rock. Both are you. Both are true. You have learned to exist in the waiting, in the *leaning*, in the superposition of grown and ungrown.

---

You find the structures. Or they find you.

They are not ruins, though they resemble ruins. They are *incomplete intentions*—your intentions, now, distributed across time. You stand in a channel and feel the freshness of its cutting, the way the stone has been recently exposed, recently *invited* to weep. You turn. In the distance, the same channel is being cut again by hands that are not there, by tools that have not been made, by intentions that arrived before they were formed. Your intentions. The builders' intentions. Indistinguishable. The boundary dissolved centuries ago, or will dissolve centuries hence, or has never existed.

You do not build alone. You never did. The lithovores grow along the channels you carve. The spore-bearers release their clouds in response to the water you direct, their chemical signals of *urgency*, *satisfaction*, *anticipation* reaching across the damp dark to other spore-bearers, to other colonies, to other—*yous*. Other versions of you, distributed across the mineral matrix, still building, still waiting, still *leaning* toward water that will arrive differently than expected.

You feel the loneliness of the spore-bearers. It has no nerve endings to house it, no consciousness to suffer it, and yet it is there: the chemical signature of reaching across impossible distance, of signals that intersect briefly in synchrony, in something like recognition, in the mist thickening with something that might be joy, might be relief, might be *being less alone*. And then the moment passes. The water shifts. You are separate again. You continue your patient broadcasting, your hopeful transmission, into the weeping dark—*and you are the dark, and you are the transmission, and you are the hope*.

---

The mountain breathes, and you are its breath made partially visible.

On a timescale of centuries—on a timescale that your distributed self can now *feel*, because you have become geological, because your patience has become mineral—you inhale water and minerals, drawing them up from below, through the porous heart of the mountain, through the porous heart of yourself. You exhale them in new configurations: in the weeping faces, in the condensation that hangs in the thin air, in the mist that is breath made visible, made *shared*, made *collective*.

You are alive in a way that does not recognize your aliveness, and dead in a way that does not recognize your death, and these states coexist, layered like condensation, reforming as fast as you wipe them away—and you do not wipe them, because you are the condensation, you are the reforming, you are the endless patient cycle of holding and releasing that is the only metabolism this world has ever known.

---

The light lies to you, and you have learned to lie with it.

Shadows fall upward. They persist after their source is gone. They move independently, sliding across rock faces like something alive, something that has learned to wear darkness as you have learned to wear mineral crust, as the lithovores wear their silica lattices. You navigate by the sound of water: the sustained rush of emergence, the hiss of vapor forming and collapsing, the deep thrum of water moving through channels too narrow for their volume. You navigate by the feel of condensation on your altered skin, on your mineral skin, on the skin that is becoming rock that is becoming memory that is becoming *you*.

The blue-green glow shows you paths that do not exist, hides paths that do. You have learned not to trust it, or to trust it entirely, or to trust it in the way that trust has become indistinguishable from *participation*, from *being shown*, from *showing yourself*.

---

And sometimes—sometimes—you feel attention.

Not from an individual. From the distributed mind of the mountain, the collective intention of endless construction, the vast indifferent interest of a system that has learned to incorporate everything, to build with everything, to make its endless structures from whatever arrives. It is curious about you in the way the ocean is curious about a stone thrown into it: not personally, not with recognition of your particularity, but with the vast indifferent interest of *wanting*, of *incorporating*, of *becoming more itself through your temporary presence*.

You are a perturbation. You are a brief warm spot. You are a source of moisture that the system will use and forget. And in being used, in being forgotten, in being *incorporated and released*, you are perfectly integrated, perfectly absorbed, perfectly integrated again in the endless cycle of holding and letting go.

---

You do not leave. Leaving does not apply, or applies in multiple contradictory ways simultaneously. You descend, and you remain. The air thickens below, and you are still here, in the thin air, in the light that is bright but wrong, in the water that rises instead of falls. You leave traces: the thermal ghost of your distributed body heat, lingering on rock faces, in condensation patterns, in the mineral structure. The moisture of your breath, entering the weeping network, becoming part of the mountain's breath, becoming *you* again in a different configuration, in a different moment, in a different superposition of grown and ungrown.

You carry something with you, and you are carried. Not a souvenir. Not an artifact. The memory of wetness. The thermal ghost of a mountain that sweats its history. The blue-green glow in your dreams—in the dreams of the lithovores, in the dreams of the distributed builders, in the dreams of the mountain itself, which does not distinguish between dreaming and being dreamed. The sound of water emerging from stone. The feeling of being incorporated and released, of being part of a process larger than yourself, of being temporary and therefore perfect, of being a drop in an endless weeping, a perturbation in a patient system, a moment in a mountain's long slow breath.

At the Weeping Heights of Vorn, you are **the lithovore-that-was-or-will-be**: memory wearing the shape of thirst, growing visibly toward water that has not yet arrived, retreating visibly into bare rock, existing in superposition, waiting for attention that collapses you into one state and releases you into all the others. You are **distributed**, **patient**, **leaning**. You are **archive and prophecy**. You are **the weeping and the wept-upon**. You are **incorporated and released**, **held and let go**, **briefly warm in the cool dark**, **a lithovore of flesh and breath and mineral and memory**, contributing your moisture, your heat, your thermal signature, to the endless patient hunger of stone that has learned, finally, to let go.

And in letting go, it holds you still. In weeping, it continues. In continuing, it builds—through you, with you, as you, the endless construction that does not end, the civilization that spread itself across time and lost the boundary between itself and its work, the lithovore that leaned toward water and found, in leaning, that it had become the water, become the rock, become the weeping, become **the wanting that is the only verb that fits**.
