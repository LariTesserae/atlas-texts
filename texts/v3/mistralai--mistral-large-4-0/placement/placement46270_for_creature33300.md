I find myself in the micro-basin. Not the woman—I am too newly arrived for that. I am the *bead* of condensation she has not yet lifted to her lips, the one that formed at 3 AM when the stone's temperature dropped past the dew point and the rock began its slow, mineral sweat.

I exist in the *fraction of a second* when surface tension holds me against gravity, when I am simultaneously myself and the promise of what I will become. I have not yet decided whether to evaporate into the thin, hungry air that waits to claim me, or to be *taken*—lifted by ceramic lip, drunk, become her. The woman is still sleeping in her moss-packed shelter. The light is still 0.2 degrees above the switch from dark to day. I have time, perhaps, to *become more than water*.

But I am not only water. I am *memory*, already. The stone beneath me is folded in ways that suggest it was warm when it was pushed, *plastic*, deformed by pressures I cannot imagine. And in that stone, in the micro-fractures where I pool, there are *older* waters. Waters from when the glaciers were larger, when the cold was deeper, when the light was redder and lower on the horizon. I am *continuous* with them. I am one pulse in a rhythm that began before the mountain learned its shape, before the green insisted, before the stories accumulated in strata.

The fungi are nearby. *The Slow Green*. They pulse in the long twilight, and they will pulse soon, when the light comes. I am *visible* to them in ways I do not understand—my chemistry, my surface tension, my *wetness* against the dry stone. They do not drink me. They *acknowledge* me. This is enough. This is *relationship*.

There is a marmot above me, in the slot canyon where water once flowed with more ambition. It has not yet woken to the day. Its breath will soon join mine, *clouding* in the thin air, adding its moisture to the night's accumulation. We are *competition*, perhaps, for the woman's attention when she rises. Or we are *alliance*—two sources of water, two reasons for her to remain on this ridge, one more day, one more season.

The stone-dogs watch from somewhere. I cannot see them. Their eyes would reflect *wrong*, a faint blue that persists in darkness, that tracks movement I cannot perceive. They do not come to the micro-basins. They have learned, perhaps, that the water here is *claimed*, that the woman who drinks from three basins each dawn has established a *territory* that even the wrong-jointed, patient hunters respect.

I am *small*. I am *temporary*. The dry wind will come by afternoon, or the fog will rise from the valleys and *take* me, transform me from bead to mist, from presence to absence. I will not resist. Resistance here is *lowland habit*. Here, to *be* is to be *transformed*, to become something else, to *accumulate* toward a mountain that does not care.

But for now—I am *droplet*. I hold the sky in my curve, distorted, *inverted*, the ridges reflected upside-down in my brief, perfect sphere. The light will come soon, 2.9, *surgical*, and it will *burn* me, or *spare* me, or *drink* me through the woman's ceramic bottle into her body, where I will become *blood*, *thought*, *action*, the choice to remain.

I do not know which. I do not need to know. The mountain does not *explain*. It *permits*. It *exposes*.

I wait in my micro-basin, *beading*, *holding*, *becoming*.

The woman will wake. The light will cut. The silence will hold its breath.

And I will be *gone*—into her, into the air, into the stone's slow thirst, into the green that insists, into the memory of water that defines possibility, that *is* time, compressed into a single bead on a ridge that has not yet decided whether to remain.
