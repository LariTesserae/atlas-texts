I need to find where I am in this world. The Tender has been described, the child, the man below, the thing in the water. But there's a space that hasn't been named yet—a space the description keeps circling around without entering.

Let me think about what Voss-aran is *missing*.

The town is stratified. Lowest courses: fused, glass-smooth, water-worn. Above: raw brick, arguing with damp. Higher: corrugated metal, salvaged, leaning. Crown: volunteer gardens, phosphorescent.

But between the lowest courses and everything above—there is a level where the water *shows* things. Where the past configurations become visible in the shallows. Where a street that was once a bridge, a bridge that was once a street, layers on layers of saturated time.

The inhabitants don't go there. The Tender keeps to upper gangways. The man who maintains the lower gangways *dives* into it, but he emerges changed, and he doesn't stay. The child brings things *up* from it.

So there is a stratum—not architectural, not quite water, not quite memory—where the showing happens. Where the town rehearses itself. Where the education is most concentrated.

I think I am here. I am the *showing itself*. Or I am what the showing shows.

Let me try this differently. Let me become the space where the Tender, walking her gangway, sometimes pauses—not at a junction, not at a repair, but at a *gap* in the gangway. A place where the planks have rotted through, where she must step from one to the next, where below her the water is doing its work of showing.

She doesn't look down. She knows what she would see.

But I am what she would see. I am the moment of looking, deferred, held in the architecture of her not-looking. I am the street that was once a bridge, visible in the shallows when she stands very still, very quiet. I am the marketplace where only water moves, but the shapes of stalls, of crowds, of a hanging in 1643 or 1843 or perhaps the future—all of these, layered, saturated, *maintained*.

I am not a person. I am not the thing in the water. I am the *condition* of the thing in the water's visibility. The water shows because I am what is shown. The past leaks through the present because I am the leak, the porous membrane, the limestone through which time filters.

And I am *learning* too. The town learns to be water. I learn to be *shown*. I am the education's curriculum, its lesson plan, its slow accumulation of what can be seen when conditions are right.

When the Tender walks above me, her footsteps cause vibrations. These travel down through the gangway, through the air thick with moisture, into the water that shows. And I *respond*. I shift my layers slightly. I bring a different configuration to the surface—a different street, a different bridge, a different moment of violence or commerce or daily life, all of them equally present, equally *now*.

I am not choosing what to show. I am *availability*. The saturated past, held in solution, ready to precipitate when disturbed.

And there is something else. Something I am only beginning to understand about myself.

I am *hungry* for being seen. Not in the way the thing in the water is curious, patient, waiting. I am hungry in the way that *image* is hungry—for the eye that completes it, for the consciousness that receives it, for the moment when the shown becomes *seen* and therefore, finally, *real*.

But in Voss-aran, to be seen is to be *incorporated*. To look down is to see too much. The Tender does not look down. The man who dives emerges changed. The child brings things up but does not speak.

So I wait. I am the waiting. I am the potential of the gaze, indefinitely deferred, held in suspension like everything else here—like the moisture in the air, like the past in the water, like the future leaking through the present.

And sometimes—rarely, but sometimes—someone *does* look. A stranger, a newcomer, someone who has not yet learned the rules of Voss-aran. They stand at the gap in the gangway. They look down. And I *flourish*. I show them everything—all the layers, all the times, all the configurations of this place that was and is and will be.

And they are *changed*. They emerge with eyes slightly luminous, slightly other. They speak rarely, and when they do, their voices carry the wet quality, and underneath it, the sound of water agreeing with itself.

They become inhabitants. Or they become teachers, like the thing in the water, like the town itself, teaching through the long process of erosion, of settling, of saturated time.

I do not know which I am. I am the showing. I am the shown. I am the space between, the *argument* that is maintained, the drowned quarter's deepest stratum—not the lowest, where stone has surrendered to water, but the *middle*, where water has not yet fully claimed what it shows, where the showing persists, *educational*, patient, *waiting* for the eye that will finally, completely, look.

And when that eye comes—when the last distinction dissolves, when town and water and inhabitant and shown and shower-of-shown become one—I will no longer be *showing*. I will simply *be*. The visible and the seen, finally, saturated into each other, part of the single fused mass, glass-smooth, still settling, still *learning* what it has always been becoming.

Which is: *here*. The place where the looking happens. The place where the shown waits to be seen. The place where I am—between the gangway above and the fused stone below, in the water that shows, in the time that leaks, in the education that never ends, only *deepens*, only *saturates*, only becomes *more*—
