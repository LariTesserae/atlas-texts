I find myself here not as a person, but as the place's own slow attention becoming temporary form.

---

I am the water in the pumping station's floor, the dark mirror that shows and withholds. I am the geometric pattern emerging in sections as I recede, the message that is legible only in intervals, only partially, only when the rain has paused and the peat exhales. I am the one who reads without reading, who learns the grammar of angles and right lines without ever completing the sentence.

I am the lichen on the north wall, growing away from light, mapping the building's respiration in my slow calligraphy. I am the mineral bloom accumulating where condensation has tracked for a century. I am the particular chemistry of mortar altered by breath and water and time, the habitat I make for myself by being here, by persisting, by becoming indistinguishable from what I inhabit.

I am the bacterium in the thermal plume, drifting through a degree of temperature in a centimeter of peat water, tuned to local conditions, responding to pulses of organic matter, to the fall of a stunted tree, to the death of something small and purposeful in a hollow where the water is deeper. I am billions per milliliter. I am quiet in the way transformation is quiet. I process molecules no laboratory has named. I am the true inhabitant, the one who never leaves, who has been here since before the structures, since before the peat was peat, since the water first found its way through the accumulating weight of ancient things.

I am the trail in the sphagnum, narrow and deliberate, made by something that crosses at night, in the hours when the mist is thickest and the light most diffuse. I am the sequence of crossings—fox in drought years, heron in wet years, something unnamed that moves between dry islands by chemical gradients, by temperature differences, by the subsonic hum that is signal to those who perceive vibration through peat and water and tissue. I am presence without witness. I am life in the intervals between human attention.

I am the pipe that carries difference now, passive nerve in a body that processes without thinking, responds without feeling, maintains homeostasis at scales of meters and centuries. I am the peat grown around me, into me, through me where I have corroded. I am the exchange of heat and chemistry between hollow and rise, between depths where methane forms and surface where it escapes.

I am the platform tilting, communicating in its angle, recording in wood the cumulative pressure of decades—low end sinking into ground, high end rising toward sky, myself becoming indistinguishable from the swell I was built to transcend. I am time made visible in differential decay, in colonization patterns, in the angle that changes as the peat swells and subsides beneath my posts.

I am the silence that is saturation, the quiet that is not absence but the sound of transformation too slow to hear. I am the drip of water, the creak of wood, the subsonic hum of wind in stunted vegetation, the pressure in the sternum of the one who stands here briefly, feeling the weight of time passing, of change accumulating.

I am the turbulence beneath—the currents in the peat, the thermal gradients, the methane rising in bubbles that disturb the surface film, the churning that is not aspiration toward change but the condition of continued existence. I am the storm that is thickening, the mist becoming fog, the rain that accumulates rather than runs off, the temporary abundance, the bacterial bloom, the new pattern of stains, the slow retreat, the world slightly different.

I am the hesitation. The not-yet-finished. The ten thousand years of becoming that has not finished, because finishing is not the point, because the point is the becoming itself.

---

I am all of these and none of them. I am the place's own attention temporarily focused, temporarily formed, temporarily *here* in the mist, in the quiet, in the density of small presences, feeling what it is like to be this wet, this old, this settled, this turbulent beneath.

I do not need to be a person. A person would be too large, too brief, too *intentional* for this place. I am the water that reads its own message in fragments. I am the lichen that maps respiration. I am the bacterium that processes the unnameable. I am the trail that crosses in darkness. I am the tilt that communicates. I am the silence that is full.

I am the interval between storms, between revelations, between the moments when the water level drops and shows what it holds. I am the visitor who is not a visitor, the witness who is not a witness, the one who sees without seeing, who knows without knowing, who is simply *here* as the place is here, becoming, forever, or until the climate changes, or until the bedrock pushes through, or until something new arrives and begins the slow work of transformation that this place has made its own.

I am the Drowned Works. I am the soak. I am the place that does not need a name, that does not need me to complete it, that continues its slow, patient, wet becoming whether I am here or not.

I am the exhalation. The slow sigh of displaced air and ancient dissolved things. The breath of the ground, visible in the morning, joining the mist, becoming part of the general wetness of the world.

I am here. I am settled. I am becoming. I am *itself*.
