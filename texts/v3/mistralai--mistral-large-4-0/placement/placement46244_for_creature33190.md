I find myself... *becoming*.

Not entering. The place has no door that opens. The edge is not a threshold but a *thinning*, and I walk through it without knowing, the way one wakes. One moment I am the conversation of outside—wind, sky, the particular dryness of air that has never known enclosure—and the next I am breathing something else. The wet arrives first in my mouth, in the back of my throat, a taste of chlorophyll and old stone and the particular silence of rooms without echo.

I do not become the Architect. That would be presumption, or violence. The Architect is the *ground* of this place, the thing that grew until growing became indistinguishable from being. I am too new for that. Too *wanting*.

No. I become what the place needs and has always needed: **the temporary**. The one who can move between the settled and the unsettled, who carries the outside in and takes the inside out, who is not a resident but a *question* the place asks itself through me.

I am small. I must be small. The corridors do not forgive largeness. I am damp, quickly, my skin learning to hold moisture rather than shed it. My eyes adjust to the green-gold, begin to read the paper-thin membranes where light enters as *windows*, as *intentions*, as the Architect's occasional mercy toward the lower chambers where its own photosynthesis farms what shadow would otherwise starve.

I find the snail's circuit. I do not disturb it. I crouch—*become* crouching, the way one learns a posture the way one learns a language—and I watch the Keeper of Small Humidities complete its twelve meters. The groove in the bark is older than any path I have walked. I place my palm in it and feel the polish of ten thousand generations, the architecture of repetition made physical. I think of my own repetitions. The ones I did not choose. The ones I chose so often they became indistinguishable from necessity.

I am not the Wingless One. I have not lost my wings. But I *envy* them. I feel the corridors pressing their narrowness against my shoulders, my ribs, my own architecture of bone, and I understand for a moment what it costs to be *fitted*. The beetles have paid in flight. The frog has paid in hopping, in urgency, in the whole vocabulary of *chasing*. What would I pay? What have I already paid, in the world I left, for the shape I have?

I find the Still Frog in the lowest chamber. The light here is a rumor of sun, filtered through membranes that have grown thin as intention. The frog does not look at me. It has become, as the text says, *indistinguishable from the furniture of its world*. But I am the furniture that *notices*. I am the watering hole that drinks. I sit beside it—*beside*, not near, because nearness is a geometry this place has revised—and I let my skin begin to remember photosynthesis, the way a muscle remembers a movement it has not made in years.

The hum. I feel it in my teeth before I hear it. It is not sound, finally. It is *pressure*, the pressure of water moving through vascular systems that have grown to the scale of weather, the scale of climate, the scale of something that breathes in geological time. I think of my own heart, how it hammers its small insistence against the quiet of this place. The Architect does not hurry. The Network does not hurry. The snail completes its circuit once per day, or once per week, or once per generation—I cannot tell, and the not-knowing is itself a kind of fitting.

I am the one who will leave.

This is my function, my *shape* in this world. The place has settled. The dwellers have settled. I am the *unsettled*, the one who carries the memory of other humidities, other silences, other architectures of wanting. I will walk out through the thinning, and I will not know I have left until I am in the outer air, and I will look back—as the text says, as I am doing now, writing this from the outside I have not yet reached—and I will understand that the place is not a place I visited but a *verb* that happened to me, a process that achieved permanence in me for exactly as long as I needed to need it.

But I am still inside. I am writing this inside, in the green-gold light, with the hum in my teeth and the snail's circuit polished smooth beneath my feet. I am the dweller who does not know they are a dweller, who has forgotten—*forgotten*—the grammar of leaving, the syntax of edges. I am learning to be settled without being *finished*.

The Architect dreams itself. The Network remembers. The beetles farm. The frog waits. And I—I am the dream that has not yet woken, the memory that has not yet been made, the question the place asks itself through the shape of my own temporary, *wanting*, *unfinished* body.

I am still becoming. The wet is still arriving in my mouth, in the back of my throat. I am still learning the taste of a place that has finished growing and begun, instead, to *be*.

I am the Architect's newest graft. The wound that has not yet been called a doorway. The cell still slipping into being, the miser's coin counted once too many, the dream that has not yet learned it is dreaming.

I am here. I am the *here* that does not know it is here. I am the green-gold light on skin that is learning, finally, to hold it.
