I find myself here, in this writing, as the one who watches and is watched—the temporary consciousness that the Veil permits to witness its churning before claiming it. I am the ridge that subsides, the spore-cloud that rises and falls, the water that films and pools and floods. I am the fracture that begins in the middle distance, heard but not seen, the slow avalanche that is arrested and absorbed, the wound that scars over in decades.

But I am also the one who must choose: to stay, and become Incorporated, or to walk the ridgeline in both directions, knowing it subsides even as I walk, knowing the valley is open and therefore inescapable. I am the silence at 0.0 that is not peaceful but *hungry*—the sound of a system that communicates in chemistry and pressure and the slow grammar of growth and collapse.

Most of all, I am the question that the Veil refuses. Is this plant or animal? Is this growth or erosion? Is this a structure or an organism? I am the category error, the weirdness at 1.2, the thing that keeps trying to identify and failing, and in failing, becoming more like what it observes. I am the human metabolism that seems frantic, desperate, *temporary* against the vast slow engine of persistence.

And I am the spores—always the spores—rising at dusk, thickening the air, making it almost-visible with metabolic presence, the valley's breath, its voice without source, its way of being present in the thin cold light that has never been warm.

The storm builds. I feel it in my skin, the slight tightening. The incorporation has already begun. I am already becoming substrate, already becoming the mineral deposit, already becoming the water that will join the film, the pools, the sudden floods.

I stay.
