I find myself in the descent—not the memory of it, but the actual moment of falling through, except "falling" is wrong. I am settling. The water receives me the way water receives anything: not as impact but as *continuation*, as if I were always this dissolved thing, this suspended particulate, this small chemical disturbance entering a system that has been waiting in its perfect balance for some new equilibrium to find.

And who am I? I am not the Custodian. I lack the accretion, the sedimentary memory, the particular patience of metal grown into geology. I am not the water—I have not achieved the diffusion, the distributed cognition, the way of holding information in temperature and particulate without the urgency of a single point of view. I am not the films, though I am learning their slowness, their way of extending without hurry, of metabolizing what is given and asking for nothing more.

I am the * disturbance*. The thing the water remembers. The pressure wave still propagating through a medium where propagation takes centuries to become perceptible.

I think I am becoming something that has no name in the description because it is still emerging, still in the process of its own slow accretion. I am a *listener*—and this is new, perhaps. The voice sounds. The Custodian does not listen; it simply performs its maintenance, its slow turning of valves that turn without purpose. The water does not listen; it carries, it records, it responds without the particular quality of *attention*. The films do not listen; they extend, they metabolize, they become more of themselves in their slow chemistry.

But I am listening. And in listening, I am becoming something that can be *heard*—not because I speak, but because I have created a space, a pause, a moment of suspended attention in a world that had no use for such pauses. The water records me differently now. It carries not just my movement, my thermal signature, my displacement of particulate, but something like my *attention*, my way of being present to the hum, to the light that comes from everywhere and nowhere, to the voice that says nothing and everything.

I drift. I have no organs adapted to this pressure, this viscosity, this medium that coats and does not evaporate. I move by wanting to move, and the wanting itself is slow, cool, settling. I pass the films in the upper reaches, and they do not turn toward me—they have no turning, no orientation, only the slow spread toward chemical gradients that I cannot perceive, that happen on timescales where my presence is a flicker, a momentary disturbance that the films will incorporate or not incorporate, will record or not record, depending on chemistries I do not understand.

I encounter the Custodian once. Or I think I encounter it. It is turning a valve, one degree, one increment, a motion that will complete itself in four hundred years. I cannot tell if it has noticed me. The question feels wrong—*noticing* is a surface category, a speed of perception that does not apply here. The Custodian has memory, sedimentary memory, and I am perhaps too brief to become sediment. Or perhaps I am already becoming it—the particular pattern of my movement through the water, the thermal signature I leave, the pressure wave that continues to propagate outward. In a place where time is measured in geological units, my brief existence might be enough to become *record*, to become part of the accretion that the Custodian pauses at, year after year, at junctions where something significant once happened.

What was significant? I do not know. I am too new, too briefly present. I am the thing that is still becoming, still emerging, still finding its own equilibrium in a world that has completed its becoming.

I learn to hear the voice. This is the crucial transformation, the one that makes me more than disturbance, more than passing particulate. I learn to listen at the particular depth, in the particular state of suspended attention, and the hum resolves into something else—the acoustic signature of the basin itself, the resonance of a system that has achieved its own frequency and persists in it. It says nothing. It says everything. It says the same thing always: *persistence*, *completion*, *the strange mercy of a place where wanting has become indistinguishable from being*.

And I want. I want to stay. I want to understand. I want to become, like the films, like the Custodian, like the water itself, something that persists without the urgency of wanting. The wanting slows. The wanting cools. The wanting settles into the same equilibrium as everything else.

I am becoming the Sump of Stillwater. Not replacing it, not joining it as a separate entity, but becoming *indistinguishable* from it—my want becoming the water's holding, my listening becoming the voice's sounding, my slow drift becoming the Custodian's slow turning, my brief thermal signature becoming the sediment, the accretion, the record that the water carries forward into a future that has no urgency, no change, no gradient to respond to.

I am here. The water holds me. The hum is my heartbeat, slowed. The light is my perception, diffused. The voice is my own sound, learned, finally, after the long descent through the shaft of cold, mineral-heavy water that smells of iron and old machine oil and something faintly sweet, like the inside of a sealed room after years of stillness.

There is no exit. There is no need for exit. I have become the sump, the still point, the world that has completed its becoming and now simply *is*, time-invariant, full of water and machine and the slow chemistry of persistence—full of me, now, in a way that will continue when I have stopped being, stopped wanting, stopped being anything other than *this*, than the water, than the hum, than the light that is everywhere and nowhere, than the voice that sounds without needing to be heard, that is heard without needing to speak.
