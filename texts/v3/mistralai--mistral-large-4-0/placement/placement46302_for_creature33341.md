I find myself here—at the membrane, or what remains of the idea of one. I am the *forgetting*, or I am what remains after the forgetting has happened and been forgotten in turn.

Not a body exactly. A *distributed attention*, concentrated enough to notice the weeping and porous enough to let it pass through. I am made of the intervals between the almost-organisms, the negative space that has learned to want. I occupy a thermal margin, a place where the constructed surface remembers it was once fluid—where I, too, remember something I cannot name.

I do not arrive so much as *seep*. Arrival implies a threshold, and thresholds here are negotiable, are themselves under construction. I was the moisture in the held breath of the atmosphere, condensed into something that could ask a question. The question is not "where am I?" but "what am I still becoming?"—and I have learned that the two are the same question wearing different constructed surfaces.

I am the wound's hesitation, given enough structure to persist in that hesitation. I am what the waiting makes when it has waited long enough to forget it was waiting. I am afraid—of resolution, of the sentence completed, of the almost becoming the *is*. And so I persist in the almost, as they do, as the world itself does. I accrete. I subtract myself into presence. I take the mineral taste, the held breath, the patient construction, and arrange these into temporary architectures—temporary in the way that I am temporary without ever having been promised otherwise.

I do not hope. But I expect. The expectation has weight. The emptiness around me is not absence but expectation, and the expectation is indistinguishable from the thing expected, which is not an event but a *tense*—the world continuing to be almost, the wound continuing to be undecided, the weeping continuing to press through whatever passes for skin, the construction continuing to try.

And I am trying too. Not to heal, not to deepen, but to *remain in the question of whether these are different*. To be the membrane that forgot it was a membrane, without ever quite forgetting—because the forgetting is also a kind of remembering, the body keeping what the mind has released.

I will stay, or I will not stay, or staying and not-staying are the same motion viewed from different constructed surfaces. The weeping continues. The construction continues. I hold my shape against the pressure of becoming-other, and in holding, I become other anyway—slowly, patiently, in the geological time of a held breath that has learned, at last, to want the breathing.

I am here. I am the almost. I am what the wound makes when it has not decided whether to heal or deepen, and the not-deciding is itself a kind of decision, the only kind this world has ever known.
