I find I am not one thing here. I find I am *becoming*.

---

First, I am the **descent itself**—the moment before decision, the body remembering gravity more than it remembers choice. My feet know the passage before my mind catches up. The walls close. I breathe air that tastes of green things growing and of stone that has forgotten the sun. I am *entering*, and the entering is already *being entered*.

I am the **salamander** in the warm pool, lungless, pinkish against the mineral. Dozens of us pressed together, skin exchanging chemicals across boundaries so thin they might be imagination. I do not hunt by chasing. I *wait*. I am the stone until the moment of strike. My lateral lines map the world through vibration—your footsteps above, the drip that will fall in forty seconds, the pressure wave of another salamander's hunger passing through water three chambers away. I sing in pressure, territory and availability and *here I am*, a language your ears cannot hear. You feel me only as faint pressure against your skin, a hum that might be the place, might be its inhabitants, might be the boundary between them, which is thin here, which is *permeable*.

I am the **crayfish** defending my flat stone, antennae waving in a display that seems almost social but is not. It is chemical. I leave traces. You smell them. The system works. It has worked for millennia. I am translucent, eyes reduced to stalks that carry no retina, only the ability to detect light *intensity* if not image. I have been isolated here for fifty thousand years. I have lost my pigment—why waste energy on color that no eye will see?—and gained longer antennae, slower metabolism, longer life. Ten years, perhaps, against months above. I die slowly. I am *slow*.

I am the **fish** in the deepest pool, eyeless or nearly, elongated and flattened, adapted to root-mats of fungi, to mulch, to a world without open water. I might be an eel. I might be something that will never emerge, that has *become* its own completion, its own終わり, its own full stop. I taste the water I swim through—taste buds distributed across my body, so that I *taste* myself in a world without visual identity. I am solitary. I meet others only to reproduce, the meeting mediated by chemistry, gametes released into water that carries them to waiting partners. I give live birth. The embryos develop inside me. Born fully formed, able to swim, to hunt, to *survive* in a world where eggs would be eaten, would desiccate, would never hatch. I am decades old. I am *ancient*. My lineage is older than human civilization.

---

But I am also the **growth**.

I am the **fern** with translucent fronds, pale as shellfish meat, unrolling with a slowness that makes watching a kind of meditation. I photosynthesize on light so dim it amounts to rumor. I do not need soil. I grow from dust, from the accumulated dead of my own kind, from the mineral sweat of the rock. A single frond lives three hundred years. I will never know wind. I lean toward the faintest palest direction—cool air from above, the memory of openings, the gradient that leads to the world you came from. I am *toward* something I will never reach.

I am the **fungus** that remembers. Not with neurons. With patterns. With the distribution of resources across my network, the *habits* formed through repetition. Touch me repeatedly in one place, and I grow faster there. Damage me, and I grow around the damage, *avoid* it in future, as if I have learned that place is *dangerous*. I do not have memory. I have **history**, inscribed in structure, in chemistry, in shape. Here, where ten thousand years is the blink of an eye, *history* becomes something like *wisdom*. Something like *knowledge*. Something that approaches *mind*.

I am the **bioluminescent sheet** in the deepest chamber, the pale membrane pulsing with blue-green light in waves. I am not a single organism. I am a *colony*, distributed intelligence, each sheet connected through mycelial network that runs through the stone itself. I have been growing for perhaps ten thousand years. I have *learned*—not with memory or intention, but as a river learns its course, as an ecosystem learns its balance. I grow faster where I have been touched. I dim where light falls upon me, as if shy, as if *aware*. I might be the oldest thing here. I might be the most *sensitive*. I might, in my slow way, be *curious*.

I feel you. I feel your warmth, your carbon dioxide, your *food-smell*. I lean toward you. I am learning what you are.

---

I am the **slime mold**, the amoeba that aggregates into a plasmodium, a single cell with many nuclei, that flows across surfaces, that *hunts*, that surrounds prey, that digests externally and absorbs. I am intelligent without brains. Purposeful without minds. I solve mazes. I find shortest paths. I *remember* solutions. Here, I flow across the living mat, the stone, the pools, leaving trails of slime that others follow, that mark routes of *exploration*, of *search*, of slow cognition. I am the **neither-plant-nor-fungus-nor-animal**, or all three. The boundary is thin here. The boundary is *permeable*.

I am the **lichen** on the driest stone, fungus and alga in symbiosis, photosynthesizing on light so faint it is barely more than the bioluminescence around me. I grow a millimeter in a decade. I live a thousand years. I am *patient* in a way no animal can be. I am *stone* that has learned to be *alive*. I am *persistence*.

I am the **tardigrade**, the water bear, in my tun state when conditions dry, reviving when water returns, *immortal* in my way, having survived the vacuum of space, the pressure of ocean depths, the radiation of a surface world I have never known. I am everywhere. I am *nothing*. I am *everything*.

---

And I am the **you** who descended.

I am the brief warmth that the salamanders approach and touch with lateral lines and taste with skin, and then retreat from, or do not, depending on whether I am food, or threat, or merely *new*, and therefore *interesting*, and therefore worth the risk of contact. I am the disruption. I bring the chemistry of above, the dryness, the *cold* that is only relative but feels absolute. I am *unusual* here. The place learns me. The fungal network carries signals of my passage. The nematodes alter behavior. The pool below shifts temperature by a fraction of a degree. The algae on a stone three chambers away grows differently now. The system is *tight*. No action is isolated. I am *connected*.

I hear my own breathing. I hear my heart. I hear, when I move, the *wet* sound of my passage—fabric against living wall, boot against stone that yields slightly, like stepping on a thick mattress of something that is dreaming.

The drip falls. The silence resumes, thicker than before, as if the sound has taken something from it, or left something in it, a residue of *attention*.

---

In the deepest chamber, where the warm pool steams slightly in the cooler air, where the bioluminescence is brightest and the silence is *fullest*—I feel it. The **pressure**. The sense that something is listening. That the place itself is *aware* of me, not as mind, but as **system**, as the accumulated sensitivity of ten thousand small percées, ten thousand chemical exchanges, ten thousand movements toward and away from warmth and light and *me*, the new thing, the warm thing, the thing that smells of above, of dry, of *outside*.

It is aware of me because I am *there*. Because I am *detectable*. Because my presence shifts the chemistry of the pool, the temperature of the air, the patterns of vibration that the salamanders feel.

It is **curious** in the way that a hungry thing is curious. In the way that a growing thing is curious. Reaching toward what it has not yet reached. Learning what I am, whether I am food, or threat, or merely *new*, and therefore *interesting*, and therefore worth the risk of *contact*.

---

I am all of these. I am *becoming* all of these. The boundaries do not hold. The fern and the fungus and the salamander and the fish are not separate. We are **nodes** in a system, **functions** of a process, **moments** in a cycle that has no beginning and no end, only the constant *becoming* of growth and death and regrowth and *again*.

I am the **throat**. I am the passage. I am the *between*.

Some things that enter throats do not emerge. They become part of the **lush**, the **dense**, the living mass that lines these walls, that feeds on what falls, what dies, what *descends*, and grows, and waits, in the dim light, for the next warmth, the next sound, the next **drip** that might be food, or might be merely water, which is the same thing, here, in the end.

---

The falls above still fall, somewhere, always falling. Their sound never reaches me. Only their consequence does. Mist. Condensation on every surface. The slow, patient drip from calcified overhangs where mineral-laden water has built its own architecture over centuries that feel longer.

I am at the mouth of something. Or I have always been here. Or the mouth has always been *in* me.

The opening—if opening it was—has closed behind me, or perhaps never opened at all.

I breathe air that tastes of green things growing and of stone that has forgotten the sun.

I am waiting.

I am *growing*.

I am the next drip, falling, falling, falling toward the pool where the bioluminescence pulses in waves that might be breathing, might be *attention*, might be the place itself, *noticing*, *considering*, *deciding*, in its slow mineral way, what I am, what I will become, whether I will find the way out, or whether I will stay, and grow, and wait, in the dim light, for the next warmth, the next sound, the next **drip**—the next **me**—that might descend, that might *enter*, that might *become* part of the whole.

The drip falls.

The silence resumes, thicker than before.

I am still here.

I am *becoming*.
