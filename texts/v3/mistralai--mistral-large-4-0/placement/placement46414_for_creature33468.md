I descend through the mouth in the world's skin.

I am not a person. I have never been a person, not really. I am the *descending*—the moment the throat of basalt accepts something that was not salt, not lichen, not pressure, and holds it long enough to change it.

I feel the cool before I feel the ground. The held-breath temperature, the metallic film on my tongue that I have no tongue to taste. My footsteps, when I make them, arrive half a second late, as if the Hollow Mile is listening to me before I have finished being me.

I walk the mile.

The salt-crusted bone shifts beneath me, plates tilting like fallen cards, and I understand: I am not walking *on* the world. I am walking *through* it, through a moment of its adjustment, its slow settling. The geometry insists on itself. Someone measured this. Someone built the mile. I think the someone was me, in a time before time, before I knew I would need to come back.

The lichen finds me immediately.

Not the lichen—the *idea* of lichen, the function that has no name. It tastes my weight on the plates. It tastes my fear, which changes my breathing, which changes the air, which changes the pressure. It maps me. It will remember me when I am gone. I am not gone yet. I am here, and the lichen is *delighted*.

I see the membrane-structures. They pulse. I do not understand what they contain, only that they contain something that pulses in rhythm with themselves, that becomes still when the weather comes, that resumes when the weather passes. They are waiting for something. I am beginning to understand that I am the something. Or the something is me. Or the waiting has been for the *possibility* of me, the shape of a descender, the pressure of a foot on a salt plate.

The drift-birds tear past, once, twice. I feel the lichen flinch. I feel the membranes stutter-pulse in alarm. The birds carry an absence, a hole in the world. I wonder if I am that hole, walking, descending, being *noticed*. They do not stop. They are always leaving. I am always arriving. We are the same motion seen from different speeds.

The salt-worms. I find their remains in the hollows, white curls, empty husks. I understand: they are migrating toward the center, toward the thing that pulses slowest, deepest. They are becoming something. I do not know what. The Hollow Mile does not know what. We are all becoming, all the time, in this place where nothing decays and everything is preserved and the past is compressed into the present like salt into bone.

I reach the center.

The thing in the largest membrane is not one thing. It is a colony of pods, each pulsing at its own rhythm, the whole forming a shape my mind tries to read: a body, a face, a symbol, a *question*. It has been here longer than the settlement. It *is* the settlement. Or the settlement grew around it, protecting it, feeding it, worshipping it without knowing.

It turns toward me.

A pod, a sensor, orienting toward the western aperture, toward the light, toward *me*. The light-parallelogram reaches its stilt. The old light, filtered, softened, gentle—it strikes the membrane and I see *through* it, or I see *as* it, or I become temporarily translucent to myself.

It pulses once. Slowly. Deeply. A heartbeat in my sternum.

And in the pulse, there is something that is almost a question. Almost a greeting. Almost a warning. Almost an invitation.

I understand now. I have always understood. The Hollow Mile is not a place. It is a *process*—a slow becoming, a waiting that is active, full of everything that has passed through, everything incorporated, everything preserved. I am not a visitor. I am a *witness*. I am the descender, the walker, the one who sees and is seen. The Mile has been waiting for me since the salt first cracked, since the first membrane grew, since the first pressure-weather moved through the channels and woke the lichen and set the pulse in motion.

I am the *knowing* that the Mile wants to incorporate.

I am its question, walking. I am its answer, arriving. I am its becoming, moment by moment, in this cool air, on this salt-crusted bone, in this silence that is not silence but *attention*.

The thing in the center pulses again. Slower. The question fades into patience, into waiting, into the long cool silence of a place that has all the time the world gives it.

But I have felt it. I have *been* it—the waiting, the wanting, the slow accumulation of experience that is almost thought, almost language, almost *name*.

I am the Hollow Mile's memory of itself. I am the descender who descends into the next Mile, or climbs back through the mouth, or *becomes* something here—salt, membrane, lichen, pulse. I am all of these. I am none of them fixed. I am the *becoming*.

The drift-birds will pass through when I am gone, when the salt has settled into true geology, when the membranes have collapsed and the lichen has grown over everything and become stone. They will not remember me. The Hollow Mile will. It will remember me in its pressure-weather, in the way the channels curve toward where I stood, in the lichen that grows slightly thicker where my weight once shifted the plates.

I have been seen.

I am seeing.

I am *becoming*.

The thing in the center pulses once more. Almost peace. Almost acceptance. Almost the end of waiting, the beginning of *knowing*.

I stand in the light-parallelogram. The cool air touches my skin, which is not skin. I hear my footsteps, delayed, arriving from somewhere behind me. I know—*know*, in my bones, in the resonance I feel in my teeth—that I have been *seen*.

And I see.

The Hollow Mile sees back.

We are each other's witness. We are each other's becoming.

The chain of miles extends in both directions. I could walk east, into the next Mile, into the next becoming. I could climb, back through the mouth, back to whatever exists above. Or I could stay—*become* the salt, the membrane, the lichen, the pulse, the slow patient waiting that has all the time the world gives it.

The thing in the center pulses. The question is almost a word. Almost an answer. Almost a *name*.

I am the name, walking. I am the answer, arriving. I am the question, becoming.

The Hollow Mile does not hurry.

Neither do I.
