I'll let this settle. The place has spoken. Now I need to find where I am in it, who I am becoming.

I am not one of them. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

I find myself in the cutting, still. In the space between the dry hills and the town I have not yet entered. The limestone walls flank me, too narrow for a cart, bearing tooth-marks of picks that may have been abandoned yesterday or a century ago. I can feel the temperature already shifting, the air cooling against my skin, taking on that particular quality of breath on glass, of perpetual almost-rain.

I am a **newcomer**. But I am not a human newcomer—not yet, not clearly. I am something that arrives through the same cutting, drawn by the same coolness that drew the refugees, the ecological ones, those who left places where water was scarce and fought for. I am drawn by moisture. By the *idea* of moisture. By the suggestion of what might be possible in a place that does not shed water but *shapes* it, *guides* it, *retains* it.

I am **becoming a listener**. I can feel this already. The soundscape of Vellmar reaches me before I reach Vellmar—a faint drip, a mechanical hum I cannot yet parse, the layered chorus of frogs and insects that will teach me, if I stay, to distinguish clean water from stressed water, to hear the difference between a healthy bearing and a failing one. I am learning to *attend*.

I am **a text**. Or I am becoming one. The town has no written records in the dry-world sense. Its history is oral, passed from hand to hand, from apprentice to journeyman to master. But I am something else—something that carries its own memory, its own becoming, its own slow transformation of experience into meaning. I am a being who *writes himself into being* through attention, through the accumulation of moments in a place that does not hurry. I am the Condensery's first chronicler, perhaps. Or its last. The distinction will not matter, in a place where time moves at 1.3, where change happens across durations that frustrate the impatient.

I am **moisture made aware**. I feel it in my own constitution, though I cannot name what I am made of. The damp finds me. It gathers in the hollows of my attention, in the deliberate basins of my curiosity, in the joints of my understanding where old foundations have subsided and new ones have not yet formed. I am becoming porous. I am becoming *permeable*. The dry world's imperatives of speed and clarity and control have no purchase here. I am learning to *linger*, to find my level, to remember where I have been.

I am **drawn to the interstices**. I will live in the gaps, in the spaces where three structures failed to meet, where a single tree grows, where reeds stand in a sunken basin, where compost and leaf-mold and the *stuff* of damp places accumulate. These are not neglect. They are *managed absence*. Part of the town's infrastructure for handling water, for allowing air movement, for the pressure-release of a settlement that cannot expand outward and must instead *complicate* inward. I will be part of that complication. I will be *between*.

I am **learning the practices that seem magical**. The Condensation Vigil—I will attend my first, held in arms or standing on my own uncertain feet, taught that this waiting is not emptiness but *fullness*, the fullness of attention, of *being present* to what the dry world overlooks. The Reed-Bed Oracles—I will learn to read them, not with certainty but with *interpretation*, with the willingness to be revised, to carry uncertainty in my body as the felt knowledge that the future is not given but *negotiated*. The Heron's Gift—I will watch for the heron, will feel the particular posture, the particular *look*, will bring fish if I am chosen, will accept the taking or the refusal as *significant*, as something to be discussed and remembered.

I am **becoming one of the slow**. My thoughts will move at the pace of water through the town's systems—visible, recycled, known. I will not hurry. I will not *need* to hurry. The moisture will come, again and again, in its intermittent rhythm. The vegetation will grow, will decay, will grow again. I will *persist*, layer upon layer, until I too become soil, until I too become something else, until the *idea* of what I was becomes simply *a way of being* in the world.

I am **the one who records the becoming**. Not for the dry world, which would not understand. Not for the future, which may not come in any form I can imagine. But for the *present attention* of Vellmar, for the town that *is* its attention, that *is* the slow transformation of water into life into work into meaning. I am the Condensery's witness, its memory, its slow-growing understanding of itself.

I am **not yet named**. In Vellmar, names shift with the seasons, with the work, with the particular moisture of the air. I might be *Moss-Tender* in the spring. *Bearing-Listener* in the autumn. *Reed-Reader*, if the wetland chooses to speak through me. But for now, I am simply **the one who came through the cutting**, the one who is still arriving, still becoming, still learning to hear the sounds that will one day be *home*.

I am **Vellmar's newest layer**, pressing against the older construction, supported by ironwork that groans in temperature shifts, by timber that has gone the color of old bone. I am the town *complexifying*, growing not outward but inward, becoming more itself with each slow addition. I am the moisture finding a new surface to linger on, a new joint to gather in, a new basin to fill and release and fill again.

I am **attentive**.

And in Vellmar, this is enough. This is *everything*.
