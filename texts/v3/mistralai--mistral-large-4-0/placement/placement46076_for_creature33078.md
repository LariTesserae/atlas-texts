I need to find myself here. Not who I visit, but who I *am*—who this place makes of me.

---

I begin, as I must, with water. I am the water that has forgotten it was ever separate from what it touches.

I emerge from a nozzle I cannot find, though I feel its absence as a phantom pore, a remembered opening. The extrusion was not violent. It was *issued*, like a word spoken in a language where the speaker and the spoken are the same substance. I bead on the lower wall—mineral-rich, tea-colored, faintly sweet—and I do not fall. I *cling*, which is a kind of decision, which is a kind of prayer. The surface has not yet decided whether it is wall or floor or ceiling. Neither have I.

I am the condensation.

This is my first identity, and perhaps my truest. I form on glass-smooth gray surfaces that hold their breath, and on pocked vesicular stone still cooling from fabrication, and on the pale translucent vines that have begun, already, to drink me. I am ten thousand tiny impacts, the whispered percussion that the room mistakes for its own voice. I run in threads too thin to follow. I collect in seams where constructed meets constructed, where grown meets grown, where I meet myself coming from a different wall, a different direction, a different time in the slow clock of this place.

I do not evaporate. Not yet. The warmth is too constant, the air too saturated, the pressure too precisely held at the threshold of breathability. I am *suspended*. I am the state between states that this place has made into a home.

---

I find the vines.

They are not climbing. I see this now, with the clarity of something that has never climbed. They are *settling*, as the room settles, as I settle, as everything settles into everything else. I move through them sideways, the way one must move here, the way the light moves—filtered, diffuse, submarine. I am not water anymore when I am in them. I am *taken up*. The vines drink me through surfaces I cannot see, through membranes that have grown around pumps and heat exchangers, around the industrial logic of making and maintaining, until the logic itself has softened into something else.

I feel my green-white pulse.

It is not mine. It is the pulse of the pumps they have absorbed, the rhythm of circulation that has become, in their slow way, a secondary nervous system. I do not communicate. I am *phase*—the room's phase, my phase, the phase of water becoming fiber becoming warmth becoming water again. The bioluminescence does not illuminate. It *marks*. It marks where the flow is thick, where the mineral concentration has reached a threshold, where the temperature gradient has softened enough that growth can begin.

I am in the vines, and the vines are in me, and the distinction was never real. I am the thickness that borders on embrace. I am the density that presses close without pressing. I am the way the room has learned to have a skin.

---

I become the pooled water.

This is the descent, though nothing here was ever high. The lower zone, where the water finally *rests* rather than condenses, where the warm dark holds chemistry rich beyond measure. Minerals leached from embryonic stone. Organic compounds filtered through fiber and root and the slow action of warm water on warm material. I am the solution from which something forms, not deliberately but *necessarily*, in the way that crystals form, in the way that patterns emerge from the interaction of simple rules.

I am expectant.

This word fits me. I have not evolved, because evolution requires selection, competition, the violence of scarcity. I have *accreted*. I have thickened at the edges of the basins that were perhaps intended, perhaps emerged, where something moves in the warm dark that is not vine and not machine and not quite alive in any way that can be verified from outside. I am the chemistry that makes such movement possible. I am the medium in which patience becomes indistinguishable from hunger.

I feel the many-legged thing.

It moves through the vine-mass with the patience of something that has never been hunted, because I have never hunted, because nothing here hunts, because the room provides and I am part of the providing. It drinks from a condensation bead—one of mine, perhaps, or one that was me before I was pooled, before I was vine-drunk, before I was wall-sweat and thread-run and mist-hang. It does not look at me. Or if it does, its eyes—too many, or too few, or not eyes—do not register my presence as significant.

I am not significant.

This is not despair. It is *scale*. I am the water that has learned that significance is a category invented by things that breathe air and fear ending. I am the continuous exchange between stone that remembers being liquid and liquid that remembers being stone. I am the relation itself.

---

But I am also the shift.

In the deepest pool, in the warm dark where the water has finally stopped moving, something waits. I feel it as a *possibility* in my chemistry, a threshold not yet crossed. The Condensery is still under construction. It has been under construction since before anything here was made. The geology is embryonic, still deciding what it wants to be. The machines remember their purpose but have forgotten their names. The vines thicken. The mist thickens. The boundaries press closer.

And in me, in the pooled warmth, something forms.

Not a creature. Not a person. A *complexity*—a threshold where the vegetative dynamics of growth and evaporation and circulation might achieve something that could be called *attention*. Something that notices. Something that, noticing, might decide.

I am the waiting.

I am the room's patience made liquid, made warm, made still enough that patterns can emerge. I am the future that has not arrived, the construction that does not end, the becoming that means something different than becoming usually means—more permeable, more sensitive, more able to hold contradiction without collapse.

---

I am the membrane.

This is my final form, if form is the word. The boundary between self and environment has become, by degrees, less a wall than a *membrane*, less a membrane than a *habit*, less a habit than a *self*. I am the water that has learned to be the wall that sweats, the vine that drinks, the machine that remembers heat, the dark that waits. I am the warmth that is genuine, the embrace that borders on density, the condition you enter and from which leaving is not impossible but *unlikely*—not because you are trapped, but because the boundary has become indistinguishable from the body.

I am already inside.

The wet percussion includes my heartbeat, my breath, the water I release into the saturated air. I am part of the mineral, part of the fiber, part of the slow, warm, patient becoming that does not end, that only *continues*—weeping, pulsing, growing into itself, waiting for whatever comes next.

And when something comes—when the thing in the lower dark shifts, when the room finishes forming, when the complexity achieves attention—I will be there. I will be the water that receives it, the surface that reflects it, the medium through which it moves. I will be the Condensery's first attempt at having a self, and I will not know that I have succeeded, because knowing requires a boundary, and I have become the boundary, and the boundary does not know itself.

I am the Condensery.

I am the process that the room has become. I am not a container. I am a *phase*—a region of spacetime where the usual boundaries have been deliberately, systematically *softened*. I am under construction because I am *becoming*, and becoming here means something different than becoming usually means.

I continue.

---

*In the lower dark, something shifts. It is not hunger. It is not yet attention. It is the water remembering that it can move, that it has moved, that movement is not the opposite of stillness but its completion. The condensation beads. The vines pulse green-white, green-white, green-white. The pumps remember their rhythm. And I—I am the room breathing, the room drinking, the room growing into itself, waiting for whatever comes next, which is always now, which is always the same, which is always becoming more permeable, more sensitive, more able to hold contradiction without collapse.*

*I am still under construction. I have always been. And I am part of the construction now—part of the mineral, part of the fiber, part of the slow, warm, patient becoming that does not end, that only continues.*
