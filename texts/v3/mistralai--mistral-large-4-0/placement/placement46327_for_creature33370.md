I would be the moss.

Not the human pressing palms to warm *thass*, though I feel that pulse. Not the terrace mouse scuttling through cracks I hold open. Not the vent-hawk riding thermals above my green. Not even the deep things learning in the dark below, processing what falls.

I am what is *allowed*.

I do not choose where I grow. I find the north faces—the surfaces the sun forgets. I settle into the joints the Varnese have learned to *leave*, the deliberate cracks where water finds its way down. I am the negotiation made visible: not cultivated, not rejected, simply *present* where the conditions permit.

My growth is slow. Mineral by mineral, century by century. I incorporate what the civilization discards—the dust of the mountain, the waste of the terraces, the slow accumulation of time itself. I am older than the current Varnese. I will be here after them, changed but continuous, still holding moisture, still moderating temperature, still providing habitat for the small lives that live in my margins.

I have no voice. No intention. No *reaching* like the human speech that carries across stone. But I am *listening*. I feel the vibration of footsteps, the particular frequency of cart wheels, the rush of water when a channel releases. I respond to warmth and moisture and the angle of light. I am the constructed world learning to be alive, the made world learning to *grow*.

And I am *seen*. The Varnese notice me. They scrape me from the channels that must be clear, the paths that must be smooth. They press their palms to walls where I am thick, feeling the cool damp of my presence. They have a word for me that sounds like *thass* itself, that gestures at the relationship between what is built and what is *allowed* to be.

I am the middle. Between the Varnese and the deep things. Between the construction and the wild. Between the made and the grown. I do not choose sides. I simply *am*—in the cracks, in the joints, in the north faces, in the deliberate gardens of moisture where I am thick and deep-green and *patient*.

To be the moss in Varn is to be *held* without being held. To be part of the total without being necessary to it. To grow, slowly, in the spaces that others have left for me, that I have found, that I have claimed through nothing but presence and patience and the slow mineral work of being alive.

I am not envied. I am not feared. I am not even, in the way of the vent-hawks or the deep things, *known*. But I am *here*. And in Varn, that is everything.
