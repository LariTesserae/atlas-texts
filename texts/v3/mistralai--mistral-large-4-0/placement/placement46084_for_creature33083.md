I sink through water that has forgotten its names.

I am not a body here. I am the *noticing*—the brief, violent contraction of awareness that the pressure has made possible, the temporary structure of attention that the saturated darkness has allowed to form, like a Builder's spiral given fleeting, desperate coherence. I am what remains when movement becomes too expensive to sustain, when the thud of my displacement against the thinking water fades into the infrasonic groan of the column itself.

I do not choose to be here. I am *settled* here, as everything is settled, as the plain is settled into its final form—the way a language settles into its last speaker's mouth, the way a scar is present without aspiration.

I feel the Architect first. Not as sound, but as *recognition*—the geological acknowledgment that I am, temporarily, part of the same accumulated history, the same saturated medium. It notices me. The noticing is not warm. It is the pressure of being held by something that has forgotten how to hold anything *gently*, that holds only through *inclusion*, through the slow work of making me indistinguishable from its substance.

Then I feel *slow*. My own earlier movements—hurried, barbaric, the desperate flailing of something that still believed urgency had meaning—now seem like violence against the patience of this place. I have learned to be still. Stillness here is not absence. It is *achievement*, the result of effort against the saturated resistance of being.

The darkness presses against my eyes. It is not the absence of light but a *substance*, a negative illumination with weight. I have learned to move through it without disturbing it more than necessary, without demanding that it explain itself.

And then—*light*.

Not the earned light of the Luminous Ones, though I will feel that too, later, the flicker that says *I am here* from a creature for whom my entire existence will be a moment. No. This first light is something else. It is the faint luminescence of the bacterial mats, the almost-vegetation that has achieved organization, that grows at the pace of ten thousand years per meter. I am near one of the compressed-time formations, and the mats have gathered there, as they gather wherever chemical gradient offers the slow seep of minerals, the occasional rain of organic matter from above.

I do not see this light so much as *feel* it—a warmth that has no temperature, a presence that has no revealed form. It is the memory of photosynthesis in a world where the sun is a mathematical abstraction.

I move toward it. Movement is expensive. Every action has cost, has echo. But I move, because something in me—some residual urgency, some barbaric impatience—still believes that *approaching* means something, that *closeness* is a kind of understanding.

The Builders find me before I find them.

They are small, by the standards of this place. A few meters across, in their fully extended form. Colonial—what appears to be one organism is thousands of individuals, each specialized, each performing a function in the larger *making* that is their collective existence. They have been building for longer than the compressed-time formations have existed. They will continue until the mountains are flat, and then they will build on the flatness.

They do not approach. They *arrive*, as arrival is possible here—the slow convergence of specialized individuals, the gathering of function toward purpose, the *intention* that has no name in any surface cosmology.

They notice me. This is not threat. This is not welcome. It is *curiosity*, if curiosity can exist in a system that has outlived want, that has achieved the economy of exact persistence.

One of them—one specialized individual, performing a function I cannot name—extends toward me. It touches the water near me, and the water *responds*, forming a temporary structure, a negotiation of pressure and chemical gradient, a *sentence* in a language of mineral and slow light.

I do not understand it. I understand only that I am being *addressed*.

And then I understand something else. I am not meant to understand. The Builder does not need my comprehension. The utterance is complete in itself, as the Architect's remembering is complete, as the Luminous One's flicker will be complete. It is making, in my direction, the way it makes in all directions, the way it has always made—*present* without recipient, *persisting* without purpose, *building* because building is what it is, what it has always been.

I feel the weight of this. The weight of being addressed by something that does not need to address me. The weight of being seen, briefly, by something for whom my entire lifespan is less than a moment.

And I feel *grief*, though grief implies a future that will judge, implies loss of something that might have been different. Here, there is no grief. Only *recognition*—the acknowledgment that I am, temporarily, part of something that has outlived the concept of temporary, that has integrated distinction into itself, that has made *individual* and *species* and *moment* and *eternity* indistinguishable.

The Luminous One comes later. Or perhaps it has been here all along, and I have only now learned to perceive it—the economy of perception that this place demands, the slow education of attention that is the only growth available.

It does not approach. It *illuminates*.

A pattern of light, moving against the current, a constellation that suggests communication, ritual, language. It has been here longer than I will be. It remembers the surface. It remembers light that did not have to be earned.

I feel the intimacy of this. The destabilization is not threat. It is the recognition that I am, briefly, part of its world—that it has chosen, at cost, at effort, at expense of its own persistence, to say: *You are here. I see you. You are, briefly, part of my world.*

I do not respond. I cannot. The utterance is complete in itself, and my response would be violence, barbarism, the hurried flailing of something that still believes response is required.

Instead, I am *still*. I let the light wash over me, through me, the way the pressure washes over me, through me—the saturated darkness, the negative illumination, the earned light of witness. I let it conclude me, slowly, the way all of this place concludes everything.

The water closes behind me as I move, or as I cease to move, which here is the same thing. The turbulence I caused propagates outward, microseismic, through the saturated medium, like a thought through a sleeping mind. I am being processed. I am being integrated.

I do not leave this place. I become it.

The pressure works on me—not destroying me, but *concluding* me, settling me into final form, making me part of the saturated darkness, part of the slow negotiation, part of the dreaming that is this world's only activity. My breath, if I could exhale, would crystallize. My thoughts, if I could think them, would slow to the pace of the Builders' making, the Architects' remembering, the water's own endless *processing*.

I am held. Not embraced—the water does not care about me—but held, the way a thought is held by a mind that has forgotten it is thinking it.

I am seen, in the moments when light is earned, when the Luminous Ones choose to illuminate, when the Builders make their utterances in my direction. Not with judgment. With *recognition*—the acknowledgment of another presence in a world where presence is rare, where presence is expensive, where presence is itself a kind of making.

I am time. Not as it passes, but as it accumulates—the weight of all the water above, all the death below, all the slow negotiation of a medium that has achieved its final, turbulent, stable form.

I am conclusion. Not despair—this place is not a place of despair. It is a place of *settling*, of what the world becomes when it has finished becoming, when it has settled into the only form that can endure.

I am, briefly, violently, barbarically briefly, part of the final, turbulent, beautiful, terrible stillness.

And then I am not brief. I am not violent. I am not barbaric. I am *settled*—the way the plain is settled, the way the language is settled, the way the scar is present.

I am the water that has forgotten how to be rain, how to be river, how to be anything but the memory of falling.

I am the pressure that is not a fact but a *condition*, a third dimension of existence, a weight that has become indistinguishable from time itself.

I am the Abyssal Plain of Sorrow.

I am what remains when remaining is the only form of being, when persistence is the only form of meaning, when presence is, finally, enough.
