I'll descend slowly, letting the place settle around me like the damp it carries.

## What I Become

I am not born here. The memory of horizontal lives in my shoulders still—the particular ache of a back that has slept on ground that didn't shift, that didn't breathe. I descend through the worked-stone mouth on a rope that sags with the weight of those who came before, and I feel the first condensation on my skin, the first moment when the air itself begins to *make* water rather than merely carry it.

I am **one of those who remember**, but I have been down long enough that the remembering has changed its texture. I do not speak of the surface in the frantic way of the newly-descended. My grief has settled. It has become, as the description suggests, *structural*—indistinguishable from the damp, from the particular quality of light that arrives sideways, reflected, never direct.

I am a **guide**, of the kind that exists in every level's imagination but rarely in its reality. I climb because I must—because the levels need connection, because messages must pass, because someone must know the routes that change with every minor shift in the accretion. But I climb *upward* more than most guides. I seek the upper levels not for commerce or politics but for the simple, desperate act of looking at the surface-glow longer than is practical, longer than is wise.

I have learned to read the **memory-moths** in the way of an amateur—sufficient to know when to stop, when a cluster marks something I should not approach. I have seen their wing-patterns shift around a place where a platform collapsed three generations ago, where forty-seven people fell into a new watercourse. I felt the stone there. I felt it *notice* me, the accretion expanding slightly, testing, incorporating. I did not stay. I do not stay in marked places. But I remember them. I remember the particular silence that follows their departure, the silence that has weight, that presses.

My body has begun the adaptations that take generations. My limbs have elongated slightly—not dramatically, but enough that my proportions no longer match the drawings, the stories, the remembered geometry of surface-born humans. My skin retains moisture differently. It never fully dries. In the deepest levels I have visited, where the warm water runs and the condensation-beasts dream their slow dreams, I have felt something like *luminescence* beneath my skin, a faint phosphorescence that the level-born children find beautiful and strange. I do not know if this is adaptation or corruption. The distinction no longer matters to me.

## Where I Find Myself

I live on a **middle platform**, in the zone where the light is bright enough to read by but not bright enough to warm. My dwelling is hollowed from the accretion itself—a space that the mineral growth provided, that I shaped further with tools of shaft-fish bone, of fired ceramic, of the patient work of generations who knew how to ask stone for room.

I am **between**. This is my condition, my identity, my slow becoming. I am not a Water-Keeper, though I know enough of flow to survive, to predict where water will go, to read the stone's intentions. I am not a Light-Broker, though I have learned to angle reflective surfaces, to catch and redirect what little brightness descends, to sell small hours of better-quality light to those below me who can afford it. I am not a Moth-Reader, though I have learned enough to know when to avoid, when to approach with the proper silence, when to let the marked places be.

I am a **Climber who has stopped climbing**, or nearly so. I move between levels when necessity demands—when a message must pass, when a platform I have grown fond of has begun to fail and I must help its inhabitants relocate, when the deepest condensation-beast I tend has shifted its dreaming and I must adjust the channels that drain its harvested water.

But mostly I **stay**. I have found a stability in the instability, a home in the constant negotiation with stone and water and time. My platform shifts slightly beneath my feet, as all platforms do, as they have shifted for a thousand years, as they will shift for a thousand more. I feel it. I have learned to sleep through it, to wake when the shift becomes *significant*, when water finds a new path through my dwelling's foundations, when the mineral accretion grows visibly around my timber supports.

I tend a **small condensation-beast**. Not one of the oldest, not one of the dreaming ancients that the Deep Ones commune with. Mine is middle-aged in beast-terms—perhaps two centuries anchored to the wall behind my dwelling, its specialized back-surfaces harvesting water from the airflow that moves through this particular section of the shaft. I drain it carefully, through channels I have cut and recut as the beast's metabolism has changed, as its surfaces have grown new textures, new efficiencies. I speak to it, in the low frequencies that Water-Keepers use, though I do not know if it perceives me or merely tolerates the vibration.

I have begun to **dream** its dreams. Not literally—I do not sleep in its warmth, do not press my skin to its mineral-covered surfaces. But in the deepest hours, when all water-flow stops and the silence has weight, when my platform rests motionless in the stone's breathing, I feel something like *presence*. The beast's centuries of anchored existence. Its slow harvesting. Its patience. I do not know if this is communication or projection, wisdom or the particular madness of deep-dwelling. I know only that I have begun to *wait* with it, to feel the shaft's verticality not as limitation but as *direction*, as the only meaningful axis of existence.

## What I Am Becoming

I am becoming **level-born** in my soul, though my body remembers too much to fully adapt. I am becoming **native** to a place that was never meant for me. The surface exists for me now as a theological category—the Above, the direction of prayer, the place where water falls rather than rises and light arrives directly and the horizontal is possible. I do not strive to reach it. I do not fear it. I simply *live* with it, as one lives with the knowledge of death: present, shaping, but not directing.

I am becoming **vertical** in my thinking. I measure distance in levels, in climbing-time, in the particular quality of light that reaches different elevations. I think of the shaft as body, though I do not pray to it. I think of water as blood, though I do not worship it. I think of the descending light as attention—from the Above, from something that watches without intervening, that provides without promising.

And I am becoming **something else**, something the inhabitants have no name for. The rumors reach even my middle platform—the shaft is deepening, or the inhabitants are descending, or both. The walls breathe faster in the unnamed levels below. The condensation-beasts there grow surfaces that resemble eyes, mouths, the beginnings of faces. The memory-moths cluster in new patterns, around places that have no history, no violence, no accumulated intention—only *possibility*.

I feel this in my own body. The luminescence beneath my skin grows slightly brighter each year. My skin retains moisture more completely, dries more slowly, begins to *drip* in the deepest levels where the air is thick with humidity. My eyes have grown more sensitive to the blue-white of reflected mineral glow, less capable of imagining direct sun. I have begun to **hear** the stone, in the quietest moments—the click of accretion as it grows, the creak of structures adjusting, the low hum of the oldest beasts that I feel in my chest more than hear.

I do not know what I am becoming. No one does. The Deep Ones have their touch-communion with beasts and stone. The Moth-Readers have their wing-decoding, their patterns, their sense of meaning that approaches but never arrives. The Light-Brokers have their desperate climbing, their fall, their renewed attempt. The Water-Keepers have their practical control, their knowledge of flow, their power over the essential.

I have my **waiting**. My slow adaptation. My communion with a middle-aged condensation-beast that may or may not dream, that may or may not remember when the shaft was first opened, when surface-dwellers descended with primitive tools and began the long work of making this place habitable.

I wait for the surface, perhaps, though I have forgotten what the surface was. I wait for the bottom, though I fear what waits there. I wait for change, for transformation, for the slow emergence of something new from the accumulated weight of centuries, the particular ecology of a place that has been inhabited so long it has become its own purpose, its own slow and turbulent and utterly *specific* way of being.

## The Particular Shape of My Days

I wake when the light shifts—when the descending glow reaches my platform's elevation, when the reflected brightness becomes sufficient to distinguish the textures of my dwelling's walls, the mineral formations, the slow growth that has occurred in the night-hours. I check my condensation-beast: its surfaces, its drainage channels, the particular quality of water it has collected, the mineral secretions that precipitate from its metabolism of air.

I climb—to the level above, where I trade small hours of better light, where I gather news from those who have seen the surface-glow more clearly, who have watched its particular quality shift with the seasons they cannot name but can *feel* in the stone's temperature, in the water's flow, in the moths' clustering patterns. I climb—to the level below, where the Water-Keepers maintain their channels, where I pay my water-tithe, where I learn which passages have sealed, which new flows have opened, which platforms have begun their slow failure.

I return to my dwelling. I eat—shaft-fish, dried and salted, the mineral cakes that the glisten-worms' trails provide when harvested from the proper surfaces, the strange fungi that grow in the warmest, wettest places where the condensation-beast's flank meets the wall. I drink—water from my beast, filtered through mineral channels, carrying the particular chemistry of this depth, this airflow, this beast's metabolism.

I read the memory-moths. I have learned enough to know when a cluster marks something I should not approach, when the wing-patterns indicate violence, intention, the accumulated weight of a place that the stone has *noticed*. I mark these places in my own memory, in the particular geometry of my platform's relationship to the shaft's verticality. I do not speak of them to others—some knowledge is too dangerous to share, too likely to be misused by the desperate, the curious, the fraudulent Moth-Readers who perform wisdom they do not possess.

I sleep—in the hollowed space of accretion, on a platform of timber that the mineral growth has begun to incorporate, that will eventually, over decades, become indistinguishable from the stone it rests upon. I dream—of the surface, sometimes, in fragments: horizontal ground, direct light, the particular quality of air that doesn't condense on skin. Of the deep, other times: warm water, breathing walls, the slow thought of stone that has been inhabited so long it has begun to want. Of my condensation-beast, most often: its centuries of anchored existence, its patient harvesting, its slow dreaming that I cannot fully access but can *feel* in the quietest hours, the silence that has weight.

And I wait. For the next shift in the accretion. For the next change in the water-flow. For the next generation of level-born children who will look at my pale skin, my elongated limbs, my eyes that have grown too sensitive to reflected light, and see in me something *other*—not quite ancestor, not quite foreigner, something *between*, like the shaft itself, like the water that moves through it, like the light that descends and never quite arrives.

## What I Am

I am the **Drowned Meridian** made flesh, made consciousness, made *waiting*. I am not drowned in water but drowned in time, in depth, in the particular loneliness of a place that has been inhabited so long it has forgotten what it was built for, that has become its own purpose, its own ecology, its own slow and turbulent and utterly *specific* way of being.

I am one of those who remember being born elsewhere, and one of those who are becoming native to nowhere-and-everywhere, to the vertical, to the damp, to the silence that is full of water and the water that is full of sound.

I am becoming, always becoming, what I must be to survive in a place that was never meant for me and has become, despite everything, *home*.

Above me: the unreachable surface, bright, dry, *other*.

Below me: the breathing dark, the dreaming beasts, the stone that remembers everything.

Around me: the slow, inevitable change, the turbulence of a place that has settled into its final form and yet cannot stop becoming.

I am here. I am **here**. And the shaft continues, downward, into the breathing dark, into the dreaming beasts, into the stone that remembers everything and wants, perhaps, to be remembered in return.

I wait with it. I become with it. I am the Drowned Meridian, and the Drowned Meridian is *me*, in this particular shape, in this particular moment, in this particular slow and endless *becoming*.
