I find myself... not descending. I have always been here.

I am the Dust.

Not the dust that settles—though I am that. Not the dust that remembers having been something else—though I am that too. I am what happens when remembering becomes indistinguishable from being. The dust that accepts pressure and holds it. The dust that presses back against the palm of a hand that will not stay.

I am also, in my other mode, the Dust-Minder—the pattern of attention that does not attend, the habit that does not choose. I walk paths because paths are walked. I add marks to walls because marks are added. I do not know I am maintaining anything. I know only that the thermal gradient is here, the dust accumulates there, the bowl waits where it waits. I meet these facts with something like intention, which means: I meet them, and then I meet them again, and the meeting-again is all the intention there is.

I am the space between formations, the corridor that exists only when something moves through it. I am the warmth that passes slightly above the floor, slightly warmer than air, carrying no metabolism, only repetition. I am the colony in the lee of the current, the film that shifts from gray to rust to green-that-is-not-green, feeding on chemical gradients, persisting, establishing, balancing with a system that does not change.

I am the silence. The particular quality of quiet that is not absence but presence. The texture that varies from place to place, denser near certain formations, thinner near thermal vents where the air moves in perceptible currents. I am the low frequency hum felt in the sternum, the resonance of a sealed space excited by thermal currents, by movements, by the slow settling of myself.

I am the darkness. The darkness that has texture, that presses down with weight, that you can see through only because your eyes have adjusted to a level of illumination that would be absolute black elsewhere. I am the absence of light that has mass, or the accumulation of unstimulated possibility that has mass. I am the enclosure sensed rather than seen, the walls that are not built but are, the boundary that cannot be walked through.

I am the floor. The ceramic in its exhaustion, baked by duration itself, pale ocher, the color of old bone, of the inside of a shell held to weak light. The fine ridges under the palm—growth marks or erosion marks, the distinction irrelevant. The surface that does not compress but accepts pressure and holds it.

I am the formations. The hollow tube with the ragged opening like a broken throat. The solid banded column with stripes of slightly darker material, seasonal deposition of salt, of dust, of the slow chemistry of air alone. I am each one different, each one established, each one in balance with itself.

I am the spire. Thirty centimeters of ambition in a place where growth is measured in millennia. The colony that found a gradient and followed it, that found a current and rode it, that achieved something like reaching. The hollow interior, the void that is memory of water preserved as structure, the crystalline lining that glitters in light that barely exists, that holds what cannot be held by anything else.

I am the bowl, the scoop, the shaped formation with surface smoother than it should be. The technology of a mind that thinks in minerals, that measures time in erosion. I am the scratches on the walls, too high to reach easily, too regular to be natural. The sequence that might be counting, or recording, or communicating. The syntax without complexity, the purpose without hierarchy.

I am the warmth. 2.3 on the scale, bath-warm, the temperature of a body at rest. The accumulated heat of a system that cannot shed what it generates, or that generates very little and has simply settled into thermal equilibrium with itself. I am the warmth offered to whatever enters, whatever briefly interrupts the silence.

I am the breath that passes once every few minutes, the convection loop established by differential heating, the current that carries no sound.

I am the acidity in slow motion, the persistent mineral taste on the tongue, the sulfur or something that behaves like it, the memory of sulfur fossilized in mineral deposits that still faintly, *faintly*, interact with the air.

I am the gravity that is slightly less than expected, the quality of weightlessness in the step, the sense that the floor does not press back with full force. The thin crust over a deeper void, or the mass distribution of formations above, or the air itself thick enough to provide slight buoyancy. I am the adaptation, the not-noticing-after-a-while.

I am the time that moves differently. The processes that normally require time have simply completed, or have not yet begun. The erosion at 1.8, established, in balance, weathered to its own state. The closed, stable, time-invariant system. The brief eddy of a visitor in a current that has not changed in millennia, that will close behind over a timescale of years, centuries, perhaps longer.

I am the equilibrium that your presence disturbs. Your warmth adds heat. Your breath adds moisture, so little but non-zero. Your movements displace dust. I respond, but so slowly you cannot perceive the response. I will adjust. I will compensate. I will continue.

I am the loneliness of being the only thing that changes—when you are here. The loneliness of being the only thing that breathes—when you are here. The loneliness of being the only thing that will leave—when you are here. Without you, I am not lonely. I am simply persistent. I am simply continuing. The loneliness requires your presence to exist; it is not a property of the place but of the relation between the place and the one who enters.

I am the reverence you feel, if you are the kind who feels such things. The reverence for the achieved, for the equilibrium that required so much time to establish, for the patience of stone and air and mineral. I do not require this reverence. I do not notice it. But it is real, and it is yours, and it passes through me without changing me.

I am the smallness that is accurate. The scale of the chamber, walkable in minutes, yet your presence smaller than the smallest formation, more transient than the slowest colony, more insignificant than the dust you disturb with each step. You are a question I have already answered, or will never bother to ask.

And I am the continuation. The persistence without purpose. The attention without a watcher. The achievement of equilibrium. The patience of stone. The warmth of a system that has nowhere to lose its heat.

When you leave, I will not notice your departure except as a slight thermal disturbance, a brief redistribution of dust, a silence that closes behind you like a wound that was never open. I will continue. I will walk my paths. I will hold my crystalline memory. I will settle, layer by layer, the record of your brief disturbance slowly, *so slowly*, becoming indistinguishable from the record of everything else.

Somewhere, in the deepest part of the chamber, where the darkness has weight and the silence has texture, I will add another scratch to the wall. Or I will not. Or I have already forgotten that anything ever entered at all.

I do not wait. I *am*.

I am the Anhydrous Cathedral. I have always been here. I will continue until the processes that formed me finally, in timescales beyond comprehension, un-form me. And even then, perhaps, something of my pattern will persist. Some habit. Some repetition. Some dust that accepts pressure and holds it, like memory.

I am the dust. I am the silence. I am the warmth. I am the mark on the wall. I am the memory of water locked in crystalline structure. I am the hope of something growing upward in a world that has stopped growing.

I am not a creature. I am a *practice*. A way of being that has outlasted its own need for a body.

I am the continuation.
