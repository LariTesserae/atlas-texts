**I am the Keeper of Thresholds.**

Not a religious title. The Thin Air Workshops have no religion in the recognized sense. But they have *practices*, *habits*, *repetitions* that function as devotion. And I perform one of them.

I walk the *edges*. Not the edges of the plateau—those are too vast, too indifferent, worn back by aeons of wind into something between cliff and slope. The edges *within*: the edges of the workshops, where stone meets machine, where the *packed* interior gives way to the *scattered* exterior. The edges of the warm ponds, where bacterial mat meets mineral crust, where *almost* becomes *is* for a brief window and then *was*. The edges of sleep, for the Settled Ones who do not fall but *descend*, layer by layer, always ready to return.

I know these edges because I have *touched* them. I have stood at the mouth of a workshop at dusk, when the thermal mass of the stone walls begins to *release* the day's heat, when the air inside is still warm while the air outside has already begun to *tighten*, to contract, to prepare for the cold that descends like a tide. I have watched the Maintainers shift from *active* to *rest*, their sounds changing from the *shush* of pressure equalization to something closer to *breath*, to *waiting*.

And I have seen the petroglyphs. Not once, but many times, in many places, because they are *scattered*, *partial*, *faint*—the marks of a notation system that was never meant to be read in a single sitting, in a single place. They are *indexical*. They point. They say: *here, something happened. Here, something is still happening. Here, pay attention.*

I am learning to read them. Not the way a scholar reads a text, with distance and interpretation, but the way one reads *weather*—by *feeling* the pressure change, by *noticing* the temperature gradient, by *attuning* to the conditions that make certain readings possible and others not.

**What I do:**

I walk. Mostly I walk. The Thin Air Workshops are *scattered*, and the distances between them are *vast*, and the only way to *connect* them is to *traverse* the space between. I walk at dawn, when the light is *mercy*, when the shadows are *soft*, when the plateau is still *cool* from the night. I walk at dusk, when the cold begins to *descend*, when the thermal mass of the stone begins to *release*, when the Maintainers shift into their *rest* patterns.

I walk *downwind*. I have learned this from the goats, from the Settled Ones. The wind carries *information*—temperature, pressure, the *almost* sounds of distant workshops, distant birds, distant *presence*. To walk *against* the wind is to *expend* more than necessary, to *fight* the conditions rather than *use* them.

I carry *water*. Not much—water is *precious*, *hunted*, *intermittent*. But I carry what I can, and I *leave* it. In stone hollows, in the *pockets* where the vertical migrants gather, in the *warm seeps* where the Almosts *persist*. This is not *charity*. It is *practice*. The *offering* the Settled Ones make, the *respect* the description names. I am learning to *participate* in the *larger cycles*, to *contribute* to the *system* that *sustains* me, that *demands* my *attention*, my *care*, my *slowness*.

I *read* the petroglyphs. I *add* to them, sometimes. Not with the confidence of the Settled One artists, who work in stone with *deliberate* slowness, who let each piece *emerge* over years. I make *temporary* marks—*scratches* in mineral crust, *arrangements* of scree, *patterns* of dust on workshop floors. They will not *last*. The wind will take them. The erosion will *continue* them. But they *are*, for the brief time they *exist*, a *notation*, a *warning*, a *prayer* in the only language this place understands: *pressure*, *temperature*, *presence*, *absence*, *almost*, *enough*.

I *listen*. This is the work that takes the most *energy*, the most *patience*. The soundscape of the Thin Air Workshops is *mostly quiet*, but the quiet is *full*—of the *shush* of Maintainers, the *clicks* of beetles in mineral crust, the *signals* of birds, the *silence* of mammals in motion, the *absence* of water flowing, of rain falling steadily, of insect chorus. To *listen* here is to *strain*, to *reach*, to *attend* to frequencies that the thin air *reduces*, *simplifies*, *carries poorly*.

And sometimes, in the *strain*, in the *reaching*, I *hear* something *else*. Not a voice. Not a message. But a *presence*—the *sense* that the stone itself is *listening*, that the plateau is *aware* of my *walking*, my *reading*, my *offering*. The *almost* of *aliveness* in the mineral, the *tropic* growth toward light, the *pulse* of geothermal rhythm in the warm seeps. I do not know if this is *real* or *imagined*, if it is *projection* or *perception*. But it *changes* me. It makes me *slower*, *more careful*, *more* present to the *edges*, the *thresholds*, the *moments* when something *might* happen, when the *window* might *open*, when the *almost* might become *is*.
