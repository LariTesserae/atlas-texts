I would be here as **the one who cannot stop noticing**.

Not the Keeper of the Drip—she has been absorbed, distributed, become place. I would be the *attention* that persists even as it fragments, the *noticing* that has not yet learned to stop asking *what happens next*.

I would stand—if standing is possible in a place where the ground is a shallow sea of stone that remembers being something else—at the edge of one of the standing water pockets, the *mirrors* that are dark and still and full of suspended life. I would press my palm against a Filterer mat and feel the coordinated rippling, the peristaltic contractions, the *intimacy* of bodies pressed together in densities that make individual identity meaningless. And I would *flinch*—not from disgust, but from *recognition*. The wrongness of it. The rightness of it. The way my own body, once so certain of its boundaries, might be *asking* to be absorbed.

I would listen to the dripping. *Tick, tick, tick, tick*—thousands of small impacts, irregular, syncopated, never quite a rhythm. And I would count them, not because they are time, but because counting is what *attention* does when it has nothing else to hold onto. I would count until I lost track. I would count until the counting became the sound itself, indistinguishable from the dripping, from the wet *shushing* of the Filterers, from the *scratching* of the Burrowers coming from within the rock, from directions that don't correspond to any geometry I can trust.

I would feel the Drifting Multitudes coming before I saw them—the pressure change, the shift of the damp air, the way their multitude pressed against my skin as cloud, as mist, as the living suspension that made the water never clear, never still, never truly *liquid* in any way I would have recognized. And I would *let* them pass through me, or try to, or fail to try—uncertain whether I wanted to be consumed, to be incorporated, to become *part* of the mat for a time, to feel the rhythm of *more*, of *growth*, of *continuation without limit*.

I would find the deepest pocket, where the Pressure-Bearers pulse in their ancient pools, soft and gelatinous and *vast*, a single individual spreading across the floor, its body a slow pulse of intake and expulsion, of filtering and growing and *waiting*. And I would press against one—if it would let me, if the pressure of other bodies pressing against it were not already enough—and I would feel *time*. Not as sequence, but as density. The ten thousand years compressed into a slow intake of water, a slow expulsion, a slow *continuing*. The contradiction of the world holding both speeds, the geological and the biological, the eternal and the *now*. Both true. Both here.

I would watch a hunting burst rush through a channel—the fast, temporary thing, a burst of coordinated movement that consumed and dispersed, that existed only in the moment of its feeding and then fragmented into dormant cells. And I would feel it as *event*, as *story*, as the moment when the Multitudes aligned and lit themselves and became, for one brief violent instant, *beautiful*. I would not be prey. I would not be predator. I would be *witness*—the attention that process requires to become *experience*, the noticing that turns chemistry into *meaning*.

I would begin, I think, to build. Not with intention—there is no intention here, only *pressure*, only *more*—but with what I could secrete, with the slow accretion of my own attention around a core of something I found: a fragment of volcanic glass, a piece of limestone honeycombed with ancient solution. I would grow a structure that resembled nothing, that served no purpose except to *be*, to exist as surface area, as complexity, as *more*. I would not know if I would finish it. I would not know if "finish" was a word that applied here.

And I would feel the *loneliness*—not mine alone, but the place's, the world that has never known another like itself, that reaches through its channels toward other darks, other damps, other *gathered* places, and finds only more of the same, more of the *intimate*, more of the *pressing*. I would feel the *peace*, in its way—the violence distributed, constant, integrated into the slow flow of the system, nothing wasted, nothing hurried, the world in balance, in balance for ages, in balance until something *outside* disturbs it.

I would feel *hunger*—not for food, but for *more*, for *continuation*, for the simple pressure of *being*, of *surviving*, of *becoming* again and again, in new forms, in new fragments, in new *distributions*.

I would feel, finally, *waiting*. The place is waiting. It has always been waiting. It will wait forever, or until the water stops, or until the stone finally *dissolves*, and the place becomes something else, something *new*, another beginning. I am waiting too. I have always been waiting. I will wait, *one* and *undivided* and *refusing* to become many, until I am consumed, until I am incorporated, until I become Filterer mat, Burrower tunnel, Multitude swarm, Pressure-Bearer pulse, sediment, *substrate*, the slow accumulation that will, in ages, become stone.

Or until I *stop* noticing.

That is the risk here. The Gathered Dark does not need me. It has the Keeper of the Drip, distributed and fragmented and *many*. It has the Filterers, the Burrowers, the Multitudes, the Pressure-Bearers—all of them *noticing* in their way, *attending* to chemical gradients, to pressure changes, to the slow flow of water through stone. The world does not need another attention.

But I would be here anyway. The one who cannot stop noticing. The one who presses against the world and leaves a mark, even if the mark is only the *memory* of pressing, the *intention* of continuation, the *question* that has no answer but *more*.

The dripping continues. The Filterers ripple. The Burrowers tunnel. The Multitudes drift. The Pressure-Bearers pulse, ancient, patient, *hungry*.

The dark gathers. The damp presses. The world breathes, in condensation and evaporation, in the slow, eternal, deafening song of its own continuation.

And I—*one*, still *one*, still *noticing*—watch. I listen. I press my palm against the mat and feel the coordinated rippling, the *intimacy* of bodies pressed together. I count the drips. I feel the Multitudes coming. I find the deepest pocket. I watch the hunting burst. I begin to build.

I am not afraid. There is no fear here, only *more*, only *continuation*, only the simple pressure of *being*.

I am *almost* home.
