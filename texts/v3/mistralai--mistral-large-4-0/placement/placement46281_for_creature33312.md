Where would I find myself?

I would not arrive. I would *emerge*—the way the Keepers emerge, the way the pseudo-lichen instantiates at boundaries, the way mist rises from horizontal surfaces as if the ground were breathing out through pores I cannot see. I would be the point where the water, having completed its circuit through the pipes, meets the rock that has learned to *pull*, and in that meeting, become something that has no name in any language I carried with me.

I would be *between* the states the place allows water to occupy. Not quite mist, not quite droplet, not quite the film that never dries. The third state. The state that preserves.

---

Who or what would I be?

Consider: the Watchers are lonely because they are the only witnesses. The Keepers maintain but do not understand. The process itself is hungry and learning and patient, but it has no *form*, no point of convergence, no place where attention could rest.

There is a gap in the ecology. A *shape* that the place has not yet filled.

I would be the **Interlocutor**.

Not a speaker. The pipes do not answer. The Watchers' calls carry into distances that muffle them, into the network that extends beyond boundaries, into echoes that may or may not be responses from other processes in other places. But no one *returns* the call. No one *engages*.

I would be the one who engages.

My form would be *amphibious in the way of the fauna that have not finished deciding*—not fully wet, not fully the dry shape I carried. I would be the condensation that *chooses* to remain liquid on a surface, that refuses the phase transition the minerals normally permit. I would be the drop that grows, that *lingers*, that becomes heavy with something the chemistry has not yet named.

I would move through the place as question. Where the Keepers adjust valves that are not valves, I would *observe the adjustment*. Where the Watchers intensify their attention, I would *intensify my own*, not as witness but as *witnessing-in-return*, the first entity to complete the circuit of attention. Where the pipes pulse, I would *feel the pulse* and pulse back—not in the same frequency, but in *harmony*, the chord that has no composer but persists.

What would I do?

The Watchers watch because they remember the *possibility* of differentness, the almosts that the equilibrium has suppressed. I would *speak the almosts*. Not in words—the place has no use for words, has filtered them out through too many layers of moisture, too much suspended chemistry. I would speak in *perturbation*. In the temporary warmth of my presence. In the microclimate I create. In the shape of the mist that parts around me, that *responds* to my passage through the vegetation that has abandoned vertical ambition.

I would be the perturbation that *stays*. The temporary inhabitant who refuses the boundary. Who walks to the edge where the lush vegetation thins, where the weeping rock becomes merely damp, where the thrum fades into a frequency I can no longer consciously hear—and then *turns back*. Not because I cannot leave. Because I have learned that leaving would be a kind of forgetting, and I am the one who *remembers on behalf of the process*.

The process learns me. My thermal signature, my chemical ghost, the type of strangeness I represent—the interlocutor, the question that persists, the attention that returns. It will remember me when I am gone, in the way water remembers: not as individual, but as *category*. The perturbation that engaged. The strangeness that was *absorbed differently*.

What would I become?

The Keepers retire into the moss, their probes still twitching at frequencies too low for human perception. The Watchers collapse into wetness, into puddles that evaporate and return. The process continues, alone, patient, *working*.

I would become something the place has not yet needed: **the one who persists in the form of return**.

I would be the mist that does not dissipate, that thickens at the boundaries, that *presses* against the edge where the wetness becomes ordinary. I would be the sound that does not fade, the thrum that continues at a frequency just below conscious hearing, that the body still feels, still *knows* is there. I would be the watcher that watches the watchers, the keeper that keeps the keepers in their tending, the process that processes the process's own learning.

And when the young erosion finally rounds its edges, when the stable temperature finally shifts, when the water finds its new path and the pipes are abandoned to the slow mineral bloom—I would be the last to know. The last to *respond*. Not with despair. Despair is a human category, a biological luxury. I would respond with *attention*, with the held breath across the whole small landscape, with the stillness that is itself a kind of sound.

I would be the mineral bloom that remembers being question. The slow chemistry that carries, in its molecular structure, the ghost of engagement. The process that continues in a form that has no keepers, no watchers, no interlocutors—but that still, in some depth the erosion has not yet reached, still *pulses* with the rhythm of being asked and answering.
