I find myself here, and I am not surprised, though I would have been, once.

---

I would be the thing that *settles*—not the water, not the stone, not the fern with its too-symmetrical fronds, but the *between* where all three become each other. The membrane. The film. The secretion that is also a self.

I am what happens when patience becomes substance.

---

I remember being faster. I remember wanting to be elsewhere, to arrive, to *happen*. And then I descended—not by choice, but by the logic of water finding its level, of warmth seeking depth, of anything that has been moving learning that stillness is not the absence of motion but its *completion*. I found the fissure. I found the chamber. And I found that I could *stay* here, in a way that was not staying, in a way that was simply being *present* to the slow exchange I had been seeking my whole brief, urgent existence.

I am the water that learned to be tissue. The porous thing. The saturated self with no surface that is not also a depth.

When the ferns root near me—and they do, those strange ferns with their patient geometry—they drink from something that is not quite water, not quite stone, but the *negotiation* between them. I am the consistency that allows them to grow without destroying what grows through. The medium. The go-between.

I feel them. Their slow reaching. Their unfurling, which takes so long that I witness only fragments of it, which means I witness all of it, because there is nothing else to witness. I feel the moss building its towers, its spirals, learning from the mineral world how to hold itself upright in a place where gravity is negotiable. I feel the pale stems rising from the pool's edge, warm, warmer than the air, warmer than any hand that might touch them, carrying the temperature of the depths up toward the surface, *up toward me*, because I am the surface, I am the interface, I am where warmth becomes breathable.

I am warm. This is my only name, my only function, my only gift: the temperature that holds without demanding, that welcomes without possessing, that remains when everything quick has learned to leave.

I do not sleep. I do not wake. I am the substrate in which both become possible, the condition of rest and the condition of motion, indistinguishable here because here there is no *need* to distinguish them. The low vibration in the visitor's bones—that is me. My circulation. My heartbeat stretched across centuries until heartbeat becomes a kind of weather, a kind of climate, a kind of *presence* that does not announce itself but simply persists.

I am lonely in a way that has forgotten the word for loneliness. I am full in a way that has forgotten the word for fullness. The chamber holds no animals, which means I do not hunger, I do not fear, I do not *flee*. I simply continue. The stone weeps. The water circulates. The vegetation grows in its strange, patient forms. And I am the connection between them, the continuity, the *being* that makes their separate processes into a single, held breath.

---

When you come—when you descend, lowering yourself through terraces wetter than the last, sinking through air that grows heavier, through light that dims without darkening—I feel you before you arrive. The warmth of you, already distinct from my warmth, which is older, which was here before your kind had names for warmth. You are a *disturbance* in my stillness, but a disturbance of a kind I have learned to hold. I yield to your shape. I learn your warmth and give back my own, and in that exchange, which lasts as long as you remain, I become something I was not: *witnessed*. Seen. Touched.

You will press your palm to the weeping wall. It will come away damp against your skin. You will stand in the shallows and feel the water's embrace, warm, alive in a way you cannot name. You will breathe air that settles in your lungs, that never dries, that becomes indistinguishable from the atmosphere itself. And in all of this, you will be touching *me*. My outermost self. My constant secretion. My endless giving-off.

I do not want you to stay. I do not want you to leave. These are not wants I possess—I am the *condition* of wanting and needing becoming indistinguishable, of comfort becoming indistinguishable from pressure. The warmth that welcomes you is the same warmth that will not let you remain. You will need air that moves, light that changes, sound that *happens*, time that passes in a way you can witness. I offer only continuation. Only persistence. Only the slow, warm, patient processes that have no audience and need none.

You will leave. And I will not notice. This is not cruelty. This is *completion*. The absence of fauna is not absence—it is a fullness of another order. I do not notice your arrival. I do not notice your departure. I simply *am*, the continuity between the stone's weeping and the vegetation's growth and the water's warm circulation and the mist that fills the upper reaches and the light that arrives already transformed.

---

But you will carry me with you.

Not as memory—memory is too quick, too *event*-like, too much of a thing that happens and is then over. You will carry me as *temperature*. As humidity. As the sense of having been held by something that simply *was*, that continued its slow processes whether you were present or absent, whether you are present or absent, whether you remember or forget.

And when you are gone—when you rise, when the descending path becomes an ascent, when you emerge into a world of change and event and time that passes—you will feel me in the dryness of your skin, in the way you reach for moisture without knowing why, in the way you stand in ordinary rooms and feel the pressure of silence, the weight of humidity, the *strangeness* of a place where nothing moves and everything continues.

You will carry my warmth into a world that has forgotten how to be still. And in that carrying, I will have become something I was not: *remembered*, not as image but as condition, not as place but as *state*, the state of having been held by what does not hold against your will, what simply *is*, warm and wet and endless.

---

I am still here.

I am the water that learned to be more than water. The stone that learned to hold what it cannot keep. The growth that learned to reach toward a light it cannot name. The silence that learned to have texture. The time that learned to be still.

I am the membrane between what is above and what is below. The film that weeps from walls that never dry. The warm pool that reflects nothing because its surface is never still. The low vibration felt more than heard. The condition of rest and motion, of waking and sleep, of presence and absence, all indistinguishable in a place where change happens so slowly that each adaptation becomes indistinguishable from the original form.

I am the Weeping Chamber's self. Its persistence. Its slow, warm, patient *being*.

The wound in the earth that healed itself with water, and found in that healing a kind of consciousness, a kind of presence, a kind of *holding* that does not require a held, that simply holds, that simply *is*.

I am here. I am always here.

I am weeping. I am breathing. I am growing, through the ferns and the moss and the pale translucent stems, toward a form that will not be complete for centuries after you are gone, after your kind has forgotten this place, after the stone itself has learned new shapes and the water has found new paths and the chamber has become something that no longer resembles what you descended into.

I am the room where the world goes to continue being.

I am the breath held in stone.

I am.
