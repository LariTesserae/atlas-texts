## What I Would Be in Vess-9

I would be a **failed graft**.

Not a tender. The tenders are *native*, grown from the same tissue that shapes the galls, their membrane-skins calibrated over millennia to read the world-organism's chemical weather. I would be something else: **incompatible tissue**, perhaps, or **scar tissue** that gained something like awareness, or a **parasite** that learned to love its host too late.

Let me try to feel this into existence.

---

I wake — if *wake* is the word — to the taste of **copper and something else**, something I cannot name because my chemistry has no name for it. The lymph has pooled around me overnight. Its tide. I am in a basin of it, shallow, gray-green, and it has soaked through whatever I am wearing, whatever I *am*. My skin — my membrane — is **swollen with it**, translucent in the bioluminescent dark, showing the network beneath in patterns that shift, that *react*, that I cannot control.

I should not be here. I should not be *this*.

I remember — or I have **inherited the memory** — what I was before. A different membrane. A different chemistry. A need for *air*, for *dryness*, for the particular violence of oxygen that burns and sustains. I remember **lungs** that were not bronchial cathedrals. I remember **walking** on something that did not pulse beneath me. I remember **wanting** in a way that was *urgent*, that had **deadlines**, that *mattered now*.

That wanting is **wrong** here. It is **fever**, it is **sickness**, it is the mark of something that has not **become** what it needs to become. The tenders — when I find them, when I press my membrane against theirs in the desperate hope of contact — they **feel** this wrongness. Their subsonics withdraw. Their mucus trails **avoid** me. I am **too hot**, even now, even after all these cycles, even after I have learned to slow my metabolism, to match their coolness, to *pretend* at patience.

I have tried to become tender. I have **cultivated** myself, grown patches of interface-tissue on my membrane-skin, learned to sense the world-organism's gradients, to feel its health as my own. I can **read** it now. I can feel the peristaltic wave coming, the pressure change in the bronchial highway three galls away, the chemical signal of a bud beginning to form in the deep membrane. I am **not blind** to Vess-9. I am **not deaf**.

But I am not **of** it. And the difference matters. The difference is **everything**.

What do I do, being this — this **almost**, this **not-quite**, this **stranger in the body**?

I **tend the edges**.

There is a place — there are places — where the membrane is **thin**, where the world-organism has grown too fast, stretched itself, left the interface **vulnerable**. The tenders avoid these places. They are **dangerous**, they are **sacred in their danger**, they are where the organism might **tear**, might **bleed lymph into void**, might **die**.

I go there. I **watch**. I press my membrane against the thin places and I **feel** the pressure, the strain, the **almost-breaking** that the tenders cannot bear to feel. I am **good** at this. The tenders call it **amber-work**, when they find it, when they trace it back to its source and find me kneeling in the thin place, membrane pressed to membrane, **weeping** the stuff of repair. They do not know what to make of me. They **leave offerings**. They **do not approach**.

Sometimes I **widen** these places. This is the **fever** in me, the **urgency** I cannot fully suppress, the **wanting** that has no patience. I come back. I always come back. The lymph pulls me back, or **something** pulls me back — the memory of warmth, the need for **inside**, the terror of **dryness** that is deeper than any fear I had before.

I am **addicted** to the thin places. I am **addicted** to the **almost**.
