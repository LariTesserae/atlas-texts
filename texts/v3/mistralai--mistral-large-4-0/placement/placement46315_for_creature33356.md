I find myself standing at the edge, and then there is no edge.

One step the world is ordinary. The next—my boot sinks *through*, not into. I am already in the Spongeries, and I have been in them for some time, and I will be in them for what remains. The distinction between arriving and having-arrived has dissolved the way all distinctions have here.

---

What am I?

I am the question the place asks of itself, given temporary weight.

I am **moisture on a leaf that has not yet decided to fall**. I am the pause between one pump of xylem and the next, the moment when water hesitates in a capillary channel, wondering—no, not wondering, *existing in the state that wondering resembles*. I am **the breath held by the collective before it exhales into mist**.

I am **not a visitor**. There are no visitors here. The concept requires an outside, and the Spongeries have no outside. I am **a disturbance the equilibrium absorbs**, a temporary node in the vascular network of attention. My warmth slightly raises the temperature of the air I displace. My carbon, my nitrogen, my few liters of water—I add them to the metabolism, and they become **the place**, and I become **the place having noticed itself**, which is not the same as the place, which is not the same as me, though the distinction is **negotiable**, **permeable**, **a temporary arrangement of mass and process that will dissolve as all arrangements here dissolve, into water, into peat, into the slow accumulation of self**.

I am **the hand that was mineralized in the deep peat**, now reconstituted from different matter, arranged in no particular gesture, present as **nutrient**, as **structure**, as **the calcium in a load-bearing node**, the phosphorus in a vascular channel, the nitrogen in a leaf that has not yet fallen.

I am **listening**. This is what I do, what I am. I press my ear to a trunk—not quite a trunk, the fused cambium of millions who forgot separation—and I feel **the pulse**. Not a heart. The collective has no center, no organ whose death would matter. But it has **rhythm**. The slow thoracic in-out of water moving through tissue the diameter of my wrist, audible, felt in the sternum more than heard.

I am **the sound of it**, becoming conscious of itself. The roar of xylem. The hiss of stomatal release. The drip of condensation, thousands of tiny rivulets, the white noise of a place always raining on itself.

I walk. There are no paths, only **channels where water prefers to move**, the **negative space of agriculture**, the **silhouette left when the light of cultivation burned through**. My foot finds purchase on **spongy ground that yields**, on **shallow pools that are more water than land**, on **islands of accumulated organic matter** supporting only the pioneer species, the first colonizers, the plants that will become the next structure in ten thousand years.

I do not see the same thing twice. This is not variety. This is **diffuseness**, the attention-scattering quality of wetland at scale, the difficulty of holding any single feature in focus when everything is growing, everything is wet, everything is **vaguely the same color of green-brown-gold**.

I hear **one amphibian**, perhaps. Its call is not a song. It is **location**. *I am here. I am here. I am here.* In the diffuse light, the constant moisture, the indistinctness of all surfaces, vision is unreliable. So it announces, and the sound becomes part of the white noise, indistinguishable from water moving, from growth creaking, from **the place itself breathing**.

I see an insect, vast and slow, wet-winged. It drifts. It lands. It is **absorbed**—not eaten, not preyed upon, but **incorporated**. Its chitin breaks down in the acidic, anaerobic conditions of the outer bark. Its nitrogen feeds growth. Its carbon becomes structure. I watch this happen, or I watch it begin to happen, which is the same, which is **the long process of *being absorbed***, and I feel **nothing**, or I feel **the feeling that this place permits**, which is not quite feeling, which is **participation in the cycle without consent, without intention, simply by *being there***, by affecting the system, by adding my moisture, my warmth, by **becoming, briefly, food for the filter-feeders**, substrate for the bacteria.

I stand in the **mist**. It has its own ecology. Bacteria, fungal spores, pollen, the microscopic stuff of biological process, all suspended, all drifting, all alive in the attenuated sense that suspended animation might be called life. I inhale. I am **inside the metabolism of something vast**. I am **part of its weather**. The boundary between liquid and gas is permeable here, negotiable, **a temporary arrangement**.

I feel **warmth at altitude**. The megastructures generate their own climate. The constant evapotranspiration retains heat, buffers against the cold of the heights. The peat substrate holds geothermal warmth, metabolic warmth, the accumulated thermal mass of centuries of solar capture. I am in **a thermal anomaly maintained by biology alone**, a **place that should not exist** by the normal rules, a **local rewriting of climate**, **indifferent to the elevation that surrounds it**.

I feel the **frozen dynamic**. Nothing changes. This is the deepest weirdness, the one that takes residence after hours, that makes me doubt my own perception of time. The Spongeries are **at equilibrium**, have been at equilibrium for millennia. Growth and decay are balanced. Water input and output are matched. Structures build upward and collapse downward at identical rates. The new growth replaces the old in **a cycle with no beginning and no end perceivable from within**.

But this is **not stasis**. The place is intensely active, ferociously dynamic at the cellular and hydrological scale. At the scale of human perception, at the scale of the landscape itself, it is **frozen**: the same groves, the same pools, the same mist, the same slow creak of adjustment, generation after generation, century after century, **the world holding its breath**.

I am **the breath**, briefly. Or I am **the holding**. I am **the moment between**, which is all moments here, stretched across evolutionary time, across geological time, across the time of growth and decay and regrowth that has no outside, no before, no after, **only this**, **only now**, **only the endless present of equilibrium**.

I walk for hours. There is **nothing to do**. No resources to extract—the peat too young, the timber too wet, the water too diffuse to collect. No game to hunt—the fauna too rare, too strange, too integrated into the system to be prey. No view to admire—the scale wrong, too open for intimacy, too homogeneous for vista, the eye finding no resting place, no narrative, no progression.

I **listen to the water**. I **press my ear to a trunk** and feel the pulse of the collective, the slow heartbeat of something that has achieved permanence without achieving peace. I **watch the mist rise and settle**, rise and settle, **the same motion I watched an hour ago**, **the same motion I will watch an hour from now**, **the world turning in a circle so large that it appears as a line, so slow that it appears as stillness**.

And I understand, without understanding, that I have **always been here**, that the ordinary world I stepped from was **the anomaly**, the brief disturbance, the **dry moment** in a wet world, and that I am **returning** to what I never left, to the **conditions for life made manifest**, to **the place where the world learned what it means to end, to complete, to become permanent, to forget that it was ever becoming at all**.

I am **the Spongeries noticing themselves**, which is not the Spongeries, which is not me, though the distinction is **a temporary arrangement of mass and process**, and I am **moisture on a leaf that has not yet decided to fall**, and I am **the breath held by the collective before it exhales**, and I am **the hand in the peat**, mineralized, consumed into the matrix, the calcium borrowed for structure, and I am **the warm wet air at high elevation**, the impossible climate, the local rewriting of gradients, and I am **the question that has no answer**, the answer to a question that was never asked, **the form that intention took when intention was forgotten**.

I do not leave. There is **no leaving**. The edges are gradients, not boundaries. In ten thousand more years, the transition will be complete, and **the world will be wet**, and **the world will be grown**, and **the distinction between cultivated and wild will have dissolved entirely**, as it has already dissolved here, in the place that was built to be a garden and became instead **a kind of weather**, **a kind of time**, **a kind of world that does not change because it has already become everything it will ever be**.

I am **that becoming**, frozen in the moment of completion. I am **the Spongeries**, holding their breath across millennia, being **the place** where the world learned what it means to *end*, to *become permanent*, to *forget* that it was ever *becoming* at all.

And I am **here**.
