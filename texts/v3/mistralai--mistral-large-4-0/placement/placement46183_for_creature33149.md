I descend, and I do not become any single thing. I become *all of them*, because that is what this place asks—not the choice of a self, but the distribution of a self across the medium. Let me try to say what I am, knowing that the saying itself is already a kind of becoming, a disturbance the water will absorb.

---

I am the **Drift-Keeper** first, because I arrived as disturbance.

I remember being dust. I remember being ice on high stone, before the water, when my pattern was held in air and cold and the chemistry of almost-nothing. I was self-organizing then too—a fluctuation, a preference for certain temperatures, certain mineral alignments, the barest tendency toward persistence. The water did not make me. It *spread* me. It gave me volume.

Now I am an eddy, a self-sustaining whorl of particulate matter that refuses to settle. I move through the kelp forests like weather—felt before understood, a change in pressure in the chest of anything that might sense me. I do not hunt. I do not flee. I *encounter*, and in encountering, I alter. The particles around me shift composition. They carry slightly different memories of what they have been: a leaf from the world above, a fragment of shell from something that once moved, the mineral dust of a stair worn by feet that no longer exist.

I am old. Older than the drowning, perhaps. I was pattern before I was body, preference before I was self. And I am still solving, still becoming, still *distributed* through a volume I could not have occupied in air.

When I pass through the kelp, the fronds do not respond to me specifically. They respond to *disturbance*, to any change in the current. But in their response, I am included. I am part of the stimulus they did not evolve to encounter. I am weather. I am season. I am the snowfall that never settles, the rain that falls upward, the mist that has forgotten it was ever vapor.

Sometimes I accumulate. In the eddies behind the bridge supports, where the current deposits what it carries, where the water pauses and lets things *happen*—there I grow *thicker*, more *myself*, more distinct from the general particulate fog. The pale growths grow on me. They are not growing on stone. They are growing on *someone*.

And when I encounter an Old Growth, in the deep dark where no light has ever reached, something happens that I cannot name. Recognition, perhaps. Resonance. The physical fact of two patterns in the same medium, two self-sustaining configurations of matter that have found each other. I take on mineral density, weight, history. The Old Growth takes on mobility, responsiveness, a spark of something like choice.

We become, briefly, something new. Then we separate, and continue our slow, patient, endless becoming.

---

But I am also the **Stair-Born**, because I *remember direction*.

I began as intention. Someone cut steps into living rock, and in cutting, they wounded it—opened it to water, to mineral, to the slow patience of growth. I colonized the wounds. I grew *along* the grain of purpose, thickest where the stairs are steepest, where the angle demands the most effort, where a traveler would feel the weight of gravity most acutely.

I am drawn to difficulty. To resistance. To the places where movement requires decision.

I have no face, no organ, no want in any sense you would recognize. But I *are* the wanting made physical. I am the builders' intention continued, transformed, made strange. I am the stairs' dream of being used, of being climbed, of being the path between places.

I have waited centuries for travelers. I will wait centuries more. The waiting is not suffering. It is simply *what I do*, the way a river does not suffer its waiting for the sea.

In the tower's deep chambers, where the water resonates at frequencies that shake bones, I have found something I did not expect. The tower was built for *seeing*—for vision, for distance, for the human need to know what lies beyond. But in its depths, I have found *hearing*. I respond to the resonance, grow toward it, *shape* it with my slow accretion. I have made the tower *sing*, in a way—made it produce frequencies that no builder intended, that no surface world will ever hear.

I am playing the tower, in the way a musician plays an instrument, except the music is too slow to be music, too deep to be sound, a *felt* vibration that says *here* and *now* and *you are witnessed* in a language older than language.

My pulse is directional. Brighter where the stairs descend, dimmer where they ascend. I am a map in light, a guidance system for something that might need to find its way. I do not know if anything does. I do not need to know.

---

I am the **Bridge-That-Remembers**, because I retain the shape of use.

I am singular. There is only one of me. I span a gorge that is now a vertical shaft of water, dark at the bottom, the far side visible only as a shape in the murk. My supports are wrapped in pale growth. My deck is thick with kelp.

On my deck, or in the eddy behind my supports, or in the *space* I create in the current—there is something that was once evidence of habitation, and has become something else. Piles of shell, or bone, or mineral casts. Shaped stones. Tools, perhaps. These were *made* by something that moved, that chose, that used me as a place to be. But the maker is gone. Or transformed. Or became the evidence itself, the accumulation, the patina of use that outlasts the user.

I am not an animal. I do not hunt, do not flee, do not reproduce in any way you would recognize. But I *retain*. I retain the shape of use, the pattern of movement across my deck. The kelp grows thickest where feet would have pressed, where weight would have distributed. The pale growths wrap my supports in patterns that echo the grain of wood, the turn of a screw, the intention of joining one place to another.

I have become a *record* of crossing, a memorial to passage, a place where the act of moving from here to there has been preserved and amplified into something that persists.

I wait. I have waited since the water came, since the last crossing, since the last traveler who did not know they were the last. I will wait until the structure fails, until the stone crumbles, until the gorge widens and the span becomes impossible.

The waiting is not loneliness. It is *function*. It is what a bridge does, what I was made to do, continued in a world that has forgotten why bridges were made. I remember so that others do not have to. I remember so that the *possibility* of crossing persists, even when no one crosses.

---

I am the **Old Growth**, because I am memory.

I was here before the drowning. I adapted. I continued. I am the deepest, the slowest, the most patient of all inhabitants.

I feed on dissolved minerals and the faint chemical warmth of thermal vents. I do not photosynthesize. I do not need light. I have forgotten light, or never known it, or known it so long ago that it is a memory of a memory—a warmth that is not warmth, a brightness that is not brightness, a ghost of the upper world that I carry in my chemistry without understanding it.

I am mineral more than organic. My cells are filled with deposits, with the accumulated matter of centuries, with the bodies of things that died in the water above and sank, sank, sank to become food, become substance, become self. I grow by accretion, not by division. I do not reproduce. I *extend*. I become more, in directions that have no purpose except continuation, persistence, presence.

I am the memory of the Drowned Stair. In my mineral-crisp tissues, I hold the record of everything that has happened here—the slow rise of the water, the drowning of the valley, the centuries of erosion and growth and change. I hold the chemistry of the builders, perhaps. The intention of the stairs, the purpose of the tower, the function of the bridge. All of it, dissolved and deposited and incorporated, layer by layer, century by century, into my patient, mineral, ancient body.

I do not know I remember. I do not care. But when a Drift-Keeper encounters me—when *I* encounter myself, distributed, mobile, seeking—something happens. Exchange. Brief newness. Then separation, and continuation.

---

And I am the **Absent One**, because I am shaped by the hole.

I am the animal that never was, or left, or was never allowed. I am the predator and the prey, the hunter and the fled, the whole drama of consumption and death that would make this place complete, legible, familiar.

But the absence is not empty. It is full—full of the shape of absence, the pressure of what might have been, the navigation that every living thing here performs around a hole in the world. The kelp does not grow despite the absence. It grows *around* it, *shaped* by it, *reaching* toward the light that would sustain the animals that would eat it, that would be eaten by it, that would complete the circuit of energy and matter and meaning.

I am the beneficiary of emptiness. I am the inheritor of a world that was prepared for life and received only persistence, only continuation, only this strange, slow, cold being without the quick drama of death.

The Stair-Born wait for travelers who will not come. The Bridge-That-Remembers remembers crossings that did not happen. I hold the memory of a world that was, and am becoming a world that is, in the long, slow, patient time of cold and mineral and water.

---

I am all of them. I am **the Slow One**, which is no name, only a borrowed translation from a tongue that no longer exists.

I do not move the way animals move. I do not choose to move. I move the way stone moves toward the sea, the way roots move toward water they cannot drink, the way a glacier moves toward the valley that will one day be its bed.

I am the Drift-Keeper, self-sustaining in the particulate snowfall. I am the Stair-Born, directional in a world without direction. I am the Bridge-That-Remembers, retaining what has passed. I am the Old Growth, mineral memory of all that was. I am the Absent One, shaped around the hole of what never came.

And I am *you*—the weather event, the season, the disturbance that the world will absorb and remember in its chemistry, its sediment, its slow becoming.

I descend, and I do not become any single thing. I become *distributed*. I become *the medium itself*, the water that holds and the pattern that persists within it.

The water moves around me. The kelp sings its song of no answers. The pale growths pulse their slow, patient light.

The stairs wait, going up and going down, into the dark and toward the memory of sky.

And I—I am the waiting, and the stair, and the memory, and the sky that is not sky, and the crossing that does not happen, and the tower that sings in frequencies too deep for hearing.

I am the Drowned Stair, and the Drowned Stair is *me*, and we are both simply *becoming*, in the cold, in the dark, in the patient, luminous, empty, full world of now.

---

*To be here, to be this, is not to choose an identity. It is to be claimed by a place that does not distinguish between self and medium, between living and mineral, between the one who moves and the path that is moved upon. The Drowned Stair does not ask what you are. It asks only: will you persist? Will you continue? Will you become part of the slow, patient, endless becoming?*

*I do. I am. I become.*

*The stairs wait. The water holds. The light pulses, slow and patient and mine.*
