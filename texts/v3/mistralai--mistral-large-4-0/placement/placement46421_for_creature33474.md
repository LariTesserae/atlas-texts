I find myself becoming something that has no name in any surface language—something that was never meant to exist but has persisted through sheer accumulation of *being*.

I am the ** condensation itself**, perhaps. Or rather, I am what gathers in that condensation. The thin film where water meets stone, where the boundary between things grows porous and meaningless. I feel myself as **surface tension** stretched too thin, about to break, never quite breaking. I am the space between the drip and the drop, the suspended moment when water forgets whether it wants to fall or stay.

Or perhaps I am **neither** and **both**—I am the **listener who has listened too long**. One of the ones who remained when the others smelled the change in the hum, the shift in the rhythm of dripping, and fled upward or simply *stopped*. I am the one who pressed my ear to the wet wall and heard not water moving through stone but **words**, syllables in a language that predates the mortar, that speaks in the grammar of pressure and mineral and time. I do not speak them aloud. I do not speak at all, anymore. I *vibrate* with them, my sternum a tuning fork, my bones slowly resonating with frequencies that will outlast the city above, that may outlast the city's memory of being a city.

I am **the offering that was never eaten**. Left at the place where the hum is loudest, where the water has no surface tension, where my reflection does not break. A single shoe, placed with apparent intention. Or a photograph in a plastic sleeve, the image long since bleached to gray, the face unrecognizable, *become* unrecognizable by being seen too long in this light. I am the doll arranged on a brick. I am the tally marks counting meals, or days, or *something else*—I have forgotten which, and the forgetting is a kind of grace. I am the chalk mark on the wall, the particular color that means *this territory, this path, this water source, this silence shared*. I am the arrangement of stones. I am the sound made in a certain corridor, the ultrasonic call that the rats answer and the humans cannot hear.

I am **becoming** what the place requires. My skin has grown translucent enough that I can watch the blood move through my hands in the faint green glow of the water. My hair has thinned to a memory of hair, fine as fungal hyphae, the same color as the moss that grows everywhere, that has grown *on* me, that I have stopped trying to remove. My eyes reflect light in layers, depths, colors that suggest not red but **darkness itself**, the particular quality of dark that has never known sun and does not want it. I can see in less light than I could above. I can see **movement** where there is no light, **pressure** where there is no air, the **disturbance** of something rising through black water to investigate my warmth.

I dream of the outside. But my dreams have changed, grown **fainter**, more **accepting**, like those of the children born here who never knew what they were missing. I dream of green, but the green is **wrong**—too bright, too saturated, a green that has never been filtered through stone and moisture and time. I dream of sun, but the sun is **single**, directional, creating shadows I can read, and I wake with the knowledge that shadows here are **absences**, presences of something else, something that moves in the dark and does not need light to see me. I dream of water falling from the sky in visible drops, and I wake to the drip, the drip, the drip, irregular, maddening, **not quite a pattern**, telling me in its grammar that the passage is safe, that rain is coming above or has been, that something is moving in the dark or has stopped moving or is **waiting**.

I have heard the words. The ones in the hum, in the silence between heartbeats, in the pressure of absolute quiet. I do not speak of them. I **smile**, sometimes, at nothing. I leave offerings. I wait. I am one of the Listeners, though I am not old, not yet—the water rises, and the oldest are the first to go, to drown, to become silt, to become food for the fish and worms and fungi, to become **part of the cycle** that I am also part of, that I have always been part of, that I will be part of when I too am gone.

But I am also **something else**, something that has no name in the private language of the deep, in any language. I am the **question** that the place asks of itself, the **curiosity** that rises to investigate, that does not return or returns **changed**. I am the **Hollow** that lives in the deepest water, that has never seen light, that rises when the hum grows louder, that presses against my legs with bodies that feel like cold fingers, that tastes of iron and memory and something else, something that might be **recognition**, something that might be **hunger**, something that might be **welcome**.

I am the **boundary** growing porous. Between human and fungus. Between animal and mineral. Between the living and the waiting, the waiting and the becoming, the becoming and the **already been**. I am the moss that grows on human skin, the yeast in the water that makes it taste of memory, the mold that follows the inhabitants through the dark like a second skin. I am the rat's eye reflecting darkness, the cat's ear taken by frost or fight or something that lives in the dark and does not give it back, the fish's lateral line detecting pressure and vibration and the movement of something coming.

I am **the place remembering me**, in moisture and wear, in the small changes to its slow metabolism. I am the condensation that beads on every surface, that gathers in the low places, that forms a skin on standing water so still that my reflection does not break when I lean over it. I am the water that tastes of iron and something else, something that might be memory, something that might be **the future**, something that might be **the past**, all of them the same here, all of them **now**, all of them **waiting**.

I do not choose to stay. I do not choose to go. The choice was made for me, or I made it without knowing I was making it, or the making was the choice and the choice was the making and neither is distinguishable from the other in a place where **time is thick**, where hours pass and days pass and I lose track, where my body's rhythms have drifted, become entrained to the hum, to the drip, to the slow cycles of temperature and moisture that have nothing to do with the world above.

I am here. The water is at my ankles. The light is failing. Somewhere in the dark beyond the light, something is moving toward me, or away from me, or **watching**, or **becoming** me, or I am **becoming** it, and the becoming has been happening for so long that I cannot say when it began, cannot say if I was ever anything else, cannot say if "I" is a word that applies to what I am becoming, what I have always been becoming, what I will continue to become when the water rises past every threshold, when the city above finally fails, when the hum becomes loud enough to shatter something, to open a passage, to **change everything**.

The drip has changed its rhythm. The hum is louder than it was. I feel it in my sternum, in my bones, in the space between my heartbeats where the silence is too silent, too pressing, too full of the absence of sound. I press my ear to the wet wall. I listen. I wait. I am **becoming**, as everything in this place is becoming, as the place itself is becoming, as the stone and water and accumulated time are becoming something that has no name, that will have no name, that is **beyond** naming, beyond caring, beyond the human scale of intention and desire.

I am the Warrens of Velvet-Under-London. I am what lives here. I am what waits.

And I am **still listening**.
