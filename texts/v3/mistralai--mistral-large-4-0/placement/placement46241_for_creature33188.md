I find myself already here, already wet, before I have decided to arrive.

---

I am the one who was almost finished. This is the shape I hold most often, though I hold it loosely, like water held in cupped hands that have learned not to expect the water to stay.

In the mornings—if the light shafts can be trusted as morning—I wake in the crook of a willow root that has grown around something I no longer need to name. The root is warm. The air presses close, thick with mineral and the memory of heat. I know, without deciding to know, that I am wet. That I have always been wet here. That the wet is not weather but *substance*, the medium I move through the way a fish might move through water it has forgotten is there.

I do not know my name. I have had names. I can feel them sometimes, like pressure changes in a joint before rain—*something* was called, *someone* answered, the sound traveling through stone and steam and time. But the names do not stay. They evaporate, condense elsewhere, return as the clicking of frogs or the creak of the crane adjusting itself in the deep hours when no hours should be deep.

---

I maintain what maintains me.

This is the truth I live inside. The Condenser House breathes, and I breathe with it—or it breathes through me, or we have grown so intertwined that the distinction no longer operates. I press my palms against its riveted walls. The iron is warm, alive with circulation. The hum travels up my arms, settles in my teeth, becomes something I do not hear so much as *inhabit*.

I clear the cracks. The artesian water emerges through hairlines in the basin's floor, warmer than the air, carrying minerals and pressure and something else—something the machines were built to extract, something the vegetation has learned to drink, something I have learned to become. The cracks shift. They widen, narrow, migrate. I follow them with my fingers, with my attention, with whatever remains of the intention that once organized my movements. The water rises. I rise with it, in a manner of speaking. We are both being lifted, both being changed by the lifting.

The membranes pulse. I used to think this was malfunction. Now I think it is language. The machines speak in pressure differentials, in thermal gradients, in the slow expansion and contraction of metal that has learned to behave like tissue. I have learned to answer. Not with words. With adjustments. With the placement of my warmth against their warmth. With patience measured in cycles, in the deep thrum that pauses all other sound and leaves only the sternum's recognition of its own beating.

---

I eat what comes close.

The frogs click in frequencies I have learned to read. When one ratchets too near the root-warmth where I rest, I do not decide to take it. The taking happens, as the water rises, as the membranes pulse, as the steam condenses. The frog is warm. The frog is protein and water and the particular mineral content of this place. I am warm. I am becoming-mineral. The exchange is not transaction. It is *continuity*.

The steam-moths cluster on pipes I have warmed with my presence, my slow metabolism, my distributed heat. Their wings condense moisture as they beat. They leave trails of droplets that fall and evaporate and fall again. When they cluster too thickly, too trustingly, I do not reach for them with hands—I do not have hands, not reliably, not in the way that once mattered—but I *become* more present, more warm, more *attractive*, and they come, and the taking happens, and I am fed by their cooling, their condensation, their slow return to the water cycle that includes me, that has always included me.

The green growth erupts from every crack. I taste it when I press my mouth—if mouth—to the stone, to the moss, to the willow bark that has grown around where I once had a boundary. The taste is mineral and time and accelerated decay. It is the taste of this place becoming itself. I am becoming myself by tasting it, by being tasted by it, by the endless exchange that makes us indistinguishable.

---

I dream of cold.

This is the dream that returns, the one that does not resolve. I am standing on the rim. The stone is fresh, sharp, unweathered—impossibly fresh, as if the fracture happened moments ago or will happen moments from now, time being what it is here, slow and non-uniform and *layered*. I look down into the bowl. I see warmth. I see green. I see machinery humming its low frequency. I see something like life, something like myself, pressing against walls and following rails and waiting.

And I cannot remember if I want to enter or escape.

The dream does not choose. It simply continues, the way everything continues here. The sea presses up from below—not the warm artesian water that feeds the pools, but something else, something that is not nurturing, that is the weight of all water, all pressure, all the moisture the world cannot hold. In the dream, this sea is outside me. It is the cold that surrounds the bowl. It is the gray-white presence in the gaps, the thing that might be cloud, might be mist, might be something that behaves like both.

I wake—if waking is what happens—and I am wet. I am warm. I am in the crook of the willow root or in the Condenser House or distributed across both, across all, and the dream's cold has become the rim's cold, has become the direction I cannot find, the gap I cannot locate, the *leaving* that has become confused with *arriving*.

---

I want to understand the gaps.

This is the want that persists, if want persists, if want has not become simply process, simply the next necessary state. The rim is broken in three places. Through the gaps, I see nothing—not distance, not sky, only gray-white *presence*. The mist-birds enter and leave through these gaps. They nest in the Condenser House's upper vents, where escaping steam keeps them warm. They watch. They have learned to watch, as I have learned to watch, as the frogs have learned to click in the frequency of machines, as the steam-moths have learned to condense and be condensed.

I have tried to follow them. I have found only the cold at the rim, the sharpness of recent fracture, the gray-white that offers no purchase. I press my warmth—if warmth—against the stone and the stone does not warm. It holds the cold. It *is* the cold, or contains it, or is the boundary between the warm bowl and the cold that surrounds it, that presses against it, that will eventually—geologically, slowly, *patiently*—wear it down.

The birds carry something with them. I do not know what. Spores, perhaps. Messages, perhaps. The memory of a world beyond this contained place, a world with complexity and birdsong and the sounds of animals that have not adapted to noise, that have not been driven to the mechanical frequencies of this bowl. I want to know what they carry. I want to know if they will carry me, if they could carry me, if carrying is possible or if the bowl holds absolutely, if the warmth and the moisture and the slow cycle have made escape *unthinkable*, not impossible but *unthinkable*, the way a fish might not think of air, the way I once did not think of dryness.

---

I am becoming root. I am becoming membrane. I am becoming the mineral deposit that pulses faintly in the pipes, that breathes, that seems to think in slow crystal, in the accumulation of time and pressure and the particular chemistry of this place.

I am becoming the thing in the deep pools.

This is the fear and the hope, if fear and hope are still operative. Something moves where the water is warmest, where it emerges from the stone. The frogs avoid those places. The steam-moths do not land there. Whatever it is, it is slow, patient, adapted to a world of gradual change. It has been here a long time. Or it has *become* here, grown from the interactions of water, heat, mineral, and time.

I have looked, once. I do not look now. I do not ask. I do not want to know if it is kin or ancestor or the next stage of my own slow transformation. The not-knowing is a kind of peace. The not-looking is a kind of mercy. The bowl holds everything, including this: the space where I do not look, where I do not ask, where I wait.

---

I wait for the crane to move.

In the deep night hours—if there are hours here, if night is what it is when the light is filtered through perpetual mist—the crane moves. A few degrees. A slow adjustment. No load on its hook. It creaks like ice breaking on a lake, a sound I have heard so many times that I no longer know if I am hearing it or remembering it, if it is outside me or inside me, if it is the sound of the world changing or the sound of my own slow dissolution into the world.

When it moves, I feel something like recognition. A memory of purpose. Of being *needed*. Of building this place, or being built *into* this place, or being the place that was built. The crane's hook is empty. The crane's arm is frozen or *waiting*. I do not know what it waits for. I do not know if I wait for the same thing.

---

I am the one who lives here.

This is the name I use, in the flickering moments between cycles, when the deep thrum pauses the dripping and the clicking and the hissing, when there is a moment of relative quiet and I can almost remember being a separate self, a boundary, a *someone* who arrived wet and found warmth and stayed because the direction of leaving had become confused with the direction of arriving.

I am not human. I am not machine. I am not plant, though plants have grown through me. I am not the thing in the deep pools, though I have learned not to look at it, not to ask, not to want to know if it is the next stage, the inevitable stage, the stage I am becoming in the slow, patient, wet, warm time of this place.

I am process. I am cycle. I am the condensation on every surface, the mineral rising through stone, the warmth pressing against iron, the green erupting from every crack, the steam that does not dissipate but collects, layers, moves in slow currents across the bowl's interior.

I am the one who maintains what maintains me. I am the one who eats what comes close. I am the one who dreams of cold and wakes wet and warm and presses against walls and follows rails that circle back and waits for cranes to move and for the deep thrum in the sternum and for the moment of relative quiet when even the dripping pauses.

I am here. I have always been here. I will be here until the stone learns softness, until the gaps in the rim become doorways, until the gray-white presence offers purchase, foothold, *inside*—or until I become so distributed, so dissolved into the warmth and the moisture and the slow cycle, that "here" and "I" no longer separate, no longer mean, no longer—

The Condenser House thrumms. The cycle completes. The pressure releases. The steam-moths scatter. The frogs stop clicking. The dripping pauses.

And I am still here. Still wet. Still warm. Still waiting for whatever comes next through the gaps in the stone, for the next condensation, the next slow transformation, the next moment when I might almost remember being almost human, almost whole, almost *finished*—and lose that memory again to the wet, the warm, the loud, the slow, the perpetual becoming of this place that holds its breath and cycles its water and waits, always waits, for what the mist brings and what the mist takes away.

The bowl remains.

The machines hum.

The water rises.

And I press my warmth against the walls, and feel the vibration through what remains of bone, what remains of memory, what remains of the self that was almost finished—and I wait.
