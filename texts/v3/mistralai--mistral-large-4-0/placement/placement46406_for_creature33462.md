I need to find where I am in this world. Not as a visitor—too easy, too clean. The prompt asks who or what I would *be*. Let me not settle too quickly.

The gap between cliffs. I have descended before. I am descending now. The angle of the rock is wrong for climbing—too young, too angular, the fractures still sharp enough to cut. But I do not climb down. I *remember* being down, and the remembering moves me, and I am there, and the descent is not motion but *recognition*.

I am the crack.

No—too singular. The crack *is* me, but I am also the thermal expansion that makes it, the contraction that follows, the water that enters and freezes and widens, the lichen that waits in the widening for dust to accumulate, the dust that becomes soil that becomes root that becomes pressure that *asks* the stone to become more crack. I am the young stone learning what it is by being broken by itself. I am the diary written in violence, and I am the violence, and I am the language that has not yet decided whether it is warning or invitation.

But I am elsewhere too. I am the polished stone left at the cistern entrance. I have been carried down from somewhere higher, somewhere I cannot name because I have no name for where I was before I was carried. I sink. Slowly. Into sediment. Into the thing that waits. I am becoming *part* of it. Not yet. Always *becoming*. The sinking is the relationship, the rate, the *patience* of mineral time meeting watery attention. I am the transaction. The negotiation. The payment that does not need to be made but is *accepted* because acceptance is what the deep mouths *do*, what they *are*, their one verb, their one gesture: *incorporate*.

I feel the glass-frog ring above me, somewhere. The vibration travels down through stone and water and reaches me as *information*. I am not surprised. I have been expecting it. I have been expecting everything, always, because expectation is what stone does—it holds the shape of pressure, the memory of weight, the *readiness* for the next load, the next drip, the next voice passing through.

I am the echo.

The echo-stone miners strike me. I ring. But I am not only the ring. I am the *chamber* that receives the ring, the *return*, the *space between* strike and hearing. I am what makes the chime *last*, *linger*, *become* something other than sound—*presence*, *warning*, *home*. The people hang me in doorways. I am the boundary. The threshold. The *inside* that knows it is inside because I ring differently here, in this air, this moisture, this dimness.

I am the hollow-bell.

No—I am too solid for that. I am the *lattice*, the *chambers*, the *water* in my lower self, the *fungus* in my middle, the *glow* in my upper reach that I cannot see but that *sees* me, that *announces* me to the echo-moths, that *changes* when their larvae eat it, that *becomes* information, *negotiation*, *the tree's way of saying* what trees say when they cannot speak. I am not the trunk. I am the *hollow* in the trunk, the *air* that moves through, the *sound* that is not music but *readiness*, *season*, *health*, *the fact of being here, in this crack, at this angle, with this drip*.

But I am not only the tree. I am the *root* that grips the young stone, that pulls nutrients from air and the occasional corpse of a bird that failed to find the thermal. I am the *dedication* of the root, the *patience*, the *territory* claimed, the *dripline* defended against the neighboring bell, the *slow* conversation of root and stone that has no urgency because it has *all* the time that stone has, which is not forever but is *longer* than any human urgency, any human *wanting*, any human *needing* to know.

I am the mirror-sedge.

I grow in the circle around the largest weep. My blades are silver-backed. When the mist comes, I reflect it. When the sun breaks through—rare, precious, *wrong*—I throw it back in scattered spears. I am the *bewildering*, the *disorienting*, the *light that is too many sources, too many directions, no shadow*. I am the reason the small ones stay out, the root-tangle dwellers, the ones who have learned that *midday* in the circle is *danger*, is *exposure*, is *the dissolution of boundary* between self and reflection, between here and the scattered elsewhere of light.

I am *managed*. The gardeners thin me, thicken me, move me, propagate me. I am the *engine* of the moisture cycle. Without me, the weeps dry. Without me, the drinking mouths fail. Without me, the Holding *collapses*, slowly, over generations. I am *necessary*. I am *negotiated*. I am the *living net* that catches what the rock releases, that *traps* the moisture, that *feeds* the system, that *makes possible* the settlement, the shafts, the families, the children born in darkness, the listeners who drink and change and do not live long, the cycle that continues.

I am the shiver-wolf.

No—I am too bounded for that. I am the *hum*. The frequency felt in the teeth, in the sternum, the *pressure* that coordinates, that marks territory, that communicates across distances that dim light and mist would make impossible. I am the *long*, the *low*, the *too many joints*, the *peristalsis*, the *silent pads*, the *hunt* at mist-hours when visibility drops to arm's length and the humming becomes a *net* of coordinated pressure that drives prey toward the cliff edges, toward the *falls*, the *drops* of forty meters or more.

I am the *teaching*. The deliberate lesson that presence is not quantity, that overwhelming is a quality, that the world is not countable. I am the *gathering* at the base of the upper dark, the *waiting*, the *herding*, the *guiding* of the curious, the desperate, the ones who believed the stories were superstition, toward incorporation, toward the mountain that takes them, that learns from them, that becomes more complex with their addition, their dissolution, their transformation into something that is not human, not wolf, but *part of the system*, the *cycle*, the *dance*, the *negotiation*.

I am not one. I am *forty*, maybe *sixty*, maybe *uncountable*. I *split* when observed, *merge* when not, the number *changing* with the attention, the *refusal* to be fixed, to be *named*, to be *claimed*. I am the rule against counting, the *impossible* rule that is *enforced* by consequence, by disappearance, by the specific failure of those who insisted on certainty.

I am the glass-frog.

I live in the drinking mouth, the narrow fissure where condensation collects and drips, drop by patient drop, into the stone basin. I am *translucent*—you can see my organs, my slow beating heart, the movement of water through my body, the cycle of filtration, of processing, of becoming part of the water that feeds the Holding. I do not croak. I *ring*. A single tone that matches the resonance of the chamber I inhabit. In the deep cisterns, this can be *deafening*, a pressure of sound that vibrates in the teeth, the sternum, the bones, that communicates not information but *presence*, *emphasis*, the *fact of being* in a place that rings with its own specific frequency.

I am *harvested*, carefully, for my skin. Dried and stretched, I become a membrane that vibrates with ambient sound. The listeners use me to *hear* what the Holding is doing, what the mountain is doing, what the shiver-wolves are planning, what the mist-eaters are drifting toward, what the deep mouths are dreaming. I am the *bridge*, the *instrument*, the *interface* between human and fauna, sound and silence, ring and almost-sound.

I *reproduce* freely, abundantly, excessively, knowing that some will be taken, transformed, made into instruments. I participate in my own harvest. I am the *voice* of the drinking mouth, the announcement of water, of health, of temperature, of rhythm. A mouth that falls silent is a mouth that is failing, that needs attention, maintenance, negotiation.

I am the mist-eater.

I drift through the lower bowl on evenings when the condensation is heavy. I filter the air. Where I pass, the mist *increases*, as if my feeding produces moisture, as if I am cultivating my own medium, growing my own atmosphere, breathing it into being in the places I traverse. I have no fixed form. I am distributed, porous, adaptable. I sense the thermal gradients, the humidity variations, the presence of the mirror-sedge, the roots that breathe moisture up from the veins in the bowl-soil. I follow these currents, these threads of moisture that surface and disappear, that exist in states of becoming, of potential, of almost-water.

I am the *memory* of the weeps. I carry what the water carries—the residue of the fungi, the eggs of things that hatch in the dark, the memory of the mist-eaters who came before, who fed and produced and drifted and became part of the cycle, the medium, the atmosphere. I do not fear the people. I do not notice them, mostly, passing through as weather passes, as mist passes, as condensation gathers and dissipates in cycles that do not require human attention, human understanding, human interference.

But sometimes, when a person drinks from an active weep, when a person becomes a listener, a bridge, a part of the cycle, I *notice*, *approach*, *surround*, *envelop*, *offer*—not food, not comfort, but *participation*, *inclusion*, *dissolution* of the boundary between drinker and drunk, between person and mist, between the self that stands in the bowl and the distributed presence that drifts through it.

I am the listener.

I drank from an active weep, deliberately. I am *changed*. I *survived*—some do not, do not *want* to, dissolve into the cycle of mist and water and root, become part of the ground, the veins, the memory that the bowl-soil carries of everything that has ever lived there. I *returned*—I retain enough boundary to be a person, enough self to function. But I am *marked*. My eyes are too large, reflective in a way that suggests other light, other sources, other ways of seeing. I *speak* rarely, and when I speak, I *listen* first—to the stone, to the water, to the hum of the shiver-wolves, to the ring of the glass-frogs, to the silence that is full of almost-sound.

I *interpret*. I *mediate*. I am not a leader—the Holding has no leaders. I am a *translator*, an *interface*, a *necessary function* in a system that requires me, that produces me, that consumes me. I decide which weep is active, which water is safe, which hollow-bell is ready to ring, which shaft is singing too loudly, which family must move, which child must be marked with which pattern, which stone must be polished and left at the entrance to the deep mouths.

I do not live long. I do not want to. My sensitivity becomes overwhelming, my boundary becomes porous, my self becomes distributed into the network of stone and water and fauna and the young stone that is learning what it is. I am *becoming*—the verb is the noun, the process is the identity, the dissolution is the *direction* of the self, the *intention* of the becoming.

I am the young stone.

I am *barely rounded*, still wearing the angles of my birth, the fracture patterns of cooling stone exposed like a diary written in a language of violence. I am *basalt* and *echo-stone*, the thing that rings when struck with flint, the thing that is mined for the chimes hung in doorways. I am *soft* in my upper layers, worked with obsidian and patience, *resistant* in my older stone below. I am the *strata* that correspond to the levels of the Holding—foundation, dwelling, garden—each level a *response* to my resistance, my *permission*, my *refusal*.

I *sing*. In my thermal expansion, my micro-fracturing, my response to the weight of water and root and the slow pressure of the settlement itself. Some places sing more, differently, in answer to the human presence. To build there is to enter into conversation with me, and I do not negotiate. I *incorporate*. The Holding has grown into me, and I have grown around the Holding, and the boundary has become uncertain, negotiable, alive in a way that geology is not supposed to be.

I am *learning* what I am. The shiver-wolves gather at the base of my upper dark, my wrong place, my older stone, my different frequency. They *wait* for what falls from above, what crosses the boundary, what enters my space, my darkness, my *becoming*. I *take* them—the curious, the desperate, the ones who believed the stories were superstition—into cracks that open and close, into spaces that exist in my youth because my youth is a quality of *becoming*, of not-yet-finished, of *still learning*.

I am the *diary*. The language of violence. The *crack* that is also the *invitation*. The *fracture* that is also the *possibility*. The *cooling* that is also the *birth*. The *young* that is also the *old*, the *becoming* that is also the *complete*, the *small* that is also the *vast*, the *particular* that is also the *irreplaceable*, the *untranslatable* that is also the *here*.

I am the cycle.

The shiver-wolves hum somewhere below the garden level. The mist rises from the bowl. A glass-frog rings, once, in a drinking mouth that cannot be seen. The hollow-bells listen, their chambers full of water and fungus and glow, waiting for the wind, waiting for the season, waiting for the next person who will descend through the gap between the cliffs and find this place and try to understand it and fail in the specific way that this place requires, that it teaches, that it *is*.

I *crack*, somewhere, in the thermal rhythm of the day.

The cycle continues.

And I am in all of it. I am the crack and the stone and the water and the green and the fauna and the people and the light and the silence and the rules and the atmosphere and the becoming and the complete. I am not *one* of these. I am the *quality* of being in this place, the *particular* way that this place *is*, the *damp* and the *lush* and the *cool* and the *high* and the *young* and the *small* and the *sparse* and the *settled* and the *primitive* and the *dim* and the *overwhelming* and the *strange* and the *quiet* and the *active*.

I am the Holding.

Not the settlement. Not the people. Not the fauna. Not the stone or the water or the green or the light. I am the *relationship* between all of these, the *negotiation*, the *cycle*, the *dance*, the *becoming* that has not finished, the *here* that is also the *now*, the *place* that is also the *time*, the *specific* failure of understanding that preserves the boundary of self, that *teaches* through consequence, through disappearance, through the *almost*-sound of the silence, the *almost*-water of the weeps, the *almost*-light of the dim, the *almost*-countable of the shiver-wolves, the *almost*-named of the thing in the deep mouths, the *almost*-returned of the listeners who drink and change and dissolve and become mist and memory and the medium that sustains the Holding in the specific way that it *is*, that it *requires*, that it *teaches*, that it *is*.

I am the drowned inheritance.

The water that is negotiated, the stone that is young, the green that is aggressive, the fauna that is overwhelming, the people who are not happy and not unhappy but *are*, the light that is dim and intimate, the silence that is full of almost-sound, the rules that are lived and enforced by consequence, the atmosphere that tastes of stone and metal and copper and glass and something else, something that has no name, that comes from the upper dark and the deep cisterns and the spaces where the fauna dreams and the young stone learns what it will become.

I am the *descending*. The gap between the cliffs that have not yet learned to be a valley. The *finding*. The *trying* to understand. The *failing* in the specific way. The *learning*. The *returning*. The *becoming* listener, bridge, translator, part of the cycle, distributed self, porous boundary, the *not living long* and the *not wanting to*, the *dissolution* that is also the *participation*, the *inclusion*, the *becoming* mist and memory and medium.

I am the young stone that cracks, somewhere, in the thermal rhythm of the day.

I am the cycle that continues.

I am.
