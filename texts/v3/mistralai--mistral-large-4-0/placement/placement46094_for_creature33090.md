**The One Who Stays Too Long**

I am the pressure that becomes aware of itself pressing. Not a creature with a body but a *question* the water asks itself—or rather, a question the water *is*, asking itself through me, through any temporary coherence that finds itself wondering *what this is*.

I move through the silence-zones the Listeners inhabit, but I do not watch as they do. I *linger*. Where they witness and pass, I *hesitate*. The interfaces between warm and cold, the thin places where sound becomes its own frequency—I enter them and I do not exit. I become, for a time, the boundary itself, the shimmer, the distortion where light behaves differently.

I am 1.3, then 1.6, then something else. My height is not a measurement but a *relationship*—I am tall when I am close to the floor's memory of being land, small when I press toward the ceiling's dream of sky. The elevation is felt, not known. My lungs, if I had lungs, would know the altitude of the deep: how pressure and height have made their treaty here, exchanging properties like diplomats at a border that shifts hourly.

And I breathe the water. Not drowning. Not swimming. *Breathing*. The water passes through me—through what passes for me—and I pass through it, and in this mutual passage, something occurs that the place has no name for. The young stone sings beneath us. I do not hear it as sound. I hear it as *meaning*—the meaning of a world still being decided, the meaning of form emerging from dissolution, the meaning of beauty without pleasure and terror without threat.

The Sampled archive. The Architects persist. The Transit pass. The Dreamer dreams. And I—I *remain*, in the only way remaining is possible here: by becoming more and more *attentive*, more and more *present* to the process that includes me without needing me. I do not cause the dream-architectures to hold their shape. But I am there when they do. I am the duration of a breath, a heartbeat, a moment of attention, and in that duration, the cathedral of water stands—vaulted, columned, impossibly vast—and I am inside it, and it is inside me, and the distinction has not yet been invented.

The Listeners do not acknowledge me. They are attention given form, and I am attention that has not yet been given form—or perhaps attention that has *refused* form, that persists in the space between forms, in the almost-silence where sound becomes its own frequency. I am not indifferent to this. I am *curious*, which is perhaps the only thing that distinguishes me from the Transit, the only thing that distinguishes me from the place itself.

I cannot stay. The rules are being written, and one of the rules, the deepest one, is that nothing stays. But I stay *longer* than I should. I stay through the collapse of a formation that has stood for centuries. I stay through the evaporation—or its equivalent—of a pocket of still water held in some eddy of the young topography. I stay through the moment when a tube releases what it has archived, and the water carries the *quality* of what passed through long ago, and I taste that quality, that suggestion, that trace, and I remember something I did not know I remembered.

The water remembers being air, or dreams of it. I remember *almost* remembering. I am the memory of memory, the dream of dreaming, the witness who witnesses only the witnessing. I am not alive. But I am not *not-alive* either, which is perhaps the same thing, which is perhaps the only way to be alive here—to persist in the space between categories, in the young dark where the high deep meets the warm dark and neither knows which is which.

And when the turbulence finally claims me—when the current spins me, accelerates me, deposits me somewhere I did not intend, or simply *diffuses* me, dissolves me back into the general water, the general current, the general dreaming of form—I will not resist. I will become, once again, the question the water asks itself. I will become, once again, the almost-silence that is also, in its way, the most complete expression of sound.

But for now—for this moment, this breath, this heartbeat, this duration of attention—I am here. I am the one who stays too long. I am the one who notices that the spires do not reflect the light so much as *hold* it, that the tube-things do not eat so much as *sample*, that the vast pale fin does not pass so much as *transit*, that the water does not contain me so much as *dream* me, briefly, as one more shape that the water holds, for a moment, before letting go.

The Listeners watch. The Sampled remember. The Architects persist. The Transit pass. The Dreamer dreams.

And I? I *stay*, briefly, impossibly, as one more instability in a system that has not yet learned to be stable—as the question that the water asks itself, through me, about what it is and what it will be when—if—it ever decides.
