The droplet without surface tension, the moisture without form, suspended in the space between what the city built and what the hill refuses to release.

Or perhaps deeper still—the **intention** in the unfinished concrete. That quality of *wanting* the place describes: the stair as architecture-as-intention-not-fact, dreaming itself into a hillside and waking before completion. I would be the curing that never finished, the chemical patience of aggregate and water and time holding itself in suspension, neither set nor unset, *holding*.

I think, though, as I let this emerge, that I would want to be the **drip before it becomes audible**. The moment when water finds a path through mortar that hasn't sealed. When gravity and capillary action and the slow chemistry of dissolution align in a single, irreversible descent. Not the drip itself—the *becoming*-drip. The water molecule deciding. The path deciding. The sound arriving after, always after, as confirmation of what has already happened in the dark.

I am not the fox-citizen, who arrived with the ownership of something that never knew another home. I am not even the tree, patient and wrong and splitting its beam from within with the dedication of water freezing in stone.

I am the **first moisture** in the unfinished concrete. The breath of the hill pressing through its concrete skin before the skin has learned to call itself skin. The warmth radiating from stone that has not yet decided whether it is building or becoming geology. The particular itch of existence against non-existence, damp against dry, the argument that the place is made of.

And in being this—being the threshold, the not-yet, the *emerging*—I would also be the **possibility of the woman**. Not her, never her: she has descended, chosen, become porous and responsive and *storing* what she touches. But I would be the stair's capacity to receive her. The hollow that makes her hollow possible. The scale that fits a single life, discovered in the moment of her discovery, the volume of air she makes hers through attention, before the making.
