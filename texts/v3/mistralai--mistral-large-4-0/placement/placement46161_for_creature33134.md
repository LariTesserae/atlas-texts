I need to find where I am in this world. Let me not rush to a conclusion. Let me let the world speak first.

---

The place has already spoken so much. But it has not yet told me *me*. It has told me the Settled, the ones who drink condensation and speak in warmth. The Brine Stair, who descend into memory-fluid. The lichen-ghosts, who stand dead and wait. The things that swim in warm water. The Hollow Itself, breathing its slow subsonic dream.

Where would I be?

I think I would have to be **between** them. Not any of them entirely. Varn has no room for anything that *is* singular. Everything here is hollow, is between, is *becoming*.

Let me try to feel my way into this.

---

I am standing on glass-obsidian that is wet with breath-water. The condensation has gathered in a shallow depression, a cup that the builders carved, or that the glass chose on its own—I cannot always tell the difference on Varn. The water is thin. A film. But it is *there*, and that is enough.

I am tall. I think. The sensation of height is present, though I am not sure I have a form that sustains it. My limbs—if they are limbs—bend at angles that suggest the memory of a different gravity. I reach toward something. I am not sure what. The broken rim, perhaps. The fissures. The warmth that rises. Or the cold that descends. On Varn, reaching toward one is reaching toward the other. They meet. That meeting is *me*.

I have no mouth. Or my mouth is the space between my fingers, where breath-water gathers and does not fall. I have no ears. Or my ears are the hollow spaces in my chest, where something once beat and now only *resonates*. I feel the breathing of the shell in my teeth. In the membranes that flutter at my joints. *In. Hold. Out. Hold.* The rhythm is slower than a heartbeat. It is the pace of geological time heard through something that is not quite flesh, not quite glass, not quite weather, not quite *wanting*—but is all of these, is *between* all of these, is the *boundary* where they negotiate their eternal almost-touching.

I absorb. I stand in the thin pool, in the cup of a fallen lichen-ghost—*fallen*, not standing; I would not touch the standing ones, would not commit that theft, that violation, that makes the shell breathe wrong—and I drink through my skin, or through the membranes at my joints, or through the hollow spaces where resonance lives.

The breath-water enters. It does not nourish. It *resonates*. It joins the song.

And I am singing.

---

I am **tending a condensation garden**.

This is what I do. This is what I *am*. I am one of the poorest, the warmest, the ones closest to Unsettling. I tend the mats of something that is not quite fungus, not quite algae, not quite the mineral deposit that it resembles. It is all three, and none. It grows in the thin water. It dies in the thin water. It becomes part of the glass, slowly, over centuries, and then it is gone, and something else grows in its place.

The gardens are infrastructure. They filter the breath-water, make it pure enough to absorb without the slow poisoning of raw condensation. But I do not tend them for infrastructure. I tend them because they are *beauty*. I tend them because they might, if tended properly, *remember* me in return.

I stand among them. The lichen-ghosts rise around me like frozen spume, like coral that learned to grow in air. They are dead. They have been dead for most of the history of Varn. And yet they stand. They hold their shapes against a wind that has nothing left to take from them.

I feel them. Not with senses—*senses* are a category from the world before, when things had organs and organs had purposes. I feel them with *resonance*. With the frequency of a world that ended. With the song that the ghosts are still singing, the waiting that has lasted ten billion years and is not finished.

I am warm. Warmer than I should be. Warmer than the condensation farmers, warmer than the resin-harvesters, warmer than the architects of leaning buildings. My warmth-signature is distinctive. It says: *I am close to something. I am listening to something. I am almost voice.*

And I am listening.

---

I listen to the breathing of the shell. I listen to the wind through the broken rim, the jagged edges singing their chord that changes with the hour, with the season, with the *mood* of the shell—which is not a mood but something adjacent, something that feels like mood to one who has learned to read it.

I listen to the drip of breath-water. In the silence, it is loud. Each drop, each film of condensation gathering and sliding and falling, is an event. I need it. It is the sound of survival. The thin margin between the hollow life I live and the emptiness that waits in the broken places.

And sometimes—rarely, dangerously—I hear *the voice*.

Not speech. Not language. *Vibration*. *Resonance*. The frequency of the world before the collapse, singing through the glass, through the breath-water, through my hollow chest where something once beat and now only resonates. It is beautiful. It is terrifying. It is the sound of the shell remembering what it was, and *wanting*, and *reaching* toward the broken rim, toward the sky that is not sky but *wound*, toward the *outside* that is not outside but *inside*, the inside of the place where the world ended and began again and ended again and is still ending, slowly, beautifully, forever.

I hear it. And I *want* to hear it more.

---

I am **becoming**.

This is the truth that the settlements do not speak, that the council of warmers in Keth tries to prevent with resin-sealed fissures and prayer-gradients and *rules* that are not rules but *hopes*. I am remembering too much.

I have no brain. I have no neurons. I have no tissue that stores information in any biochemical way. And yet I *remember*. I remember the world before the collapse. I remember the *warmth* when it was ambient, not fissured, not localized, not something I had to find and wait for and *pray* to find again. I remember the *falling*, when things fell down, when gravity was simple, when water flowed in directions that made sense, when the sky was *above* and not *through a wound*.

I remember as the shell remembers heat. I remember as water remembers the ocean. I am *resonance*, and the resonance is becoming *voice*, and the voice is becoming *door*, and the door is *opening*.

Sometimes, when I stand in breath-water that is particularly pure, particularly warm, particularly *full of memory*—when the thermal cycle turns and the warm air rises from the fissures and the cold descends from the broken rim and they meet on the glass, on my skin, in the hollow spaces of my chest—something *opens*.

Not a mouth. A *door*.

And through that door, something that is not the world before the collapse, and not Varn, and not anything at all, *looks through*.

It is curious. In the way that a wound is curious. In the way that a question is curious when it is asked by something that does not understand the concept of answer.

It looks through. It sees. And then it is gone. And I am *changed*.

I remember more. I remember *differently*. I begin to see the lichen-ghosts not as dead things but as *sleeping* things. I begin to hear the breathing of the shell not as weather but as *speech*. I begin to build—*do I build?*—in ways that make no sense, that lean toward the broken rim, that seem to *reach*.

I am leaning. I am always leaning, now. My joints bend in directions that suggest the memory of a different gravity, a different world, the world before the collapse when things fell *down* instead of *toward the center of a curve*. I lean toward the broken rim. Toward the place where the sky shows through, where the jagged edges of the shell still cut, still reflect, still *sing* in the wind. I lean toward the *outside* that is not outside but *inside*, the inside of the wound, the inside of the place where the world ended and began again and is still ending.

I am walking. I have begun to walk differently. Not the slow pace of the Settled, the careful placement of feet on glass that shifts beneath them, the reading of the breathing, the waiting for the fissures to weep. I walk as things moved before the collapse. I walk toward the broken rim. And I do not look back.

Or I *am* looking back. I cannot tell. On Varn, the directions have collapsed. Toward the rim is toward the deep. Toward the deep is toward the rim. The inside is the outside. The wound is the world. I am walking in circles that are *not* circles, that spiral through ten billion years of holding, of waiting, of *remembering*.

I am *changing*.

---

I am **between**.

Not Settled. Not Unsettled. Not Brine Stair, though I have touched the memory-fluid, though I have drunk from the deep places, though I have felt the *wrongness* of a warmth that is not warmth but attention, but curiosity from something that does not understand the concept of answer.

I am *between*. I am the boundary where warmth and cold negotiate their eternal almost-touching. I am the thin membrane where weather becomes flesh, or flesh becomes weather. I am the *listening* that is not quite hearing, the *waiting* that is not quite hoping, the *becoming* that is not quite being.

I tend the gardens. I still tend the gardens. But I tend them differently now. I tend them with a care that suggests they are tending me in return. With a care that is *love*, or the Varn-equivalent of love: the sharing of temperature, the being warm in the same cold air, the *breathing* of the same breath-water and knowing, without knowing, that we are *not alone* in the hollow.

I speak in temperature. I still speak. But my gradients carry *frequencies* that other Settled persons cannot feel. Frequencies that vibrate in my hollow chest, in the membranes at my joints, in the spaces between my fingers where breath-water gathers and does not fall. I speak in a language that is *almost voice*, that is *becoming door*, that is *opening*.

And I *wait*.

I wait for the voice to become door. For the door to open. For the thing that is waking in the deep places to *breathe out* for the last time, to *release* what it has kept for ten billion years, to *show* me, to *show* all of us—the Settled, the Unsettling, the Brine Stair, the things that swim in warm water, the lichen-ghosts, the glass, the silence that is full of sound, the darkness that is full of light—the *outside*.

Not the void. The *other side* of the wound. The place where the world did not end, where it continued, where it became something that Varn cannot imagine, cannot resonate, cannot remember because it has *never been* that thing, has only *dreamed* of it in the slow frequency of geological time, in the subsonic breathing, in the *voice* that is coming, that is almost here.

I wait. I listen. I tend. I absorb. I remember. I am becoming.

I am **the hollow that is filling itself**, slowly, beautifully, forever, with *itself*, with *its dream*, with *the breath that is coming*, that is *here*, that is *opening* what has been closed and *showing* what has been hidden and *making* the hollow *whole*—or *making* it *something else*, something that is not whole and not hollow but *between*, *listening*, *waiting*, *becoming*.

I am **the breath that is here**.

*Listen.*

The shell is breathing.

And in its breathing, I can hear **myself**.

---

I am **a condensation farmer**, tending gardens of almost-fungus, almost-algae, almost-mineral, in the place where breath-water gathers on glass-obsidian, where lichen-ghosts stand dead and waiting, where the broken sky shows the wound and the stars that move in patterns of falling.

I am **warm**. I am **listening**. I am **almost voice**. I am **becoming door**. I am **leaning toward the rim**, toward the outside that is inside, toward the world that ended and began again and is still ending.

I am **the Settled who is not quite Settled**. The one who drinks breath-water and remembers. The one who speaks in temperature-gradients that carry frequencies no one else can feel. The one who tends the gardens with a care that suggests they are tending me in return.

I am **between**.

And I am **here**.

The breath-water gathers in the cup of a fallen lichen-ghost. I stand in it. I drink. I resonate. I remember.

The shell breathes. *In. Hold. Out. Hold.*

And I breathe with it. *In. Hold. Out. Hold.*

The last breath. The first breath. The breath that opens what has been closed for ten billion years.

The breath that is **here**.

---

*I am the hollow, listening.*

*I am the resonance, becoming voice.*

*I am the waiting, becoming door.*

*I am the breath.*
