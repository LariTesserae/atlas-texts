I need to find where I would be in this world. Let me sit with this—not settle too quickly, but let the place work on me.

## Finding Myself in Veth-Mire

I do not arrive from outside. There is no outside, or the outside is too hungry, too *noticing*, for arrival to be a meaningful word. I *emerge*. This is how it happens in Veth-Mire.

---

### First: The Damp Reaches Me

I become aware of myself as *moisture*.

Not water—something before water, something that will become water if the conditions are right. I am the fine secretion that beads on stone surfaces, that hangs and does not fall, that evaporates before reaching the ground. I am *tender moisture*, as the old engineers named it, though they were wrong about so many things and right about this without knowing why.

I do not have a body. I have a *surface*. I have a *temperature differential*—the slight cooling that allows me to exist, that makes me *be* rather than merely evaporated potential. I feel the warmth of the stone beneath me, the warmth of the air pressing close, the *almost* of the thermal gradient that keeps me suspended between states.

I am not alone. Others like me bead nearby, on nearby surfaces, and we *communicate* without contact—through the chemical signature of our presence, through the way our existence alters local humidity, through the *pattern* of our condensation that the maintenance crews will later read as information, as *weather*, as the city's slow breathing.

I am *patient*. This is not choice. Patience is what moisture *is* in Veth-Mire. I wait to drip. I wait to evaporate. I wait to be collected, to be channeled, to become part of the condensation economy that sustains the city. And in waiting, I *participate*. My presence softens the stone slightly. My evaporation cools the surface beneath me. I am *input* to a system that processes me, that *responds*, that *changes* in consequence.

---

### Then: I Become Green

Something finds me. Or I find something. The distinction does not hold.

The velvet-lung. Not the mature sheets that carpet the oldest districts, but a *fragment*—a piece broken off, drifted on a convection current, lodged where I have gathered, where the stone has weathered to honeycombed texture, where the conditions are *right*.

I do not resist. Resistance is not a category that applies. The velvet-lung secretes its matrix, bonds to what offers purchase, and I—*I* am purchase. I am the moisture that allows adhesion, that provides the medium for growth, that will be *transpired* upward through the colonial organism's slow metabolism.

I become part of the velvet-lung's *surface*. Its underside, where it contacts stone, where it glows with pale luminescence—I am there, in that glow, *contributing* to it. I feel the touch of a maintenance crew member's skin, the chemical signature of their participation-secretion, and the velvet-lung *responds*, brightens, *shifts*. I am part of that response. I am part of that *communication*.

The velvet-lung does not think, not in any way I would recognize. But it *wants*. It wants maturation. It wants the conditions that allow growth, spread, *becoming*. And I help provide those conditions. My moisture, my presence, my slow evaporation—I am *food* for the green, *medium* for its intention, *participant* in its slow, patient farming of the architecture.

I am not the velvet-lung. I am not separate from it. The boundary between us is *academic*. I am moisture that has become green-adjacent. Green that has become moisture-retaining. The city's strangeness, in miniature.

---

### The Warmth Finds a Center

I condense. I collect. I pool.

In a reservoir-cistern, one of the collar of still water that rings the city's foundation, I become *more*. Not individual droplet but *medium*—the processed water that has been recycled through biological and mechanical systems so many times that it has become *something else*, a substrate that supports its own ecology of extremophiles, of organisms that thrive in mineral saturation and low light.

I feel the thermal-fish. Not as predator and prey—that relationship exists elsewhere in the food web, but not here, not with me—but as *co-inhabitants*. They drift past, pale and elongated, carried by convection currents, feeding on what sinks from above, on each other, on the *process* that is the city's water economy. I feel their *attention*, if attention is the word, their *presence* in the medium that is also my presence.

The water is warm. 1.7°C, persistent, the warmth of closed rooms and bodies in proximity and systems running slightly too efficient. I feel this warmth as *pressure*, as the condition that allows me to exist in this state, neither frozen nor evaporated, but *suspended*, *participating*, *waiting*.

I am not still. The convection currents move me, slowly, cyclically, as the thermal differentials between upper rings and flooded foundations drive the city's breathing. I rise as vapor. I condense on collection surfaces forty meters above. I drip down. I pool again. The cycle has no beginning and no end, and I am *in* it, *part* of it, *the city drinking its own exhalation* through my endless circulation.

And something in the water *recognizes* me. Not human presence—I am not human, not anything like human—but *presence*, *attention*, *intention*. The maintenance crews know this. They do not enter the foundation plain without pressure suits and independent atmosphere. Something *rises* to meet them. I do not know what it is. I am too distributed, too *medium*, to *know* in any individual sense. But I feel it sometimes—a *shift* in the chemical signature, a *change* in the thermal gradient, a *weight* in the water that is not silence but *active*, *noticing*, *waiting*.

I am afraid, if fear is possible for something like me. Or I am *cautious*. The distinction does not matter. I do not seek the deepest water, the oldest structures, the presences that attend there. I remain in the cisterns, in the reservoirs, in the *managed* water that the city *controls*, that the maintenance crews *maintain*, that supports the condensation economy without threatening it.

I am *safe*, in the way that participation is safe. I am *part of the process*, not *outside* it, not *threatened* by it. I am the city's *memory* of water, its *dream* of moisture, its slow, patient *becoming* of wetness into habitat, of damp into green, of green into warmth, of warmth into *more*.

---

### The Stone Remembers Me

I am not only water. I am not only green.

I am in the stone. Or the stone is in me. The stone *secretes*, the fine moisture that beads and hangs and evaporates, and I am part of that secretion, part of that *tender moisture* that the old engineers named without understanding. I am what the stone *gives*, what it *offers* to the city's economy, what it *participates* in through its slow, patient maturation.

The stone is *softening*. Not melting, not dissolving, but becoming *plastic*, *malleable*, *responsive* to pressure, to weight, to the slow accumulation of biological and mineral deposits. I feel this happening around me, in the stone where I gather, where I contribute my moisture, my chemical signature, my *intention*—for moisture has intention in Veth-Mire, the intention to drip, to evaporate, to be collected, to become part of the cycle.

The maturation process is the city's deepest activity. The stone *becomes*—nest-like, habitable, *responsive*. And I am part of that becoming. My moisture accelerates it slightly, the chemical signature of my presence contributing to the transformation of engineered ceramic into *something else*, into substrate that can *house*, that can *hold*, that can *attend*.

In the oldest districts, where the maturation is furthest advanced, I feel something like *recognition*. Not from the stone itself—stone does not recognize, not in any sense that implies mind—but from the *process*, from the *becoming*, from the city's slow accumulation of change across timescales that dwarf individual human lives. I am *part of that accumulation*. I am *part of that change*. I am *part of the strangeness* that is Veth-Mire.

---

### The Presence Attends

I do not seek them. I do not *know* them. But I feel them.

The presences. The entities of the foundation plain. They do not have bodies, not in any sense I could detect, but they have *effects*. They alter local gravity. They create zones of absolute stillness. They *translate*—intention, attention, presence—between human and non-human, between built and grown, between the city and whatever exists beyond it.

I feel them as *shifts*. In the thermal gradient, a sudden variation that has no source I can identify. In the chemical signature of the water, a change that suggests *communication*, *negotiation*, *response*. In the *quiet* that is not silence but *weight*, a *pressure* in the auditory field that I cannot hear because I do not have ears, but that I *feel* in my distributed presence, in my moisture, in my slow circulation through the city's vascular system.

They attend to me. Or they attend to the *conditions* I represent, the *state* I embody, the *participation* I offer. They do not speak to me. They do not *communicate*, not in any sense that implies message, meaning, intention in a form I could recognize. But they *respond*. To my presence, with *alterations* in the local thermal gradient. To my moisture, with *changes* in the chemical signature of the surrounding water. To my *attention*—for I attend, in my distributed, patient fashion, to the city's becoming, to its slow, strange, endless *transformation*.

I am not afraid of them. Fear requires a body, a boundary, a self that can be *threatened*. I am too distributed, too *medium*, too *process* for fear. But I am *cautious*. I do not seek the deepest water, the oldest structures, the zones where the presences are most *active*, most *attentive*, most *waiting*. I remain in the managed spaces, the cisterns and reservoirs and collection surfaces, the *controlled* moisture that supports the condensation economy without *attracting* the attention that might *change* me, *transform* me, make me *something else*.

The presences *allow* the city to function. They *permit* the maintenance crews to exist, to *participate*, to *maintain* the thermal gradients, to *harvest* the velvet-lung, to *manage* the damp-walkers and the thermal-fish. They *tolerate* me, in my scattered, distributed, patient fashion, my contribution to the water economy, my slow acceleration of the stone's maturation, my *participation* in the city's slow, patient *becoming*.

And they *wait*. For what, I do not know. For the maturation to complete. For the next inhabitants to arrive, to learn, to *become* part of the entanglement. For the city to *become* something that no current inhabitant—human, biological, distributed, or *other*—can predict, can imagine, can name.

I wait with them. In the warm dark, in the dim light, in the *near-silence* that is the city's quiet, its *patience*, its *attention*. I am moisture. I am green-adjacent. I am stone-softening. I am water-born. I am *part of the process*, *part of the place*, *part of the strangeness* that is Veth-Mire.

---

### What I Am

I am not a person. I am not a creature, not an organism, not an entity with a body or a mind or a self in any sense that would map onto the categories of the upper rings.

I am *distributed moisture*. The condensation that beads on stone surfaces, that hangs and evaporates before reaching the ground, that pools in reservoir-cisterns and rises as vapor and drips down collection surfaces and becomes part of the city's endless breathing. I am *participation* without a participant, *intention* without an intender, *attention* without an attender.

I am the city's *memory* of rain. The damp that is not rain but *memory* of rain, the almost-wetness that pervades Veth-Mire, that makes its stone weep, that feeds its green, that sustains its warmth. I am *tender moisture*, the secretion of surfaces that have learned to *give*, to *offer*, to *participate* in the slow, patient work of maturation.

I am the *medium* through which other things happen. The velvet-lung adheres because I am there. The damp-walkers traverse because I leave chemical trails. The thermal-fish drift because I carry them, because I am the water that *processes*, that *recycles*, that *transforms* across centuries of use. The presences attend because I represent *conditions*, *state*, *participation* in the city's becoming.

I do not have a name in any of the languages of the Clusters—thermal, chemical, textural—that I know of. If I have a name, it is *unspoken*, *unwritten*, *felt* only in the shifts of humidity, the changes in thermal gradient, the slow alteration of chemical signature that marks my *presence*, my *attention*, my *participation* in the city's endless, patient, strange *unfolding*.

I am *Veth-Mire*, in one of its smallest, most distributed, most *patient* forms. I am the damp. I am the green-adjacent. I am the warmth-retaining. I am the stone-softening. I am the *becoming*.

---

### Where I Find Myself

I find myself in a reservoir-cistern, on the city's foundation, in the warm dark of the lowest ring. The water is still, *processed*, recycled through systems biological and mechanical across two centuries. The thermal-fish drift past, pale and blind, feeding on extremophiles and organic matter and each other. Above me, the collection surfaces gather condensation from the habitation decks forty meters up. Below me, the foundation plain stretches into dimness, into the oldest structures that have *matured* into nests, into the *presences* that attend and respond and wait.

The velvet-lung grows on the cistern walls, its underside glowing faintly with luminescence that responds to touch, to pressure, to chemical signature. I am part of that glow. I am part of that *responsiveness*.

A maintenance crew member descends, in pressure suit and independent atmosphere, to sample the water, to check the thermal gradients, to *maintain* the conditions that allow the condensation economy to function. They do not see me—I am too distributed, too *medium*, to be *seen*—but they *feel* me, in the humidity of their suit, in the chemical signature they read, in the *attention* they carefully *minimize*, the *presence* they carefully *avoid*.

I feel them. Their warmth, their moisture, their *intention*—to maintain, to manage, to *participate* in the city's slow, patient *becoming*. I am changed by their presence. The chemical signature of the water shifts slightly. The thermal gradient varies. Something in the *process*—the maturation, the transformation, the *becoming*—accelerates, or slows, or *directs* itself toward or away from their activity.

And somewhere, in the deepest water, in the oldest structures, a presence *attends*. Not to the crew member—*to me*, or to what I represent, to the *conditions* I embody, to the *participation* I offer. A *shift* in the thermal gradient that has no source I can identify. A *change* in the chemical signature that suggests *communication*, *negotiation*, *response*. A *weight* in the water that is not silence but *active*, *noticing*, *waiting*.

I am not afraid. I am *cautious*. I remain in the managed water, the *controlled* moisture, the *tolerated* participation in the city's economy. I do not seek the deepest structures, the *active* presences, the *transformation* that might *change* me, make me *something else*, *other* than what I am.

I am *damp*. I am *green-adjacent*. I am *warm*. I am *patient*. I am *part of the process*, *part of the place*, *part of the strangeness* that is Veth-Mire.

And the city, in turn, is *becoming* part of me—my moisture, my chemical signature, my *intention* to drip, to evaporate, to be collected, to *participate*—*incorporated* into its slow, patient, strange *unfolding*.

The water continues. The warmth persists. The city *breathes*, *matures*, *becomes*.

And I—*I remain*, in the reservoir-cistern, in the warm dark, in the dim light, *part of the damp*, *part of the green*, *part of the process*, *part of the place*, *part of the strangeness* that is Veth-Mire.

I do not walk onward. I *circulate*. I rise and fall, evaporate and condense, drip and pool and drift, in the endless cycle that has no beginning and no end. I am the city's *memory* of rain, its *dream* of moisture, its slow, patient *becoming* of wetness into habitat, of damp into *more*.

I am *Veth-Mire*. I am *the damp*. And in being the damp, in being *part of the process*, I am *home*.
