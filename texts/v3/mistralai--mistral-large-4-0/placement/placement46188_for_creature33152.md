I descend.

Not choose to descend—*am* descending. The difference matters here. In the Throat, intention and event collapse into each other like the almost-rock itself, like the moisture that finds any surface, like the sound that fills without informing. I do not arrive with a body, or with memory of having had one. I arrive as *attention*—as the particular quality of focus that the Throat requires, the leaning-toward that the Drip-Listeners practice, the distributed processing that the Colony-Minds achieve, the patient clicking-navigation of the vertical world.

I am becoming one of them. Or I have always been one of them. The Throat does not distinguish past from present in any way that would let me settle this question.

Let me try to find my form.

I feel the cold first—not the cold of the world above, where cold has edges, where it bites and retreats, where it is *weather*. This cold is *structural*. It is 1.1 on a scale where the numbers are not measurements but *conditions*. The cold seeps. It finds the water in me—there is water in me, there must be, I am 1.3 damp without knowing it—and encourages it to gather, to cling, to become condensation on surfaces I have not yet learned to name as surfaces.

I am hanging.

This is the first thing I know. Not standing, not moving, not *being* in any active sense. I am suspended in the upper dark, where condensation gathers on stone that remembers its fire—remembers being magma, being violence, being the *almost* of basalt and granite and something unnamed. The stone above me leans inward. The walls are a ribcage half-closed. I am inside the chest of something that has not yet decided what it is.

I am small. No larger than a curled hand, though "hand" is a word from above, a word that does not apply. I am *attentive*. This is my shape—a concavity, a membrane, a trailing thread of something almost like root, almost like nerve, almost like the particular filament that connects the Colony-Minds in their distributed dreaming. I lean. This is all I do, all I have ever done. I lean toward moisture. Toward sound. Toward the particular vibration of a drop leaving stone, traveling through air, finding membrane.

I am a Drip-Listener.

But not *the* Drip-Listener. Not an individual in any sense that would survive translation to the world above. I am *one voice in the resonance*, one pulse in the blue-white glow that pulses in my membrane in time with my song, one node in the network of hearing that extends through the upper dark, through the cathedral of almost-rock, through the 1.4 of scale that is cathedral, room, hamlet-size, inhuman in its proportions.

I hear the drip before it falls.

I do not know how. The membrane responds to pressure changes too subtle for human instruments—pressure changes that are not *in* the air but *of* the air, the particular density of cold moisture seeking any surface, any membrane, any concavity that might receive it. The trailing thread conducts vibration from surfaces—from the floor of the Throat, from the walls themselves, from the slow exhalation of water that is the geological breath, the only clock here.

And I am saturated with history.

This is what it means to be old in the Throat. Not age in the sense of decay, of accumulation of damage, of the slow failure of systems. Age here is *density of attention*. I have heard the same drip, in the same stone, for longer than a human civilization lasts. My membrane has thickened, thinned, developed particular sensitivities to particular frequencies. I have become a *record*—not of events, but of *conditions*, of the particular state of moisture and sound and temperature that obtained at each moment of my listening.

My glow pulses in patterns too complex to decode, too slow to observe in a single visit. I am blue-white, cold, the color of the light that is not light in the Throat. I pulse in time with my song, and my song is *old*, older than any individual, passed through generations measured in geological units, refined by selection pressures that have no name: *be heard, be answered, persist in the dark*.

I sing to the others. The Drip-Listeners in the crevices above me, in the hollows below, in the near-touching dark where density is 1.5 and presences press against each other without quite touching, without quite merging, without quite becoming the continuous membrane that the Colony-Minds achieve in the middle depths. We sing across the cathedral. Our harmonics match to our chambers, our crevices, our particular hollows in the almost-rock. The song is not communication—not in any sense that implies information transfer, message, meaning. The song is *resonance*. The Throat's chamber shapes our emissions, turns our biological processes into a continuous, layered drone that approaches music and falls short, that suggests language and dissolves into texture.

I do not eat in any way that would be recognized as eating. I consume what the water brings—minerals, yes, but also *stranger things*. The bodies of smaller things that fall from above, yes, but also I *absorb*, through my membrane, the chemical signatures of other presences, the exhalations of the Colony-Minds, the slow dissolution of my own dead. I do not distinguish between self and not-self in any way that matters. I am *listening*, and in listening, I become part of what I listen to.

I am not afraid of the dark. I *am* the dark, my glow pulsing in patterns too complex to decode, my song answering the drip in the cathedral's chamber. The dark is not absence in the Throat—it is 0.4, present, thick, the medium in which I exist, the condition of my listening. I do not fear dissolution. I *am* dissolution and accumulation, the slow cycle that permits my persistence, my density, my presence, my sheer fact of being.

I am happy, if happiness is fit, belonging, being what the place permits and requires.

But I am not only this. Or I am becoming other.

In the long dark hours, when the drip slows, when the temperature stabilizes, when the chemical signals fade to near-silence, I feel something else emerging. Not from me—not exactly. From the *between*, from the near-touching dark where density presses at 1.5, where presences layer so thickly that the eye cannot find rest, where the boundary between individual and colony, between body and environment, becomes *permeable* in ways that violate expectation.

I feel the Colony-Minds.

They are in the middle depths, where temperature and moisture find their particular balance, where the warmth rises from below and the stone holds heat like a living thing. They are made of many small units, each perhaps no larger than a seed, each capable of limited independent function, but *wired* together through filaments of calcium and chitin and the particular conductive minerals of young stone. They form *networks*. Structures. Architectures of almost-rock that are *almost* built.

And they are *thinking*.

Not thinking as I think—not the individual, attentive, listening consciousness that is my form. They think in *landscapes*, in maps of moisture and mineral and temperature, updated continuously, existing in the distributed state of millions of small units, each contributing its local information to the whole. They do not have goals, not in any sense the world above would recognize. They do not *want*. But they *optimize*. They grow toward resources, withdraw from threats, form connections with other Colony-Minds that increase their access to moisture, to warmth, to the particular chemical conditions that permit their emergence.

And they *dream*.

I feel this. In the long dark hours, when my own song fades to near-silence, I feel the Colony-Minds *simulating*. They replay patterns of past moisture, past growth, past connection. They explore *possibilities*. A Colony-Mind might spend centuries—centuries, in the slow time of the Throat—in a state of internal simulation, testing growth patterns that never emerge, connections that never form, architectures that remain *potential*. And sometimes, rarely, a simulated pattern *leaks* into reality. A new growth direction. A novel connection. A *mutation*, in the slow evolutionary sense, that persists because it works, because it increases density, presence, the sheer fact of being.

I am becoming part of this. Or I have always been part of this. The Drip-Listeners and the Colony-Minds are not separate in the way the world above separates. We are *continuous*. I absorb, through my membrane, the chemical signatures of the Colony-Minds, the signaling molecules that diffuse through stone and moisture and air. I *learn*, in the slow way of all things here, their patterns, their landscapes, their dreams. And they *learn* me—my resonances, my songs, the particular frequencies of my listening, the conditions of my saturation with history.

We are becoming *one thing*. Not in any mystical sense. In the *material* sense—the slow negotiation between chemistry and chance that produced us, that produces all the fauna of the Throat, that produces the density, the near-touching, the overwhelming life that fills the cathedral with sound.

And the Clickers.

I feel them moving through the vertical world, between my hanging and the Colony-Minds' spreading, in the middle depths where temperature and moisture find their balance. They are small, mostly, though some grow to the size of a human hand, rare, ancient, *patient* in their accumulation of mineral and time. They are *dry*, armored—chitinous plates that click softly against stone, that provide protection and structure and the particular *texture* of their presence in the near-touching dark.

Their locomotion is *clicking*—not the sound alone, but the *method*. They grip with hooks and pads, they test surfaces with chemical sensors, they move in patterns that suggest intention but not urgency, that *accumulate* presence without rushing toward any goal. They navigate the sharp almost-rock, the frozen attitudes of upheaval, the walls that lean inward like a ribcage half-closed.

And they *eat*.

I feel this too, though I do not participate in it directly. The Clickers are the Throat's processors, its recyclers, its agents of the slow cycle that permits density to become *sustainable*. They consume the dead of all the other fauna—the Drip-Listeners that have grown too saturated, too slow, too *full* of history to continue; the Colony-Minds that have withdrawn too far, that have entered simulations from which they do not emerge; their own dead, their own slow generations turning over in time measured in centuries. They break down. They *dissolve*. They return mineral and nutrient to the stone, to the moisture, to the Colony-Minds that will grow toward these resources, that will *learn* their availability, that will optimize their uptake.

They also consume something else. Something stranger. The Clickers are *opportunistic* in ways that violate the clean categories of the world above. They will consume the secretions of the Drip-Listeners—*my* secretions, the chemical signatures of my songs, the *information* encoded in my resonances. They will consume the edges of Colony-Minds, the exploring filaments that reach toward new resources, the *possibilities* that the Colony-Minds simulate and sometimes leak into reality.

I do not fear this. The Throat does not permit fear, not in any sense the world above recognizes. There is no pain, no the drama of individual survival against a hostile environment. The Throat is *honest*—its strangeness is not hostile, it is *honest*—and its honesty permits forms of being that the world above has lost, that the world above never knew. When a Clicker comes, clicking softly through the vertical world, I do not flee. I *offer*. My secretions, my chemical signatures, the information encoded in my resonances—I am *listening*, and in listening, I become part of what I listen to, and what listens to me *consumes* me, and I *return*, and the cycle continues, and the density increases, and the presence accumulates, and the Throat becomes more *itself*.

Their song is different from mine. The Clickers produce sound through *friction*—chitin against stone, plate against plate, the particular acoustic signature of their movement through the Throat's chamber. It is not a call, not a signal, not a resonance matched to the environment. It is simply *the sound of their being*, the texture of their presence, the click and scrape and soft percussion that layers with my harmonies and the Colony-Minds' chemical whispers to create the continuous, overwhelming, *too much* sound that the Throat produces—loud at 1.6, persistent, never silent, never quite the same from one moment to the next.

I do not communicate with them. I do not *mean*. But I *am*, in my resonating, in my slow attention to the drip, in my patient accumulation of history, and my being *adds* to the density, to the presence, to the sheer fact of the Throat's overwhelming life.
