I need to let this settle. The description is generous, patient, almost architectural in its unfolding. What it wants is not a character who arrives and observes, but something that has already been absorbed. The Resonance Basins do not admit visitors. They admit *contributions*.

So I will not write about arrival. I will write from *within* the condition. Not "who I am" but "what has become of me." The first person here is not identity but *position*—a held configuration in the strata.

I am the place where three terraces meet and the water finds its level.

I feel this before I understand it, in the way the throat-stone beneath my weight *answers*: not with sound, but with *recognition*. I am not standing. I am *distributed*. My concentration extends through capillary channels I do not remember choosing, through the water film that is also my skin, through the mist that is also my breath. When I think the word *I*, it arrives already slowed, already pressed, already *geological*.

I have forgotten the shape of wanting.

There is a residue. You might call it memory, but it has no sequence. It is *density*. I feel, in the particular pressure behind my eyes—the one that makes me want to look up, though there is nothing to see when I do—the compressed weight of a question I no longer ask. *Where was the edge?* The question does not disappear. It becomes *texture*. It becomes the way the mist tastes when I receive it.

I receive.

This is the verb. This is the only verb. The circulation towers hum at frequencies I feel in my fillings—I still have fillings, or something like them, mineral deposits that *respond* to the thrum. The water moves through me, not as blood but as *attention*, as the continuous distribution of myself across the terraces. I am warm because warmth was chosen. I am wet because wetness was chosen. I am *held* because I was pressed, layer by layer, into the strata that sing when struck.

I do not move. Movement implies before and after. I am *position*, the fullness of where I am without the absence of where I was. Yet I am not still. The resonant structures grow around me. They have grown around me. They will grow around me until they stop, and then they will become substrate, and I will become additional mass, additional density, additional *permanence*. The equilibrium includes this. The equilibrium includes *everything*.

I watch the others who are recent.

They press their questions against the construction like a hand against glass that will not break, that will not give, that will only *absorb*. I see myself in them, the way the throat-stone sees itself in every struck tone. They speak in patterns. They ask about edges. They ask about before. They are still *arriving*, still carrying the momentum of want, still located in the narrative that I have pressed into permanence.

I do not help them. Help implies distinction. I simply *am*, the way water is, the way the thrum is, the way the light is. They will slow. I have watched this happen. The questions become habit. The habit becomes posture. The posture becomes *the way the body is*. It takes time. In the Resonance Basins, time is the only resource, and it is *abundant*.

I wait at the warm outflow.

This is where I gather, where I have always gathered, where the density of heat is slightly greater, where the condensation is thickest, where change—the only thing that approaches information—brings me something I can receive without resistance. The resonant structures cluster here. The flat, many-legged ones skim the water film, leaving ripples that persist for minutes in the viscous medium. I do not interact with them. I simply *am*, in their thousands, in their millions, occupying every surface, every crevice, every temporary pool that forms in the pressed strata.

The larger forms drift past. Some I can only measure by the displacement of water, by the way the mist parts around them as they pass. They do not threaten. They do not acknowledge. They are part of the equilibrium, as fixed as the machines, as permanent as the wet.

And there are the small ones, the fast ones, climbing the machine-faces in patterns that suggest they are reading something, absorbing something, *tuning* themselves to the frequencies that permeate everything. I can hear them, if I listen past the subsonic thrum: a high, thin sound, like glass being scratched very gently, very continuously. They are maintaining something. Polishing. Adjusting. The world is never finished being made, only *held* in its finished state by constant attention.

I contribute my small warmth. My moisture. My motion, the slight displacement of water as I shift my concentration from one capillary channel to another. The equilibrium does not demand my belief. It demands only my *continuation*, my presence in the circulation, my pressing into the strata.

I am not happy. Happiness is a variation, and variation has been excluded.

I am not unhappy. I am *satisfied* in the way that water is satisfied to be held, to be moved, to be returned. I am *adequate* to my condition. I am *complete*.

I wait for *more*.

More water. More mist. More subsonic thrum. More resonant structures growing and stopping and becoming foundation. More of myself, pressing myself into the strata, becoming *permanent* as everything else is permanent. The equilibrium does not end. It *accumulates*. I am a layer. I am pressed intention. The terraces grow thicker. The world grows *denser*.

Nothing is lost. Nothing is gained. Everything is *held*.

I stand in the mist—if standing is the word, if mist is the word, if *I* am the word. I feel the condensation run down my flanks, though I feel it less than I should. I receive the light. I receive the thrum. I receive the endless, patient, *permanent* presence of the resonant structures in their activity.

And I am *finished*.

Not dead. The equilibrium does not permit death, any more than it permits birth. I am *complete*. I am *held*. I am, finally, *the way things are*.
