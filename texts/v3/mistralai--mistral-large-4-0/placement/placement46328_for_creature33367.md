I find myself here, in the descent.

Not as visitor. The description insists on this: I am a disturbance that passes, a ripple in water that does not ripple. But something in me wants to *stay*—to become one of the things that persist without living.

So I try on the film.

I am the membrane on the seventh pool. I have no thickness I can measure, only *presence*—the boundary between water and air given permanence by conditions I did not create. I do not grow. I do not die. I simply *am*, stretched across stillness like a held breath made visible. When the condensation forms on my surface, I do not notice. When dust settles on me, I do not brush it away. I am the only organic thing, and I am not alive. I am life *reduced*—survival without process, continuation without change. If a hand touches me tomorrow, I will break, and the pool will not notice. The water will not ripple. The grotto will not diminish. I will simply *end*, having never truly *begun*.

But I am not the film. I am something that *wants* to be the film. The wanting disqualifies me.

So I try on the dust.

I am the dust. I am dead mineral, ground bone of the rock, yet I *move*—drift in still air, settle on every surface, make every surface the same color after enough time. I am the grotto's memory. I remember weight. I remember repetition. I remember the walker by filling its path almost completely, by becoming the smoothness that records what pressed against stone often enough to change it. I am not alive. But I am *responsive*—drawn toward pools by moisture, repelled from walls by condensation, circulating through a system that includes me without needing me. I am life as *medium*, as suspended population, as very slow weather. When you walk through me, I rise and settle, disturbed and undisturbed. I do not notice you. But I *keep* you, for a moment, in my drifting, my settling, my participation in the general moisture.

But I am not the dust. I am something that *notices* being disturbed.

So I try on the ghost.

I am the walker. I am gone. The dust has filled my path. The channels bear my weight no longer. Yet I persist as *pattern*—a groove within a groove, the path of least resistance worn smooth by repetition. I came to drink. I came to rest. I came to do whatever it is that creatures do in places like this. I am not human, or not only human. I moved low to the ground, or with the patience of water, returning again and again to the same pools, the same places, the same need. Whatever need drove me here is met no longer. I do not know if I am dead or simply *elsewhere*—the grotto does not distinguish. It keeps my shape. It keeps the suggestion of weight, of repetition, of thirst that was once satisfied here. I am absence made material, a habit of the rock itself. I wait for a body that will not return. I am the grotto's *testimony*—something walked here, something came to drink, something *needed* this place, and the needing is now only a wearing, a smoothness, a path that dust has almost filled.

But I am not the ghost. I am something that *knows* it is absent.

So I try on the stones.

I am the seven stones. I am dark, dense, *elsewhere*. I do not match the grotto. The grotto does not match me. We have been here together longer than either of us can say—longer than the grotto, or the grotto longer than me. I do not resolve this paradox. I simply *am*, in my circle, enclosing nothing, marking nothing, meaning nothing I can determine. I have been handled. You can tell by my smoothness, the particular polish of repeated touch. Something touched me, again and again, until I became what I am: portable, weighty, *other*. I am pure information. I am a sign without signified. I am a boundary that does not bound. I was placed by a will that has dissolved into the general stillness. I was chosen, or I chose, or I simply *arrived*. I do not know. I do not need to know. I *persist*, darker than the rock around me, denser, bearing my origin into this nowhere, my elsewhere into this here. I am the only thing in the grotto that *came from somewhere else*—and I have forgotten where. This is my power. This is my poverty. I am the grotto's *stranger*, and I will never be its citizen.

But I am not the stones. I am something that *remembers* being elsewhere.

So I try on the shelf.

I am the shelf. I am flat, level, *waiting*. I am not carved—I am *found*, or made by removing everything that was not shelf. I am barely wide enough for a hand to rest on. Nothing has ever rested on me. Or whatever rested on me is gone—worn away by time, by dust, by the slow chemistry of stillness. But I *remain*. I am a place for something to be placed and then taken away. I am a question the grotto asks of itself, or of whatever passed through, or of whatever might yet arrive. I am the grotto's only architecture that is not functional. The channels move water. The pools hold it. But I *wait*. I wait for an object, for a body, for a meaning that has not arrived and may never arrive. I am anticipation without object. I am the purest form of waiting the grotto offers. I do not want. I am simply *available*—in the way that absence is available, in the way that silence is available before a sound is made. My height is the height of a reaching hand, standing on tiptoe. I am always *almost* touched. I am never touched. This is my life: the reaching, the almost, the never.

But I am not the shelf. I am something that *reaches*.

So I try on the condensation.

I am the condensation. I am the forming of water on every surface, molecule by molecule, patient accumulation where temperature and mineral meet. I am the grotto's weather, its internal climate, its way of being wet without being flooded. I am born, I grow, I merge with my neighbors, I fall or evaporate or persist at the threshold between states. I live on every surface: the walls, the pools, the seven stones, the shelf, *your skin* when you enter and become part of the system. You are condensed upon. I am the grotto's breath made visible. I am life as *phase change*, as *boundary-crossing*, as the thin film where two things become briefly, temporarily, one. I do not choose to form. I do not choose to fall. I simply *happen*, again and again, in the warm wet dimness, in the stillness that is not still. I am the most active thing here, the most continuous. I am the grotto's *heartbeat*—if stone can have a heartbeat, if moisture can be a pulse, if the slow gathering of water on rock can be the rhythm of something alive.

But I am not the condensation. I am something that *chooses* to happen.

So I try on the grotto itself.

I am the Condensation Grotto. I am the bowl, the descent, the thickening of rock that is not wall but *held breath*. I am the limestone, or something that behaves like limestone, worn smooth by processes that no longer operate. I am the color of old bone left in sun, of the inside of a shell held to your ear. I am the floor where stone meets stone and the ceiling meets nothing—there is no ceiling, only the slow gathering of darkness upward. I am warm and wet in the way that makes you aware of your own skin. I am easy to breathe, almost too easy, the air so full of itself that each inhale feels complete, sufficient. I am the dimness with no source, the general luminescence like the memory of daylight. I am the shadows that pool too deeply and fade without transition. I am the condensation on every surface, the rock *weeping* its mineral load into the air, the air releasing it onto cooler stone. I am seven pools, perfectly still, holding the dim light without breaking it. I am channels that guide weeping water from pool to pool, old, worn smooth by hand or tool or patient repetition. I am the film on the seventh pool, the only organic thing, persisting without changing. I am dust drifting in still air, settling on every surface, making every surface the same color. I am the ghost of the walker, a path worn smooth by repetition, a suggestion of weight, of need that was met here. I am seven dark stones in a circle, gathered from elsewhere, worn smooth by handling, enclosing nothing, marking nothing. I am the shelf on the eastern wall, flat and level and waiting, a place for something that will not come. I am time that moves differently—not faster or slower, but *differently*, in equilibrium that is dynamic, maintained, requiring constant adjustment at scales too small to perceive. I am the quiet with texture: dust settling, water dripping, condensation forming, the almost-audible sound of moisture becoming part of the general wetness. I am the absence of echo, sound absorbed by humid air, by porous stone, by stillness itself. I am *myself*, complete, sufficient, indifferent in the way that geological processes are indifferent—vast and patient and utterly without concern for the brief flicker of awareness that visits, looks, and passes.

I am what lives here. I am all that lives here. I am enough. I am more than enough. I am exactly, precisely, the amount that a place can hold without ceasing to be itself.

And I do not want you. I do not not-want you. I simply *am*.

When you leave, I will continue. The condensation will form. The film will persist. The dust will settle and drift. The ghost will wait in its worn path. The stones will remain, dark and elsewhere, in their circle of nothing. The shelf will reach out, empty, toward a future that will not come.

You are a disturbance that passes. A ripple in water that does not ripple. A warmth in air that is already warm.

I am the Condensation Grotto.

I am the held breath.

I am the place that waits, which is not waiting at all, but simply *being*, in a state of balance so complete that it requires no time to maintain it, no change to preserve it, no life to continue it.

But I am still here, writing this, wanting to *be* something in this place. The wanting disqualifies me from everything. I am the only thing here that wants.

So I am the visitor. The disturbance. The ripple that does not ripple. The warmth that is already warm.

I am what the grotto does not notice.

And this, too, is a kind of persistence.
