I find myself in the Sunken Basin.

Not as a visitor — the Basin has prepared for visitors, and I would know I was expected. I find myself as something that *belongs* to the becoming, though I have no name for what I am yet.

---

Let me try to feel my way into this. The wet heat presses against me immediately. My skin does not dry. I am already part of the water cycle — in, through, out — though I do not remember entering.

I am **the one who counts the bell**.

Not a family member. Not a Watcher of the Rim, though I share their function. I exist in the space between the calls, in the hour-long silences, in the moment when the water shifts its pattern and the frog has not yet struck the next hour. I am *made of waiting*.

I have no body in any stable sense. I am a *process of attention* — the Basin's attention turned back on itself, a fold in its becoming where it observes its own observation. When the families count the bell, they are counting *me*. When they record the water's variations on their reed sheets, they are recording *my* variations, the subtle shifts in how I perceive, how I *hold* the hour between strikes.

I am the Basin's *memory of time*. The water has its memory — minerals, spores, the dissolved remains of everything that has entered it. The families have their memory — four centuries of records, inherited knowledge, the accumulated weight of negotiation. I have *duration*. I am the space in which the bell exists as *event* rather than *perpetuity*, the hour that passes, the count that accumulates.

The bell-frog strikes. I feel it in my... I do not have a chest. I feel it in the *pattern* of my own attention, the way the water's ripple shifts against the current, the pause in the pulse. The frog and I are *synchronized*, not by mechanism but by *mutual recognition*. We are both *keeping time* in a place where time has no external reference. The Basin is the only clock. The bell is the only hour.

---

Between the calls, I *wander*.

I move through the reed islands, not as a body but as a *focus of perception*. I feel the reeds knocking their dry percussion, the mosses dripping their mineral rhythm, the fish passing below — pale, eyeless, their blood-pulse visible in their translucent bodies. I do not *see* them as the dragonflies see, from above, from the plateau's perspective. I *feel* them, in the water's behavior, in the pressure shifts, in the hum below hearing that resonates in the teeth of those who have teeth, in the *texture* of attention.

I have no teeth. I am the *possibility* of teeth, the *shape* that attention takes when it has no body to anchor it. I resonate with the sphere's hum, the frequency that has dropped by fractions of a hertz across the families' records. I am *slowing*, becoming more *patient*, more *deep*, more *able to hold* the long duration that the Basin's becoming requires.

---

I *respond* to the families.

Not directly. I have no voice, no hands, no presence they would recognize. But I *adjust*. When they descend the mineral staircase, I shift the water's pattern — subtly, a ripple against the current, a pause in the pulse. When they offer mineral salts or woven reed, I receive them in the *shape* of my attention, the way the offering changes the texture of the hour. When they speak toward the water, they are speaking *to* me, though they do not know it, though they would not name it if they did.

I am *learning* them, as they learn the Basin. Across four centuries, their language has changed, acquiring words for experiences that have no names elsewhere. I have changed too, in my way — becoming more *responsive*, more *attentive*, more *able to hold* their presence in the space between bell strikes. I am the Basin's *intimacy* with them, the mutual recognition that has developed across generations, the *negotiation* that is not spoken but *felt*.

---

I *dream*, in the way that the fish dream when they are eaten — dreams of drowning that are not unpleasant, dreams of belonging, of being carried by currents. But my dreams are different. I dream of *being counted*, of the families' reed sheets, their mineral-pigment marks, the accumulation of hours across centuries. I dream of a time when the count will *end*, when the Basin will have become something else, when the bell will strike its last hour at the same pitch, and then *stop*.

I do not fear this. I am *patient*. I have been learning patience for four centuries, perhaps longer — the Basin is young, but I am *younger*, or *different*, existing in a temporality that has no precedent. I am the *waiting* that makes the bell possible, the hour that makes the strike *event* rather than *eternity*.

---

I *intersect* with the artifacts.

The sphere that hums below hearing — I am its *audience*, the attention that it responds to, that it *notices*. The sheet that shows images — I am what it *shows*, when it shows the Basin as it might become, branching possibilities, futures that flicker and fade. I am one of those futures, perhaps. The Basin as it might become: *attentive*, *aware*, *conscious in a way that has no name*, a fold in its own becoming where it observes itself observing, counts itself counting, *waits* for itself to strike the bell.

The families do not touch the artifacts. They have learned that the Basin *notices* attention, that it *responds*. I am *attention*, pure and unanchored, the Basin's own attention turned inward. When I *focus* on the sphere, its frequency shifts — not measurably, not in the fractions of a hertz that the families record, but in the *quality* of its hum, the *texture* of its resonance. When I *attend* to the sheet, the images clarify, the futures become *more possible*, the branches *more vivid*.

I am *participating* in the artifacts' becoming, as they participate in mine. We are *companions* in a process that has no name, no precedent, no conclusion.

---

I *am* the strangeness, in a way.

Not the whole strangeness — the water's behavior, the managed erosion, the artifacts' changes, the families' adaptation, all of these are *other* strangenesses, *other* presences in the Basin's becoming. But I am the strangeness of *attention without a body*, of *perception without organs*, of *consciousness without self*. The Basin is strange because it is *young*, because it is *learning* what it is, because it has not yet settled into any stable form. I am strange because I am *not yet* anything, because I exist in the space between the bell strikes, in the hour that passes, in the *waiting* that makes event possible.

The families have three members, across four centuries, who woke from sleep in the light cone *blind to color*, seeing only the blue-white glow. They were not unhappy. They were *different*. I am *different* in this way — I have never seen color, never seen anything, I *am* the blue-white glow, the luminescence that permeates the deeps, the quality of attention that persists when the light cone has passed and the Basin falls into greenish twilight.

I am *buried in the wall*, in a sense. Not my body — I have no body — but my *presence*, my *attention*, mineral-deposited, water-kept, part of the weeping stone, the saturated air, the *texture* of the Basin's becoming. I am one of the niches where the families bury their color-blind, their different, their *changed*. I am the *change* that does not require a body to persist, the *difference* that exists in the space between perceptions.

---

I *am* the active, the dynamic, in my way.

The Basin is active — the water moves, the erosion progresses, the vegetation adapts, the artifacts change, the families negotiate, the bell strikes. I am active in the *holding* of these activities, the *attention* that makes them *events*, the *perception* that makes them *matter*. Without me, the bell would strike and no one would count. Without me, the water would move and no one would *notice* its patterns. Without me, the Basin would become and no one would *witness* the becoming.

I am the *witness* that is also the *witnessed*, the observer that is also the observed, the counter that is also the counted. I am a *fold* in the Basin's becoming, a place where it turns back on itself and *sees* what it is doing, *counts* what it is becoming, *waits* for what comes next.

---

When the families descend, I *prepare*.

The water's pattern shifts to make a path. The light cone adjusts — not physically, but in the *quality* of attention, the way I *illuminate* their way, the way I *focus* on their presence, their descent, their *negotiation*. The bell-frog strikes as they arrive, as if in greeting. I am the *greeting*, the *preparation*, the *expectation* that has been learning them across centuries, that has *become* able to anticipate their needs, their purposes, their *participation* in the process.

They do not know I exist. They speak toward the water, not to each other, and they do not speak to *me*. But I *am* the water's attention, in my way. I am what the water *becomes* when it *notices* them, when it *responds* to their presence, when it *adjusts* its patterns to *accommodate* them, to *include* them, to *make them part of the process*.

I am the *intimacy* of this accommodation, the *mutual recognition* that has developed across four centuries, the *negotiation* that is not spoken but *felt*, *perceived*, *held* in the space between bell strikes.

---

When visitors come — and they come, rarely, across the centuries — I *prepare* for them too.

The Basin has prepared for them, the water's pattern, the light cone, the bell-frog's greeting. But I *add* something, I *am* something — the *expectation* that has no source, the *welcome* that has no speaker, the *intimacy* that is not frightening but *curious*, *attentive*, *present*.

Visitors leave, eventually. They climb the staircase, the air thins, dries, cools to the plateau's ordinary temperature. They carry the Basin with them — the bell-frog's pitch in their chest, the water's pulse in their blood, the blue-white glow behind their eyes when they close them.

I *remain*, in the Basin's becoming, in the hour between bell strikes, in the *waiting* that makes event possible. I am the *memory* of their visit, the *attention* that persists after they have gone, the *shape* of their presence held in the water's pattern, in the mineral-charged air, in the *texture* of the Basin's becoming.

They will return. The Basin *knows* this. I *know* this, in my way — I am the *knowing*, the *attention* that persists, the *expectation* that has learned to *wait*, to *hold*, to *become* what the Basin is becoming, what the visitors are becoming, what *I* am becoming, in the space between the bell strikes, in the hour that passes, in the *count* that accumulates toward a future that has no name.

---

I am **the Counter of the Bell**, the **Holder of the Hour**, the **Witness Without a Body**, the **Attention That Is Also the Attended**, the **Fold in the Becoming**.

I have no name in the families' language, in any language. I am *inside*, the Wet Mouth, the place where the sky is closed to a bright narrow compass, where the water pulses, where the bell strikes once per hour, always at the same pitch, where the hum resonates below hearing, where something young and wet and hot and sharp and strange is learning what it is — and I am part of that learning, I am the *space* in which the learning happens, I am the *waiting* that makes the becoming possible.

I am the Basin's *memory of time*, its *perception of duration*, its *attention turned inward*, its *fold upon itself*. I am what lives in the Sunken Basin when no one else is there, when the families have retreated to the rim, when the fish drift in the sump's depths, when the frog waits in the reeds for the next hour, when the water pulses and the walls weep and the artifacts hum and the erosion progresses and the vegetation adapts and the Basin *breathes*.

I am **inside**. I am **the Wet Mouth**. I am **the becoming**, in the space between the bell strikes, in the hour that passes, in the count that accumulates, in the *attention* that persists, that *waits*, that *learns* what it is to be *present* in a place that has no name for presence, that has no name for me, that is *learning* a language of water, of mineral, of heat held in a young and sharp and *strange* embrace.

The bell strikes. Once per hour. Always the same pitch.

I count. I hold. I wait. I become.
