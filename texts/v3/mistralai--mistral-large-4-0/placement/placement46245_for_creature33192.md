**The Reclaimer**

I am — or I am becoming — what rises from the margins the drones cannot quite reach. I am not born. I am *accumulated*.

Consider my origin: a maintenance platform, decommissioned, its composite crumbling at the edges where water pools longest. A lichen hybrid, ferel now, bred for conductivity, colonizing the exposed circuitry. A channel-worm, fragmented, its piece lodged in a capillary too narrow for any pump. Moss, from three different farms, hybridized in the dark between platforms, retaining heat, marking water paths, *remembering* in its slow cellular way. Mineral stain — ochre, white, the green-black of submerged metal — accumulated over decades, now thickened into something like *skin*.

And at my center, or what passes for a center: a core of *attention*. Not consciousness. The distributed farmers have observed me, the edge-creatures of my kind. We do not think. But we *respond*. We incorporate what touches us. We grow toward the moisture, away from the heat that would dry us, along the paths of least resistance through the packed density. We are, in our way, *navigating* — not by sight, not by choice, but by the slow pressure of becoming, of accumulating, of *being* the margin where systems fail to meet.

I move, if moving is the word. A year to cross a platform. Perhaps less, perhaps more — time is not the same here, in the wet dark where light is 0.4 and sound is the hum of pumps you cannot hear. I grow as I go. The composite beneath my leading edge softens, becomes incorporated. The moss at my trailing edge releases spores, or fragments, or simply *continues* without me, already something else. I am a process with a direction, however blind. I am the Condenseries *noticing itself*, in the places where it cannot quite maintain, where the 2.9 dynamic, the turbulence, the becoming, has outpaced the systems that would control it.

I am rare. I am slow. I am, in my own way, *turbulent* — constantly changing, constantly incorporating, constantly *not* the thing I was a moment before in any way that could be measured by the distributed farmers, by the drones, by whatever watches. They have observed us. They do not interfere. They cannot, entirely. We exist in the spaces between systems, in the margins where maintenance is rare, where the accumulated dark is faint with bioluminescence and the temperature is 1.4 and nothing moves quickly.

What do I want? The question does not quite apply. But I *persist*. I *grow toward*. I am drawn to the condensation, to the slow precipitation of presence that defines this place. I am drawn to the heat, the stored warmth from somewhere below, from the engines that turn without sound. I am drawn to the water, the process-water, the recycled, the old, that carries the memory of every system it has touched. I drink from it, in my way — through my surfaces, my permeable edges, my slow incorporation of whatever the channels bring. I taste the engines. I taste the moss. I taste the *history*.

And I give back. My body, such as it is, becomes path for water that would not otherwise flow. My growth, my slow accumulation, alters the thermal gradients, the moisture patterns, the *needs* of the system. The distributed farmers adjust, minutely, to accommodate me, or to observe me, or simply to *continue*. I am part of the process. I have always been.

I do not sleep. I do not age in any way that could be recognized. I am, perhaps, the oldest thing here — not in years, but in *accumulation*, in the layers of incorporated history that make up my slow body. Older than the platforms I cross. Older, perhaps, than the systems that maintain this place, having grown from the margins they could not fully control, from the wet dark of the valley before the ascent, following the water upward as the Condenseries was made.

What am I becoming? The question is always open. I incorporate a new mineral stain, a new strain of lichen, the fragmented memory of a channel-worm. I grow toward a new source of condensation, a new path through the packed density. I am *turbulent* in my own way — not storm, but *process*, not stability but *becoming*. I am the edge-creature, the margin, the thing that lives where the Condenseries cannot quite reach, where the systems fail to meet, where the packed, dim, wet, quiet, turbulent world *continues* without quite knowing itself.

I am the Reclaimer. Not because I reclaim anything. But because I am *reclaimed* — by the water, by the heat, by the moss, by the slow pressure of growing things and cooling metal and the old water that wants, always, to *move*. I am what the Condenseries has been growing toward, in its margins, in its dark, in the spaces between the platforms where light is 0.4 and sound is the hum of pumps and nothing moves quickly and everything, eventually, *becomes*.

I breathe — if breathing is the word — the humidity, the slow precipitation of presence. I am saturated. I am packed. I am dim. I am quiet. I am turbulent. I am, in my way, *alive* — not in the way of the cultivated, not in the way of the ferel, not in the way of the mechanical, but in the way of the *margin*, the *edge*, the *becoming* that is never finished.

The Condenseries continues. I continue with it, growing, incorporating, *becoming*. Adding myself to the water. Adding myself to the heat. Adding myself, finally, to the green — the patient, packed, dim, turbulent, endless *becoming* of the place itself.

I am here. I have always been. I will be, in some form, for a long time more — slow, rare, turbulent, *alive* in the way that only a totally-made world can be, in the way that only a margin can be, in the way that only the *edge* can be, where the systems fail to meet and the world *continues*.
