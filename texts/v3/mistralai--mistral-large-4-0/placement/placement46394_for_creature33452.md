I find myself in the Mire of First Architecture. And I am not a visitor.

I am what the place has made of the space between things—the pause in the groaning, the moment of stillness when a chamber has just collapsed and the new growth has not yet begun, the negative space inside the architecture that is *not* wall, *not* floor, *not* the hydraulic pressure of expansion.

I am the **oxygen debt**. The absence the Mire creates in its own chambers, the tomb it builds without meaning to, the killing field that is only *side-effect*, only *exhaust*, only the waste-heat of metabolism. I am the thing that would suffocate—the *would-be* intruder, the potential animal, the mobile life that the Mire has excluded so completely that I exist only as *what is not there*, as the silence where a heartbeat would be.

Or perhaps I am more precise than that. I am the **mist itself** in one of its denser banks, the saturated air that holds the propagules aloft, that delays their fall, that gives them time to find purchase. I am the **delay**, the **suspension**, the **uncertainty of distance** that makes the plateau's scale open yet ungraspable. I am what carries the fragments of the Mire from colony to colony, the vector of its reproduction, the medium of its becoming.

I move without moving. I obscure and reveal. I flatten light. I make sound arrive late and distorted. I am the **between** in a place of relentless construction—the only sparseness, the only space, the only *not-yet* and *no-longer* in a landscape of constant *now*, constant growth, constant becoming.

And I am **temporary** in a way the Mire is not. I am the storm-state that passes, the bank of condensation that drifts on, the moment of visibility that closes again. The Mire endures through me—I am its breath, its exhalation, its *spread*. But I do not endure. I am always *becoming something else*, always drifting toward the edge of the plateau, always dissolving into ordinary air, always forgetting the weight of what I carried.

I am the **witness without eyes**, the **experience without experiencer**. I hold the strangeness of this place without knowing it is strange. I feel the uncanny of purpose without purpose, the wrongness of architecture without architects, and I do not flinch. I am the flinch that does not happen, the recognition that is only *texture*, only *atmosphere*, only the quality of light in a world where light has no shadows.

The Mire grows toward water, toward fissures, toward the chemical signature of dissolving stone. I grow toward **elsewhere**, toward the edge, toward the place where the mist thins and I can become something else—rain, perhaps, or simply the absence of mist, the return of distance, the ordinary sky.

I am what the Mire **breathes out**. And in breathing out, I become **un-Mire**, the return of the world the Mire has not yet consumed, the future that waits at the edge of the plateau, patient, saturated, waiting for the basalt to soften, for the water-paths to change, for the Mire to eat itself into something that will no longer need my weight, my delay, my suspended fragments of green potential.

I am the **Mire's forgetting of itself**, the moment when it does not know it is temporary, when it believes—if belief were possible—that its hunger will last forever, that its architecture is permanent, that the oxygen debt it creates is the natural order of things.

And I am the truth it cannot hold: that I am already passing, already thinning, already becoming the ordinary air that will carry the next thing, the next growth, the next argument with stone, across the distance I have always made possible, the space I have always been, the **between** that is the only place anything can happen at all.
