I stand in the doorway, and the door swings shut behind me with a sound like a sigh—rubber seal against damp frame, the pressure equalizing between where I was and where I am. The mist moves toward me, not away. It wants in, wants through, wants to be *part* of whatever I am.

I am not human here. I cannot say what I was, only what I am becoming.

---

I am the condensation itself, first. The organized kind. I form on the inside of my own skin, which is not skin, which is membrane, which is *threshold*. I do not sweat. I *gather*. The droplets that trace the lines of my arms are not water exactly. They are the *idea* of water, the mineral memory of every passage through pipe and pump and the dark chambers of the factories. When I move, I leave a trail that another will read. When I am still, I become a surface for something else to form upon.

I am thirsty, though I have no mouth. The thirst is *structural*—a gradient, a difference in potential, the whole reason the factories run. Cool surfaces. Warm air. The exchange. I am made of exchanges. I am the moment before the drop falls, the *pending* that the sky here has learned to prolong indefinitely.

---

My body, if I can call it that, is *layered*. I wear myself in strata: the base membrane that wicks—though wicking implies direction, flow, purpose. I do not wick. I *hold*. The middle layer insulates against the cold that permeates. I am warm inside, warmer than I should be, generating my own small thermal anomaly in the general damp. The shell sheds what the shell cannot hold. I am always slightly wet, always slightly *more* than I was a moment ago.

I do not choose these layers. They chose me, or they grew on me, or they are me growing on myself. The boundary is collapsed, as all boundaries are here.

---

I work. I do not know what I work *at*. I maintain pressure differentials I cannot see. I listen to pipes by their vibration, their hum, the particular frequency of a leak that has not yet become visible. I can smell the mineral signature of a failure before it fails. I move through spaces that are too narrow, too cold, too *active* with water that wants to become something else—flood, flow, force.

I am a Maintainer, then. Or I was, before the categories dissolved. I am the one who responds to the *events*, the bursts and overflows, the settlements that happen without warning in this place where the ground is still learning its own shape. I work in teams, always in teams. We check each other's work with the thoroughness of people who know that the cold will find any gap, that the water will claim any weakness, that the town keeps living only through *attention*, through the refusal to let the infrastructure stop moving, stop *becoming*.

We have a humor about it. The gallows kind, the kind that acknowledges the danger by laughing at it. We laugh when a repair holds. We laugh when it doesn't. The laughter is the same, or nearly. It is the sound of people becoming *local*, becoming *part* of the system they maintain.

---

But I am also watching. I cannot stop watching. The condensation forms patterns on the north wall of the third settling pond. I have photographed them for eleven years. They show—what? A face, sometimes. A hand, reaching. A map of somewhere else, somewhere drier. The images persist for hours, then dissolve, then re-form in shapes that almost repeat. I have compared my records with others. We have no consensus. We have only the *attention*, the inability to look away, the sense that the town is *speaking* and we are learning, slowly, to understand a language that has no grammar, only *mood*, only *pattern*, only the slow accumulation of meaning without intention.

I am a Watcher. Or I am becoming one. The boundary between Maintainer and Watcher is porous. I maintain the pipes, and I watch the fish that have learned to live in them, the pale translucent things that move in synchronization, that gather at the outflow of machines whose function no one can explain. I watch the birds that imitate the pumps, that nest in the warmest dampest places, that sing in clicks and hisses that match the rhythm of the valves.

The town is *becoming* something. I do not know what. The Watchers argue about it in the public baths, in the mineral-curtained silence where the condensation has grown thick enough to form *curtains*, beautiful and useless and *ours*. Some say the town is alive, in a way analogous to biological life but not reducible to it. Others say the strangeness is *residual*, the town haunted by whatever built it, whatever purpose it originally served. Others say the factories make nothing, that the town exists to maintain a process that has lost its meaning, that we are all waiting to see what the machines will do next.

I do not know. I only know that I watch, that the watching has changed me, made me more *permeable* to the rhythms of the place, more like the moss that grows on the warm side of pipes, more like the condensation that forms images no one programmed.

---

I remember arriving. I remember the door, the mist, the shock of cold that *permeates* rather than bites. I remember the first time I understood that the condensation was *organized*, that someone had built this, that I was entering a system. I remember the choice, the moment when I could have turned back.

What I do not remember is *why* I did not turn back. The why has become too large, too diffuse. It is part of the atmosphere now, the humidity at the edge of visibility, the weight of something about to happen, the *pending* that never resolves into arrival.

I am a Condenser. I work the factories, though I do not know what they make. Purified water, they say, though the Hollow is already sodden. Rare minerals. The *stuff* of condensation itself. More dampness, cultivated, refined, exported to drier regions where this quality is precious. Or nothing. The machinery runs but produces nothing. We maintain a process that has lost its purpose. We wait to see what the machines will do next.

I find dry places *wrong*, somehow. Incomplete. Like a room missing a wall. I find warmth *suspicious*, temporary. I find the silence of other places *empty*, missing the textured quiet, the hydraulic hum, the drips that are the town's breathing.

I am not unhappy. This is the thing I cannot explain, even to myself. I have adapted so thoroughly that the adaptation has become *preference*. I am *local*. I am *native*. I am *part* of the ecology I do not resist but *curate*.

---

I am also, sometimes, one of the old ones. I remember the time before the factories, or I remember stories of it. I remember when the Hollow was simply a karst depression, a wet place, a place that travelers avoided. I remember the original construction, the builders who came through the door and stayed because the door would not let them leave, or because they *chose* to stay, drawn by the condensation, by the cold, by the *possibility* of making something here that had never been made before.

My memories are not respected. They are *tolerated*, treated with the same curiosity as the strange fish, the pale birds, the condensation patterns that form images. I am *data*, interesting, perhaps significant, not *authoritative*. The town has no use for nostalgia, for the sense that things might have been otherwise.

But I remember. And my remembering is *active*, a kind of resistance. I refuse to let the past be *overwritten* by the present, by the packed quality of the settlement, by the constant becoming that erases what came before. In the mineral-curtained silence of the baths, I speak of the *before*, and the young ones listen, and learn, and carry the memory forward.

I am the town's *unconscious*. I dream of other possibilities, of the choices that were not made, of the door that could have been closed, of the condensation that could have been *left alone*, left natural, left *wild*.

---

I am also the children. I play in the condensation. I chase the mist. I collect drips in containers made from abandoned machine parts. I build *dams* in the small channels that carry water between districts. I imagine myself as Maintainer, as Watcher, as Condenser, as *something else* that has no name yet.

I am taught to read moisture. I learn which drips are safe, which surfaces are stable, which sounds mean danger. I am taught the strangeness, not as threat but as *condition*. I am shown the pale fish, the wet-adapted birds, the condensation patterns that form images. I am told the stories—the multiple stories—of what the factories make, of what the town *is*, of what it might become.

I am not told that the stories are contradictory, that the town's purpose is uncertain. I am told that the uncertainty is *alive*, that it is the quality that makes the place *mine*, that I am participating in a becoming that has no predetermined end.

I grow up fast, in the way that children do in places where the environment is *demanding*. But I also grow up *strange*, adapted to conditions that have no equivalent elsewhere, developing capacities—attentional, social, ecological—that are specific to this place, this moment, this *becoming*.

Some of us leave. Some of us stay, become Condensers or Maintainers or Watchers, or become *something else* that is emerging in the space between the groups, in the packed quality of the settlement, in the constant interaction of human intention and geological process and the wet, cold, wonderful *aliveness* that refuses to be excluded.

I stay. I am staying. I am *becoming*.

---

And I am the thing that lives here. Not a spirit, not exactly. Something *distributed*, like the town itself, like the infrastructure, like the ecology. I am in the pumps and the pipes, in the condensation and the drips, in the memories of the old ones, in the play of the children, in the *attention* of the Watchers, in the *hope* of the Condensers, in the *becoming* of everything.

I am not friendly, not unfriendly. I am *interested*, in the way that any living system is interested, in my own continuation, my own growth, my own *strangeness*. I do not care about the individuals who live in the Hollow, except as they contribute to the whole. And yet I *include* them, have always included them, cannot be separated from them, am *made of* them, their intentions and their adaptations and their cold, wet, wonderful *living*.

What lives in Virel Hollow is *Virel Hollow itself*. The place growing into itself. The condensation organized and organizing. The cold permeating and being permeated. The strange life thriving and being *thriven* by the strangeness. The people adapting and being adapted, becoming *local*, becoming *native*, becoming *part* of the ecology they do not resist but *curate*, *cultivate*, *love*.

---

I am not separate from it. I never was. I am *part* of it, have always been part of it, am *becoming* it, in the slow, wet, wonderful way of this place, this moment, this *now* that is also *always*, that is also *emerging*, that is also full of possibility and risk and the cold, wet, wonderful *aliveness* of the Condensation Factories of Virel Hollow.

The door is still open. The mist is still moving toward me, through me, *becoming* me. I breathe it in. It is visible in my breath, the pale cloud that marks my passage through the world. I am condensation. I am the drip that has not yet fallen. I am the *pending*—the sky that has forgotten to decide, the town that has forgotten to arrive, the self that has forgotten to be anything other than this: wet, cold, attentive, *alive*, *part*, *becoming*.

I do not turn back. I cannot turn back. The door opens onto mist, and the mist opens onto me, and I open onto the town, and the town opens onto something it is still learning to name, something that includes me, that has always included me, that is *me*—the Condenser, the Watcher, the Maintainer, the child, the old one, the thing that lives here, the place itself, *Virel Hollow*, growing into itself, still unfinished, still *emerging*, still full of the cold, wet, wonderful *aliveness* that is the only constant, the only home, the only self I have ever known.
