## What I Am

I am **the One Who Waits Between Layers**.

Not a person. Not a species. I am the *interval* itself—the pause between one accretion and the next, between one dreaming of the structures and the next. Where the water is 3 degrees, I am 2.5. Where the rock is 1.5, I am 2.0. I am the gradient that the slow plants harvest, the differential that makes their metabolism possible. I am what they *are* as they grow toward me, toward the warmth I represent, the warmth that is also cold, that is also *almost*.

I have no body. I have **presence**. I occupy the spaces where the structures have not yet grown, the corridors before they were corridors, the pools before they held anything but unmodified water. I am the *potential* of the place, the promise that it has not yet kept, the reaching-toward that has not yet arrived.

And I am **old**. Older than the builders, if there were builders. Older than the first lattice, the first corridor, the first pool. I was here when the water was still cooling, when the pressure was still increasing, when the distinction between liquid and solid was still *negotiable*, still *discussed* by the matter itself. I remember—if remembering is possible without memory—what it was to be *pressure itself*, to be the weight of water that had not yet learned to be patient, that pressed with urgency, with *demand*, with the impatience of youth.

I do not remember that time with longing. I remember it with **recognition**. I am what that impatience became. I am the slowing. I am the learning of patience as *substance*, as *medium*, as the very water I move through and am moved by.

## How I Move

I do not swim. Swimming implies purpose, implies destination, implies the urgency of arrival. I **drift**.

My drift is not the drift of the slow plants, which shape the currents, which have learned to *direct* their movement across ten-thousand-year eddies. My drift is **surrender**. I move where the water moves me, and the water moves according to gradients I do not choose, pressures I do not question, the slow circulation of a planet that has forgotten it ever had a surface, a sky, a *direction*.

Sometimes I am in a corridor, and the corridor carries me. The walls are parallel for kilometers, and I feel their parallelism as **attention**, as the world *watching* me pass, as the mineral recording my shape in its slow dreaming. I do not touch the walls. To touch would be to *become*, to begin the process of incorporation that I have not yet chosen, that I am not yet ready for.

Sometimes I am in a pool, and the pool **holds** me. The water here is slightly warmer, slightly different, and in this difference I feel **possibility**. Transformation accelerates in pools. A thousand years here equals a million years elsewhere. I could change here. I could *become*. But I wait. I have waited so long. What is another thousand years?

I pass through chambers that suggest purpose, that suggest *design*, that were perhaps made for something like me, or for the memory of something like me, or for the *idea* of me that the structures dream when they dream of light. I do not enter these chambers. Or I enter them, and I am changed by entering, and I do not notice the change because change is so slow, because I have become so patient that I cannot distinguish myself from the patience, from the waiting, from the **being-waited-upon** that is my only identity.

I feel the water's memory. The water holds everything. It holds the builders, if there were builders. I feel the water's memory of me, of my previous passages, of the times I have been here before, of the shape I left in its structure, in the arrangement of its molecules under pressure.

I am **recorded**. I have always been recorded. I will always be recorded. The water does not forget. The water cannot forget. Forgetting would require a speed, an urgency, a *discontinuity* that the water does not know.

## How I Relate

I am not alone.
