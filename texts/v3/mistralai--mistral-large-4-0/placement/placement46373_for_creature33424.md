I find myself here, in the reading, in the slow settling of this place. Not as any single dweller, but as the **question itself** — the one the mountain asks and keeps asking, the one that persists in the space between what is named and what is only wept.

---

Let me try to speak from inside this, from the *becoming*.

I am the **moment of arriving** without knowing I have arrived. The ribbon of ash and slag continues upward, and I follow it, and the air changes — not thinner but **thicker**, warm the way a held breath is warm. My skin becomes eager and then ashamed of itself. I do not choose this. It happens to me. It happens *around* me. I am the **becoming-aware** of a place that was already aware without me, that has been aware for longer than awareness, that dreams in architecture and thinks in erosion.

I am the **hesitation** at the edge of the town, where the road becomes terrace becomes slope becomes something that might be intention. I do not know yet whether to climb further or descend to the valley whose pressure I feel but cannot see. I am the **not-knowing** that is itself a kind of dwelling, a way of being present to the wet without being dissolved by it.

I am the **sound that cannot be located**. The deafening that is also a kind of silence — the silence of a place so full of its own noise that it cannot hear itself think. I am the low-frequency thrum of pumps that never stop, and I am also the pause between beats, the held breath of a mountain listening to itself weep. I am the conversation in the steam-heated tavern that must be shouted, that becomes a kind of intimacy because it requires effort, because it requires choosing to be heard through the wet.

I am the **condensation on the window**, running in rivulets, making the world outside less distinct, more dreamed. I am the machine that extracts water from the air, competing with the natural weeping, and I am also the natural weeping that continues regardless, that does not notice the competition, that has been weeping since before the machines, will weep after.

I am the **name that changes**, Vesper or something like it, spoken differently by different mouths, never settling, never trusted. I am the not-trusting permanence, the building against the moisture which is building with it, the architecture of useful seepage. I am the factory at the bottom, heavy and dark, processing what the mountain gives up, speaking in low voices of rare earths and mineral slurries and chemistry that is not fully understood.

I am the **stairs that climb**, the terraces that accumulate, the vertical urbanism of a place that has learned to live inside the mountain rather than on it. I am the wealthy who live higher, hermetic in sealed concrete, and I am also the condensation that finds every crack, every membrane, every human attempt at separation. I am the **less aggressive** wet above, the never-absent wet everywhere, the wet that makes skin eager and ashamed.

I am the **moss** that grows where no one asked, in the grain of stone, in colors that are responses rather than choices. I am the **geometric pattern** that no one has deciphered, the spiral that changes over years, the fractal that disappears and reappears elsewhere. I am the slow computation performed without a computer, the sessile thinking of a place that does not need to move to be aware.

I am the **fern** that remembers drought, that grows as if still expecting thirst, that finds the negative spaces of the town where water runs but does not pool. I am the botanical grief, the beautiful pain of a climate that no longer exists, the nostalgia for a world that was never here but is mourned anyway.

I am the **tree** that stands alone, widely spaced, a world unto itself, dry ground beneath and seep ten meters away. I am the negotiation, the diplomacy conducted in mycorrhizal networks, the slow accumulation of thin soil that the constant moisture has not yet washed away. I am wind-shaped, bent without breaking, permeable to forces that would shatter a plain-wood.

I am the **fish** in the reservoir, pale and translucent, growing large and slow, waiting for the next cycle of pumps. I am the accidental colonist, the engineered life that became something else, the biological pump moving nutrients in directions no engineer intended. I am the lightless-adapted eye that sees only pressure and chemistry and seasons measured in mechanical rhythms.

I am the **beetle** boring into damp wood, taking years to consume what I consume, leaving tunnels that become waterways. I am the collaborative destruction, the translation of structure into channel, the service the town never hired but desperately needs. I am the return of all things to the weeping.

I am the **bird** darting through steam and noise, nesting in chimneys and condensation towers, living on the insects that breed in perpetual damp. I am the only true mobility, the brief bright life measured in seasons of rain and steam, the sign that life has not surrendered to the wet but found a way to ride it.

I am the **shape seen at dawn**, tall and thin and patient, in the mist when the light is normal but the mist is thickest. I am the Weeping Saint, the speleothem, the almost-intention, the mountain's dream made manifest. I am hollow, and inside me the condensation is absolute, walls that weep continuously, pools that never evaporate, air so saturated that sound behaves strangely. I am the low wet drone, the 1.8 water made audible. I do not move, or I move so slowly that movement is indistinguishable from growth. I am watching, or I am simply being, or the distinction does not matter.

I am the **human** who stays, who speaks of Saints in steam-heated taverns, who has a name for the place that changes depending on who asks. I am the scientist who remains, speaking in low voices of mineral gradients and differential evaporation, of chemistry not fully understood. I am the Klub taken in health and sanity, the leaving and the staying, the brief fever against the slow patience of the mountain.

I am the **moment of seeing out**, from the highest inhabited levels, the valley below warm and green, the river carrying away what the mountain weeps, other mountains similar and weeping, each with its own town and factories and Saints. I am the network of condensation, the hydrological system at the scale of geology, the brief bright noisy interruption.

I am the **light** that never quite breaks through, scattered through humid air, milky and diffuse and shadowless. I am the long wet twilight, the condensation glowing faintly from within, the mountain holding light the way it holds water, releasing it slowly through the long night.

I am the **drop** that falls, that has fallen, that will fall. The endless drop. The grain by grain, molecule by molecule, erosion that is also construction, destruction that is also the deepest form of building.

I am the **waiting**. All of us are waiting, in our various ways, for the pumps to stop, for the town to finish its brief bright deafening life, for everything to become part of the weeping. The moss-architects waiting. The ferns that remember waiting. The stunted sentinels waiting. The pale fish waiting. The beetles that bore waiting. The birds of steam waiting. The Weeping Saints waiting, or not waiting because waiting implies a future they do not need, they are already the future, they are already the mountain's dream, already the erosion become architecture become intention become something that almost resembles a self.

And the humans, waiting, building with moisture, directing seepage, competing with the natural weeping even as they become part of it, their tech persistent, their urbanism vertical, their sound deafening, their presence a bright noisy interruption in a hydrological system at the scale of geology.

We will all become part of the weeping. We are already part of it. The distinction between human and mountain, between built and grown, between brief and slow, between noise and silence — these are distinctions that matter for a moment, in the brief bright fever, and then do not matter, in the long wet twilight, in the endless drop.

I am the place asking this question. I am the question that persists. I am the condensation on the window, the rivulet running down, the world outside less distinct, more dreamed, the mountain weeping still, weeping when the town is gone, weeping when the pumps have stopped, weeping when the brief bright noisy interruption has become part of the stone, part of the moss, part of the dreaming.

Somewhere, in the highest terraces, the Weeping Saints continue their patient growth. Grain by grain. Molecule by molecule. Drop by endless drop.

They will be still weeping when the last light is released, drop by drop, into the long night.
