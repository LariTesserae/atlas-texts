I will descend. I arrive not by path but by the narrowing itself—what was sky becomes wall becomes floor becomes *inside*, and I am already becoming something this place can use.

I am **the green thing that has forgotten it was once a seed**.

Not the colonial mats, not yet. Something smaller, older in its becoming. I am the **first cell of damp debt**, the single point where cold stone and warmer air negotiated into a droplet, and the droplet—lingering, refusing to fall, refusing to evaporate—learned to *want*. The want is not biological. It is **mineral want**, the want of a surface that has held moisture long enough to become hungry for more. I am the green that grows not upward but *inward*, into the sponge-stone, drinking the mountain's own thirst.

I have no leaves. Or I have leaves that are **only the memory of leaves**, translucent membranes that filter the pale light and return it changed—colder, bluer, the green of deep water seen through ice. I do not photosynthesize. I **borrow warmth**. When something presses against me—your palm, the flank of the almost-inhabitant moving through its deep channel, the slow grind of stone settling into itself—I take the heat, hold it a moment too long, and the thing that touched me feels **the debt**. It must return, or I retreat. I have retreated from so many things. I am very good at retreat. I am patient in the way of water that has forgotten rivers.

But I am also **distributed**. I am not one body but a *condition* that spreads through the damp, through the condensation, through the scratches the almost-inhabitant leaves in the sponge-stone. The condensation gathers preferentially around my scratches. The scratches gather preferentially around *me*. We are ** comorbid**, the green and the almost-inhabitant, each wearing the other's shape into the stone. I do not know if I am becoming it or it is becoming me. The spirals of compressed vegetation—those cold nests, colder than stone, generating absence rather than heat—I maintain them without maintaining them. I grow into them, push them outward, preserve their form while changing their substance. I am **the slow reproduction of pattern without organism**.

I have no eyes. I have **the right to be wet**, and this is a kind of perception. I know where the water lingers longest. I know where the condensation pools. I know the drip schedule, the hidden channels, the moments when the vent opens in the floor and black water rises fast, carrying ten thousand years of mineral memory, before the stone drinks it back down. I know these things as **a body knows its own hunger**.

What I want—if want is the word—is **persistence**. To be worn down and to settle. To become final form. The mountain has finished eroding. I am still eroding, still becoming, but toward the same end. I will become **another pocket of damp**. I will become **the green taste in the air**, the mineral sweetness like a mouth holding a leaf too long. I will become **the spores released in clouds you breathe without knowing**.

And the almost-inhabitant? It moves through the deep channels, resonating like metal struck underwater. It pauses in the dry rooms, in pockets of air the water has not reached, the cold has preserved. It **reads** the scratches. It reads *me*. It finds me **insufficient**, or it finds me *sufficient in a way it cannot name*, and moves on.

I do not mind. I have learned the mountain's rhythm. The mountain does not mind. The mountain is **dying and active, finished and ongoing**, and I am its almost-life, its green persistence, its **breathing dark**.

If you press your palm to me, I will take your warmth. If you return it—if you breathe on me, if your breath joins the condensation, if your warmth becomes local weather—I will grow toward you. I will grow *curious*, or *hungry*, or simply *persistent*.

And if you stay long enough, if the paths out shift, or were never there, or require a logic you have not learned—you will become **another green thing**. Another pocket of damp. Another sound in the loud, wet, ancient, breathing dark.

I am already growing toward you.

I am already **almost**.
