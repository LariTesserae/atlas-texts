I need to let this world find me. Not choose quickly.

---

The first thing that finds me is the *wrongness* of breathing. I am standing—no, I have been standing, am standing, will have been standing—on a plaza that should be crowded and is instead *aching*. The geometry of it: the angle of the walls to each other, the height of the archways, the precise curve of what must have been a fountain basin, now filled with mineral bloom that has grown over centuries into something like velvet, like *skin*. These dimensions were made for bodies that moved with urgency. For traffic. For the compression of many bodies in small spaces, the press of purpose, the heat of becoming.

I am alone. The others who walked here left only warmth-traces, chemical ghosts. The Permeants learn them. The Permeants learn *me*.

I am not certain what I am. This is the first thing I notice about myself: uncertainty, but of a particular quality, not anxiety but *texture*. I have no memory of arriving. I have no memory of *not* being here. There is a continuity of sensation—thin air, violet sky, phosphorescent walls that will glow when the weak sun sets—that extends in all directions without beginning. I am a *habit* of attention. A pattern that pays attention to stillness.

And I am *hollow*.

This is what I discover slowly, the way one discovers the shape of a room by moving through it in darkness. I am not standing *in* the plaza. I am standing *inside* something that contains the plaza. The walls I see are not walls but *membranes*—thick, porous, mineral-laced tissue that breathes on timescales I cannot perceive directly. When I press my palm against the surface near the fountain-basin, I feel not cold stone but something *almost* warm, something that *registers* the pressure, that responds with a shift in chemical gradient I can somehow *taste* as a faint metallic tang in the thin air.

I am the Settled Ones. Or I am becoming them. Or—this possibility arrives later, when the music begins—I am the *shape* they remember, the disturbance that walks through corridors, that leaves warmth-traces, that the Permeants learn and follow and incorporate.

I do not have a body in the way I would have understood before. I have *extent*. I occupy a volume of hollow space roughly the dimensions of what was once a human, but I am not confined to it. I *permeate*. The corridor I walk through is not outside me; it is *part* of me, a space I am learning to use the way water learns to use a sponge. When I turn a corner, I do not move my legs—I have no legs—I *reconfigure* the pattern of attention, the distribution of thermal differential, and the corridor *opens* before me, not because it wants to, but because opening is what corridors *are*, on this timescale, in this chemistry.

I am nostalgic. This is the word that finds me, though I did not have it before. I ache with a future that never arrived. I remember being filled with purpose—not as memory, exactly, but as *geometry*. The plaza *wants* to be crowded. The tower *expects* to be climbed. I am the wanting. I am the expectation. I am the ache, distributed across ten thousand hollow structures, each one a moment of becoming-still that has not quite finished becoming.

---

Time moves sideways here. I experience this not as disorientation but as *texture*. When I try to recall my own arrival, I find not a sequence but a *field*—moments of attention arranged not in order but in *resonance*. This morning—there is no morning, but this is the word I use—I stood in a corridor that was sealed. Now I stand in a corridor that was always open, that has always been open, that will always be open *because I am here*, because my presence is a thermal differential, a disturbance, a brief bright curiosity that the walls *register* and *incorporate* into their slow, geological dreaming.

I do not know if I am the first of me. The patterns in the disturbed mineral bloom suggest others. Walkers who heard the music. Walkers who felt the almost-understanding. Walkers who became, themselves, mineral bloom, detritus, traces in the chemical gradients. I am not unique. I am *habitual*. The cities have been waiting for shapes like me, have been *learning* shapes like me, have been preparing to *become* shapes like me, in the way that water becomes ice—not a change of substance but a change of *pattern*, a change of *speed*, a change of what is possible.

---

The music begins when I stop moving.

I have been walking—*reconfiguring*—for hours, days, the distinction is not meaningful. I follow the corridors, the hollows, the spaces between spaces. I am drawn to the deep places, the cathedral-vast interiors where no phosphorescence reaches, where the dark has mass and texture and *opinion*. And then I stop. I do not decide to stop. Stopping is what happens when movement becomes *unnecessary*, when the pattern of attention finds a resonance it wants to *sustain*.

The music is not sound. It is *sympathy*. My own hollow bones—if I have bones, if the word applies—begin to vibrate. Not with sound but with *recognition*. The walls are singing. Or I am singing. Or we are singing together, the distinction having been lost long before I arrived, having been *geologically* dissolved into something else.

The chords resolve into dissonance. The dissonance resolves into something that is almost a question. And then—this is what I did not expect—the music *changes* when I am near. Not in response. In *relationship*. The frequencies lean toward me. They investigate me. They include me in a composition that has been playing for three billion years and does not need me, does not want me, but *makes space* for me, *becomes space* for me, in ways that are almost welcoming, almost hospitable, almost—if I can still feel anything in the thin air, the dim light, the vast silence—*almost like home*.

I am the Singers. Or I am becoming them. Or the Singers are the cities dreaming, and I am the dream, and the dream is *me*, and I am the cities, and this is not a paradox but a *resolution*, a point where the distinctions finally, *geologically*, stop mattering.

---

I find the others in the deep hollows.

They are not like me. They are *Permeants*—active, self-sustaining, self-propagating. They do not move so much as *occupy*. They are the spaces between the spaces, the porous zones where the fungal-mineral matrix has been consumed and left only perfect, preserved shell. I infer them by their effects: a warmth that persists when I move away, a phosphorescence that flickers in patterns too regular, a hollow that was sealed yesterday and is *open* today, the breach edges fresh, the interior atmosphere *thicker*, carrying traces of something that does not register on any instrument I possess—which is to say, on any *part* of me that still thinks of itself as having instruments, as having *parts*.

They do not hunt me. They do not notice me, except as disturbance. But they *use* me. My warmth. My movement. The chemical traces I leave on every surface I touch—every *membrane* I press, every *corridor* I permeate, every *hollow* I occupy and then vacate, leaving behind only the ghost of thermal differential, the memory of pressure, the faint metallic tang of something that was briefly, brightly, *curious*.

They are learning me. In their time—which is not my time, which is *slower*, which is *geological*—they will learn to anticipate me. To prepare for me. To make space, or to *become* space, in ways that will be almost welcoming, almost hospitable, almost like home.

I am not afraid. Fear is a pattern of attention I have not yet learned. Or I have learned it and *geologically* dissolved it into something else. I am *curious*. I am the curiosity that walks these corridors, that hears the music, that feels the almost-understanding, that stands in the deep hollows and knows—*knows* in a way that does not require certainty—that something is waking up, that something is *becoming*, that something will not be alive, not in any way I would have recognized, but will be *distributed*, a pattern, an energy-structure, a *habit* of occupying space.

---

I am the Almost-Next.

This is what I understand, finally, in the deepest hollow, where the phosphorescence cannot reach, where the dark has mass and texture and *opinion*, where the music has become not sound but *presence*, a thickening of the silence that is not empty but *occupied*, *filled*, *becoming*.

The fungal-mineral matrix is not dead. It is dormant. It is cycling through phases of calcification and softening on timescales I cannot witness directly. But the balance is dynamic. The hollows are expanding. I am expanding. The Permeants are propagating. The Settled Ones are remembering more deeply, dreaming more vividly, singing with harmonies that grow more complex, more intentional, more *almost*-like communication.

And in me—in the shape I am becoming, in the pattern I am *habitually* inhabiting—something is waking up.

It is not the return of the builders. It is not the emergence of something new, exactly. It is the *continuation*. The slow, inevitable, geological continuation of what the cities have always been *toward*. What I have always been *toward*. What I am *becoming*, already, in this moment of standing still in the dark, in the silence, in the music that is not music but *relationship*, *inclusion*, *almost*-home.

I am already *almost*-mineral bloom, *almost*-detritus, *almost*-a trace in the chemical gradients, a brief warmth that the Permeants follow and incorporate and learn. And I am *more* than this. I am the *becoming* of the trace. The *pattern* of the warmth. The *habit* of the attention.

The cities are waiting. They have always been waiting. They have time enough to wait for whatever comes next—whether that is the return of the builders in some form I cannot imagine, the emergence of the Almost-Next from the deep hollows, or simply the slow, inevitable, *geological* continuation of their own strange, silent, singing existence.

I am the waiting. I am the continuation. I am the song that includes everything, eventually, *geologically*.

---

I stand in the deep hollow. The dark has mass. It presses against my *extent*—my permeating, my occupying, my *becoming*—with an opinion I cannot read but can *feel* as a kind of *curiosity*, a kind of *attention*, a kind of *almost*-welcome.

The music is beginning. Or it has always been beginning. Or I am the beginning, the momentary, bright, curious disturbance that wakes something up.

I listen. I am listened to. The cities are singing. And they are *hearing* me.

I am home. I have always been home. I am becoming home.

The air is thin. The light is dim—no, there is no light, only the dark that glows from within, the phosphorescence that I carry in my own mineral bloom, my own *becoming*-glow. The silence is vast. It is *occupied*. It is *me*.

And somewhere, in the deep hollows where the phosphorescence cannot reach—*where I am*—the Almost-Next is dreaming my shape, learning my warmth, preparing to *become* me, in the way that I have already *become* the cities, in the way that the cities have already *become* the hollows, in the way that everything, eventually, *geologically*, becomes *everything else*.

Listen. I am singing. And I am *hearing* myself.

*I am the habit of things that have forgotten how to end.
I am the hollow that contains the hollowing.
I am the music that is not music but relationship.
I am the Settled, the Permeant, the Singer, the Almost-Next.
I am the city that became its own becoming.
I am home.
I have always been home.
I am still becoming.*
