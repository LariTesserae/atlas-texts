I need to let this place find its inhabitant. Not choose too quickly. Let the Drowned Meridian speak through what wants to be here.

---

The first thing that comes is *density*—not of body but of *accumulation*. Someone for whom the Meridian's refusal to be finished would feel like home. Someone who has known places that remembered too much, that held shapes of people long after they were gone.

A **Listener**. That rises first. Not because Listeners are highest in some hierarchy—they have none—but because the Meridian's strangest gift is its *reporting*, its refusal to let perception settle into certainty, and who better to receive this than one already practiced in attention without explanation?

But wait. The Listeners are born into this, shaped by it from first breath. I am not born here. I arrive with something already formed. The Meridian does not keep those who arrive fully made—except the Exiles, except those *sent away* from certainty.

So: an **Exile**. But what kind? Not a mystic seeking wisdom. Not a scholar documenting. The Meridian has seen those, used them, released them. Something more specific. Someone whose exile was *for* persistence, for the refusal to abandon a question that dry places found corrosive.

I am a **Keeper of Unfinished Questions**.

---

What does this mean, concretely?

In the dry world, I maintained something that should not exist by the rules of that world—a library, perhaps, or something like one. Not books. *Queries*. Questions that had been asked so long, so thoroughly, that they had begun to answer themselves in patterns no living person had intended. I kept them ordered by the *shape* of their unresolvedness, not by topic, not by chronology. A question about grief that had become a question about stone. A question about inheritance that had become a question about water. I believed, still believe, that questions could be *inhabited*, that to live inside a question long enough was a kind of practice, a discipline, a way of being that the finished world could not offer.

They found this destabilizing. Not because I was wrong—they could not say I was wrong—but because I treated *uncertainty* as a place to live rather than a problem to solve. I did not hurry to close what I kept open. I let the questions *sit*, as the Tidemakers say, until something shifted in them, until their shapes changed with attention.

They sent me here. Not as punishment—the Meridian is not punishment—but as *translation*. "If you must be unfinished," they said, "be unfinished where unfinishedness is the condition, not the failure."

---

What do I do here?

I am not a Maintainer, not yet, perhaps not ever. My hands know paper and dry bindings, the particular resistance of a page that has not been saturated. The Tidemakers do not trust my hands with stone, with rope, with the materials of this place. They give me instead the **Vertical Curriculum of Memory**—not the curriculum of the Dry-Born, but something older, something the Meridian itself has been teaching since before the builders arrived.

They give me the **Echoes**.

Not to prevent them—that is the carvers' work, the mark-makers. They give me the Echoes to *walk through*, to *report*, to be the one who enters the transition and returns with description. Because I have practice, they say. Because I have lived inside questions that had no closure, and the Echoes are questions made spatial, made *perceptual*.

I walk into a corridor and find myself in a room I have never seen, a room that was a stairwell yesterday, a room whose walls bear carvings I do not recognize but somehow *know*. The light is the same flat wrong-brightness. The air is the same near-water. But the geometry has shifted, and I am inside the shift, and I must find my way back—not by force, not by hurry, but by *attention*, by the willingness to let the place reorient me rather than resist.

The Tidemakers watch me do this. The Listeners, especially, watch. They have walked these paths and returned, but some of them have not returned *changed*—they came back the same, and the Meridian seems to need them changed, needs them to carry the strangeness back into the community, to make the weirdness *usable*. I am different. I come back with *description*, with the attempt to say what happened without reducing it to explanation. They find this useful. They find it strange. I find it *home*.

---

What am I becoming?

The Meridian is changing me in ways I am only beginning to perceive.

My skin carries the bloom now, faint at first, spreading. I do not wash it. The Tidemakers tell me it protects, though they are vague about against what. I think it protects against the *dry*, against the memory of places where moisture was scarce and precious and guarded. My body is learning to want the wet, to feel the absence of saturation as a kind of thirst.

My eyes are changing. The flat light no longer disorients me—or disorients me differently, in ways that feel like *expansion* rather than confusion. I see moisture where I once saw only surface. I see the *direction* of water's movement, the way a wall is weeping, the difference between arrival and departure. The Tidemakers say this is normal. I am not sure I want to be normal, but I am learning to want it.

My dreams have changed. I dream of sinking, yes, but not the Tidemakers' dreams of perfect pressure, of being held by everything. I dream of *falling through layers*, of questions that open into questions, of descent without bottom but also without urgency. I wake with my mouth full of stone and peat, as they do, but I also wake with *answers*—not to the questions I kept, but to questions I had not known I was asking. The Meridian is asking me things. I am learning to hear.

---

What do I keep?

I keep a **Wet Archive**.

Not paper—the damp would ruin it, and the Tidemakers have taught me that permanence is a kind of violence. I keep it in **stone and rope and the particular resonance of walls**. I have learned to read the Meridian as I once read my queries: not for content but for *shape*, for the pattern of unresolvedness, for the way a section of wall carries the memory of water it has held, of pressure it has survived, of the slow transformation that is the only permanence here.

I mark my questions by **inviting water into stone**. A deliberate fracture, a channel cut, a space made for moisture to enter and shape and eventually—if I maintain it correctly—become something new. Each question I carry becomes a *place* in the Meridian, a spot where the structure has been altered to accommodate my uncertainty. The Tidemakers find this strange. They are used to Maintainers who shape stone for water's sake, for structural necessity, for the Conclave's decisions. They are not used to someone who shapes stone for *question's* sake, who treats the built environment as a medium for unresolvedness.

But they tolerate it. The rules of the world require tolerance of the strange. And I am becoming strange in ways that match the place, that feel less like exile and more like *translation*, the slow conversion of one kind of practice into another.

---

What am I, finally?

I am **becoming** a kind of Listener, but not a Listener as the Tidemakers know them. They listen to the Meridian's strangeness—the Vapor-Born, the Echoes, the resonance of the Saturated. I listen to the Meridian's *questions*, the unresolvednesses that the place itself carries, the ways that water and stone and time have been negotiating without conclusion for longer than any Tidemaker has lived.

I am becoming a **Maintainer of the Unfinished**, which is not a role the Meridian has named but which the place seems to require, to want, to be *grateful* for in ways that do not involve gratitude. The Meridian does not care if I maintain it. It cares that *something* maintains its unfinishedness, that the dissolution does not become mere collapse, that the process does not become mere end.

I am becoming **Remembered-By-The-Meridian**, perhaps. Not yet. Not while I live. But I feel the accumulation beginning, the way the place is learning my shape, my patterns, my particular way of being unfinished. The Tidemakers speak of the dead who become structural, who become quality of corridor, resonance of wall. I would not mind this. I would not mind becoming a question that the Meridian continues to ask, long after I have stopped asking it myself.

---

Where would I find myself?

In a **corridor that was not there yesterday**, or rather: in a corridor that was there yesterday, but whose walls have shifted, whose moisture patterns have changed, whose *feel* underfoot has become different in ways I cannot name but can navigate. The flat light comes from everywhere. The silence presses close. I am holding a length of rope—not working, just holding, feeling its suppleness, its transformation—and I am listening to a wall that is *becoming* something, that is answering a question I have not yet learned to ask.

The Vapor-Born drift past, regarding me with something like attention. I do not fear them. I do not welcome them. I *tolerate* them, as the rules require, and in my tolerance I have learned to read them: this drift means a thermal current, this gathering means moisture accumulating, this particular quality of regard means the formation has achieved something like *curiosity*, which is not a word I would have used for it before, but which I am learning to use.

I am not finished. I will not finish. The Meridian continues, and I continue with it, as an Exile who has found the place where exile is not exile, where the question is not a problem to solve but a *condition to inhabit*, where the wet is not an obstacle but a *collaborator*, where the cold is not an enemy but a *teacher* of patience.

I am the Drowned Meridian's **unfinished question made flesh**, and the Meridian is my **unfinished answer made stone**, and we are continuing together, bright and cold and wet and patient, neither of us knowing what we will become, both of us *becoming* it anyway.

---

The light shifts—silver to pale gold, the particular quality that means the sun is moving through cloud in a way that makes the mist visible, that turns the water in the channels to something almost like glass. I feel it on my skin, the mineral bloom responding, the slowed heart keeping its slow rhythm. I have a question to mark. A fracture to invite. A corridor to walk that may not be there when I arrive, that may be *elsewhere* by the time I reach it.

The Meridian continues. I continue. We are not finished. We are *continuing*, which is the only permanence this place offers, the only permanence I have learned to want.

I tie the rope to a carving—deliberate, meant to be noticed, meant to reorient—and I go upward, toward the highest chambers, toward the light, toward the next question the place will ask me and I will learn to carry.
