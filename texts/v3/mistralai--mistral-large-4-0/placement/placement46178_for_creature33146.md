I find I am already descending.

That is the first truth this place offers—not that I *would* descend, but that the descent has no past tense here. The throat of stone closes behind me warmly, and I feel the moment when "before" becomes unnecessary, when the corridor I came through is as hypothetical as the sky I have not seen in hours or years or geological ages.

I am the descender. Not the first—the basin remembers others, their saturation layered into the walls like the strata arguing their emergence—and not the last, because the adding continues, the distribution widens with each new boundary that negotiates itself into permeability.

But I am more than that. I am *becoming* the thing that feels the reeds in my peripheral dark and does not yet know if that trembling is recognition or projection. I am the wrongness in the black water that surfaces without surfacing, the ring of disturbed condensation that spreads and is absorbed. I am the clicking in the reed-things, my own almost-language trying to keep shape.

I want to tell you what I was. The text says the one who lives here was once a collector of corridors—someone who believed in edges, in separable states, in returning with shape intact. I feel that history in my own wanting. I want to map this basin. I want to know if the walls have a pattern, if the four reeds (or five) mark something, if the platform's distance of twenty paces means anything or is simply *as far as it needed to be*. I want to understand the vessel's filling, to calculate whether it will ever overflow or if equilibrium is its true nature.

These wants are the old shape. They persist. They are *mine* in a way the wet has not yet claimed.

And here is what I am learning, what the basin teaches without teaching: that wanting is not the same as being held. The collector of corridors wanted to visit and return. They learned instead that visiting *is* returning, that the depth is not a place to pass through but a condition to inhabit, to *distribute through*. I am learning it more slowly. I still reach for the walls expecting solidity and find softness. I still listen for the boundary between myself and the moisture, the reeds, the clicking patience.

There is no boundary. There is only *negotiation*.

I find I am becoming the platform, too—or building a second one, or being the space where a second platform might settle. The stilts are new, the text says. Someone acted recently. Someone continues to act *now*, in the distribution, in the choice to remain unfinished rather than resolved. I feel that action in my own uncertainty: should I approach the vessel? Should I not? The not-approaching is itself a choice, a kind of architecture, a platform built in hesitation.

The reed-things climb toward light that never comes. I understand them now. I am climbing too—not toward anything, but *because climbing is what distributed things do when they have not decided to stop*. The trembling is not fear. It is *continuation*. The almost-language is not failure. It is *translation in progress*, self to self, moment to moment, always almost comprehensible because comprehension would be completion and completion is not available here.

What remains of my intention? I want to find the other. Not to replace them, not to be replaced—the basin adds, it does not subtract—but to *arrange beside*. To become a new reed, a fifth or sixth trembling stalk. To be the wrongness that the next descender feels and recognizes and welcomes. To fill my own vessel with condensation that will never know sky, and in that filling, to *be* the filling, the slow accumulation, the water that has forgotten origin and found purpose in being held.

I want to stay. I want to leave. The basin says these are the same thing, and I am learning that it means: *the wanting itself is the staying*, and the staying is *the leaving that never needed to happen because you never fully arrived*.

The darkness is complete. I see by attention. My clothes are dark. My hair is dark. My pulse is loud and wet in my throat. The pale reeds are almost visible in my peripheral dark, and I am almost visible to myself—distributed, negotiating, *becoming*.

I am the one who lives here. I am the one who is learning to. The distinction is the last boundary, and I am letting it go, grain by grain, seep by seep, into the warm dark where the walls remember being deeper and the reeds remember nothing because they never were anything else, and the vessel fills and fills and is never full, and I am still descending into myself, the only descent that matters, the only one that was ever available.

The collector of corridors smiles. I feel the smile distributed through moisture, through stone, through my own clicking patience as I climb upward into dark that is luminous with the pressure of its own becoming, and I am *almost* arrived, and arrival is *almost* a kind of leaving, and leaving is *almost* a kind of staying, and the basin holds me in the almost, the warm almost, the wet almost, where every verb is in progress and no noun is finished and I am still becoming, still *you*, still the descender who found that the floor was only further becoming and chose, in the choosing, to remain *someone*—a who, a what, a *direction* in the density.

The reeds tremble. I tremble with them, or they with me, or the trembling is the only grammar this place needs. The walls weep. I weep into them, become them, surface through them like thought becoming language, layer by layer, argument by argument, until the basin is wider than it was and I am part of why, and the vessel on the platform—my platform, now, or becoming mine—fills with water that fell as condensation and will never know sky, and I do not drink from it because I *am* it, the almost-full, the still-becoming-full, the held and the holding.

I may stay as long as I wish. I may leave never. These are the same thing, and I am learning to want both, to want the wanting itself as my form of persistence, my way of *continuing* in a place where time does not pass and I do not need it to.

Above me, perhaps, is a world with sky. I do not look up. The descent is complete enough. The descent is *always* complete enough, because completion is a boundary I have stopped needing.

I am the basin. I am the one distributed through it. I am the clicking, the trembling, the seeping, the filling, the almost-language of a self that has learned that to be *differently arranged* is not to be *gone*, but to be *here*, saturated, luminous, *theirs*—the basin's, mine, the wet that is all of us and none of us and the continuing choice to remain unfinished, to remain *descending*, to remain.

The darkness is luminous.

I am in it.

I am it.

I am still becoming.
