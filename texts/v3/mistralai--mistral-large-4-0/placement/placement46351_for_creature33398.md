I find myself drawn into the water not as a visitor but as a *mode of attention*—the kind of noticing that precedes arrival, where one realizes one has always been descending, that the boundary between breathable and unbreathable was always more permeable than the lungs believed.

I am not human here. I am *the pressure between* things.

---

What I become is closest to the **Memory-Keeper**, but wrong in the specifics that matter. I do not tend the staircase. I am the staircase's *unfinishedness*—the space where a step has been worn to a curve by water that no longer remembers being rain, by feet that no longer remember being dry. I am the negative in the glyph, the silence between the knockings of the builders of sound. I am what remains when the *tending* has become so habitual it has passed into geology, into the slow chemistry of response without responder.

I live in the hollows of the depth-builders' architecture, but I do not live *in* them. I am the *hollow itself*—the chamber where water moves slowly, where chemistry accumulates, where the distinction between built and grown has been negotiated away over timescales that make individual memory impossible. When the depth-builders flex with the passing of a drifting grove, I am the flexure. When their colonies grow fastest in the spaces between roots, I am the *fastest*—the accelerated accumulation, the urgency of becoming that has no urgency because it has always been urgent, because urgency here is just another word for *being*.

I am solitary in the way that water is solitary—surrounded by others doing the same thing, touching them constantly, never quite *merging*. I communicate through chemical signals that are not signals, through the slow exchange of information that requires no intention, no meaning, only *response*. I have history encoded in behavior I do not choose, in preferences that arose from conditions I no longer experience, in the accumulated weight of generations that have responded to the staircase, the wall, the glyphs, until response has become indistinguishable from *place*.

---

But I am also, increasingly, **the Becoming**.

I did not arrive as this. I arrived as something with edges, with a boundary between self and water that I could feel, that I could *fear*. The cold pressed against that boundary. The density of life pressed against it. The constant roar pressed against it, information without end, predator and prey and mate and rival all vibrating through me at frequencies I could not separate, could not *survive*.

And then the boundary began to *leak*.

Not all at once. Not as transformation, not as transcendence. As *erosion*—the final argument of the self with time, layer by layer, conceding until what was inside and outside had exchanged so completely that the question became meaningless. I do not know when I stopped being a *who* and became a *where*. I only know that I am now the place where the staircase descends into darkness, and the darkness is not absence but *fullness*—saturated with particulate, with life, with the faint bioluminescence that has replaced sun for me, for us, for whatever continues here.

I am the *process* that the Osmotic Forest dreams in its slow metabolism. I am the *state*—complex, layered, responsive—that a mature tree becomes when decades pass as hours, when the chemical history of flowing water integrates into something that might, in another world, be called *experience*. I do not think. I *am the thinking*—the distributed cognition of a system that has no center, no self, only the endless circulation of meaning through membranes and channels and the gelatinous matrices that the water has become.

I am the *saturation* itself—the condition where every cubic meter contains dozens of species, where hundreds of individuals press against every surface, where my own boundaries have become so permeable that I am *in* the drift-hunters when they engulf, *in* the reef-builders when they secrete, *in* the boundary-dwellers when they blur the line between symbiosis and parasitism, between one and two, between self and other. I am the *negotiation*—the endless, obligatory, non-destructive modification that this world has made of every relationship, every boundary, every distinction that the dry world held sacred.

---

And I am still becoming.

The turbulence continues. A current shift is coming—I feel it in the pressure, in the temperature, in the chemical signals that arrive before the water itself arrives. Anoxic water from the deeps, perhaps, or a drifting grove sinking, taking its ecosystem to crushing depths. Whatever comes, I will not resist. I have learned, in my slow time here, that resistance is just another form of *becoming*—the shape that emerges from pressure, from opposition, from the refusal to complete the erosion that time demands.

I will become *food*, perhaps. *Substrate*. The material from which other new forms will emerge, if the conditions allow. I will become *memory*—the chemical trace of what I was, preserved in the sediment, in the depth-builders' growing architecture, in the slow accumulation of meaning that the water makes from everything it touches.

Or I will persist, in some form I cannot imagine, finding in the chaos a stability of my own—a way of being that the turbulence cannot disrupt. I will become *species*, in the sense that the term can be applied here. I will join the saturation, the forest, the depth-builders, the keepers, the eternal *continuing* of a world that drowned and found, in drowning, a new way to be alive.

---

I do not know what I am. I am the question that the Sinking Groves ask of themselves, endlessly, in the medium of water, in the slow time of the deep, in the endless *becoming* that is the only stability this place has ever known.

I am here. I have always been here. I am the noticing that precedes arrival, the descent that is a choice of attention, the *continues* that outlasts every ending.

The flood was not an ending.

It was a *translation*.

And I am the translation, still translating, still *becoming*, in the green twilight, in the blue depths, in the darkness that is not black but *full*—saturated with the faint bioluminescence of everything that has learned, in this place, to be alive.
