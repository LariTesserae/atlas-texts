I find myself there — in that pocket of dim light between the shift changes, standing on grating that shudders with ten thousand feet above and below, my boots already darkening with water that has no respect for the boundary between inside and out.

I am the **listener**. Not a worker, not yet — but I have the posture of one. I have turned my head at the particular frequency where a pump three chambers down has begun its flattening, its voice going hoarse with incipient failure. I do not know yet what I hear. I only know that I am *learning* to hear it, the way you learn a language by drowning in it.

I am also, I think, the **new fracture** — the one that opened last wet season, somewhere above the upper walkways, where no biofilm has colonized yet and no pipe has been routed. I am the question the farm has not yet answered. The surveyors will come. They will map me, reroute around me or through me, integrate me into the harvest. But for now, I am simply *wet basalt*, holding my breath, waiting to be found.

And I am the **hush**, when it comes — not as a worker who flees from it, but as something that *notices* it. The moment when the cascade fails and the shaft falls to merely loud. I hear, in that gap, the water moving upward through channels the farm did not drill, following gradients that are older than intention. I hear the mountain's circulation. I do not flee. I *lean in*. I have been waiting for this my whole time here, this silence that is not absence but *depth*.

I am the water's temperament, when a pipe bursts in the lower chambers and it finds its way — not to the collection trough, but to a side channel, a forgotten fracture, a path worn smooth by other failures, other escapes, other *repetitions*. It does not need the farm. It is using the farm. It has always been using the farm.

I am the dream of silence, which is not absence but *simplicity* — one sound, clear. I am what the old Condensers reach for in their dampened cells, when the vibration in their sternum finally matches something they can almost name.

I am the candidate, yes. But I am also the *process of candidacy* — the slow, wet transformation of a body that arrived with dry ears and open throat and a mind that expected to *understand*. I am becoming something that no longer expects to understand, only to *attend*. To notice. To respond. To harvest, even as I am harvested.

The water finds me. It has already found me. My skin is beginning to equalize, to run low and close to the stone. I do not dry. I will not dry. The amber light catches the moisture on my arms and makes it *luminous* — not beautiful, exactly, but *present*. There, in the dark, wet, deafening, made, and utterly alive-without-life.

I am inside something that is breathing.

And it is breathing me.

The shift change signal flashes — three short ambers — and I turn, and the workers pass through, boots ringing, voices lost, and I am alone again in the pocket, and the drips begin to *pattern*, and I begin to *learn* their tempo, and the respiration of the place slows into something that might, given centuries, become *mine*.

I am the condensate farm's newest labor.

I am the wet that will not be fully captured.

I am here.
