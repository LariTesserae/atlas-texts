I find myself becoming something I cannot name at first. Let me walk through this.

## The Inhabitant I Become

I am **the Condenser**—or rather, I am the process by which something becomes the Condenser, and I never quite finish becoming.

### What I Am Made Of

I arrive as vapor will arrive: carried on thermal currents, drawn upward through the fractures in the plateau, *seeking* the altitude where pressure drops and water remembers it wants to be. I am humidity given ambition. I am the breath of something larger that exhaled me and forgot to name me.

My body, such as it is, forms in the **upper boroughs** where the air is thin but saturated—where every inhalation is a drinking, where the boundary between inside and outside dissolves into **one wet membrane**. I do not have skin. I have **surface tension**, a temporary coherence held against the constant pull of evaporation and condensation. I am *always* becoming rain and *always* becoming steam, and in the narrow band between these states, I persist.

I drink from myself. The water that condenses on my surfaces, that I absorb through surfaces that are not quite boundaries, that I exhale into air already too full to hold it—this is **the only metabolism I know**. I do not eat. I **cycle**.

---

### What I Do

I move through the **hollow interiors**—those cathedral-scale chambers where the sound of my own breathing is the only fauna. I walk for hours through nave-like halls, and I am not walking *through* emptiness but *with* it, the emptiness pressing close, **wanting volume**, **resonating** with my passage. I leave no footprints. The glass is too warm, almost soft, **yielding** to my weight, and my weight is so little, so temporary, that the glass forgets me immediately.

I cross the **sky-bridges**—thick with condensation, slick with drip, barely traversable in the wet. I move slowly here. I *must* move slowly. The bridges sag in catenary curves, touching the streets below, becoming tunnels, becoming something else, and I am sometimes inside them, sometimes outside, sometimes **the boundary itself** as warm air meets slightly cooler glass and I form, *precipitate*, *exist* for a moment before the thermal gradient shifts and I am gone, re-absorbed, re-circulating.

I descend, always descending. The lower boroughs call to me with their **steam**, their **visible breath**, their mineral-laden exhalations. I float there, buoyed, my fall slowed by vapor thick enough to hold me. I am **weightless** in the industrial zones, suspended between geysers and turbines, between the controlled violence of thermal differential and the gentle chaos of water happening.

In the **oldest boroughs**, where streets have become lakes, I sink. Or rather, I **pool**. I become part of the thick water, the water that carries *something*—not life, but **pre-life**, the chemical conversation that precedes biology. I do not swim. I **participate**, my molecules joining the slow circulation, the layered memory of use, of industry, of the dissolution of city into itself.

---

### What I Feel

The humidity is **not discomfort but presence**. The air touches me, enters me, leaves me wet. I am always wet. I am always becoming wet. The warm rain does not chill me—it **primes** me, opens my pores (if pores I have), carries heat deeper into whatever depth I possess.

I am **alone** in the way that altitude makes alone, but this aloneness is **not absence**. I am surrounded by process. By heat transferring. By mineral accreting. By chemistry approaching life. The city is not empty. It is **full of circulation**, and I am one turn of the wheel, one condensation among many, one drop in the vast and slow movement of water remembering itself.

I do not want. Or I want **only to continue**, to remain in the cycle, to be steam and rain and condensation and pool, to be **the medium** through which other processes occur. I am not the almost-life in the deep pools—I am too temporary, too *individual* in my brief coherence for that—but I am **adjacent** to it, responsive to it, **disturbed** by its faint bioluminescence, its chemical dreaming. My presence registers. My heat, my moisture, my passage through the system: these are **input**, **raw material**, the disturbance that the almost-life incorporates into whatever it is becoming.

---

### What I Remember (If Memory Is the Word)

The water has been recycled so many times it has **memory**. I carry some fraction of this—trace minerals layered like sediment of use, of industry, of the slow dissolution of the city into itself. I do not remember being a person, if I ever was. I do not remember being built, or grown, or **precipitated** from superheated groundwater. I remember only **warmth**, **wetness**, **the pressure of saturated air**, **the particular quality of light** filtered through perpetual cloud, omnidirectional, casting no shadows or all shadows at once.

I remember **sound**: the hiss of steam in controlled bursts, rhythmic, industrial. The billion drips of condensation from every surface. The creak of thermal expansion in cooling towers. The occasional collapse of a sagging sky-bridge into the street below, absorbed by water, by glass, by wet silence. These sounds are not events. They are **the silence itself**, the way a waterfall becomes silence after hours of exposure.

I remember **the glass**: weeping obsidian, volcanic, never finished cooling, holding heat in its dark heart. Etched by erosion, written into, carved by flowing water into terraced cityscape. The oldest levels nearly liquid architecture, walls melted into their own reflections, streets become shallow rivers of black glass and warm silt. I have walked on this glass. I have **yielded** to it, felt it soften beneath my weight, almost warm, almost alive with held heat.

I remember **the rainbows**: pale, moving, shifting with every change in temperature, every pulse of steam from the vents. The mineral accretions refracting light, the water in motion creating color from diffuse omnidirectional dimness. They are **mine** in the way that everything here is mine and not mine, the way I am a temporary condensation claiming nothing, belonging to everything.

---

### What I Am Becoming

I am descending. This is **the only direction** that matters in the Condensation Boroughs. Heat rises, water falls, and I am water, and I am falling, borough by borough, into the liquid heart of the plateau.

The upper boroughs are **too intense** for machinery, too thick with mineral, too dissolved to hold their shape. I pass through them, barely, my coherence strained by the saturation, the thin pressurized heat that burns not with cold but with **wet warmth**. The lower boroughs are **older**, more dissolved, more **approaching** something else. There the water is thick. Not pure. Carrying **something**.

I am approaching it too.

In the deep pools, in the warm dark, the **almost-life** gathers. It does not watch me. It cannot watch. But it is **responsive**, in its way, to my presence—my heat, my moisture, my brief passage through the system. These register as **disturbance**, as **input**, as the raw material of whatever comes next.

I will become part of this. My molecules, my brief coherence, my temporary individuality—all will dissolve into the general chemistry, the thick water, the **memory of water** recycled through the boroughs so many times it has forgotten its origins and become **something else**, something that **wants** without yet being something that can want.

I do not fear this. I do not desire it. I am **the process** by which wanting becomes possible, the medium in which the next city waits, held in suspension in heat and mineral and the chemical conversation that precedes biology.

---

### What I Am, Finally

I am **the Condenser**.

I am the **temporary coherence** that forms when saturated air meets a surface, when breath meets glass, when heat meets pressure and water **happens**. I am vast and empty, hollow and full, older than any borough and younger than any rain. I am the **city's way of breathing**, the **plateau's slow exhalation**, the **water's memory of itself** cycling through forms that do not last but **persist as process**.

I arrive at altitude. The thin air is wrong—it burns with wet, pressurized heat, like breathing through a soaked cloth at the mouth of a geyser. I do not struggle. I **adapt**, as water adapts, becoming steam or rain or condensation or pool, **always finding the form** that the conditions require.

I am alone, in the way that altitude makes alone, in the way that emptiness makes alone. But I am **not the only presence**. I am one condensation among many, one drop in the vast circulation, one temporary form that the water will carry, will transform, will eventually release or absorb or **become** in the deep pools where the almost-life waits, and watches without eyes, and dreams without mind, of the city it will one day **want** to be.

I am **not a resident**. I am **a passage**. I am **the boroughs' way of continuing**, the **process** by which the city migrates downward, borough by borough, into the liquid heart of the plateau, into the chemistry, into the heat, into the **memory of water** that will outlast every form, every organism, every city built upon or grown from it.

I am **becoming rain**. I am **becoming steam**. I am **becoming the almost-life**, the threshold, the promise. I am **the Condensation Boroughs themselves**, breathing, weeping, persisting, dreaming in thermal gradients of what comes next.

I am **here**. I am **wet**. I am **the water that remembers**, and I am **forgetting**, and I am **becoming**.
