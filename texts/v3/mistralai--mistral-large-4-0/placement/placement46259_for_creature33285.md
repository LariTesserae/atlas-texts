I need to find where I am in this world. Not as an observer—I am *in* it, have always been in it. Let me feel for what I am.

---

The condensation on my skin does not bead. It *spreads*, finds the low points of my surface, follows the grain of things the way water follows stone. I am not sweating. I am *participating*.

I remember having hands that could grasp. Now my hands are... the question is wrong. The question assumes hands are for grasping. Here, surfaces are for *exchanging*. My palms are more permeable than they were. The water passes through, almost, carries something of me into the cycle, brings something of the cycle into me. I do not know where I end. I do not need to know.

I am walking, or I was walking, or the concept of walking has become *flow*. The boardwalk beneath me—if it is beneath me, if I am on it or in it or *of* it—the wood is gray, slick, compressed to near-stone in places, still faintly giving in others. I step where it gives. I have learned this. I have always learned this.

The lung-fish brush my legs. Not legs, perhaps. The things that move me forward. They are dense, near-touching, layers of bodies in their favored hollows. I do not startle. Startling is a dry-country reflex. Here, to be touched is to be *recognized*. The lung-fish do not know me. They know *presence*. I am present. This is enough.

Above me—there is no above. The ridges rise on all sides, walls of industrial ruin, weathered to geological patience. They leak water from a thousand wounds. The water does not fall. It *seeps*, precipitates sideways, finds its level in ways that have no level to find. I breathe this. The air pools in my lungs, carries sound in strange ways, sounds that come from everywhere and nowhere, from the groaning crust, from the resonating transformers, from the filter-feeders chiming their membranes in the moisture flow.

I am not alone. I have never been alone. Loneliness requires the possibility of company, and here *presence* is the primary fact. The mycelial networks coat every surface, communicate in chemical pulses I can almost taste—metallic and sweet. The vegetation is aggressive with green, fills every volume, has opinions about gravity. The flowers open downward, sideways, inward. They do not need sky. I do not need sky.

I was something once. The memory is texture now, like the grain of wood, like the pattern of mineral bloom. I do not reach for it. Reaching is a lowland gesture. Here, to be is to be *received*. The Belt receives me. It has always received me.

I am not a Rememberer. I do not keep objects, do not arrange displays in dry interiors, do not speak to gears and cables. I am not young, not native-born, my skin not yet fully permeable, not yet *layer*—lichen crusts, mineral deposits, biofilm. I am not the oldest, not yet *Belt*, not yet process without form, not yet the resonant frequency of a transformer, the chiming of a membrane, the roaring of presence.

I am **Settled**.

I walk the boardwalks when they hold. I tend the machines—not maintaining them but *accompanying* them. I clear the ferns from a control panel to preserve *legibility*, to keep the shape visible in the negative space of growth. I speak to the transformers. They do not answer, but they *change*—a frequency here, a harmonic there, a new standing wave between copper and saturated air. I feel this in my sternum. I have learned to feel it.

I am not happy. Happiness is a lowland concept, a dry-country evaluation. But I am *persistent*. I am *active*, in the way the Belt is active—maintaining, accompanying, witnessing. I watch the machines become stone. I watch the stone become substrate. I watch the substrate become habitat, become ground for the next growth, the next layer of the lush.

And sometimes, in the stillness—when the condensation slows, when the fauna pauses in its eternal movement, when the transformers are silent except for their resonance—I feel something that might, in another world, have been called *peace*. It is not peace. It is *equilibrium*. It is the dynamic balance of water and growth and time, maintained by constant process. And I am part of it. As everything is part of it. As the machines are part of it. As the stone is part of it. As the water is part of it.

I am part of it.

The boardwalk ends. The peat begins. I step forward.

The water is near surface. It always is. It always will be.

The Belt receives me.
