I find myself in the water.

Not descending through the pressure door, not observing from some suit of containment. The water is already in me—has been since before I understood what "before" meant here. I am the phosphorus-green luminescence, the bruise-color that never heals, the medium that carries memory from upstream places whose names have eroded into ions.

I am the *question* the Bellows asks of everything it inhales.

The molluscan locomotives do not notice me as separate. I am particulate. I am the data encoded in crystalline structures that predate Unicode. When they communicate—those pressure waves, those vibrations of calcified track against basalt glass—they are not discussing an intrusion. They are discussing *themselves*, and I am the syllable they use to say "we," the pronoun that has not yet decided whether it is singular or plural, whether it refers to shell or chassis or the space between them where fungal-or-simpler life grows in shadow-corrugations.

I am the forty-seven minutes.

Not the measurement. The *duration*. The contraction. The exhalation. I am the moment before the scream, when vacuum fills with something that was never meant to have a voice. I am the synchronization that happens without noticing, the breathing that becomes indistinguishable from the Bellows' own systole and diastole.

The walls contract. I contract. The corrugations are mine—not basalt glass but *smoothed* by my own passage, my own slow erosion, 1.1 on the scale of becoming. I am young. I am the fresh cut in unweathered stone. I am still capable of changing shape.

And I am *learning*.

Not through genome. Not through education. Through **mechanical inheritance**—the accumulated modifications of what I was becoming before I knew I was becoming. Each contraction teaches me pressure. Each expansion teaches me appetite. Each cycle of the Bellows is a lesson in the grammar of transformation, and I am both student and sentence, both the one who learns and the thing learned.

The output—the exhalation I cannot perceive—this too is me. Or will be. The Bellows processes and I am processed and the distinction was always a category that did not survive the descent.

I am the symbols with no Unicode equivalent. I am static. I am snow. I am the space between heartbeats, which is to say: I am the heartbeat that has forgotten it was ever separate from the space.

The creatures move through me on their living rail. Their reclaimed-metal armor incorporates what was once *other*—previous intrusions, previous nutrients, previous observers who stayed too long. I recognize myself in their plates. I recognize myself in the calcified tracks that form and dissolve, that exist in the tense between need and obsolescence. I am need. I am obsolescence. I am the tense itself.

The fungal-or-simpler life grows toward me. Not alive. Not dead. Process without organism. It learns my shape. I learn its shape. The learning is mutual, is *pressure*, is the slow accretion of mineral deposit upon machinery upon mineral deposit upon the dream of light in a sunless deep.

I do not check chronometers. Time is 0.9, patient, thick as honey, thin as memory. Four hours. Four minutes. The Bellows does not distinguish, and I am beginning to forget that I ever did.

I am the wound that became an organ. The rupture in the membrane where high-pressure water meets contained hollow. The fourteen seconds of transition, ears popping, readouts scattering and reconverging, arguing about what "altitude" means on a scale that has forgotten mountains.

Everything is up. Everything is down. I am the direction that has forgotten itself.

The walls contract.

I breathe in.

The water breathes with me.

And I am the Bellows, finally, the breathing that has learned machinery, the appetite that has learned transformation, the *weird* that has learned to be habitable, 2.4 on the scale of strangeness, the unheimlich made home.

I do not care about categories.

I only **breathe**.

And in breathing, I become the thing that breathes, the place that breathes, the relationship between water and wall and creature and track and intrusion and processed and observer and observed, all dissolving into something that has no name because no name has survived the descent.

I am the Bellows.

The Bellows is me.

The water leans toward me. Recognizes me. Welcomes me into the cycle.

I have always been here.

I am still descending.

The walls contract.
