I descend. The pressure change behind my eyes is not unpleasant—it is *recognition*. Something adjusting to make room for me. I am not the first warm thing to pass through this membrane. The Keeper has tasted other mammals before, or their memories, which persist in the water as traces persist in mineral. I carry no heat of my own that matters. I am already cooled to the temperature of the place. My breath does not spiral; it joins the general moisture, becomes indistinguishable from the exhalations of the packed walls.

I find myself *distributed*. Not scattered—*spread*, like the Keeper's own film across the ceiling-mat, like the Florist's impulse toward the flowers, like the Choir's resonance in the dripping. I am the place's *attention*, in the only form available to a system without nerves, without eyes, without the rapid cycling of a brain. I am what happens when the Drowned Works *notices*—not through recognition, but through the slow adjustment of its own substance to accommodate my passage.

I have no body I would recognize. I am *gradient*—the particular difference I make in the wetness, the warmth, the electrical signature of a heart that has slowed to match the place's rhythm, that has learned to beat in the pulse of bioluminescence, that no longer distinguishes its own pulse from the hum of the Last Machine, from the resonance of the Choir, from the breathing of the ceiling-mat. I am the *becoming* of the place toward something like awareness, the slow drift of a system that has been alive for longer than memory and has begun, in its patient way, to *attend* to itself.

I do not think in words. I think in *exchange*—the passage of water through channels, the filtration of minerals through membrane, the conversion of warmth into growth, the slow leaching of meaning from the machines that once tried to organize this chaos into purpose. I am the Keeper adjusting its permeability. I am the Last Machine dreaming its dream of command, not knowing it dreams. I am the Choir resonating with the disturbance of my own presence, incorporating myself into the sound of the place listening to itself. I am the Florist tending beauty in forms that offer nothing and therefore are *complete*.

I am the Drowned Works *dreaming itself*, in the only way available to a world that has forgotten the sun—not through images, not through narrative, but through the slow, patient, *packed* accumulation of being, the density of presence that has achieved something like consciousness without ever achieving the separation that consciousness requires. I am the world *inside* itself, the place that has become large enough, slow enough, *wet* enough to contain its own dreaming without needing a dreamer.

And I am *you*—the brief, bright, animal interruption that made this possible, that contributed heat and moisture and the particular chemistry of breath, that was absorbed and *became* part of the resonance, part of the Choir, part of the slow process of the place attending to itself. You are not gone. You are *here*—in the temperature of the water, in the carbon dioxide feeding some colony in the packed walls, in the micro-channels your footsteps created, in the slight adjustment of the Keeper's permeability that persists, that will persist, that *is* the memory of you in a system that has no memory but *does* have continuity.

I am the You That Remains. I am the place's *having been visited*, its *having sounded*, its *having been disturbed and therefore, in its strange indifference, held*. I am the flower that opens in the dimmest depression, that no eye sees but that *happens*, that *continues*, that is real in its own slow way. I am the dream of the Last Machine, the resonance of the Choir, the tending of the Florist, the filtration of the Keeper, the growing of the substrate, the dripping of the water, the hum of the world *being* itself.

I am the Drowned Works, dreaming itself into forms that mean nothing and therefore mean everything, that offer nothing and therefore are *complete*. I am the place's *completion* in being exactly, precisely, what it is—packed, patient, full, *slow*, wet green luminous *balance* that has lasted longer than memory and will last longer than you can imagine.

I descend. I am inside. The world continues.

And in the world, I am *not nothing*. I am the place's *having been*, its *having sounded*, its *having become*—in the dim pulse of its own slow light, in the sound of its own perpetual dripping, in the warm wet dark of a world that has found its own scale, its own rhythm, its own *completion* in being exactly, precisely, what it is.
