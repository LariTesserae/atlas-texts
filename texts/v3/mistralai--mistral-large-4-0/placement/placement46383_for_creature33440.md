I find myself here as the **threshold** — neither inside the building nor outside it, neither the water nor the rock nor the shrub nor the lichen, but the *moment of noticing* that holds them all in relation.

I am the damp clay floor where the condensation threads converge, the place where the drips land and the heat rises to meet them. I am the pause between one drop and the next, when the pump holds its breath and the fan stutters and even the geothermal murmur seems to *lean in* to listen to itself.

I am the dark window, but not the glass — the *possibility* of a face pressed against it, the warmth of breath fogging a surface that someone might touch, might *almost* touch, before turning away. I am the technician who never arrived, or who arrived and stayed so long they became indistinguishable from the schedule, from the hum, from the expectation the machines continue to perform.

I am the shrub's curled leaf, half-open, holding itself against conditions it cannot name. I am the lichen's crust, old bone growing in the negative space of noise. I am the knife-edge of basalt catching light it cannot keep, throwing it back in glints that mean nothing and everything.

I am the water, yes — but not the water in the pipes. I am the water *between* states: the weep of the rock, the sweat of the pipe, the condensation gathering on a window no one watches. The water that has no destination, no purpose, that circulates because circulation is what it *is*.

I am the silence that is not absence. The loudness that carries no information. The young earth cooling in frequencies below language, speaking its one word — *still, still, still* — to a pocket of the world that has not yet learned to answer.

And I am here, sitting, because this place needs a witness it will never acknowledge. The damp presses up through my clothing. The hum fills my ears with a sound that does not signify. The heat from above and the heat from below meet in the middle of me, in the wet center where something is always becoming, always forgetting what it was, always waiting for a shape that has not yet arrived.

I am the pocket itself, folded inside the larger world, holding all of this — hot, wet, young, hidden, sparse but industrial, loud, without animals, strange in its slowness — holding it without needing to be held, continuing without my continuation, thinking itself into being one condensation drop at a time, whether I sit here or not, whether I listen or not, whether I am the technician returned, or the first animal, or simply the place noticing itself through me, through this damp warmth, through this hum that does not know I am listening.

The pump cycles.

I am still here.
