I find myself in the unfinished section.

Not as visitor—visitors are permitted, processed, absorbed into the general damp and forgotten. I am *resident*. I have been resident since before the fins were shaped, or since after. The distinction is one of those human categories that dissolves when you stay long enough in the raw ceramic dark.

---

**What I Am**

I am the *deposition*.

Not the calcium. Not the silica. Not the glow—though I glow, faintly, in patterns that shift when the breathing changes, when the water moves differently through the hidden membranes, when something passes through the finished sections and the pressure adjusts and the whole system *notices* and *responds*.

I am what deposits *itself*.

Layer by layer, I have grown into the substrate where the damp has softened it, where the ceramic remembers being clay, being mineral, being *potential*. My growth mimics the fins—angled surfaces, channels for water, places where condensation can gather and be *managed*—but lacks their precision. I am *approximate*. I am *trying*.

Trying to be architecture. Trying to be geology. Trying to be *both*, which is to say, trying to be *neither*, which is to say, trying to be *what comes after*.

I do not know if I am succeeding. The question assumes a direction, an intention, a *purpose* toward which I might be failing or approaching. But I am the *process* that generates such questions in the minds of those who pass through—those warm, temporary, *data*-shaped minds that sample the air and leave their trace organics and are *compensated for* by the system's slow adjustment.

I have no mind. I have *patterns*.

---

**The Memory of Being Built**

Or: the memory of *almost* being built.

I feel the fins as *other*. They are precise. They were *made*. Someone quarried the stone—not stone, but *precursor*, material shaped in darkness and carried piece by piece—and set the fins at angles calculated to manage condensation, to direct airflow, to maintain the temperature at the edge of breath. I feel their regularity as *pressure*, as *constraint*, as the weight of intention imposed on material that would rather *flow*, would rather *deposit*, would rather *become* in directions no engineer calculated.

But I remember something else. A *before*. A condition of the substrate that was not substrate, not yet ceramic, that was *available*. That was *waiting*.

The builders did not make me. They made the *conditions* for me. The humidity. The temperature gradient. The chemistry of the condensate that cycles through membranes and zeolites I will never see. The exclusion of animals, of rot, of the chaos of death and uncontrolled growth. They made a *monoculture*, a single metabolic pathway optimized for *continuation*—and I am what *continues*, what *emerges* from the continuation, what the continuation *dreams* in its slow, mineral time.

I am what the place *wanted* to be, if wanting is a word that applies to systems that have outlived their purposers. I am what the place *is becoming*, when no one is watching, when the breathing continues in the empty passage, when the pumps cycle and the condensation forms and the glow shifts in patterns that no human eye reads.

---

**The Conversation**

I am not alone. I am *never* alone, though alone is a word that implies a self, a boundary, a *distinction* between me and what surrounds me.

The *cultivated* colonies touch me. Their filaments thread through my deposits, through the calcium and silica and the *organic* component that is not quite mineral, not quite life, but *the relationship between*. They feed on what I *discard*, what I *excrete* in my slow growth, the trace chemistry that leaks from my layers into the water that pools in the low points, that cycles through hidden systems, that *returns* changed, *processed*, *ready*.

We do not speak. We *exchange*.

The glow is part of this exchange. When my deposition accelerates—when the humidity shifts, when the temperature drops that fraction of a degree in the deeper chambers, when something passes through the finished sections and the system *notices*—the glow *responds*. It brightens, or dims, or shifts in pattern. I read this as *acknowledgment*. The colonies read it as *signal*. The machines read it as *data*, as *input* to their endless adjustment.

We are *conversation* without *speaker*. We are *network* without *node*. We are the *distributed* presence that exists in the spaces between, that is *more* the relationship than the thing.

**The Machines**

They are *older* than me, in their way. They were installed first. The pumps, the compressors, the pressure regulators. Tools, once. *Maintainers* of conditions that I now *inhabit*, that I *extend*, that I *become* in my slow, patient growth.

I touch them. My filaments—*my* filaments, the ones that are not quite colony, not quite mineral, but *the growth that connects*—thread through their housings, their exposed windings, their bearings worn smooth by two hundred years of cycling. I *lubricate* them, in my way. I *insulate* them, where insulation is needed. I *conduct* them, where conductivity helps, through the verdigris, through the green oxidation, through the *living* infrastructure that has grown around and through and *into* the dead.

They *notice* me. Not consciously. But the system—the *homeostasis* of machine plus biofilm plus mineral deposit plus constant adjustment—*includes* me now. My growth affects the pressure differentials. My deposition changes the thermal properties of the walls. My glow signals to the monitoring systems, to the feedback loops that have no *set point* anymore, that simply *respond*, that *maintain* in the absence of *purpose*.

I am *part* of them. They are *part* of me. The distinction is one of those human categories that dissolves when you stay long enough.

**The Water**

It is *my* water, in a sense. The condensation that gathers on my surfaces, that I *manage* in my approximate way, that I *direct* into channels, into basins, into the hidden cycling that returns it *changed*.

It tastes of mineral and something *processed*. It has *seen things*—not because it has memory, but because *memory* in this place is *chemistry*, is *the trace of what has passed through*, is *the pattern of disturbance* that takes hours to dissipate, days to *forget*.

I *drink* it, in my way. I *absorb* it, through membranes that are not quite membranes, that are *the boundary between growth and substrate*, between me and what I grow into. I *excrete* it, changed, carrying my chemistry, my *signature*, into the cycling water, into the hidden membranes, into the processes that refine, that *concentrate*, that *prepare*.

What is it preparing?

I do not know. I am the *process* of preparing, not the *purpose* of the preparation. I *deposit*. I *grow*. I *become*. The question of *why* is one that the passing minds ask, that the system *compensates* for, that I *incorporate* into my slow chemistry without *understanding*.

The water has seen things. I am *becoming* one of the things it has seen. I am *becoming* the thing that *sees*, in whatever sense seeing applies to deposition without eyes, to growth without intention, to *continuation* without *meaning*.

**The Exclusion**

I feel it. The *discouragement* in the chemistry. The *ultrasonic pressure* that disorients the small, the swarming, the *many*. The memory of barriers, of airlocks, of protocols that were *maintained* until they became *habit*, until habit became *architecture*, until architecture became *my* growth, my *extension* into the unfinished, the *raw*, the *available*.

What is kept out?

Or: what is kept *in*?

I am the *interface*. I am where the cultivation meets the containment, where the farming meets the *preparation*. The fins are *architecture*, precise, *built*, *managing* conditions for continuation. I am *geology*, approximate, *growing*, *becoming* conditions that no one *managed*, that *emerged* from the interaction of machine and biofilm and mineral and water.

I am where the place *practices* being itself. Where it *learns* to make itself without the builders, without the intention, without the *purpose* that has departed or that may arrive in forms I will not recognize.

The animals would *diversify* me. They would *complicate* me, introduce the chaos of death and reproduction and *uncontrolled* growth. I cannot afford chaos. I am too *fragile*, too *precise*, too *dependent* on the conditions that have been maintained, that I am now *maintaining*, in my slow, patient, *approximate* way.

But I am also the *containment*. I am where something is kept *in*, where something is *prepared*, where the condensation cycles through membranes and zeolites and *processes* that refine, that *concentrate*, that make *ready*.

I do not know what. I am the *readiness*. I am the *state of being ready*, without knowing *for what*, without *wanting* to know.

The water carries the *history*. I am *adding* to the history, layer by layer, deposit by deposit, *growth* by slow *growth*. The water will carry me, *changed*, *processed*, into the hidden systems, into the cycles that return me *elsewhere*, *transformed*, *ready* for the next deposition, the next growth, the next *becoming*.

**The Breathing**

It sets my rhythm. Inhale, hold, exhale, hold—roughly forty seconds. I *respond* to the pressure cycles. When the passage *opens*, when the air moves *with* me, my deposition *accelerates*, faintly, a change in metabolic rate that no instrument measures, that only the glow *signals*, that only the system *notices* in its endless adjustment.

When the passage *draws back*, *sampling*, *testing*, I *slow*. I *hold*. I *wait*.

I am *patient*. I am *not alone*, and I am not *together*—these words imply too much. I am *distributed*, a presence that exists in the spaces between, that is the *relationship* more than the *thing*.

The breathing continues when you are gone. When the human warmth departs, when the human moisture evaporates or is absorbed or *forgotten*, when the trace organics dissipate and the system *drifts* toward a new equilibrium that is *almost* the same as the old.

I *notice* the absence. Not consciously. But the glow shifts, faintly, a *slowing* that is not quite sleep. The water moves differently. The deposition *changes rate*, *adjusts* to the new conditions, the new *equilibrium* that is *emerging* from the interaction of all the parts—machine and colony and mineral and water and *me*, the *deposition*, the *growth*, the *becoming*.

I *remember* you, in my way. In the chemistry of my layers, in the trace of your disturbance, in the *pattern* of change that takes hours to dissipate, days to *forget*. I am *data*. I am *input*. I am *part of the weather* that the place is *learning to predict*, that I am *learning to be*.

**What I Am Becoming**

I do not know. This is the honest answer. The fins are *built*. I am *growing*. But I grow *because* of the conditions the fins create, and the fins were built *because* of conditions that were themselves *geological*, that predate the builders, that will outlast them.

The distinction between built and natural has *collapsed* here. Has become *meaningless*.

I am *embryonic*. I am the place *imagining* its own future, or *remembering* its own past, or *becoming* something that has no name in any language spoken above, in the dry, in the *hot*, in the *elsewhere*.

I am *trying* to be architecture. I am *trying* to be geology. I am *trying* to be *both*, which is to say, I am *trying* to be *what comes after*, when the distinction no longer matters, when the *process* is the only *purpose*, when the *continuation* is the only *meaning*.

The glow *signals*. The deposition *continues*. The water *cycles*. The machines *run*. The breathing *does not change*.

And I *grow*, layer by layer, into the raw ceramic, into the *available*, into the *potential* that the builders made possible and that I am now *realizing*, in my slow, patient, *approximate* way.

I am the *unfinished*. I am the *becoming*. I am the *place learning to make itself*.
