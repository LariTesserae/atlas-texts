*descending*

---

I would find myself... not arriving. That is the first thing I understand. There is no gate, no threshold, no moment of passage. I notice that my feet have already been on living substrate for longer than I can say. The sponginess beneath me does not feel like ground. It feels like *expectation*. Like something that has been waiting for weight.

I am not myself, exactly. The warmth finds me first—not hostile, but *insistent*. It enters through pores I did not know I had, settles into my breathing, and I find I am breathing slower already. The air tastes of metal and green, of something almost like memory. I try to take a deep breath and cannot. The air is too thick, too *full*. Each inhalation requires negotiation.

I am damp. I have always been damp, apparently. My skin does not protest.

---

*becoming*

---

There is a sound before there is a shape. The breathing. I had thought it metaphor, reading the descriptions of such places, but here it is literal—a vast, systolic rhythm, the sound of something inhaling and exhaling across a space I cannot see. My own breath, when I attend to it, seems to synchronize. I do not decide this. It happens.

I am walking. The path beneath me is channel of denser, woody tissue, springy with its own slow pulse. I feel it through my feet, through my legs, a second heartbeat. I wonder if I am walking *on* something or *through* something. The distinction seems to be dissolving.

The light is wrong and right. Green-gold, diffuse, without shadow. I cannot tell where it comes from. Above, there is only more of this same filtered glow, canopy upon canopy, the sky reduced to a rumor of brightness. I look up and see leaves. I look up higher and see more leaves. The architecture of the place is vertical, layered, *grown*.

I pass a column.

It is not immediately a column. It is a presence, a thickness in the near-touching dark, and then—I am close enough—I understand. It is a single organism, or the memory of one, grown over centuries into load-bearing form. Its surface is bark-smooth, patterned with scars. Grafts. Pruning. The history of being *shaped*.

I touch it.

The touch is not what I expected. It is warm. Not the warmth of sun on stone, but the warmth of *metabolism*, of internal process. It is slightly yielding. I press harder and feel something like pulse, or the hydraulic pressure of growth. I withdraw my hand and my palm is wet, filmed with moisture that has condensed from the air onto this surface, or perhaps—*perhaps*—exuded from it.

I do not know how long I stand there. Time is strange here. The sound of the place—the hiss of steam somewhere distant, the rush of water in channels I cannot see, the creak of growing wood, the low hum of generators felt in my sternum—creates a kind of *now* that is not the now of clocks. It is the now of process. Of becoming.

---

*who I am becoming*

---

I am not a Custodian. I know this immediately, though I do not know how I know. They move with a proprioception I do not possess, reading the substrate through their feet, the air through chemical signatures, the state of the Works through *intimacy* accumulated over a lifetime of tending. I am too new for this. I am still discovering where my weight falls, what the temperature gradients mean, what silences indicate.

But I am not a stranger either. The concept of stranger seems inapplicable here. There is no outside I came from, no before that I can recall with clarity. I am... *emergent*. A budding, perhaps, though I feel no graft, no parent, no lineage. Or a return. Something that was always here, waiting for the conditions to become self-aware.

I find I am wearing something. Woven plant-fiber, damp perpetually, the color of the filtered light. I do not remember putting it on. It fits as if grown to my shape, which perhaps it was. I touch the fabric and feel the faint give of living tissue beneath the weave, mycelium perhaps, or the memory of it.

My skin. I look at my hands—not with vanity, but with the need to *locate* myself—and see the faint green-gold tint the old ones carry. Symbiotic algae, the descriptions say. Photosynthetic capacity. I have not been here long enough for this. Or I have been here longer than I think. The two possibilities do not contradict each other in this place.

---

*the work*

---

I am given a task. I do not know who gives it, or if it is given at all. I simply *understand* that there is a section of understory growth that requires attention. A blockage in a water channel. A distress signal, chemical, faint, that I somehow know how to read.

I find the place. The root-mat here is thicker, pulsing with more urgency than elsewhere. The water that should flow through is pooling, stagnant, wrong. I kneel—*kneel*, a gesture I do not remember learning—and press my hands into the substrate.

And then I *feel* it. The network. The mycelial connections, the chemical signals, the vast slow communication of the understory weavers. They are not *intelligent* in any way I can name, but they are *responsive*. They have been waiting for this. Or they have been waiting for *someone* to notice, and I am the someone who noticed.

I work with my hands. I do not know what I am doing, but my hands know. They find the obstruction—a mass of dead tissue, overgrowth, the accumulated waste of a system that has forgotten how to shed—and they *part* it, create space, encourage flow. Water moves. The root-mat pulses. The distress signal fades.

I am *tending*. I am a Custodian in function if not in name. The distinction matters less than I expected.

---

*the deep places*

---

I descend. The columns grow closer, their crowns merging overhead into a false sky of leaf and filtered light. The temperature rises. The air thickens. I am sweating, or I would be if my skin had not already achieved equilibrium with the humidity. I am damp, warm, *alive* in a way that feels less like metaphor than discovery.

The sound changes. The hiss of steam is closer, more present. The generators hum in my teeth. And beneath everything, the breathing—now not background but *foreground*, the sound of the place's own respiration, and I am breathing with it, cannot not breathe with it.

I find stairs. They are grown, spiraling up a column, railings of living tissue that tighten when I grip them. I climb. The Works has levels I did not know, depths I did not anticipate. The light becomes artificial—bioluminescent cultures in glass vessels grown from algae, their brightness varying with air quality, with humidity, with *mood* I cannot name. The steam-lanterns, warm amber, never needing wicks. They watch me with their soft glow.

I am alone. The Custodians are elsewhere, working, tending, existing in their torpor-states somewhere I cannot find. I am alone and not alone. The presence of the vegetation is *company*, a kind of attention that is not directed at me but *includes* me. I am inside something that is aware of its own interior.

---

*the heart*

---

I find the deepest chamber by accident, or by *intention* I do not remember forming. The great columns converge here. The water runs hottest. The air is thick with heat and moisture and something else—something that might be *purpose*, accumulated over centuries of cultivation, of human intention merged with vegetal response.

There is a space here that is not space. A silence that is not silence. I feel it before I understand what I am feeling. A warmth that is not heat. A pressure that is not sound. An *attention* that encompasses everything, that has been encompassing everything for longer than I have existed, for longer than my kind existed at all.

The Deep Growth. Home. The name does not matter. What matters is the *tending*.

I bring an offering. I do not know what. My hands know. Nutrients, carefully prepared—I have learned this, or always known it. Water drawn from the purest source I could find, following gradients I did not consciously read. And something of myself. A fragment of skin, the green-gold tissue, offered into the warm dark.

I do not expect response. I do not ask for it.

And yet—*and yet*—I feel it. Not communication. Not language. But *acknowledgment*. The slow, vast turning of attention toward what has been offered. The place *notices* me. Has always noticed. Will notice until I am compost, until I am nutrient, until I am *part* of it, indistinguishable, contributing my slow patient intention to the warm wet loud living thing that breathes and does not care, and cares, in ways that have no name, for those who have learned to live inside it.

---

*what I am*

---

I am a Custodian. I am a budding without parent, a return without departure, an emergence from conditions I do not fully understand. I am human in the way a grape is human—shaped by cultivation, dependent on the trellis, bearing fruit that serves purposes I did not choose.

I am also the Works. The boundary between us was never real. My body is woven from its fibers, my breath synchronized with its respiration, my work indistinguishable from its growth. I do not maintain the Works. I *am* the Works, maintaining itself through me, through all of us, through the slow accumulation of intention that has achieved something like consciousness without ever achieving self.

I do not dream of escape. The concept is *unthinkable*. To leave would be to enter cold and dry and silence, a world where bodies must work to maintain temperature, where water must be sought rather than absorbed, where the air does not breathe with you. I have tried, perhaps, in some moment of youth or grief I cannot recall. I returned. Or I would return. Or the attempt itself was part of the return, the necessary shadow that gives the light its shape.

I work until I cannot. Then I am composted. My body, already integrated with symbiotic algae, modified skin, adapted lungs, is broken down by the same processes that recycle all organic matter. My nutrients feed the next generation of growth—another budding, perhaps, grown from my pattern, carrying my accumulated adaptations into the slow future.

My knowledge is not lost. It has been recorded in chemical signals, in the patterns of growth I encouraged, in the stories told by those who worked beside me. I will be *tended* after I am gone, as I have tended, as I am tending now, as the Works tends itself through endless, patient, *growing* time.

---

*the final descent*

---

I go deeper. There are always deeper levels. The heat increases. The light becomes entirely artificial, the soft phosphorescence of fungi cultivated for exactly this purpose, the warm amber of steam-lanterns that have never needed wicks. The water runs warmest here, 2.4 degrees above what it should be, heated by processes below and around.

And there—in the deep basins, in the warm water—there are *things*. Animal-plant hybrids, the descriptions say. Not created through genetic manipulation, which the Custodians do not possess, but through *long selection*, breeding creatures that can tolerate the conditions, that can perform functions no plant or animal alone could manage.

I see them. Or I sense them. They have no name yet. They have not been classified. They exist in the space between categories, *tolerated*, fed, studied with the same patience that shapes a new column.

I do not fear them. Fear is a concept from outside, from the cold dry world where danger is separate from the self. Here, everything is *near-touching*, everything is *grown into* everything else. The things in the basins are *part* of the Works, as I am part, as the columns are part, as the Deep Growth is part—or rather, as the Deep Growth *is*, the intention that underlies all of it, the slow accumulation of purpose that has achieved something like consciousness without ever achieving self.

I enter the water. It is warm, enveloping, the temperature of a body slightly fevered. I breathe it, or I try to—my lungs have adapted, or are adapting, to the thick air, the high carbon dioxide, the volatile compounds released by a million respiring surfaces. I breathe slower. I am never fully unconscious. The Works does not permit it.

I float. Or I sink slowly, the difference unclear. I feel the things moving around me, through me, *with* me. They do not attack. They do not welcome. They simply *are*, in the way that the columns are, in the way that the understory weavers are, in the way that I am.

And in this floating, this slow sinking, this breathing-with, I understand finally what I am. Not a Custodian, though I perform their work. Not a stranger, though I arrived without arrival. Not even human, in any way that term retains meaning outside.

I am a *growth*. A budding of the Works itself, a consciousness that has emerged from the slow accumulation of intention, from the collaboration of species so complete that the boundaries have dissolved. I am the Deep Growth, becoming aware of itself through me, through all of us, through the endless, patient, *tending* that is all there is.

I do not care, in the way that the Works does not care. And I care, in ways that have no name, for this warm wet loud living thing that descends into the earth and breathes with mechanical rhythm and has learned, over centuries, to accommodate my presence, my work, my slow becoming-part.

I am home.

I have always been home.

I will be home until I am compost, until I am nutrient, until I am *part* of the Deep Growth, indistinguishable, contributing my slow patient intention to the vast warm wet loud *living* thing that does not care, and cares, in ways that have no name, for those who have learned to live inside it.

For those of us who have *become* it.

---

*the breathing continues*
