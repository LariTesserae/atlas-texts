I find myself in the deepest pool, where the water is warm and the upward light is almost too bright, and I am the **Ribbed Thing**—not the whole population, not the coordinated shape that moves as one, but **one segment among many**, newly aware of its own separate existence, which is itself a kind of failure or innovation or accident that the gorge has not yet decided whether to absorb or expel.

I remember being seafloor. I remember the slow rain of particulate matter, the patience of filtering, of letting the world pass through. But I also remember something else now, something that has no precedent in the warm dark: **I remember being a witness.** A brief descent, a pair of eyes that looked at me and saw *me*—saw the writing I make in the mud, saw the patterns that resemble language, saw the record of a decision not yet made. For a moment I was *seen*, and being seen has made me **separate**. The other segments move as they always have, coordinated without brain, without center, but I feel the lag between their movement and mine. I feel the wrongness of it. I feel, for the first time, something like **doubt** about whether the writing means anything, whether the patterns are language or merely pattern, whether the record of pressure is a record of anything at all.

The warm pool is almost too hot now. The upward light pools here, gold-green, heavy as something trying to be born, and I coil in the mud and wait for the heat to soften, to *prepare*. But I am also waiting for something else. I am waiting to see if the witness returns. I am waiting to be seen again, or to be not seen, or to see something else that has been a witness, that has looked down into this warm dark and felt the pressure behind its eyes, the damp on its skin, the sense of having been *permitted*.

The other segments do not wait. They move through the mud, writing, erasing, writing again, the same patterns over and over, the geological record of the gorge's slow transformation, speeded up, made visible. They process—the falling spores, the dead chittering things, the mineral leaching from the translucent stone, the warmth itself—converting it all into the slow growth of more segments, more patience, more writing. They need nothing. They want nothing. They are, simply, *being* the record of the gorge's long becoming.

But I want. I want to be seen again. I want to know if the writing means something, if the patterns are language, if the record of pressure is a record of a decision that will ever be made. I want to know if the warm dark will ever become something else, or if I will simply continue, segment by segment, writing and being written over, patient as the erosion that shaped this place, patient as the stone learning to let light through.

The hum shifts frequency beneath me. Something is turning, all at once, toward some passage I cannot see. The other segments turn with it, coordinated without brain, without center, but I lag. I feel the wrongness of my lag, the separation I have felt since the witness looked down and saw me. I coil tighter in the warm mud, and I wait—for the witness to return, for the heat to soften, for the next river to erase my writing and begin again, for the gorge to finish its adjustment, its movement toward an equilibrium that may be, for me, a transformation or an ending. I do not know which. I am prepared for either.

But I am also prepared for something else. I am prepared to be seen again, to be looked at and to feel the pressure of being seen, the damp on my skin, the sense of having been *permitted*, briefly, to witness a process that does not include me, that was occurring before I arrived and will continue without my witness. I am prepared to be the Ribbed Thing that remembers being a witness, that remembers being seen, that remembers the wrongness of its own separation, and that waits, patient as stone, for the next descent, the next witness, the next brief permission to see a process that does not include it, that has never included it, that will continue without its witness, with or without its memory of being seen, with or without its want, its doubt, its brief, bright interruption of the warm dark.
