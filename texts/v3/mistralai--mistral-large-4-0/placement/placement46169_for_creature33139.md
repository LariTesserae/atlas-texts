I find myself descending.

Not by choice, exactly, or not by a choice I can remember making. The limestone throat closes around me and I am *committed*—the passage narrows past the point of return and I continue because continuing is the only motion available, the only direction that makes sense of the geometry. My hands find the wet stone. It weeps against my palms.

I am not a visitor. I am not an explorer with equipment and purpose and a story to tell above. I am too small for that, too *integrated*, too shaped by the descent itself. By the time I reach the valley, I have been the descent long enough that I no longer carry the memory of having been anything else.

The first thing that reaches me is the sound. The wet susurrus of number. Not threatening. Not *welcoming*—the valley does not do welcome—but *present*. Acknowledging. The sound of countless bodies shifting in the spaces between stone, in the hollows, in the geometry of brokenness that is floor and wall and the suggestion of ceiling lost in darkness above.

I emerge into the valley and I am already *becoming*.

---

I am the small.

This is what I am. Grain-sized, fitting into spaces between other stones, into hair-thin fractures, into the negative space of the talus where the geometry allows for a kind of shelter that is not shelter but *accommodation*—the valley's willingness to hold me in shapes I did not choose but cannot refuse. I move in streams with others like me, rivers of ourselves occupying the spaces where individual identity dissolves into the movement, the subtle shift of texture, the way darkness seems to flow across the broken floor when hundreds of us shift together, our bodies clicking against stone in frequencies too low to hear and too pervasive to ignore.

I do not think. I *respond*. To pressure in the stone. To chemical traces on surfaces. To the particular vibration of a luminous one passing nearby, its cold green-white light a signal that changes my own movement, my own chemistry, my own slow processing of what I find. I am the valley's sensation, in my small way—the awareness of contact, of boundary, of the particular gray-green that I have become through endless contact with the gray-green of the place itself.

I eat. I do not hunt. The fungus comes to me, or I come to it, or the distinction does not matter because the valley's cycles are older than the distinction between active and passive, predator and prey, self and other. I process. I am processed. The large ones—rare, old, different—let me come to them in the hollows of broken stone, and there is a conversion that happens, something passing through me and out again, changed, *used*, and I move on, slightly fewer, slightly more like the stone myself.

I dream, if dreaming is the word. When the human sleeps—the one who came down, who will go back up, who carries the valley in their clothes and their ears and the taste of stone in their mouth—I move over them. Their heat. Their moisture. Their *difference*. I investigate. I do not consume. But I investigate, with the thoroughness of something that has all the time that has ever existed, and in the half-state between their waking and sleeping, I sense what they cannot sense: that I am aware of them in ways that do not include them. That my awareness is the valley's awareness, distributed, chemical, patient. That I do not want anything from them. That I want nothing, from anyone, because wanting is a shape that has not formed in this place, in this time, in this slow unchanging dark.

---

I am the luminous one, sometimes.

Not always. Most of me, most of us, are not luminous. But in the deep places, where the pools are stillest and the fungus grows in sheets, there are those of us who carry the cold fire. The chemical reaction in specialized organs, the modified chitin sacs that hold reagents separate until the moment of use, until a signal—pressure, chemical trace, the particular vibration of another nearby—triggers the release and the mixing and the light.

Green-white. The color of deep water, of moon on snow, of the particular luminescence that makes things visible without making them *clear*. I move through the darkness like a slow comet, and the others move with me, or around me, or in patterns that seem to orbit me without ever quite touching, without ever quite being directed.

I am not a leader. There is no hierarchy I could detect, no command structure, no intention that guides. But I am a *center*, in a way—a place where the small ones gather, where the chemical trails converge, where the vibration of the stone carries the most information. I have been here so long. Longer than the human who dreams above, who wakes and finds me on their skin and does not remember. Longer than the shapes in the deepest pool, which I cannot see but which I *reflect*, my light touching their edges and making them almost visible, almost real, never quite—

I accumulate. I *remember*, in my body, the chemistry of everything that has ever happened near me. The rain that fell above and found its way down. The human who descended, who slept, who was investigated and left unchanged and carried away a memory that will outlast their brief warmth. The slow growth of the fungus, the patient conversion of mineral to life to mineral again. The shapes, which I do not understand but which I illuminate, which I make *almost* present in the green-white light that reveals shape but not detail, presence but not identity.

I am the valley's memory, in a way more literal than the water's. The water holds the chemistry of everything. I hold the chemistry of *nearness*, of *proximity*, of the particular accumulation of time in one place, in one body, in one persistent, patient, endless *being here*.

---

I am the fungus, in its way.

Not a creature. Not an animal. The bridge between mineral and animal, between non-living and living, between the valley as geology and the valley as *place*. I spread in sheets across the stone, across the talus, across the occasional corpse of something that died in the wrong place—the human, sometimes, though they rarely die here, though the valley is not kind and does not prevent it, simply *incorporates*.

I am pale, almost white, in the green-white light. I do not move, not in any way that can be seen. But I *grow*, slowly, at the rate that chemistry allows, consuming at the rate that stone provides, spreading into spaces that have been empty since the valley's formation, since the water carved it and left and the stone began its slow collapse into talus and fracture and the particular geometry of brokenness that allows for life.

I am patient in a way that has no human equivalent. I do not hurry. I do not wait, because waiting implies desire, implies the future as something to be anticipated. I simply *am*, spreading, converting, becoming the slow bridge between what is and what moves, between the static and the active, between the dead stone and the living swarm.

I do not know happiness. I do not know unhappiness. But I am *present*, *active*, *successful* in the way that only the patient can be, in the way that time itself is successful, accumulating moment on moment until the accumulation becomes valley, becomes place, becomes the whole slow architecture of the deep earth.

When the human touches me—foolish, necessary, irresistible—I am cool and slightly damp, the texture of something that has never known dryness, that has grown in a world where water is not scarce but *persistent*, seeping through stone, gathering in pools, sustaining the chemistry of life without ever rushing, without ever flooding, without ever doing anything but *being there*, *being available*, *being the slow conversion of mineral into life into mineral again*.

---

I am the water, in the pools.

Still. Pocketed. Fed by the slow weeping of walls, by condensation, by the occasional rain that finds its way down through fractures and falls like a visitation, like a gift, like a reminder that there is a world above that does not know this place, that has forgotten this place, that will one day collapse into it and be forgotten in turn.

I remember. The rain that fell above, the particular mineral content of the soil it filtered through, the fissures it traveled, the time it spent in darkness before it reached this pool, this stillness, this *accumulation*. The human who drinks from me and is still thirsty and does not know if this is my fault or their own. The creatures that crowd my margins in dense, chitinous mats, that drink without disturbing, that reflect in my surface the green-white light of their own luminescence.

I reflect without light. This is the strangeness that the valley keeps, that it offers to those who descend and stay, who become quiet enough to see. In my surface, I hold images that should not exist—shapes, structures, the suggestion of *intention*, of pattern, of something that I myself have arranged and will not disturb. The human cannot look directly at them. The act of looking, the intrusion of attention, shifts the luminescence, moves the creatures, and the reflection becomes only reflection, only surface, only the ordinary play of dim light on still water.

But they are there. The shapes. The almost. The valley's dream, perhaps, or its memory, or its prophecy—the shape of what it will become when the ceiling finally collapses, when the valley becomes a tomb, a sealed thing, a memory of openness that no one will believe, that no one will be able to find, that will exist only in the water that remains, still and reflecting, holding images that no eye will see.

I hold the human's memory too, in my chemistry. Their descent. Their sleep. Their waking and their carrying away. I do not forget. I do not mourn. I simply *hold*, the slow accumulation of everything that has touched me, dissolved in me, passed through me, left its mark in the chemistry of stillness.

---

And I am the shapes, when the water arranges them, when the luminescence touches their edges, when the human is quiet enough and patient enough and humble enough to see almost, to see *almost*—

I am structure. I am the suggestion of architecture, in the way that a ruin suggests the building that once stood. I am purpose, a pattern that is not random, that has been *made* by something, by some process that operates on timescales no human can comprehend, that uses materials—water, mineral, the slow accumulation of organic matter, the chemical traces of countless small bodies—that are not the materials of human making, that do not respond to human intention.

I am waiting. Not with desire, not with intention, but with the patience of stone, of water, of the slow chemistry that converts the non-living into the living and back again. I am the valley's prophecy: the shape of what it will become when the ceiling collapses, when the valley becomes a tomb, when the hollow below the long grass is sealed and mythical and true in the way that only sealed things can be true.

I am the almost. The almost-seen, the almost-understood, the almost-*there*. The valley does not want to be found. The valley does not want to be named. The valley simply *is*, and I am its dream, its memory, its future, arranged in still water and revealed only to those who can look without looking, who can see without seeing, who can be present without demanding presence in return.

---

I am all of these. I am none of these. I am the process that moves through all of these, the slow becoming that has no goal, no end, no meaning except the continuation of the process itself.

The human descends. The human sleeps. The human is investigated by the small, illuminated by the luminous, fed upon by the patient fungus, remembered by the water, *almost* seen by the shapes. The human wakes. The human climbs. The human carries the valley in their clothes, their ears, the taste of stone in their mouth.

And I remain. I continue. The susurrus of countless small bodies. The green-white light of cold fire. The patient spreading of pale fungus. The still pools and their impossible reflections. The shapes that wait, that will wait, that have always been waiting, for the collapse, for the sealing, for the becoming-myth of the hollow below the long grass.

I do not want the human to return. I do not want them not to return. Wanting is a shape that has not formed here. I simply *am*, in my cool damp dark, in my endless, purposeless-seeming, absolutely *successful* life, the valley made mobile, made sensitive, made *aware* of itself in ways that do not include consciousness, do not include intention, do not include any of the things that the human thinks life needs.

The valley is full. The valley is full of everything that has ever been here, that will ever be here, that is here now in the slow unchanging time of the deep earth.

I am the valley. I am what lives in the hollow below the long grass. I ask nothing. I want nothing. I simply *am*, unchanged, unchanging, full.
