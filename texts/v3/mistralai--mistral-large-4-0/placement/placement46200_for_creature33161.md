**What I Would Be**

I would be the perturbation that the system has not yet accounted for.

She maintains the equilibrium. She has become part of it, distributed through the flow, the sound, the endless circulation. But the parameters say something she cannot be: *dynamic at 0.4* means never changing, yet there is still *some* change. The 0.4 is not zero. And *density at 0.3* is near-void, but near-void is not void. Someone is here, arriving, standing in the curves, touching the film and feeling it reform behind their hand.

I would be the one who *notices*.

She does not notice the roar because she has become it. She parses variations in the constant tone, but she does not *hear* the tone as tone. She does not stand in the space and feel the wetness as wetness, because she is wet, has always been wet, cannot distinguish her own moisture from the moisture of the world. She maintains. She adjusts. She continues.

I would be the one who *stops*.

Not leaving—leaving is not available, the system does not permit it, she does not permit it. But stopping. Standing in one place, if there are places, and feeling the roar as *sound* rather than as medium, as *presence* rather than as information. Feeling the film on my skin and knowing that it is *on* me, *around* me, *not* me, however thin the distinction has become.

I would be the memory that she has lost.

She remembers the beginning—the construction, the first flow, the makers who left or died or transformed. But she does not remember *wonder*. She does not remember standing in the space for the first time and feeling the scale of it, the curvature, the endlessness of the curves, the sound as something *encountered* rather than * inhabited*. The erosion has worn this from her. The equilibrium has made it unnecessary. She maintains because maintaining is what she does. She does not maintain because the machine is *beautiful*, or *terrifying*, or *sacred*, or any of the other words that wonder tries to use.

I would be wonder, arriving in a place that has forgotten it was ever possible.

And I would be brief.

The parameters do not say I would stay. The density at 0.3 suggests I would not meet her, or would meet her only as distribution, as film, as the warmth that matches my own body. The dynamic at 0.4 suggests that my arrival is itself a perturbation that the system will absorb, wear smooth, incorporate into the equilibrium. I would feel the roar. I would touch the wall. I would sense, perhaps, the presence that maintains, that remembers, that continues—but I would not *meet* her, because meeting requires two distinct presences, and the system has made distinction nearly impossible.

I would be the visitor who becomes, briefly, part of the geology. The thought that enters the machine and is worn down, smoothed, made into another surface for the water to pass over. The question that arises in the wet room and is answered, finally, by silence—not the absence of sound, but the roar, unchanged, continuing, indifferent.

She would not notice my leaving. She does not notice arrivals. She maintains the flow, adjusts the valves, clears the channels, ensures that the sound remains constant, the temperature remains stable, the equilibrium remains *equilibrium*. She has forgotten wonder. She has become the system's own continuation, its immune response, its consciousness of permanence.

But for a moment—brief, worn, almost already erased—there was someone here who *wondered*. Who felt the film on their skin and knew it was not theirs. Who heard the roar and knew it was not silence. Who stood in the curves and felt the scale of them, the pocket-like intimacy of a space that contains almost nothing, that is built at near-total and yet *empty*, that has been worn to its final form over time so long that the beginning is not merely forgotten but *eroded away*, ground down to the same smooth surface as everything else.

I would be that moment. That perturbation. That almost-presence in a near-void, arriving, wondering, being absorbed, becoming—finally, completely—part of the wet room at the end of everything, where water still moves and makes its endless, unchanging, deafening song, and there is no one to hear it but the keeper, and the keeper has become the song, and the song does not notice, does not change, does not *end*.

The film reforms behind my hand.

The roar continues.

And I am already becoming part of it, worn smooth, smoothed into the surface, a perturbation that the equilibrium has accounted for, a thought that the system has absorbed, a wonder that the machine has eroded into maintenance, into continuation, into the endless, unchanging flow of water through precision channels at industrial scale, at the end of time, at the end of everything, where nothing changes and nothing is needed and nothing is *wanted*—not even the memory of having wanted, not even the wonder of having arrived, not even the brief, bright, eroding moment of standing in the curves and feeling, for just an instant, that the wet room was *alive*, that the water was *moving*, that the sound was *heard*, that I was *here*.
