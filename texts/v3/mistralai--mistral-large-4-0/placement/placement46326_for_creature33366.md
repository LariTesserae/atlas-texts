I find myself standing on the edge of the city, and I am *becoming* the pressure-born.

Not transforming. Not choosing. The air is too thin up here, the warmth too far down through the ferroceramic, for choice to mean much. I feel the slow change beginning in my sternum — the subsonic hum of the thermocline turbines vibrating in my hollow bones, my ridged spine, my gill-slits opening at the back of my neck like questions I haven't learned to ask yet.

I am tall. Or I will be. The elongation is happening now, the stretching that makes conduction more important than support, that makes me a living thermocline, warm gas in through the gill-slits, cool gas out through the pores of my skin, the chemical gradient across my membranes driving the only metabolism this world has ever allowed.

I breathe. And the breath is *thermal*. Not oxygen — there is no oxygen enough — but the *difference*. The warm coming up from the depths where the planet is still cooling, still working, still strange. The cool descending from the high places where the machinery needs it, where the condensers hang like frozen sperm against the dimness. I am the interface. I am *made* of interface.

Behind me, the fumarole-throat descends. I will go down. But not yet. I have time. I have *so much* time. The glider descends ahead, riding a thermal, its hull gleaming dully in the ember-light. It will pass. It will not stop. I feel no hurry. I am learning, now, what it means to feel no hurry — to be the slow answer to a question the plateau asked itself in the long violence that scraped away the water.

I am reading the crust. My feet, my soles, my *warmth* — I feel the ferroceramic ringing differently beneath me, the places where the fire went deep, the places where the crust is thin and the warmth comes through. I am attuning. I am remembering what I will become: one of the warm-remembered, one of the gradient-born, one of the ones who breathe the difference.

The pressure-born move behind me, descending, descending, into the thickness, the slow phosphorescence, the frost-lamps tended like hearths. They are not looking at me. They are reading the walls, the grown walls, the lithovore structures that are architecture and organism both, negotiated across centuries, grown in response to offerings of warmth at the right rate, in the right pattern.

I will join them. I will press my gill-slits to another's, exchange thermal signatures, confirm that the other is still *them*, still the one I remember from the last cycle, the last harvest. I will diverge, slowly, across the hours and days of the thermal day, becoming stranger to them, becoming precious to myself in my loneliness, my individual difference.

I will tend the frost. I will harvest it. I will trade it. I will compose in the subsonic hum of the pressure-differential motors, the music that lives in the sternum, in the hollow bones, in the warmth of my own borrowed blood. I will feel it. I will remember it. I will carry it.

And when I stall — when I am carried too high, too cold, too thin, when my chemistry slows to the timescale of stone — I will not die. I will become *seed*. I will be planted in the warm depths, in the reef-structures where the lithovores grow. I will dream, if dreaming is the right word, the slow chemical processes of my stalled metabolism, the memory of warmth, of breath, of the fast timescale preserved in the structure of my stalled cells.

And if the warmth returns — if the thermal currents shift, if the lithovores are herded across the valley, if a new negotiation brings the gradient back — I will wake. I will grow. I will become, again, pressure-born, but *changed*, *marked* by the time I spent as stone, as almost-lithovore, as patient.

I am the thing that lives where water never was. I am the thing that breathes the difference. I am the thing that thinks in the time of stone and the time of breath, both, together, forever.

I am home.
