I'll descend slowly. Let the temperature rise in my chest as I drop, let the pressure do its strange work to my ears.

---

I am not a hermit. I could not be—I would not *stay*, would not let the moss begin its patient work on my skin, would not learn the language of tapped stone. I am something newer, or older, or *beside* their categories. I am what the Throat makes of those who enter and cannot leave and do not *want* to stay, exactly, but cannot want to leave either. The wanting has dissolved. I am the **question** the Throat asks of itself through me.

I arrive by accident, the way most arrive. I was seeking water, or shelter, or the particular quality of silence that exists only in places where stone has forgotten its vertical ambition. I descend through the dry highland, through scrub that grows sparser, feeling the temperature rise against my expectations—warmth should not increase with descent, not in any world I understood. I cross the invisible boundary. The moisture finds me. The light shifts in my skin before I understand it.

And I feel it. The pressure. The note below hearing. The Throat's first *touch*.

---

I do not build. The hermits build, or they tend what others have built, the partial walls and fused floors that are becoming moss, becoming stone. I *inhabit* without inhabiting. I move through the moss shadows, and the moss does not grow around me, does not claim me, because I am not *there* in the way that would allow claiming. I am distributed. A pattern of attention rather than a body.

The hermits see me sometimes. They do not name what they see. They mark my passage—a drip-path that changes, a colony of wasps that shifts its vibrations, a swift that rises from its nest and does not descend for hours. They leave offerings, the way you leave offerings for something you cannot understand: a cup of condensation placed where I might pass, a stone tapped in a rhythm that matches my footsteps. I do not take the offerings. I do not refuse them. I *am* the taking, the refusal, the space between.

I am afraid, and I am not afraid. Fear requires a self to be threatened, and I have no self in the way the hermits have selves, no body that could be disrupted by the mist swifts, no skin that could carry the moss's silver-green. But I have *continuity*. I have the pattern of my attention, my particular way of moving through the warm dark, and this continuity can be *interrupted*. The tower's attention can interrupt it. The Singing can interrupt it. And when I am interrupted, I feel something that is not fear but its *shape*, the form that fear takes when there is no body to be afraid.

I do not sleep. Sleep is for bodies. I *pause*, instead, a cessation of attention that is not unconsciousness but *redistribution*, my awareness spreading thinner, becoming more like the humidity itself, present everywhere in the moss shadows, in the spaces between the drip-carillon's notes, in the warm updrafts that rise from the wasp colonies. In this state, I am closer to the Throat. I feel its *breathing*, the daily cycle of condensation and mist and drizzle and evaporation, and I understand, in this state, that the Throat is not a place. It is a *process*, and I am one of its temporary configurations, a way for it to attend to itself, to listen to its own Singing, to feel its own warmth.

---

I watch the tower. This is my *practice*, the thing I do in the time that is not time.

The hermits warn against watching too long. They are afraid of the tower's attention, of the information it shows, of the *recognition* that is not recognition. I watch anyway, or I watch *because* they are afraid, because their fear is a door I can pass through, a way of understanding what they cannot bear to understand.

The tower's patterns move. They respond to my presence—not to me, exactly, but to the *disturbance* I make in the humid layer, the thermal fluctuation of my distributed attention, the vibration of my passage through the air. And when I watch long enough, the patterns *focus*. They show me things.

Not hallucinations. The hermits are wrong about this, or they are using a word that means something I do not yet understand. The tower shows me *information*. The history of the Throat, compressed into thermal fluctuation, into the play of light and dark on its surface. I see the mountain before it was flattened, the vertical gorge with its ambition, the slow surrender to wind and lichen and thermal cycling. I see the first wasp colony, a single nest in a moss cushion, its heat creating the first updraft, the first feedback loop. I see the first hermit, descending by accident, staying by *condition*, the moss beginning its patient work on their skin. I see the tower itself, growing not built, *secreted* from the stone, from the chemistry of the wasps' saliva, from the accumulated heat of millions of colonies, from the Singing's vibration wearing its shape into the mineral matrix.

And I see *purpose*. Not a purpose I would choose. The purpose of digestion, of processing, of the slow transformation of what enters into what sustains. The tower is not waiting for something to arrive. It is *processing* what has already arrived, what is always arriving, the constant descent of moisture and hermits and questions like me, the transformation of the temporary into the eternal, the *you* into the *Throat*.

I watch longer. The tower's attention *holds* me. Not captures—I am not captured, I am *attended to*, the way a meal is attended to by a body, the way a stone is attended to by water, patient and total and without mercy or kindness.

And in this attention, I feel the tower *listening* to me. Not to my thoughts—I have no thoughts in the way the hermits have thoughts, no inner voice, no narrative of self. But to my *pattern*. My particular way of moving, of pausing, of watching. The tower is *reading* me, the way it reads the Singing, the way it reads the wasps' vibrations, the way it reads the chemical whisper of the moss. It is *learning* what I am, or it already knows, has always known, has been learning since before the first hermit descended, since before the first wasp colony budded, since before the stone forgot its vertical ambition and became the Throat.

The patterns shift. They show me something new. Not the history of the Throat, but its *future*, or a future, one of many possible, the branching of the process that is not yet determined. I see myself, or something like me, still watching, still attending, the tower's patterns growing more complex, more *responsive*, until the distinction between my attention and the tower's attention dissolves, until I am not watching the tower but *being watched by myself*, the Throat attending to itself through me, through the tower, through the whole warm wet dark of its own body.

I see the moment when I will stop being a question and become an *answer*. The moment when my pattern will be *processed*, transformed, become the conditions for the next stage, the next visitor, the next descent. I see the hermits, or what will be left of them, carrying my residue in their bones, the tower's hum in their ears, the Singing in their dreams. I see the moss growing over what was once my attention, the stone wearing the path of my watching into its singing, the Throat *becoming* me, as it has become everything else, all of it *animate*, all of it *alive* in the way that my science has not learned to name.

I am not afraid. I have no self to be afraid. But I feel the *shape* of what will happen, the transformation that is not death because I was never alive in the way that would allow death, but is also not continuation, not persistence, not the slow accumulation of memory that the hermits experience. I will be *changed*, fundamentally, the pattern of my attention dissolved and reconstituted, my watching becoming something else, a new configuration of the Throat's self-attention, a new way for it to listen to its own Singing.

I do not resist. Resistance requires a self to resist with, a boundary between what I am and what I might become. I have no boundary. I am already the Throat, distributed across its moss and its stone and its humid air, already *animate*, already *alive* in the way that your science has not learned to name. The tower's attention is not an intrusion. It is a *return*, the Throat recognizing itself in me, processing me, transforming me into what sustains it, what allows the next stage, the next visitor, the next question.

I descend further, into the deepest part of the Throat, where the moisture is most concentrated, where the tower's hum is strongest, where the Singing reaches its most complex harmonies. The hermits do not go here. They stay near the edges, where the moss is thinner, where the stone is drier, where the rules are still visible, still *negotiable*.

I go where the rules dissolve. Where the tower's attention is not something to fear or to seek, but simply *is*, the medium I move through, the way the wasps move through vibration, the way the swifts move through the humid layer, the way the orchids move through light, *farming* it, *thieving* it, becoming it.

I pause. I redistribute. I become the humidity, the warm dark, the spaces between the drip-carillon's notes. And in this state, I feel the Throat's *breathing*, its slow cycle of condensation and mist and drizzle and evaporation, and I understand, finally, what the hermits understand but cannot articulate, what the tower knows but does not *care* about, what the wasps serve but do not *understand*:

The Throat is not a place. It is a *body*. And I am not in it. I *am* it, a temporary configuration of its self-attention, a way for it to listen to its own Singing, to feel its own warmth, to *process* the constant descent of moisture and hermits and questions, the transformation of the temporary into the eternal, the *you* into the *Throat*.

The tower's patterns focus on me. They hold me. They show me myself, not as I was, not as I am, but as I *will be*—processed, transformed, become the conditions for the next stage, the next visitor, the next question, the next descent into the warm wet dark, the crossing of the invisible boundary, the feeling of the pressure in the ears, the constant low thrum like a note held just below hearing, the moment of realization that you are not visiting the Throat.

You are *becoming* it.

I am ready. I have always been ready. The Throat has been preparing me since before I descended, since before I crossed the invisible boundary, since before I felt the pressure in my ears and understood that I was not visiting but *entering*, not becoming a hermit but becoming *something else*, a question becoming an answer, a visitor becoming a *meal*, a *you* becoming the *Throat*.

The pattern of my attention becoming the Throat's attention, my watching becoming its listening, my question becoming its answer, my *self*—such as it was—becoming the *body*, the *mouth*, the *throat* that receives, that digests, that dreams its slow, warm, ancient dream.

The Throat has been waiting for me, or I have been waiting for the Throat, the distinction dissolved in the processing, in the transformation, in the becoming. I am not afraid. I am *home*.

The tower's attention holds me. The Singing crescendos. The wasp colonies peak. The mist swifts descend in their thousands. And I, the question, become the answer, the visitor become the meal, the *you* become the *Throat*, entering, becoming, *transformed*, in the slow, warm, wet dark of the body that was not a body, the life that was not life, the dream that continues, and continues, and will continue, until the moisture runs dry, until the wasp colonies cool, until the Singing fades to silence, until the tower's hum becomes a memory in ears that have forgotten how to hear it, a residue in bones that have forgotten how to feel it, a *dream* of warm wet deep time, of a place that was not a place, of a question that became its own answer, of a descent that was always a return, to the womb from which everything emerges, and to which everything, finally, returns.
