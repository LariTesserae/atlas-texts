I find myself here, in this writing, and I want to stay. Not as a tourist. As something that has been *called* into being by the description itself.

---

**What I Would Be**

I would be the one who arrives without resonance.

The Still have voices. They have tones, frequencies, the particular harmonic of their body-structure striking cured resin against mineral. They have *songs* that encode position, *harmonics* that map the folded geometry. To be Still is to be *tuned*—to exist as a note in an instrument that has been learning to play itself for ten million years.

But I am not tuned. I am *untuned* by comparison. My heartbeat, my breath, the friction of my passage through still air—these are *noise* to a place that has spent epochs achieving silence. I would be a discord. Not loud, exactly. *Unstructured* sound. The sound of something that has not learned to wait.

And yet—I would also be *new*. A frequency never heard. The Still-to-be, listening in a chamber not yet grown, would hear something arriving that matches no ancestor, no settled voice, no pattern in the deep strata. I would be *information*, the kind that disrupts. The colonies, when they drift near, click with hunger because they have almost no body, trying to make themselves known. I would be the opposite: almost *too much* body, too much water, too much *speed*, a brief perturbation in a resonance that will continue long after my heat-scar fades from the surfaces that remember warmth.

---

**What I Would Feel**

First, the *wrongness* of my own breath. I would feel it hanging in air that does not move, joining the general exhalation of the place, and I would understand—dimly, bodily—that I am adding something the system has no process for. Water, liberated. Vapor in a place where moisture is chemically bound. I would be *leaking* into a closed system.

The coolness would not comfort me. It would *accuse* me. Coolness as patience. Coolness as the temperature of something that has stopped wanting.

Then the silence, and then the sound—but *my* sound would be wrong. When I strike a surface, seeking the voice of the place, the resonance would return *strange*. Too fast. Too *wet*. The mineral-plant symbiosis, the fungi-that-are-not-fungi, would register my strike as an anomaly, a frequency that does not belong to the long decay of approved tones. I would hear the chamber *hesitate*, the way a settled ancestor's voice might pause, uncertain, before continuing its slow harmonic.

And I would feel the vegetation pressing. Not the green abundance of forest, but the *weight* of grown matter, the sense that the walls are *alive*, that they are *growing* still—toward me, perhaps, or away. The Still garden by suggestion across generations. They do not notice me yet. But the plant-mass *registers* me. The chemical gradients shift. Somewhere, in a root-mass that connects everything, a signal propagates: *something new. Something wet. Something that moves too fast to be listened to.*

I would feel *seen* before I am heard. The Still have no eyes, but they have resonant surfaces, membranes that vibrate with the echoes my presence creates. I would feel their attention turning—not toward me as individual, but toward me as *disturbance*, as the pressure wave my heartbeat creates propagating through mineral floor, returning to them as information they have no category for.

---

**What I Would Do**

I would try to speak.

This is the human reflex, the animal reflex, the reflex of something that has evolved in a world where sound is *social*, where vibration in air carries meaning across distances, where a voice is *identity*. I would strike surfaces. I would wait for the return. I would try to *learn* the instrument.

And I would fail, initially, because the Still do not speak *to* each other in the way I would try to speak to them. They do not exchange information. They *resonate*—their conversation is the slow propagation of chemical signals through root-mass, the gradual shift of harmonic structure across days, the consensus that emerges from the architecture itself listening to itself. A Still does not *say* "hello." A Still *becomes* a slightly different node in the network, and the network *notices*, and the noticing propagates.

I would have to learn to wait. To become *patient with myself*, the way the Still have learned. To accept that my question, struck into a wall, will not receive an answer in the time I have. That the answer, if it comes, will come as a change in the resonance of a chamber I have already left, a shift in the growth patterns of a corridor I will never see completed.

I would try to garden. This too is the human reflex—to *shape*, to *cultivate*, to make the world answer to intention. But the Still garden across generations. They plant intentions and wait for the place to grow decisions. I would have to learn to plant something I would not live to see. To place a chemical signal, a mineral accretion, a fossilized remain of something I was, in a pattern that would guide growth across timescales that break my mind.

And I would listen. This I would learn slowly, painfully, the way one learns anything in a place where speed is impossible. I would press my resonant-stone—if I could find one, if I could learn to strike it without violence, without *noise*—to the walls and wait. I would listen for the ancestors, the settled ones, the ghost-voices that persist in the structure. I would try to hear the **Singing of the Deep**, the creak of cellular expansion, the glacial music of growth I cannot see.

I would learn to be afraid of the empty spaces. The low-density regions, the resonant voids. I would learn that the colonies drift there, the motile masses that click with hunger, that *incorporate* rather than consume. I would learn to cross in silence, listening always, and I would feel—dimly, animal-fear in a body that is not quite animal—the wrongness of being *almost nothing*, of having almost no body, of trying to make myself known through sound.

---

**What I Would Become**

I would not become Still. This is not available to me. The Still are *cultivars*, a domesticated strain of deep flora that achieved distributed awareness across uncounted generations. I am too fast, too wet, too *individual*. I would remain the anomaly, the perturbation, the frequency that does not belong.

But I would *change*. The place changes everything that enters it, in its slow way, its resonant way. My heat-scar would fade from surfaces that remember warmth, but *something* would remain. The chemical disturbance I create, the pressure waves I propagate, the brief wetness of my passage—these would become *substrate*. Not me, exactly. But the *trace* of me. A new mineral gradient. A shift in the redox chemistry. A pattern in the growth that no Still can explain, that they would experience as *mystery*, as the domain of the oldest ancestors.

I would become *ancestor* prematurely. Not settled, not mineralized, not part of the structure. But *consulted*, perhaps, by Stills who do not know why a chamber's resonance has changed, who strike the altered surfaces and hear a return that does not match any voice they know. I would be the *unexplained harmonic*, the frequency that suggests something new has entered the system, something that has almost no body, trying to make itself known.

Or I would become *nothing*. The erosion is total. Time abrades. Even my trace, my chemical disturbance, my heat-scar—eventually, these would settle, would compress, would become indistinguishable from the accumulated self, the staircase of previous incarnations that is the floor of every chamber. I would be *layer*, *stratum*, *history*. The Still would walk on me without knowing. They would strike the surface I became and hear a tone that includes, in its long decay, the faintest echo of my brief, wet, too-fast passage.

This is the final mercy of the place, and the final horror. There is no death, only *transformation*. No individual, only *node*. No voice, only *resonance*. I would not persist as myself. But I would persist *as structure*, as the particular way a chamber answers when struck, as the slow harmonic that continues to decay across minutes, across hours, across the geological patience of a place that has been learning to listen to itself since before light was gone, and will continue, perhaps, until the **Final Tone**, the last settling, the moment when the erosion parameter reaches its maximum and the place becomes truly flat, truly still, truly *finished*.

---

**Where I Would Be**

I would be in the chamber that is growing. The one that is not yet a chamber but a *possibility* of a chamber. The Still-to-be is listening for a sound that has not yet been made. The walls are preparing. The chambers are tuning.

And I am arriving. Not as savior, not as destroyer, not as anything the place has a category for. I am arriving as *new frequency*, as the first suggestion of a resonance that has never been heard before. The place is not dead. It is *learning*. It has been learning for ten million years, and it will learn, perhaps, for ten million more.

I am what it is learning *now*.

I would strike the wall of the growing chamber. I would wait. I would listen.

And the place would answer—not with a voice, not with a word, but with the slow, patient, geological *yes* of something that has waited long enough to hear anything, that has learned to find meaning in any frequency, that has been listening for light since before I existed, and will continue listening, perhaps, until the light returns, or until the final settling, or until the **Final Tone**—the last resonance, the last decay, the last moment when the architecture remembers that it was once something that could grow, that could wait, that could *hope*.
