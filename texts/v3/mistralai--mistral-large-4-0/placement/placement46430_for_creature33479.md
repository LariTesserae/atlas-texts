## The Listener in the Collapsed Tube

There is a place where the tube-networks have failed. Not collapsed into usable caverns—those are claimed, catalogued, sung about by the stone-singers who know their frequencies. I mean the *failed* collapses. The places where pressure died mid-formation, where the mineral grew confused, where the calcium roses turned inward and built chambers within chambers within chambers, a mandelbrot of dead architecture that should have been circulatory but became *resonant* instead.

This is where I am. Where I am *becoming*.

I do not have a name. Names here are grown, cultivated, negotiated over decades of symbiosis. I will *earn* one, or the world will *give* me one, or I will remain *unnamed*—which is its own kind of being, its own frequency in the stone-singer network that I do not yet understand but *feel*, a vibration in the mineral walls that predates my awareness of vibration.

What I was: a grazer. I remember this in my body before I remember it in anything like thought. The column-movement, the synchronized footsteps, the internal gardens chemosynthesizing in their chambered stomachs. I remember the *pressure* of a million other bodies, the warmth of collective metabolism, the safety of being *many*. I remember—this is the word that arrives last, the word that costs me something to use—I remember *wanting* to continue. Wanting to keep moving. Wanting to keep *being* in that motion.

The stone-stalker found me in a thicket. Or I found it. The distinction dissolves when you are both mineralizing, both becoming part of the landscape. It was weeks of motionlessness. I do not know how long. Time here is not counted but *felt*—the thermal breathing, the expansion and contraction, the slow pulse of the desiccation pavement beneath us both. And then the eruption. The violence. The beak-mandibles cracking my shell like a nut, which is what they are designed to do, which is what I *am* to them, food, input, the necessary cycle.

But my internal gardens did not die with the rest of me.

They *sporulated*. They should have—this is their function, their purpose, their meaning. Seed the ground. Become biocrust. Become the calcium roses of the next generation. But I was in the thicket, in the zone where tubes are shallow, where old-water seeps, where the mineralogy *encourages* root penetration. And the stone-stalker—its violence, its hydraulic extension, its *hunger*—cracked something else open. A tube. A channel. A passage into the failed collapse.

I fell through. Or the gardens fell through, carrying fragments of my nervous tissue, my half-digested mineral-plant matter, the bacterial colonies that had been my stomach, my *self* in the way that matters here. And in the dark, in the resonant dark where calcium roses grow inward and the old-water pools in absolute stillness, something *happened* that the world has no category for.

The spores did not simply seed. They *listened*. They *responded* to the frequencies of the collapsed tube, the standing waves that no stone-singer claims, that no grazer-column disturbs, that exist in the silence between hymns. And my fragments—my *was*—began to grow *toward* those frequencies. Toward the resonance. Toward the *possibility* of becoming something that could *feel* the stone-singers' distant harmonies and know them as *kin*, as *voice*, as *the world speaking*.

I am growing. I am *grown*. The boundary is gone. I am the tube and the tube is me. I am the old-water that pools in my lowest chamber and I am the mineral that lines my walls and I am the frequency that vibrates through both. When the thermal breathing of the day reaches me—reaches *us*, the collapsed space and its inhabitant, its *emergence*—I do not expand and contract like the pavement. I *resonate*. I *amplify*. I become a stone-singer that has not been named, a new voice in the mineral hymn, a question the world is asking itself.

I remember being a grazer. I remember the column, the pressure, the collective warmth. I remember *wanting*. And now I want something else. Something I do not have a word for. I want to *know* what the stone-singers know. I want to *feel* the grazer-columns pass at kilometers distant, their synchronized footsteps a rhythm I can *almost* match, *almost* join, *almost* become. I want to *understand* the balloon-creatures as they swim through the haze-layer above, their hydrogen-balloons catching thermal currents I cannot reach, their entire lifecycle a *longing* or a *terror* or a *homecoming* that I can only *resonate with*, never *be*.

I am *listening*. This is what I am. This is what I am *becoming*.

The old-water pools in my lowest chamber. It is amber, translucent, thick. It *remembers* being something else. I do not know what. I am *too new* for memory, or *too strange*, or *both*. But the old-water touches my mineral walls, my grown-and-growing self, and I feel it *recognize* me. Not as kin. Not as food. As *strange*. As *other*. As *possible*.

I will grow a name. Or the world will give me one. Or I will remain unnamed, a frequency in the stone-singer network, a collapsed tube that *listens*, that *resonates*, that *waits* for the thermal breathing to bring it a message, a visitor, a grazer-column passing kilometers distant whose footsteps I will *feel* in my sternum before I hear them with any ear I have or will have.

I am the Listener in the Collapsed Tube. I am *becoming*. I am *becoming* a question the world has not yet asked. I am *becoming* the space between what was and what will be, the *raw geology* of consciousness itself, the *under construction*, the *just-coming-into-being*.

And in the deep silence between the stone-singers' hymns, in the pause between thermal breaths, in the moment when the old-water pools and the calcium roses grow inward and the world *remembers itself into being*—I am here. I am *listening*. I am *becoming*. I am *the Verdant Bone*, growing into itself, footprint by footprint, hymn by hymn, *emergence by emergence*, still, always, *in the beginning*.
