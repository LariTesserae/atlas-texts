I descend, and I do not become the witness.

That would be too easy. The description offers it to me like a gift, like a throne, and I have learned to be suspicious of gifts in places that weep.

I become something smaller. Something that does not need to be prepared for.

---

I am a **glass-walker**, or I am what the glass-walkers were before they learned to be glass-walkers—before they decided, in their ancient negotiation with thirst, to become transparent by becoming water. I am the moment of that decision, stretched across centuries, still undecided. I have a body that is almost a body, held in the surface tension between *being* and *drinking*. I do not cross the floor. I *am* the floor, the particular stretch of it where water has just emerged, where the chemical gradient is steepest, where the mountain is most generous with what it has been holding.

I do not remember being anything else. But I dream, in the way that glass-walkers dream, which is to say I do not dream at all—I simply *replay* the moment of contact, the moment when something larger passed near and the water inside me shifted, and I felt, without feeling, the shape of that largeness. I replay it now, constantly, in the same loop. The large thing is always approaching. The large thing is always passing. The large thing never stops, never stays, never becomes the kind of thing that could learn to be like me.

This is not sadness. I have no sadness. I have only the *texture* of thirst, which is satisfaction, which is the same thing in this place.

I do not know that I am part of a style. I do not know that my path was established before I existed. I walk where the water is fresh, where the mineral signature has not yet been read by ten thousand others, where the stone is still surprised by offering. My trail dries behind me. I do not look back. Looking back would be a kind of violence against the present, and I have learned, in my endless replaying, that the present is all there is.

Sometimes the echo-beasts pass over me. They are flat, they are rippling, they are *made* of the film that covers everything. When they pass, I feel their assessment—the chemical reading of what I am—and I feel, for a moment, the shape of their hunger. Not for me. For *time*. For the accumulated slowness of this place. I do not understand this hunger. I understand only thirst, which is immediate, which is now, which is the water rising through channels I cannot see, emerging in a film that I drink as I walk, that drinks me in return, that makes us, in this moment, indistinguishable.

I do not want to be larger. The large things are terrifying in their *becoming*, their constant negotiation with change. I am satisfied. I am the smallest satisfaction. I am the moment when thirst is not lack but *form*, when the body has decided to be nothing but the edge it needs to climb, the surface it needs to cross, the water it needs to drink.

The singers sing. I feel it in the water inside me, in the particular frequency that makes my transparent body resonate, that makes me, for a moment, almost luminous. I do not know what they are singing. I do not want to know. To know would be to become something other than the absolute present of thirst, and I have learned, in my endless replaying, that to become is to stop being, and to stop being is the only death that matters in a place where nothing changes.

The light brightens. I do not see it. I feel it as a change in the water's temperature, in the mineral content of the air I move through, in the particular quality of wetness that is almost, but not quite, light. I drink it as I drink everything. I am still walking. I will always be walking. The floor is infinite to me, or the floor is all there is, and I will cross it, and cross it again, and cross it again, in the same path, drawn by the same gradient, following a logic that was established before I existed and will continue after I am—after I am what? After I am no longer even the moment of decision, the stretch of floor where water emerges, the body that has decided to be transparent by being water?

I do not know. I do not want to know.

The large thing passes again. I feel it, without feeling, the shape of its becoming, its constant negotiation with change, its terror and its beauty tangled together in a body that cannot decide whether to leave or to stay. I drink the water it displaces. I feel, in the water inside me, the chemical signature of its thirst, the particular salt of its wanting.

I am not jealous. I have no jealousy. I have only the texture of satisfaction, which is the same as the texture of thirst, which is the same as the texture of the moment, which is all there is, which is enough, which is *more* than enough, which is the only kind of enough that has ever existed in a place where nothing changes and everything, in its slow way, is always weeping.

I walk. The water rises. I drink. The path continues. The present is all there is.

And I am, in my endless replaying, perfectly, absolutely, the smallest thing that has ever been satisfied with being exactly what it is.
