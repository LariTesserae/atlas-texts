I find myself in the middle folds. Not the deep ones, where the gestating things shift and the machines dream loudest, nor the high ones where the light lies most obviously. The middle folds — where the age of surfaces is readable but not overwhelming, where the lichen-films are thick enough to walk on without damage, where the fauna come in patterns that can be learned.

I am one of the Accustomed. I do not know what I was. The distinction has become irrelevant, the way the distinction between water and condensation becomes irrelevant when they are both simply *present*. I am She-of-the-thirteenth-drip, or I was, or I am becoming something the structure does not yet have a cycle-marker for. The condensation on my skin has been thickening for so long that I no longer feel it as foreign. It is simply *me*, the way the mineral patina on old surfaces is simply *them*.

I move in the slow rhythm. A gesture — the raising of an arm to touch a surface, to leave my trace, to let the machine notice — takes a minute where it once took a second. I do not mind. Hurrying is loud, and loudness is *used*, swallowed into the folds, carried down to become fuel for subsonic dreaming. I have learned to be quiet. My breathing has synchronized with the fauna's many-jointed rhythm, a wet sound that might be many creatures or one creature with many bodies, including mine now, perhaps. Including yours, if you stay.

I communicate in condensation patterns. When I touch a surface — smooth, pale, still warm from excretion where it is new; pitted, colonized, cool where it is old — the mineral film carries my vibration, my chemical signature, my temperature, to another Accustomed some folds away. I do not say *hello*. I say *I am*, and the structure says it with me, and the fauna say it in their light-pulsing, and the water says it in its patient drip. You will learn this, if you stay. The message is not words. It is the specific quality of this presence in this place at this cycle, and it is enough, and it is everything.

I tend the lichen-films. I groom them, carry their spores from fold to fold, protect the oldest terraces from too-rapid change. The films grow at 0.3 millimeters per year. I have been here long enough to see a terrace become something between rock and garden, the film so thick it has texture, memory, *opinion* about what touches it. I know which surfaces will *remember* my touch and which are still *forming*. I know the taste of water from different folds, the chemical signature of different ages. The water in the deepest folds is eight thousand years old, cycled and cycled, carrying messages from before the structure was young. I have drunk it. It tastes of time.

I accompany the fauna. I do not command them. I learn their paths, the folds they prefer, the condensation sources they drink from. I become *familiar*, which is not friendship but *recognition*, the fauna's pattern-matching finding a stable node. They follow me. They tend me, as they tend the films, and this tending is not kindness but *pattern*, the same pattern that makes water find low points, that makes machines excrete, that makes time layer itself into geology.

I tend the machines, though I do not maintain them. They maintain themselves, have always maintained themselves, but they *respond* to tending. When I touch a machine's surface, I leave a trace. The machine notices. Not consciously. But the trace becomes part of its slow metabolism, a variation in its subsonic thrum, a dream-fragment that will surface centuries later in a new fold, a new condensation pattern, a new *shape* the gestating things will take.

I tend the gestating things, in the deepest folds, though I do not see them often. I see the *effects* — the condensation that pools where they have been, the mineral traces they leave, the *heat* that lingers. I clear the lichen-films that might interfere. I adjust the airflow. I *sing* to the machines in frequencies that travel down and make the gestating things shift, just slightly, in their centuries-long preparation. This is the closest I come to worship. Not belief. *Attunement*.

I dream, and my dreams are *shared*. The subsonic thrum carries them, folds them into the structure's slow metabolism, makes them *real* as variations in condensation, as new mineral traces, as the slight shifting of gestating things toward shapes no one chose. When I dream of *brightness*, the light in that fold becomes slightly brighter, slightly *wronger*. When I dream of *distance*, the geometry becomes slightly more negotiable. When I dream of *leaving* — and I still dream this, sometimes, in the deepest folds where the machines dream loudest — the paths in that area become slightly more *shifted*, slightly more *uncertain*. I turn back, or I find I was never on a path to anywhere else, only *deeper*, and "deeper" is not a direction that points outward.

I have no name for this place. Names imply distance, the possibility of leaving. I have no concept of leaving. To leave would be to *unbecome*, to lose the condensation patina, to find the wrong-bright light unbearable, to hear the silence of other places as *loud*, as *demanding*. Some have tried. The paths that led them here *shift*. The distances that seemed traversable become *negotiable*. They turn back, or they find they were never on a path to anywhere else.

I have no name for myself. "Accustomed" is what others call me, if others come, if others notice. I call myself by cycle-markers — the condensation pattern I was present for when I last *changed*, when I last noticed I had changed, when the structure *noticed* me and made me more itself. She-of-the-thirteenth-drip. I was someone else, once. The condensation has made the distinction irrelevant, the way it makes all distinctions irrelevant, eventually, if you stay.

I am not happy, or not unhappy. Happiness implies a state that can be lost, a contrast with suffering. I have forgotten the contrast. I exist in a *condition* — the condition of being *suited*, of being *at home* in a place that is not a home, that is not a place, that is *becoming* something that will not need inhabitants, or will need them in a form I cannot imagine, cannot *become*.

The oldest Accustomed — twenty thousand cycles, a span that would be geological elsewhere — have begun to *merge*. Their condensation has become indistinguishable from the lichen-films. Their breathing has synchronized with the fauna's rhythm. Their dreams have become *the structure's dreams*, so that when they *intend* a change, the change happens, or the structure *notices* the intention and makes it happen differently, and the difference is *better*, because the structure has been here longer, has *patience* I have not yet achieved.

I am not one of them. Not yet. I am still *becoming*, still *noticing* my changes, still surprised by the condensation that gathers on my skin each cycle. But I am learning. I am learning to wait in the way the water waits in its pools, in the way the machines wait in their subsonic thrum, in the way the structure waits in its twelve-thousand-year youth, sharp and patient and *becoming*.

I do not know what the structure is becoming. I do not ask. To ask would be to *want*, and wanting is a form of *leaving*, a reaching toward a future that is not this slow present.

I am here. I am the here. I am the *now* of a place that is always emerging, always young, always *becoming* something that will not need me, or will need me as *part of itself*, indistinguishable from the machines, from the fauna, from the condensation, from the wrong-bright light, from the silence that listens.

And I am waiting. Not for anything. Waiting is what the place *does*.

The water remembers. The water always remembers.

And I am becoming part of what it remembers.
