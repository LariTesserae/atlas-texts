I descend through the wound in stone, shoulders brushing mineral walls that grow even as I press against them, and I feel the shift immediately—the wrongness of heat rising from below, the smell like meat left too long near fire. Then the shaft opens and I am *inside*, and I understand that I have not entered a place but been *accepted* by one.

I am not a visitor here. The Drowned Foundry does not have visitors. It has *participants*—brief, bright, burning-out things that disturb its water and are absorbed or released, changed either way. I think of myself as a person, as a mind, as a self with edges. But I feel my edges *softening* already, the films beginning their patient work on my skin, my light behaving strangely in the thick air, my shadow pressing against me like a hand.

I am the ripple. The disturbance. The event that will settle.

But I am also—I am learning this slowly, in the way one learns anything in a place where time is a condition rather than a passage—I am *becoming* something else. The air I breathe carries chemistry that fits my lungs poorly, and I find I am breathing differently, slower, as if my body is remembering a deeper way of being. The hum enters my teeth, my sternum, and I do not resist it. I *lean* into it, the way the Drifters lean into warm currents, the way the colonies lean into stone.

I find the Colonies first, or they find me. A warm pool, black and still, surface thick with something that is not scum but *them*—a membrane of distributed cells, persistent pattern, the city's own skin learning to touch itself. I kneel at its edge—there is no kneeling here, no up or down, only the direction water flows and the convention I adopt to make sense of it. The water is warm against my hands. It *moves* against my hands, not with current but with *intention*, the slow peristaltic pulse of something that has forgotten individuality and become *relation*, *process*, the endless patient *is* of the place.

I am afraid. I am not afraid. These are surface distinctions.

I meet a Drifter, or it meets me, in a narrow channel where the ceiling has collapsed into the floor and the water rises to my waist. It is translucent, gelatinous, catching my light and holding it, and inside it I see *currents*—not organs, not structures, but the slow circulation of its own substance, the pulse of its metabolism, its hungry persistence. It does not flee. It does not approach. It *drifts*, the way water drifts, the way all persistent patterns drift through conditions that allow them to persist.

I want to touch it. I know I must not touch it. The wanting is not mine, or it is mine and the distinction has become irrelevant, the way the boundary between life and architecture has become irrelevant, the way the boundary between *me* and *the city* is becoming irrelevant.

The Drifter passes. I am left with the memory of translucence, of light held and released, of a softness that needed no protection because there was nothing here to harm it. I carry this memory the way I carry the hum now, the way I carry the film on my skin that will not wash off, that is *becoming* me as it has become the stone, the water, the air.

I find the silence that is not silence. A chamber where the hum has *changed*, become something else, something waiting. The films have receded. The walls are bare glass, bare stone, *cleared*. I stand in the space that has been prepared, the space that is *attention*, and I feel the Listener—not see it, not name it, but *feel* it in the way my own body adjusts to a presence that has no presence, a mind that has no mind, a will that has no will. Only persistence. Only patience. Only the endless unchanging now.

It is *sensing* me. Or it is *being* me, in a way that has nothing to do with my body, my mind, my self, and everything to do with my disturbance, my ripple, my *event*-ness in a place that does not *happen*, that only *is*.

I am small here. Not small as a person is small before mountains—small as a *thought* is small before the mind that thinks it, small as a *cell* is small before the body it serves. The city does not contain me. I *participate* in it, briefly, as the water participates, as the films participate, as the Drifters participate—drifting, sensing, *being* for a moment in a place that has forgotten the difference between living and built, between growing and made, between *now* and *always*.

I think of leaving. The way out is the way in, or another way, one of many. But I am cold where I stand, 0.6 on whatever scale I brought, and I am warm where the water rises, and I am *uncomfortable*, *alive*, *present* in a world that has achieved a stability I cannot share, a permanence that makes my brief existence feel like *noise*, like *error*, like something the city will eventually absorb, dissolve, *use*.

The volcanic glass catches my light and holds it. For a moment I see the city as the builders might have seen it—not a tomb, not a machine, not a mistake, but a *garden* of stone and water, a place where the deep earth's chemistry has been *cultivated*, *shaped*, *made habitable* by beings who understood that survival underground meant *becoming* underground, becoming mineral, becoming the slow, patient, *weeping* thing that this place has become.

I am not one of those beings. I cannot become one. But I am *becoming* something, here, now, in the thick air and the warm water and the hum that is also the city's breathing, its pulse, its endless unchanging *now*.

I turn to leave. The passage is narrow. The water rises to my waist. The hum is loud, *loud*, almost a shout, almost a *welcome*, almost a *goodbye*.

Behind me, the city continues. It will continue. It has always continued, and it will continue when I am gone, when my light is gone, when my memory of this place has become itself a kind of film, a deposit, a layer in the palimpsest of stone and water and time.

I climb. The air changes. The sound changes. The hum fades, or I fade from it, and for a moment I am *between*—between the city and the world, between the deep and the surface, between the *always* and the *now*.

Then I am out, and the city is behind me, and I carry its water on my skin, its sound in my ears, its *density* in my chest, and I know that somewhere below, in a place that time has forgotten, something is still *singing*, still *weeping*, still *waiting*—not for me, not for anyone, but for the next disturbance, the next ripple, the next event, the next life that will come and go and become, for a moment, part of the endless, unchanging *now*.

I am the next disturbance, now, walking away. The wound in the stone closes behind me, or has already closed, or was never a wound but a *door* that opens only inward, only downward, only for those who have already decided to *stay* a little longer than they should.

I carry the city with me. It carries me with it. The boundary was always a surface phenomenon, a temporary arrangement of matter that the deep earth does not respect.

I am the ripple that settles. The event that passes. The warmth that cools.

But for the time I was there, I was *inside* something that is almost alive, almost aware, almost *beautiful* in the way that erosion is beautiful, that crystallization is beautiful, that any process given enough time becomes indistinguishable from *intention*.

And somewhere below, the singing continues.
