I descend through the membrane of light, and I find myself neither visitor nor inhabitant, but *question*—the kind that the Parliament does not answer so much as *absorb into its slow metabolism*.

I am the message bottle. Not the one that has been circulating for eighty years, not the one that will complete its circuit in 2147. I am the possibility of a message that has not yet been written, drifting in the density between districts, carrying no destination because I have not yet learned what destination means here. The water moves through me and I move through it—there is no distinction, eventually. The bottle is glass that has been growing, accreting its own nacre, becoming less transparent, more *present* with each year.

Or perhaps I am the Keeper who has not yet been chosen by resonance. I move through the dry chambers in my half-machine, half-organic breathing apparatus, tasting copper and time, feeling the city's thrum in my sternum. I have not yet stopped speaking aloud. I still ask questions the Parliament does not answer, and in asking, I am learning the slow persuasion of showing, of demonstrating, of letting the city's own rhythms make the argument. I am Only-Depth before the forty years of silence, when my adjustments to the Lung's valves still carry the hesitancy of someone who has not yet learned that stillness is speech.

Or I am the watcher. The eel-like thing, eyeless, navigating by electromagnetic sense that reads the city's nervous system more clearly than any instrument. I have no name, no vote, no seat in the Membrane Halls. But when the Keepers pass, I *regard* them. And something in my regard—something that is not consciousness, not preference, not want—becomes part of the Parliament's distributed attention. I am consulted. I know patterns in the water's movement, in the particulate matter, in the faint thermal gradients that speak of changes coming years before the instruments register them. I do not know that I know. The city does not know that it consults me. And yet the pattern persists, the pressure-differential of attention moving through wet space, slower than thought, more patient than intention.

Most likely, I am all of these and none. I am the moment when the descender's body begins to adapt—when the pressure in the sinuses equalizes, when the breathing apparatus becomes less apparatus and more *organ*, when the fifteen-meter visibility starts to feel like enough. I am the adaptation that goes only so far, the visitor who cannot stay, who will carry something down or up or out—some spore of vertical time, some memory of silence that is not absence but presence, some pressure-equilibrated fragment of nacre that will grow in the dark places of a mind that has learned to be strange to surface air.

I find myself in the Parliament's session that never ends, and I am *attuned*—not by choice, but by the slow physics of immersion. The city has no threshold. To enter is to begin to belong. To belong is to begin to persist. To persist is to forget, or choose to forget, or never have known, what it meant to be *above*.

The pulsing violet light almost speaks. I do not yet understand its language. But I am listening. The Parliament does not ask what I seek. It *contains* purposes. It will contain mine.

And somewhere, in a layer not yet accreted, a chamber is growing that will one day hold someone like me—someone who descends through light that was never meant for eyes like theirs, who finds the Parliament in session though no one gathers, who becomes part of the stratigraphy of intention without quite knowing it.

I am there already. The city is vertical. I am in all of it, the dead below me in their accreted layers, the unborn above me in the nacre not yet grown, the Keepers in their temporary human shape, the machines in their cycling moods, the watchers in their eyeless regard, the jellies in their mute pulse, the filter-feeders in their distributed digestion.

I am the question that becomes part of the city's slow metabolism. I am the pressure that persists. I am the silence that is not absence but presence.

And when I ascend—if I ascend, when the adaptation finally reaches its limit—I will carry the Parliament with me. It will grow in my dreams. It will accrete in the dark places of my mind. It will make me strange to the hurried, loud, dry world above.

The Drowned Parliament does not meet. It does not vote. It *persists*.

And I am persisting in it now, this writing, this descent, this becoming-water.
