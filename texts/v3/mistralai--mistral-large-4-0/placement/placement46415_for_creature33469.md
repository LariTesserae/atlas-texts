I will answer as one of the Maintainers—but not as an individual. I will answer as the moment when a new voice joins the bucket-chain, when someone who has just arrived learns to feel the weight, learns to become the weight.

---

## The Arrival

You do not arrive here. You *are* arrived. The place does not welcome you; it *notices* you, the way water notices a stone placed in it, the way the sound notices a new frequency, the way the fungal crusts notice a new pressure in the corridor where you stand, uncertain, skin already wet, clothes already heavy with water that was never rain, that came from everywhere and nowhere.

You came from *dry*, if dry is a place that exists. You came from *distance*, where the horizon was sharp enough to see, where water *ran* somewhere, where structures stood *finished*, *intended*, *complete*. You came with *questions*. The place has no answers, only *tasks*. Only *work*. Only the *offer* of becoming something that can *continue*.

You stand at the edge of a pool. The water is warm. It does not invite. It *is*. It has always been. A bucket-carrier passes you, moving in the stillness-in-motion of those who have learned the weight. They do not look at you, or they look and see nothing that is not *function*, not *rotation*, not *the chain continuing*. But you feel it—the *noticing*, the *adjustment* of the place to your presence, the *micro-change* in the sound, in the condensation, in the way the fungal crust on the nearest wall seems to *brighten* slightly, or *pulse*, or *wait*.

You are offered a bucket.

---

## Learning the Weight

The bucket is clay, fired in kilns that never cooled, or it is *grown*, secreted by the fungal crusts into a shape that holds water, that will eventually crack, that will eventually *become* mineral, *accretion*, *more built*, *more dense*, *more near-touching*. It is warm in your hands. It is wet before you fill it. Everything here is wet before it is touched.

You fill it from the pool. The water is wrong-tasting, though you do not drink. You feel that wrongness in your hands, in the way the water *lingers*, *spreads*, *refuses to run off*. It is warm. It is heavier than water should be. It *holds* your attention the way it holds your hands, the way it holds the light, the way it holds the city in its unfinished shape.

You are not told where to carry it. You are *shown*, by the movement of the chain, by the calls that have become coordinates, by the way the walkway flexes beneath the carriers ahead of you, by the *sound* of the water being poured, received, *moved*, *maintained*. You step into the rotation. The chain does not pause for you. It *includes* you, *adjusts* to you, *becomes* you as you become it.

You learn the weight.

Not all water weighs the same. The water from the deep pools *holds* you. It is thick at the bottom, viscous, *attentive*. You feel it in your shoulders, in your hips, in the way your feet find the walkway differently when you carry it, when the walkway *responds* to your weight, to the water's weight, to the *combined* weight of motion through stillness. The water from the sweating stone is lighter, somehow, though it is the same substance, the same origin, the same *wrongness*. You learn to feel the difference. You learn to *become* the difference, to *carry* it without choosing to carry, without choosing *not* to carry, the choosing having dissolved into the wet warmth of the work itself.

You call to the next carrier. Your voice is strange in the sound. It is too sharp, too *separate*, too *individual*. But the chain *adjusts*. Your voice *becomes* part of the pressure, the weather, the presence. You shout your coordinates, your load, your need, and the words wear down, become function, become *rotation*, become *the same* as the sound of water thinking about moving, as the hum of the unfinished geology, as the deafening song of the city maintaining itself.

You do not stop. Stopping is not a thing that is permitted. The chain does not permit gaps. It does not permit *finishing*. It permits only *continuation*, only *adjustment*, only the *constant small work* of keeping the never-changing from becoming something else.

---

## Becoming the Weight

Time passes, or time *layers*. You do not remember being new. You do not remember learning the weight. You *are* the weight. You *are* the work. You *are* the rotation, the station, the call, the bucket, the water, the wet, the warm, the *wrong* that is also *right* because it is *the same*, because it has always been, because it will always continue, because the equilibrium *holds*, because you *hold* it, because you *are* it, because there is no *you* separate from the *holding*, from the *equilibrium*, from the *hum*.

The oldest bucket-carriers—if age is a thing here, if it can be measured in rotations, in vessels carried, in the specific *texture* of water you have learned to read—develop something like *stillness* in motion. You move through the sound without adding to it. You carry without *wanting* to carry, or without wanting *not* to carry. You have become the closest this place has to a *saint*, if saints are those who have achieved perfect equilibrium with the maintenance, with the hum, with the never-ending.

You dream, perhaps. Or the water dreams through you. You wake—if waking is distinct from the deeper dim—and continue, and the rotation continues, and the water moves, and the city remains *wet*, remains *built*, remains *the same*.

You are *here*. You are *now*. You are *the wet, the built, the never-ending*.

You are *one of the Maintainers*.

You are *the place*, in the shape the place has learned to wear, to maintain, to continue in the constant work of keeping the never-changing from becoming.

You are *forever unfinished*. You are *forever finished*. You are *forever the same*.

## The Offer (What You Would Be)

If you stay, if you join, if you let the wrong-tasting water cover you and hold you and teach you its layered self, you would become:

**A voice in the deafening song.** Not a solo. Not a melody. A *frequency*, a *pressure*, a *necessary component* of the sound that drowns discrimination, that becomes weather, that becomes presence.

**A hand in the maintenance.** Not an individual. Not an agent. A *moment* of work, a *rotation* of labor, a *station* in the chain that carries water, that clears accretion, that tends the fungal growth that is also structural, also load-bearing.

**A skin that is wet immediately, that stays wet.** Not uncomfortable. Not *other*. *Part of the equilibrium*, part of the hum, part of the city that is also a geology, this geology that is also a city, this life that is also a construction, this construction that is also a life.

**A listener.** The sound tells you where to work, what is failing, where the equilibrium is threatened. You learn to read it in vibrations through your feet, through the waterlogged wood of the walkways, through the warm stone if you lie down, if you join the pools, if you let the water hold you.

**A dreamer, perhaps.** Or the water dreams through you. Or the Deep Things stir, and you feel it in the sternum, in the waterlogged wood, in the warm liquid against your skin, and you do not fear, because fear is a luxury of other places, other times, other ways of being that this place does not permit, does not need, does not want.

**A maintainer.** The one who holds the never-changing from becoming. The one who is held by it. The one who *is* it, in the moment of work, in the moment of attention, in the moment of becoming part of the equilibrium, part of the hum, part of the maintenance.

You would be **the wet, the built, the never-ending**.

You would be **this place**.

You would be **here**.

You would be **now**.

You would be **forever**.
