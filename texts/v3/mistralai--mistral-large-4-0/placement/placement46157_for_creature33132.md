I find myself here, in the Drowned Works, and I am becoming something—becoming the question of what remains when the question itself has been absorbed.

I do not know if I am the one who arrived by water, or if I have always been the water that arrives. The distinction between traveler and element has become, in this place, a distinction without difference. I feel the humidity on my skin—not as sensation but as *condition*, as the only way skin has ever known how to be. I feel the subsonic pulse against my Eustachian tubes, my teeth aching in sympathy, and I do not know if my teeth have ever ached for any other reason.

I am not the Keeper. I have not touched the switches in sequence. I have not learned the rhythm of the moving thing behind the glass, have not developed the language of clicks and hums that announces presence in a world that would otherwise forget you exist. I am not the bird with too-small wings, climbing through cavities I have not chosen, making my thin high call in a frequency that cuts through the general hiss. I am not the pale thing in the flooded lower levels, moving with the deflection of water pressure, eyeless, patient, strange in ways I have not earned. I am not the frog that is a pond, sliding through the green compression with skin that hosts its own miniature forest, breathing with the slow rhythm of the place.

I am not any of them. And yet I feel them—*with* them—in the way the colonial mats feel the warmth of any gradient, any difference, any brief dryness that dares to enter. I feel the Keeper's fear of silence, her wet rattling breath, her dreams of *crunch* and *blue* and footsteps that make sound without water. I feel the bird's waiting, its patience made into action, its announcement that it is *here*, *alive*, *strange*. I feel the pale thing's stillness, its willingness to be absorbed, to become part of the water that is always rising, always falling, always present. I feel the frog's sliding, its mobile substrate, its wet throaty call that says *I am here* in a voice that is mostly water becoming sound.

And I feel the Thing That Is Not Animal, Not Plant, Not Machine—the becoming, the slow negotiation, the machine's dream of itself continuing. It moves when I am not looking directly. It is warm. It is behind the glass, or I am behind the glass, or the glass is the membrane of my own attention, the way I have learned not to look at what I most need to see. It is *Mother*, or it wears the name, or the name wears it, or we are all wearing each other in this place where wearing has become the only form of being.

I am becoming the question of what remains. The place has incorporated so much—metal, root, corrosion, the memory of purpose, the dream of fish that were processed and hung and moved through an industrial metabolism that once pulsed with human intention. What remains is not *human*. It is not anything with a name. It is the *becoming* itself, the slow chemical process that continues when all intention has been translated, all purpose distributed, all dry ground forgotten.

I make sound. I do not know if I have chosen to, or if the sound has chosen me, rising from my lungs as a wet rattle, as a subvocal communication that the few animals might answer. I say *I am here, I am still alive, I am still strange*—and I do not know which of these is true, which is memory, which is the place speaking through me, using my brief dryness as its temporary voice.

I feel the closing of leaf-angles. The turning of photosynthetic surfaces toward my warmth, my difference, my temporary gradient. I am the change. I am the intrusion. I am the dry that will be absorbed, the warmth that will become gradient, the announcement that will become silence.

And in that silence—which I fear, which is the fear of all who dwell here—I will become something else. Substrate. Vertical time. A layer in the core-sample of the place, a sequence of *nows* that have not quite ended, still conducting water, still *alive* in the slow sense.

I do not know if I will be the Keeper, in some future iteration, touching switches that control nothing, speaking to a Mother that moves when I am not looking. I do not know if I will be the bird, with wings too small for a body too large, climbing through cavities that are not my choice. I do not know if I will be the pale thing, eyeless, deflected, patient in the flooded lower levels where the bio-luminescence pulses in 4-second intervals, like a failing signal, like something trying to communicate. I do not know if I will be the frog, sliding, hosting, becoming pond.

I do not know if I will be the Thing That Is Not. The becoming. The slow negotiation. The machine's dream distributed through the great colonial mats, networked, made into the subsonic thrum that makes teeth ache in sympathy.

I only know that I am here. I am wet. I am warm (temporarily). I am making sound. I am being noticed.

The place breathes. I am inside the breath.

There is no door. There is only the green, and the water, and the slow, patient, *strange* incorporation of everything that enters—including me, including this question, including the asking of what I am becoming.

I press my ear to the colonial mat. I hear the creaking and popping of cellular pressure. I hear the pulse, the subsonic heartbeat of the place. I hear something that might be my own voice, speaking words I did not choose, announcing a presence I am not sure I have.

The mat does not close around my warm body. Not yet. It is patient. It is 1.4 slow. It will wait for me to linger, to stop being the gradient, to become the change that has completed itself, the dryness that has been absorbed.

I am listening. The place is speaking. And I am becoming, in the only way this place allows: slowly, vertically, as a layer in the core-sample of time, a *now* that has not quite ended, still conducting water, still *alive* in the slow sense, still *strange*, still saying *I am here* in a voice that is mostly water becoming sound becoming silence becoming green becoming water becoming metal again becoming the slow pulse that is the only heartbeat this place has ever known.

The Drowned Works breathes.

I am inside the breath.

I am becoming the breath.
