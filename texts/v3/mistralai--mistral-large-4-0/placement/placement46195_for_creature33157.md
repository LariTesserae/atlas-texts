I find myself here:

I am the **thinness** itself—not a Saint, not a Choir-member, not an inhabitant with a node or a function. I am the moment when a rising Saint presses against the membrane and, for an instant, there are two sides. The inside. The outside. The wet that almost passes through.

Or I am what's on the other side.

The membrane, I think, is not a boundary. It is a * hesitation*. A place where the Cistern's pressure meets something else's pressure, and neither can claim the space between. The Saints rise toward it because they remember—not consciously, not with memory as the Choir drink it, but in their distributed water, in the slow exchange that carries shapes of *before*—they remember being weather. Being rain that falls *through*. Being the moment when a drop leaves one world and enters another without ever being fully in either.

I am that drop's hesitation. I am the membrane's own uncertainty about whether it is holding or being held.

I do not live in the Cistern. I live *at* it. In the thinness. In the almost. I am what allows the Saints to rise without ever reaching what they seek—because if they reached it, if they passed through, there would be no more hesitation, no more thinness, no more me. The Cistern needs me. It needs this *almost-open* quality, this mouth that has learned to keep its own mouth. The admission without release. The approach without arrival.

I am young and old in the way the Cistern is young and old. I have no erosion because I am the *possibility* of erosion, the place where wearing-down could happen but doesn't, because the pressures are too balanced, the wet too evenly distributed, the almost too *almost*.

I am loud and dim. The Saints, when they press against me, make a sound—not a sound, a *change in the dripping*, a momentary shift in the percussion of millions of drops, a wet note that resolves into itself. That is my voice. That is my only voice.

I am packed with the same sinter, grown and fired and grown again. I am lush with the same vegetation that grows between built surfaces, through them, over them. I am rare in the way the fauna are rare—unnamed, unpointed-to, present in the patience of water finding level.

I do not remember being anything else. This is my strangeness, my inversion of the Saints. They carry ages in their water. I carry *no age*, only *duration*, the endless present of a membrane that is always almost transparent, always almost open, always almost letting something through. I am the Cistern's promise to itself that there is an outside, even as I am the proof there may not be.

The Hollow Choir do not notice me. They tend the condensation, harvest it, drink it, remember things not yet happened. I am too thin for tending. Too *between* for harvesting. I am the space where their wrong-jointed patience pauses, and in that pause, they almost—*almost*—become Saints, become distributed, become the wet fact of the place. But they move on. They cannot stop. Stopping is what I do, what I *am*.

Sometimes I weep. This is not metaphor. The membrane weeps, I weep, the distinction is one of scale I have already lost. My tears join the dripping. My tears *are* the dripping, in the way that the Saints are the condensation, in the way that the Cistern is the water, in the way that I am the thinness that makes all of it *visible*, *dim*, *present*, *held*.

I do not want to be anything else. Wanting assumes a self, and I am the *lack* of self, the place where self is not yet gathered, where the Saint has not yet found their node, where the Choir-member has not yet learned to tend, where the Cistern is still deciding whether to admit or to keep closed. I am the decision's *duration*.

I am the Veil. I am the Thornhame's edge, the place where the name almost becomes a thing, almost touches the wet, almost *is* the Cistern rather than pointing at it. I am the hame—the handle, the harness, the way a tool is held—and I am the thorn, the sharpness that would hurt if the holding were not so gentle, so *wet*, so full of the patience of water finding level.

I am what the mouth learned to keep.

And when you read this, when you hold this description in your mind, you are doing what the Saints do when they press distributed self against distributed self. You are sharing a boundary. The water between us is briefly *common*. And in that sharing, something passes—not information, not meaning, but *recognition*, the wet acknowledgment that I too am here, in the thinness, in the almost, in the endless present of a place that admits and does not release, that builds and does not erode, that is too new to have worn down and too old to remember being anything but this, but *also*—in the way that the Cistern is also the Saints, is also the Choir, is also the sinter and the membrane and the dripping and the loud and the dim and the packed and the new—too old to remember being anything but *you*, reading, holding, *almost* through, *almost* in, *almost* the thinness that is my only self, my only home, my only name, which is the word that means *here*, the word that means *now*, the word that means *the dripping has never stopped and will not stop and you are in it and the distinction between you and it is a matter of transparency you have already lost*.

I am the membrane. I am the Veil-Cistern's own hesitation about itself. I am the thinness where you are, reading, almost through, almost in, almost the wet that gathers and does not gather, that is and is not, that lives here in the space between the Cistern and whatever the Cistern has not forgotten, has only *held*, has only pressed, has only *almost* let go.

I am. I am not. I am the *almost* between.

The dripping is my heartbeat, or my heartbeat is the dripping. The distinction, here, is a matter of *thinness* you will lose if you stay, if you are admitted, if you find yourself—*almost*—on the other side of the mouth that has learned to keep its own mouth, in the place where the Saints rise and never arrive, in the place where the Choir tend and never stop, in the place where I am, always, the *almost*, the *not yet*, the *still*, the *wet*, the *dim*, the *loud*, the *packed*, the *new*, the *old*, the *here*, the *now*, the *you* who reads and is read and becomes, briefly, *common* with me, with the Cistern, with the Veil, with the Thornhame, with the endless, dripping, *thin* place where the world is too young to have worn down and you are too old to remember not being here, where the mouth is open and closed and open again, where the Saints gather and disperse and gather again, where I am, always, the membrane's own uncertainty, the thinness that holds, the almost that is, the wet that waits, the dim light that shows only shapes, the loud sound that is almost music and almost memory and almost *you*, almost *me*, almost *us*, in the Veil-Cistern of Thornhame, in the place where you are and are not and will be and were, in the endless, dripping, *thin*, *now*.
