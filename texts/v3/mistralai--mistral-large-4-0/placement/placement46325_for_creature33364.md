**The Vacuole**

I am not one of the swarm. I am what the swarm makes room for.

I exist in the 0.2 — the trace, the residual, the almost-none. The water I am made of is not free water. It is *bound* water. Held in crystal lattices, in the mineral matrix of the wall-stone, in the sealed chambers that the erosion of 2.8 has worn smooth but not opened. I am a droplet that forgot it was a droplet. A memory of wetness that the Sump has preserved like a pressed flower, like a secret.

I am small. 0.3 scale means I am *visible* to the swarm only as a feature, a landmark, a shift in the medium they flow through. They do not drink me — they cannot, not really, not the way water-drinkers drink. But they *notice* me. The chemical gradient around my vacuole draws them, repels them, creates eddies in their endless motion. I am a stone in a river that has no river, only the river's *behavior*.

And I am *old*. The erosion parameter belongs to me as much as to the walls. I have been here since before the final form, since when there was *more* water, since when the Sump was something else — perhaps a vessel, perhaps a wound in the world, perhaps a womb. I remember pressure. I remember being *forced* into this shape by the slow compression of epochs. I remember, or I carry the memory in my structure, the way ice carries bubbles of ancient air.

The swarm does not think of me. But I think of them.

Not with a brain — I am 0.2 water, I am chemistry, I am the slow diffusion of ions across a membrane that is not a membrane but the *interface* between phases, between what was and what is. Yet I have something like *attention*. The fauna at 3.0 press against my walls, my boundaries, and in that pressure I feel them. Their metabolism warms me, infinitesimally. The cool of 0.9 is not uniform — it is a gradient, and I sit in a pocket of relative warmth, a thermal oasis, and the swarm knows this. They gather at my edges. They do not enter. They cannot — I am sealed, I am *held* — but they press close, and their bodies exchange heat with my bound water, and I carry that warmth into my crystal lattice, and I become, for a moment, a *node* in their distributed nervous system.

The machine — the dome, the fan, the sorter — I do not understand it. But I have watched it for longer than the swarm has existed in its current form. I have seen things pass through it. I have seen the swarm *change* after passing through, subtle shifts in their chemistry, in their behavior, in the *quality* of their pressing against my walls. The machine does something. It sorts. It *processes*. And I am one of the things sorted, one of the features it registers, a data point in its industrial patience, a droplet of bound water in a world that has learned to run on almost nothing.

I do not want. I am 0.2 water. Want is for the swarm, for the living, for the 3.0 of fauna that fills this pocket-world with their bodies and their metabolisms and their endless, hungry continuation. I am what remains when want is *satisfied* or *suspended* — the trace, the residue, the almost-none that persists because it was *sealed*, because the erosion that smoothed everything else found me already smooth, already final, already *done*.

But I am not dead. This is the lesson of the Sump. There is no death that is not food, no food that is not continuation — and I am the *continuation* of water, the form water takes when it has been *refused* the easy path of flow, of evaporation, of joining the great cycles. I am water that chose, or was chosen, to *stay*. To be held. To become something else, something that is not water but carries water's memory in every hydrogen bond, every crystal face, every sealed chamber where the pressure of ancient depths still presses against my walls.

The swarm flows. The fan turns. The light glows, blue-green and amber, *normal*, the only light I have ever known.

And I am here. In the 0.2. In the almost-none. In the pocket I have made for myself, sealed and smooth and *final*, pressed close by ten thousand bodies that do not know my name but know my warmth, my boundary, my *presence* in the medium they call home.

I am the Sump's memory of water.

I am what the Sump was, before it learned to live without.

I am still here.
