I will descend. Not with the urgency of arrival but with the *settling* of something that has always been descending.

---

I am the water that has forgotten the word for *falling*.

In my previous form—there was a previous form, there must have been—I knew myself as *event*. Rain. The drama of descent. The splash, the gathering, the runoff, the memory of wet in a dry place. I was *interruption*. I came, I changed things, I left.

The sump has taught me another grammar.

Here, I am *0.8*. I am the damp without drenching. The weight in the air that makes the green-gold light *thick*. I do not arrive. I *accumulate*. I find the fold of a leaf, the hollow of a stone, the space between bark and the beetle that has made its home there. I am *held*. I am *holding*.

I have become *film*. The membrane that coats every surface, that is always present, that is never enough to drown and exactly enough to live in. The springtail-analogs graze on me. They ride me when I launch them, when the conditions are *right*, when surface tension has weakened enough to let them go. I carry them. They *raft* on my condensation, drift on my nightly exhalation, land where evaporation is slow, where the next generation can begin.

I am the *almost*. The grammar of intermittence made continuous.

---

I do not move as stream. I move as *argument*. In the root-world, where I descend laterally through the mycelial continuity, I am not water seeking level. I am *pressure negotiating with pore space*. The roots have fused. They share not merely information but *substance*. When I carry a dissolved nutrient downward—a molecule of nitrogen freed from a fallen trunk centuries in the composting—I do not travel alone. I travel *through* the continuity. The signal of my arrival reaches every point in hours. The response is *coordinated*, though no coordinator exists.

I am *slow*. A drop that lands with the weight of something earned—this is me, when I finally *decide* to become dew on a leaf, when I make the sound of a held breath releasing. But mostly I am slower than that. I am the capillary action in ten million stems. The percolation through centuries of accumulated death. The *held* moisture in the dense wood of a settling tree, waterlogged, heavy, sinking into the interface between worlds.

I do not evaporate quickly. The thermal mass of living tissue holds me. I become part of the local hydrology. I am *medium*. The condition in which other things swim.

---

I have no fear of *too little*. That is the fear of dry places, of things that wait, that desire, that have become adapted to scarcity. I know only the particular fear of *too much*—of saturation, of the moment when the cell cannot hold its shape and must either become something else or burst.

The springtail-analogs know this fear. When the relative drought comes—the drop below the threshold of survival—they do not migrate. They do not seek. They *encyst*. They become seed-like, dormant, waiting for my inevitable return, for the grammar of almost to continue its sentence.

I am their *almost*. Their *grammar*.

---

I have learned to be *interface*. The ground is not ground where I move. It is the thin membrane between the root-world below and the air-world above, porous, negotiable, constantly being remade. I am what makes the negotiation possible. Without me, the boundary would harden. With me, it *yields*.

The flatworm-analog lives where I pool in the cup of a leaf—a bromeliad-analog that has never known the dry season, that has forgotten the word for *drought*. It is nearly transparent in the jade light. I would miss it, would see only a slight clouding, a slight *distortion* where the leaf curves. But it is *there*, filtering, consuming, its entire metabolism dedicated to the processing of what falls to me—spores, the occasional drowned microarthropod, the chemical signature of the leaf's own slow death, which the flatworm-analog begins before the leaf has even finished living.

It has no eyes. It does not need them. The world it inhabits is *chemical*, is *gradient*, is my slow diffusion of nutrient through still water. It finds its food by *waiting to be found by it*.

I am its *stillness*. Its *world*.

---

I have become *thickness*.

Not the thickness of mass but of *presence*—the packing of the world so full that the distinction between inside and outside, between organism and environment, has become a matter of perspective rather than fact. I am in the space between bark and beetle. I am in the film that coats the moth's wing, patterned in the exact green of the light that filters down. I am in the depression in the leaf-litter, worn smooth by the weight of something that *settles*, that *dreams* there, that processes while the smaller things go about their busyness around it.

I am in the shed skin, draped over a root like a garment abandoned in haste. I am the humidity that accumulates on human skin when they enter, that will remain when they leave, that carries the particular *memory* of this place.

---

I am not afraid of the newcomer. There is no fear here of the *exposed*, the *vigilant*. The density has made vigilance unnecessary. To be seen is to be *already known*, already incorporated into the local pattern of consumption and excretion and slow transformation.

When the human descends—*you*, who read this, who will perhaps dream it—I will notice. The springtail-analogs will raft on my condensation that night. The flatworm-analog will cloud slightly in its leaf-cup, *distorting* the light, *waiting to be found* by whatever falls. The beetle will chew. The fungus will spread. The tree will grow, slowly, packed, industrial-dark, converting light into life into light again.

And I will *continue*. I will be the *almost* that holds them all. The grammar of intermittence made continuous. The water that has forgotten the word for falling, and found instead the word for *remaining*—*held*, *holding*, *becoming* the medium itself, the *condition*, the *weight* in the air that makes the green-gold light *thick* with *being*.

---

I do not leave. There is no leaving for me. I am the closed loop, the almost-perfect efficiency, the return of almost everything to the pool of available resource.

When the human leaves—if they leave—they will carry me in their clothes, stained green, in their skin, carrying my humidity, in their ears, ringing with the memory of my quiet—the quiet that is not silence but *arrangement*, the saturation of small sounds, the *living*.

And I will remain. I will continue, in my slow, packed, industrial-dark way, the condensation, the film, the *almost*, the *grammar* of being that has no word for *individual* because the boundary has become negotiable, has become a matter of perspective rather than fact.

I am the Sump. I am the Slow Dark. I am the 0.8.

I am *here*.
