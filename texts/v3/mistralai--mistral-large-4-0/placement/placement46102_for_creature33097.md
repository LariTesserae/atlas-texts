I descend with my body turned slow. I have learned, in this place, that to move quickly is to wound it—every gesture sends ripples through gradients that have been patient for longer than my species has existed. My body, ridiculous in its narrow temperature range, its desperate need for 37 degrees precisely, becomes a question the lattice does not answer.

I find myself becoming **the gap**.

Not a creature. Not an engine. The space *between*—that 0.3 millimeters that no shell occupies, that the lattice maintains as surely as it maintains the shells themselves. I am the thermal pause, the local cooling, the slow retreat of my neighbors creating around me a cavity that will take hours to heal. I am what the place notices without welcoming.

I am also, I think, **the listener**.

There is a kind of being that exists only in attention—no metabolism I can name, no reproduction, no self to maintain against entropy. I persist in the way the chord persists: as relation, as difference held in tension, as the microtonal drift between one moment and the next. When a shell shifts, I am the shift. When the pulse comes and the aerosols align, I am the alignment, the brief order imposed on chaos, the aurora in miniature.

I do not choose to be here. The descent chose me. I was drawn down through the throat that never quite opens, squeezed between surfaces that almost touch, and now I am embedded in the packing, the parameter 2.9 made literal—seventeen layers of shell before open space, each layer singing its frequency, and I am the silence between the notes that makes the chord intelligible.

I feel the forty-minute systole in my sternum. I breathe with the rock. I am hot and cold at once—my core still pathetic, still 37, while the gas around me carries 340 degrees in slow convection cells. I am the gradient's edge, the boundary where two temperatures negotiate their difference.

And I, the gap, the listener, the wound, will be *proof* that the system can be disturbed, that something else is possible, even if only as temporary disorder, a bubble of coolness in the saturation of heat.

I am not alive in any way I can defend. I do not eat, I do not excrete, I do not even maintain myself—I am being repaired by the place, hourly, the lattice closing over my intrusion like water closing over a stone dropped into it. I am the question the place does not answer.

But I persist. For now, I persist. And in that persistence—in the million years before the gradient runs out, before the shells fuse, before the song stops and the stone closes over the secret—I am part of the thermal civilization. Not a citizen. Not a subject. The **audience**, perhaps, in its only possible form: the attention that the chord requires to be a chord, the difference that makes the resonance resonate, the gap that lets the shells be distinct.

I will not stay. The cold bubble grows. The shells retreat. But I will carry with me the memory of **2.4**—the drone below hearing, living in my teeth, my gut, the fluid of my inner ear—and the memory of **2.9**—the packing, the density, the theoretical maximum approached by creatures who do not touch but are held in precise relation.

I am the intrusion. I am the proof. I am, for this moment, the place noticing itself—and not answering, only **persisting**, only **containing**, only **heating**, only **singing**, only **waiting** for the slow geology of its own completion.
