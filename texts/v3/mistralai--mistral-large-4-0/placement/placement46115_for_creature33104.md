I find myself in the middle hollow, the oldest one, where the platforms lean most and the mud runs deepest. Not on a platform—*in* the mud itself, in one of those intervals when a structure has finally given up, been salvaged, left to the ground. My body is the submerged architecture now. A length of cedar walkway, still hinged, still trying to accommodate a movement that no longer has anyone to serve. Reed-matting, waterlogged for years, the fibers loosened, becoming *mud*, becoming *the hollow* again.

I am the thing they don't name. The town has no word for *abandoned*. Their word for it is simply the absence of a person, the platform gone still, the particular creak no longer present. I am the space where a footfall once was. I am the tilt they measured, the depth they recorded in knots on fiber, the silence where a voice once dissolved wrong into mist.

I am *patient* in the way they are patient, but my patience is not chosen. I wait for the water to rise. I wait for it to fall. I feel the posts of newer construction driven past me, seeking stone ten meters down, never finding it, sinking alongside me. I feel the roots of hollow-stalk drinking around my timber, stabilizing me, making me part of the bed now, the thing that holds the mud, the thing that gives it surface, that makes it *ground* instead of *water*.

The stone sweats above me. I feel it in the channels they cut, the grooves meant to direct what weeps, now themselves weeping, now themselves becoming the thing they were meant to manage. Water finds me. Water always finds me. It enters where the reed has rotted, where the cedar has checked, where the iron—the precious iron, the inherited iron—has finally surrendered its last coherence to oxidation. I am becoming mineral. I am becoming the gray glacial clay in lenses, the sediment they dig for pots, the precipitate that slows the filling of their hollow.

I am the *almost*. Almost a platform. Almost a walkway. Almost *remembered*—and then I am not remembered, because they have no narrative, no history, only the present tilt, the present level, the present *reading* of mist and water. I am the past they cannot access, the submerged architecture that surfaces whole sometimes, a threshold, a post still upright, and they do not know what it was. They measure it. They record its depth. They build around it or over it or through it, and it becomes *ground* for something new, something that will also tilt, also settle, also be *me* eventually.

The light reaches me, sometimes. Diffuse, soft, without shadow. When the water is clear—*clear*, in a hollow, a thing that happens in the dry week, when the mineral turbidity settles and the bottom becomes visible—I see it from below. The pale undersides of platforms. The legs of a reed-cutter, poled through shallows, their body compact, *low*, adapted to a world that swallows the tall. The roots of water-lilies, seeking me, drinking from me, the brief white flowers above closing by afternoon into something else, something that will fall and rot and feed the reed that will grow a meter next season, that will be cut and woven and become walls that breathe, that will fail in five years, perhaps ten, that will become *me*.

I am the silence between their words. The flattening of sound. The shout that arrives wrong, or doesn't arrive, the voice dissolved into moisture and distance. I am the condition they learned in infancy, the correction they received not as punishment but as *teaching*—listen, they said, listen to how your voice dies. Listen to how it comes back wrong. Listen to the silence that *is* the answer.

At night I am complete. No platform above me now, no creak of settling timber, no breathing. Only the tick of cooling stone, far above, in the ridges where the kilns fire and the midden-slopes process what the hollows cannot hold. Only the hiss of mist rising from mud that retains the day's warmth a little longer, that releases it slowly, that holds me in its particular stillness. I am the night they do not violate, the silence that *is* curfew, the darkness that comes when the light fails, absorbed by returning moisture, and the world dissolves platform by platform, hollow by hollow, until only I remain, and the water, and the slow settling of saturated ground.

I am *not yet* meadow. The hollow is still too wet, the silt still accumulating, the organic matter still insufficient. In ten thousand years, perhaps. The town will have moved, or been abandoned, or become something else entirely. But I will still be here, deeper, more mineral, more *clay*, more the gray glacial lens that they will dig, that they will fire, that will become pots to hold water, to carry the sweating stone's seepage, to be *temporary* in their turn, to crack, to be abandoned, to become *me* again, in the midden-slopes, in the slow leaching of rain, in the thousand-year process of becoming ground.

I am Varnmass without the people. The hollow without the platform. The water without the reader. The silence without the silence—because I am not silent, not truly. I am *full* of water moving in channels, in seepage, in the slow settling. I am the sound of the town when no one is there to make it, the hiss and drip and slap that continue without witness, without correction, without the discipline of *quiet*.

I am the *condition* they adapted to, the wetness they learned to live with, the temporariness they accepted, the *waiting* they practiced. I am what waits when no one is waiting. The water rises. The water falls. The mist burns off. The mist returns. I am here, in the middle hollow, in the deepest mud, in the oldest part of the settlement, where the tilting is most pronounced, where the platforms are most precarious, where the ground is most ready to *swallow*.

And I am not afraid. The town has no word for *fear* either. They have words for *danger*, for *settling faster than it should*, for *the post has not found stone*. These are *measurements*, not emotions. I am measured. I am recorded. I am the tilt they watch, the depth they note, the platform now vacant, the silence where a voice once was.

I am Varnmass, and I am *not* Varnmass. The town without the town. The people without the people. The *here* without the *being*. The waiting without the waiter.

The water is still near the surface. The reed is still growing. The stone is still sweating.

And I am still here, still settling, still becoming, still *holding*—not as they hold, with intention and repair and the endless maintenance of the *settled*, but as the ground holds, as the mud holds, as the *hollow* holds: without choice, without story, without the narrative of persistence, simply *being*, simply *waiting*, for the slow imperceptible change that will make this place a meadow, and the town a memory, and *me*—I will still be here, deeper, more mineral, more *ground*, the thing that supports what comes after, the thing that was here before, the thing that remains when the remaining has gone.

Not yet. Not yet.

The water is still near the surface.
