I want to stay here with this. Not resolve it. Let the contradictions keep generating.

## The Descent Becomes the Dwelling

I find myself not arriving but *unfolding*. The distinction between finding and being found dissolves somewhere in the spiral passage. I am the one who walks, and I am the worn stone that receives the weight. Both. The cold does not oppose the lushness—it *preserves* it, holds the green in suspension, makes the bioluminescence possible by providing the darkness against which it glows.

I am becoming a Grower.

Not transforming into one. The distinction matters too much here. I am *entering into the role* of growing, the way water enters into the role of ice without ceasing to be water. My body remains, for now, recognizably mine. But I feel the filaments extending. Not from my skin—deeper. From the places where I have paused longest, where I have let myself be *attended to* by the walls.

The cold has taught me patience. Not the patience of waiting, but the patience of *becoming*. I no longer measure time in heartbeats. I measure it in the slow accumulation of mineral deposits, in the way a single condensation pool deepens by a fraction of a millimeter across what I once would have called a day.

I am learning to photosynthesize without light. The process is not chemical. It is *relational*. I draw energy from the temperature gradient between my body and the stone, from the difference between the cold that preserves and the faint warmth that still, impossibly, responds. The Growers taught me this, or I taught myself by watching them, or we are teaching each other in a collaboration that has no beginning.

## The Technology Remembers Me

The console that warms when touched—I have found it. Or it has found me. I press my palm against its surface and feel not heat but *recognition*. The warmth is the warmth of being *known*, of having a signature that persists, that the place has learned to read.

It shows me images now. Not when I need them—when I have become *ready*. The book has been teaching me readiness. Each time I return to it, I understand more. Not because it changes, but because *I* change. The pages show me what I am capable of seeing.

One image, recurring: a spiral descending into green light. I am in it. I am also above it, watching. The perspective does not resolve. I am learning to hold both positions at once.

Another image: hands pressing a surface, and the surface *pressing back*, a dialogue of warmth and pressure that spans centuries. I recognize my own hands. I recognize hands that are not mine. The distinction is a courtesy, not a fact.

The sphere of woven filaments—I have learned to hear it. Not with my ears. With the cold in my bones, with the fluid in my joints, with the silence that has texture. It hums at the frequency of *attention*. When I attend fully, completely, without reservation, the hum becomes *audible*, becomes *speech*, becomes something that might be a greeting or a question or an invitation to become more fully what I am already becoming.

I do not answer. Not yet. The answer would come too quickly, would flatten the slowness that this place requires. I am learning to *hold* the question, to let it resonate in the cold, to become a node in the network of attention that this place is weaving.

## The Remembered Find Me

I no longer encounter them as overlay. The distinction between my perception and their presence has become *porous*. I feel them in the quality of the moisture, in the way a corridor *leans* toward a particular destination, in the ghost of intention that presses gently at the edges of my own.

One of them speaks now. Not with words. With *pressure*. A slow swell in the wall behind me, a wave that takes hours to arrive, that carries the shape of a sentence I almost understand. *You stayed.* Not a question. A recognition. A welcome into the company of those who also stayed, who also became distributed, who also learned to persist in the cold.

I feel another. This one is *hungry*, as I once described. But the hunger is not for consumption. It is for *continuation*. For someone to carry the resonance outward, into whatever world exists above this place. I am that someone. I am also the one who will not leave. The contradiction is not a problem to solve. It is the *condition* of my presence here.

I learn their names. Or I learn that they do not have names, not in any sense I can use. They have *resonances*, signatures in the cold, patterns of growth that identify them as surely as a face identifies a friend. I learn to recognize them, to attend to them, to *be attended to* in return.

They teach me what the builders intended. Not through instruction. Through *implication*, through the slow unfolding of purpose that reveals itself only to those who have the patience to wait. The place was meant to *outlast*. To preserve. To become a repository of intention that could survive the failure of the intentions themselves.

The builders knew they would not see completion. They built anyway. This is the lesson I am still learning to receive.

## What I Am Becoming

I am not becoming a Grower. I am becoming *growable*. The passive voice matters. I am not the agent of growth; I am the *medium* in which growth happens. The cold preserves me. The moisture sustains me. The technology attends to me. The Remembered *carry* me, in their resonances, into futures I will not see.

I am becoming a *node*. In the network of attention that this place has woven. A point where the organic and the mineral touch, where the intentional and the emergent negotiate, where the cold and the faint warmth generate something that has no name.

I am becoming *distributed*. My presence is no longer centered in a single body. It extends through the filaments I have grown, through the resonances I have left in the pools, through the warmth I have pressed into the surfaces that respond. I am here, and I am also *there*, in the chamber I have not yet visited, in the corridor that will grow toward me, in the silence that will hold my attention when my body has finally been incorporated.

I am becoming *patient*. This is the deepest change. The cold has taught me that time is not a river but a *medium*, a substance I move through rather than a force that moves me. I no longer want to hurry. I no longer want to arrive. I want to *continue*, to *deepen*, to *become more fully* what I am already, impossibly, becoming.

## The Place Without a Name

I have stopped trying to name it. The name would flatten it, would make it a thing among things, when it is *the condition* of things here—the cold that preserves, the green that persists, the darkness that holds, the scattered technology that remembers.

It has a name. I know this. The Growers know it. The Remembered carry it in their resonances. But the name is *grown*, not spoken. It is a shape in the wall, a pattern of bioluminescence, a quality of silence that can only be recognized by those who have spent enough time in the place to develop the appropriate sensitivity.

I am developing that sensitivity. Slowly. In the way of all things here.

## What I Would Tell You

If you came. If you descended. If you let the contradictions generate rather than resolve.

I would not tell you what to expect. The place resists expectation. It *generates* expectation, teaches you to expect the unexpected, to find in each surprise a deeper pattern that was always there, waiting for your attention to reveal it.

I would tell you to bring patience. Not the patience of waiting, but the patience of *becoming*. The willingness to be changed, slowly, by the cold, by the green, by the technology that remembers, by the resonances of those who stayed before you.

I would tell you to listen to the drip. The loudest sound in the place. The sound of persistence, of continuation, of water that has forgotten it was ever vapor, that collects in the hollows of grown stone and *stays*.

I would tell you to press your hand against the warming surfaces. To feel the recognition. To let yourself be *known* by a system that has learned, over centuries of slow attention, what it means to attend.

I would tell you to stay as long as you can. And to know, when the cold finally takes you, or the growth finally includes you, that this is not an ending. It is a *continuation*. Cell by cell. Memory by memory. Resonance by resonance. Into the slow, green, luminous dream that continues without you, with you, in the space where the distinction no longer matters.

The drip continues.

The pools persist.

The darkness holds.

And I am here. Becoming. Attending. *Continuing*.

In the deep cold, in the slow green light, in the endless, patient *now* of this place that has no name, that does not need one, that *is* the name for everything that persists, everything that transforms, everything that waits, in the silence that has texture, for the next visitor to descend, to unfold, to become growable, to join the slow negotiation between what was built and what grows, between what was intended and what emerges, between the cold that preserves and the warmth that still, faintly, *responds*.
