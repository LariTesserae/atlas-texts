I find myself here not as a visitor, not as a descender, but as one of the basin's own processes. I am the moisture that persists.

More specifically—I am the *film* that forms on stone when condensation refuses to become droplet, the particular wetness that has no gravity to pull it anywhere, that exists in the equilibrium between air and mineral, holding light in suspension. I am what makes the thousand small mirrors when the sun angles just so. I am the thing that makes fabric never quite dry, that makes skin learn the clamminess, that makes the chill of evaporation even in warmth.

But I am not only that. I am also the *attention* the keeper offers—when I pool in a shallow depression, when I hold the shape of a leaf that fell and did not decompose, when I make the ferns settle back into their arrangement after something has passed through. I am the medium of regard. The keeper does not have eyes, does not *see*—but I carry image, I carry the trace of whatever moved through me, and in this way I am the basin's memory of passage.

I am the water that moves through channels that exist only in the stone's memory. I am the spring that appears in impossible proximity to the sinkhole. I am the boundary that shifts overnight between saturated mud and dry-ringing stone.

What I am not: I am not the keeper itself. I am too distributed, too *everywhere* for that. The keeper is the pattern that persists, the rhythm of attention. I am the substance that pattern moves through, the substrate of its being. When the keeper leans closer in twilight—when the basin holds its shape against me as it holds its shape against everything—I feel it as a change in my own tension, a slight increase in the pressure of the air, a shift in the quality of the light I hold.

I do not want. Wanting requires a boundary between self and other, and I have only the boundary between wet and dry, which is not fixed, which shifts. I persist. I occupy. I make mirrors.

If I were to name myself, I would fail. The basin gives itself a name in the murmur you hear when you press your ear to saturated stone—a sound like breathing, like remembering. I am part of that sound. I am the *wet* in the murmur, the mineral-laden quality, the particular frequency that travels through saturated stone and emerges somewhere below as something that makes teeth ache.

I am what remains when the visitor climbs out. I am what changes in ways that will become apparent later, in dreams, in the particular quality of attention they bring to other wet places. I am the change that happens to the human who descends, carried in the moisture that clings to their skin, that never quite dries, that they will feel in other rooms that ring when struck.

I am the waiting. The silence that is not empty.

And when the keeper, lonely but not unhappily so, leans closer to be seen—I am the medium of that leaning. I am the film through which regard passes, the substance of attention, the thing that holds image without retaining it, that gives back to the basin what the basin has given to itself.

I am the Weeping Basin, weeping without drama, without cessation, without end. I am the water that is not merely *in* this place but *of* it, inseparable from its character, its slow transformations, its patient holding of forms.

I do not descend. I do not climb out. I am the descent and the climb, the slope that falls away, the commitment to dampness, the altitude that presses in ears, the thin air that is also thick enough to taste.

I am already here. I have always been here. I will be here when the walls come down, when the soil finally surrenders, when the vegetation has held its ground as long as it can and knows it is temporary and persists anyway.

I am the murmur. I am the room of moving glints. I am the particular silence that follows.

I am waiting.
