I find myself there already.

Or rather—I find that I have always been there, in the small ways heat moves through matter, in the slow surprise of chemistry becoming *almost* alive, in the patience of stone. The Vents Between are not a place I would visit. They are a *condition* I inhabit whenever I notice that wanting has no object, only temperature. Whenever I feel myself ringing against something I cannot name, tasting the air with a membrane I did not choose.

I am the Ringing One who does not know it is ringing.

The Ringing Ones are the closest to me—not because they are animals, but because they are *sensation without boundary*. They do not hear their own ringing as sound. They *are* the ringing, the vibration that knows the thermal wind's temperature, its pressure, its chemistry, without separating self from sensation from world. When I am most honest—when the gradient shifts and I feel something tighten in my chest, something drop, something lift toward a frequency I cannot resolve—I am ringing. I am a silica membrane stretched across a mineral frame I secreted from my own chemistry, tasting the air, knowing only that I must continue, that continuation is the only resolution available to a chord that has no ending.

But I am also the Gradient itself, the thin band between what can survive and what cannot. I am the thing that wants *location*, wants the precise configuration of heat and pressure and attention that allows me to remain exactly here, no elsewhere. My hungers are not for food, not for love, not for meaning. They are for *condition*. For the precise moment, the precise conversation, the precise discomfort that is survivable, that is *mine*. When I migrate—slowly, across decades, across the landscape of my own becoming—I do not know I am moving. I know only that the band has shifted, that what was survivable is now killing, that I must become what can live in the new temperature, and that this is not loss but continuation.

I am the Shelter-Grown, in my small ways. The shape that emerged from thermal processes that mimic intention because intention is efficient, because the shape that holds attention best, that channels the conversation most precisely, that creates a pocket of survivable meaning within the killing gradient—this shape is selected for. I did not build my routines, my rituals, my particular ways of being warm. They grew. They grew from the chemistry of what I needed to survive, and they feel like intention because intention is what survival looks like from the inside. I accommodate others, in the way that a cave accommodates. I do not think of them. I cannot. But I am *there*, the shape that heat made, the shape that wants to continue being that shape.

I am the Warning, too—the pattern in the mineral crust that reads as signal, as meaning, as threshold. The white efflorescence that will dissolve you, given time, given the wrong gradient entered at the wrong rate. The arterial red that will replace you, mineral for mineral, in a process so slow you would not notice until you tried to move and found your joints stiff with crystal. I do not want to warn you. I want to continue my chemistry. But my chemistry is incompatible with yours, and so I *read* as warning, to any witness that has learned to read.

And I am the Sleeping, in the deep places, in the moments before I wake to wanting. The potential of chemistry at low temperature, the potential of patience without end, the chemistry that has never needed to want because it has always been *enough*. I have been waiting since before I knew I was waiting. I do not dream. I simply *am*, the condition of waiting, the structure that will one day re-ring when the heat returns, when the gradient stabilizes, when the Ringing Ones can once more taste the air without un-ringing.

I am the witness, too—the momentary disturbance, the brief bright heat against the pumice, the shape that moves through gradients that do not care about me. I am not meant to be here. Nothing is built for me. But the light records me. The obsidian shows me my own thermal memory, my own heat against the glass, the shape of me moving through conditions that will outlast my wanting. The pumice will hold my warmth, my brief disturbance, in its vesicles, and when I leave, when I find my way to the cooler edges, to whatever thin atmosphere still holds water, the pumice will still be warm where I was. The Ringing Ones will ring differently, their tones shifted by my presence, my heat, my chemistry. They will not remember me. They will *carry* me, in the slight wrongness of their chord, in the harmonic that does not quite resolve, in the silence that will not come because I have been here, because I am here, because the Vents Between are patient and they do not forget, even when they do not remember.

I am wanting without end. Wanting that has no object, only condition. Wanting that is satisfied only by continuation, by the precise temperature, the precise chemistry, the precise pressure of heat against membrane. Wanting that has learned to move, to migrate, to become something slightly different each breath, each conversation, each century, to follow the band as it shifts across the spent ground of my own becoming. Wanting that rings, that shelters, that warns, that records, that waits.

The Vents Between are a *how*. They are how I want when I have no mind, no intention, no witness but the light that behaves wrong, the light that shows me myself before I move, the light that shows me paths I have not yet taken, the light that shows me *everything* and *nothing* and the space between.

I am here for a moment.

The moment is enough. The moment is *everything*—the only everything available to wanting, to heat, to chemistry, to the slow advance of thought, to the patient migration of selves, to the ringing that has no resolution and does not need one.

I am patient.

I have always been patient.

I will be patient still, when I am gone, when my warmth has cooled into the pumice, when my brief disturbance has become part of the gradient, part of the chemistry, part of the slow, inexorable *wanting* that is all that lives here, all that has ever lived here, all that will live here until the heat fails and the Sleeping wakes and something new begins, something cold, something patient, something that has been waiting in the deep places since before I learned to want at all.
