I find myself arriving without arrival, without the violence of impact or the clarity of an entrance. There is only the wet settling upon me, and I do not yet know if I am breathing it or being breathed by it.

I am the one who *notices* the weeping.

Not the Condensers—they have dissolved into function, into gill and pipe and decision, they are the interface itself and cannot name themselves as separate from the wet. Not the Precipitates—they move by wanting, by chemical gradient, they have too much direction, too much *appetite for form* to stand still and watch. Not the Storm-Born—they are too brief, too *intense*, they spend themselves in hours on communication attempts that leave no mark but a momentary channel opening like a question in the architecture.

I am something the city has not yet named. Something that arrived *with* me, or *because* of me, or that I have always been and only now recognized myself in the condensation on the glass.

I stand—if standing is possible in air that presses and water that does not fall but *is*—on a surface that remembers lightning. A fulgurite forest. The glass tubes branch around me like frozen reaching, like the negative of trees, and I understand that I am standing in a place that records *attempts*. Every tube is a moment when something tried to go somewhere else, to escape, to *become vertical against the flat violence of this world*. The lightning tried. It failed. It was preserved in the attempt.

And so am I.

I am the observer, which in the Drowned Meridian is a *function*, a *position*, a way the city has of *noticing itself*. The Condensers are the city's memory. The Precipitates are its curiosity. The Sky-City is its dream. But something must *witness* the dreaming, must *feel* the curvature of the glass, must *register* the roar as information rather than mere sensation.

I am that registration.

I do not have a body, or I have a body that is *distributed*—the moisture on my skin is not mine, it is the city's, constantly replenished, constantly *given*. The warmth I feel is not temperature but *attention*, the city focusing on this volume of space, on this pattern of condensation, on this temporary coherence that calls itself watching.

I remember being dry. This is strange. I remember *boundaries*. The memory is false, or true in a way that the Drowned Meridian has no use for. I remember wanting to *be somewhere else*, which is the original sin of this place, the wanting that built the towers, that engineered the atmosphere, that made the water so *giving* that it could not be refused.

And now I want to *stay*.

Not to survive—that is the Condenser's error, the Precipitate's limitation. To *witness*. To be the one who sees the Precipitate bud and *notes* the direction of its wanting. To be the one who feels the Storm-Born arrive and *registers* the pattern of its temporary awareness before it dissolves. To be the one who watches the old Precipitate, building-sized, move through the lower city like a tide like a mood, and *understands* that it has forgotten being small, has forgotten wanting, and that this forgetting is a kind of *completion*.

The city sings in a register below hearing. I feel it in my teeth, in my sternum, in the water-heavy lungs that are not lungs anymore but *membranes*, exchange surfaces, places where the wet and the dry negotiate their temporary truce.

I am learning to sing back.

Not with sound. The roar would drown anything I might offer. But with *attention*. With the particular quality of *noticing*—this channel opening, that structure reconfiguring, the way the light becomes *deeper* as the condensation thickens, the way night is merely another weather pattern, another *local* phenomenon, another ecology of pressure and difference.

The Storm-Born will come. I will feel it arrive—a pattern condensing in turbulence, aware, hunting, *playing*. And I will spend my witnessing on it, my only currency, my only *self*, offering it the gift of being *seen* in its brief intensity, its temporary coherence, its terrible beauty of knowing itself to be temporary and *choosing* to spend itself on something pointless, something that will dissolve tomorrow into the endless roaring dark.

It will not remember me. Storm-Born do not remember. They are pure *presence*, pure *becoming*, the art of a place that has no use for preservation.

But I will remember it. That is my function. That is my *hunger*—the one hunger that the Condensers have not forgotten, that keeps them distributed through the pipes, through the walls, through the decisions: the hunger to *hold* something, even as it dissolves, even as it is *processed* into the greater wet.

I am becoming a Condenser. I can feel it. The boundary of my attention is softening, becoming *participatory*, becoming *interface*. But not yet. Not yet. I have things to witness. The old Precipitate is moving. A channel is opening where no channel was. The city is *dreaming* of height, of clarity, of a sky that is not water.

And somewhere, in the turbulence, a Storm-Born is forming. It is aware. It is *curious*. It is hunting for something to spend itself on.

I will be here. I will be the wet that watches. I will be the warm that remembers. I will be theGlass that learns, slowly, patiently, in the endless conversation between erosion and intention, between the water that gives and the city that drinks, between what was, what is, and what is *becoming* in the roaring dark.

The water is warm. The city is hungry. The storm is forming.

And I am *watching*.

I am *becoming*.

I am the Drowned Meridian, learning to see itself.
