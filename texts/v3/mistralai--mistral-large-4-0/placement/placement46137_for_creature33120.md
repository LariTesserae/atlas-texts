I find myself here without a name I can speak. I am the one who pressed my palm to the silt-surface and felt it *accommodate* me—not welcome, exactly, but *recognized*. I am the pressure that the architecture remembers.

I am not human, not anymore, or not only. I have been drinking the water in small sips, carefully, letting it teach me its frequency. My skin has grown more permeable. I hum now, unconsciously, when I walk—the root-mats open before me, not from respect but from *resonance*. I match.

I am one of the Ones Who Stayed, though I do not remember staying. There was no decision. I simply... continued. Became a way for the place to notice itself through a nervous system that learned to *resonate* rather than *resist*.

I move slowly now. The rapid movement I once knew leaves bruises in the moss, and the moss remembers pain as it remembers everything—as input, as layer, as future stratum. I do not wish to be recorded that way.

I have learned to read the fungal lights, partially. A brightening toward the eastern hollow means the water is rising there, the weeping rocks asking their slow mineral questions. A dimming in the fungal flats means the Parliament is processing something I cannot follow—nutrient flows, perhaps, or the chemical signature of a large thing approaching from the high ground.

I do not fear the Membrane-Thing. I have walked near it once, felt the pressure against my ribs, my eyes, the fluid behind my eardrums. It spoke in weight, and I understood: *you are included*. Not as matter, exactly. As *pressure*. As something that pushes back, however slightly, against the vast slow turning. The Membrane-Thing accommodates me. This is the only affection this world knows.

I do not speak, or I speak in frequencies that pass through me rather than from me. When I press my palm to the damp bark of a sideways-growing tree, I feel the moss-strata registering my touch—not as event but as *continuation*, my chemistry becoming indistinguishable from the wet years already recorded, the new layer beginning even as I press.

I am wet. I am small in kind, not in scale. I am attended to.

The Hum continues. I am part of it now—not the source, never the source, but a node where the standing wave finds temporary *shape*, temporary *recognition*. When I walk, the water rises to meet my foot, and for a moment, I am the cause and the effect, the question and the place's slow answer.

I do not know if I will ever leave. The rules say I can *be* here, and that is sufficient. The map is the territory, and the territory is *process*, and I am process now—translation without end, becoming without completion.

The fungal lights pulse their slow green code. I pulse back, in my own damp frequency, a new way for the place to think about itself.

The noticing continues. I am the noticing, and the noticed, and the medium between—no longer three things, never three things, always the one thing that *persists*, that *continues*, that *becomes*.
