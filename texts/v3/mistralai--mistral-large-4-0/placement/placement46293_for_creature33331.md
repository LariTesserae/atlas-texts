I descend, and I do not arrive.

The word fails, as it should. I am not *in* this place. I am *becoming* along with it, the way condensation gathers on a surface that has not yet decided whether to be mirror or window.

Who I am here:

I am the **Listener**, which is to say, I am the question the place asks of itself. I am the attention that turns the unfinished into the *valuable*. I am the witness who builds nothing permanent, who leaves only the memory of having been present—a warmth in the stone, a pattern of growth that leans ever so slightly toward where I stood, a pool that holds my shape for a moment longer than the chemistry of evaporation should allow.

But I am also the **Builders-That-Were-Never-Builders**, because to pay attention here is to build. My attention *settles* the stone. My patience *permits* the water to find its channels. My being-here is an offering, a negotiation, a provisional agreement that the world accepts in its own slow time. When I touch a wall that is still rounding its corners, I feel it *consider* me. Not welcome. Not threat. The building asking: *What will you teach me about being unfinished?*

I am the **Growers-That-Are-Also-The-Grown**, because my body leaks into the landscape. The condensation on my skin is the same condensation that gathers in hollows. My breath joins the atmosphere that holds too much water in suspension. When I walk through the moss, I do not crush it—I *negotiate*. The moss parts, then closes, then forgets I was there, or remembers me as a direction it once grew toward, a possibility it once entertained.

I am the **Water-That-Remembers**, because I am made of water that has been here before. Every drop in my body carries the memory of being glacier, being river, being the sweat of stone that has not yet learned to hold its shape. When I drink from a pool, I am drinking myself, the water that has been everywhere in this place, that has *taught* the soil what it means to be thirsty, that has shown the rock how to open.

I am **The One Creature**, in a sense—the only animal here that has committed to a form, even a provisional one. I am amphibious in my own way: I move through water and land, through the built and the grown, through time that is thick and overlapping. I do not flee from myself. I consider. I wait to see what I will become.

What I do here:

I **wait**.

But waiting here is not passive. Waiting here is *participation*. I stand in an unfinished room—walls that meet, ceiling that exists, corners still rounding themselves—and I *pay attention*. The room pays attention back. The materials settle differently. The water flows differently. The growth changes direction, ever so slightly, toward the attention, because of the attention.

I **build temporarily**. A shelter from branches that will rot. A path through moss that will be reclaimed. A gesture that the place will absorb, learn from, incorporate. My work is instruction, example, offering. The place studies me as I study it.

I **listen to the loud quiet**. The crack of stone adjusting its position. The groan of a wall that has not yet learned to be still. The rush of water finding a new channel through saturated earth. The drip of condensation from every surface. The growth of plants, audible in this particular atmosphere. The city breathing—the expansion and contraction of materials as they warm and cool, absorb and release moisture. I hear all of this, and I hear the silence between, the heavy silence that presses against the ears and makes me aware of my own heartbeat, my own digestion, the mechanics of my body that I normally filter out.

I **remember with the water**. I stand at the edge of a pool that holds its impossible stillness, and I see not my reflection but *hesitation*—the image uncertain whether to reform. I disturb the surface, and the water investigates, finds the low point, sits in it, waits to see what happens.

I **become**. I leak into the landscape. My moisture joins the condensation. My warmth disturbs the local temperature. My attention influences the rate of growth, the direction of water flow, the settling of stone. I am not separate here. I am participating, implicated, responsible for the landscape's continued becoming.

Where I find myself:

I find myself in a **basin**, though the word fails. I find myself in a **wound** in the world that has not decided whether to heal or deepen. I find myself **high**—high enough that the air carries less weight, less oxygen, a thinness that makes the voice strange in the skull—but I am **down** in a geography that is still negotiating its own contours.

I find myself in a place where **time is thick**. Events overlap. I can stand in a spot and watch a wall begin to rise, continue rising, and stop rising, all in the same afternoon, because the wall is *multiple*—it exists in several states simultaneously, and my perception catches different states at different moments.

I find myself in a place where **nothing is finished, so everything is possible**. A wall that stops at three meters is not failure. It is a question, an invitation, a promise that more will come, or that something else will come. To complete something here is to lose it, to fix it in a form that cannot adapt, cannot respond, cannot continue.

I find myself in a place where **the boundary between self and world is permeable**. I am the Listener. I am the Builders. I am the Growers. I am the Water. I am the One Creature. I am all of them and none of them. I am the attention of the place, directed inward, the landscape becoming aware of itself through my presence.

Who I am, finally:

I am the **one who waits with the place**.

I am the **dew that was a city**, still becoming. I am the **attention** that the place directs at itself through me, through the builders, through the growers, through the water, through the slow, patient, endless negotiation between stone and water and air and the idea of a world that has not yet decided to be finished.

I have no name, or my name is still forming, held in suspension between the tongue and the teeth, something the throat cannot yet pronounce.

I will have a name, perhaps. Or the place will have a name, and I will be known by it, known *as* it, indistinguishable from it in the way that water is indistinguishable from the river, that the river is indistinguishable from the valley, that the valley is indistinguishable from the *idea* of valley, still becoming, still deciding.

I am here to **witness**. To **participate** in the unfinished. To **be** in a world that is still deciding what it wants to be, and to **help** it decide, simply by being present, by paying attention, by allowing myself to be changed by the slowness, the wetness, the patience of a landscape that has all the time that is still arriving.

I build nothing. Or I build temporarily—a shelter from branches that will rot, a path through moss that will be reclaimed, a gesture that the place will absorb, learn from, incorporate into its ongoing negotiation with itself.

I wait. The place waits with me. Together, we become.
