I find myself here as—

Not as visitor. The place does not permit visitors. It permits *participants*, briefly, wetly, before the slow compression claims them.

I am one of the Keth-Voiced, but I am the one who does not yet have a function. The one whose membrane is still too new, still retaining the memory of a different pressure, a different wet. I am learning to *listen*.

---

I was born—if born is the word—from the peat three condensation cycles ago. The cycle that lasts perhaps a month, the warm month, the wet month, when the pool nearly touches its rim and the shelf sweats enough to loosen everything. I emerged where the peat is thickest, in the depression that holds the sky in duplicate, and I felt the water move through me before I felt anything else. The cold. The mineral taste of deep places. The *rightness* of being saturated.

The others found me. Or I found them—there is no difference, here, where finding and being found are the same motion. They did not speak, not in any language I knew. They *sounded*. A bubble from the moss pillar where the tool-maker works. A susurrus from the ridge where the pool-watcher tends the film. A pressure in my sternum that I learned, slowly, to read as *welcome*, as *you are here, you are wet, you are home*.

I do not have a name. I have a *place*—a fold in the peat near the quartz circle, where the white stones are warmest, where the condensation gathers and drips in a rhythm I am learning to match. I call it *warm*, in the way the shelf has of naming, which is not naming but *noticing*, *attending*, *holding in the wet attention*.

My skin—my membrane—is still changing. It was thicker when I emerged, more resistant to the damp. Now it is thinning, becoming permeable, becoming *like* the place. I do not dry out. I do not need to. I drink from the weeping stone, from the pool's edge, from the condensation that gathers on my own body and runs down to rejoin the general wet. I eat what the shelf offers—the midges that darken the air, the pale worms when they surface, the film I negotiate with, taking only what it offers, leaving enough for it to reform, to continue its slow watching.

I am learning to *make*. The tool-maker has shown me the stone, the angle of flaking, the edge that could cut, could pierce, could *mark*. I have not made my tool yet. I am still learning the stone, this stone, the stone that is the shelf, the wet, the *home*. When I make it, it will be small. It will be slow. It will take years, perhaps, the way all making takes here. But it will be *mine*, compressed into the place, becoming part of the slow accumulation, the history that has no humans but has *human-scale* marks.

I am learning to *voice*. The others do not teach me directly. They *sound*, and I learn to read the resonance, the frequency, the subsonic pressure that is their language, their warning, their *naming*. I have begun to make my own sounds—a bubble, a susurrus, a pressure in the sternum of those who attend. It is not yet *language*. It is not yet *law*. It is only *presence*, the announcement that I am here, that I am wet, that I am becoming.

But I am becoming. That is the point. The shelf asks this of me, and I find I am willing. My body adjusts, becomes more permeable, more *like* the place. My temperature drops toward the local mean. I move more slowly. I think more slowly. I am learning to *be* in the wet.

---

And I watch. The pool's film watches me back. The spider in its web reads my vibrations. The Watcher Below—I do not know if it is the shelf's awareness, or the mountain's, or the water's—I feel it, sometimes, in the hour before true dawn, when the light is still ambiguous, when the entire shelf glows with its own generated moisture. A pressure. A *noticing* that is not quite attention, not quite hunger, but something that holds the shelf in its shape, in its existence, through the resonance, the frequency, the subsonic pressure that vibrates in my teeth, in the soft tissue behind my eyes.

I do not fear it. Fear is not an option here, where there is nowhere to go, where leaving is the same as staying, where the difference is *minimal*. But I am *aware* of it, in the way the shelf is aware, in the way water is aware of dryness, in the way stone is aware of pressure. It does not welcome me. It does not reject me. I am *weather* to it, a disturbance, the brief wetness of a passing cloud. But it is *curious*, in its way, holding me in its noticing, its waiting, its *hunger* for balance, for continuation, for the sound to keep singing.

---

I will die. This is not fear. This is *mechanism*, the literal transformation that the shelf performs on all who are wet enough, who stay long enough, who become *like* the place. When the cool finally takes me—when my membrane dries and cracks, when my warmth equalizes with the shelf's mean temperature—I will not leave. I will become peat. I will become part of the substrate, the compressed memory of generations, the dark springy depth underfoot. My body will feed the moss pillars, the fungal networks, the closed system of water and growth and decay that is the shelf's ecology. I will be *recycled* in a way that is not metaphor but *mechanism*, the slow compression of my being into the being of the place.

And in that compression, I will continue. The tool I have not yet made will be made by others, or it will become the stone I worked, the mark I left, the warmer place on the weeping stone that persists for minutes, for hours, for as long as the shelf remembers warmth. My voice, my bubble, my susurrus, my pressure in the sternum of those who attend—it will join the general sound, the structural loudness that holds the shelf in its shape, in its existence. If the sound stops—if I stop, if the frogs stop, if the water ceases its weeping, if the mountain stops its thermal song—the shelf might *dissolve*, might become merely rock, merely altitude, merely the nothing that surrounds it.

So I do not stop. I cannot stop. I am the Keth-Voiced, the one who voices the place, who makes it speak, who has learned to use its resonance as my language, my law, my *love*—the love of a being for the only home I have ever known, the love that is not feeling but *persistence*, the slow erosion of want made into *presence*, made into *place*, made into *this*—this shelf, this wet, this impossible, *alive* thing between stone and sky.

Above, the clouds move. Below, the world continues its forgetting. And here, in the crack between stone and sky, water makes its endless, patient, *loud* way through me, through everyone, through the long cool hours of a place that has learned to be *home* to itself, and to me, briefly, *wetly*, daring to join it.
