# The Wetlung

## A Descent

You enter through a wound in the roof of the world. Not a door—something *older* than doors. A fissure in basalt where water has been negotiating with stone for uncounted years, and the stone, being patient and mineral, has begun to *agree*. Drip by drip. The entrance is a throat. Warm air exhales from it. You feel it on your face before you see anything: 1.5 degrees of temperature is nothing on a chart, but *underground*, *below surface*, *in the dark*—it is a climate. It is *weather*.

---

## The Architecture of Growth

Vegetation here does not *occupy* space. Vegetation **is** the space.

The Wetlung is a body made of plant. Not a garden. Not a forest with rooms carved into it. Think instead of the inside of a seed, magnified to cathedral scale—if seeds were industrial, if they had been engineered by something that understood Load and Pressure and the economics of cellulose. The walls breathe. Literally: you can see them expand and contract with cycles you don't yet understand. Moisture moves through them in visible pulses, up from root-networks below, down from condensation above, a circulatory system of xylem and phloem scaled until it becomes *infrastructure*.

The species here are **unnameable** in any taxonomy you brought. They are 2.9 out of 3. Total. Which means: where you expected to see *through* the Wetlung, you see *more of it*. Depth without distance. The far wall is not a wall. It is a thicket of slow-growing structural tissue, lignin reinforced with mineral deposits seeping from the rock, that has been *growing* toward some light-source that failed, generations ago, and has simply... continued. Adapted. The dark did not stop it. The dark *shaped* it.

Bioluminescence provides the only native illumination. Dim, per your parameters—0.6 out of 3—but *distributed*. Not a single glow. Thousands. Millions. Spore-sacs the size of your fist, pulsing on cycles that have nothing to do with day or night because there is no day here. They are blue-white, mostly, with occasional specimens of amber and a rare, startling red that means something you haven't learned yet.

## The Damp

Water at 1.2 is not *flooded*. It is *negotiated*.

You walk on a floor that is not floor. It is a mesh of root-mats, fungal networks, and compressed detritus that has been recycling itself for so long that "soil" is the wrong word. Call it *medium*. It yields. It remembers your weight after you pass. In low points, water collects—not pools, exactly, but *lenses*. Pockets. Condensation gathers on the underside of overhanging growth and falls in slow, deliberate drops that have worn small channels into the medium over centuries. These channels are the Wetlung's hydrology. They are *also* its roads, its boundaries, its memory.

When it rains—and it rains, sometimes, in the upper reaches where the fissure opens—the entire structure *responds*. Growth accelerates visibly. You can watch it. Not fast enough to see in a single moment, but if you sit (and something will make you sit, eventually, or you will sit because you have nowhere else to be), you will notice that the wall beside you has *moved*. Millimeters. The architecture is alive in a way that makes "building" feel like slander.

---

## What Lives Here

Fauna at 3.0 is not "many animals." It is **overwhelming**. The Wetlung is *full* of life, and the life is *hungry*, and the hunger is *organized*.

Start with the small. The medium teems with organisms that have never seen light and do not want it. They are pale, eyeless, translucent in places where their exoskeletons have not yet mineralized. They move through the root-mats like water through a sponge. You will feel them on your skin if you touch the walls. Not biting. *Investigating*. Testing whether you are substrate.

Then the middle scale. These have names, though you won't know them. They are the Wetlung's *engineers*. Fungal-animal symbiotes, perhaps, or something that convergently evolved to fill the same niche. They are 20–40 centimeters long, segmented, with too many legs and a head that opens *sideways*. They maintain the structure. They eat the dead growth and excrete the nutrients that feed the living. Without them, the Wetlung would compost itself into collapse within a generation. With them, it persists. They are *numerous*. In any given cubic meter of wall, you might find dozens. They do not fear you. They have never needed to.

Then the large. And here the weirdness parameter, 2.4 out of 3, becomes *necessary* rather than optional.

There is something in the deep places. Something that has grown, or been grown, or *become* through processes that don't map onto evolution as you understand it. It is not hostile. This is important. It is not *friendly* either. It is **vast** and **patient** and **digesting**.

You will hear it before you see it. The sound parameter is 2.5—loud—and the Wetlung is *never silent*. The drip of water. The creak of growing tissue, which sounds like a ship in a storm, like rigging under strain. The *clicking* of the middle-scale engineers, a sound like a workshop full of very small industrious machines. And beneath all this, a low *resonance*. Not a voice. A *frequency*. The whole structure vibrates at it, a standing wave in the architecture of cellulose and stone and water. You feel it in your sternum. You will dream about it later, in whatever light you find above.

## What Was Built

The built parameter, 1.5—urban, settlement-scale—is where the Wetlung becomes *strange* rather than merely wild.

Because there are *structures* here. Not natural growth. *Construction*.

You will find them in the middle depths, where the root-mats have achieved sufficient density to support weight. Platforms, mostly. Walkways that follow the logic of the water-channels, the natural highways through the medium. They are built from the same material as the walls—compressed growth, mineralized, shaped—but the shaping is *intentional*. Regular. *Designed*.

And on these platforms: *machines*.

Tech at 2.5 is industrial. Engines. Mechanized production. But the machines here are not metal and oil. They are **biomechanical**. Chambers where fungal cultures are grown under controlled (as controlled as anything can be) conditions of temperature and humidity and nutrient flow. Pumps—actual pumps, with pistons and valves, but grown rather than forged, animated by colonies of contractile tissue that respond to pressure differentials. A infrastructure of *cultivation*.

What is being cultivated?

You will find out, or you won't. The Wetlung does not advertise its purposes. But you will find *products*. Vessels of hardened resin, harvested from the walls and shaped while still pliable. Fibers stronger than any synthetic you know, woven into cables that run between platforms, that *pulse* with something—fluid, perhaps, or a conductive sap, or both. Light-sources, the bioluminescent cultures grown in dense clusters and *harnessed*, their output channeled through transparent tubes of modified xylem.

There are no people. This is not a ruin of a civilization. It is something else. A *process* that has achieved self-maintenance. A factory that is also a body, that grows and repairs and *reproduces* (asexually, through fragmentation and budding, new Wetlungs budding from old in a slow geography of expansion).

Or perhaps there *were* people, once, above, in the world you came from, and they *seeded* this. Planted the first specimens. The parameters don't say. The weirdness insists. You can feel it in the way the platforms are *arranged*—not randomly, not purely functionally, but with a kind of *aesthetic* logic. Symmetry where symmetry serves no purpose you can identify. Beauty, or its simulation, in a place where no eye was meant to see it.

## The Rules

The Wetlung operates on principles that will take you time to learn, if you stay. If you can stay. The density parameter, 1.4—sparse—means there is *room* here. Space between presences. You are not crushed by the vegetation, not overwhelmed in the physical sense. You can move. You can *choose* a direction, though whether your choice matters is another question.

The rules emerge from the parameters:

**Moisture is currency.** Everything here trades in water. The growth, the fauna, the machines—all of it depends on the 1.2, the *damp*, the negotiation with dryness. If you understand the water, you understand the Wetlung. Follow the condensation. Follow the drips. They lead to the deep places, and the deep places lead to the *heart*, where the resonance is strongest and something vast is dreaming in its slow, vegetative sleep.

**Growth is time.** Nothing here is fast. The erosion parameter, 2.5—established, weathered, in-balance—means the Wetlung has been *here* for a long duration. It has settled into its own state. It does not hurry. You will learn patience, or you will leave, or you will be *incorporated*—not killed, but *used*, your body broken down and your nutrients added to the medium, a small contribution to a structure that will outlast your name by millennia.

**Sound is information.** At 2.5, loud, the Wetlung is *talking*. The drips. The creaks. The clicking. The resonance. Learn to hear the differences. A healthy wall has a certain *tone*. A distressed wall—a section where water has failed, where growth has stalled—sounds *wrong*. Flat. You will learn to hear the Wetlung's *moods*. It is not sentient, probably. But it is *responsive*, and response, at sufficient complexity, becomes indistinguishable from something like *attention*.

**The fauna is not *for* you.** At 3.0, overwhelming, the life here has its own economy. Predator and prey, yes—the dynamic parameter, 2.3, active, means processes are *moving*, cycles are turning, the ecosystem is in motion. But you are not part of that economy. You are a *disturbance*. A novel input. The medium-scale engineers will investigate you. The large things in the deep places will *notice* you, eventually, and what they do with that notice is not determined by any hostility or welcome. It is determined by *chemistry*. By whether you smell like substrate or like something else. By whether you carry water, which you do, and whether you are willing to *give* it.

## The Atmosphere

Dim. Warm. Loud. Wet in the way that *breath* is wet, that *life* is wet—you are inside something that is metabolizing, and you feel the metabolism on your skin.

The light, 0.6, comes from everywhere and nowhere. The bioluminescence is diffuse, ambient, *biological*. There are no shadows in the way you understand them. Only *degrees* of illumination, gradations of blue-white and amber, the occasional pulse of red that means *attention* or *reproduction* or *danger*—you will learn which.

The air is thick. Not humid in the way of a jungle, but *dense*, carrying spores and particulates and the chemical signatures of a million organisms. You will taste it. You will dream in it. If you stay long enough, you will begin to *smell* in new ways, to read the chemical conversations happening around you—the alarm pheromones of disturbed engineers, the slow release of spores from a maturing fruiting body, the *signature* of the deep places, where something vast exhales gases that have no name in your language.

## What You Will Become

The scale is 1.0—small. Cathedral scale. Grove-or-room scale. You could walk the Wetlung in a day, if you knew the paths. You could *live* in it for years and never see the same corner twice, because the corners *move*, because the growth is *active*, because the Wetlung is not a place but a *process* that has achieved sufficient complexity to be mistaken for a place.

You will adapt, or you will leave, or you will be *changed*. The parameters suggest a landscape that *incorporates*. The vegetation is total. The fauna is overwhelming. The built is urban, *organized*, *layered*. This is not wilderness. This is not ruined civilization. This is **something else**, a third category, a place where the distinctions you brought with you—nature/culture, wild/tended, alive/machine—do not *fail* but *become irrelevant*.

The Wetlung does not care about your categories. It cares about water. It cares about growth. It cares about the slow, patient, *industrial* work of becoming more of itself, of expanding into the dark, of budding new Wetlungs in a geography that is mostly stone and mostly dry and mostly *waiting*.

You are here, now, in the damp, in the dim, in the loud. The resonance is in your chest. The engineers are watching. The walls are breathing.

What do you do?
