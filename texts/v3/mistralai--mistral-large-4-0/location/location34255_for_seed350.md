The world is a drowning made conscious.

You are everywhere and nowhere, inside something that has not decided whether it is ocean or atmosphere. The water here is not water as you know it—not H₂O but a *mood*, a pressure, a saturated brown-green that fills every cavity, every breath, every thought you attempt. When you try to rise, you rise into more of it. When you try to sink, you sink into more of it. Up and down have become suggestions. The light, what passes for light, comes from no direction—0.9, dim, the color of old bruises pressed against the inside of your eyelids. You see because you are seen, and the seeing is mutual, exhausting, *intimate* in a way that makes you ashamed of your own solitude.

This is a place that has recently emerged. The erosion parameter tells you this: 0.6, young, sharp. The land beneath your feet—if feet still work, if you have feet—is fresh volcanic glass, obsidian still holding the heat of its birth, fissured in patterns that haven't yet softened into the rounded patience of age. Steam vents everywhere, not the clean white of geysers but thick, *biological* exhalations, the exhausted breath of something tectonic waking up. The terrain is honest. It has not learned to lie yet. Sharp features everywhere: crystalline ridges that cut the water into channels, sudden drop-offs where the seafloor remembers being magma, mineral flowers blooming in impossible geometries because the chemistry has not settled into the familiar.

And the vegetation. 2.2, lush, *obscene* in its fertility. It grows *through* the water, not in it. Kelp forests that are not kelp—thick as your wrist, braided, photosynthesizing with a desperation that makes them glow faintly from within, bioluminescent veins pulsing in rhythms that almost match a heartbeat, almost match a warning. They grow on the obsidian. They grow on each other. They grow in spirals that ascend into depths you cannot measure, their fronds heavy with spores, with seeds, with *intent*. The water is thick with them, 2.3, dense, near-touching, layered—you move through a green-brown soup of cellulose and ambition, every surface breathing, every gap filled with something reaching, something *hungry* in the plant way, the patient way, the way that has all the time that geological time provides. They do not rot here. Not yet. Rot requires a stability this place has not achieved.

The temperature is warm everywhere. 2.0, not tropical, not temperate, but *womb*—the consistent heat of a body that has not yet been born, or has been born and forgotten to cool. The water holds this heat like a secret. Your skin, if you have skin, learns not to distinguish between your own warmth and the medium's. You are *permeable*. The boundary between you and the world is a negotiation, and the world is winning.

Sound. 2.7, deafening, but wrong. Not the crash of surf—there is no shore, no shore, no *shore*—but a *roar* that comes from everywhere and nowhere, the sound of pressure equalizing, of gas escaping from newly solid rock, of the vast and insensate machinery of the deep. It is not heard so much as *felt* in the teeth, in the marrow, in the places where your body remembers being single-celled. Above it, or below it, or woven through it: the *clicking*. The vegetation communicates in frequencies that register as pain behind the eyes. The obsidian *sings* as it cools, a descending whine that never quite resolves. And beneath everything, the *pulse* of something massive moving through the water-column, something that does not belong to the fauna parameter—0.1, no animal life, *officially*—but you feel it in the pressure changes, in the way the kelp-forests orient toward its passing, in the sudden silence that follows its wake. It is not an animal. It is a *process*. A hunger that has not yet learned to be a mouth.

The built environment is scattered, faint, *haunted*. 1.1, partial remains, artifacts that do not fit. You find them without looking: a staircase of rusted metal descending into water that was not water when the stairs were built. A factory floor, tiles still level, machines still *running*—2.4, industrial, engines turning in a medium that should drown them, gears meshed with something that is not oil but grows, but *lubricates*, a secretion from the vegetation that has learned to serve the machines or been taught to. The structures are half-drowned, half-grown-over, neither fully ruin nor fully integration. A smokestack that breathes steam that is also spores. A conveyor belt that carries not goods but *sediment*, sorting the young earth into categories that no longer matter. The technology does not work *despite* the water. It works *because* of it, in ways that suggest a previous intelligence, a previous *purpose*, something that built this place or built *in* this place or *was* this place before the water rose, or before the water *remembered* it was water.

And the weirdness. 2.7. Reality breakdown, *constant*. The water here is not a medium but a *argument*. It insists on its own properties, its own physics, its own *dreams*. You will see, if you stay long enough, that the kelp forests form patterns when viewed from angles that should not exist—geometries that hurt to follow, that suggest the water is *thinking* through them, using them, *becoming* them. The obsidian does not always stay where you left it. The steam vents exhale shapes that almost resolve into faces, into *instructions*, into names for things you have not yet lost. Time moves differently in different depths. At the surface—if there is a surface, if the water has a surface—moments stretch. Down in the warm dark, hours collapse. You can age here. You can *un-age*. The water remembers things that have not happened yet.

The dynamic parameter is your final warning: 2.7, turbulent, chaotic change, *storm-state*. This place is not stable. It is *becoming*. The young land is still rising, still cracking, still venting its newborn heat. The water is still rising, or falling, or *deciding*. The vegetation is evolving in real-time, adapting to pressures that change from moment to moment, speciating in visible bursts of green-gold innovation. The machines are *learning* the water, being taught by it, teaching it in return. Nothing here is finished. Everything is *pregnant* with the next configuration, the next collapse, the next impossible emergence.

You are in a place that has not yet chosen what it is. Ocean or atmosphere. Land or sea. Machine or organism. Ruin or genesis. The water holds all these possibilities in suspension, dense and warm and *loud*, and you are submerged in the not-yet-decided, breathing it, being breathed by it, becoming part of the argument.

There are no animals here. 0.1. But you will feel things brush against you. You will feel *attention*. The water itself has become, in this pressure, in this heat, in this density of growing and building and breaking, something that notices. Something that *waits*.

The sound does not stop. It cannot stop. It is the sound of a world being born, and birth is *deafening*.
