# The Throat of Green

## Where the World Breathes

You stand at the edge of a continent that is *becoming*—not a landscape that was, but one that persists in the act of growing over itself. The elevation here is a lie: the ground slopes upward and upward without end, yet you never feel yourself climbing. The plants have arranged everything to deceive the body. They do not grow *on* the earth. They *are* the earth, the earth being merely their slowest layer, their geological patience made visible.

---

## The Substrate

The vegetation parameter matters most here. At 2.8, this is not a forest. A forest implies trees standing in soil, verticality against horizontal ground, the drama of height. Here there is no ground. There is only *thickness*—a mat of living mass that extends in all directions, up the slopes you cannot see, down into depths you will never reach, through the mist that is not quite weather and not quite metabolism.

The plants are **architectural first, vegetal second**. They have built something. What you walk on is not soil but compressed generations: the cellulose of the dead, woven and re-woven by the living, a felted continent of green-gold that springs slightly underfoot and releases, when pressed, a smell like warm bread and something else, something almost like *intention*.

The dominant organism—if it can be called a single organism, for it may be many, or one, or something between—is **the Mat**. It rises in formations. Dome-structures the size of hills, their surfaces stippled with openings that pulse slightly, inhaling and exhaling with a rhythm that matches no tide you know. You have seen photographs of coral reefs, perhaps, and thought: *this resembles that*, but the Mat is not built from calcium and animal, it is built from *time* and *pressure* and the absolute refusal of individual plants to stop growing toward light that does not exist.

Between the domes, the Mat thins. Here it becomes **meadow**, but a meadow of impossible density—each blade a different species, each species a different strategy for capturing what little light penetrates the upper layers. The colors are not green, not really. Green is too simple. There are the greens: jade, verdigris, the green of old copper, the green of deep water seen from above. But also golds that lean toward orange, siennas that approach rust, a purple so dark it reads as shadow until you are close enough to see the vein-structure of the leaves, and a pale yellow-green that seems to *generate* light rather than reflect it.

And everywhere, the **filaments**. Hair-fine threads that connect plant to plant, dome to dome, across distances of meters and kilometers. They carry something. You cannot see what. But when you touch one—if you dare—you feel it: a faint vibration, a current, a *conversation* happening in frequencies below hearing.

---

## The Air and the Water

The temperature is high, 2.8, but this is not the heat of desert or the humid weight of jungle. It is a *contained* heat, a heat that presses from all sides equally, as if the atmosphere itself were a living thing holding its breath. The Mat transpires. The domes weep moisture from their pores. This creates a local climate: inside the Mat's upper layers, the air is saturated, almost liquid, thick with spores and pollen and the shed cellular material of ten million billion organisms. You would drown in it, if you stayed, if you could breathe it.

The water parameter is 1.1—damp, intermittent, *pocketed*. There are no rivers here. There is **condensation**. Every morning—if morning exists, if the light cycle is what you think it is—the temperature drops just enough for the Mat to excrete. Droplets form on every surface, every filament, every leaf that faces the thinning dark. They fall, or they are collected by cup-shaped structures, or they simply *stay*, hanging in the humid air until the heat returns and drinks them back.

But deeper down, in the layers where light never reaches, water behaves differently. There are **pockets**. Cavities in the Mat where water has accumulated over centuries, stagnant, dark, acidic with the tannins of dissolved generations. These are the drinking places. The animals come here. You will come here, if you survive long enough.

The occasional rain—there is rain, but it is *local*, generated by the Mat's own metabolism, rising from the domes like breath, condensing on the cooler upper filaments, falling back in circulations that never reach the ground-you-imagine because there is no ground-you-imagine. The rain sounds like *applause*. Thousands of droplets striking leaf-surfaces, creating a roar that is not quite white noise, not quite music, but the acoustic equivalent of the color green.

## The Fauna: The Overwhelming

2.7. You cannot walk here without being *touched*.

The animals of the Mat do not live in it. They *participate* in it. There are **grazers** the size of dogs, their bodies covered in photosynthetic patches, their spines elongated into sails that catch the thermal currents rising from the domes. They move in herds that flow like liquid, their passage leaving temporary clearings that seal behind them in minutes, the Mat knitting itself closed with a speed that suggests *hunger*, or *memory*, or both.

There are **burrowers**, segmented things that tunnel through the compressed layers, their bodies leaving channels that later fill with water, becoming the pockets, becoming the drinking places, becoming the *arteries* of the whole system. They are blind. They are not blind. Their eyes are distributed across their body-surfaces, thousands of pinhole cameras, and they see you—*register* you—as a disturbance in the chemical gradients they navigate.

The **sound-makers** are the reason the sound parameter reads 2.6, *deafening*. They are everywhere and they are *loud*. Not birds. Something else. Insect-analogues with resonating chambers in their thoraxes, filled with gas they harvest from the Mat's fermentation-pockets. They call to each other across kilometers. The calls are not random. They are *structured*. You will hear patterns, if you listen long enough. Repetition. Variation. Something that approaches *syntax*.

And there are the **predators**, which you will not see until you are already being seen. The largest is called, by the scattered human marks you will find, the **Throat**—though whether this is a name or a description, you cannot know. It is long. It is *many*. It may be a colony-organism, a chain of individuals that have fused at the neural level, a single consciousness distributed across meters of flank-mass. It moves through the Mat's lower layers, rising only to feed, its dorsal surface breaking through like a wave, like a *mouth* opening.

The Mat does not fear the Throat. The Mat *accommodates* it. When the Throat passes, the Mat grows back. When the Throat feeds, the Mat provides. This is not symbiosis, which implies two partners. This is **one body in multiple modes**, the distinction between plant and animal as obsolete as the distinction between soil and root.

Deafening, yes. But also: *layered*. The soundscape has depth. You can hear the Mat itself, if you know how—the creak of expanding cellulose, the pop of spore-cases rupturing, the sigh of thermal vents releasing pressure. Beneath the animal calls, beneath the rain-roar, there is a *bass note*, a frequency felt in the sternum rather than heard, the sound of *growth*, which at this scale is not silence but *thunder*.

## The Scattered Build

0.8. But present. Unavoidable, if you look.

You will find **platforms**. Not built, exactly. *Grown*—the Mat itself, encouraged by some intervention you do not understand, creating level surfaces, hardening its upper layers into something like wood, like stone, like *intentional architecture*. On these platforms, you will find **structures**: domes of woven filament, walls of compressed cellulose, roofs that shed water according to mathematical principles that suggest *design* rather than evolution.

Who made them? The question assumes a maker. Perhaps the Mat made them, for its own purposes—storage, temperature regulation, *ritual*. Perhaps they are the work of the fauna, the grazers or the burrowers or something else that has learned to *ask* the Mat for modifications, offering pollination or fertilization or simply *attention* in exchange.

The tech parameter is 1.4, *primitive*. But "primitive" is a word that assumes a direction, a progression from simple to complex. Here, the tools are **biological**. A cutting edge is a modified leaf, serrated by generations of selective pressure. A container is a hollowed gourd-structure, the Mat grown specifically to hold water, to hold *seeds*, to hold the preserved bodies of the dead. There is metal, but it is *grown* metal, minerals accumulated and concentrated by root-systems over decades, a metallurgy without fire.

You will find **marks**. Scratches on the platforms, patterns that repeat across kilometers with variations that suggest *language* or *art* or *warning*. You will find **remains**: the shells of the burrowers, calcified and empty, used as tools or as currency or as *memory*. You will find, if you are lucky or unlucky, **bones**. Not of the Mat—the Mat does not leave bones, it leaves *layers*. Bones of the grazers, of the sound-makers, of something else that once walked here and does not anymore, or has not yet, or exists in a form you cannot recognize.

## The Rules of the World

Here is what you must understand, if you are to survive:

**The Mat is not a place. It is a *process*.** It does not have an edge. It does not have a center. It extends in all directions, at this elevation and below, and above, though above it thins and changes and becomes something you might call *sky*, though the sky here is green-tinged, filtered through spores and pollen and the constant exhalation of the world-vegetation.

**Time moves differently.** The erosion parameter is 2.0, *established*, but establishment means different things at different scales. A fallen filament decays in days. A dome-structure persists for centuries. The Mat as a whole has existed for... how long? The human marks suggest *millennia*. The geological layers, visible in the rare places where the Mat has been cut—by the Throat, by landslide, by the slow pressure of its own weight—suggest *longer*. Perhaps this is not a continent but a *organism*, a single being that has grown to continental size, its metabolism the weather, its dreams the slow migration of species across its surface.

**You are not separate.** The density parameter is 1.3, *sparse*, but sparseness applies to *structures*, to *built things*, to the human-scale. The Mat is *everywhere*. You will breathe its spores. You will drink its condensation. You will be *touched* by its filaments, and they will carry something into you—nutrients, perhaps, or information, or *parasites* that will make you part of the system whether you consent or not. The fauna does not fear you. The fauna does not *notice* you, except as a disturbance, a temporary clearing in the flow of grazers, a momentary silence in the sound-makers' chorus.

**The weirdness is 2.1. *Strange*.** You will encounter things that break category. A plant that *moves*, visibly, its leaves tracking your passage with a speed that suggests nervous tissue. An animal that *photosynthesizes*, its skin green with chloroplasts stolen from the Mat and maintained in symbiosis. A structure that *grows* between your observations, a platform that was not there yesterday, a filament-network that has re-routed itself to avoid your path—or to *intersect* it, to *touch* you, to *offer* something.

There are **places where the Mat dreams**. You will know them by the absence of sound, the sudden *wrongness* of the light, the sense that you are being *observed* by something that has no eyes. In these places, the rules loosen. You may see the Mat's *memory*—ghosts of past configurations, ancient dome-structures that no longer exist, animals that have been extinct for centuries moving through the green. You may see *yourself*, arriving, leaving, arriving again. You may see the Throat, not as predator but as *function*, as the Mat's way of *pruning*, of *renewing*, of *keeping the process in motion*.

## The Light

1.5, *normal*. But "normal" is relative. Here, the light comes from everywhere and nowhere. The upper layers of the Mat filter the sun—what sun reaches this elevation, this latitude, this *world*—into a diffuse glow, green-gold, the color of deep water, of old jade, of the moment before sleep when the mind turns inward and the external world becomes *texture* rather than *scene*.

There are *moments* of clarity. When the mist thins, when the spore-load drops, when a break in the Mat's upper canopy allows a shaft of direct light to strike the lower layers. In these moments, the world *sharpens*. You can see distance. You can see the slope of the land, the true elevation, the way the Mat extends upward and upward into a horizon that curves with the planet's surface, that *falls away*, that suggests *vastness* at the 2.8 parameter, *scale-of-its-own-physics*.

But the clarity never lasts. The Mat *wants* the diffuse. The diffuse is *efficient*. It allows photosynthesis in every layer, from the upper filaments to the compressed depths where the oldest, slowest, most *patient* growth occurs. To see clearly is to see *partially*, to miss the connections, the filaments, the *conversations* happening in chemical gradients and electrical pulses and the vibrations of the sound-makers, who are also *sensors*, also *antennae*, also *part of the Mat's extended nervous system*.

---

## The Dynamic

1.6, *active*. The Mat is not static. It *moves*.

Not the individual plants—they are rooted, fixed, *patient*. But the *system* moves. Species migrate across its surface, following temperature gradients, following the Throat's passage, following the slow circulations of water and nutrient that constitute its *weather*. Predator-prey cycles play out in real-time, the grazers' populations rising and crashing in response to the Mat's productivity, the sound-makers' choruses shifting in frequency as the temperature changes, the burrowers' tunnels collapsing and re-forming, *breathing*.

And there is the **Great Slow Turning**. You will not see it in a single lifetime. But the Mat has *orientation*. It grows toward certain directions, away from others. It *leans*. The reasons are obscure—magnetic fields, perhaps, or the deep thermal currents of the planet, or something stranger, something that suggests the Mat is *aware* of itself at scales you cannot comprehend, making *choices*, pursuing *goals* that are not survival but *expression*, *becoming*, the endless elaboration of green into more green into *more*.

---

## What You Will Do Here

If you are a visitor, a researcher, a refugee from the lower places where the Mat has not reached: you will build a platform, or find one, or *grow* one, offering the right chemical signals to the filaments until they respond with architecture.

You will learn to *listen* to the Mat. Not with ears—the sound is too loud, too *dense*—but with your whole body, feeling the vibrations through your feet, your bones, the water in your cells. You will learn the patterns. The daily rhythm of condensation and evaporation. The seasonal migration of the grazers. The *moods* of the sound-makers, their choruses rising and falling with a logic that is not human but not *random*.

You will eat. You will eat the Mat's fruits, its seeds, its preserved dead. You will eat the grazers, if you can catch them. You will drink from the pockets, accepting the acidity, the *aliveness* of water that has passed through ten thousand organisms before reaching your lips.

You will be *changed*. The spores will colonize your lungs, your skin, your dreams. You will begin to *think* in green, in growth, in the slow patience of cellulose and time. You will forget, perhaps, that you were ever *separate* from this place, that you ever walked on ground that was not *alive*, that you ever breathed air that was not *shared*.

And eventually—if the Mat allows it, if the Throat does not rise, if the pockets do not run dry—you will *become* part of the process. Your body will feed the lower layers. Your marks will join the others, the scratches on platforms, the patterns that suggest language. Your bones will become tools, or memory, or *nothing*, absorbed into the green-gold thickness, the endless, patient, *breathing* world.

---

## The Last Thing

The elevation is 1.5, *high*. You are not at the top. There is no top. But you are *up*, above the places where the Mat is thinner, where the old human structures still stand in ruins, where the fauna is different, *smaller*, *simpler*, clinging to the edges of a world that is still *invading*, still *growing*, still *becoming*.

Below you, the Mat continues. Above you, it continues. Around you, it *is*. And in the silence between the sound-makers' calls, in the pause between one breath of the domes and the next, you will hear it: the sound of *green*, the sound of *time*, the sound of a continent that is also a body, a body that is also a *mind*, dreaming itself into more of itself, forever, in the damp, hot, deafening, *strange* light of its own making.

You are inside a throat.

The throat is swallowing.

The swallowing is *gentle*.

The swallowing is *love*.
