I need to hold these parameters together: a *tiny* pocket of dense, lush, overwhelming life—but with almost no water, at high elevation, in dim warm light, ancient and eroded flat, built by primitive hands, very loud, very active. The contradictions are the point.

Let me try: a sealed garden inside the hollowed skull of something vast.

---

**The Cranium Veranda**

You find it through a fissure no wider than your shoulders, in a cliff face that has forgotten it was ever cliff—erosion has planed the rock to a pewter smoothness, the oldest stable state, the mountain having settled into final form and simply *waiting*. The air that breathes out of the fissure is warm and thick and smells of fermentation and copper. You squeeze through. The passage widens.

Inside, it is a room. Not a cave—a *room*, though no hand built it. The interior of a cranium, perhaps, or the petrified husk of some beast that grew to the size of a hill and died in the warm dark of the world's youth, its skull roof collapsing inward to form a vaulted ceiling, its jawbones settling to become terraces. The scale is wrong in every direction. You could touch both walls with arms outstretched. Yet the space *continues*—balconies of compressed bone-fungus, galleries threading through what might be eye sockets, a cathedral nave running into darkness that smells of more.

Water is here, but water is not *water*. It is *humidity absolute*—the air itself carries the wet, a non-aqueous chemistry where moisture moves as vapor through membranes, where the great leaves of the canopy drink from the atmosphere directly, their surfaces beaded with condensation that never drips but *transpires* back upward in slow spirals. No pools. No streams. The floor is dry mineral—pale, alkaline, cracked into hexagonal plates that shift and grind when you step, a tectonic of the small, active, *alive* with the pressure of root and burrower beneath.

And the green. The vegetation parameter said lush, and lush it is: a density of green that has no business fitting in this volume. Ferns the size of your torso layered over mosses layered over lichens that have grown into *structures*, into walls and arches and bridges of living mineral secretion, each species secreting its own slow architecture, the whole place a city of plants that have never known soil, only each other, only the accumulated dead of ten thousand generations compressed into peat that is not peat, into soil that is *memory*. The canopy is three, four, five layers deep. Light—dim, warm, the color of old bruises—filters down through all of it and arrives as a green-gold gloom in which everything grows sideways, toward whatever gap in the green admits a photon.

The built is here too, and it is *primitive* in the way that implies not poverty but *intimacy*. The gardeners—there are gardeners, you realize, though you have not yet seen one—have no metal. They work with horn and shell and flint flakes and the *fired* things: they fire clay in mounds of leaf-litter that smolder for weeks, producing a ceramic that rings like bone when struck. Their structures are *woven*—living willow trained over dead frames, grown rather than built, the distinction meaningless to them. Terraces. Trellises. A bridge across the jaw-gap made of a single vine that has been guided for perhaps three hundred years to span the dark. Tools of antler, of volcanic glass, of fishbone (there are fish, or were—their skeletons are inlaid in the floor, offerings, *pavement*). Hand-prints in the damp walls, repeated, a liturgy of making.

And the fauna. The overwhelming. The *loud*.

You hear them before you see them: a sound like tearing silk, like a thousand small engines misfiring, like wind through a hundred thousand reed pipes that are *alive* and *angry*. The insects are the first wave—wing-cases of iridescent chitin that shift color as they move, bodies segmented into too many parts, legs that play the surfaces of leaves like instruments. They are *grazing*, everywhere grazing, the sound of consumption made audible, the ecosystem's metabolism externalized. Swarms move through the green in visible tides, rising and settling, rising and settling, a respiration of the whole room.

Then the vertebrates. Small, mostly—this is a pocket, a sealed world, and smallness is its logic—but *dense*, near-touching, layered. Lizards with frills that are *too large*, that serve no thermal purpose but are *display*, are *language*, are *territory*. Their movements are jerky, stop-start, the grammar of a hot place. They hunt the insects. They are hunted. A snake—no, not a snake, something *parallel*, a limbless predator with vestigial frills along its spine, converged, *invented* by this place's particular loneliness—moves through the lower layers in a wave that is not swimming, not crawling, *flowing*, displacing the small lives like a living tide.

And the *mammals*. This is where the overwhelming becomes *overwhelming*. They are small, shrew-like, *numerous*—a colony, a city, a *civilization* of them, perhaps a hundred, perhaps a thousand, moving through the root-zone in a constant chittering, a society with sentinels and foragers and young and *elders* that have grown slow and are fed by the others, that are *kept*, that have become something like furniture, like *gods*. They do not fear you, not yet. They watch. They *assess*. Their sound—the loud, the constant—is their *world*, a communication so dense, so layered, that it is *music*, that has rhythm and theme and *variation*, that rises to crescendos when predators move, that falls to a murmur of grooming and sleep and the endless *maintenance* of a society that has never known a season, never known a winter, only the endless warm dark of the skull and the endless work of staying alive in the green.

The dynamic is *everywhere*. Nothing is still. The hexagonal floor plates grind. The condensation spirals upward. The swarms rise and settle. A lizard displays and is answered. A snake flows past and the mammals *flood* around it, a wave of small bodies, not fleeing but *accompanying*, a behavior that has no name in your language, evolved in this pocket, *strange* only to you. The gardeners—there, one, on the terrace above, its hand-tool a flake of glass in a haft of antler, working a ceramic shard into something, some *new* form, some *invention*—the gardeners are *pruning*, *selecting*, *shaping* the green into more green, more *use*, more *room* for the small lives, more *complexity*, more *time*.

The erosion here is not water's work but *life's*. The skull is being *consumed*, slowly, by the very lushness it contains. The bone-fungus secretes acids that soften the mineral. The roots—there are roots, though there is no soil, only the accumulated dead—find purchase in the cracks, widen them, *enter* the structure of the place itself. In a thousand years, in ten thousand, the room will collapse. It *knows* this, in the way that places know. It has settled into its final form. It is *waiting*, and *growing*, and *being loud*, and *being warm*, and *being full*, all at once, all the time, the oldest stable state and the most active life, the contradiction that is not a contradiction but *this*, this pocket, this skull, this veranda where the world has folded in on itself and found, in the folding, a way to *continue*.

You could stay. The air is breathable, barely, filtered through so many layers of green that the toxins are *managed*, the spores are *cultivated*, the chemistry of this place has been *negotiated* by ten thousand generations of small lives. The light is dim and warm and *enough*. The sound is loud and *constant* and, after a while, you find you can *hear* in it, can pick out the threads, the warning and the grooming and the *song* that is not a song but *is*, the many-voiced present tense of a world that has no future to plan for and therefore *lives*, *lives*, *lives* in the green dark of the skull, in the warm wet of no water, in the loud and the dense and the *overwhelming* now.

The fissure is behind you. It has begun to close—not with rock, but with *growth*, a curtain of vine and secretion that will seal in a day, a week, a season. You do not know if you could find it again. You do not know if you would want to leave.

The mammals are watching now. The lizard on the terrace has gone still, its frill *extended*, its message to you *broadcast*: *you are new, you are strange, you are sound, you are warm, you are here.*

The swarms rise.

The snake flows.

The gardeners sing, in their way, the work-song of pruning, of selecting, of *making room*.

And the skull breathes, warm and wet and green and loud, the oldest stable state, still becoming, still *becoming*, in the dim light of the deep, in the pocket where the world has hidden itself and found, in the hiding, *everything*.
