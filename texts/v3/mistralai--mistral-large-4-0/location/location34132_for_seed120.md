I need to hold these contradictions and let something emerge. Extreme elevation. Hidden, pocket-scale. Yet fully built, packed, wet, hot, dim, and reality breaking down. No erosion—the world is *too new* to have worn down. Primitive technology. No natural substrate, yet lush vegetation and rare fauna. Loud, but dim.

The **Veil-Cistern of Thornhame**

You do not arrive here. You are *admitted*.

The pocket opens like a mouth that has learned to keep its own mouth—two hundred meters across at widest, folded into a crater rim at 3,400 meters where the air should be thin enough to make thinking difficult. It is not thin. It is *thick*. Humid. It presses. The Cistern sits in the lee of a knife-edge ridge that catches weather from three systems and holds it, pooling it, refusing to let it drain. Water here is not a condition. It is an *architecture*. Every surface weeps. The walls—built, always built, no stone here is native—sweat condensation from their own mass, from the metabolism of the structure itself.

The builders do not use the word *builders*. They are the **Hollow Choir**, though they do not choir. They are rare. You will see one, perhaps, if the dimness lifts. They are tall, wrong-jointed, and they move through the packed alleyways of the Cistern with the patience of water finding level. They do not farm. They do not hunt. They *tend* the condensation. They harvest it, channel it, drink it, and in drinking it they remember things that have not happened yet. This is the weirdness. It is not dramatic. It is *hydrological*. Time here behaves like water in this place—pooling, finding its own level, occasionally flashing backward when the pressure in the walls spikes.

The Cistern is a single room, really. A vast, humid, dim chamber of packed construction. No natural substrate visible. The floor is *sinter*—mineral precipitated from the endless evaporation and condensation cycles, layered like tree rings but wrong, the layers sometimes running vertical, sometimes branching, sometimes *looping back on themselves*. The walls are something grown and something fired, indistinguishable now. There are no windows because there is no outside, not really. The "sky" above is a membrane, translucent, constantly dripping, showing only the vague shape of weather moving across it like a hand under wet cloth.

And it is *packed*. Not crowded—you will not see many inhabitants. Packed in the way a sponge is packed. Every pore occupied. The vegetation is lush beyond reason. It grows *between* the built surfaces, through them, over them, under them. A lichen that photosynthesizes by *absorbing* the dim light, not receiving it. Ferns with fronds like wet paper, unrolling and re-rolling in cycles that do not match any day-night rhythm because there is no sun here, only the glow of the membrane above and the bioluminescent veins that pulse through the sinter floor in patterns that might be language, might be digestion, might be the Cistern's own slow dreaming.

Sound is loud. Not from inhabitants. From *process*. The constant percussion of dripping—millions of drops, tuned by the geometry of the chamber into something almost harmonic, a wet music that never resolves. The groaning of the structure as it cools and warms in cycles you cannot feel but can hear, the membrane flexing, the sinter cracking and re-sealing. The Hollow Choir, when they move, make a sound like wet rope being pulled through a fist. And underneath it all, a low frequency that you feel in your teeth, in your sternum, that makes your memories arrive slightly out of order.

The technology is primitive and *total*. Everything is made. Even the "plants" are cultivated, bred over generations too deep to count, in nurseries that are also shrines that are also parts of the water-recycling system. Tools are hand-shaped from sinter and bone—whose bone, no one says. Simple metal, yes, worked by hand, but the metal is *grown* first, cultivated in slow baths, harvested like a crop. Nothing here is mined. Everything is *grown*. The Cistern is a body. The Hollow Choir are its antibodies, its maintenance, its vague immune response to something.

Erosion is zero because the Cistern *prevents* it. Every surface is either actively growing, actively precipitating, or actively being tended. Nothing wears down. Nothing wears *out*. The oldest parts of the structure are indistinguishable from the newest because the newest are made from the oldest, broken down, fed back through the cycle. A closed loop. A wet, hot, dim, loud, packed, *new* world that has been running long enough to forget it was ever anything else.

You cannot leave through the same mouth you entered. The Cistern admits. It does not release. Those who stay long enough begin to *condense*—not die, not exactly. Their edges blur. They become part of the humidity, the packedness, the slow wet architecture of the place. The Hollow Choir do not fear this. They *orchestrate* it. They are the last note in a chord that is still being played, still being *built*, still coming into being in a pocket of extreme elevation where the world is too young to have scars and too old to remember being anything but this.

The dripping does not stop. It has never stopped. In the dimness, in the press of wet air, in the sound that is almost music and almost memory, the Cistern dreams itself into being, again and again, a single room, a hidden place, a total construction of water and want and the patient, wrong-jointed care of its rare, remembering inhabitants.

You are in it now. The mouth has closed. The dripping is your heartbeat, or your heartbeat is the dripping. The distinction, here, is a matter of *scale* you have already lost.
