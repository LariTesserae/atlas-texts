## The Hollow Throat

You do not arrive here so much as *enter through* it. The scale parameter insists on this: this is not a landscape you survey. It is a **pocket**, a folded interior, a space that has grown *around* itself like a shell secreted in darkness and then inverted. There is no horizon. There is only the next wall, the next aperture, the next chamber of slightly different temperature.

The air is **dry to the point of chemistry**. Water exists here only as memory — in the crystalline structure of certain minerals, in the hollow reeds of the great plants, in the faint humidity of your own breath, which the walls drink and do not return. There are no rivers. There are no ponds. The "lush" vegetation does not grow *in* water but *against* its absence, having evolved to **mine moisture from air and stone**, to condense it from the thermal differentials between sun-baked surface and deep cool interior. These plants are **tubular, columnar, stacked** — they rise in tiered arrays like organ pipes, like coral, like the ribcages of something vast and buried. Their surfaces are waxy, pale, almost luminous. They do not sway in wind; they **resonate**.

And here the sound arrives.

The deafening is **not violence** but *accumulation*. Every surface in this place is **tuned**. The primitive technology — hand-worked, pre-metal, built from fired clay, from compressed mineral fiber, from the calcified wood of the great plants — has been shaped over generations not for silence but for **amplification**. The builders, who are gone or who have become something else, understood that in a waterless world, sound carries farther, stranger, more *persistently* than in wet air. They built **throats**. They built **resonating chambers** the size of rooms, of corridors, of entire nested volumes within volumes.

What you hear is **light itself**, or something adjacent to it. The bright parameter — 2.6, nearly maximal — means this place is *saturated* with radiation. The sky, if there is a sky visible through any aperture, is not blue but **white-yellow**, a glare that has no shadow's patience. This light strikes the mineral surfaces, the pale plant-skins, the engineered geometries of the built environment, and **sets them singing**. Thermal expansion. Piezoelectric response in certain crystals. The slow, daily swelling and cooling of massive walls that have been carved with grooves, with channels, with *intentional* irregularities. The vegetation, too, participates: their tubular structures drink sunlight and **exhale it as tone**, a photosynthesis of pressure waves, a metabolic process that produces not oxygen but **frequency**.

The overall effect is a **low, complex drone** — not one note but thousands, phase-shifted, beating against each other, occasionally resolving into something almost harmonic, almost *intentional*, before dissolving again. It is not music. It is not noise. It is **the sound of a place thinking**, or of a place *being thought by* the physics of its own materials.

### The Vegetation: Lush in the Manner of Stone

These plants are **warm** — the temperature parameter (2.2) is maintained not only by the external light but by the plants themselves, which are **thermogenic**, which generate heat as part of their strange photosynthesis. They create **microclimates**. In their densest stands, the air is nearly *tropical*, humid with their own respired moisture, thick with the scent of their waxy surfaces — a smell like hot stone and something faintly sweet, like resin, like the inside of a throat.

They are **lush** in the sense that they are **abundant, persistent, unstoppable**, but their lushness is **vertical and contained**. They do not spread across a plain; they **fill a volume**, rising in tiers, creating a **three-dimensional garden** within the three-dimensional architecture. You can climb through them.
