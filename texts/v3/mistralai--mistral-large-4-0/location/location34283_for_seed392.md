## The Condensation Gardens of Veth-Mar

You arrive not by path but by *unbecoming aware* of having arrived. The ground beneath your feet is not ground. It is a membrane — pale, slightly warm, faintly translucent — stretched over something that breathes. You feel this breathing as a slow rise and fall, two meters per cycle, somewhere beneath the thirty centimeters of cultivated muck you stand upon.

The water is *everywhere but never falls*. At 1.9, it has learned to exist in suspension, in sheets, in the architecture of wetness itself. The sky does not rain. Instead, the sky *weeps* — not droplets but films, curtains of moisture that descend from invisible heights, sliding down surfaces that have grown to receive them. Every built surface is a water-receiving surface. The structures do not shed water; they *conduct* it, channeling it through grooves and capillaries into vast reservoir bellies beneath the city, where it waits, patient, to be lifted again by the slow pulse of the deep pumps.

You are in a place that was built *yesterday* in geological terms — the erosion parameter at 0.7 means these sharp concrete edges, these crisp angles of ferrocrete and woven reed-mat, have not yet softened. Nothing here is rounded by time. The construction is fresh, the surfaces still smell of the fires that baked the kiln-bricks. Yet the *dynamic* parameter holds at 0.4: nothing changes. The freshness is eternal. A building half-finished stands half-finished forever, its rebar exposed to the condensing air, neither rusting nor not-rusting. The wetness preserves and destroys in equal measure, and the balance never tips.

### The Terrain

Elevation 1.0 — you are barely above the water table, and in many places *below* it. The city is a raft-city, but the rafts have grown together, accreted, until they form a continuous plane punctuated by towers and depressions. The "ground" is a constructed wetland, a deliberate marsh built into the architecture. Water stands in shallow pools between districts, black and reflective, reflecting not the sky but the undersides of structures — the city sees itself from below, inverted, in every puddle.

The material vocabulary is strange and limited. **Ferrocrete** — a volcanic ash and fiber composite that sets harder than stone, poured into forms made of split bamboo. **Kiln-brick** — fired from the clay of the reservoir beds, porous, drinking the atmospheric moisture, growing slightly darker each decade (though decades here are theoretical). **Reed-mat** — woven from the *Phragmites thermalis*, a grass that grows in the shallows, harvested by hand, treated with render made from boiled tree-resins. **Bone** — yes, bone, from the abundant fauna, carved and lashed, used for joints and fastenings where flexibility matters.

There is no metal beyond **copper**, worked cold, hammered into sheets for roofing and waterproofing. No iron. The technology is primitive not from poverty but from *choice* — or from the peculiar chemistry of this place, where iron objects left in the open air do not rust nor remain unchanged but *transform*, becoming something else, something that crumbles to red dust in exactly seven years. The people have learned not to fight this. They build with what endures: the ferrocrete, the fired clay, the woven grass, the patient copper.

### The Structures

Built at 3.0 — everything is made. There is no natural substrate anywhere. Even the soil is *compost*, manufactured in the great turning-districts where the city's wastes are processed by human labor and the digestive assistance of domesticated fauna. The "wild" vegetation grows only where it is *planted*, in the deliberate gaps between structures, in the water-channels, on the rooves that have been seeded with intention.

The architecture is **hydraulic-organic**. Buildings rise as inverted bells, their curved surfaces shedding the condensing moisture into gutters, their bases submerged in the perpetual shallow flood. Lower levels are open, colonnaded, allowing water to flow through — the city has no streets so much as *canals at ground level*, some navigable by the flat-bottomed boats that serve as transport. Upper levels connect by bridges of woven reed and lashed timber, arcing between towers, their undersides dripping constantly, so that crossing the city means accepting wetness, a slow soaking that the treated fabrics of clothing resist but never fully prevent.

The scale at 1.5 gives you room to see across it. From any tower, you can watch weather move across the whole condensation plain — though "weather" is the wrong word, for the atmosphere is *engineered*, maintained by the great **Mist Engines** that stand at the city's edges, vast structures of copper coil and reservoir, drawing water from the deep and releasing it as vapor, keeping the humidity at the precise level where the ferrocrete cures properly, where the reeds grow straight, where the fauna thrives. The city is a *climate*, a deliberate atmosphere held in being by constant human attention.

### The Atmosphere & Light

Light at 3.0 — but this is not the light of clear sun. The sky is a permanent luminous haze, the condensing atmosphere scattering brightness from all directions. Shadows exist but are soft, blue, diffuse. The sun, when it appears (and its appearance is *scheduled*, released by the Mist Engine operators through adjustable vents in the dome that covers the city — yes, the city is domed, or half-domed, a vast skin of treated reed-mat and copper sheeting that holds the climate), the sun appears as a bright disc that moves slowly, predictably, never setting fully but dimming to a glow that the city's own luminescence — fungal, cultivated, harvested from the deep reservoirs — replaces.

The air is *thick*. You feel it in your lungs, a warm wetness that makes exertion difficult, that slows movement, that encourages the sparse density of settlement — why crowd when space is abundant, when the wet air carries sound poorly, when privacy is the natural condition?

And the **weirdness** — 2.6, reality breakdown. It lives in the water. The condensation does not behave *correctly*. Droplets form on surfaces and then *rise*, defying gravity, climbing upward along the ferrocrete until they reach the gutters. The city's engineers do not understand this. They have built their systems to accommodate it, to *use* it, but the phenomenon itself remains unexplained — a local physics, a wound in the world where the usual rules have been *negotiated* rather than broken. Water remembers things. The oldest residents speak of dreams that come from the reservoirs, shared dreams, enacted in the waking hours of the citizens who drink the treated water, who bathe in it, who live immersed in its memory.

There are places where the membrane-ground grows thin, where you can see *down* into the reservoir depths, and what moves there is not only the engineered pumps and the domesticated fish but *shapes* that have no names, that the primitive technology cannot classify, that the abundant fauna — the 2.2, the teeming life of this wet place — avoids or worships or simply *coexists with* in the complex ecology of mutual tolerance.

### Ecology & Fauna

Life here is **aquatic-terrestrial**, adapted to the permanent humidity, the warm stillness, the absence of seasonal change. The dynamic-eternal quality means no evolution, no adaptation *to change* — only adaptation to the *steady state*. Species have had infinite time to specialize, to become perfectly fitted to this unchanging world, and so they are strange, baroque, excessive in their specialization.

**The reed-eels** — serpentine, limbless, breathing through gills and skin, living in the channels, harvested for their oil, their meat, their translucent bones that are carved into the *oracles*, the decision-tools of the city's councils. They are abundant. They are *everywhere* in the water.

**The mist-birds** — large, flightless, with wings that have become fans, fanning the air, creating local currents, their calls the primary sound of the waking city — a low, resonant *thrumming* that the quiet landscape (sound at 0.6) carries poorly, so that you hear them only when near, a privacy of sound in a visually open world.

**The membrane-grazers** — the domesticated fauna, descended from something like capybaras or water-hogs, now bred for their milk, their meat, their *attention* — they are kept on the lower levels, in the flood-zones, where they wade through the channels, their presence a constant reminder of the city's dependence on the wet, the warm, the *alive*.

And then the **others** — the fauna that is not domesticated, not harvested, simply *present* in the abundance that 2.2 implies. The **condensation-frogs**, which do not croak but *hum*, resonating with the city's own frequencies, their skins used for waterproofing, their bodies for food, their presence so ubiquitous that to walk the lower levels is to walk among them, to be *perceived* by thousands of eyes that reflect the luminous haze. The **reed-serpents**, venomous, avoided, their territories marked by the absence of human construction — the built environment has its *excluded zones*, places where the membrane-ground is allowed to grow wild, where the water stands deeper, where the weirdness concentrates.

The vegetation at 2.5 — lush, but *cultivated* lushness. The **thermal reeds** grow in dense stands, harvested, processed, the source of fiber and fuel and form. The **mist-orchids**, flowering constantly in the humid air, their blooms harvested for scent, for medicine, for the *drugs* that allow the citizens to dream the shared dreams of the reservoirs. The **floating gardens**, rafts of vegetation that drift in the reservoirs themselves, tended by the deep-dwellers, the specialists who live on the water, who never touch the membrane-ground, who are *other* in a city that is otherwise so homogeneous.

### Rules of the World

Here is what is true in Veth-Mar, what the inhabitants know without questioning, what the strange physics enforces:

**Water is time.** The reservoirs hold the past. To drink is to remember. The shared dreams are not madness but *communication* with what has been, with the city's own accumulated intention, its thousand years of human attention compressed into the water that sustains it. The primitive technology is not primitive in the sense of lacking — it is *appropriate* to a world where memory is liquid, where the past is *accessible* through the proper preparation and consumption.

**Nothing finishes.** The erosion-sharp edges remain sharp. The half-built tower remains half-built. This is not decay but *potential*, the city perpetually becoming, never arrived. The dynamic-eternal quality means that change is *horizontal*, not vertical — things move across the surface, water circulates, people migrate between districts, but the fundamental state does not progress. There is no history, only *weather*.

**The membrane is alive.** The ground breathes. It is not a metaphor. The city is built on something that respires, that has a metabolism, that perhaps *dreams* in its slow cycles. The inhabitants maintain it, feed it, harvest from it, but they do not control it. They are *tenants* of a living substrate, and the rent is paid in attention, in care, in the constant labor of the primitive tools that keep the constructed world from being reabsorbed.

**The fauna knows.** The abundant life of this place is not *below* humanity but *alongside* it, in a relationship of mutual observation. The reed-eels carry messages. The mist-birds mark time. The condensation-frogs *witness*. To harm them is not a crime but a *category error*, a confusion of kinds, and the city's social order — its councils, its oracles, its slow deliberative processes — exists to maintain the proper *boundaries* between the built and the grown, the human and the other.

**Reality is negotiable.** The weirdness at 2.6 means that the world has *edges* where the rules thin. Water rises. Dreams are shared. The membrane breathes. These are not secrets but *features*, openings in the fabric of the possible that the citizens have learned to use, to navigate, to *inhabit* without fully understanding. The primitive technology is a technology of *accommodation*, of working with these openings rather than closing them, of building a civilization that is itself *porous*, permeable to the strange physics of its environment.

### What You Do Here

You walk the channels, your footsteps absorbed by the membrane-ground, the wet air pressing against your skin. You climb the towers — and they are *tall*, ferrocrete and reed-mat rising into the luminous haze, the Mist Engines visible in the distance, their copper coils glinting, their vents releasing the scheduled bursts of vapor that maintain the climate.
