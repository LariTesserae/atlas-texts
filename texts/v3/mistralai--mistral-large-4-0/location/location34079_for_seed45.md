*The Throat of a Dying Star*

---

You stand on ground that is still deciding what it wants to be.

The elevation presses down and up at once—2.9 on some scale of meaning, though here the sensation is of *thickness* rather than height. The air is warm, 2.3, a heat that comes from below and from everywhere, the thermal memory of formation still radiating upward through stone that has not finished cooling into itself. You breathe it in and it tastes of copper and the particular silence of things too hot to have water in them. No water. 0.1. Not a drop, not a memory of a drop, not even the molecular ghost of H₂O clinging to mineral surfaces. The chemistry here runs on other hungers—sulfur and silicon and the slow hunger of crystal for crystal.

The terrain is *emerging*. Erosion at 0.1, still in its first gesture toward becoming anything. You walk on surfaces that have not yet learned the difference between wall and floor, between here and there. The ground rises in folds that look almost deliberate, almost architectural, but they are too raw for architecture—these are the contractions of a body still being born, the muscle spasms of geology in its first minutes. Glass in places, volcanic glass, black and sharp and singing faintly in the heat, though why it sings and what it sings you cannot say. It sings the way a struck bell sings, if a bell were made of frozen lightning and regret.

Scale opens around you. 2.2. The folds repeat and vary across a distance that your eyes cannot close, that your mind keeps trying to measure and failing. This is not a valley. Valleys imply water, imply time enough for water to have been. This is a *throat*—the narrow passage between formations that are still moving, still arguing about their final shapes. You can see three ridges ahead, each one warm-lit from within, each one casting shadows that fall in directions that do not agree with each other. The light is wrong. 0.4, darkness, but a darkness that is *inhabited*. Something phosphoresces in the deep folds. Something that is not light, exactly, but the *possibility* of light, the way a held breath is the possibility of speech.

There is no plant. 0.5. But there is *something* that occupies the niche of plant, the slow colonizer, the patient converter of mineral into structure. Fungal, perhaps, or simpler—*pre-life*, the chemistry that rehearses biology without committing to it. It grows in the warmest seams, the places where heat pools against glass that has not yet decided to be obsidian or to be something else. It is the color of old bruises, of deep water, of the moment before dawn when the sky cannot decide. It spreads in patterns that look like writing but resolve, on closer inspection, into nothing so legible. Maps of a territory that does not exist. Routes for journeys that will not be taken.

Sound: mostly quiet. 1.2. But the quiet has *texture*. The glass sings, very faintly, a harmonic series that your teeth feel more than your ears. The warm air moves in slow currents that make no sound at all, that you perceive only as pressure changes on your skin, as the subtle wrongness of a room where the temperature is not uniform. And beneath everything, a low vibration, almost below hearing, the *growth* of the stone, the slow rearrangement of crystal lattice, the sound of mountains being born at a rate too slow for any single listener to witness but fast enough to feel in the bones if you lie down and press your ear to the glass.

There are marks. Built, but only at 1.2—scattered, partial, *faint*. You find them without looking for them, which is how you know they are not for you. A depression in a ridge that is too regular to be natural, too eroded to be recent. A line of darker glass, fused differently, running for twenty meters before it simply *stops*, as if whatever made it was interrupted. Small structures, or the memory of small structures—hutments of fused mineral, windowless, doorless, their purpose lost in the same emergence that is still reshaping the ground around them. They are not ruins. Ruins imply a past that is finished. These are *interruptions*, things that began and were not continued, or things that are still beginning and have not yet learned how to be finished.

There is no technology here. 0.2. But there is *intention*, or the shadow of intention. The marks are too regular for accident. The glass in places is *too* glass, too clear, too free of the bubbles and fractures that violence leaves. Someone—something—has been *watching* this place form. Tending it. Or waiting for it to form enough to be tended.

And the turbulence. 2.8. This is the heart of it, the reason the place feels alive without being alive, animated without being inhabited. The folds *move*. Not fast. Not on any scale you could measure while standing still. But if you return to a ridge you have seen before—if you can find it again, in a landscape that refuses to hold still long enough to be mapped—you will find it *changed*. A new fissure, glowing from within. A glass flow that yesterday was a wall, now a floor. The heat pulses in waves, 2.3 degrees of warmth becoming 2.4, becoming 2.2, a respiration of geology, the slow breathing of something that is still *deciding* whether to be a mountain or a plain or a throat through which something else will one day pass.

The ecology is *mineral*. There is no fauna, 0.2, no animal, no insect, no the simplest crawling thing that might be called a body with wants. But there is *hunger*. The crystal grows at the expense of crystal. The glass devitrifies, slowly, into something harder, something that will outlast the glass by eons. The fungal-pre-life spreads into new seams as they open, dies back when the heat shifts, begins again where the conditions suit it. It is not alive, not in any way you would recognize. But it is *responsive*. It is *becoming*. It is doing the work that life does, without the machinery of life, in a world that has not yet committed to the aqueous chemistry that your kind of biology requires.

The weirdness sits at 0.6—unusual, but not *hostile* to strangeness. It is the weirdness of *process*, of a world caught in the act of becoming itself. You could walk here for days and never see the same thing twice. You could walk here for days and see nothing *happen*. Both would be true. The change is too slow for impatience, too constant for boredom. It is the pace of thought, of dream, of the moment when you are falling asleep and the room is still the room but also *almost* not the room, the walls breathing with you, the floor holding you with a gentleness that is almost weightlessness.

The darkness is not empty. It holds the glow of cooling stone, the phosphorescence of the fungal-pre-life, the deep warm light that comes from *below*, from the 2.3 that is also the 2.9, the heat that is also the height, the pressure that is also the elevation. You are high up and low down at once, in a throat that connects the world to itself, that has not yet decided whether it is a passage or a destination.

And somewhere, in the folds you cannot see, something is *forming*. A structure that is not a structure. A sound that is not a sound. A shape that the glass is singing toward, that the heat is pushing toward, that the slow patient chemistry of this place is *becoming*.

You wait. The waiting is not boredom. The waiting is *attention*. The world is still under construction. Erosion has only just begun. And you are here, in the 0.1 water, the 0.5 vegetation, the 2.3 warmth, the 2.9 elevation, the emerging, the open, the sparse, the scattered, the none, the darkness, the mostly-quiet, the turbulent—

—here, in the throat of a dying star that has not yet decided whether to be a mountain, a plain, or a door.

*The glass sings. You do not answer. You are still learning the language.*
