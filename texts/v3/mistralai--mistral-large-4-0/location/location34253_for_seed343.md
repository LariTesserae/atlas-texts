This is the **High Choir** — a continental plateau that has been growing upward for so long that it has forgotten it was ever ground. Elevation 2.8, erosion 2.8: the two forces have reached their ancient stalemate. The plateau is near-flattened now, a tableland the size of a small country, its edges so worn by eons that they slope away into foothills over distances measured in days of walking. What elevation remains is *presence* rather than height — you feel it in the thinness that doesn't quite thin, in the way sound behaves strangely, in the cold that never quite arrives despite the altitude. Temperature 1.5: warm. The plateau holds heat like a held breath. Days are mild, nights are mild, and the air carries a perpetual damp warmth — water 1.2, expressed not as rain but as *condensation memory*. Moisture gathers in the vast shallows, in the countless sunken places where the ancient surface has sagged, and evaporates before it can become weather. The damp is everywhere and nowhere. You wake to surfaces beaded, to the smell of wet stone that is almost stone, to the particular silence of water held in suspension.

But the plateau is not stone. That is the first thing you must understand.

Vegetation 2.6: **total**. The High Choir is a single organism pretending to be geography. What began as lichen, then moss, then some desperate pioneer that could root in dust, has become *substrate itself*. The plant mass is kilometers thick in places. It has grown over its own dead, compressed and re-compressed, until the "ground" you walk on is not rock but something like peat older than coal, older than the concept of coal. The surface is a sponge of living and once-living matter, firm enough to bear weight but with the wrong kind of give — you sink slightly with each step, and the surface closes behind you with a sound like a mouth. Density 0.5: **empty**. This is the paradox at the heart of the High Choir. The plant mass is continuous, it fills everything, yet within it are vast hollow spaces — chambers, galleries, the preserved interiors of collapsed mega-structures from the ecosystem's middle age, now grown around and into. You can walk for hours through corridors of compressed cellulose, through cathedral spaces where the walls breathe with slow gas exchange, through chambers where water collects in perfect stillness and nothing has disturbed it for millennia. The emptiness is *inside* the fullness. Scale 2.5: **open**. The plateau extends past any single view. You can see for kilometers in some directions, across a surface that undulates with the slow swells of underlying structure, broken only by the occasional emergence — a tower of harder material that the plant mass could not consume, a spine of something older than itself poking through like a rib through skin.

And the fauna. Fauna 2.9: **overwhelming**. They are everywhere. They are *of* the plant mass, *in* it, *made* from it in ways that suggest the boundary was never real. The High Choir has no insects as you would recognize them — it has *motile extensions* of the substrate, ambulatory pseudopods of chitin and cellulose and something else, something that might be nervous tissue distributed through the plant mass and given temporary coherence. They range from the size of your thumb to the size of a dog. They are herbivores, detritivores, filter-feeders, but also predators of one another in complex cascades that the plant mass seems to *encourage*, to cultivate. The sound — sound 2.7: **deafening** — comes from them. Not from any single creature but from the aggregate: the skittering of ten thousand small legs on the cellulose surface, the resonant booming of larger organisms moving through the hollow galleries below, the vast susurrus of the plant mass itself exchanging gases, growing, *dreaming* in whatever way a distributed nervous system might dream. You cannot hear yourself think. You cannot hear another person speak unless you press your mouth to their ear. The sound has a rhythm to it, almost a pattern, and after days you begin to suspect it is *meaning* something, that the High Choir is *singing*.

Weirdness 1.0: **unusual**. The High Choir is strange but not *weird* in the sense of inexplicable. It follows rules. The rules are simply not the rules you brought. Water moves upward through the plant mass against gravity, drawn by capillary action scaled to kilometers, by osmotic gradients that span continents. The "rain" that occasionally falls is not rain — it is the plant mass *sweating*, releasing moisture it has drawn from deep reservoirs, from the water table that exists somewhere below the kilometers of compressed organic matter, where actual rock still remembers being rock. Built 1.4: **scattered**. There are structures, but they are not built *on* the Choir — they are built *by* it, or grown by it, or perhaps they are simply *excretions*, the way a coral reef builds its architecture from the accumulated labor of countless small organisms. Tech 1.3: **primitive**. The things that live here do not build with tools. They build with *time* and *body* and *patience*. There are bridges of woven root-mass spanning the larger hollows. There are chambers lined with something like nacre, secreted over centuries. There are places where the plant mass has grown into shapes that serve no purpose you can identify — and yet they are *used*, by the fauna, by the processes of the place, in ways that suggest intention without quite achieving it.

Light 2.2: **normal**. The sun rises and sets. The sky is the sky. But the light that reaches the surface is *filtered*, *conditioned* by kilometers of atmosphere that is slightly different from yours — slightly more moisture, slightly more organic particulate, slightly more of the volatile compounds that the plant mass releases in its endless metabolism. The light is warm. It makes everything look slightly golden, slightly *remembered*. Shadows are soft, diffuse, as if the sun is simultaneously everywhere and nowhere.

Dynamic 1.8: **active**. The High Choir is not static. It is *process*. The surface grows — slowly, a millimeter a year in some places, faster in the hollows where water collects and life concentrates. It subsides, as the lower layers continue their slow transformation into something denser, something that might one day become rock again, might become the next plateau, the next Choir, in a cycle that has repeated perhaps a dozen times across the history of this world. The fauna move through it in vast migrations that follow the moisture, the warmth, the chemical signals that ripple through the plant nervous system like weather. Predator-prey cascades play out across square kilometers. The sound changes by hour, by season, by the slow shifts of the great growing things that dominate the ecology — the *architects*, the mega-organisms that are perhaps individual, perhaps colonial, perhaps something in between, that rise above the general surface like islands, that seed the hollows with their spores, that *dream* the dreams that become the patterns you almost hear in the noise.

And you. You are here. The High Choir does not notice you. It is too large, too distributed, too *patient*. But it *uses* you. Your body heat is a signal in the thermal landscape. Your footsteps are seismic events, minor but real. Your breath is carbon dioxide, a gift. You will leave tracks that the surface will close over, that will persist as faint disturbances in the layering, that might be read by something, in a thousand years, as *weather*, as *event*, as *story*.

The damp gathers on your skin. The sound presses against your ears until you hear your own heartbeat as a kind of silence. Somewhere below, water moves through channels that have not changed in ten thousand years. Somewhere above, something vast shifts its weight and the surface gives, just slightly, under your foot.

The High Choir breathes. You are breathing with it. You are, for now, part of the total.
