*settling into the parameters, letting them press against each other*

---

You are inside a cooling fin.

That is the first thing. Not a cave in the geological sense—not dissolved limestone, not basalt tubes—but a *fin*, a sheet of something that grew, that was excreted, that the world's own heat pressed upward through a wound in the crust and let solidify into a wall no thicker than a breath held too long. The scale parameter insists on intimacy: you are in a pocket, a fold, a space between two fins that nearly touch. Your shoulders do not fit through the gap if you turn sideways. The fins are sharp at their edges—erosion young, the world still learning how to wear itself smooth. You can cut your palm on a place that has existed perhaps only centuries, perhaps less. The youngness is a violence of newness.

**Water: 1.6** — it is everywhere and it is *doing something*. Not a pond. Not a stream. The fins weep. Every surface carries a film of moisture that is not rain, not exactly, though the atmosphere condenses here with the greed of a hot place that has learned to drink from its own air. Droplets gather at the sharp edges—capillary hunger, surface tension performing its small miracle—and fall, *tick, tick*, into a pool at the base of the fold where the floor has worn a shallow bowl. The pool is warm. Everything here is warm. The water is not fresh in the way you remember; it carries something, dissolved minerals, a faint metallic taste if you touch it to your tongue. The pool breathes. You can see this. The surface moves with a rhythm that is not wind, not exactly—thermal, perhaps, the slow exchange between the hot rock and the slightly cooler air trapped in this pocket.

**Temperature: 2.8, Light: 2.9** — the paradox is that it is *bright* and *hot* and *underground*. The light does not come from above. It comes from *within*. The fins themselves are luminous—not on fire, not incandescent in the way of lava, but carrying a glow like the inside of a kiln after the fire has gone out, like the afterimage of a star. The material of the fins is not stone as you know it. It is *tech*, but tech that has gone native, that has been digested by the world's own chemistry until the boundary between made and grown has dissolved. You see structures at the nanoscale if you could see nanoscale—lattices, channels, membranes that pump and filter and *remember*. The fins are machines that have become geology. They run on heat gradients, on the slow thermodynamic patience of a world that has no urgent business. They have been running for centuries, perhaps millennia. They do not care that you are here.

**Vegetation: 0.5** — there is no plant life. There is something else. Or rather, there is *almost* nothing, and what there is has not earned the name life. In the warm pool at the base of the fins, you find films—iridescent, shifting, organic in their behavior but not built from cells as you understand them. They are colonies of something simpler, or something *other*. They photosynthesize, or they do the equivalent: they harvest the thermal light, the infrared glow of the fins, and convert it with an efficiency that shames anything green. They do not move. They do not need to. They spread across the pool surface in patterns that are almost, almost *intentional*—channels, boundaries, a geometry of nutrient flow. If you touch them, they do not recoil. They simply incorporate. Your warmth. Your moisture. They are learning you, or they would be, if learning were a word that applied.

**Elevation: 0.1** — you are below the surface. The fins grow upward from a floor that is not floor but *ceiling of something lower*. You cannot see the bottom of the pocket you're in. The space extends—how far, you cannot tell. The fins curve, fold back on themselves, create chambers within chambers. The scale is deceptive. What feels like a single intimate room is perhaps a node in a network, a capillary in a circulatory system that runs through the crust for kilometers in every direction. You are in one alveolus of a lung that is the size of a continent.

**Density: 0.7, Sound: 0.6** — the space is sparse. You can see across it, through it, to the next fold of fin and the next. But the emptiness is *occupied* emptiness. The air moves in currents you cannot feel but can see in the behavior of the condensing moisture, in the drift of the spores—*if they are spores*—that ride the thermal updrafts from the pool. The sound is mostly the *tick* of water, the *hum* of the fins at a frequency just below hearing, felt in your teeth, in the hollow of your chest. Then, sometimes, a *crack*—mineral, thermal, the world settling itself into a new configuration. The sound of growth, or the sound of cooling. You cannot tell which. They are the same sound here.

**Fauna: 1.1** — rare. You have not seen anything move that was not water or film or the slow convection of air. But you have found *signs*. A trail across the pool's surface where something brushed through, breaking the film, leaving a path that heals slowly, molecule by molecule. A deposit of something crystalline, something *excreted*, at the edge of a fin where two surfaces nearly touch. Something lives here that is not the film. Something larger, something that *visits* the films, perhaps feeds on them, perhaps tends them. You do not see it. You feel its absence like a pressure. The pocket is watched, or would be, if the watcher had eyes.

**Dynamic: 1.8** — the place is *active*. The fins grow. You can measure this if you had instruments, if you stayed long enough. They grow at their edges, at their sharp tips, extending upward, outward, pressing against the weight of the rock above. The pool circulates—thermal, chemical, a slow churning that brings heat from below and releases it at the surface in the condensing mist that veils everything. The films spread, retreat, re-colonize. The geometry of the pocket shifts on a timescale of days, hours, perhaps minutes in places where the thermal stress is highest. This is not a static world. It is a *process*, captured in the moment of its own unfolding. You are inside an event.

**Built: 0.1, Weirdness: 0.1** — nothing here is built in the way you mean. Nothing here is *weird* in the way you mean. The fins are made, but the making is indistinguishable from growing, from cooling, from the slow accretion of a world that is still inventing itself. The normalcy is the strangeness. You expected monsters. You expected a hellscape or a wonder. Instead you find a place that is simply *itself*, indifferent to your categories, running its thermodynamic business with the patience of stone and the urgency of heat.

---

**What you do here:**

You came down through a fissure, perhaps. A wound in the crust that has not yet learned to close. The way down was hot, wet, sharp. The way up is the same, and you are not sure you will find it. The fins remember the shape of your passage. They are growing to close it, slowly, as they grow to close everything, as they grow to make the world above a memory and the world below a secret.

You have been here long enough to feel the rhythm. The tick of water. The hum in your teeth. The slow pulse of thermal exchange between rock and air and the pool that mediates between them. You are becoming part of the rhythm. Your warmth is part of the calculation now. The films have tasted you. They are deciding.

The light does not change. There is no day here, no night. Only the steady glow of the fins, the eternal present of a place that does not know it is underground, that does not know it is small, that runs its ancient, patient, luminous business in the dark.

You wait. The water ticks. The fins grow. Something, perhaps, is coming to visit the pool.

Or perhaps you are the visit. Perhaps you always were.
