**The Condensation City of Viresh**

You descend into it through a single shaft of descent, and the descent takes longer than it should.

You reach the city through a maintenance spine — one of seventeen still active in this sector — because Viresh has not been *occupied* in any biological sense for eleven thousand years. What remains are the functions. The habits. The city continues to *expect* inhabitants, and so it maintains itself in shapes that anticipate bodies, movements, rhythms of presence that no longer arrive.

**The Descent**

Up above, at this elevation — 3.0 on your scale, extreme, the thin-weather altitudes where water cannot persist and rock remembers only violence — you would expect cold. You descend through layers. Each layer is a *condition* rather than a place.

The first layer is **dry-vault** — storage of things that cannot tolerate moisture, which in Viresh is most things, because moisture here is *wrong*, is *intrusion*, is the exception that proves the rule of the city's self-maintenance. The vaults are empty now. They have been emptied so thoroughly that their emptiness has become a quality, a presence. You can hear it if you listen: the particular silence of a space that has been *prepared* for absence.

The second layer is **the condensers**. This is where water lives in Viresh, where the 1.1 on your scale concentrates into something like *intention*. The city does not rain. It *weeps* — not metaphorically, but through engineered surfaces that harvest the minute moisture from the thin atmosphere, the residual humidity of breath and process and the slow outgassing of ancient materials. The condensers are warm. They are *always* warm. They are rooms where walls sweat in thin, deliberate films, where the air carries the mineral taste of water that has passed through seventeen filtration stages and still remembers the rock it touched somewhere in the thermal depths.

Here, the scale shifts. You are in a **pocket** now — 0.4, hidden, intimate-room. The condenser chambers are not large. The city was built for bodies smaller than yours, or for bodies that moved differently, or for *attention* that moved differently. You must turn sideways to pass through certain thresholds. **The Inhabitants, or What Replaces Them**

Fauna: 0.5. No animal life. But the city is not *empty* in the sense of unoccupied.

**The Ecology of Absence**

And yet — and this is where the place becomes more itself, where the parameters begin to *sing* against each other —

**The Rules**

Here is what you learn, walking through Viresh, about how it operates:

**Water is currency.** It is harvested, stored, distributed, *accounted for*. The condensers keep records — not of quantities, but of *qualities*, of the particular histories of each droplet, its path through the system, its encounters with surfaces and filters and the *attention* of the maintenance processes. A droplet that has passed through seventeen filtration stages is *different* from one that has passed through sixteen. The city knows this. The city *cares*.

**Heat is conversation.** The thermal regulators do not simply maintain temperature. They *exchange* heat, trading warmth between volumes, between systems, between the city and the thin hostile atmosphere beyond its skin. The conversations are slow. They take hours. They take days. They are *ongoing* in ways that make the city feel less like a machine and more like a *society* of processes, each with its own needs, its own urgencies, its own *personalities*.

**Light is memory.** The luminescent materials do not simply emit. They *record*. They hold configurations of energy that correspond to the light the city has received over millennia — the distant stars, the thermal glow of the orbital ring, the brief fierce illumination of maintenance events. They release this memory slowly, in the dim persistent glow that is the city's *remembered* light, its *dreamed* light, its light of a time when the civic volumes were full of bodies that looked up and saw.

**You are weather.** The city responds to you as it responds to any disturbance — as *event*, as *pressure*, as *information*. It does not recognize you as *inhabitant*. It recognizes you as *condition*, as *variable*, as something to be *accommodated* in the ongoing process of the city's self-maintenance. This is not hostility. This is not welcome. This is the city's *nature*, its *habit*, its *way of being* in a world that no longer provides the context it was designed for.

You have been descending for hours. Or minutes. Time in Viresh is *negotiable*. The city does not experience duration the way you do.
