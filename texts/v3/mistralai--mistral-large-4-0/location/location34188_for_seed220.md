**The Hollow Meridian**

You descend. The mouth of entry is not marked—no arch, no threshold ritual—only a pressure change, a sudden coolness against skin already damp with the world's ambient humidity. The shaft is narrow, barely shoulder-width, cut through material that resists naming. It is not stone, exactly. It has the *memory* of stone, the weight-distribution of it, but when you press your palm against the wall it yields slightly, like very old bread, then firms again behind your touch. The builders—there were builders, once, or something that functioned as builders—worked in a medium that has not finished curing.

Water finds you here. 0.9 is not *wet*. It is *almost wet*, the way a held breath is almost exhaled. Condensation gathers on surfaces that should be impossible surfaces—inverted curves, pockets where the ceiling dips *below* the floor it overhangs. These pockets hold water. Small reservoirs no larger than a cupped hand, and they never spill, never evaporate fully, never quite empty. They sit at the low points of a geometry that keeps recalculating itself. Miners who worked these depths—there were miners, there are their descendants, or something wearing the shape of descendants—spoke of drinking from the same pocket for decades, finding it always half-full, always the exact temperature of the surrounding air, always tasting of copper and something else, something like the memory of rain.

The hollow extends. Scale 1.4 suggests a room, a cathedral, a single bounded feature. But density 0.2 empties it of consequence. You walk for hours—time moves strangely here, neither fast nor slow but *thick*—through corridors that branch and rejoin, through chambers that open onto other chambers without transition. The architecture is *urban*, 2.5, but the population has abandoned it, or been abandoned by it, or never existed in the sense of bodies needing shelter. You find infrastructure: channels that carry no water but *hum* with something moving through them, conduits that terminate in walls that later open to show the conduit continuing on the other side, staircases that ascend into ceilings and descend from floors. Layered construction, yes—generations of modification, of response to need, of *repair* applied so many times that the original purpose has become unreadable, like a palimpsest written in a language that keeps changing its alphabet.

The Hollow Meridian is warm. 2.4, tropical, feverish. But warmth here is not sunlight—light is 0.3, almost absent, a suggestion of glow at the edges of vision that might be bioluminescence or might be the persistence of retinal impression. The warmth comes from below, from the world's own metabolic processes, from the slow digestion of whatever the builders left behind. It is humid warmth, the kind that makes fabric cling and skin prickle with the awareness of pores opening. Occasional rain falls upward. This is not metaphor. Droplets rise from the condensation pockets, slow as falling snow, gathering into rivulets that flow along ceilings, pooling in inverted basins, eventually—hours later, days later—releasing to fall again in new configurations. The hydrology here is *conversational*. Water speaks to water across distances, negotiating its own paths through evaporation and condensation and the strange capillary action of the walls.

Sound is 2.4: loud. The Hollow Meridian *resonates*. Footsteps multiply into processions. A dropped stone becomes a minor percussion event, felt in the sternum before it is heard. The city's infrastructure—dead or dormant or *dreaming*—generates its own acoustic ecology. Conduits carry pressure differentials that produce tones, subsonic and audible, that shift with the thermal cycles of the deep earth. There are *machines* here, tech 2.6, operating on principles that predate and postdate the physics your surface world accepts. Nanoscale lattices that reconfigure in response to stress. Macro-scale structures that exploit geometries not available in three-dimensional space—you will walk in a straight line and find yourself returned to a point you passed twenty minutes ago, not because the corridor curved, but because the *space* was folded, briefly, by something that needed the connection. The machines do not serve human purposes. They serve the purposes of the Hollow itself, which are not hostile and not benevolent but *maintaining*: keeping the temperature stable, managing the water cycle, preserving the structural integrity of a place that has been eroding for so long that erosion itself has become a kind of equilibrium.

Erosion 2.4: established, settled, in-balance. The walls are weathered. You can see it in the way surfaces have softened their edges, in the patina of mineral accretion that records the passage of epochs in layers thin as breath. But the erosion is *complete* in the way that a river's course is complete—it has found its form, its state of maximum entropy for the local conditions, and now it simply *persists*. Nothing is being worn away that doesn't immediately begin rebuilding itself from the chemistry of the damp. The Hollow is old. The Hollow is *finished* being old. It has settled into a condition beyond the reach of time's ordinary arrows.

Vegetation 1.1: sparse. But present. Fungi, mostly—colonies that do not spread but *hold territory*, each patch maintaining precise boundaries against its neighbors, communicating through chemical signals you cannot perceive. They grow on the damp walls, in the condensation pockets, along the paths of upward rain. Some are luminescent, providing the 0.3 light, pulsing in rhythms that might be circadian or might be responses to the machines' operations. There are no plants with leaves, no photosynthesis, no green. The Hollow's ecology is *chemosynthetic*, drawing energy from the mineral metabolism of the walls, from the heat differential between deep earth and the cooler upper reaches you descended through. Rare as the vegetation is, it is *old* vegetation, lineages that have adapted to conditions that existed nowhere else, that continued evolving while the surface world cycled through its ages of ice and fire and ocean.

Fauna 1.4: rare. You will travel long stretches seeing nothing that moves with intention. Then you will find a creature—small, eyeless, skin like the walls, warm to the touch—pausing in a corridor as if listening to frequencies below your hearing. It will watch you with organs that are not eyes, and you will feel *assessed*, weighed against some criterion you cannot know. There are perhaps twelve species in the entire Hollow, or perhaps three, or perhaps one that has diversified and re-converged upon similar forms. They do not breed in any way you would recognize. They *accrete*, gathering material from the damp, from the condensation, from the slow dissolution of the walls, growing until they reach a threshold, then *dividing* into forms that bear no obvious relation to the parent. Predation exists but is *ceremonial*: the rare fauna hunt the fungi, but always leave spore-pockets intact, always take only what allows continuation. The ecology is in motion, dynamic 1.9, but the motion is *slow weather*, the pace of geological time made biological.

Weirdness 0.9: unusual, but not *alien*. The strangeness here is *domesticated*. You will adapt to the upward rain. You will learn to read the acoustic signatures of structural stress, to navigate by the temperature gradients that mark safe passage. The Hollow Meridian does not want to confuse you. It wants to *contain* you, to process you through its cycles, to add your moisture and your heat and your chemistry to its ongoing metabolism. The weirdness is the weirdness of a place that has been *inhabited* by intelligence for so long that the intelligence has become indistinguishable from the environment. The builders—if they were builders—did not leave. They *became* the infrastructure. Their technology is 2.6 because it is *grown*, not constructed, evolved through iterations of self-modification that blur the line between machine and organism, between tool and user, between the city and the citizens who once animated it.

You will find habitation, if you look. Not current habitation. *Evidence* of habitation, layered and fossilized: sleeping niches worn smooth by use, food-preparation surfaces (though what was prepared, what was eaten, remains unclear), communication panels that respond to touch with patterns of light that might be language, might be art, might be the equivalent of a diary entry left by someone who knew they would not be read for millennia. The built environment is 2.5, urban, but the population density was always low—this was never a city of millions, but a city of *thousands* spread across volumes that your surface intuition cannot encompass, connected by transit systems that moved at speeds requiring physics you don't have words for.

And the sound. The sound is *loud* because the Hollow is *listening*. The machines attend to acoustic signatures, using them to monitor structural integrity, to detect the presence of water, to track the slow migration of thermal fronts. Your presence generates sound. Your presence *matters* to the system, not as intrusion but as *data*, as a perturbation to be measured and incorporated. The loudness is not aggression. It is *attention*. The Hollow Meridian hears you. It has always been hearing. It will continue hearing after you are gone, after your moisture has joined its condensation pockets, after your warmth has dissipated into its thermal mass, after your chemistry has been processed through its endless cycles of accretion and dissolution.

You descend further, or you turn back. The choice is yours, though the Hollow has opinions. The upward rain gathers. The conduits hum their subsonic songs. Somewhere, a creature pauses in its chemical foraging to assess your passage. The walls yield slightly under your palm, then firm again, remembering, containing, *persisting*.

This is the Hollow Meridian. It is below surface. It is warm. It is almost wet. It is empty of you, and full of everything that came before, and patient in its waiting for whatever comes after.

The sound continues. The water moves. The city dreams its slow, mineral dreams, and you are—briefly, beautifully, inevitably—part of the dream.
