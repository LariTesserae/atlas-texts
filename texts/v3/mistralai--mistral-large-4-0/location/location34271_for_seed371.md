**The rules of this place**

1. *Nothing is stable.* The only constant is change, and the change is *fast*. What you see now will not be here in an hour. What was here an hour ago is gone. Memory is a liability. Adaptation is the only strategy.

2. *Heat and cold are in negotiation.* The vents provide warmth. The elevation, 2.6, brings thin air, rapid cooling, the chill of altitude. The interface between these is where life concentrates, in the *boundary zones*, the steam-mist lines, the pool edges, the mineral terraces. The center of any zone is less alive than its edge.

3. *Water is currency.* It falls, condenses, collects, flashes to steam, precipitates as minerals, pools, evaporates, returns. Every process in the caldera involves water in some phase. The lushness, the geology, the very shape of the place — all are *water stories*.

4. *Sound is information.* The vents have voices. A change in pitch means a change in pressure, in flow, in the system's mood. The loudness, 1.7, is not noise but *signal*, dense, layered, *readable* if you learn the language. The silence, when it comes — and it comes, briefly, when a vent chokes and the system holds its breath — is *meaningful*, a pause in a sentence that has not yet ended.

5. *Scale is a trap.* The caldera is small, 1.2, bounded, *knowable*. You can see all of it. But the processes within it are *cosmic* in their implications — the same processes that built the earth, compressed, accelerated, made visible. To stand here is to stand inside a microscope slide of planetary formation, the dark lens of geologic time focused to a point you can touch.

6. *There is no outside.* The caldera is a world. The rim is not an escape but a boundary, a membrane, the place where the system meets the *other* — the older rock, the settled landscape, the world that has finished its turbulence and grown quiet. To cross the rim is to step out of *becoming* into *being*. Most who come do not cross back. The caldera does not let go easily. Its humidity, its sound, its perpetual motion — these become *habit*, then *need*, then *home*.
