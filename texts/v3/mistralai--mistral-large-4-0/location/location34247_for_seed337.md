# The Drowned Workshops

## A Place Emerging From Stone

---

You descend. That is the first thing to understand about this place—it is *down*. The elevation parameter does not mean a valley or a hollow in any ordinary sense. The land has not yet finished deciding whether it is above or below. You walk through passages that were never dug, between chambers that were never hollowed, past walls that are still learning to be walls. The stone here is *young geology*. It remembers pressure. It remembers being seafloor, being compressed, being something else entirely. Now it is becoming architecture, and the becoming is incomplete, visible in every surface.

Water moves through this place at 1.0—not abundant, not absent. Damp. The stone sweats. In certain chambers, condensation gathers on surfaces that have no business being cold enough to weep, and drips with a patience that predates the people who built here. There are pools. Not many. They collect in depressions that the erosion has not yet smoothed away, in the embryonic stage of becoming floors or ceilings or something the current inhabitants do not yet have a word for. The water is not fresh exactly, nor is it salt. It carries mineral signatures from depths that the builders tapped and did not fully understand. It tastes of copper and something older, something that might be memory.

---

## The Packed Dark

Density at 2.8 means no air between things. This is a place where presences saturate space. You cannot walk through the main galleries without your shoulder brushing something—a hanging tool, a bundle of cured material, a person standing very still. The "streets" between structures are shoulder-width, sometimes less. In the central warrens, you move in single file or not at all. The packed quality extends to time as well. Events overlap. A construction project continues while a meal is prepared three meters away. A birth happens in a chamber where someone is also dying, and the dying person is also helping to mix mortar. Nothing has its own space. Everything leans on everything else.

Yet this density is not chaotic. It is *organized saturation*. The builders—call them the Hollowed, though they have many names, most of them unspoken aloud—have arranged their packed world with a logic that outsiders find maddening and residents find obvious. Every surface is used. Every pocket of non-stone contains something. The rules of this place are rules of *no waste*, *no empty*, *no pause without purpose*. A person standing still is either resting (allowed, for the duration of three breaths) or waiting for something specific (also allowed, but the waiting must be *active*—watching, listening, holding a tool ready).

---

## The Urban Below

Built at 1.8: this is settlement-scale. Not a city in any surface-world sense, but a town that happens to be underground, or perhaps *becoming* underground, or perhaps always was and the concept of "underground" is a surface prejudice. The Hollowed have constructed across a regional extent visible only to those who know the connecting passages. There are districts, though they do not have names so much as *qualities*. The Wet Quarter, where the condensation is heaviest and the stone is youngest. The Deep Stair, a single continuous descent that no one has yet reached the bottom of, though people have lived on its landings for generations. The Mending Yards, where broken things are brought to be un-broken, and where the un-breaking sometimes takes longer than a lifetime.

Infrastructure exists but is *grown* rather than built, or built in a way that imitates growth. Ventilation shafts follow the paths of ancient root systems, or perhaps the roots followed the shafts—causality here is negotiable. Lighting comes from the normal-light parameter (2.0) in strange ways: bioluminescence cultivated in specific chambers, reflective minerals embedded in plaster, the occasional shaft that admits actual sky-light at angles that make the packed darkness feel like a kind of weather, a pressure, a condition of being *inside* even when you are not.

---

## The Sparse Green

Vegetation at 0.6: there is almost none, and what there is has been *chosen* with ferocious deliberation. The Hollowed do not garden in any romantic sense. They cultivate specific organisms for specific functions. A pale lichen that grows only on north-facing (which is to say, certain mineral-facing) stone, used to mark passages. A tuber that fruits in total darkness, the staple of the diet, harvested by touch alone since the chambers where it grows are kept at the edge of visibility. Fungal colonies that process the mineral sweat of the stone into something drinkable, though the process takes months and the result is barely water, more a memory of water, more a promise.

There are no trees. There is no canopy. The sparse green exists in the cracks, at the margins, in the places where the emerging geology has not yet claimed full territory. A plant growing here is a *statement*. It says: this space is not yet finished. It says: I am holding this place open. The Hollowed read these statements. They know which passages are still becoming, which chambers are still settling, which walls are still learning their final shape. The vegetation is a map of incompletion, and the Hollowed are a people who live in incompletion, who have made their settlement from the raw material of a world still under construction.

---

Everything grows slowly here. The stone moves. Not quickly—you would not see it in a human lifetime—but it moves. Chambers shift by fractions of a centimeter. Passages narrow or widen. The builders account for this. They build with *give*, with flexible joints, with materials that can compress and expand. They build knowing that their grandchildren will need to adjust, to re-plaster, to re-route, to continue the work of making habitable a place that does not wish to be inhabited. This is not despair. It is *practice*. The erosion parameter at 0.0 means the landscape has not yet entered its erosional phase, has not yet begun the long work of wearing down. It is still *forming*. The Hollowed are living inside a formation event. They are, in a sense, fossils-in-waiting, or perhaps they are the first generation of a geology that will outlast them by epochs.

---

## The Primitive High

Technology at 1.4: early-tool-use, simple metal, hand-craft. The Hollowed work stone with stone, still. They have copper, which they find in the youngest veins and do not fully understand—they know it is *different*, that it can be shaped without breaking, that it holds an edge in ways that flint does not, but they do not know that it is metal, do not have the category. They work it with the same tools they use for bone and wood and certain dense fungi. They have fire, carefully managed, carried in ceramic vessels that are themselves still becoming-ceramic, fired in kilns that are new constructions, experimental, sometimes collapsing.

Their "machines" are mostly *arrangements*. A system of pulleys made from wood and sinew and the occasional copper pin. A lever system for moving stone blocks that weigh more than any individual should move. Water-wheels in the few places where water moves with enough force, grinding grain, powering bellows for the copper-work. They have writing, of a sort—notched tokens, arrangements of pebbles, a system of knots in cord that records debts and obligations and the names of the dead. They do not have the wheel in any general sense. They do not have glass. They have *technique*, accumulated across generations, transmitted by apprenticeship, by the packed proximity of teacher and student in chambers where space is so saturated that learning happens by accident as much as by intention.

---

## The Rare Lives

Fauna at 1.0: rare, but not absent. The Hollowed do not domesticate animals in any surface-world sense. They do not keep livestock, do not ride, do not pull carts with beasts. What animals exist here are *co-inhabitants*, tolerated, occasionally used, never numerous enough to change the ecology. There are fish in the deepest pools, blind, pale, edible but not prized. There are insects—fungus-moths, stone-crickets, a creature that resembles a centipede but is something else, something that has never been named because it has never been observed to do anything *useful*, and in the packed economy of this place, that is nearly the same as not existing.

The rarest fauna is human. Children. The Hollowed reproduce slowly, deliberately, in conditions of saturation that would seem to discourage it. A birth is an event that reorganizes a chamber, that requires space to be made, that is celebrated with a ferocity that suggests the rarity is felt deeply. Children are *precious* in a way that is almost painful to witness. They are the only ones allowed to be inefficient, to waste time, to explore without purpose. They grow up fast, though—the packed world does not permit prolonged immaturity—and by adolescence they are already contributing, already learning the techniques that keep the settlement in motion.

There are no predators. This is not a place where something hunts you. The danger is the stone, the water, the slow shift of a wall, the gas that collects in certain youngest chambers and that the Hollowed know to avoid by smell, by the particular quality of the silence. The dynamic parameter at 2.0 means the world is *active*—weather cycles underground (pressure changes, moisture shifts, the slow breathing of the deep stone), biological pace (the slow metabolism of everything that lives here), the constant low-grade motion of a settlement that never sleeps because sleep is a luxury of space, and space is the one thing this place does not have.

---

## The Quiet

Sound at 0.4: silence, or near it. The Hollowed do not speak loudly. The packed chambers would carry any raised voice into a dozen other spaces, a dozen other lives. They communicate in a register that surface-dwellers would call whispering, would find exhausting, would mistake for secrecy. But it is not secrecy. It is *consideration*. The silence is full of meaning. A pause in the work is a statement. The particular rhythm of a tool on stone carries information—who is working, what they are making, whether they are in difficulty. The drip of condensation is a clock. The particular quality of the dark (for the Hollowed navigate much of their world in near-total darkness, their eyes having adapted to read the faint bioluminescence, the mineral gleam, the afterimage of any light source) is a map.

There is music, rarely. A single instrument, passed between players, its sound swallowed immediately by the packed stone. Singing, when it happens, is a communal act, a marking of time, a way of saying *we are still here* in a world that is still deciding whether to let them be.

---

## The Rules

The world operates on principles that emerge from the parameters:

**No space is empty.** If you find an unoccupied corner, it is either in use for something you do not yet understand, or it is *becoming*—reserved for a future construction, a future birth, a future death.

**Water is borrowed.** The pools, the condensation, the mineral sweat—all of it is on loan from the deep stone, and must be used with gratitude, with return, with the understanding that it will be taken back.

**The stone is alive.** Not metaphorically. The Hollowed experience the slow movement of their world as *presence*, as negotiation. They do not fight the shifting walls. They *accommodate* them. A crack is not damage. It is *communication*.

**Children are the future's only argument.** In a place of emerging geology, of incomplete formation, the only proof that the settlement will continue is the presence of those who will outlast the current generation's compromises with the stone.

**Silence is respect.** To fill the quiet with noise is to claim space that belongs to others, to the stone, to the slow processes that are still making this place *habitable*.

---

You stand in the central gathering-place, such as it is—a widening in a passage, packed with people, with tools, with the materials of ongoing construction. Around you, the stone sweats. Somewhere above (or is it below?), a child is learning to read the condensation patterns. The copper-worker is shaping something that might become a pin, a blade, a promise. The fire-keeper tends a flame that has been tended, in this lineage, for longer than any surface culture has existed.

This is a town. This is a world under construction. This is a place that has not yet decided what it is, and finds, in that not-yet, a kind of freedom that more settled places have forgotten.

The water drips. The stone moves. The people work, packed together, in the quiet dark, making a home from the raw material of becoming.
