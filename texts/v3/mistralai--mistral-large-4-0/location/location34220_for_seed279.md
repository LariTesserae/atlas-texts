# The Condensing Plain

---

The water does not fall here so much as *arrive*. It gathers in the hollows between black glass ridges, appears on every surface in beads and sheets, drips from the undersides of overhangs that seem to have no above. The atmosphere holds it suspended—1.4 cool, just cool enough that breath clouds briefly, just warm enough that nothing freezes. The condensation is constant, patient, *loud*.

Sound first. The plain is never silent. Water moves everywhere: percolating up through fractured stone, running in threads down vertical faces, pooling and overflowing, dripping from the tips of mineral formations that resemble nothing so much as frozen fountains—stalactites inverted, growing upward from the saturated ground, each one a column of translucent amber or grey or faint rose, depending on what trace minerals the water carries this week. The dripping is a dense chorus, 2.3 loud, a thousand small impacts that merge into a continuous *shushing* roar, like wind through reeds that do not exist. When a larger flow releases from a ceiling of glass, it strikes stone with a sound like a handclap, sharp and startling, and the plain *notices*—vibrations run through the mineral substrate, and somewhere, something that is not quite an animal shifts in response.

The glass. The plain is young, 1.4 erosion, and what youth means here is that the surface has not yet worn smooth. Volcanic glass, obsidian in its many moods—black where it is thick, green at the edges, rainbow-iridescent where water has begun, *very slowly*, to etch it. The formations are sharp. Walking here is impossible without cutting oneself; the glass fractures with conchoidal curves, edges like blue-white blades. The ridges run in parallel, northeast to southwest, suggesting some pressure from below, some upwelling that happened recently, geologically speaking. Between the ridges, the ground is not soil—there is no soil, 0.1 vegetation means no plants, no decay, no organic layer—but a saturated mat of mineral felt, of precipitated salts and amorphous silica, of things dissolving and re-precipitating in the constant moisture. It squelches. It releases bubbles of gas that smell of nothing, of *outside*, when they break the surface.

The water table is at the surface, has been long enough to carve karst-like features into material that should not karst, glass being too young, too fresh, too *recent* to have developed the chemistry. But here it is: solution channels, underground rivers audible as a low thrum beneath the louder dripping, cavities that open suddenly into chambers where the air is so saturated that the walls weep continuously, where standing water has developed a skin, a surface tension so thick with dissolved minerals that it supports weight, that carries floating islands of precipitated crust, each one a miniature landscape of its own, humped and cratered, still *growing*, still slow.

1.6 scale—this is not a single feature but a *region*. The ridges extend past sight in both directions. There are at least three distinct zones visible from any high point: the *weeping* zone, where vertical faces dominate and the air is thickest with moisture; the *pooling* zone, a broad depression where water stands in shallow lakes that never warm enough to evaporate quickly, that breed strange geometries of mineral deposition; and the *rising* zone, where the glass has formed upward-growing structures, soda-straw stalagmites in reverse, delicate and hollow, that chime against each other when the air moves, a glass music, high and thin and *wrong* in a way that makes the rare fauna pause and orient.

Fauna: 1.2, rare. But *present*. What moves here has adapted to glass and water and no plants. There are things like elongated crabs, but their shells are not chitin—they are *mineralized*, calcium drawn from the water, deposited in layers, so that they click when they move, a dry sound in all this wet. They filter the water for particles, for the sparse organic matter that washes in from somewhere, some edge of this world where the parameters change, where something eats and excretes and begins the long slow work of soil. They are slow, 0.6 dynamic, moving only when the temperature gradient shifts across the day, which is slight, 1.4 cool holding steady through a narrow band. They retreat into crevices when the light changes—0.7, dim, a diffuse glow from everywhere and nowhere, the sky a permanent overcast of condensing moisture, the sun a brighter region that never quite resolves into a disk.

And there is something else. The weirdness, 1.6, demands it. In the pooling zone, where the water stands deepest, the surface crust sometimes *opens*. Not from below—there is nothing below that pushes—but from *within*. The crust fractures along lines that follow no stress pattern, no weakness in the material, and through the opening rises something that is not water, not gas, but *light*, a pale luminescence that persists for minutes, that stains the surrounding crust with a faint phosphorescence, that the crab-things will approach and then retreat from, their mineral shells clicking in a rhythm that might be fear or might be something else, some recognition. The light has no source that can be found. It is not electrical—there are no storms here, the moisture condenses too gently for charge to build. It is not biological—there are no cells, no metabolism, nothing that could produce it. It simply *is*, a property of the deep water, of the glass below it, of the slow chemistry of this young world that has not yet settled into anything stable.

The scattered builds, 1.2, are small and *partial*. They do not dominate. A platform of fused glass, raised on pillars, with no roof, no walls, simply a place to stand above the weeping—abandoned or never finished, the purpose unclear. A cairn of stones that are not local, water-rounded, *imported* from somewhere with actual rivers, actual erosion, actual time. A wall, three meters long, that follows no contour, that seems to mark nothing, made of the same black glass as the ridges but *worked*, smoothed, fitted together with a precision that suggests intention, tool, 0.5 tech—none of which should be here. The wall is the strangest thing. It faces the rising zone. It has stood long enough for water to begin to etch it, to deposit its minerals on one side, the side that faces away from the luminescence. As if it *blocks* something. As if whatever made it knew.

The ecology, such as it is, is *mineral-first*. The glass weathers, slowly, into forms that resemble life—dendrites, branching structures, patterns that the eye wants to read as ferns or coral or something from a warmer world. But there is no carbon cycle here, no photosynthesis, no green. The closest thing to a food chain is chemical: water dissolves, transports, deposits, in endless slow loops. The crab-things are near the top, such as it is, feeding on particles, on each other when one dies and sinks, its mineral shell too heavy to float, becoming substrate for the next generation of crust-formers. When they die, they do not decay. They *persist*, become part of the growing mineral landscape, fossils before there was life to fossilize.

The temperature holds at 1.4, cool, and this is the engine of everything. Cool enough for condensation, for water to be *everywhere*, for the glass to weather by hydration rather than by abrasion, for the strange chemistry to proceed at its own mineral pace. The dynamic is 0.6, slow—nothing changes quickly here. The luminescences come and go over hours. The pools deepen by millimeters per year. The glass ridges erode, but the erosion is *chemical*, the water eating the silica molecule by molecule, so that what looks like sharpness is actually the *preservation* of sharpness, the slow revelation of forms that were always there, waiting for the water to show them.

But something is happening. The plain is young. The parameters are not stable. The density, 2.4—many presences per volume, layered, near-touching—means that the water, the glass, the mineral crust, the rare fauna, the faint luminescence, the partial structures, all of it *presses* against everything else. There are edges where the glass meets the pool and the chemistry is different. There are boundaries where the crust meets the air and the exchange is constant. There are places, in the deepest chambers, where the water has been standing so long, moving so slowly, that it has begun to *remember* things—shapes that are almost structures, patterns that almost resolve into meaning. The weirdness is not random. It is *emergent*, a property of density and slowness and water and glass in combination, something that the plain is *growing into*, deliberately or not, a direction it is taking that has no name.

Sound again, because it never stops. The dripping. The chiming of the rising glass. The clicking of the crab-things, rare but *present*, a punctuation in the wet roar. The low thrum of underground water, felt more than heard, a vibration in the sternum when one stands still long enough. And sometimes, from the wall, a sound like *breathing*—not the wind, because the wind here is too gentle, too saturated, to make such a sound. The wall breathes. Or something behind it does. Or the glass itself, cooling and contracting in the slight temperature shifts, makes a sound that the ear interprets as breath, as presence, as *attention*.

The light is 0.7, dim, but it is *everywhere*. There are no shadows, not really—water diffuses, glass refracts, the overcast sky turns the whole plain into a soft-box, a place of no edges, only gradients. The luminescences are the only *points* of light, and they draw the eye, the rare fauna, the crab-things pausing in their mineral clicking, orienting toward something that is not food, not threat, not mating—something *else*, a category the plain is still inventing.

You would not stay. The water would find every opening, every warmth, every place where your body tries to hold its heat. You would become part of the condensation, part of the slow chemistry, part of the glass that is always growing, always *weeping*, always arriving at a wetness that never dries.

But you would return. The sound would call you back. The light from the pools, the breathing of the wall, the click of shells on mineral felt. The plain is not finished. It is *becoming*, 0.6 slow, 1.4 young, patient in a way that feels like intention, like watching, like waiting for something—maybe for you, maybe for the parameters to shift, maybe for the glass to finally, after all this water, begin to *melt*.

The water arrives. It always arrives. It gathers, drips, pools, rises, luminesces, waits.
