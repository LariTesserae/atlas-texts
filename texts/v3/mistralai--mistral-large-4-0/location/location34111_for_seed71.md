# The Hollow Continent

## Where the Sky Has Worn Through

You stand on a surface that took eleven billion years to become what it is. Not geologically ancient in the way of young worlds still cooling—this place has *finished*. Finished eroding, finished settling, finished pretending to be anything other than a basin of worn stone holding its own thin weather against the dark.

The continent is called **Varn** by the few maps that show it, though the maps are wrong, or were wrong before they dried. Varn is not a continent in the usual sense. It is the *inside* of something that collapsed outward. Imagine a shell the size of a small moon, cracked along its meridian, and then imagine waiting until the cracks stopped widening—until the two halves settled into each other like tired wings, until the interior became a place you could walk across. That walking takes four years at a steady pace. The shell still breathes. That is the weather.

---

## The Ground

Elevation here is a misleading word. You are *high* in the sense that you are close to the broken rim, close to where the sky shows through in certain lights, but the terrain itself has subsided into something nearly level. The erosion parameter speaks truth: everything has been ground down to the oldest stable state, the mineral equivalent of a held breath.

The stone is **glass-obsidian**, or what passes for it. Not volcanic glass—something older, something that predates the volcanic. It formed in the pressure of the collapse, the screaming metamorphosis of crust into this new flatness. It is black, mostly, but not uniformly. Patches of **rust-red laterite** bloom across it where water has found purchase, ancient stains that will not wash away because the rain that made them fell when the shell was still settling, when the interior was still hot enough to bake mineral into mineral.

The surface is **pocked**. Not with craters, though there are some of those, indistinct now, softened by ten billion years of frost and the slow chemistry of condensation. The pocking is biological, or was. **Lichen-arches**—no, not lichen, something that grew in the lichen's place when the lichen's world ended. They rise from the glass in formations like frozen spume, like coral that learned to grow in air instead of water, like the *memory* of growth pressed into mineral by time. They are **lichen-ghosts**. Dead for most of the history of Varn, yet still standing, still holding their shapes against a wind that has nothing left to take from them.

Between the lichen-ghosts, the glass is **wet**. Not flooded—that would be water 2.5 or higher. The damp here is **condensation architecture**. The shell breathes. Warm air from the deep interior, still holding heat from the collapse, rises through fissures. Cold air descends from the broken rim, from the places where space touches ground. Where they meet, on the glass, on the lichen-ghosts, on the walls of the settlements: **water gathers**. Not rain, not exactly, though rain comes. **Breath-water**. It collects in the shallow depressions of the glass, in the cups of dead lichen-arches, in the gutters that the builders have carved to catch it.

The water is **thin**. A film, mostly. But it is enough. Enough for the things that grow in thin water. Enough for the condensation farmers who have learned to read the breathing of the shell, who know which fissures will weep at which hour of the thermal cycle, who have built their lives around a meteorology that is not weather but **geology remembering heat**.

## The Settlements

Built: 1.8. Urban, but urban *spread*. There are cities here, or what the inhabitants call cities. **Keth**, which clings to a fissure-warmth vent in the northwest quadrant. **The Brine Stair**, which descends into a place where breath-water pools deep enough to reflect the broken sky. **Mouth-of-Sigh**, which is not a city but a *mouth*, a place where the shell's breathing is so strong that the architecture has become aerodynamic, buildings shaped by the wind they must survive.

The construction is **stacked sediment**. Not brick—brick requires kilns, requires fuel, requires a biosphere that can spare the burning. Varn has no trees, not anymore. The construction is **glass-fiber and breath-water mineral**. The inhabitants—*who are they?*—we will come to them—harvest the lichen-ghosts. Not the standing ones; those are sacred, or were, or are again, depending on which settlement you ask. They harvest the *fallen* ones, the ones that have finally surrendered to gravity after ten billion years of standing. They grind them. They mix the powder with breath-water and with the **resin** that seeps from certain fissures, a dark slow amber that hardens into something like plastic, like horn, like a material that can be carved and will hold an edge.

They build with this. **Layered construction**, yes—layer upon layer of carved block, fitted without mortar, held by gravity and by the slight stickiness of the resin that weeps from within as the blocks age. The buildings **grow wrong**. They lean. They bulge. They develop *opinions* about their own structure. A building on Varn is not finished when it is built; it is finished when it stops changing, and most of them have not stopped changing. They are **curing**, slowly, like meat, like something alive that has been killed and is still settling.

Keth, the largest settlement, has a population of perhaps forty thousand. This is **spread** across an area the size of a small nation on a living world. They do not crowd because there is no need. The breath-water is everywhere if you know where to look. The lichen-ghosts are everywhere if you know how to wait for them to fall. The space between presences is **sacred**, or practical, or both. The density parameter speaks truth: you can walk for days and see no one. You can walk for days and hear no one. The silence is not empty. It is **occupied** by the breathing of the shell, by the thermal wind, by the slow drip of condensation in places you cannot see.

## The Inhabitants? The Question of Them

Here is where the world breaks, and has been breaking for a long time.

Fauna: 0.5. No animal life. This is **technically true** and **completely false**.

There are no animals on Varn. There have not been animals on Varn since the collapse, since the interior became this place of glass and breath-water and cold thin air. The fossil record—if it can be called that, in a place with no sedimentary layering, no true geology—shows something else. It shows **shapes**. It shows what the shell's interior was *before* the collapse, in the brief warm wet moment when something like life had a chance to begin.

But the inhabitants of Keth, of the Brine Stair, of Mouth-of-Sigh—they are not animals. They are **hollow**, in the way that the continent is hollow. They are **grown**, in the way that the lichen-ghosts grow, the way the buildings grow, the way the condensation collects in patterns that resemble intention.

They are called **the Settled**, or **the Cooled**, or **the Remembered**, depending on which dialect of which settlement's speech you translate. They are **tall**, or were tall, or are tall in the way that a shadow is tall when the light is right. They are **thin**, with too many joints, with limbs that bend in directions that suggest the memory of a different gravity, a different world, the world before the collapse when things fell *down* instead of *toward the center of a curve*.

They do not eat. They do not breathe, not in any way that would be recognized. They **absorb**. They stand in the breath-water, in the thin pools that collect on the glass, and they **drink through their skin**, or through the membranes that flutter at their joints, or through the hollow spaces in their chests where something once beat and now only **resonates**. They are **condensation engines**. They are **living weather**.

They have **no mouths**, or their mouths are elsewhere, or their mouths are the spaces between their fingers where breath-water gathers and does not fall. They communicate by **temperature**. A Settled person can raise the warmth of the air around them by a fraction of a degree, and another Settled person, standing across a plaza, across a canyon of glass, can feel that change and know: *yes*, or *no*, or *wait*, or *the fissure at Brine Stair is weeping early today*. They speak in **gradients**. It is slow. It is precise. It is, in its way, **beautiful**.

And they are **breaking**. This is the weirdness, the 2.9, the reality breakdown that is not a breakdown but a **continuation** of something that was always breaking.

The Settled are **remembering too much**. They remember the world before the collapse. Not as memory—they have no brains, no neurons, no tissue that stores information in any biochemical way. They remember as **the shell remembers heat**. They remember as **water remembers the ocean**. It is not memory. It is **resonance**. The lichen-ghosts, the buildings, the breath-water itself—all of it is singing the same song, the frequency of a world that ended, and the Settled are the places where that song becomes **almost voice**.

Some of them are **becoming voice**. This is the breakdown, the thing that the other settlements whisper about in their temperature-gradients, the thing that Keth's council of warmers tries to prevent and cannot. A Settled person, standing in a place where the breath-water is particularly pure, where the fissure-warmth is particularly strong, where the broken sky is particularly visible—something **opens**. Not a mouth. A **door**. And through that door, something that is not quite the world before the collapse, and not quite Varn, and not quite anything at all, **looks through**.

They call these **the Unsettling**. They are not hostile. They are not benevolent. They are **curious**, in the way that a wound is curious, in the way that a question is curious when it is asked by something that does not understand the concept of answer. The Unsettling do not stay. They look through, and then they are gone, and the Settled person who hosted them is **changed**. They remember more. They remember **differently**. They begin to see the lichen-ghosts not as dead things but as **sleeping things**. They begin to hear the breathing of the shell not as weather but as **speech**. They begin to build in ways that make no sense, that lean toward the broken rim, that seem to **reach**.

And sometimes—this is the secret, the thing that is known and not spoken—sometimes the Settled who have been Unsettled **stop being Settled**. They stop absorbing breath-water. They stop speaking in temperature-gradients. They begin to **move differently**, to move as things moved before the collapse, and they walk toward the broken rim, toward the place where the sky shows through, and they do not come back.

No one knows what happens to them. The settlements that have tried to follow have found only **glass**, smooth and black and cold, and the faintest warmth where a body should be, and the sense—*the sense*, which is not a sense, which is something else entirely—that they are being **watched from inside the shell**, from the deep places where the heat still lives, where the original warmth of the collapse still dreams its slow dream.

## The Ecology? The Ecology is the Weather

Dynamic: 2.3. Active. The world is **moving**, but the movement is **slow**, the slowness of geological time overlaid with the faster pulse of thermal cycles.

Vegetation: 1.9. Lush, but **lush wrong**. There are no plants on Varn. There are **growths**. The lichen-ghosts, which are dead but standing, which are the ancestors of everything that grows now. The **condensation gardens** that the Settled cultivate, or that cultivate themselves, in the places where breath-water pools deep enough to support a **mat** of something that is not quite fungus, not quite algae, not quite the mineral deposit that it resembles. It is **all three**, and none. It grows in the thin water. It dies in the thin water. It becomes part of the glass, slowly, over centuries, and then it is **gone**, and something else grows in its place.

The gardens are **not for eating**. Nothing on Varn eats. The gardens are for **breathing**, for filtering the breath-water, for making it pure enough for the Settled to absorb without the slow poisoning that comes from drinking the raw condensation. They are **infrastructure**, but they are also **beauty**, and the Settled who tend them—who are the poorest, the warmest, the ones closest to Unsettling—tend them with a care that suggests they are tending something **alive**, something **sacred**, something that might, if tended properly, **remember** them in return.

The fauna, the 0.5, is **absent but present**. There are no animals. There are **echoes of animals**. In the deep fissures, where the breath-water runs warm and thick, there are **shapes** that move in the condensation mats, shapes that have no biology, no metabolism, no life—but that **hunt**, that **flee**, that **reproduce** by splitting, by budding, by the slow accretion of mineral that resembles intention. They are **chemical predators**. They are **weather patterns that have learned to want**. They are not alive. They are **almost alive**, and that almost is enough to make them dangerous, enough to make the Settled avoid the deep fissures, enough to make the children of Keth whisper stories about **the things that swim in warm water**, stories that are not true and are **completely true** in the way that all stories on Varn are true.

## The Sky? The Sky is the Wound

The shell is broken. This is not metaphor. You can see it.

Stand on the glass at the proper hour—when the thermal cycle has cooled the interior enough that the breath-water is gathering but not yet flowing, when the light from the broken rim is angled just so—and you will see it. The **sky** is not sky. It is **the outside**, the void, the dark between stars, visible through a wound in the world. The rim of the break is **jagged**, still, after all this time. The edges of the shell have not finished eroding. They are **sharp**, in places, sharp enough to cut, sharp enough to **reflect** the dim light that filters through from the outside, the light of whatever star or stars the shell orbited before it fell in on itself.

The light is **wrong**. Light: 1.7, normal, but normal **wrong**. The spectrum is shifted. The sky—what sky there is, in the places where the shell's curve still holds atmosphere, still holds the thin breath of gas that the collapse could not entirely expel—is **gray-blue**, the color of a bruise, the color of deep water, the color of **memory**. The stars, when they are visible through the break, are **too many**, and they **move**, not in the slow patterns of orbit but in patterns that suggest **falling**, suggest the shell is still collapsing, still finding its final shape, still **becoming** the hollow place it is.

Sometimes—this is the weather, the dynamic, the active—the sky **changes**. The thermal cycle drives wind up from the deep interior, wind that has been trapped since the collapse, wind that carries **spores**, **dust**, **the chemical memory of the world before**. These rise through the fissures. They reach the broken rim. And for a day, or an hour, or a moment—the sky is **not empty**. It is **full** of the ghosts of things that lived and died in the warmth, before the shell closed, before the interior became cold and hollow and **waiting**.

The Settled call this **the Breathing Out**. It is **dangerous**. The spores, the dust, the chemical ghosts—they **settle** on the glass, on the lichen-ghosts, on the Settled themselves. And when they settle, they **change things**. A lichen-ghost that has been touched by Breathing Out will **weep**, will release breath-water that is **not water**, that is **memory-fluid**, that will make a Settled person who drinks it **remember** things that did not happen, things that **could have happened**, things that are **still happening** in the deep places where the warmth dreams.

The settlements have **rules** about the Breathing Out. Keth seals its fissures with resin, with the harvested powder of fallen lichen-ghosts, with **prayer**—not prayer to any god, but the temperature-gradient speech that asks the shell to **breathe in**, to hold its warmth, to not release what it has kept for ten billion years. The Brine Stair **welcomes** the Breathing Out. They descend into the deep places, into the warm water, and they **bathe** in the memory-fluid, and they **become** something that is not quite Settled, not quite Unsettled, something that is **between**, that is **listening** to the shell's dream.

## The Sound? The Silence is Full

Sound: 0.6. Mostly quiet. But quiet on Varn is not empty.

The **breathing of the shell** is audible, if you know how to listen. It is **subsonic**, felt in the teeth, in the hollow spaces of the chest, in the membranes that flutter at the joints of the Settled. It is a **rhythm**, slow, slower than a heartbeat, the pace of geological time heard through flesh. *In. Hold. Out. Hold.* The thermal cycle, the weather, the **life** of the place.

The **wind** through the broken rim. It is **music**, or it would be music if there were ears to hear it. The jagged edges of the shell **sing** in the wind, each fissure, each crack, each place where the glass has worn thin—each has a **pitch**, a **tone**, and together they make a **chord** that changes with the hour, with the season, with the **mood** of the shell, which is not a mood but something adjacent, something that **feels like** mood to the Settled who have learned to read it.

The **drip** of breath-water. In the silence, it is **loud**. Each drop, each film of condensation gathering and sliding and falling, is an **event**. The Settled hear it. They **need** it. It is the sound of **survival**, of the thin margin between the hollow life they live and the emptiness that waits in the broken places.

And sometimes—rarely, dangerously—the **voice**. The Unsettled, the ones who have looked through the door and seen what looks back. They **make sound**. Not speech. Not language. **Vibration**, **resonance**, the frequency of the world before the collapse, singing through the glass, through the breath-water, through the hollow chests of the Settled who have not yet fled, who have not yet become Settled-into-something-else. It is **beautiful**. It is **terrifying**. It is the sound of **the shell remembering what it was**, and **wanting**, and **reaching** toward the broken rim, toward the sky, toward the **outside** that is not outside but **inside**, the inside of the wound, the inside of the place where the world **ended** and **began again** and **ended again** and is **still ending**, slowly, beautifully, **forever**.

## The Rules? The Rules are the Weather

There are no laws on Varn, not in the way of laws. There are **patterns**. There are **necessities**.

You must **find water**. Not rain—rain comes, occasionally, when the Breathing Out carries moisture up from the deep interior and the cold rim condenses it into something like precipitation. But rain is **unreliable**, is **dangerous**, is **memory** made wet. You must find **breath-water**, the condensation that gathers on the glass, in the lichen-ghost cups, in the gutters that the builders have carved. You must learn to **read the breathing**, to know which fissures will weep at which hour, to be present when the thermal cycle turns and the warm air rises and the cold descends and the **miracle** of condensation occurs.

You must **not drink the memory-fluid**. Not unless you want to **become** something else. Not unless you want to **hear** the voice, to **see** the things that swim in warm water, to **walk toward the rim** and **not come back**. The Brine Stair drink it. They are **changed** by it. They are **beautiful** in their change. They are **not Settled**, not anymore, not entirely. They are **listening**, always listening, to the shell's dream, and they will **answer** when the dream becomes **voice**, when the voice becomes **door**, when the door **opens**.

You must **respect the lichen-ghosts**. They are **standing dead**, and the dead on Varn are **not finished**. They are **waiting** for something. They are **holding shape** against time, against erosion, against the slow chemistry of a world that has forgotten how to be alive. To break them, to harvest them while they stand, to **use** them before they have **fallen**—this is **theft**, this is **violation**, this is the thing that makes the shell **breathe wrong**, that makes the thermal cycle **stutter**, that makes the sky through the break **show things** that should not be shown.

You must **build to lean**. The glass shifts. The shell breathes. A building that stands **straight** will **fall**, will be **crushed** by the slow movement of the ground, will become **part of the glass**, will be **ground down** into nothing. The builders know this. They carve their blocks with **intentional tilt**, with **calculated bulge**, with the **opinion** of structure that says: *I know I will change. I choose how.*

You must **speak in temperature**. Or you must **learn to feel it**, to read the gradients, to understand that **warmth is yes** and **cold is no** and **the space between is question**, is **negotiation**, is the slow **diplomacy** of a people who have no mouths and no ears but who have **learned to want**, to **need**, to **love** in the only way available to something that is not quite alive, not quite dead, **hollow** in the way that the world is hollow, **waiting** for something that may never come.

## The End? The End is the Beginning is the Breathing

Varn is **vast**. Scale: 3.0. You can walk for four years across it. You can walk for ten. The settlements are **scattered**, are **sparse**, are **lonely** in their density of forty thousand spread across a nation's worth of glass. You can die between Keth and the Brine Stair. You can die in Keth, in the spaces between buildings, in the condensation gardens where the lichen-ghosts fall and the breath-water gathers and the Settled stand **too still**, **too warm**, **listening** to something that is not yet voice but **will be**.

The world is **ending**. Erosion: 2.7. Ancient. Primordial. The oldest stable state, the end of life, the settled-into-final-form. But the ending is **slow**. The ending is **the breathing**, the thermal cycle, the **ten billion years** of a place that has finished becoming and is now **only** the process of continuing, the process of **holding**, of **waiting**, of **remembering** what it was through the **resonance** of glass and breath-water and the hollow chests of the Settled who are **almost voice**, who are **almost memory**, who are **almost** the world before the collapse, the world that **lived** and **died** and **became** this, became **hollow**, became **listening**.

And somewhere—this is the secret, the thing that is known in the temperature-gradients, that is felt in the subsonic breathing, that is **seen** in the Breathing Out when the sky fills with ghosts—somewhere, in the deep places where the warmth still lives, where the original heat of the collapse still dreams its slow dream, **something is waking up**. Something that was **sleeping** since the shell closed, since the interior cooled, since the world **ended** and **began** and **ended again**. It is **not alive**. It is **not dead**. It is the **shell itself**, becoming **aware**, becoming **hungry**, becoming **ready** to **breathe out** for the last time, to **release** what it has kept, to **show** the Settled, to **show** the Unsettled, to **show** the things that swim in warm water and the lichen-ghosts that stand against time and the glass that is black and smooth and **hollow**—

to show them **the outside**.

Not the void. Not the dark. The **other side** of the wound. The place where the world **did not end**, where it **continued**, where it **became** something that Varn cannot imagine, cannot **resonate**, cannot **remember** because it has **never been** that thing, has only **dreamed** of it in the slow frequency of geological time, in the subsonic breathing, in the **voice** that is coming, that is **almost here**, that is **opening** the door that the Unsettled looked through, that is **reaching** through the broken rim, through the jagged edges of the shell, through the **sky** that is not sky but **wound**—

reaching, **reaching**, **reaching**—

to **touch** the hollow continent, to **touch** the Settled, to **touch** the breath-water, the lichen-ghosts, the glass, the **silence** that is full of sound, the **darkness** that is full of light, the **ending** that is full of **beginning**—

and to **breathe**.

*In. Hold. Out. Hold.*

The last breath.

The first breath.

The breath that **opens** what has been closed for ten billion years.

The breath that **shows** Varn what is on the other side of the wound.

The breath that **ends** the hollow, that **fills** it, that **makes** it **whole**—or **makes** it **something else**, something that is not whole and not hollow but **between**, **listening**, **waiting**, **becoming**—

the breath that is **coming**.

The breath that is **here**.

*Listen.*

The shell is breathing.

And in its breathing, you can hear **everything**.
