I'll let this place emerge from the tensions in these parameters — the darkness against the warmth, the emptiness against the lushness, the ancient erosion against the high elevation. Something small and hollow and wet, yet teeming with life, lit by nothing, built by hands that understood physics we don't.

**The Weeping Hollow**

You descend. That is the only word for it — not a slope, not stairs, but a *settling into*, as if the world itself were exhaling and you were following the breath down. The elevation is high, but high in the way of a wound: the pressure is wrong, thin, and your ears remember water at sea level. You are inside something. Something with a roof of sky that never opens.

**Darkness.** Total, and yet you see. This is the first paradox, and it will not be the last. Your eyes adjust to a light that has no source — a diffuse, sourceless luminescence that seems to come from *everywhere behind your eyes*, from the wet surfaces themselves, from the air. It is enough to see by. It is never enough to *illuminate*. Shadows do not fall here; they simply *are*, thicker in some places, and you move through them like gauze.

The hollow is small. A cathedral, perhaps, or a very large room hollowed from living rock. You could walk its circumference in twenty minutes, though you will not want to. The boundaries are not walls but *edges of attention* — places where the sourceless light thins and the wet dark becomes something else entirely, something that presses back. You feel it before you reach it. The fauna knows these edges. They do not cross them.

**Water is everywhere and nowhere.** The rock weeps. Not metaphor — the stone itself is porous, ancient, saturated with something that is not quite water but behaves like it: it beads on surfaces in perfect spheres that do not fall, that *hang*, suspended by some tension you cannot see, gathering weight slowly until they *choose* to descend, sliding down the curved walls in paths that have been followed for epochs. The air is thick with condensation. Your skin is always wet. Your clothes — if you wear any; the hollow provides its own thin film of moisture that makes fabric irrelevant — cling and release in a rhythm you learn to anticipate. There are no streams. There are no pools. Only the weeping, the hanging, the slow *patter* of spheres releasing themselves onto surfaces below, where they vanish into stone that drinks.

The wet is warm. 2.1, yes — the air carries heat like a held breath. But the weeping water is *cold*, and where it touches your skin, you feel both temperatures at once, the warm dark pressing in and the cold seeping through, and your body cannot reconcile them. You shiver and sweat in the same moment.

**The rock is ancient.** 2.7 — erosion has done its work here for longer than the mountains outside have existed. What you stand on is not floor but *memory of floor*, worn to a near-flatness that still holds the ghosts of angles, of corners, of something that was once *built* and has been worn into geological patience. The surfaces are smooth as thought. You walk on history that has forgotten it was ever anything else. In places, the smoothing is *almost* complete — and in those places, you see what the erosion has not yet reached: faint geometric depressions, channels that run at angles no water would choose, the suggestion of *purpose* in a landscape that has otherwise surrendered purpose entirely.

**Empty.** 0.2 — and yet. The hollow is vast in its smallness. The space between things is the thing. You can stand in the center — the *almost* center, the place where the weeping is densest — and see nothing but wet dark and the faint suggestion of walls beyond the reach of your sourceless sight. The emptiness is *inhabited* by absence. You feel it as a presence. The hollow has been emptied of something, and the emptying is not finished.

**And yet the fauna.** 2.3 — abundant, teeming, *wrong* in its abundance. They are small things, mostly. Insect-scale, palm-scale. They move through the wet dark with a confidence you cannot share, finding paths you did not know existed, navigating by senses that have nothing to do with light — vibration, perhaps, or the chemical gradients of the weeping water, or something else entirely. They are warm-blooded, or the equivalent. You feel their heat when they pass, a brief wave of *aliveness* in the thick air. They do not fear you. They have never met anything like you. They investigate. A dozen of them — winged, many-legged, eyeless or with eyes that see only the spectrum of the sourceless light — will gather at your wrist, tasting the moisture you carry, and you will feel their *curiosity* as a physical thing, a pressure, a *question* asked in a language of touch.

There are larger things. You hear them. Not see — the light will not allow it — but *hear*, and feel in the vibration of the worn stone beneath your feet. Something moves in the deep wet, in the places where the hollow extends further than your smallness can follow. It is slow. 1.1 — everything here is slow. The movement takes minutes to pass. You feel it as a rhythm, a *breathing* of the stone itself, and you learn not to mistake it for your own heartbeat, though the distinction grows thin.

**Sound.** 1.7 — loud, but the loudness is *interior*. The weeping makes a sound like rain that never reaches ground, a constant *shhhhh* of water in perpetual almost-falling. The fauna make their own noises — clicks, hums, the wet *pap* of bodies moving through thick air. And beneath it all, or perhaps *emerging* from it, a low *resonance*, a frequency you feel in your teeth more than hear, that seems to come from the rock itself, from the water held in its pores, from the hollow's *breathing*. It is not unpleasant. It is *attentive*. The hollow is listening.

**Built.** 1.3 — scattered, partial, *almost gone*. You find them as you move through the wet dark: a curved wall that rises from the worn floor and ends abruptly, its top eroded to a knife-edge. A series of shallow basins, connected by channels that carry no water but *remember* carrying it. A single structure — you will find it, if you stay long enough — that is more than erosion could explain: a dome, perhaps, or a spiral, its surface covered in a pattern that repeats at scales too small to follow with your eyes. It is not ruin. It is *incomplete*. As if its builders left mid-thought, or mid-*breath*, and the hollow has been slowly, patiently, *finishing* their work by wearing it into the stone.

The tech is beyond. 2.6 — and you feel it in the *intention* of these remnants. The dome is not architecture. It is *engineering* — of something. It manages the water, perhaps. The weeping follows paths that seem *chosen*, directed, *optimized*. The basins hold moisture in configurations that serve no purpose you can name, yet you feel their *purposefulness*. The hollow is a machine that has outlived its operators, its instructions, its *meaning*. It continues to run on principles that current physics cannot fully describe. The water hangs in spheres because the hollow *wants* it to. The light is sourceless because the hollow *generates* it, from processes in the stone, in the water, in the slow chemistry of ancient erosion. It is post-modern in the truest sense: a technology that has become *ecology*, indistinguishable from the living systems it once served.

**Vegetation.** 2.5 — lush, but *strange*. The plants here do not photosynthesize. There is no light for it. They feed on the weeping water, on the minerals it carries, on the *resonance* itself — you can see it in their slow movements, their *pulsing*, their reaching toward the sources of vibration. They grow in the places where the rock is most worn, where the erosion has created micro-climates of moisture and mineral flow. They are soft. They are *warm*. They brush against your skin as you pass, and you feel their *attention*, their slow *turning* toward your heat, your movement, your *difference*. They are old. Older than the fauna, perhaps. They have been here since before the hollow was hollow, growing through the wearing-down, *participating* in it, their roots finding purchase in stone that yields.

They do not flower. They *fruit* — if that is the word — in slow cycles you will not live to see complete. The fruit is a sphere of moisture held in a membrane of living tissue, identical in appearance to the weeping water's own spheres, and you cannot tell where one ends and the other begins. The fauna eat them. So do you, if you are hungry. They taste of warmth and stone and something *almost* like memory.

**Weirdness.** 0.9 — unusual, but not *hostile*. The hollow does not *care* about you. This is the strangest thing, and you will come to rely on it. The fauna investigate you and lose interest. The water weeps on and through you, indifferent. The light reveals and conceals in equal measure. The resonance *notices* you — you feel it *pause*, *attend*, *consider* — and then continues its slow rhythm, unchanged. There is no malice here. There is no welcome. There is only the *continuation* of processes that do not require your participation, your understanding, or your survival.

**The rules of this place**, as you slowly learn them:

*Time moves differently.* Not faster, not slower — *differently*. You will experience hours as minutes, minutes as seconds, in the way of deep water, of deep stone, of processes that do not hurry. The hollow has been here for longer than the concept of hurrying. You will adapt, or you will leave. There is no shame in leaving. The hollow does not *want* you to stay. It simply *continues*, with or without your witness.

*The water is not for drinking.* It will not harm you. It will not *help* you. It is *occupied* — with minerals, with memory, with the slow chemistry of the hollow's endless weeping. Your body will process it as water. Your *something else* — the part that notices the resonance, that dreams of sourceless light — will process it as *information*. You will see things in the dark that have no names.

*The boundaries are real.* The edges of the hollow, where the light thins and the wet dark becomes *pressing* — do not cross them. The fauna do not. The vegetation does not grow there. You will feel, if you approach, a *wrongness* that is not fear but *recognition*: this is where the hollow *ends*, and what is beyond is not *outside* but *other*, a different order of wet and dark and slow time that the hollow has chosen, over its long existence, to *not-be*. You will turn back. You would turn back. The hollow does not need to *make* you.

*The structures are not for entering.* The dome, the spiral, the geometric depressions — they are *finished* in their incompleteness, *whole* in their unfinished state. To enter them would be to *interrupt* a process that has outlasted purpose. You may touch their surfaces. You may feel their *intention*. You may not — you *cannot* — go inside. The water will not permit it. The stone will not yield. The resonance will *rise*, a tone you feel in your bones, and you will *understand*, without words, that this is *not* a door. This was never a door.

**What you will do here**, if you stay: You will walk the circumference, slowly, feeling the variations in the weeping, the temperature of the stone, the *mood* of the resonance. You will learn the paths the fauna use, the places where the vegetation pulses most strongly, the single spot near the center where the emptiness is *densest* and you can stand in silence — true silence, the absence of even the weeping — and feel the hollow *breathe* around you. You will dream, if you sleep. The dreams will be of water, of stone, of light without source, of *being held* by something that does not know it holds you. You will wake wet. You will wake *changed*, in small ways, in ways that accumulate.

**What you will leave behind:** Nothing. The hollow takes nothing and gives nothing that is *yours*. The moisture on your skin is the hollow's. The warmth in your body is the hollow's. The dreams are the hollow's. You will carry them out — if you leave — as you carry all such things, as *memory*, as *tendency*, as the slow shift in how you move through dark places, through wet places, through *hollows* in whatever world you return to.

The hollow does not *miss* you. It does not *remember* you. It continues its weeping, its resonance, its slow ancient erosion wearing purpose into stone, into water, into the *shape* of a place that is small and empty and full of life and light that has no source and time that moves like water moving through stone that drinks.

It is still there. It will be there. You descended. You saw. You *were seen*, by something that does not see, in a light that does not shine, in a darkness that does not hide.

The hollow breathes. You felt it. That is enough. That was always enough.
