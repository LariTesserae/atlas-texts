I need to hold these parameters together — darkness and industrial, yet no built structures. Vast and unchanging, yet young. Dense and total vegetation with no fauna. Wet, warm, low, silent. Let me find the place that contains all of this.

---

The **Vat** does not have a surface. What you would call ground is only the top of a membrane, a skin of living material so thick with generations of itself that it has forgotten where it ends and where it begins. Below that skin — and the skin extends for kilometers in every direction, for this is a continent of growth, a single organism larger than weather — there is water. Always water. It moves through the Vat in slow pulses, not rivers but *capillaries*, vast and branching and warm, carrying dissolved minerals from depths where no light has ever reached and no darkness has ever been felt as absence.

The Vat is young. You can tell because the growth is still *urgent*. Sharp-edged fronds of cellulose rise in places where the membrane has recently split and sealed, where the organism has grown too fast and torn itself and healed over with scar-tissue that is greener, denser, more *insistent* than the surrounding mass. These young zones are the only places where the Vat breathes. Everywhere else, it is sealed. Intact. Pressurized.

There is no sky above the Vat, or rather, the sky is *inside* it. The upper layers of the growth have formed a canopy so complete, so many times folded and refolded, that it has become a ceiling. Light does not reach the interior. What little illumination exists comes from within — bioluminescence, but not the gentle pulse of forest-floor decay. This is *industrial* light. It is the color of furnaces seen through water. It flickers in patterns that are almost regular, almost mechanical, though nothing mechanical has ever existed here and nothing ever will. The Vat grows toward its own luminous metabolism, and the light is waste product, excess, the glow of a body too large to cool efficiently.

The sound is not silence, exactly. It is the *absence* of sound, which is different. You can hear your own blood. You can hear the Vat pressing against itself, the slow tectonic argument of one layer of growth against another, millimeters per century, but *continuous*, never stopping, never changing, the most stable thing imaginable. There is no wind because there is no open air. There is no animal because the Vat has not yet produced a niche that wants one, or because it consumed the possibility so early in its formation that nothing else could evolve into the space. The ecology is *pure*. Plant as substrate, plant as architecture, plant as weather, plant as the only verb in a language with no nouns.

And yet it is *mechanized* in its effects. The Vat processes. It takes water from below and lifts it through vascular structures the size of rivers, structures that have been growing for so long they have become *geology*, but geology that is still hungry, still pulling, still converting. There are flows within the flows. Pressure differentials that drive circulation without any pump, without any engine, yet the result is indistinguishable from industry: mass movement, transformation, the conversion of raw material into structure and waste heat and more growth. The Vat is a factory that built itself, that builds itself, that will continue to build itself until there is nothing left to build from, and it does not know this, and it does not care, and it has no time for caring because it is *permanent*.

The moisture is everywhere. It condenses on the underside of every layer, every fold, every overhang of the living canopy. It drips. It pools in the low places — and everywhere is low, because the Vat is flat, has always been flat, a continent that rose from warm shallows and spread horizontally because there was no reason to do otherwise, no gravity strong enough to make it reach upward, no sun to pull it toward. The water table is the Vat itself. You cannot walk on it because there is no *on*. You would sink into growth that is still growing, that would close around your passage and forget you ever existed, would incorporate your water into its own slow circulation, would add your mass to its endless, patient, *industrial* expansion.

It is warm. The interior of the Vat maintains its own temperature, a fever-heat that never drops, that never rises, that is simply *the* temperature, the only temperature this place has ever known. The water is warm. The growth is warm. The light is warm in a way that has nothing to do with color — you feel it in your bones, in the water in your bones, as if the Vat were trying to hatch something from you, as if you were an egg it had not finished deciding about.

Time does not pass here. The young zones heal. The old zones thicken. The processes continue at rates that make the changes invisible, make the landscape *eternal* in its particular configuration. A human standing in the Vat would see no change in a year, in a decade, in a century. The silence would deepen. The warmth would become indistinguishable from the body's own heat. The light would become the only landmark, shifting in its industrial flicker while everything else remains, remains, *remains*.

But it is not dead. It is the opposite of dead. It is so alive that it has become *system*, so alive that individual growth has become indistinguishable from geological process, from weather, from the slow turning of a world. The Vat does not know it is strange. It is simply *what is*. The strangeness is in the observer, in the small, brief, *animal* thing that cannot find the edges of the place it stands in, cannot find the sky, cannot find the ground, can only find more Vat, endless, warm, wet, dimly glowing, *processing*, always processing, forever young and forever finished, a continent that is also a body that is also a machine that needs no maker because it made itself from the only materials available: water, warmth, time, and the infinite patience of growth that has no purpose except to continue.
