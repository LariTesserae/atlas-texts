I need to find a place that holds all these parameters together — extreme elevation, ancient erosion, lush vegetation, yet nearly empty and turbulent. The tension is the engine here.

---

**The Throat**

You stand where the world has been worn down to its vocal cords.

Elevation first: you are *up*. Not mountain-up, not the sharp drama of peaks. Up in the way that old things are up — the Tibetan plateau sense of altitude, the Andean altiplano, but higher still, pressed against a sky so thin it barely holds color. The air is warm, impossibly warm for this height, because the Throat breathes. Geothermal. The mountain is not dead; it is *panting*. Steam rises from rifts in the stone in slow, regular pulses, and you learn to time your steps between them.

The erosion is the deeper story. This is not a young range with ambition. This is a range that *finished*. Two billion years of water — but water only 1.2, intermittent, obsessive rather than abundant — has carved this place into something like a mandible. Valleys that were once fjords, once glacial, now hold only the *memory* of depth. The slopes are near-flattened at impossible angles, forty degrees of incline that should have collapsed but haven't, held by some mineral patience, some cement of ancient microbes that ate the stone and excreted stability. The rock is *pre-sedimentary*. It predates the categories we use. You will not find fossils. The Throat ate them, digested them, exhaled the chemistry into new forms.

And yet: vegetation 2.3. Lush. This is where the parameters argue, and the place wins through *weirdness*.

The plants here do not grow in soil. There is almost no soil — the erosion took it, or it was never made, the mountain rising from mantle directly, skipping the crustal middle-management. Instead, the lushness lives in *suspension*. Great curtains of filamentous growth — not quite moss, not quite lichen, something older than both, colonial organisms that communicate through mineral conductivity — drape from every surface that holds still long enough. They drink from the condensation, from the steam-vents, from the occasional rain that arrives sideways, horizontal, a weather system that has forgotten how to fall. The color is wrong. Not green, exactly, but *green-adjacent*: chartreuse, verdigris, the green of old copper, the green of bruises healing. In the steam-light, which is constant and diffuse, they glow faintly. Bioluminescence, or something like it. The Throat is warm, and warmth at this altitude, in this thinness, produces growth strategies that look like desperation and function like genius.

The density is 0.5. Empty. You can see for kilometers in some directions, across basins that hold nothing but their own flattened echoes, and then the land rises again to another rim, another lip of the mandible, and the emptiness continues. This is not desert-empty. Desert implies heat, implies sand, implies a logic of dryness. This is *hollowed*. The Throat has been mined by something — not human, not any animal you'd recognize — and the mining left chambers, galleries, vast internal spaces that open occasionally as sinkholes, as steam-vents widened into maws, as the occasional collapse that reveals, for a moment, a darkness with structure in it. You hear the emptiness. It has a *size*.

Fauna is rare, but not absent. You will see, perhaps once in a day of walking, something *cross* a ridge — a silhouette that resolves into: too many legs, or too few, or legs that are not legs but extensions of a body that has flattened itself against the wind-scoured stone. They do not approach. They are not curious. They are *passing through*, following the steam, following the mineral veins that the plants drink from, following the deep warmth that makes this altitude survivable. When they move, they make no sound. The Throat has taught them silence.

Because the sound is elsewhere.

Sound: 2.7. Deafening. The Throat *sings*. Not metaphorically. The steam vents are tuned — not by design, not by any intelligence, but by the physics of their formation, the particular diameters and depths of the channels, the temperature gradients — and they produce tones. Low tones. Infrasonic mostly, felt in the sternum, in the teeth, in the fluid of the inner ear. But occasionally, when the thermal gradient shifts, when a new fissure opens or an old one seals, the tuning changes and you hear *overtones*. Harmonics. The mountain has a *voice*, and it is not singing for you. It is singing because pressure must escape, and the only way out is through resonance, through the making of sound that is as much structural necessity as it is accident. You will find yourself humming. You will not remember starting. The locals — the rare fauna, the older growths, the things that have lived here long enough to adapt — have all learned to *resonate*. Their bodies have become instruments. A beetle that drums its abdomen against a stone to match the frequency of a nearby vent, and in matching it, *opens* the vent slightly, releases a puff of mineral-rich steam, feeds. Symbiosis through acoustics. The Throat is an instrument, and everything that lives here is learning to play it.

This is what makes the dynamic turbulent. Nothing is stable. The steam vents migrate. The condensation patterns shift with thermal tides. The plants, in their mineral-conductive networks, are *negotiating* constantly — not thought, not intention, but chemical consensus, a democracy of ions that shifts the growth-patterns of the curtains hour by hour. A new vent opens. The local ecology *reorganizes* around it within a day. The sound changes. The light changes (the steam carries phosphorescent minerals, and when they precipitate, they glow, faintly, in the diffuse dark of a Throat-night that is not quite night because the vents never fully cool). The erosion continues, but *differently* now — not the patient grinding of before, but *accelerated*, because the thermal cycling is breaking the stone from within, and the ancient stability is becoming something else, something *young*, something that will take another two billion years to settle again, or will collapse, or will become a different kind of mountain entirely. You cannot predict which. The Throat is in *becoming*, and becoming is the only permanence.

The built is 0.6. Scattered. You will find them if you look for the *wrong* things. Not ruins — the Throat predates anything that could leave ruins in the conventional sense. But *arrangements*. Stones set in patterns that are too regular, too *intentional*, on a ridge where no stone should naturally rest. A cairn that is not a cairn, that is something else, a marker or a machine or a memory. Occasionally, in the steam-vents themselves, you will find *deposits* — not built, but *grown*, mineral accretions that have formed around some core, some seed of metal or ceramic or something organic that the vent has cooked and encased and partially digested. The tech is primitive because the *need* for tech is different here. Tools are made of the same mineral-ceramic that the vents produce, chipped and ground by hand, used to *tune* the vents, to *encourage* particular resonances, to harvest the phosphorescent deposits without being burned. It is not industry. It is *gardening*. The people — if there are people, if the scattered fauna includes something that remembers being human, or something that was never human but has learned to *be* human-shaped — do not build cities. They build *relationships* with the vents. They move with the thermal tides. They are as temporary as the condensation, as persistent as the erosion.

And you? You are passing through. The Throat does not want you, does not reject you. It is *indifferent* in the way that deep time is indifferent — you are a brief turbulence in a system that has already forgotten harder turbulences. But you will *change*. The sound will get into your bones. The warmth will make you sweat in air that should be freezing. The lushness will seem obscene, *wrong*, a fecundity without the usual justifications of soil and rain and the slow accumulation of generations. And then, if you stay long enough, if you learn to *resonate*, you will find that the emptiness is not absence but *potential* — the hollow where the next vent will open, the silence between the tones where something else might be heard, the space where the next form of life is already negotiating its conditions of existence.

The Throat is old. The Throat is *becoming*. It has been both, simultaneously, for longer than the categories hold. And it will continue, singing its deafening song, growing its impossible green, wearing down its own stone into new shapes, until the warmth finally fails, or finally *transforms*, and the resonance shifts to a frequency that no living thing can match.

You cannot stay. But you will not forget the humming.
