I think this is a **condenser lung**.

Not a metaphor — I mean it literally, or as literally as the strange allows. A low, warm depression in young, sharp terrain, where the earth has cracked open recently and something has grown down into the crack, something industrial, something that breathes.

The terrain: a flat-bottomed fissure, maybe three hundred meters across at its widest, walled by basalt that still holds the heat of its formation. The walls are *new* — you can see where the rock is still glassy in places, where the cooling has made it fracture into plates that stand at angles like broken teeth. The floor is lower than the surrounding country by perhaps fifteen meters. Warm air rises from it in visible shimmers. The elevation parameter is low — this is near sea level or a river plain, somewhere the water table is high but the rain is sparse, so the damp comes from *below* and from *within*.

Water: condensation, mostly. The walls sweat. At dawn, the basalt plates are wet with it, and the wetness runs down into a shallow pool at the lowest point — never more than knee-deep, never more than a few square meters, but always there, always replenishing, always slightly warm. "Intermittent" in the sense that the sweating follows a cycle: strongest in the cool morning, ceasing by midday when the sun hits the floor directly, then returning as the walls cool again. There are also *pockets* — hollows in the fractured floor where water collects and stays, stagnant and warm and green with something.

Vegetation: sparse, but *persistent*. Things that live in the condensation line, in the thin film of moisture on the rock. Lichens in sheets, pale gray-green, that crumble underfoot. The weirdness: let me sharpen it.
