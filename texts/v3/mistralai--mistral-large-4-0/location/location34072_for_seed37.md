I want to find where this place lives before I describe it. Below surface, but bright. Cool, but with abundant fauna. Sparse vegetation yet active ecosystem. These tensions are the place itself — let me let them pull.

**The Throat of a Forgetting Mountain**

You descend through a wound in the world, and the mountain does not close behind you. It *cannot*. The rock here is young — geologically infant, still deciding what it wants to be. Erosion has not carved so much as *suggested*: channels that might become canyons, hollows that remember being deeper, stone that weeps without knowing why.

The light comes from everywhere and nowhere. Above, the real sky exists — I think — but here, illumination is *internal*. The rock itself is luminous, not glowing, exactly, but *lit from within*, as if the mountain is still molten at some depth and has not yet learned to hide it. Phosphorescent colonies crust the walls in fractal patterns, pale blue-white, and where water moves, they flare brighter, as if the stone were *happy* to be touched.

And water moves. Not much — a parameter of damp, not drowned. Condensation weeps from ceiling to floor in slow, patient architecture. Pockets of it collect in basins that may be natural or may be *remembering* being natural — the distinction has not yet been settled. When you disturb these pools, something rises. Not quite animals. The fauna here is abundant but *wrong-abundant*: pale, translucent, many-segmented things that move in coordinated hesitation, that gather in the warm places and dissolve when light falls too directly upon them. They do not eat what you would recognize as food. They seem to eat *absence* — the silence between sounds, the space between your thoughts when you stop moving. They are the reason the place stays quiet. Sound is a parameter of quiet here, not emptiness — there is always the drip, the settling stone, the faint *chiming* of mineral growth, like ice thinking.

The structures are scattered, partial, *argued-over*. Some are clearly built — small, domed, made of a material that might be compressed fungus or might be the mountain's own extruded dream-stuff. Others are *ambiguously* built: hollows that wear their edges too smooth, alignments of stone that suggest intention without confirming it. The builders, if builders there were, left no tools. Tech is none — and yet the structures persist, maintained by processes no one witnesses. The mountain grows around them. The mountain grows *through* them. They are becoming geology at the same rate geology is becoming architecture.

Vegetation is sparse but *insistent*. Where water touches light, something grows — not plants, not quite, but *analogues*: frond-structures that photosynthesize the internal glow rather than any sun, that orient themselves to thermal gradients, that reproduce by *fragmenting under stress*, each piece a new individual arguing for its own territory. They do not form forests. They form *negotiations*. A single frond, a colony, a negotiation that has not yet concluded.

The coolness is absolute and yet the fauna thrives in it. They are cold-blooded in a cold that should freeze them, and yet they move, they gather, they *watch*. They do not seem to see you, exactly. They seem to see *the shape of your attention*. When you focus on one, it will shift its pattern, not to hide, but to *test* — are you perceiving accurately? Are you a threat? Are you *real*?

And reality is the final parameter, broken almost to the point of narrative. The spaces here do not obey consistent geometry. You can walk in one direction and arrive somewhere that was, moments ago, behind you. The mountain is *dreaming itself into being*, and dreams do not maintain continuity. The erosion is not finished. New passages open while you watch — not violently, but *politely*, as if the rock is apologetic for the inconvenience. Old passages close, sealing with a soft mineral sigh, and if you listen closely, you can hear whatever was inside *continuing*, just no longer accessible to you.

The scale is a pocket. A room, perhaps, that happens to be a mountain's indigestion. You could spend a lifetime here and never map it. You could spend an hour and emerge weeping, or laughing, or *hungry in a way you cannot name*.

The silence is not empty. It is *occupied* — by the negotiation of stone and water, by the patient metabolism of things that grow without sun, by the abundant fauna that make no sound but whose presence *presses* against your ears like depth underwater. When you speak, your voice does not echo. It is *absorbed*, and later, when you have left, or when you have stopped moving, you will hear it again — not as memory, but as *the mountain's imitation of you*, practicing being human.

This place is being born. It does not know if it will be a cave, a canyon, a cathedral, or a grave. It is all of these, potential and simultaneous, and the light from within illuminates not what is, but what *might still become*.

You are inside its becoming. You are part of its erosion, its damp, its sparse and stubborn and *abundant* life.

The mountain is still deciding whether to keep you.
