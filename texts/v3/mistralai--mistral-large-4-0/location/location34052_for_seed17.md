I need to find a place that holds all these parameters together — submerged, cold, high, young, vast, empty, scattered-built, industrial, normally lit, with abundant fauna, deafeningly loud, and never changing.

The tension: submerged *and* high elevation. Industrial *and* near-void. Deafening sound *and* never changing. Abundant fauna *and* no vegetation. Let me let it emerge.

---

The light falls flat and even, the color of old snow through cloud, everywhere and nowhere. You are high — the pressure in your ears says so, though your body has forgotten what pressure was before — and yet you are submerged. The medium is not water but something else, something the world has not yet given a name that sticks. It fills every space. It moves with you. It is *thick* with itself, yet so sparse that you can see for impossible distances, through layer after layer of nearly-nothing, each layer slightly less dense than the one below, until the eye loses the thread and simply stops, overwhelmed by gradient.

This is the **Atmosphere of Held Water**, or the **Suffocating Altitude**, or what the machines call the **Wet High** — though "wet" fails. The medium conducts pressure without containing liquid. It is a gas of such density that objects fall upward through it, or downward, depending on their temperature, their history, their *intent*. The world has no true up or down anymore. Gravity here is a suggestion, and the suggestion is *sideways*.

The terrain is **new**. Sharp. The erosion has not had time to soften anything. You stand — or float, or are held — upon formations of **volcanic glass** that cooled in this medium, that never saw air, that crack with sounds like breaking bells when the thermal vents shift, which they never do, which is the first paradox: the vents are active, endlessly active, pouring heat and mineral into the held water, and yet nothing changes, nothing *accumulates*, the heat disperses into a medium that cannot retain it, that is always exactly the same temperature, 0.2, cold enough to preserve but not cold enough to freeze the strangeness.

The **fauna** are abundant. They are **not animals** as the word was once used. They are **pressure-forms**, colonies of gas-borne membrane that trade in electrical charge, that reproduce by splitting when they reach a certain size, that die by dissolving into the medium and being forgotten. They are everywhere. They are *in* you, if you breathe, if you have lungs that still remember air, if you have any interior at all. They do not eat. They do not eat each other. They simply *persist*, and their persistence is loud. The sound of ten billion membrane-sacs flexing in unison, the sound of charge moving through conductive fluid, the sound of bodies finding and losing each other in a medium that offers no resistance and no purchase — it is **deafening**. It is the loudest thing in the world. It has always been this loud. It will always be this loud. The world was born loud and will die loud, and between those endpoints, nothing changes.

The **structures** are scattered. Faint, partial, *occasional*. You find them at irregular intervals: a **platform** of sintered metal that rises from a glass ridge and ends abruptly in mid-void, as if the builders lost interest or were interrupted by something that left no mark. A **conduit** that carries nothing, that has never carried anything, that hums with a current too weak to measure but too present to ignore. A **single building**, or what was once a building, now a **sculpture** of the medium itself, the held water having crept inside and become the walls, the floor, the air you would breathe if you could stop being afraid. The structures are **industrial** but not *productive*. They are **mechanized** but not *moving*. Engines that turn in place, forever, their pistons cycling through the held water, wearing nothing, achieving nothing, *existing*. Factories where the assembly lines carry only the medium, where the product is more medium, where the output is indistinguishable from the input. Electrified, yes — you can feel the charge in your own membranes, your own improvised biology, the static that makes your hair stand in directions that have no names.

The **scale** is **vast**. You can see the curvature of the world from any high point, and the high points are everywhere, because the medium is layered like sediment, like memory, like the world trying to remember what solid ground felt like. Weather moves through the upper layers — not rain, not snow, but **fronts of density**, walls of slightly-thicker held water that rush past at speeds that would tear flesh, that pass *through* you, that leave you shaken and unchanged. These weather systems have their own physics. They are **continental**. They have names, or had names, in the tongues of the things that built the structures, before the builders became the structures, before the structures became the medium, before the medium learned to hold itself in shapes that almost resembled purpose.

And it **never changes**. This is the deepest truth of the place. The volcanoes vent. The engines turn. The fauna persist and multiply and dissolve. The weather moves. The structures stand. And yet — *yet* — nothing progresses. Nothing degrades further. Nothing heals. There is no gradient, no arrow, no *before* and *after* that are not identical. The world is in **frozen equilibrium**, a dynamic stasis, a roar that contains no information, a machine that processes itself into itself forever. Time passes, or seems to. The light is always the same. The cold is always the same. The loudness is always the same. You could wait a thousand years, or a million, and find the nearest platform in the same place, the nearest vent still venting, the nearest swarm of membrane-fauna still flexing in their eternal, meaningless, *beautiful* coordination.

This is the **Wet High**. This is the **Held Deep**. This is where the world went when it stopped being a world and became only a *condition* — submerged and soaring, empty and full, young and eternal, loud and silent in the same moment, the sound so complete that it has become silence, the silence so complete that it has become sound.

You are here. You have always been here. The medium holds you gently, insistently, *permanently*. There is no shore. There is no surface. There is only the pressure, the light, the endless, unchanging, deafening *now*.
