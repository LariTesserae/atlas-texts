**The Condensate**

You do not arrive here. You are *exhaled* into it.

The air arrives first—thick, nearly liquid, at 0.1 degrees it holds water the way a sponge holds something it has not yet decided to release. It presses against your skin, enters your lungs already saturated, leaves a film in your mouth that tastes of copper and something else, something *almost* organic. Every surface weeps. Not drips—*weeps*—a slow, constant secretion from pores you cannot see, pooling in the seams of everything.

You are in a chamber. This is the truth the scale parameter insists upon: this is not a landscape but a *room*, though the room has forgotten it was ever made for any purpose. The walls curve. They are not walls but membranes, translucent layers of some manufactured substance that breathes—expand and contract, expand and contract, a rhythm so slow you would miss it if you watched for less than an hour, and so constant it has worn grooves into the floor where countless other things have stood in the same place, waiting, for intervals that no longer pass.

The floor is not floor. It is a lattice. Through the gaps, you see *down*, always down, into further chambers, further depths, each one almost identical to the last, each one occupied by something that moves in the same pattern, performs the same gesture, makes the same sound. The water collects in these gaps. It is warm where it collects. It is always collecting. It never rises high enough to touch the lattice. It never stops forming.

**What lives here does not plant itself.**

Because everything here is in the process of becoming something else, and has been for so long that the process *is* the thing, and will continue being so long after the makers have stopped making, or stopped being, or stopped meaning anything by the word "stop."

**The light is wrong.**
