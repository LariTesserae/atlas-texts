**The Lithophyll**

You stand on what you first mistake for stone, then as wood, then as neither. The ground breathes. Not in any state you would recognize. The chemistry runs on dissolved minerals in supercritical gas, on electrochemical gradients across membranes that have never known H₂O, on a solvent of pressurized ammonia-methane clathrates that behaves, in certain lights, like honey and in others like glass.

The Lithophyll are old. Older than the erosion that has worn this place. The erosion parameter — 2.6, ancient, primordial — refers to the *mountains* that once stood here, now ground down to a plain so vast it curves with the planet's forgotten geometry. What remains of elevation is *built*: the 2.4 is not geological anymore. The high places are grown, or were grown, or were grown-into-being by something that thinks in centuries the way you think in breaths.

Cool. 0.6. The temperature sits in a narrow band where the ammonia-methane solvent stays liquid, where the great fronds of the lithophyll-mats can maintain their turgor without boiling or freezing, where the fauna — abundant, 1.8 — can move without spending everything on thermoregulation. You feel this cool in your borrowed lungs, which process the thick atmosphere through filters that taste of copper and something else, something that might be memory.

**The Vegetation**

Vegetation is 3.0. Total. Plant-as-substrate, plant-as-architecture, all-living-mass.

Everything that is solid was once growing, or is growing, or is the *intention* of growth made temporarily stable. The lithophyll-mats — call them that, for now — cover the plain in patches that the density parameter (0.8, sparse) says should not cover anything. They cover anyway. Between the mats: *space*. Each mat is a city of biological construction. Towering structures — 2.4 elevation, remember, and most of it organic — rise from the plain like inverted roots, like frozen lightning, like the ribcages of creatures that never died but *became* the weather. The oldest parts are *mineralized*, indistinguishable from stone, but still vascular, still *dimly* alive, still participating in the slow circulation of nutrients through channels no wider than your finger.

The mats are not single organisms. They are *collaborations*. Thousands of species of lithophyll, each specialized, each dependent, each contributing to a whole that thinks — no, that *processes*, that *resolves*, that has opinions about where you are standing and whether you should stay.

**The Fauna**

Abundant. 1.8.

They move through the mats in three dimensions. The ground-dwellers are the slow ones: segmented, many-legged, *patient*, feeding on the chemical gradients that the lithophyll excrete and reabsorb in cycles older than the mountains. The flyers are the loud ones.

The flyers are abundant but *sparse* — the density parameter again. Dim. 1.0.

**The Rules**

Here is what you learn, standing on the breathing ground, listening to the loud silence of the Lithophyll:

*Water is not necessary.* Life — this life, this *strange* life — has found other solvents, other chemistries, other ways of being *wet* without being water. The absence of water is not a limitation. It is a *choice*, made by evolution, made by physics, made by the *place* itself in its long slow aging.

*Growth is not slow.* Not here. The lithophyll grow in *pulses*, in *responses*, in *negotiations* with the weather and the fauna and the ancient machines. A tower that took centuries to raise can be *reconfigured* in days, if the conditions are right, if the *song* is sung correctly.

*Sound is not secondary.* It is *primary*. The lithophyll *hear*. The fauna *speak*. The weather — dynamic, active, multiple processes, cycles of pressure and temperature and chemistry — *sings* to itself in frequencies that shape the growth, the movement, the *evolution* of everything that lives here.

*Erosion is not ending.* It is *settling*. The mountains are gone, yes, worn down by time and chemistry and the patient work of the lithophyll themselves. But the *settlement* is not a death. It is a *form*. The oldest stable state. The end of one kind of change, the beginning of another, *deeper*, *slower*, *stranger*.

*The built is not separate from the grown.* It never was. The distinction is a *category error*, a human need for *clarity* in a place that *refuses* it.

*The technology is not human.* It never was. It is *lithophyll*, it is *fauna*, it is *weather*, it is *time* — all of them, *negotiating*, *collaborating*, *becoming* something that the word "technology" can only *point at*, never *hold*.
