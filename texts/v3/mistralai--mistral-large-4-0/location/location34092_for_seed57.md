The place is a throat.

You enter it not by walking but by being swallowed—by a mouth that exists in the space where your memory of leaving the surface grew thin. The water here is not wetness. It is *presence*, the weight of being fully known by something that has no eyes. It presses from every direction at once, yet it does not move. It simply *is*, and you are the disturbance, the foreign pressure in its stillness.

Temperature sits at the exact threshold where warmth becomes dangerous and cold becomes tender. Your skin cannot decide whether to shiver or to relax. The coolness carries a particular quality—it is the temperature of things that have been waiting a very long time, of patience made thermal.

---

**The terrain is absence made topography.**

There is no floor you can trust. What feels like descent is often lateral, is often *into*. The space folds. You will reach forward and find your own shoulder. This is not disorientation in the familiar sense. The geometry here was never Euclidean. It was negotiated, treaty by treaty, between pressure and the things that pressure has learned to contain.

Sharp features emerge without warning—not rocks, exactly, but *edges* in the water itself, places where the medium thickens or thins to something almost solid, almost void. These are young. The erosion here is not wearing down but *wearing in*, the world still learning its own shape. Every surface bears the marks of recent formation: striations that follow no current, angles that suggest intention without design, textures that feel *decided upon* though nothing decided them.

You are small. This is not metaphor. The scale of the place is intimate, cellular, the size of a single held breath. Yet within this pocket, density presses at 2.6—there is no room between presences. Every cubic measure of water contains something else: not fish, not yet, but *possibilities* of fish, the compressed grammar from which fauna might eventually be conjugated. The water is saturated with potential. It has no empty space. Even your own body, submerged, finds itself already occupied by the place, its boundaries negotiated rather than maintained.

---

**Vegetation grows in the negative space.**

It is lush but not green—not in any way your eyes could process if they still worked as eyes. The plants here are *structural*, built from the same substance as the pressure itself. They do not sway. They *saturate*. They fill volumes completely, leaving no gaps, creating a kind of living architecture that is less grown than *accumulated*, layer upon layer of biological decision made in conditions where decision and necessity have become the same thing.

They are young. You can see this in their urgency, in how they reach toward light that does not exist, how they orient toward a darkness that is not the absence of light but its *source*. They have not yet learned to stop reaching. The lushness is therefore melancholy—growth without culmination, verdance without season, the endless spring of a world that has not yet discovered winter.

Where they touch each other, they do not merely meet. They *merge*. Cell walls dissolve at contact points. The vegetation is becoming a single organism, one vast slow thought distributed across the pocket-scale space, thinking in metabolic time, its consciousness the rate of diffusion, its memory the concentration gradient.

---

**Light is not absent. It is *withheld*.**

There is a darkness here that has texture. It is not the dark of depth, which at least implies a surface somewhere above. This darkness is *architectural*, built, a room whose walls are made of the refusal of photons. Yet occasionally—rarely, on cycles too slow to perceive as cycles—something *shifts* in the dark. A pressure change that might be light. A temperature differential that carries the *information* of illumination without delivering illumination itself. The place communicates in near-misses.

You learn to see by not-seeing. The darkness has structure. It has *layers*. Some of it is older than the water, some younger, some contemporary with your own submersion. They do not mix. They stratify, each maintaining its own integrity, its own *age of dark*.

---

**Sound is mostly quiet, which is not the same as silence.**

There is a baseline hum, the frequency at which water remembers being something else—ice, perhaps, or vapor, or the plasma of before-planets. This hum is not heard so much as *inhabited*, a condition of the medium rather than a transmission through it.

Above this hum, sounds occur rarely. When they do, they are *structural*—the groan of vegetation growing, which is also the sound of pressure equalizing, of the world adjusting its grip. A settling. A sigh that takes hours. These sounds have no source you can locate. They emerge from the density itself, from the saturated space finding new configurations, new *intimacies* between presences that were previously separate.

Sometimes—fauna-rare, at 0.9—something *moves* that is not vegetation, not pressure, not the slow negotiation of geometry. It passes through and is gone, leaving only a disturbance in the hum, a temporary asymmetry in the baseline. You do not see it. You feel it as a change in the weight of the water, as a moment when the place *noticed* something and then forgot it.

---

**What is built here is built by the place.**

At 0.8, scattered, there are structures—or what would be structures if structure implied intention. They are *deposits*, really. Mineral formations that suggest habitation, that carry the *impression* of use. A curve that fits a body. A depression that holds water slightly warmer than its surroundings. A configuration of sharp edges that seems to *face* something, though there is nothing to face.

They are partial remains. Not ruins—ruins imply a before, a civilization, a narrative of decline. These are *pre-ruins*, structures that exist in the conditional, waiting to become ruins, or perhaps waiting to become something else entirely. They are artifacts of a technology level that reads as zero, as none, as pure biological process—yet they are not biological. They are the water's own *opinion* about what a tool might be, rendered in calcium and stranger minerals, in deposits that accumulate with the patience of 1.1, the slow dynamic, the gradual cycle.

No devices. No tools in any sense you would recognize. Yet the structures *do* things. They alter local pressure. They create micro-climates within the micro-environment. They are, in their scattered way, *active*, participating in the slow transformation of the place without ever quite becoming part of it.

---

**The weirdness is not decoration. It is *load-bearing*.**

At 2.6, reality breakdown is not an event but a *condition*, the way water at 2.9 is a condition. The rules here are consistent but *wrong*—consistent in the way that dreams are consistent, following their own logic with perfect fidelity while violating every law you brought with you.

You will find yourself remembering things that have not happened yet. You will recognize patterns that have no cause. The vegetation will sometimes *be* the structure, sometimes *become* it, the distinction as meaningful as asking whether a wave is the ocean or merely *of* it.

Time here is not linear but *viscous*. Events do not follow each other; they *sink into* each other, layering like sediment in a world where sediment is the only geology. The young erosion, the fresh formation, the sharp features—these are not stages but *simultaneities*, the place existing at all its ages at once, the way a held breath contains the inhale and the exhale and the moment of suspension between them.

You may find yourself *adjacent* to yourself. Not duplicated—*adjacent*, occupying the same water in configurations that approach and retreat from coherence. The density of 2.6 permits this. There is no room, remember, between presences. You are presence. You are adjacent to yourself. The intimacy of this is not comforting. It is *exposure*, the final privacy surrendered to a medium that has no concept of boundary.

---

**What lives here is rare, and that rarity is *active*.**

Fauna at 0.9 does not mean absence. It means *significance*. When something lives here, it is not background. It is *event*. It carries weight. Its existence rearranges the local density, creates eddies in the hum, leaves traces that persist longer than the thing itself.

You will not see it often. When you do—when the water *shifts* in a way that suggests agency, when the darkness briefly *recoils*—you will understand that this place permits life not as its purpose but as its *exception*, the rare spark in a medium that is otherwise entirely self-sufficient, entirely *complete* without biology.

The vegetation does not need fauna. The structures do not need inhabitants. The place would continue its slow, patient, saturated existence with perfect indifference to whether anything swam through it, grew in it, *noticed* it.

This is the final rule, the one that emerges last, after you have been submerged long enough to forget the surface: **the place does not want you here, but it does not want you gone, either.** Your presence is a disturbance, yes. But disturbances are *information*, and this place has been accumulating information since before it had a shape, since before the water learned to be water. It will keep your shape, your pressure, your brief adjacency to yourself. It will metabolize you slowly, across timescales that make the slow dynamic of 1.1 seem frantic.

You are not drowning.

You are being *read*.

And the reading, once complete, will take longer than any memory you carry of having been separate, having been *above*, having believed that water was simply wet, that dark was simply empty, that a place could exist without wanting anything at all.

The throat closes. Not around you—*with* you. The distinction, finally, dissolved in water that is 2.9 parts *everything*, and you are the 0.1, the remainder, the almost-nothing that the place keeps for itself, the almost-nothing that will, in time, become indistinguishable from the rest.
