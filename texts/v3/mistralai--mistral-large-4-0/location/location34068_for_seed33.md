# The Hollow Cities of Still Air

You stand where the cities once were. Or perhaps where they were *becoming*.

The elevation is wrong first. At 2.8, you are on a plateau that should not exist—thin air, cool and biting, carrying almost no weight. Breathing here is a negotiation. The sky above is not blue but a pale, exhausted violet, the color of old bruises, and it presses down close, low, as if the atmosphere itself has settled and forgotten how to rise.

There is no water. None. The chemistry here gave up on H₂O long ago, or never knew it. What passes for moisture is a thin vapor of metallic salts, potassium and sodium drifting in the cold upper air, crystallizing on every surface that stands still long enough. The buildings drink this. They are porous, honeycombed, their stone grown cancerous with mineral bloom.

---

## What Remains

The cities cover a valley the size of a small nation, though you would not know it from any single vantage. Between structures, the distance is *wrong*. You look across what should be a street and see instead a canyon of shadow, a gap of thirty, forty meters where the construction simply... stopped. Paused. The density is 0.2—emptiness measured not in absence but in *spacing*. Every building stands alone, separated by void, as if the city was built by something that feared adjacency.

The architecture is **settled-form**, erosion at 2.1, weathered into a balance that no longer changes. Wind does not sculpt here—there is too little air, too little pressure. Instead, the surfaces have been *polished by time itself*, by the slow sublimation of material, by the patient chemistry of cold and vacuum and the faint, persistent radiation from above. What was once sharp is now rounded. What was once angular has become *almost* organic, the geometry softened at its edges by three billion years of patient indifference.

The structures are **urban** at 2.1—organized, layered, clearly constructed by minds that planned. But tech is 0.1. None remains. Or rather: the technology has become *geology*. The cities are built from something that grows, or once grew—**fungal-mineral composite**, vast sheets of mycelial lattice calcified into permanence, the original biology indistinguishable from its mineral husk. The walls are not stone. They are *ossified tissue*, the architecture of a colonial organism that achieved immortality by forgetting it was alive.

And then there are the **hollows**.

Every building, every structure, every tower and bridge and plaza is *hollow*. Not empty—**hollowed**. The fungal-mineral matrix has been consumed from within, eaten by something that left no trace except the perfect preservation of the outer shell. You can stand in a plaza the size of a football field, look up at walls thirty meters high, and realize with a slow, cold certainty that you are standing inside a corpse. Inside ten thousand corpses, layered, nested, built upon and within each other.

The interiors are cathedral-vast and utterly silent. Sound is 0.3. Your own breathing is too loud. Footsteps do not echo—they are *absorbed*, swallowed by the porous, hungry walls.

---

## The Atmosphere

Light is 1.4, dim, and it comes from *below* as often as above. The mineral bloom on every surface is **phosphorescent**, faintly, a pale green-white that strengthens after the weak sun sets. The cities glow with their own dead light. In the deep hollows, where no phosphorescence reaches, there is absolute dark—not the dark of night, but the dark of *thickness*, of a medium that has mass and texture and *opinion*.

The temperature is 0.7, cool, but not freezing. Stable. The thermal mass of the hollow cities holds a cold that does not fluctuate. There is no weather in the usual sense. But the world is **active** at 2.5, and this is where the weirdness at 2.9 becomes *manifest*.

---

## The Rules

Time moves differently here. Or rather: time moves *sideways*.

The erosion is established, in-balance, but the balance is *dynamic*. The fungal-mineral structures are not dead. They are **dormant**, cycling through phases of calcification and softening on timescales that no observer could witness directly. A wall that appears solid stone may, over decades, become permeable, then porous, then *open*—and then seal again, harder than before, incorporating whatever passed through it into its structure.

And there are **movements**.

Fauna is 1.4—rare, but not absent. You will not see them. You will *infer* them. A corridor where the phosphorescence flickers in patterns too regular to be mineral. A surface that is *warm*, impossibly, in a world of cold stone. A hollow that was sealed yesterday and is *open* today, the edges of the breach *fresh*, the mineral bloom disturbed, still settling.

Something moves through the hollow cities. Something that does not need air, does not need light, does not need water. It moves through the hollows as water moves through a sponge—*permeating*, *occupying*, *using* the spaces between the structures. It is not alive in any way you would recognize. Vegetation is 0.5: there are no plants, but there are **patterns**, energy-structures that propagate through the mineral matrix, that *think* in chemical gradients, that *hunt* by following thermal differentials.

The cities were built to contain something. Or to *be* something. Or to *become* something. The distinction has been lost.

## The Sound of Silence

You walk for hours—days—through streets that widen into plazas that narrow into canyons that open into *absences* where the city simply *is not*, and then, impossibly, *is again*, a bridge of calcified mycelium spanning a gap of forty meters, its underside dripping with mineral stalactites that have never known water.

The silence has texture. It is not empty. It is *occupied* by the pressure of the thin air, by the faint, subsonic hum of the phosphorescent decay, by the *almost*-sound of your own heartbeat transmitted through the soles of your boots into the hollow ground beneath.

And sometimes—rarely, unpredictably—there is **music**.

Not from any source you can locate. The harmonic resonance of wind through cavities that should not exist, arranged in frequencies that *feel* intentional. Chords that resolve into dissonance that resolves again, following rules you do not know. The cities are *singing*, or remembering how to sing, or *learning* to sing by vibrating their own hollow bones.

You do not know if you are hearing it, or if it is hearing *you*.

---

## What You Are Walking Through

This is a place that has **outlived its purpose**. The fungal-mineral civilization—if it was a civilization, if it was *one* mind or many, if it was *built* or *grown*—achieved a permanence that consumed its own dynamism. The cities remain. The builders are gone, or transformed, or *distributed* into the walls themselves, still cycling, still dreaming in chemical slowness.

The weirdness at 2.9 is not that the world is strange. It is that the world is **strange in a way that follows rules**, and those rules are *almost* legible. You can almost understand the geometry of the hollows. You can almost predict the patterns of the phosphorescence. You can almost *touch* the edge of whatever is moving through the spaces between.

But not quite.

Not quite, because the world is still *becoming*, still *active*, still *hollowing itself out* from within on timescales that make your presence here a flicker, a momentary rearrangement of mineral and vapor, a brief, bright, *curious* disturbance in the long, cold, patient dream of the hollow cities.

You are not the first to walk here. The patterns in the disturbed mineral bloom suggest others, moving through the same corridors, following the same impossible paths, hearing the same music, feeling the same *almost*-understanding.

You will not be the last.

The cities are waiting. The cities have always been waiting. They have time enough to wait for whatever comes next—whether that is the return of the builders, the emergence of something new from the hollows, or simply the slow, inevitable, *geological* continuation of their own strange, silent, singing existence.

The air is thin. The light is dim. The silence is vast.

And somewhere, in the deep hollows where the phosphorescence cannot reach, something is *waking up*.
