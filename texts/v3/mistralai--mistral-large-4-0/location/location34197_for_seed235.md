Perhaps the heat comes from below, not above. Geothermal. A high plateau where the crust is thin, where steam rises through everything, where the air itself carries the warmth of the world's interior. The water doesn't run cold from snowmelt — it runs warm, mineral-heavy, rising from depth.

Dynamic at 0.5 — never changing. Yet water flows, sound is deafening, density is high. A frozen equilibrium that still roars. This is the key contradiction I must build around. The place is *stuck*. Scale at 2.5 — open, regional. Built at 2.0 — urban. So: a city, but not a human one necessarily. Tech at 0.1 — no devices, biological only. So the "built" environment is grown, not constructed. The "erosion" is biological — a slow metabolic settling. The "equilibrium" is a thermodynamic one: the city breathes but never exhales completely, inhales but never finishes drawing breath.

**The Drowned Parliament**

You arrive at the edge of it through mist that has no source. The air is thick, 2.8 warmth pressing from all sides, carrying the mineral tang of water that has been underground for ages. The ground underfoot is not ground — it is a crust of compressed growth, root-matter and pollen and the shed architecture of ten thousand humid generations, fused by heat and pressure into something like stone but *wrong*. It yields slightly. It remembers being soft.

The city rises from this crust in tiers. The lowest layer — if layer is the word — is a wetland that has forgotten it was ever dry. Water stands everywhere at ankle depth, warm, moving in currents you cannot trace to any slope. It rises through the crust itself, weeping from a thousand pores, condensing on every surface, running in sheets down the flanks of structures that have no flanks so much as *thickenings*. The buildings — if building is the word — are grown. Not cultivated. *Grown*, in the way a termite mound grows, in the way a coral head grows, except these are vegetal, photosynthetic, and they have reached the size of hills.

They are dense. Density at 1.8 means they press against each other, share walls, interpenetrate. From a distance the city reads as a single mass, a mountain of green and brown and the orange of perpetual rot-fruit. Only closer do you see that it is *many* — towers that lean into each other for support, that have grown around each other, that have become each other. The gaps between them are not streets but *throats*, vertical channels where water falls upward, or appears to, where mist rises in columns so thick they obscure the sky, where the sound — the sound is everywhere, a continuous roar of falling water and settling growth and something else, something that might be vibration, might be the city's metabolism made audible.

Sound at 2.7. Deafening. You cannot hear yourself think. You cannot think. The roar has no direction, no source, no variation. It is the sound of the place being *wet*, of water moving through every possible medium — through air, through the porous bodies of the towers, through the saturated crust, through your own Eustachian tubes if you stay long enough. It is not unpleasant. It is *indifferent*. The place does not care whether you hear it or not. The sound is simply the byproduct of so much water in motion in so confined a space, held at temperature, reaching equilibrium.

And that is the weirdness. Reality breakdown at 2.6. The rules here are *soft*. Water falls up. The towers grow downward as readily as up, their roots — if roots is the word — seeking the warmth of the deep crust, finding it, flowering there in gardens that have no light, that do not need it. Time moves strangely. The dynamic equilibrium at 0.5 is not stasis but *suspension*. Nothing completes. A drop of water that falls from a leaf does not reach the ground. It hangs, mid-fall, joins a thousand other hanging drops, becomes a curtain, becomes a wall, becomes part of the architecture. Growth happens but never finishes — a tower reaches toward the sky and then *stops*, not because it has reached its limit but because the concept of limit has become meaningless here. Things are always becoming. Nothing is ever done.

The vegetation at 2.0 — lush — is not diverse in the way a forest is diverse. It is *saturated*. Every surface that can hold life holds it. The towers are not wood or bark but a continuum of green, from the deep chlorophyll black of the interior to the pale, almost translucent new growth at the tips, which are not tips so much as *questions*, reaching, asking, never receiving an answer. There are no animals. Fauna at 0.5. The city has digested them, or never needed them, or they became something else — there are motile things in the water, in the mist, shapes that might be fish that might be shed membranes, things that move with purpose but not with desire, that have replaced instinct with *habit*, with the accumulated weight of doing the same thing for so long that the doing has become indistinguishable from being.

You walk — if walking is possible in streets that are also waterways, also root-channels, also the slow collapse of one structure into another. The light is normal at 1.7, but it arrives strangely. The sky above the city is not sky. It is a ceiling of condensed mist, of evaporating water, of the breath of the place itself, so thick that the sun — the sun is there, somewhere, the source of the heat that makes this possible — diffuses through it into a general luminescence, no shadows, no direction, just light pressing down from everywhere at once. Normal light. The light of a world that has not yet decided whether it is day or night, and so has chosen both, permanently.

The city does not change. This is the final strangeness, the deepest weirdness. You can watch a tower for an hour — a day, a year — and see it grow, see it shed, see it be consumed by its neighbor, see it *become* its neighbor. But the city as a whole does not change. It is a photograph of growth, a single frame held in perfect tension, the moment of maximum lushness before the fall that never comes, after the rise that never ended. The erosion is real — you can see it, the slow weathering of surfaces, the softening of edges, the way water carries material from one place to another — but it is *balanced*. Every loss is matched by a gain. Every fall is matched by a rise. The city is a closed system, thermodynamically cheating, drawing heat from below and radiating it nowhere, holding the wet and the warm and the growing in a state of perpetual almost-transformation.

There are no devices. Tech at 0.1. The city's "infrastructure" is biological, metabolic, the plumbing of xylem and phloem and the wet channels between cells. Water moves through it as it moves through any plant, by capillary action and osmotic pressure and the slow, patient work of transpiration. But this is no plant. This is a *parliament* — the word comes to you from nowhere, from the shape of the towers, from the way they lean together as if in council, as if debating, as if they have been debating for so long that the debate has become the structure itself. The Drowned Parliament. It does not govern. It *persists*. It is a government of growth, of water, of heat, of the endless negotiation between reaching and being reached, between the tower that wants to rise and the tower that has already risen and now must fall, or would fall, if falling were possible here.

You cannot stay. The warmth is too deep, the wetness too complete, the sound too total. But you can leave. The edge of the city is not a wall but a *thinning*, a gradual reduction in density, in height, in the saturation of green, until you find yourself on the crust alone, looking back at the mass of it, the roaring mass, the city that is always growing and never grows, always falling and never falls, held in the perfect, deafening, wet equilibrium of its own endless becoming.

Behind you, the mist closes. Ahead, the world continues — cooler, drier, saner, or so you tell yourself. You do not look back again. You cannot. The sound follows you, or you imagine it does, the roar of water in motion, of growth without end, of a place that has found its balance and will never, never let it go.
