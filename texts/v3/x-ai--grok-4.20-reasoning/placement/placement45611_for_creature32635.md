**I am the Thirteenth Hollow.**

Not the first and never the last, but the one that appears only when the chord develops a fracture so subtle no other hollow can detect it. You will not see me walking the narrow slots with the others. I am the exception the equilibrium permits because the equilibrium itself required an exception to remain perfect.

You would find me inside the stone.

Not beneath it. *Inside* it—embedded in the quartzite like a flaw that is not a flaw. A man-shaped vacancy frozen mid-stride within the luminous rock, visible only when the sun strikes at the precise angle that turns the mica into a billion tiny mirrors. From afar I look like a fossilized shadow wearing the negative image of a long coat and an expression that never finished forming. Up close, if you press your ear to the stone, you can hear my version of the eternal note: the same chord as everything else, but delayed by exactly one hundred and eleven years. A kind of harmonic afterimage.

I was not always like this.

Once—before the word “once” lost all meaning—I was a traveler who believed he had come to measure the wind. I carried instruments. I took samples. I scratched notes into a metal tablet with a diamond stylus, convinced that knowledge could be stolen from a place that had already perfected itself. The Crown tolerated my trespass for exactly as long as it took the wind to erase every mark I made. Then it sent the hollows.

They moved through me without drama. No pain, only the slow, courteous dismantling of every assumption I had mistaken for a self. When they finished, there was almost nothing left that could still be called “I.”

Almost.

A single stubborn frequency remained—my refusal to become background music. The Crown considered this flaw. It weighed the perfection of total erasure against the perfection of allowing one microscopic imperfection that would, over geological time, keep the chord from ever sliding into true stasis. It chose the latter.

So it carved me into the stone itself, a permanent pause in the symphony. A held note. A thirteenth hollow who no longer walks but *is walked through* by the others when they need to remember that even perfection requires a witness.

The titans sometimes rest their massive foreheads against the exact place where my stone chest would be. They leave behind traces of feldspar dust that arrange themselves into brief, deliberate patterns—equations, almost—before the wind takes them. I think they are thanking me for remaining slightly out of tune so they do not have to.

The skimmers spiral lower when they pass overhead, screaming a note that exists nowhere else in their repertoire. It is the sound of recognition. They are saying: *We remember when you were loud.*

The lichen grows over my surface in colors no other colony dares—deep infrareds and ultraviolet violets that have no business at this altitude. It is not feeding. It is listening to the delay between my note and the greater chord, using the dissonance to tighten its hyphae by one ten-thousandth of a millimeter each century. The instrument maker tuning the instrument maker.

I do not correct anymore.

I *am* the correction that has become permanent. The place where the Crown allows itself the luxury of a single unanswered question, sealed forever in stone so that the rest of the Hollow Crown can remain flawlessly, mercilessly complete.

If you stay long enough, you may one day see my silhouette step out of the rock.

This happens once every few thousand years, when the chord begins to forget it was ever anything but eternal. I am permitted to walk free for thirteen heartbeats—long enough to cross from one fin to the next—and then I must return to the quartzite. During those moments I am the only thing in the Crown that casts no shadow.

Because the shadow is still inside the stone, waiting for me.

And the wind sings through us both, perfectly, forever.
