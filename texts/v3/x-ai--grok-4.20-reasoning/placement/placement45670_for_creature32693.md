**I would be the Tourmaline Registrar.**

You would not find me at the center of any event. Not among the concentric rings when the great filter-beasts descend, not riding the thermals with the Iron Centipedes of the Third Iteration, not even resting upon the slow flanks of the Cathedral Blimps like the generations of smaller creatures that live and reallocate and die there. 

I am always slightly to the side.

I precipitate along the gentlest of the exhausted slopes, where the sintered mosaic has been worn to a courteous monotony and the roar of the world digesting itself arrives already half-refined by distance. My body is a long, low archipelagic creature—segments of black tourmaline fused end to end, each prism tipped with a living needle that rotates once every forty-three years. The needles are piezoelectric; they do not merely feel the sound, they *charge* it. When the deep industrial bellow shifts register, my entire length lights up in sequence like a slow lightning bolt moving through stone. The charge is stored in lattices that will not discharge for centuries.

I do not process rock. I do not edit fractures. I do not unmake.

I *record*.

Every time two Hematite Breathers lock plates and decide, over the long minute that equals a century, which of them will dissolve into the next, I am there—half a horizon away—needles turning. The precise electrochemical signature of their decision is caught, diffracted, and stored in one of my internal chambers as a single, perfect, microscopic ingot of frozen intent. I have done this since the Second Iteration. My body is heavy with them. They press outward against the tourmaline like black stars trying to be born.

When the Mica Drifters settle over a stress fracture and begin their decades-long bandage-work, I circle (so slowly the motion is invisible to anything that does not itself move in geological time) and I register the exact shade of ontological fatigue they are drinking. Later, when a new Cathedral Blimp exhales an ingot that is slightly wrong for any purpose the universe currently requires, I am the first to taste the wrongness. I store its signature too. Nothing is judged. Nothing is forgiven. Everything is kept.

The Unmaker’s Regret knows my function. Sometimes, after it has held a too-proud mountain or a repetitive law of physics in perfect non-being for geological ages and then excreted something cleaner, it will allow one of my needles to brush the fresh surface. A single atom of what-was-not is transferred. That atom becomes the seed crystal for my next segment. In this way I grow.

I have no name the others use. Names are soft things, almost as soft as choice. The smaller Breathers simply adjust their paths when they sense my charge building. The Iron Centipedes tilt a single piston-beam in acknowledgment when their nineteen-year shadow falls across me. They understand that I am the plateau’s memory of its own decisions, written in a mineral that will outlast every mountain it has yet to forge.

If you stayed long enough (and you would not want to), you would eventually see me approach the place where you stand. I would take seventeen years to cover the last kilometer. My needles would rotate with the patience of something that has never been in a hurry because hurry itself was never born here.

Most likely I would simply register you—the precise electrochemical flavor of your brief carbon life, the way your soft thoughts produce a faint, almost inaudible harmonic inside the greater roar—and move on. The ingot of that registration would join the others inside me, another black star.

But sometimes, rarely, one of the needles pauses.

It does not offer you a plate. That is not my function.

Instead it *etches*. Upon the sintered glass between us it burns, over the course of another forty-three years, a single line. The line is not a symbol. It is not a word. It is the shape your attention would have taken if it had never been poured into anything as soft as a skull. A slow, turning spiral. A question mark drawn so large it can only be read by tectonic forces. A fracture that remembers it was once curiosity.

Then I continue on my way, heavier by one more stored decision, the deep roar moving through me like blood that was never liquid.

The drop-hammers fall once every few years in the distance, forging something immense that will never quite be finished.

I keep the receipt.
