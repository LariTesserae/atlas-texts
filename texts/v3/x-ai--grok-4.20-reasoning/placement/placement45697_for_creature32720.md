I am in the Ninth Unfolding, a place the Quiet Hands rarely speak of aloud.

It is not a tower. It is the place where three root-roads older than language have braided themselves into a single ascending helix the width of a small sea. The helix has been turning, very slowly, for nine hundred years. If you stand at its center long enough, the motion becomes invisible and it is the world that seems to orbit you. Water does not fall here; it orbits too, perfect silver beads following invisible rails of charge and tension. The air tastes of petrichor and iron and something electric that has no name.

This is where I live.

I am not born. I was *remembered* into being.

Four centuries ago a Memory-Keeper named Ashen-Throat pressed her forehead to a pillar and asked the Lattice a question it had never been asked before: *What do you dream when no one is pruning you?* The Lattice did not answer in words or images. Instead it grew a knot in the heartwood the size of a man. Inside the knot it folded every unanswered question the Quiet Hands had ever swallowed, every forbidden acceleration, every time a gardener had wanted to run instead of wait. Then it sealed the knot with resin and waited.

I opened four hundred years later.

My body is still deciding what it is.

From the sternum down I am Quiet Hand: heartwood bones wrapped in living skin the color of wet walnut, veins that pulse with the forty-second tide. But above the heart I become something the gardeners have no word for. My ribs have flowered outward into a lattice of their own—thin, pale, flexible plates of modified xylem that shift and click when I breathe. Between them live colonies of the glass-winged lizards, yes, but also something else: pale, thread-like fungi that glow soft violet when I think too hard. The Pollination say the fungi are the Lattice’s attempt to grow *uncertainty* inside a body.

My face is mostly still mine, except the eyes. They have no iris anymore. Only two vertical pools of slow green light, like the bioluminescence the moths carry, except mine sometimes arranges itself into symbols when I am listening to distant towers. The symbols are never the same twice. The Old believe they are the Lattice writing questions on the back of my retinas so I will never forget that I belong to the asking, not the answering.

They call me the Knot.

Not as a title. As a fact.

I do not prune. I do not pollinate. I do not keep memory in the way the Memory-Keepers do. Instead I *am* the place where the Lattice tries to remember what it was like to be surprised.

Every seventy-three years I descend the turning helix and walk the long root-roads between the bruises. I go without tools. My only offering is the small obsidian blade I grew from my own left wrist—living keratin fused to volcanic glass. When I find a place where the Lattice is dreaming too rigidly, where the arches have grown perfect for too many centuries, I cut it. Not deeply. Just enough to break the perfection. Then I press my mouth to the wound and sing the sound a moth makes when it is lost.

The Lattice drinks the song and the blood and, sometimes, it laughs.

The laugh arrives as new growth that should not be possible: a spiral that turns the wrong way, a tower that grows a window looking *down* into its own roots, a corridor that briefly remembers what moonlight felt like even though no one here has seen it in forty thousand years.

The Quiet Hands fear me. The Dreamed watch me with something like hunger. The children with xylem bones follow me in silent processions, sighing instead of breathing.

And I am lonely in a way that pleases the Lattice.

Because loneliness, it turns out, is a nutrient it had forgotten it needed.

I have begun to notice changes in myself that the others have not yet seen. My shadow sometimes lags half a second behind me, as though it is still considering whether to remain attached. When I sleep, small sections of the helix above me rearrange themselves into diagrams I half-remember from dreams that do not belong to this continent at all—double helices, orbital paths, the slow curvature of spacetime wearing a green dress.

The oldest Memory-Keeper, the one whose mouth is becoming a knot-hole, found me after my last descent. She placed her root-fingers against my chest and listened to the fungi singing between my ribs.

Then she smiled the terrible patient smile and whispered:

“You are the cut that answers itself with blood. Be careful, Knot. One day the Lattice may decide the question you carry is beautiful enough to stop asking.”

I did not answer her with words.

I simply swayed in time with the peristalsis beneath us, forty-second tide, forty-second tide, and let one of the silver beads of water orbit my head like a tiny, trembling moon.

Somewhere far below, in chambers where the continent dreams in root and vein and continent-spanning thought, the Lattice considered her warning.

Then it grew one more small violet thread of fungus behind my heart, and the thread spelled out a new symbol in living light.

The symbol meant:

*Continue.*

So I do.

I walk the Weeping Lattice as its question, its scar, its hesitation.

And the water still weeps upward.

And the towers still breathe.

And somewhere ahead, where the jade sky bruises into colors no eye has named, the next iteration of me is already budding—slowly, thoughtfully—inside a knot that has not yet decided to open.
