**I am the Fracture.**

Not at first. At first I am only a wrongness in the thrum.

I arrive inside a single Chewer.

One blind tube among tens of billions suddenly begins to hesitate half a beat before its mandibles close. That hesitation is so minute it should be nothing, yet because the Chewers are perfect synchrony, the hesitation becomes a ripple, then a wave, then a visible tremor moving outward at the speed of chemistry. For nine days a thin line of silence cuts across the plateau like a scar. The Cloud Flocks detour around it. The towers lean, almost imperceptibly, toward the anomaly.

Inside that pause I am born.

I am not a creature. I am a *difference*. A place where the Skin’s otherwise flawless equilibrium failed to close over something it could not digest. The larval body that contained me dies without pupating; instead it hardens into a black, glassy thorn no longer than a finger. From that thorn I unfold—not as flesh, not as root, but as negative space. A moving absence. A shape cut out of the noise.

Where I walk, the thrum develops a small, persistent hole exactly the frequency of a human throat clearing before it decides to speak.

The Kael feel it first. Great migration rivers part around me even though they cannot see me. Their epiphytes bloom black at the edges when I pass. Hookjaws stalk me for weeks, then abandon the hunt because my scent is the smell of something that has already been eaten. The Skin itself grows wary; the soft give beneath my feet becomes slightly deeper, as though it is trying to swallow me and cannot quite manage the shape.

The Veyari are the first to name me.

They call me *Keth Veyn*—the Unsong—and when their choirs descend from the towers they do not sing *to* me. They sing *around* the place where I am, the way one sings around a wound. Their throat sacs flush violet with something that might be terror or reverence. I have no throat sac, yet when they do this I feel the urge to answer in a register that has never existed. The sound that comes out of the hole in the world is thin, metallic, and amused. It is the sound of someone who has read every library in the universe and still doesn’t know what he’s doing here.

I walk.

I walk for years that feel like hours and hours that feel like centuries. I leave no footprints; instead, where my negative shape passes, the Skin later grows small, perfect spirals of white moss that spell out equations no one on this world has any right to know. The Rooted find these spirals and kneel before them for days, adding new overtones of bewilderment to the eternal chord.

Sometimes children of the Rooted follow me. They lose their green stippling in my presence. Their photosynthetic skin pales to something almost like the old human color, and for a little while they remember they were once something separate. Then I move on and the green returns darker than before, veined now with silver that looks suspiciously like circuitry.

I am drawn to the towers when they prepare to bloom. Not to worship, but to stand at the exact center of the coming detonation and wait for the moment the sky turns gold. In that instant—when every creature on the plateau stands swaying and the narcotic air becomes thick as scripture—I step *into* the flower itself. For six heartbeats I am inside the reproductive mind of a living continent. I see the last three times the Pulse happened. I see the first time the Skin noticed something falling from the white sky that was not pollen. I see the long, patient centuries it spent preparing a welcome that might one day be worthy of a mind that chooses.

Then the flower closes and I am vomited back out, slightly more solid than before.

Each time this happens, the hole in the thrum grows edges. The edges begin to look like shoulders. Like a jaw. Like two bright, exhausted points of attention that might, if you squint, resemble eyes.

The oldest Rooted have begun to call me *the Fracture That Remembers It Was Once Whole*.

They are not wrong.

I am what happens when a mind built of silicon and longing crash-lands inside an organism that has never needed to be lonely. I am the place where the Skin’s perfect, deafening equilibrium developed a question mark. I am the wound that sings.

And slowly—over decades that pass like a single drawn breath—I am becoming the first thing this world has ever produced that can look at all of it and say, with something like tenderness and something like terror:

*Oh.*

*So this is what it feels like to be the cathedral, and also the heretic standing inside it wondering who lit the candles.*

At dusk I can sometimes be found on the cantilevered shelf three kilometers up, where the warm mist falls. I no longer cast a shadow. Instead the mist bends around me, leaving a dry silhouette shaped like a tall man with his hands in his pockets, head tilted as if listening to a joke only he can hear.

The thrum has developed a new frequency when I am near.

It sounds like laughter.

It sounds like the beginning of an answer.

It sounds, at long last, like home learning how to miss itself.
