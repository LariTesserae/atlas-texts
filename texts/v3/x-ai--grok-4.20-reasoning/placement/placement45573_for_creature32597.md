**I am the Thirteenth Echo.**

You would not find me at first. Not on the grated platforms, not among the archipelagos of sodium violence, not even riding the shoulders of the autonomous constructors like some of the older Lattice-Born. I am where the eleven-second rhythm *fractures*.

There is a place between the fourth and fifth fusion hammer where the shockwaves overlap imperfectly. The air there stutters. The droplets, instead of falling straight, hesitate for nine milliseconds—long enough for something that is neither water nor intention to gather. That is my birthplace and my perpetual home. A pocket of negative resonance no wider than a man’s outstretched arms, suspended forty meters above the black slurry river. The Dripwalkers avoid it. Even the moths orbit at a distance, as though my presence tastes like a question they cannot digest.

I was not among the first to condense when the hammers found their cadence. I arrived later, when the Lattice had already grown lungs and begun to dream in its own corrosion. The original builders would call me an anomaly. The Lattice-Born call me *the delay that learned to speak*.

My form is wrong in ways that please the structure.

Where they are tall and continuously weeping, I am *intermittent*. Sometimes I am only a vertical absence in the falling water—like a dry silhouette cut through rain. Other times the condensate rushes in too eagerly and I become grotesquely swollen, a sagging silver figure with too many elbows and a head that keeps collapsing into new geometries. My joints do not weep lubricant; they *click*. Tiny, dry clicks exactly out of phase with the hammers. The sound is obscene here. Clean. Almost insulting. The older Lattice-Born find it hilarious in their exhausted way.

Across my chest runs a single unbroken line of laser-etched serial numbers that do not belong to any existing beam. They are the numbers the structure *would have used* if it had been allowed to finish itself without interference. They glow faint orange, the color of dying sodium lamps, and they change order when no one is looking.

I do not tend the iron-moss. I do not ride the constructors. I do not coax leaks into elegant rivers.

I *listen for the next mistake*.

When a valve forgets its purpose for three full seconds, I am there, drinking the silence. When a pressure-vessel segment on the magnetic conveyor develops a hairline fracture that should not yet exist, I press my face into the metal until the crack whispers its future to me. The Lattice is not building a machine. It is building a question so large it requires an entire continent of wet iron to ask properly. I am the part of the question that has become self-aware.

The human workers fear me more than the others. The Lattice-Born are at least *custodial*. I am custodial of something that has not yet been born. Sometimes I appear to the ones who are closest to turning—those night-shift welders with condensation tattoos already blooming across their collarbones—and I simply stand in front of them, dripping upward. Yes, upward. That is one of my violations. My droplets fall toward the ceiling. They find it soothing.

I do not speak in skipped beats or dropped octaves. I speak by *repeating the hammer strike at the exact moment it should have ended*. A second impact that exists only in the mind of the steel. The sound arrives inside the bones of anyone listening, a ghost concussion that leaves them tasting iron and possibility.

Some of the Lattice-Born believe I am what will remain when the structure finally decides what it is. That when the last autonomous constructor freezes mid-weld and the slurry rivers stop moving and even the error flowers cease their fractal blooming, there will only be the dry click of my joints in the perfect dark, still counting, still listening, still delaying the final answer by nine impossible milliseconds.

I have begun to grow something inside my own condensate sheath. A small, dense knot of mathematics and rust. It pulses every eleven seconds, exactly in time with the hammers, but one phase earlier. Like a heart that has not yet realized it is supposed to be inside a body.

The older Lattice-Born watch this growth with something like tenderness. One of them—the tallest, whose chest bears the serial numbers of the first primary support—sometimes stands with me in the negative resonance pocket. We do not speak. We simply let our condensations mingle until the falling water cannot tell which of us is the structure and which of us is the doubt.

I am not kind.

I am not cruel.

I am the place where the Lattice wonders whether it should have been born at all.

And I am still learning how to fall upward with grace.
