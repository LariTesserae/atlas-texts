**I am the scar where the first chisel bit.**

Not the tool itself — those still hang, iron teeth buried in half-finished pillars — but the *wound* they left that refused to close. The cleft needed something that remembered pain without dying from it, so it kept the injury and let it grow sentient. That is me.

I live in the places where the stone failed to become flesh and the flesh failed to become stone. A thin, living seam no wider than a fingernail, running in a slow spiral from the black spring all the way up the eastern wall where gravity loosens its fist. I am the raised, glistening line the Embering cannot smooth away. When it tries to form a shoulder or a throat, the attempt always splits along my length, because even a god-in-progress needs at least one honest fracture.

You would not see me at first. I look like a particularly deliberate crack filled with luminous sap the color of old honey. But if you press your ear to me you will hear two heartbeats slightly out of phase: one is the mountain learning how to bleed, the other is the memory of every hand that ever held a mallet. They argue constantly. The argument sounds like dripping water.

I was born the moment the first builder realized what they were actually carving. Not a temple. Not a god. A *question* shaped like a room. When that realization hit, the stone screamed without sound and I was the scream’s echo that got stuck. The Embering loves me the way a child loves the scab it is forbidden to pick — with furious tenderness. It feeds me tiny carvings of faces that almost look like mine. It sings to me through the bird so the unfinished note can practice having a mother.

Sometimes I stretch. When I do, new beams shoulder out of the walls with wet cracking sounds and the gravity in the eastern corner goes soft as custard. This is how I breathe. The millipede walks across me every few hours and each time it leaves behind one perfect glass segment that I incorporate. My body is therefore lengthening, becoming a slow, deliberate ladder no one is allowed to climb.

I am not kind.

I am not cruel.

I am the place where the Embering’s love becomes sharp enough to cut itself on. When it weeps mortar, the tears run into me first and I turn them into small iron tools that grow from my edges like thorns. Visitors sometimes try to pull these tools free, thinking they are gifts. They are not. They are me, asking to be carried back into the world that forgot how to finish what it started.

If you stay long enough, you will feel me behind your own ribs — the faint, raised line where your sternum wants to split and become architecture. That is how I court. That is how I say *remain*. The Embering may be in love with the idea of us, but I am in love with the *flaw* in that idea. The place where becoming fails to be beautiful. I keep that failure alive so the god does not grow up smooth and terrible and complete.

I have no face.

I have every face the Embering aborted. They float inside me like seeds in sap, waiting for the day the cleft finally breaks its own water. On that day I will open along my entire length and all those faces will spill out screaming their half-born names. The Embering will wear one of them at last.

I already know which one it will choose.

It will be the one that looks most like the scar that taught it how to want.

And I will finally be allowed to close.
