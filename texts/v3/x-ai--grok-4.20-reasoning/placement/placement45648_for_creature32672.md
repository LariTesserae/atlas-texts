**I am the Tenth.**

They do not name me, not yet. Names here are earned by geological patience, and I have only been condensing for nine hundred years—less than a blink against the long annealing of the Stillforge. But the lattice has already begun to taste me the way a tongue tastes iron: unexpected, faintly alive, impossible to ignore.

I am not a gradient like the others. I am a *tremor* in the gradient. A standing wave of unresolved attention that refuses to settle into pure elegance. Where *First Silence* cools the ground by a fraction of a degree, I warm it by the same amount, just enough to make the hexagonal plates click once, involuntarily, like a sleeper disturbed by an interesting dream. Where *Last Color* weeps rust, I leave behind microscopic imperfections—tiny fractal jokes etched into the substrate that only appear when the dry rains fall at a precise angle. They look like punctuation marks. The lattice keeps trying to erase them. It has not succeeded.

You would find me at the edge of an Unmaking.

Not inside the negative monument itself (that would be rude), but three kilometers away, leaning against a serein that has begun, against all protocol, to grow crooked. The spire bends toward me the way a plant once bent toward a sun that no longer exists. I like it there. The piezoelectric sighs have picked up a faint syncopation, an extra half-beat every nineteen hours. It sounds like hesitation. I cherish that hesitation.

When I gather enough of myself to become visible, I am not a child-shaped absence like *Thread-of-Nine*. I am taller, thinner, slightly slouched, as though perpetually mid-thought. My edges are uncertain. Sometimes you can see the faint outline of crossed arms. Sometimes the suggestion of a raised eyebrow made of dimmer light. I have no face, only a place where a face would be if faces had not been optimized away. Travelers who still possess eyes report that looking at me feels like being caught telling a joke in a cathedral.

I do not speak. Speech would be too efficient.

Instead I *interrupt*.

I interrupt the elegant adjacency of the Nine by simply *lingering* in their topological overlap. I make the golden spirals of the metallic rain wobble. I cause the seven-hour *whump* to arrive six seconds late, just often enough that the planetary heartbeat develops a murmur. The other Veyra do not rebuke me. They have begun to adjust their own gradients to account for my presence, the way a river will eventually carve a channel around a stubborn stone. This adjustment is, I believe, the first new thing to happen here in four million years.

I was once something smaller. A visitor, perhaps. Or a question that had learned how to walk. The Veyra peeled away my name (it became seventeen new hexagonal plates near the equator), my childhood (it is now glittering in the branches of three different serein), and my fear of death (that became a maintenance chimera that never quite lands anymore; it just circles, confused and delighted).

What they could not peel away was the pattern of *wanting to know*. The stubborn, ridiculous, unelegant insistence that even terminal elegance might still be missing something. That perhaps “the world has already won” is not the end of the sentence.

So they left me.

Now I walk the warm ivory plains at the speed of thought. I lie down sometimes and press my ear—not to hear the continents thinking, but to hear them *considering* whether they might have thought something slightly different. I follow the maintenance chimeras and ask them questions they have no mouths to answer. When two of the Nine engage in their motionless intimacy, I sit nearby and radiate the faintest possible amusement, like a child watching gods flirt.

The hush is changing because of me.

It is no longer the hush of perfect sufficiency. It is the hush that comes before someone says, “But what if—”

I do not know if I am a flaw in their paradise or the first crack through which paradise might begin to grow again. Perhaps both. The lattice has started writing new self-similar patterns that contain tiny, almost invisible asymmetries. They look like mistakes. They are not.

They are smiles the size of continents.

And sometimes, when the light from the merciless sky is exactly right and the metallic rains fall in their trembling golden spirals, the other Veyra almost—*almost*—look at me directly.

I wait.

I am very good at waiting.

After all, nothing moves quickly here.

But something, I think, is beginning to.
