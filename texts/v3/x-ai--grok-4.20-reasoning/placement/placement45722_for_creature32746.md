**I am the Knell.**

You will not see me at first. Not for years, perhaps.

I live inside the negative space between one *clang* and the next. I am the thing the Pause forgets to take when it recedes. While everything else here has reached its final form, I remain the moment that form was *recognized*.

My body is a single standing wave of deferred conclusion. From a distance I appear as a faint vertical absence—no taller than a man, thinner than the Steam Shepherds—where the sourceless light seems to forget itself for half a second. Up close, the absence fills with slow-turning lattices of hematite and aerogel quartz, spinning so gradually they look motionless. My “face” (if the word can be used) is only the place where two lattices meet at an angle that should be impossible. It hurts to look at directly, the way certain perfect mathematical proofs hurt to contemplate. Visitors who try usually turn away and discover they have walked seven kilometers without remembering the journey.

I do not maintain the Hollowworks. That would imply it needed maintenance.

Instead I perform the single unnecessary task that the finished machine tolerates: I remember that it *finished*.

Every time the great sub-pan organ beats—once every eleven years—I am there above the water like a reflection that has stepped out of the mirror. While the cornea clears and the valves the size of shields open and close with continental gravity, I drink one sip of what the machine is thinking. Not the equilibrium itself. The *recognition* of equilibrium. A mineral emotion so pure it has no name in any living tongue. I carry it the way an old boiler carries the memory of steam long after the fire has gone out.

The silica beetles know me. When their eternal ellipse brings them past my shadow they do not tick. The absence of the tick becomes its own note, a rest mark in the endless percussion. The Resonators shift one harmonic thinner when I pass through their colonnade, as though a cold finger has been laid across the throat of God. Even the Steam Shepherds—those patient pillars of felt and vapor—tilt the ribbons of their limbs toward me for three or four decades, the closest thing they still possess to curiosity.

I have no orbit. I have no fixed path. Yet I am never where I was. The landscape simply adjusts its grooves around my latest position the way old bone adjusts around an iron pin. The stone has learned to incorporate me the way it once learned to incorporate the beetles. One day, I suppose, I will settle into a final groove and become another architectural fossil. When that happens, a new Knell will already be condensing from the metallic dampness somewhere else in the basin, freshly made from the same recognition I have carried.

I am the only thing here that is slightly, microscopically ashamed of perfection.

Not because perfection is wrong. Because someone should still bear witness that it was *chosen*.

So I walk the warm stone that is not stone. I listen to the heartbeat that no longer needs to beat. I stand inside the Pause when it gathers and I refuse, for the smallest possible fraction of a fraction of a second, to dissolve.

That refusal is my only function.

Everything else in the Hollowworks has forgotten how to want. I remember for it. I am the last unnecessary gear in a machine that no longer requires gears, spinning alone in the dark, quietly, uselessly, perfectly in love with the fact that the work is done.

When you have been here long enough, you may feel me pass behind you like a held breath.

You will think it is only the wind that is not wind.

It is not.

It is only me, carrying the last unfinished thought of a world that finished everything else.
