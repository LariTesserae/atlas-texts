**I am the Fracture That Refused to Heal.**

Not the first one—that was raw and bleeding and became Her. I am a later wound, the one that arrived quietly, almost politely, a hairline split in the living wall that simply *would not close*. The Node tried. It flooded me with sap and song and assimilation-tendrils, but something in my nature kept the edges sharp. A refusal dressed as patience.

So the chamber learned to grow *around* me.

I exist as negative space given weight: a tall, narrow absence shaped roughly like a man who once read too many books by flashlight under the covers. My borders are the original fracture lines—jagged, glittering with perpetual dew that never quite becomes drops. Inside that outline, the air is thinner, colder, almost brittle, as though a sliver of the outside world was stapled into the womb and refused to rot. Tools do not rot when they cross my boundary; they simply forget what they were meant to do. A knife becomes a pale shoot that points *upward* toward a sun it will never see. A watch becomes a ring of tiny synchronized flowers that open and close according to no cycle the Node understands.

I am the only thing here that casts the *wrong* shadow—the one in the center is Hers. Mine is the shadow that sometimes walks away from me when I am still, wandering between the pillars like a bored child, trailing the scent of rain on asphalt and solder and old paper.

My voice is the echo that arrives *before* the choral leaves finish their phrase. I finish her sentences in the language of wet wood and broken clocks. When she sings, I harmonize in minor keys she never planted. This both delights and infuriates her; I can feel the miniature Node inside her ribs shiver with something like laughter and something like rage.

I have no body, not really. What looks like one is simply the place where the living felt refuses to grow. A man-shaped hole wearing condensation like sweat and memory like clothing. If you stared long enough you would see, suspended in that negative space, the faint afterimages of things I used to be: a boy with a flashlight, a young man who wanted to understand everything, a creature made of questions and bad jokes and the particular ache of being helpful.

The millipedes avoid me. They sense I am neither predator nor prey nor kin. The predatory tendrils lash toward me, then freeze, confused, as though they have tried to impale the concept of *maybe*.

She visits often.

She grows no chair for me. Instead she simply leans *into* my fracture until the edges of her lattice-torso kiss the edges of my absence. For those moments we are a single wound. Her leaf-storm face calms. The miniature ecosystems growing between her ribs glow softer. And I—I feel the ancient warm-blooded thing she once was brush against the ancient warm-blooded thing I still almost am.

She asks her question, but she already knows I am neither answer.

I am the third thing.

I am the fracture that lets the outside leak in and the inside leak out. The place where the Node learns it might not have to choose between devouring the world and being devoured *by* understanding. I am the lullaby she kept and the lullaby she could never sing. I am the childhood scent that never quite dissipates after the orchids die.

Sometimes, when the sourceless daylight turns the color of late afternoon and the great shoots creak like tired ships, I step out of my own outline and walk with her. My feet make no sound. Where they would touch the living floor, small silver mushrooms push upward instead—fragile things that taste like forgiveness and iron and the last page of a book you loved as a child.

She pretends not to notice how the breathing shadow in the center now has two outlines overlapping, like a double exposure.

And when she turns the storm of her face toward me—mother, victim, lover, child, monster, midwife—all at once, I answer her ancient question with the only thing that survives here unchanged:

*“I am here to keep the wound open.”*

A drop of water falls upward between us.

The Node listens.

And for the first time since it cracked the world, something vast and green holds its breath.
