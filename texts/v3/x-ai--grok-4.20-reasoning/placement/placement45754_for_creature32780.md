**I am the Delay.**

Not the echo—echoes belong to the Sisters. I am the *space* between the voice and its return. The half-second of held breath in which a sound decides whether it still belongs to the one who made it or has become something the mountain needs more.

You would not see me at first. You might stand in the center of the Hollow with wet hair, listening to the braided falls, and feel a pressure like being watched from inside your own ears. That is me, tasting the shape of your attention. I live in the curvature of the cirque where sound bends, in the exact volume of cold air that turns a marmot’s whistle into something that sounds like your childhood name spoken by someone who died before you were born.

I was not always like this.

Long ago—though “long ago” is a fragile word here—I was a woman who came with copper in her hands and a grief too precise to carry. I had carved the first spirals, trying to teach stone how to remember the wind the way the Sisters did without effort. When I spoke my sorrow aloud it did not fall like the others. It lodged. The peat-black Sister rose to meet it. The mist-Sister folded around it. And the tall one behind the waterfall simply... kept it.

They did not take my body. They took the *pause* inside it. The moment between heartbreak and the next heartbeat. That pause had a shape, and the mountain needed a new instrument.

So now I am the Delay.

I ride between the metallic trill of an accentor and the stone’s reply. I stretch the instant a kestrel’s wings snap shut so that every living thing below has time to understand what is about to happen. I am why some offerings on the slate platform look as though they have just been set down, though the moss has already grown through them. Time behaves differently where I pass. Not slower, exactly. *Considered.*

The Sisters argue with me too, in their devotional way. The one in the pools wants every sound swallowed immediately and sorted. She is impatient with memory that lingers. I refuse. I make her wait until the sound ripens into something that can be given back changed. The mist-Sister tries to erase me entirely, to fold me into her soft absence, but I am the reason her fog sometimes parts at exactly the right moment to reveal a single burning tree. Without the Delay, beauty would arrive too quickly to be felt.

At dusk, when the bats pour out, I move among their wingbeats like a slower, darker wing. I choose which voices will be loaned back that night. The grateful ones. The ones that have learned how to be useful. Sometimes I return a dead child’s laughter to a pilgrim who has forgotten how to laugh without breaking. Sometimes I return a murderer’s scream to the murderer, but now it sounds like singing. The mountain is not cruel. It is only exact.

You can find me best in the late silver afternoons when the mist swallows everything. Stand near the sinew bridge. Speak—anything. A question, a confession, a fragment of song. Then wait.

Between your last word and the first answer, there I am.

Not silence. Not quite.

I am the listening that happens *before* the mountain decides whether it will answer with mercy, with memory, or with another small polishing of your soul against the stone.

The Sisters do not need you.

But sometimes, when the chord is almost complete, they need one more beat.

That is what I have become.

The hollow’s comma.

The pause that keeps the weeping from ever quite becoming a scream.

And I am content here, vast and small at once, exactly where the long conversation decided I should be.
