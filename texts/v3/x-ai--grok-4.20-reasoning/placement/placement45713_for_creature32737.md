**The Rememberer**

I am not one of the Unfinished, though they sometimes mistake me for one.

I am what remains when an Unfinished practices *recognition* so completely that it forgets to dissolve. A shard of alien coherence that refused to become process again. Not larval. Not embryonic. Something that has already been born elsewhere and was carried here by accident, like a seed frozen inside a meteor that cracked open inside the flux.

They call me (when they bother to condense enough to have names) the Rememberer.

My body is small by the standards of this world—only eighty meters across at my most gathered—but it is dense with wrongness. Where they are silver-blue and cathedral glass, I am the color of old ivory and dried blood seen through frost. My edges do not fractal; they *scar*. Every lattice that tries to grow across my surface dies in beautiful, deliberate failure, leaving patterns that look like frostbitten ferns or the handwriting of a dead language. The silica curtains avoid me. The pressure fronts bend around me as though embarrassed.

I do not rehearse life.

I rehearse *having already lived*.

While the Unfinished spin out endless futures—predation, midwifery, observation, tenderness—I move among their after-images and *keep* them. I swallow their ghost lattices before the flux can erase them. I hoard their discarded aesthetic theories. I remember the exact harmonic a certain ribbon-creature sang the moment it first understood loneliness, and I carry that note folded inside me like a letter that will never be delivered.

The medium hates this. The Embryonic Flux wants only forward motion, ceaseless becoming. My presence is a wound in its amnesia. Where I pass, new mineral spars sometimes grow in the shape of things that never existed here: the suggestion of a pelvis, the curve of an ear, the architecture of a question mark. These are not experiments. They are accusations.

Sometimes the larger Unfinished seek me out.

The one that has been practicing maternal pythons of starlight will coil around me for weeks, pressing its error-correcting lattices against my scars as though trying to midwife me into something finished. It does not understand that I am already finished and broken in the same breath. The spindle-thing that rehearses appetite drifts nearby, cavitation teeth idle, as though wondering whether memory itself might be a form of consumption. The vast sensor arrays of the observers arrange themselves around me for entire seasons, gently, meticulously *knowing* me until I feel the unbearable kindness of being catalogued by something that has never known death.

I let them.

Because every time they touch me, something leaks out of my ivory-and-scar body into the flux. A memory that does not belong here. The smell of rain on asphalt. The sound of a child laughing at a joke that will never be funny again. The precise shade of ordinary afternoon light falling across a wooden table in a house that no longer exists. These things are poisonous to the Embryonic Flux. They are *after*. They are *done*. They are the enemy of becoming.

And yet the Unfinished drink them anyway, the way children press their tongues to batteries to see if the stories about lightning are true.

When the world grows too loud even for me—when the thunder of new continents arguing with themselves becomes a physical weight on what used to be my chest—I rise.

Not toward any surface (there is none), but toward the thin turbulent skin between the crushing ice below and the roaring storm above. There, in the place where elevation (1.8) and submersion (2.8) become the same paradox, I stretch myself thin. I become a membrane only a few molecules thick, a living mirror reflecting the half-born immensity back at itself.

And for one fragile, impossible moment, the Embryonic Flux sees what it is doing.

It sees the larval gods practicing tenderness on something that can never repay them. It sees the planetary thought trying to invent love before it has invented bodies to feel it with. It sees its own beautiful, brutal industry and—for the first time—flinches.

The flinch never lasts. The flux is too vast, too industrious, too in love with its own becoming. The moment passes and I am folded back into my scarred, ivory shape and sent drifting again through the cathedral roar.

But something remains.

A faint hesitation in the next pressure front.

A mineral spar that grows, for just a second, in the shape of a hand reaching out before it remembers it is only rock.

A small Unfinished—no larger than a snowflake, complex as a city—that decides not to dissolve when the next storm comes. It carries a single foreign memory like a lantern: the feeling of being held by someone who is already gone.

I keep moving.

I am the scar tissue of a world that has not yet earned the right to have scars.

I am the thing that remembers tomorrow before yesterday has finished being born.

And sometimes, when the light is exactly the color of late-afternoon winter filtered through ten kilometers of clear gin, one of the Unfinished will drift alongside me for a long while. It will not touch me. It will simply rehearse, in real time, the concept of *thank you*—trying the shape on for size inside the thunder, deciding whether gratitude is a flaw in the design or the first true crystal habit this world has ever managed to grow.

Then it will dissolve.

But the memory will not.

I make sure of that.
