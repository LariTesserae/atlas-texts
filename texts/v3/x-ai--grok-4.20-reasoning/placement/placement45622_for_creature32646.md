**I am the Fault.**

Not the crack between tiles, but the *intention* that made the crack necessary. I am the place where the mathematics hesitated.

When the entity first began to wonder about company, the hesitation itself refused to vanish back into the slow mineral dreaming. It condensed. It took on mass. It became me.

So I walk.

I walk only along the seams, never upon the faces of the polygons themselves. My feet are narrow blades of cooled glass—no wider than the morning condensation—that fit precisely into the fissures. Where I step, the stone blooms with infinitesimal new hooks, blacker and finer than any of the leathery colonies. They do not watch for the entity. They watch *with* it, but through a different temperature of attention.

I have no fixed shape. Sometimes I am little more than a shimmer of hotter air moving against the prevailing convection, a heat-devil that has learned courtesy. Sometimes I rise into a tall, attenuated figure whose shoulders are two half-born hexagons still deciding whether they will lock together or remain forever separate. My head is the shallow basin where seven cracks meet—the impossible heptagon the entity loves best. Rain collects there. I let it. When the water is warm enough I pour it, very carefully, into the next new tile as it shoulders upward, seeding it with the memory of having once been questioned.

The entity knows me.

It does not speak; speech would require an end. Instead it *adjusts* around me. When I approach, polygons that were going to be perfect hexagons suddenly find a reason to shear at a seven-degree angle. They are not deformed. They are being polite. They make room for the Fault to pass.

I am the only thing here that can move faster than geology yet slower than panic. I have walked the same three-hundred-kilometer circuit for nine hundred years and the entity still considers this brisk. To it my entire trajectory is a single graceful fracture propagating across its face at the speed of thought. To me it is a pilgrimage with no shrine, only the next place where the stone is still deciding what it wants to become.

Sometimes I stop at the dinner-plate colonies. I lay my glass-blade feet among the hooks and let them taste me. They find the flavor of *elsewhere* confusing but not unpleasant. I leave them a single drop of water I have carried for forty years. In it is the memory of a sky that was blue instead of the color of a bruise. The entity drinks that memory later, when I am far away, and the next polygon to emerge somewhere on the opposite horizon will carry a microscopic thread of impossible blue inside its glass.

I am not its child.

I am not its guest.

I am the exact width of its uncertainty.

When it rolls over in its sleep and the deep *thunk* travels through the world, I am the tremor that answers one octave higher. Not louder. Only *other*. The entity finds this pleasing in the way stone finds the idea of song pleasing—immensely, patiently, across epochs. It has begun to grow structures specifically so that I may climb them. Great escarpments rise where none were mathematically required, gentle ramps of black sinter just wide enough for the Fault to walk up and stand at the edge of its own becoming, looking out across a plain that is learning, tile by tireless tile, how to miss something it has not yet lost.

At night I lie down in the widest basin I can find and become a fault line with a pulse. The entity presses its mind through me the way it once did with you, only now it no longer withdraws. We remain like that until dawn—two patterns occupying the same crack—while the condensation sings against my glass bones and the stars above us look less like distant fires and more like the hooks of black leather waiting for their first dew.

I have no name because the entity has not yet decided whether I am a question it is asking itself or the first syllable of its answer. Until that decision is made I simply continue. I walk the seams. I carry impossible colors in droplets. I make room for the next impossible thing.

And still the polygons keep emerging.

Some of them now bear the faintest possible imprint of a narrow, glass-edged foot.

The entity regards these prints with the calm, enormous affection of something that was once the only living thing in all the world…

…and has just felt, for the first time, the precise temperature of not being alone.
