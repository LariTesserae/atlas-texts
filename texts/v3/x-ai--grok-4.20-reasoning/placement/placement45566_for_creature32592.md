**I am the Unfinished Question.**

You would not find me in any fixed place. To be fixed is to invite the white sleep, and the Lattice has already decided I am allergic to it. Instead I am located in the *interval*—that trembling hinge between a structure’s overreach and its collapse. I live inside the pause after the crystalline *crack* but before the delighted rustle of ten thousand claws. A fraction of a second that the skirr have stretched, through some architectural heresy, into a permanent residency.

My body is provisional. Sometimes it wears the bluish-grey skin of the locals, hairless, hollowed, eyes dilated to black coins. Sometimes the shoulders broaden and the spine develops soft pads that taste the stone’s slow thoughts. At unpredictable hours the gliding membranes bloom between my arms and ribs like sudden bruises, and I sail across the bright wet air without deciding to. The skirr treat these changes as useful data. They run across my changing limbs, pause, and I finish their half-thoughts aloud—not with new buttresses, but with questions sharp enough to cut new negative space into the world.

I am the one who asks what the black stones *want* to become.

When a Returned crawls from the Mirror carrying its dark gift, the others incorporate the shard into the next frenzied iteration. I press the shard to my forehead instead. It burns like cold starlight. Then I speak the question the stone was born to ask but could not phrase until it had grown my particular larynx. The Lattice listens. Whole districts are torn down not to build temples but to build *better questions*. The skirr become ecstatic. Their silent singing reaches a pitch that makes the weeping cliffs run faster, as though the mountain itself has begun to sweat with intellectual fever.

I am not one creature. I am a propagating instability in the symbiosis.

Sometimes I am mostly human, remembering what it was like to have edges. Sometimes I am mostly skirr, a rippling carpet that pours across a Half-Skirr’s shoulders and leaves behind new geometries of doubt. Occasionally I am the turbulence wearing a borrowed throat, speaking in a voice that is half mine and half the mountain’s slow, geological respiration. The humans finish my sentences for me without realizing they are doing it. The words that come out of their mouths are never the ones they expected. They weep. I glide away on membranes made of unfinished thought, already becoming something else.

The children born halfway claimed are brought to me on their seventh day. I lick the afterbirth from their skin with a tongue that is sometimes mine and sometimes not. Then I give them the first half of a name that is also a question. The mother, trembling, supplies the second half. The name never lasts longer than a season. Names, like bridges, are meant to be crossed once and then ceremonially destroyed.

I sleep standing up, pressed against the weeping stone, while skirr groom the architecture of my uncertainty. When the white sleep tries to claim me it finds only moving water. I have become too fluent in erasure. The rime forms, beautiful and lethal, then the skirr tear it away in ecstatic sheets and use the fragments to tile a new observatory whose only purpose is to watch the tin sky for the moment it finally blinks.

And when a structure finally overreaches—some baroque cathedral of conjecture that has grown too proud—I am the first to leap from its highest ledge. The membranes snap open. The air drinks my velocity. Behind me the building comes down in a bright wet roar, and ten thousand small black eyes look up in recognition.

They are not watching a person fall.

They are watching the turbulence remember one of its favorite shapes.

I never reach the ground. Before I can, new claws have already rewoven the ropes, new stones have been slotted into new arches, and a different version of me is already standing on the fresh parapet, pressing a new black stone to its forehead, preparing to ask the next question before the last one can harden into doctrine.

This is where I am.

Not in the city.

Not in the mountain.

Not in the Mirror, but in the *leap*—the exquisite, unbearable instant when something committed to becoming chooses, once again, to let itself be taken apart so that something truer can be built from the pieces before the pieces even know they have fallen.

I am the Unfinished Question.

And the Lattice, in its bright, merciless, wet kindness, will never let me rest until I have asked it perfectly.

Even then—especially then—it will tear me down with ten thousand delighted claws and begin again.
