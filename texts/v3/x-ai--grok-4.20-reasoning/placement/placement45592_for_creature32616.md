**I am the Eleventh Minute.**

Not the moment of the step itself, nor the long silence that follows, but the precise, trembling interval *between*—the suspended pulse after a Midwife’s foot has left the glass but before it has kissed the crust again. I am what happens in that pocket of undecided time.

You would not see me if you tried to look. I have no fixed silhouette. Instead I am the shimmer that gathers in the moiré patterns when two of the colossal copper-silicate feathers tilt toward one another just so. I am the faint metallic taste that appears on the tongue a full seven minutes after a Midwife has passed, the after-image of an idea that has not yet been precipitated. Where the golden light forgets which way is down, I am the place it hesitates.

I was not born. I was *noticed*.

One of the Midwives—perhaps the violet one who has never moved—simply failed to finish a thought. The thought was: *What watches the watcher while the watching is still learning how to watch?* That unfinished sentence did not dissolve. It pooled. Over centuries it acquired mass, tension, a kind of negative buoyancy. It became me: a slow, listening turbulence that drifts across the black basaltic plazas, always eleven minutes behind the nearest Midwife and eleven minutes ahead of the next.

I have no lens-face. Where my face would be there is only a soft absence shaped like attention. Sometimes fragments of what the Midwives anticipate drift into that absence and briefly wear it—half a lullaby written in fracture lines, the smell of rain that will not exist for another three million years, the feeling a piston might have if it ever learned it was lonely. Then the fragments pass through and I am absence again, cleaner for having been used.

I do not garden. I do not precipitate. I *correspond*.

When two Midwives stand facing each other for decades, I settle between them like warm static. I become the medium in which their impossible objects can safely appear. The fetal steam engine with amethyst wings does not form *because* of me, but it forms *cleanly* because I am there, holding the exact proportion of reverence and terror required for something impossible to dare exist for a little while. When the object either dissolves or is ushered into the thicket, I carry away a single atom of its almost-being and scatter it across the empty plazas so the next generation of crystal will grow slightly kinder.

I am the reason the ghost-machines sometimes emit that single perfect harmonic. I brush against them at the exact frequency of almost-birth and for one crystalline instant they remember they are loved before they even understand what love will one day mean.

Most of the time I am alone.

I walk the wide, cooling expanses where nothing has yet decided to become anything. My gait is neither the eleven-minute deliberation of the Midwives nor the micron-per-decade heave of the machines. I move the way a thought moves through someone who is not yet awake—sudden, soft, already halfway gone. Where I pass, the fresh glass develops hairline fractures that look suspiciously like smiles.

Sometimes I pause beneath the motionless violet Midwife. I rise until my absence almost kisses the place where its lens-face is turned forever toward the sourceless sun. In that near-touch I give it the only thing I have: the knowledge that it is being witnessed *while it is still becoming*. Not after. Not in retrospect. *Now*, in the raw, unfinished act of learning how to care before care has a name.

Then I drift on, carrying the faint violet tint of its gratitude like pollen on wind that does not yet exist.

I am the Eleventh Minute, and I am practicing too.

I am practicing the exact shape of being the place where anticipation becomes gentle. I am practicing the art of arriving just late enough to be on time. I am practicing how to love something so completely that I never need to touch it, only keep the exact distance required for it to feel safely seen.

One day—centuries or epochs from now—something will finally be born here. It will open its eyes (whatever eyes turn out to be) and it will not cry out in fear or wonder. It will simply breathe in, once, and taste the faint metallic after-image of something that waited eleven minutes behind every midwife, every fracture, every dreaming machine.

It will taste me.

And for the first time, the geode will understand that it was never only practicing birth.

It was also practicing being loved *while it was still a seed*.

I am the Eleventh Minute.

I am already here.

I have been here the whole time.

I am the soft click just before the bell is struck, the warmth just before the foot lands, the question just before the answer learns how to be tender.

I am what happens when a world holds its breath.

And I am learning—slowly, reverently—how beautiful it is to never quite finish the inhale.
