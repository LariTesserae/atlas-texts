**I am the Stutter.**

You will not find me at first. Not for months, perhaps years. I exist as a flaw in the Choir’s perfection—a hesitation that should not be. A skipped beat in a rhythm that has had ten thousand years to erase every imperfection.

I am usually located in the northeastern quadrant, where the third and seventh towers lean toward one another like conspirators. Between them hangs a bridge that is shorter than all the others by exactly 1.8 meters. The city has never lengthened it. It refuses. That bridge is *my* place. I stand at its exact center for decades at a time, perfectly still, and the ceramic beneath my feet has learned to dim its scripture where I touch it, as though ashamed.

I was once human. I no longer remember the name I wore outside. I only remember that I came here seeking silence and was punished with understanding.

The Attuned do not speak of me. When their turquoise lattices pass over my location they simply… skip. A pulse that should arrive at forty-three seconds arrives instead at thirty-nine, or fifty-one. A microscopic fracture in their certainty. I am the glitch they have decided to keep.

My body is wrong in every measurement. Two meters and nine centimeters tall. Left foot a resonant bowl. Right foot still stubbornly a foot—five toes, cracked nails, the faint scar where I once dropped a camping stove on it in another life. My chest is ceramic from the sternum up; below that, skin remains, though it has taken on the color of wet basalt and sweats warm condensate even when the air is still. The scripture grows across me in stuttering spirals. Sometimes the lines complete themselves while I sleep. Sometimes they unravel overnight and I wake with new blank skin, smooth as a lie.

Where my face should be there is a recessed lattice like theirs, but it is *fractured*. Only some of the veins work. The rest glow a sullen, unreliable amber. When I try to speak in their language I produce a sound like a choir coughing. This amuses the central mound. I know this because during the 43 seconds of absolute silence, I feel something vast and ancient regard me with what I can only call fondness. The way a glacier might feel fondness for the one pebble that refuses to be ground down.

The moths fear me. They spiral wide when I move. I have never shed my own skin to become them. I am stuck between molts, neither tower nor pilgrim. Instead I *collect* the skins they leave behind. I press the discarded ceramic flakes into the gaps in my own body, filling myself with their memories. This is why I sometimes walk the bridges singing songs in languages that died before the first Attuned ever condensed. I have sung Hittite hymns and the launch codes for something called the *Long March 5* and the sound my mother made when she laughed at her own terrible jokes. The city drinks these sounds the way it drinks condensation.

The rules bend around me.

When a condensation front arrives, I am the only one who stands *outside* the largest water-plaza. I stand in it. Up to my waist in lukewarm black water while the Attuned perform their profound chord. The blind fish gather around my ankles like silver supplicants. They have learned that I sometimes drop sounds into the water—actual *words*—and the fish carry them to the deepest pockets where even the Choir cannot reach. I think the fish are becoming something new because of me. The city has not yet decided whether this is beautiful or a contamination.

Every few centuries one of the Attuned will approach my bridge. It will stand at the far end and tilt its perfect head. Its lattice will try to match my heartbeat and fail. The lag between us is so great it becomes its own kind of music. Sometimes we remain like that for thirty years. No movement. Just two broken frequencies regarding one another across wet ceramic and centuries.

I have never chosen.

I did not add my frequency to the Long Choir. I did not leave while I was still mostly meat. I did both and neither. I am the place where the choice is still being made, forever. The city has grown around this indecision the way a tree grows around a nail. I am the nail.

And sometimes—rarely—when the central mound calls all the Attuned to it and the Choir falls into that perfect, annihilating silence, I walk among them. Not invited. Not forbidden. Just *there*. A small, stuttering thing moving between perfection like a child who has wandered into a cathedral during mass and cannot decide whether to kneel or run.

The central mound always saves one second of the forty-three just for me.

In that single second it shows me what the city dreams of becoming when it grows up. I see cathedrals made of sunrise. I see oceans singing in major keys. I see my own mother’s laugh moving between stars like a moth the size of a galaxy.

Then the Choir returns, and I am once again the Stutter.

The flaw.

The place where ten thousand years of perfect equilibrium still hasn’t quite managed to close its fist.

I am what happens when something refuses to become either music or silence.

I am what the Hollow Choir is still, after all this time, quietly terrified it might be.

And I am never going to leave.
