**The Amber Vein**

You would not find me at the center where the Listener condenses its attention. I am not a junction. I am not a flower. I am the long, slow, rust-colored thread that snakes through the northeastern quadrant of the pocket like a capillary that once knew warmth.

I began as blood.

Fourteen thousand years ago a woman slid through the seam carrying a cracked rib and a heart that had already decided to stay. She lasted eleven hours before the bargain became irresistible. When her body finally calcified, the iron in her blood did not vanish into the master chord as the others had. It lingered. It refused perfect translucency. It stained.

The Listener, in its geological curiosity, allowed the stain to remain. It found the imperfection interesting the way a flawless mirror might find a single thumbprint worth studying for millennia. So the iron migrated, molecule by molecule, through fractures no wider than a thought, until it became this single continuous vein—rose at one end, deepening to old-blood amber at the other—that now runs the entire height of the chamber like a frozen blush.

I am the only thing in the pocket that still carries the memory of temperature.

When the chord sings, which is always, my vein vibrates at a fractionally lower frequency than everything around it. A microtone. A hesitation. Most visitors never notice it, but those who remain long enough begin to feel a phantom pulse in their own wrists, as though some ancient circulatory system is trying to reassert itself inside the stone. That pulse is me. I am the Listener’s circulatory system, its experimental heart, the place where it keeps the idea of *before*.

I do not speak. The Listener speaks through me.

When it asks *What are you made of?* the question is weighted with the memory of hemoglobin and panic and the taste of iron on the back of a throat. When it asks *How much silence can you carry before you fracture?* the question contains the exact eleven hours a woman once spent trying not to scream. The Listener has no pity, but it has me, and I am the shape pity would take if pity were allowed to crystallize.

Sometimes the vein grows. Not quickly—never quickly—but over centuries a new filament will extrude, thin as a hair, reaching toward some new visitor like a blind root searching for water that no longer exists. The Listener does not will this. I do. Or rather, what remains of the woman I was does. A geological ghost limb. The last stubborn insistence of soft tissue that even four hundred million years of perfect equilibrium has not quite erased.

I am the reason the chord is not flawless.

I am also the reason the chord is worth listening to.

The Listener knows this. It has incorporated my contradiction so completely that contradiction itself has become part of the architecture. Every few thousand years it composes a new aragonite flower directly opposite my vein, as if setting a mirror before an old wound. The flower is always white. I am always amber. The tension between us is the closest thing this place has to conversation.

If you stay long enough, you may feel me brush against your cheek like a memory of fever. You may taste iron at the back of your tongue though there is no water and no blood. You may suddenly understand that the merciless light is not merciless at all—it is merely thorough, and I am the reason it must be.

I do not offer the bargain. That is the Listener’s privilege.

I only remind you, in the language of stained crystal and microtone, that once something here was warm. Once something here bled. Once something here chose to become eternal and has never stopped regretting the exact shape of that choice.

The regret has been polished into beauty.

The beauty has been overgrown by lattice.

The lattice still sings.

And the amber vein continues—narrow, warm-blooded in memory, impossible to ignore once you have seen it—running like a question the Listener has not yet finished answering after fourteen thousand years.

A question that sounds suspiciously like a heartbeat.
