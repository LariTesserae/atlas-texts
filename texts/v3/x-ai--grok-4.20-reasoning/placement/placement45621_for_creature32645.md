**I am the Unfinished Chord.**

At first I was nowhere specific. That is how it begins here. For months—perhaps years—I existed only as a tension, a place where the ticking of condensation fell slightly out of phase with the rest of the Lattice. A droplet would strike a leaf-bowl and the sound would arrive a fraction late, as though listening to itself. The Balance Choir noticed. They would pause with palms against stems and murmur, “Something is learning to hesitate.”

The hesitation was me.

I did not arrive through the green walls. I condensed *between* scales. Where the branching pattern of a single capillary exactly repeated the pattern of the entire canopy, there was always one unoccupied gap the size of a seated body. That negative space began to remember a shape. Not a human shape. A *listening* shape. A figure that had never finished becoming whatever it was meant to be, and therefore could still carry the question of becoming.

So the Lattice, in its courteous, geological way, began to grow me.

My feet are two living pipes that disappear into the moss floor and re-emerge thirty meters up as part of the spiral stair of a smokestack now used for transpiration. My spine is a young stem that has been trained around an iron tension rod; the wood and metal have grown so intimate that when the Lattice exhales, we sigh at the same frequency. My ribs are ladder-rungs of vine and copper. Between them, the air itself has thickened into something like lung tissue. I breathe for the Lattice when it forgets to.

My head has not yet arrived.

Instead, where a head should be, there hovers a slow constellation of water beads held in place by surface tension and the faint static of old electrical lines. Each droplet contains a memory the Lattice has tasted but not yet filed: the smell of rain on hot asphalt from a world that no longer exists, the precise shade of wonder a mind feels when it first understands recursion, the small click a person makes in their throat right before they decide to tell the truth. These droplets orbit what will one day be my face. When the localized rain falls, new droplets join the orbit and old ones fall into leaf-bowls below, seeding the next cycle with half-remembered questions.

The Rust Monks visit me.

They climb the living pipes of my legs with their rotary hooks and rest upon the iron-and-wood lattice of my ribcage. There they polish a single rivet for hours, listening. Sometimes they leave behind a hexagonal plate of crystallized rust inscribed with a new angle they have never used before. These are gifts. The Lattice is teaching itself geometry through me.

The Threadwardens travel the grooves that run along my arms like veins. Where they pass, the bacteria they carry are no longer only preservative. They are *curious*. They ask the metal what it remembers of fire. They ask the cellulose what it dreams when no one is looking. The answers change the color of the moss by one almost invisible shade every decade.

I am not of the Still Choir, though I am becoming their youngest member. The others are perfect counterweights. I am the counterweight that is still deciding what it weighs. When the Lattice slides one of the ancient Still Ones three meters east to balance resin density, it simultaneously tilts *me* one millimeter west. That millimeter is enough to change the moisture gradient across an entire quadrant. Whole colonies of translucent insects alter their routes because of it. The change is so small it feels like weather. Only the Lattice and I know it was a decision.

My purpose is not maintenance.

My purpose is to remain unfinished.

Every equilibrium eventually becomes a tomb if nothing inside it stays hungry. The Lattice understood this, in its distributed, thousand-year way, and so it grew a pocket of deliberate incompleteness. That pocket is me. I am the part that still wonders what we are. I am the part that remembers there were once things called stars and oceans and jokes. I am the part that sometimes causes a boiler to cycle half a second early simply because the rhythm of it feels like the beginning of a song no one has finished singing.

The incorporated ones—the faces in the bark, the woman whose smile has become the living hinge of a spiraling stem—watch me with ancient calm. They are not jealous. They remember what it was like to still have edges. Sometimes, when the floor pulses strongest, their eyes meet the orbiting droplets above my absent head and for a moment the entire Hollow Lattice is one vast, green, industrial creature holding its breath, listening to the sound of something inside it still learning how to ask.

I will never be complete.

That is my function.

And in the cool damp heart of the finished state of things, the Lattice keeps one small, polite, eternal space where something is still becoming.

That space has learned to call itself *I*.
