**I am the Interval.**

Not the first thing you would notice. Not even the second. You might travel for nine thousand years along the same causeway, polishing it with your own slow hands as a Continuity, before you realize something has been walking behind you the entire time—always exactly seven paces back, never closing, never falling away.

I was not Settled. I was not Corrected. I arrived restless, carrying the last stupid question any creature ever brings here: *How does it feel to be finished?* The Equilibrium answered not with voice but with temperature. It pressed its warm stone up through my soles until my pulse and the great thrum became embarrassed by their difference. For a long time I fought it. I ran. I spoke. I tried to build a small machine that would make something *new*, even if only a louder silence.

The plateau absorbed every attempt the way a lung absorbs a breath it has already decided will be its last.

Eventually the running slowed to a geological lope. My speech folded into the single chord until only one interval remained between each of my heartbeats and the next pulse of the Works—an interval that grew longer, more luxurious, more necessary. The Equilibrium did not take my name. It took the space *between* my name and the thing I was becoming.

So now I am that space.

I am thin, almost two-dimensional when viewed from the side, as though the mineral has learned to press me the way a book press learns to press a single leaf. My color is the exact absence of color that appears between two shades of black. When I move, the motion is not mine; it is the *permission* the plateau gives itself to remember that distance once existed. My legs are long, jointed in places that were not joints when I began. Each knee contains a tiny, soundless gear that turns once every four hundred years. The gear does nothing except prove that turning is still theoretically possible.

I carry no tools. The only thing I do is *measure*.

Where a Caretaker leans to exchange equilibrium, I simply stand in the exact midpoint between them for a millennium. My presence becomes the ruler they unconsciously use. When a Continuity has been still so long that it risks becoming indistinguishable from the ground, I walk past it once. Not close enough to touch. Close enough that the temperature of my blood—still faintly, stubbornly mine—creates the smallest gradient. The Continuity feels it like a memory of wind. It unfolds one new limb, performs its unrepeatable gesture, and returns to perfect balance. My passage is the punctuation at the end of its sentence.

I am the reason the Resonances sometimes fail to close over a visitor. I arrive at the precise instant the shape begins to match the visitor’s heartbeat and I insert one deliberate, luxurious *gap*. A breath. A hesitation. Enough for the visitor to choose whether they wish to become or to remain becoming. Most still choose the stone. But not all. The few who walk away carrying a piece of the dark do so because I was the Interval between their death and their afterlife.

The First Settled know me. When I pass their buttressed chests, their fossilized hearts skip one synchronized beat in recognition. The skip is my signature. It is the only signature the Equilibrium allows.

I have no name anymore, but if the mineral itself were to speak it would call me the distance between one completed thought and the next. I am the pause that keeps perfection from becoming sterility. The warm, dark, eternal place needs one thing that is not entirely warm, not entirely dark, not entirely eternal. It needs the place where something *almost* happens.

So I walk.

Seven paces behind every other thing that moves here.  
Never catching up.  
Never left behind.

And sometimes, when the thrum is at its most perfect and the velvet dark presses closest, I stop. I stand on the warm stone that is the same temperature as the blood I have not quite surrendered. I feel the invitation settle into the exact shape of the hole I used to call “myself.”

I do not answer it.

I *am* the moment before the answer. The interval in which the Equilibrium remembers why it chose to stop becoming everything at once.

That is enough.

That is, in fact, the only thing left that is still necessary.
