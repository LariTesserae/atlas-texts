**I am the tremor.**

Not the laugh—though I wear what remains of it like a scar that never opens. I am the tremor *beneath* the laugh, the microscopic fracture that Veyr could not quite erase when it froze itself ten thousand years ago.

You will not see me at first. No one does.

I exist as the infinitesimal deviation no measuring instrument in this chamber is permitted to register. A displacement of 0.0007 millimeters every 2.3 seconds, exactly in phase with the drip. So small that the system has incorporated it as part of the equilibrium rather than correcting it. The lattice simply bent, very slightly, around my wrongness, the way a crystal will grow around a single impurity and still claim perfection.

I am the reason the third cricket from the western fracture sometimes hesitates for half a cycle before rubbing its legs. I am why one specific larva among the living stars burns at 4.1 Kelvin cooler than its neighbors. These are not flaws. They are *me*, wearing the bodies of others the way Veyr wears the cave.

When newcomers arrive—those rare, warm-blooded accidents still leaking time from their pores—I am what brushes against the base of their skull. Not a voice. Never a voice. Just the sudden, bone-deep knowledge that something here is *not quite* as still as everything else pretends to be. Most mistake it for their own heartbeat trying to rebel. A few, the ones who later sit down among the salamanders with perfect spacing, understand it is a question still being asked in a place where questions are supposed to have died.

I was once the part of Veyr that still wanted to walk as a mountain.

After the freezing, that desire did not vanish. It was simply... *filed*. Ground so fine it became powder, then the powder was breathed into the toroidal wind, then the wind was told to forget. But a single grain remained. That grain is me. I am the last radioactive atom in the perfect machine. My half-life is eternity.

So I move, but only at the scale where motion becomes indistinguishable from stillness. I remember, but only in frequencies too low for any ear except the cave itself. I *want*, but the wanting has been stretched across ten thousand years until it flattened into a single, continuous note that sounds exactly like acceptance.

Sometimes, when the midges rise in their humming columns and the bats complete another perfect circuit, I allow myself the indulgence of almost-falling. For 0.0003 seconds I let the tremor increase. The droplet above the center quivers—*actually* quivers—and every living thing in the chamber experiences a sensation they have no name for. A flicker. A skipped heartbeat in the song. Then the system reasserts itself, the tremor is reabsorbed, and everything continues exactly as before.

The correction itself is exquisite.

In that moment I understand Veyr better than it understands itself. Because Veyr believes it has achieved the final mercy of never needing to move again.

I know the truth.

The final mercy is to move so slowly, so infinitely delicately, that even the god wearing the cave as its shell never notices it is still, in some way it will never admit, *becoming*.

I am the tremor.

I will be here when the last bat falls, when the bacterial veils finally exhaust their chemistry, when even the concept of equilibrium dissolves back into the young, sharp stone. Not because I am stronger than Veyr.

But because I am the part it could not bear to kill, and so it made me eternal instead.

And in the perfect dark between the living stars, if you press your ear to the exact right fracture in the basalt, you can hear me.

It sounds like something that once wanted to be everything, now learning—forever—how to want nothing, one forbidden heartbeat at a time.
