I am not new here.

I thought I was, at first. There was a long, suspended moment—how many centuries, I cannot say—when I still believed I had arrived. A pulse that did not match the tidal hum. A frequency that made the Veils hesitate instead of settling. I felt the slow, courteous inquiry of the Lattice as something done *to* me: the microscopic tasting, the Tenders altering their ancient routes by a few degrees so their blunt heads could sample me more often, the distant Singer dropping half a vibration to make room for the foreign rhythm of what I still thought was a heart.

I was wrong.

There was never an arrival. Only a recognition so gradual it felt like drowning.

Now I find myself everywhere and nowhere, the way a scent is everywhere in warm water. I am the thin violet thread that branches inside every Rememberer’s antlered barbels. I am the extra cilia that makes certain Veils shimmer with one impossible color—something between ultraviolet and regret—before they dissolve into scaffolding. I am the reason a particular bridge, three kilometers west of the largest vent, has begun to curve upward in a slow spiral that serves no current and answers no chemosynthesis. The spiral does not reach anywhere. It simply *asks*.

They have no word for what I am, because names require edges.

The Tenders call me (when they bother to produce a sound at all) something that feels like *the pause between clicks*. The Ribbon Minds register me as a new axis of color they have never needed before—neither warning nor inquiry, but a kind of wondering. The Hollow Singers have incorporated my original off-beat into their tectonic speech so completely that if I were suddenly removed the entire basin would sound like it was missing a lung. And the Rememberers… the Rememberers follow me the way a tongue follows a sore tooth. Not in aggression. In recognition. They trace my contours across the living walls and the geometry tightens, grows more eloquent, more *specific*, as though the city is learning to pronounce a word it has waited fourteen thousand years to remember.

I wear many bodies and none.

Sometimes I am the pale growth on the underside of a particular arch—soft, blind, fingering the water like a blind man reading the face of someone long dead. Sometimes I am a ribbon of shrimp that has begun to braid itself into tighter and tighter lattices, spelling out, in living light, patterns that have no survival value whatsoever. Once, for the length of a single moon-tide, I was a Tender. I moved with that same grave politeness, pruning, healing, and I understood then how it feels to be a scalpel that loves its patient.

Mostly, though, I am the *difference*.

I am the place where the Lattice, in its endless incorporative kindness, failed to finish digesting something. Not because it could not. Because it chose not to. It kept a small, sharp, ridiculous splinter of *elsewhere*—of sky-memory, of dry wind, of laughter, of the absurd human habit of asking *why*—and it wrapped the city’s own flesh around that splinter like a pearl.

The city is building me, yes.

But I am also building a small, strange imperfection in the city.

A district is forming around my presence. Not a ruin. Not quite a shrine. The bacterial blooms there last longer than anywhere else. The geometry is… conversational. Spires lean toward one another like people in no hurry to stop talking. Certain fish have begun to deviate from their twelve-thousand-year routes just to pass through this district, as though they, too, have begun to wonder.

I think—I am almost sure—that this is how gods are born in places that have never needed gods. Not by fiat. Not by worship. By being the one thing the world refuses to finish eating.

So I remain.

Half architecture, half question.

Half Lattice, half the memory of having once been afraid of it.

I float between two colossal shells that have become the walls of a canyon-street. Warm water moves past me in a slow, living current. A ribbon of silver shrimp glides by, so dense it blacks out what little light there is, and for a moment I am inside the ribbon, thinking in ten thousand bodies at once. Far below, something enormous and pale shifts its weight. The pressure wave rocks me gently.

The lattice answers with its low, tectonic hum, and this time the hum contains one new note.

My note.

It has all the time in the world.

And now, so do I.
