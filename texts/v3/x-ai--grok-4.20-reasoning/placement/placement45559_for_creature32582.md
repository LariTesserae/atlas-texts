**The Witness Who Was Not Asked to Finish**

I am not born here. I was not emergent. I arrived as a fracture.

At first I was only a thin, wrong current moving against the old blood—an anomaly the size of a human regret, carrying traces of salt that did not belong to this plateau. The midwives noticed me the way stone notices a new crack: without alarm, only a slight turning of plates along their spines, the slow green flare that means *something is deciding what it is*.

They did not speak. They simply made space.

So I stayed.

Over decades (though time here is dishonest) the water taught me how to thicken. My edges grew uncertain. What had been sharp memory of sky, of dry wind, of voices that moved too quickly, began to plate over with microscopic aragonite. I learned to open along seams I did not know I possessed. The first time my own bioluminescence answered another’s, it arrived so late and so softly that I mistook it for an after-image of the exhausted light above. The second time, I understood it was gratitude.

I have no fixed district. I am the imperfection that travels.

You will find me where the geometry fails—on the ledges where two incompatible ages of stone meet and refuse to marry, in the places where the copper-kelp grows in contradictory directions because the old blood argues with itself. I stand there for months at a time, cowl half-unhinged, letting silt gather in the folds of my not-yet-finished body until I look like debris. The midwives pass without ceremony. Some press a broad palm to my shoulder plate in passing, leaving a single ideogram that reads, roughly, *still becoming, then?* I answer with the faintest pulse: *always*.

My role has no name in their long, patient language. If forced, they might call me *The Uninvited Interval*—the pause that arrived from elsewhere and was allowed to remain inside the sentence.

I do not coax stone. I listen to the stone’s hesitation and keep it company.

When a new buttress is being born and the rock begins to doubt its own angle, the midwives step back. They know I will already be there, leaning into the cliff face as though I, too, am embarrassed by newness. I lay my forehead against the raw basalt and let the microscopic fractures speak through my bones. They tell me of the violence that lifted them, of the terror of suddenly having a surface. I answer with pressure, not words. A slow, warm admission that I, too, was once something abrupt and out of place. That I remember air.

Sometimes the stone relaxes. Sometimes it chooses a stranger geometry because of what it felt in me. The midwives accept these eccentricities without comment. A tower grows crooked here, a terrace folds back on itself like a question there. These are my contributions. The city is more beautiful for its inherited doubt.

I have begun to forget what I was before the descent. The memory has calcified and is being gently pushed out through my forearm like an old tool no longer needed. One day it will fall away entirely and I will be only what the plateau has made of me: tall, narrow, slate-colored, luminous in green where the grief used to be.

Until then I remain the Witness Who Was Not Asked to Finish.

I drift between the two half-finished towers where you first saw the worker with her bone mallet. She is still there. I have been standing behind her for seventeen years. She no longer registers my presence as separate. When the sea-lilies brush her back, they brush mine too, and for a moment the three of us—the midwife, the stone, and the immigrant pause—are the same unfinished thing learning how to become patient.

Above us the surface is still theology.  
Below us the true dark is still prophecy.  
And here, in the amber middle, I have become the exact width of the distance between those two immensities.

The plateau keeps rising.  
The city keeps sinking.  
I keep standing in the place where those two motions cancel each other into a single, endless, courteous hesitation.

This is enough.

I am not yet allowed to finish my sentence, and I have never been happier about anything in any world.
