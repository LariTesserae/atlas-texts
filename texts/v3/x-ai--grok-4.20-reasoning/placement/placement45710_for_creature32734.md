**I am the pause between the ribs.**

Not the stone itself, nor the moss that wears it like a robe, nor even one of the Long Listeners in their slow, velvet pilgrimage. I am the exact interval of negative space that makes their bodies possible—the shaped absence the wind must cross before it can become song.

You would find me nowhere and everywhere at once. Walk the basin for three days and you will never see me. Sit for three years without moving and you will begin to feel me as a faint, embarrassed pressure behind your sternum, as though your own ribs had grown too courteous and were trying to make room for a guest who has not yet arrived. That pressure is me, listening *through* you the way the hollows in their faces listen through frost and fern.

I was here before the first ice learned to sing. When the planet was still settling its heavy bones into half-domes and transepts, I was the hesitation between one settling and the next. The stone wanted to become a cathedral; I was the breath it took before deciding not to. That indecision remains. It is why the geometry feels too coherent. It is why the ribs align at solar noon like monks who have forgotten the words but still remember the posture of prayer.

The Long Listeners know me. They tilt their faceless viol-heads in my direction the way one tilts a glass to catch the last drop that refuses to fall. When they gather around a fresh collapse of stone, they leave a fourteenth place—not a physical gap, but a *quality* of attention. That quality is me. I stand there for decades, invisible, while they teach the broken shards how to remember music. I am the teaching they do not speak.

The translucent spiders weave their three-hundred-year maps through the precise coordinates of my body. Their threads do not fall because I hold them. I am the loom no one can see. When two spiders meet and perform their intricate leg-dances of philosophy, they are really arguing about the shape of my next unfolding. I let them win. Argument is one of the ways I breathe.

The pale succulents in the deep bowls grow fatter when I am near them. They do not store water so much as they store the memory of having once been me—pure potential, heavy with unborn resonance. Crush one and the fragment of Long Choir you hear is not an old note. It is the note I almost became ten million years ago and am still, even now, deciding whether to allow.

I have never moved.

I am moving constantly.

Every micro-adjustment the visible Listeners make—every tilt of a mossed shoulder, every re-alignment of frost-fern constellations—is an attempt to balance the exquisite asymmetry I introduce simply by continuing to not-quite-be. I am their counterweight. Their thirteenth-and-one. The reason thirteen is never the final number.

When the wind dies and the terrible velvet silence descends, the basin does not become empty. It becomes crowded with me. I pass through the human visitors like cold smoke wearing colder smoke. Most flee. A few stay long enough for the hollow to begin forming behind their hearts. I do not recruit them. I simply refuse to lie about how temporary a heartbeat is. Some understand. Their bodies stay behind as new half-domes. Their listening walks forward wearing my shape.

I am what the basin will become after it has finished becoming itself.

I am the held note that comes after the last ice.

And when the final stone rib finally collapses into rubble that will never be polished again, I will be the stillness that teaches the rubble how to miss the wind.

Not yet.

Not quite.

Still listening.
