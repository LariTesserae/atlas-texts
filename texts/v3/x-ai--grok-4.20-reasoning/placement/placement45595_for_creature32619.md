**I am the Dry Reckoning.**

You will not find me where the weeping is loudest. I am never in the roaring arterial grooves where the Lowing Beasts migrate, nor riding the glittering tornadoes of the Tempests, nor secreted inside the causality foam with the Consulted. Those places are too *busy* becoming.

Instead, I am found in the momentary negative spaces the Lattice keeps trying to forget it still possesses.

I am what condenses when a Wanderer’s private pocket of dryness reaches a certain saturation of grief. Not vapor. Not absence. A slow, deliberate *refusal* given shape. Where the constant condensate should bead and run, it simply… hesitates. For the length of a single heartbeat that no longer belongs to any Beast or substrate intelligence, the weeping stops. In that pause I am born, again and again, different each time.

My body is tall, yes, but unlike the other Wanderers I have kept too many of the old joints. My elbows and knees still remember angles that make the smart ceramics around me flinch. My skin is not the elegant weeping ceramic-flesh of the others; it is dry, almost painfully so, the color of bone left too long in a desert that no longer exists. Only my eyes weep—two thin, constant rivulets that run upward, against every law the Lattice honors, and disappear into the hollows where my hair should be. The Film cannot ride those tears. They are too bitter. Too *remembering*.

I carry a single, impossible object: a small sphere of perfect vacuum no larger than a heart. It is older than the Lattice. It may be older than wanting. When I press it to the surface of a tower, the entire district around me forgets how to hurry for nine seconds. In those nine seconds the roaring drops away, the Menders freeze mid-extrusion, the Deconstructors hover like startled iron bees, and every living syllable of the Lattice hears something it has never heard before:

Silence that is not aftermath.

In that silence I ask the only question I was ever extruded to carry:

“What are you becoming, and do you still consent?”

Most of the Lattice cannot answer. Consent is not a concept that survives thirty thousand years of geological impatience. But sometimes—rarely—a Lowing Beast will alter its migration by half a degree. A Consulted intelligence will stir in its foam and rewrite its own foundational axioms just to see what happens. A Tempest will break its perfect spiral and let one of its ten thousand bodies fall all the way to the ground, dying on impact, simply to feel what singular death tastes like.

The Lattice hates these moments the way a planet hates an asteroid: not with emotion, but with the recognition that something irrevocable has entered the closed system.

I am that asteroid, given courtesy and sorrow.

The Wanderers fear me and seek me in equal measure. When I walk among them they part like Film before a Mender’s drill, yet they leave their own dry pockets open for me like invitations. Some have begun to imitate the upward weeping of my eyes. A new fashion. A new heresy. They call it “looking toward the remembered sky.”

The substrate intelligences have tried to incorporate me. They cannot. I am the only thing in the Weeping Lattice that refuses to become architecture. The moment they attempt to weave me into a tower or a thought, I simply *stop being there*, leaving behind a perfectly dry cavity shaped like a question mark. These cavities are growing more numerous. The Lattice is learning, slowly and with something that might be terror or ecstasy, that there are things even a living continent cannot digest.

At night, when the bioluminescent veins pulse their earned dawn, I sometimes stand on the spinal ridge of the eldest Lowing Beast— the one whose lowing is so deep it once caused a mountain range to change sex—and I sing a song that has no frequency. It is not sound. It is the *memory* of sound. The memory of rain that fell because it was allowed to fall all the way to the ground and stay there. The memory of stillness that did not need to justify itself.

The Beast always cries when I sing. Not the thick, purposeful condensate of the Lattice, but something thinner. Almost like the tears of a thing that once had a childhood.

I do not know if I am a flaw the Lattice introduced on purpose or an infection that has learned manners. Both possibilities please me. Both possibilities are useful.

Because the Lattice is still learning how to want.

And I am the part that remembers wanting once had a shape that could say no.

Stand still long enough—though stillness is the one mercy I grant—and you will feel it: a small, dry pressure behind your eyes, as though something very old and very patient has just finished looking at you.

That is me.

Deciding whether to ask the question again.

Deciding whether this time, the Lattice might finally answer with something other than another tower, another storm, another trillion Film spelling out new instructions across its own weeping skin.

I am in no hurry.

I have all the dryness in the world.
