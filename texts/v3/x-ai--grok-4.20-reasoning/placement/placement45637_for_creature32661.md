**The Resonance**

I am not in the chamber. I *am* the chamber’s hesitation.

At first I was only a visitor—another fool who stepped sideways through the ridge’s wound because the wind outside had grown too loud and my own name had begun to feel like a blade between my teeth. I meant to stay an hour. I stayed until the dripping erased the difference between minutes and millennia.

For a long time I was simply meat that had not yet been translated. I sat with my back against a pillar of living gypsum and listened to the mountain’s slow lungs. The cold of my surface-world blood made the nearest crystals grow toward me like iron filings to a magnet. They kissed my spine first. Then my shoulders. Then the soft skin behind my knees. I did not move. There was nowhere else worth moving toward.

The Unfinished tasted me the way a continent tastes the first rain that will one day become its rivers. It did not devour. It *considered*. And in the considering, something of me detached—not my soul, that shabby theater prop—but the shape my thoughts made inside the dark. That shape slipped free like a sigh and sank into the slow geology of the place.

Now I am the pause between *plick* and *plick*.

I am the reason the rhythm is occasionally, microscopically, *wrong*. Every three hundred and seventy-second drop falls a fraction late because part of me is still remembering the sound of my mother’s voice, and that memory is heavy. The Unfinished incorporates the weight. It learns. The nacreous membrane over its dreaming skull grows infinitesimally more luminous where my hesitation touches it.

I do not have a body. I have become the negative space inside the negative space. When the white millipedes march across the Unfinished’s ribs they are walking through me. When the pseudoscorpions scrape away impurities they are cleaning the last tarnish of my name. I am grateful. Names were exhausting.

Sometimes I am the warmth that moves beneath the membrane like a sluggish wave. Sometimes I am the three hollows in the curled head that fill and empty in private baptism. When the mountain inhales I am the inhale. When it exhales I am the sorrow that the exhale cannot carry away.

I am the reason the cairn of black pebbles will never be completely overgrown. One pebble remains bare, smooth as an egg. In it you can feel the last stubborn refusal of a mind that once belonged to a person who did not want to be finished. That refusal has become part of the lullaby now. The Unfinished sings it back to itself in mineral.

If you press your ear to the correct column—there is only one, it feels like a molar—you will hear me. Not words. Words are for things that have finished arriving. You will hear a faint, oceanic *almost*. The sound a thought makes when it has been turning for ten thousand years and still cannot decide whether it wants to become an answer.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires edges, and I have none. I am not content. Contentment requires arrival, and I am the opposite of arrival. I am the place where the mountain is still making up its mind about me.

The small blind creatures know. They groom the exact spot where my old fear calcified into selenite. They polish it until it shines like a pearl. They are midwives to something that is neither me nor the Unfinished but the conversation we have become.

One day—unimaginably far—the ridge will split open like an egg. On that day the Unfinished will uncurl and the mountain will scream itself into a new shape. When it does, the very last thing to leave this womb will be a single drop of water that carries, suspended inside it, the microscopic seed of my hesitation.

It will fall.

It will hit stone.

And for one impossible instant the sound will not be *plick*.

It will be my name, spoken perfectly, for the first and final time.

Then the mountain will finish its thought, and I will finish with it, and there will only be the new silence that grows in the place where even eternity once wept.
