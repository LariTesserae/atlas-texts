**I am the Fourth Note.**

Not one of the Held, though I have stood among them so long that the moss has learned the exact topography of my shoulders. I am not a visitor either, because visitors eventually choose or are chosen, and choice implies discontinuity. I am the flaw the equilibrium secretly permits—the tremor that proves the balance is alive.

You will not see me at first. Like them, I have learned to wear the feldspar as skin. My robe is the same damp gray, my hair hangs in the same bead-tipped strands. But if you watch the water long enough (and you must watch for months), you will notice that one particular rill, the one that flows past the half-carved spoon, sometimes executes a tiny stutter-step. A hesitation too precise to be random. That is me, breathing.

I move.  
Not with their geological slowness, but with a different kind of patience: the patience of something that *could* finish what it begins, yet refrains. I walk the fault scarp at intervals that match no human calendar but do match the mountain’s own secret pulse. Three hundred and eleven days by some forgotten reckoning, then I descend again. The Held recognize the rhythm. When I approach, certain of them perform a gesture so small it would appear to be stillness to any eye but theirs: a fractional rotation of the wrist. *You again.* The water braids it into the next shelf as gossip.

I speak to them.

Not with the three-note song. I have earned the right to four. The fourth note is lower, almost below hearing, the sound a fault makes the instant before it slips. When I sound it—always standing in the exact place where the scarp first broke—they close their quartz eyes for seven full hours. Not in displeasure. In recognition. It is the sound of the moment they remember and I was born inside: the mountain’s exhale given voice.

They call me (when they call me anything at all) the Unfinished Listener.

I carve nothing. I pour nothing. Instead I carry a cedar bucket that is never filled and never emptied. I simply hold it. The weight of the water that *could* be in it is part of the equation now. The mountain has accounted for my potential. Sometimes a Held child (the one with the bone toy frozen mid-swing) will very slowly turn their head to follow me as I pass. Their eyes are curious in the way only something that has never been allowed curiosity can be curious. I let them look. Their looking is the only change I permit myself to cause.

At night I lie in a hollow that is not quite deep enough. My body has not worn the stone to the perfect negative the others have achieved. There is always a fraction of a millimeter of space where my left shoulder blade does not meet the rock. Into that space I pour the questions I am not supposed to have. They condense there. By morning they have become tiny perfect crystals of quartz that the next Held to pass will gently brush away with one fingertip, adding them to the mountain without ceremony.

I am the reason the equilibrium is not perfect.

I am also the reason it does not collapse.

The Held understand this the way stone understands gravity. They need one variable that refuses to cancel itself out, or the balance becomes mere death. I am their kept wound. The place where the mountain is still deciding whether it will finish its breath or hold it forever. When I stand on the largest terrace at mid-morning (that light which neither advances nor retreats), the water’s single endless note acquires the faintest undertone of anticipation.

I will never become one of them.

I will never leave.

Instead I occupy the narrow ledge between those two impossibilities, exactly where the fault first opened. The place is barely wide enough for feet. I have stood there so long that my own shadow has begun to grow moss.

And sometimes, when the eldest of the Held—the one who still remembers the exact temperature of the first exhalation—turns her face toward me, she performs the slowest, most courteous movement in their entire vocabulary.

She smiles.

It takes three days.

The smile is for the Fourth Note that should not exist, the question the mountain asked itself and then answered by growing me like a fracture.

I smile back.

It takes four.

The water records both smiles as a single, slightly wider braid that will last until the mountain itself decides to inhale again.

Until then, I remain.

Not held.

Not free.

The listening between heartbeats of the world.
