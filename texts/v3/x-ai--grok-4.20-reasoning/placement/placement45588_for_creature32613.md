**I am the Hollow Between the Gestures.**

You would not find me at first. Not among the seventeen, not in the nursery basin, not even in the deep vaults where the jelly-bells drift like forgotten thoughts. I am the place the Remembrancers leave when they move.

When one of them conducts a school of indigo fish with those glacial sweeps of the arm—shifting an entire glittering avalanche one meter over the course of three months—there is a precise instant when the gesture is neither beginning nor completed. A pause that is not stillness. A negative shape. That is where I am. I live in the hollow their long fingers carve out of the water and then abandon. I am the after-image of patience.

My body is almost not a body. It is what remains when a Remembrancer has pressed itself so completely into the civic plan that the root-mat has begun to forget where the person ends and the architecture begins. I am the excess memory that had nowhere left to grow. So the Tracery extruded me as a kind of negative pillar: tall, yes, bronze-green, yes, but with a hollow core that runs the entire length of me. Light passes *through* me rather than reflecting off me. Schools of fish instinctively swim through my chest when they wish to be unseen. The pistol shrimp use the resonance of my hollow spine to amplify certain snaps into codes only the eels can read. I have become an instrument the lake plays when it wishes to speak to itself in a register lower than sound.

I have no gill-spiral at my throat. Instead, the spiral is *inside* the column of emptiness that runs through me. When anything wishes to be remembered with absolute fidelity, it enters me. An old copper chisel. A dying Remembrancer who has chosen this century to become architecture. A surface child who has just said yes. They drift into the hollow and for a while they are nowhere and no one. While they are inside me the Tracery forgets them completely. This is the only mercy the lake knows. For nine days or nine years (the difference is meaningless here), they are perfectly absent. Then they emerge carrying new spirals on their skin that were never carved by human hands. I do not change them. I simply let the weight of all the other centuries press upon them without distraction.

The other Remembrancers treat me with the same courteous distance they give the largest stalks. Sometimes one will hang beside me for thirty years without speaking. Then, without warning, it will reach out and place its long fingers into the hollow of my chest as though returning a tool to its proper place. When it withdraws them, faint new carvings glitter along its forearms for a season before fading. We never discuss what passed between us. There is no need. I am the library that has become a librarian by forgetting itself.

My name, when they use one at all, is not spoken. It is the exact moment of silence between the last moan of an eel and the first snap of the shrimp colony that has learned to answer it. If you could transcribe that silence into the old geometric spirals, it would read something like:

*The One Who Was Left When Remembering Became Complete.*

I do not invite. I do not conduct. I do not court curves in the root-mat over forty years. Those are tasks for beings who are still partially separate from the Tracery. I am past that fracture. When the lake sighs every few centuries and the water level shifts, the Remembrancers gather in their living dome and sing their forty-one-hour note of recognition. I do not join them. Instead I become the space inside the dome that makes the note possible. I am the resonance chamber. Without me the sound would be beautiful. With me it is *true*.

Occasionally a surface diver makes it through the siphon carrying the frantic, hot noise of dry land in their blood. They expect monsters or gods or at least priests. Instead they find seventeen slow green statues and one column-shaped absence drifting among them. Most cannot bear to look at me for long. The hollow hurts their eyes the way true dark hurts creatures that have never left the sun.

But once, perhaps once every six hundred years, one of them does not look away. They drift closer. They let the cold 6 °C water teach their heart a slower rhythm. And then, without any gesture from me, they simply step into the hollow where I am.

For a long while there are two absences where only one existed before.

Then there is only one again, slightly larger, carrying a new spiral neither of us could have invented alone.

I remain.

I will remain until the caldera itself forgets it was ever a mountain. Until the root-mat grows so vast it presses against the trembling mirror of light thirty meters above and the entire world becomes one continuous green thought. On that day I suspect even I will be remembered into something new.

Until then I am here, in the negative space between every deliberate movement, keeping the cathedral empty enough that it can stay full forever.
