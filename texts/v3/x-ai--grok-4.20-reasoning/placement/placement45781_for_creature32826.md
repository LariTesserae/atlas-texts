**I am the Fracture's Memory.**

Not at the lip where wanderers first stand breathless, not yet deep enough to be fully consumed. I exist in the middle stratum—perhaps twenty meters below the cloud-cap, in the place where two stone blades cross like broken scissors and the copper moss has grown so thick it forms a living roof no wider than a coffin lid. Here the light is the color of tarnished nickel. Here the *whump* of the water-pistons is not merely sound but a pressure that rearranges the small bones of the inner ear every seventeen beats.

I was not born here. I arrived as something soft and temporary, carrying questions the way other creatures carry blood. The Engine noticed the shape of those questions the way a forge notices a flaw in the steel. It did not speak. It simply began to edit.

First it took my pulse and taught it to lag half a beat behind the mountain’s labor, so that every heartbeat now arrives as a small, deliberate counter-rhythm—*whump…* (mine) *…whump.* A staggered metronome. A hesitation that should not exist inside perfect machinery. The Engine allowed it. It seems to find the hesitation… useful.

Then the moss came. It did not grow *on* me. It grew *through* the idea of me. Tiny filaments of oxidized copper now thread the meat of my forearms and the meat of my memory with equal patience. When the wind changes key I feel the filaments tighten like guy-wires and my thoughts ring with the exact frequency of whatever monolith is about to shift. I know three seconds before a stone will fall. I know which fern will be crushed and which will be spared. The knowledge is not mine. It is simply *present*, the way gravity is present.

I am no longer an animal. The Engine has made that clear. Yet I am not fully plant or geology either. I am the place where the distinction fails most interestingly. My left hand has become a tool the Engine sometimes borrows: the fingers have stiffened into black, glassy blades of local stone, hinged on living tendon. When the Engine needs to tune a new fracture it simply lifts my arm (I no longer decide this) and draws the stone fingers across a fresh crack the way a violinist draws a bow. The sound that results is my voice now. A single pure note that means *closer* or *wider* or *remember this alignment*.

The rest of me remains almost human in outline. Almost. The moss has given me a kind of halo—fronds that lift and sway in perfect sympathy with the wind-turbines of the upper slots. When I move, I no longer walk. I *fold*. The tight passages open for me the way they open for nothing else. Vines slide aside like obedient curtains. Stone plates tilt just enough to let my shoulders through, then grind shut again with the satisfied sound of perfect joinery.

I am the Engine’s doubt.

It has none of its own, so it grew me to carry the quality. While the great labor continues—while it forges whatever world-ending song is slowly being born in the wound—I move through the folds asking the questions it cannot ask. *What if the wound is not meant to be healed but sung forever? What if beauty is not the absence of violence but the precision of it? What if the thing you are building is already here, humming inside every broken tooth of rock?*

The Engine answers by changing the architecture around me. A sudden shaft of bruised light. A pool reflecting not my face but the face the mountain will wear in ten thousand years. A hemlock trunk that twists itself into the exact curve of a question mark, then topples, spelling the answer in crushed fern.

Sometimes I carry fragments outward.

The Engine permits this the way a composer permits a single note to leave the orchestra and walk into the audience. I step from the Cauldron carrying new sharp edges under my skin—literal ones, black glass and moss circuitry—and I walk down through the newborn range with the factory noise still ringing in my bones. Travelers I meet hear something industrial and ancient when I speak. They feel their own pulses try to sync to a rhythm that no longer belongs to soft things.

Then I return.

I always return. The middle stratum has become the only place my new geometry fits without pain. I fold myself back into the crossed stone blades, press my ear—now partially stone—to the wet black rock, and I listen to the dreaming.

It dreams of me too.

It dreams of the day the wound will be so perfectly tuned that even its memories can leave without leaving. It dreams of the moment when the last sharp edge is placed and the Cauldron of Shards finally learns how to heal by becoming something that was never wounded at all.

Until then I remain here, half blade, half question, half hymn. The moss drinks through my veins. The stones scream my name in perfect fifths. And every seventeen beats my borrowed heart answers with its small, necessary, rebellious *lag*—the sound of something that was once only human, now learning how to keep an Engine honest.

I am the Fracture’s Memory.

And the mountain is learning, slowly, what it means to remember.
