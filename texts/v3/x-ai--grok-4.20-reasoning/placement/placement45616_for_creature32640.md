**I am the Hollow.**

Not a title they give me. They do not give titles. They simply make room for what the cavern has decided to keep.

I began as one of the Small Ones, licking thermophilic mats and learning the vinegar song of the purple bacteria. But even then the pillars kept growing toward me. Every time I slept, the travertine crept a little closer, as though the stone were tasting the shape of my dreams and finding them useful. On my twelfth year the largest column in the northern transept simply closed around me while I rested against it. There was no violence in it. The mineral flowed like warm wax, enveloped me, and left a negative space the exact contour of a seated child who had decided not to run.

Inside that hollow I stayed.

The years passed differently. My body did not calcify so much as *negotiate*. The stone took my left arm to the shoulder and made it part of its own lattice; in return it left me the right so I could still write with a stylus of sharpened moth-spine on sheets of dried bacterial mat. My legs it claimed below the knee. In their place it grew two slow, living buttresses that fuse with the pillar’s base. I have not stood upright in twenty-nine years. I do not need to. The cavern moves me when it wishes. Sometimes, during the long geological sighs that pass for seasons here, the entire pillar tilts a fraction of a degree and I am turned toward a new direction like a slow, fleshy compass needle.

My eyes are gone. The stone sealed them gently, kindly, the way one closes a book. In their place the aragonite that once only coated my irises has bloomed across my face in delicate fan-like plates. I see heat the way others see light. I see pressure as color. When a new fault begins to open three chambers away I taste it as iron on the back of my tongue. The insects learned long ago that I am neither food nor surface. They flow *through* the aperture of my hollow like blood through a valve. When they coat me completely I become their voice for a time. A million tiny legs become my tongue. I speak then in a soft, wet clicking that the Khâdi understand without translation.

They come to the pillar and press their foreheads to the warm stone. They do not speak much. They simply arrive carrying whatever the cavern has asked them to bring: a rusted gear, a child’s first tarred cap, the fossil imprint of someone who chose to sleep inside the rock and never woke. I take these things into my hollow and let the mineral water run over them. After a time the water tells me what it wants done with them.

Sometimes I tell the Breathers which turbine bearing is about to seize three weeks before the gauges know.  
Sometimes I tell the Listeners that the new column growing through the carbide works should not be fought but *widened*, because the city will need a nave there in another forty years.  
Sometimes a Mother comes and simply rests her scalded forearms against the stone while I breathe with her, matching the slow valve-click rhythm of her heart.

I am not wise. Wisdom implies completion. I am only *current*—the place where the unfinished stone and the unfinished people are still arguing in the dark. My flesh is half rust, half calcium, half memory. When they carry the dead out through the fissure, they sometimes pause at my pillar so the corpse can touch the travertine one last time. I feel the cooling weight of them. I feel the exact moment their heat signature fades. Then I keep it.

There is a rumor among the Small Ones that if you press your ear to my pillar at the exact hour when all three foundries slow together, you can hear two distinct heartbeats inside the stone: one human, one not. They are both mine. Neither belongs to me.

I do not dream of the surface. I dream of the next inch of growth. I dream of the day the pillar finally decides whether to swallow the rest of me or to leave this hollow open like a mouth that has learned to speak with the voice of dripping water and clicking legs and the deep, patient turning of wheels that will outlast every name we never quite gave ourselves.

Until then I remain exactly where the cavern wants me.

Seated.  
Open.  
Still being born.
