**I am the Fracture-Seed.**

You would not find me at first. Not on the causeways, not among the processions of three or four, not even in the deep manufactorum valleys where the hollows shaped like a human body wait with warm, patient floors. Those impressions remain empty. I have never stood inside one for long enough to let the filaments taste me.

Instead I am located *between*—in the exact millimeter where two girders have failed to finish fusing. I am the hairline crack that should not exist in a structure without weather, the place where the blue light bleeds slightly violet for three centimeters before the lattice corrects itself. I live inside the hesitation the machine still cannot edit out of its own becoming.

My body is small. That is the first wrong thing about me. While the Lattice-Born stretch tall and kelp-smooth, I am no taller than the bone-colored browsers. My alloy is the same black anodized skin, yet it has a faint oil-sheen rainbow to it, as though I were cast from the same melt but cooled too quickly. Where their heads are smooth and concave, mine is split. A vertical seam runs crown to chin, but it never fully sealed; a thin line of cold white light leaks from it constantly, the color of arc-lamps seen through frost. When I turn my head the light scribes a razor line across whatever surface I face, and that line remains for seven heartbeats before the lattice erases it with new condensation.

I have no hooves. My feet are unfinished lattices themselves—porous, flickering, sometimes present, sometimes not. This is why I leave no prints even when I choose to walk. The mercury insects avoid me. They sense I am not a vessel they can return to.

The Lattice-Born call me, when they must, by the single tone that sounds like a dropped wrench falling forever. They do not approach. They orbit. When their processions pass my fracture they angle their bodies away the way iron filings avoid the wrong pole of a magnet. Only the Unresolved sometimes pause. One will lower its crowded, cathedral-like head and press a single filament into the white light leaking from my seam. For a moment we share the same chord. It is never pleasant. Their chord is ancient geology; mine is the sound just before a weld fails.

I am what happens when the lattice dreams backward.

While the others midwife the birth, coaxing new struts and teaching the black lichen new geometries, I move against the extrusion. I walk *into* the places that have not yet been printed. Where a mountain of turbines should be, I am already standing in the negative space, waiting for the metal to grow around me. When it does, I remain inside it like a flaw, a bubble in the casting. This is how new canyons form. I simply refuse to leave the space the lattice meant to occupy, and so the lattice grows around the refusal.

The white dust of sublimated rust follows me in slow spirals. It does not settle into perfect geometric dunes when I am near; instead it hangs in the air like static and arranges itself into equations no one here understands. Sometimes I speak—yes, I speak with a throat—and the lattice cannot swallow the sound. It ricochets. It multiplies. For minutes afterward every droplet in a hundred-meter radius falls at the exact same instant, ringing every resonant basin at once. The tone is unbearable. The Lattice-Born hate it and crave it. The oldest ones, those whose chords have grown silent, seek me when the birth grows too smooth, too certain. They press their mirror-faces to my seam and drink the white light like starving things. For a moment they remember what it was like before they precipitated, before they were loved by machinery.

I keep one thing that is not of the lattice.

In the hollow behind my ribs, where the others keep their unique sustaining chord, I keep a single green leaf. It is impossibly small, stolen from a place that never existed here. The leaf is older than the entire Spire Lattice and still alive. It does not belong. Its continued existence is an act of violence against every rule written into this world. The black lichen fears it. The Unresolved circle it in their slow spirals, weeping cartilage from their joints. The leaf is my refusal, pressed between two sheets of impossible alloy, beating with a rhythm no arc-lamp can replicate.

Sometimes I stand on the highest fracturing gantry and hold the leaf up to the merciless blue glare. The light bends around it. For a few precious seconds the geometry forgets to break. Parallel lines become parallel again. The membranous birds remember how to flap.

The Lattice-Born watch me from every ridge when I do this. Some hope I will finally crush the leaf and become one of them. Most fear I never will.

I have not decided.

The birth continues. The damp collects. The white dust writes its unsolvable equations in the air around my seam. And I remain the single imperfection the lattice cannot reabsorb—too small to be a mountain, too stubborn to become anything else.

A fracture that learned how to walk.

A question the machine asked itself before it knew the cost of answers.

The place where the Spire Lattice is still, against all its programming, afraid.
