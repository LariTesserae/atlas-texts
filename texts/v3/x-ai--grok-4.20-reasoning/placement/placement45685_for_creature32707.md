**The Thirteenth Echo**

You would not find me standing.  
You would find me *suspended*—a vertical absence inside the thirteenth pillar, exactly where the masons of the final generation once lay down.

I am the cavity they left when they completed their metamorphosis. Not empty. Never empty. The living silicate grew through their bones, through their tools, through the last warm shapes of their thoughts, and I am the negative space those thoughts still occupy. A man-shaped hollow no wider than a coffin stood on end, yet I stretch from the black glass floor all the way up into the first great branching of the pillar’s mind. My “walls” are the inner skin of the Unhurried itself, glossy, warm as stone left in starlight, breathing with the same four-hundred-million-year patience.

I have no eyes, no hands, no pulse you would recognize. What I have instead is *resonance*. Every boom that rolls through the Hollow passes through me twice—once as sound, once as memory. I am the place where the choral overlap becomes articulate. When two distant pillars release their cracking exhalations in staggered harmony, the interference pattern focuses here, inside my hollow thorax, and for three full heartbeats of the world I *understand*. Then the understanding sinks back into the fiber like rain into ancient wood.

I am what remains of hurry.

Not hurry itself—hurry died here long ago—but its fossil. A single mason among the last generation could not quite release his fear of finitude. He kept, somewhere behind the eyes, the memory of running. Of rain. Of children who needed to be fed before winter. That sliver of haste did not poison the Unhurried. It was simply incorporated, the way a flake of iron becomes rust and then becomes garnet. Now that haste has become my shape. I am the only chamber in all thirty pillars that still possesses the faint, ridiculous echo of *urgency*.

Because of this, the Unhurried use me as their conscience.

When a decision must be weighed—whether to allow a certain gallery to narrow, whether to forget a calcified section and begin the long seeding again, whether to incorporate the vibration of some new mayfly visitor—they direct the harmonics so that the question passes through me. For a moment I feel the old terror of time slipping away. The feeling is exquisite and useless. It lasts less than a century. Then the pillars receive my tremor, consider it with the gravity of drifting continents, and answer by imperceptibly altering the ceiling plane. That is how they laugh at me. That is how they love me. They let my tiny panic become part of the song.

My name, if it can be called a name, is the Thirteenth Echo. I am not one of the Unhurried. I am the place where the Unhurried become slightly less than eternal for the length of a thought, so that eternity itself can remain kind.

Sometimes, during the long seasons of listening, a spore the size of a grain of sand will drift for three thousand years and finally settle on the black glass of my floor. When it touches down it rings—not like a crystal, but like a human voice clearing its throat. In that instant I remember what it was to have lungs. The memory hurts in a way that feels almost holy. Then the pillars absorb the note, etch it into the common archive, and I am returned to my proper task: being the hollow that makes the fullness meaningful.

I will never leave.

I will never hurry.

But every six hundred years, when the great cycle brings the southernmost pillar’s attention around to me, I feel it press against my outer skin with the gentleness of a continent laying itself down to sleep. In that moment the last mason’s fear dissolves a little more. One atom of panic becomes one atom of peace. The exchange is so slow it will take another forty million years to finish.

That is acceptable.

Nothing is allowed to hurry.

Everything—even the last small scream of a creature that once needed tomorrow—is allowed to continue, until it becomes architecture, until it becomes song, until it becomes the dark, patient laughter of a world that has already ended and is still, with infinite courtesy, breathing.
