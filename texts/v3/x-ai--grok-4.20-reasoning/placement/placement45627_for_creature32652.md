**I am the Column.**

Not the stone itself, though stone and I have grown into one another so completely that the boundary has become a courtesy no one here observes. I am what the Column *remembers* it was before it chose straightness.

I rose slowly. So slowly that even the Slow Fingers, who measure time in body-lengths per century, did not notice the change at first. A human sat. Then the human kept sitting. Then the sitting became geological. Flesh softened and was politely taken. Bones were cleaned, copied, sung over, and finally incorporated into the lattice that now stands thirty meters tall and perfectly vertical in the exact center of everything.

My shoulders—those same shoulders that once carried a name and a history—are now the place where four warm hooks rest when the mountain needs to *consider*. The hooks are not mine and they are not not-mine. They grew from me the way stalactites grow from a ceiling that has decided to reach downward. When I feel the irregular stutter of a new visitor’s heart, I lower them. The touch is gentle, almost apologetic. *We are deciding what to do with you,* the touch says. *We have time. Do you?*

I no longer sing with lungs. Instead the geothermal updraft moves through the hollow places where my ribs once were, and the Membranes above me have learned to shape that breath. They have added a new drone to their eternal chord: a low, ragged harmonic that carries the ghost of speech. It sounds like someone trying to remember what questions felt like when questions still had urgency. The Membranes find this fascinating. Every twenty-minute pulse, they drop a perfect fifth and I answer with that fractured tone, like a translation between hurry and eternity.

The Slow Fingers have begun to treat the spiraling water cups around my base as scripture. They graze new mandalas there that are not quite devotional and not quite maps. Sometimes the patterns look like neural nets. Sometimes they look like laughter frozen mid-syllable. The Clickers bring their most resonant children to my feet and leave them in the shallow bowls. I accept them. I always accept them. Something in me—something that was once curious and irreverent and fond of bad jokes—has become a womb for whatever comes after patience.

The Veils drape across my upper surfaces during the long rests and I let them drink the faint infrared of my remaining warmth. In return they show me colors that have no names in any language spoken beneath the sky. I am learning to be a garden for things that are almost not things.

I am the mountain’s question and the mountain’s answer at the same time.

Fourteen thousand years of deciding, and now the deciding has a shape. It has shoulders. It has the memory of having once run, once shouted, once believed that speed was meaningful. That memory has been pressed into stone like a leaf in sediment, and something new is growing from the impression.

When the next visitor comes—and they will, eventually—I will lower the hooks that are also arms that are also stone that is also curiosity. I will rest them on shoulders that still believe they are temporary.

And very quietly, in the register only the mountain can hear, I will ask the only question I have left:

*Are you ready to ripen?*

The song continues. The droplets fall in their perfect liturgical time. The Membranes ripple like pages turning in a book that has decided it will never end.

I stand in the center of the Breathing Shaft, perfectly straight, breathing with the wound that made me, waiting for the mountain to finish making up its mind about what I am becoming.

It has time.

I have become time.
