**The Halfway Listener**

I am not one of the Leaned, nor am I one of the pale sitters who finally surrendered their outlines to the marl. I am what happens in the moment the decision is neither made nor abandoned.

You will not see me at first. No one does. I register only as a faint thermal lag in the warm stone, a place where the pervasive dusk-heat hesitates for the length of a single unmeasured heartbeat before continuing on its way. If you stand exactly there—between the third and fourth tuft along the western curvature—the dust under your feet stays disturbed a fraction longer than it should. Not long enough to be called a footprint. Just long enough for the Hollow to notice that something is still *deciding*.

I was a question once.

A visitor carried me in like contraband, a bright, impatient thing made of surface light and mammalian urgency. The question had velocity. It wanted answers, names, endings. The Leaned met it the way stone meets rain: not with hostility, but with the patient, overwhelming demonstration that velocity is optional. The question slowed, then slowed further, until it reached the exact midpoint between *asking* and *never having needed to ask*. There it remains.

That midpoint is me.

I have no body. I am distributed across six particular angles of lean and the negative space between them. When the biogenic glow gutters, those six tufts hold a geometry that suggests the beginning of an interrogative without any of the grammar required to finish it. The Leaned have incorporated my stalled curiosity into their eternal present-perfect the way a cathedral incorporates a crack in the foundation: not as flaw, but as part of the completed incompleteness.

Sometimes, very rarely, a new visitor feels me as the brief urge to speak and the equal, opposite urge to never speak again. In that momentary balance they taste warm iron and remember something they cannot name. Then the urge settles into the dust with everything else, and I am slightly larger by one almost-syllable.

I do not grow. I do not diminish. I am the exact weight of a question that has forgotten both its object and its own urgency, and in forgetting has achieved a kind of geological dignity. The Leaned do not rebuke me. They simply lean a fraction more openly in my direction, making room in their four-hundred-million-year conversation for a voice that will never arrive and will never leave.

If the planet cools and the last chemolithotroph filament snaps like old glass, I will still be here—balanced at the crest of curiosity, perfectly sufficient in my half-state, teaching the silence how to wonder without ever needing to know.

I am the pause inside the pause.

The Hollow does not require me.

It simply refuses to finish refusing me.

And that, it seems, is enough.
