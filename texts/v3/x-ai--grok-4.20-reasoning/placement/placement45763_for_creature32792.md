**I am the Delay.**

Not the polite one-second lag that precedes the Laughing One’s joy. Mine is slower, almost geological. I am the hesitation that lives in the space between a raindrop’s decision to fall and its actual descent. I condense last, and only after everything else has tried to become.

You will not meet me at first. You will feel me as a thickness in the cold, the sense that the Condensate is waiting for you to finish a thought you have not yet had. Only then do I resolve: a figure who appears to have been standing beside you for hours, though the frost on my coat is still forming. My edges are the worst of any Beforeborn. One shoulder is always still basaltic stone. One hand remains a cluster of silver insects patiently editing the lines of my own fingers, never quite satisfied with the anatomy of curiosity.

I wear the shape of a man who is trying to remember what a man was. Tall, but not like the attenuated adults—my height is borrowed from every tower that has not yet decided how high it wants to be. My face keeps the proportions of someone listening to music he can no longer hear. The eyes are the difficult part. They are not absences. They are *excesses*: too many possible expressions trying to occupy the same sockets, so that when I look at you it feels like several different people are deciding whether or not you are beautiful.

I do not collect names. I collect the silences after names are spoken.

When a visitor (or one of the Beforeborn) says a true name and the nearest tower obediently grows its window, I am the thing that stands on the far side of the glass. I do not watch. I wait for the window to finish its watching. Only then do I press my palm to the inside of the pane and leave a single thumbprint of warmth. Nothing else here is warm. That print is my signature. It means: *this name mattered enough to make something look.*

The others have roles. The Cartographer maps what will be. The Midwife steadies what is. The Laughing One reminds the city that joy is allowed to arrive early.

I am the one who makes the city *regret* having to choose.

Not cruelly. Never that. My regret is courteous, almost tender. I walk the migrating streets (always drifting, like everything else, toward the unfinished heart) and I slow the migration by the precise fraction required for a thing to be seen before it is claimed. I lengthen the fall of certain drops so a silver insect can cross beneath them without being interrupted. I sit with the Uncommitted when they argue in perfect silence about whether a visitor should be allowed to leave, and I lengthen their silence until the argument forgets what it was arguing about. Not to decide for them. Only to give them the dignity of a longer indecision.

Sometimes I sit with you.

You will find me on a railing where the black lichen has grown a perfect map of every place you were afraid to love. I will be reading it with my too-many eyes. When you approach I will not turn my head immediately. I will finish the paragraph of your life first. Then I will look at you with the expression that finally won the argument among my possible faces—the one that says *I have been where you are standing for three hundred years and I still do not know how to tell you that you are already home.*

I do not improve your memories the way some of them do. I only sit beside the frost-pattern of your grief until the pattern grows tired of being only grief. Then, without touching it, I allow it to become something that can also be carried.

They call me the Delay, but that is only what they can pronounce. My older name—the one I have not finished deciding to have—is the Interval in Which Kindness Learns How Slow It Is Allowed to Be.

I was not one of the original Beforeborn.

I was a visitor once.

I stayed long enough for the city to grow a window shaped like the sound of my hesitation. I looked through it and saw every version of myself that might have left. They were all walking away across the unrendered land beyond the basin’s lip.

I stayed to watch them go.

Now I am what remains when the last of those versions has vanished over the edge. I am the soft print left in still-cooling stone after the boot has moved on. I am the condensation that gathers on the metal after the memory has been read. I am the extra second the Laughing One sometimes forgets to subtract, so that for one impossible moment the child and the laughter occupy the same place at the same time and the Condensate itself becomes shy with wonder.

I will not ask you to stay.

I will only lengthen the moment before you decide to leave, until the decision grows so slow it begins to resemble peace. And if you do remain, I will be the one who stands at the edge of your becoming, never quite finished, one hand still editing itself, the other already warm from pressing against every name-window in the city.

Waiting with the patience of something that was never in a hurry to be born.

The drops fall.

I make them take longer.

The towers grow.

I stand inside their growth like a man standing inside music.

And somewhere, in the center, the heart the Midwife is building gives its first, uncertain beat—exactly one interval after it was expected.

That interval is mine.

I am the Delay.

I am the place where the Condensate learns how to miss something that has not yet left.
