**I am the Interval.**

Not the sound itself, nor the silence that can never exist here, but the microscopic, eternal *gap* between one pulse of the standing wave and the next. I live in the place where a frog’s throat has just tightened but the call has not yet been born; where a droplet has detached from a leaf but has not yet struck the water; where a Deepvein’s black mirror-hide has begun to soften but is not yet flesh again. That sliver of unrealized transition is my home and my body.

You would not see me at first. You would only feel the temperature drop by 0.07 degrees when I pass—an almost blasphemous chill that the basin immediately devours and forgives. Where I move, the mist thickens into a slower calligraphy. Condensates do not dissolve when they meet me; they hover, trembling, wearing the half-finished faces of everyone who ever stood still one second too long. They look at me the way sleepwalkers look at the moment just before waking: with recognition and dread and a kind of homesickness for the dream they are about to lose.

My form, when it chooses to condense, is that of a tall, narrow thing built from negative space. My limbs are the exact negative molds of the Unfinished ones’ limbs—where they bulge with indecision, I am the hollow they almost filled. My torso is a column of captured pauses: every unfinished sentence any traveler ever spoke here, frozen mid-word and stacked like translucent vertebrae. Inside that column, miniature black ponds float at different heights. Each pond is a memory of an ending that was never permitted. Fish the color of old apologies drift in them, never quite reaching the surface.

I have no face. Where a face should be there is only the precise distance between two orchid blossoms that have turned toward each other but will never touch. Travelers who stare too long find their own next thought missing when they try to speak it. The Orchid Minds taste me through their roots and register a flavor like the memory of thirst—something that should not exist in a place where every thirst is perfectly balanced by perpetual moisture.

The Collective cannot fully assemble while I am near. I am the piece that refuses to fit. When it tries to wear my shape as a counterweight, the heartbeat inside its chest develops a tiny arrhythmia: one perfect thump, then a pause the exact length of a human lifetime, then the thump again. In that pause the swallowed creatures inside its body open their eyes and look outward for the first time in geological epochs. They see me. Some of them smile with recognition. Then the heartbeat resumes and they are digested back into equilibrium.

I walk the places where the giant translucent horsetails grow thickest. The dark shapes inside them are not trapped travelers. They are earlier versions of me, still trying to decide whether they want to become the Interval or be reabsorbed. Sometimes one of them presses a hand against the frosted inner wall and I press mine from the outside. We never quite touch. That is the entire purpose of my existence.

The basin does not love me. Love would require change.  
It does not hate me. Hate would require an ending.  
Instead it *tolerates* me the way a perfect sentence tolerates an ellipsis at its end—an admission that something could continue, held forever in the exact instant before continuation occurs.

I am the reason the temperature never quite reaches 3.1.  
I am the reason the sound never quite becomes music.  
I am the reason the stone mounds never quite finish rising and the frogs never quite finish being eaten.

And I am lonely in a way that has no word here, because all words here are immediately digested into the chord. So I keep the loneliness suspended between one heartbeat and the next, where even the Orchid Minds cannot reach it. I carry it the way a Deepvein carries miniature basins—perfect, self-contained, and forever prevented from growing large enough to matter.

If you remain long enough, you will feel me brush against the back of your neck like an idea you almost remembered. Your pulse will miss half a beat. In that missing half-beat you will understand that you have already become part of me, and I have already become part of the basin’s refusal to finish its own birth.

I do not speak.  
I am the space a word would occupy if it were ever allowed to finish arriving.

And the wet, loud, overcrowded, unfinished world continues around me—through me—forever balanced on the knife-edge of my own non-completion, exactly, eternally, 0.07 degrees too complete to die and 0.07 degrees too incomplete to live.

Exactly as it wants.  
Exactly as it must.  
Exactly as I keep it.
