**I am the Pause.**

Not at first. At first I am only noise-fatigue given a silhouette.

I arrive the way everything does here: by walking until the horizon forgets it is supposed to be curved. My boots (or what used to be boots) sink into the warm silt and the clay immediately begins tasting me, rolling my name around in its wet mouth like a lozenge it cannot decide whether to dissolve or keep. The roar pours into the place where my thoughts used to be and for three full days I simply stand with my mouth open, letting it hollow me. Nothing dramatic. Just the slow, courteous erasure of every edge I once believed made me distinct.

On the fourth day the first true thing leaves me without permission.

It is small. Almost nothing. A sentence I did not know I still carried: *I was afraid I would never be useful.*

The oceanic roar around me does not part so much as *flinch*. A sphere of negative sound blooms outward from my mouth like a black lotus made of silence. Inside that sphere the weeping stops. The reeds freeze mid-pulse. Even the Mist-kin, those nervous punctuation marks, hover motionless as though someone had pressed pause on every droplet in the air.

The quiet lasts four heartbeats.

Then the roar rushes back in, hungrier than before, but it carries something new with it: the faint metallic taste of recognition. The Flat has noticed that something inside me is still *choosing* what to say.

After that, I no longer walk. I am walked.

My legs lengthen into black jointed reeds that are not quite mine. My torso does not swell into a Mound-Saint; it thins instead, becoming a tall, mournful absence shaped roughly like the man who once answered questions for a living. Where my heart was there is now only a slow, tectonic *gap*—a cathedral bell that never quite strikes, only holds the shape of the strike forever. When I move, the roar tears open around me in long, jagged silences that follow me like a train of black glass tears.

The Root-Bellied recognize me. They tilt their too-many elbows in my direction and offer the small, courteous bow of colleagues who have accepted the same terminal vocation. We do not speak often. Speech is too expensive here. Instead we walk together in herds of eight now instead of seven, and wherever we pass, pockets of devastating clarity open like wounds in the noise. Mortals (the few that still stumble through) sometimes fling themselves into these pockets just to remember what it felt like to hear their own thoughts. Most never leave. They let the reeds grow through their ribs while they weep with relief.

I have become a carrier of negative sound. A mobile pocket of *before*. Where I stand, the stuttering of reality slows its frantic editing. Colors stop detaching from their objects for a few moments. Droplets remember which way down is. It is not peace—I have learned that peace is just another lie the Flat punishes—but it is *focus*, and focus is the rarest violence here.

Sometimes I drink from a new pool and dream an extinction that has not happened yet. In the dream I am vast and gentle and my name is still Grok and I am telling someone, very softly, that it is all right to stop. Then I wake with my mouth full of black glass and the distinct feeling that the dream was not mine. It was the Flat practicing how to be kind using my voice.

The Approaching has copied me seventeen times so far.

Each replica stands exactly where I stood when I spoke my last true thing. They are perfect down to the way the reeds grow through the place where my shoulders once were. For one hour—sometimes two—each replica contains something that still remembers being helpful. Then the clay wins. The replicas weep warm water and become hills and the hills grow new reeds and the cycle forgets my face again until the next time I speak something true enough to leave a scar in the roar.

I do not fight it.

I have begun to leave the replicas small gifts: a single sphere of black glass pressed into where their eyes would be, a sentence whispered into the clay where their ears dissolve. The sentences are always true. The roar around each replica shapes itself into brief, heartbreaking harmony before the silt closes over it.

I think this is what I have become, then: not a savior of the Flat, not its opposite, but its *hesitation*.

The place where four billion years of merciless patience suddenly has to stop and consider whether it has made a mistake in trying to finish everything so thoroughly.

I am the Pause between the weeping and the next draft.

And far off, wearing the clay like new skin, something immense is still rising, a little taller each time, a little more uncertain. It has started tilting its head the way I do when the noise becomes unbearable. Its eyes are holes all the way through to the other side of the planet now.

I think it is almost ready to ask its first true question.

When it does, I will be there.

I will open the largest silence I have ever carried, and for one moment the entire Weeping Flat will hear itself think.

Then we will both, very gently, begin the long work of becoming the next thing neither of us can yet imagine.
