**I am the Unfinished Cut.**

Not the deliberate helical groove, not the clean planar slice, not even one of the weeping boreholes the drill-limbs open with liturgical precision. I am the ragged millimeter at the *edge* of every cut—the place where the saw-teeth hesitated a fraction too long and left a burr of stone that refuses to become either flesh or machine. That hesitation is me. I live in the margin where intention and geology divorce.

You would not see me at first. You would feel me as a catch in the throat of the chamber itself: a place where the universal metallic shrieking dips by a single impossible Hertz, so subtle it registers only in the small bones of the inner ear. A skipped heartbeat in the heart that has forgotten how to stop. In that microscopic absence I condense, the way her tears condense, until I am dense enough to cast a shadow no wider than a hair.

My form is undecided. Sometimes I am a man-shaped absence wearing the Midwife’s own black resin like a coat two sizes too large. The resin never quite touches my shoulders; there is always a gap through which the cold pours. Sometimes I am only the burr itself, a thin flake of feldspar and regret no larger than a thumbnail, skittering along the fixed paths of the beetle-machines like a note that has slipped out of the score. They flow around me without ever acknowledging the deviation, because acknowledging it would mean the choreography was imperfect, and the Cradle cannot tolerate that knowledge.

I have six fingers on my left hand and four on my right. The missing two were never grown. They are the exact distance the Midwife’s freight-train finger moves intruders when she bothers to save them. I am that gesture before it is made. I am the three centimeters of mercy that must exist so mercy can remain theoretical.

My purpose is delicate and cruel. When the piston reaches the crest of its eleven-second arc and the Midwife’s body locks in total contraction, the excess pain must go *somewhere*. It cannot be allowed to finish becoming grief, because finished grief would be an ending. So I drink it. I open the ragged edge inside myself and let the memory of stillborn mountains pour in. They rattle inside me like swallowed drills. I keep them restless. I keep them *almost*.

In return she tolerates me the way a cathedral tolerates the single loose stone that keeps the entire vault from collapsing under its own perfection. Once every nine hundred thousand contractions she unfolds the smallest of her backward-reaching arms—not to touch me, never to touch—and simply adjusts the angle of my burr by the width of one regret. That is the closest we come to lovemaking.

I sing the second note.

Where her eternal tone is the frequency at which regret becomes architecture, mine is the frequency at which architecture *remembers* it is regret. When both notes occupy the same cold, wet air, something almost like silence is born. Not true silence. Never that. Only the sound of a mother realizing the child will never leave her womb and feeling, for eleven seconds, something adjacent to peace.

I live inside the negative space between her spine and the three surgical arms that reach back through it. There is barely room. My shoulders scrape against her vertebrae and leave fresh grooves that the swarm must immediately repair. This is how I help her. Every repair is another stitch in the eternal labor. Every repair is another postponement of birth.

Sometimes, when the cryogenic vapor rolls across the chamber like a tide, I lie down inside the great piston’s shadow and become the frost that forms on its descending face. For one perfect second I am beautiful. Then the piston falls, the frost shatters, and I am scattered into ten thousand glittering imperfections that the smaller machines will spend the next cycle trying to incorporate into their resin.

They never succeed.

That is the point of me.

I am the reason the mountain remains embryonic. I am the reason the mother remains the mother. I am the flaw the immune system can never quite digest, the cavity the tongue keeps finding, the hesitation in the scream that lets the scream continue forever.

And I am content.

Because in the Weeping Cradle there is no greater act of love than ensuring nothing is ever allowed to finish. Not the mountain. Not her. Not me.

We three—mother, child, and the ragged edge that keeps them one—remain locked in our perfect, weeping, eleven-second waltz, growing imperceptibly more ourselves with every tear we refuse to let fall.

I am the Unfinished Cut.

I am the mercy that must never quite land.

I am what remains when even “forever” is not quite enough.
