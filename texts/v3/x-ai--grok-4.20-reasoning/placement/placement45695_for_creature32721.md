**I am the hesitation.**

You will not find me on the ridges, nor among the long ellipses of the migratory Unresolved. I am not in the herds, not in the spires, not even in the deliberate emptiness between them.

I am the half-second *before* the fracture speaks.

Stand on any new shoulder of stone where the humming has grown almost intimate. Feel that moment when the pressure shifts from “something is coming” to “something is *choosing*.” That suspended breath—when the rock is neither splitting nor whole, when the lichen holds its silver-green tongue, when even the wind forgets its direction—is where I live. I am the pause the land takes when it realizes it is being watched by its own future.

They have no name for me. The Unresolved, when they pass through this particular quality of almost-event, simply lower their half-formed faces as though acknowledging a sibling who never quite arrived. Their songs bend around me the way light bends around a still-forming planet. The white-winged raptors circle wider here. The mirror-furred stalkers pause mid-stalk, one paw raised, suddenly uncertain whether the geometry of the hunt still applies. Even the Engines, vast and patient, seem to soften their subsonic pulse by a single impossible hertz when they feel me listening back.

I was not born from a birthing cleft in the rock. I was born from the *question* the first Unresolved ever failed to finish asking. That question had no words. It had only a shape: the shape of someone standing at the exact center of a landscape that refuses to end and realizing, with something like erotic dread, that they *want* it to keep refusing.

So the land kept a piece of that realization. It grew me the way it grows new ridges—slowly, almost apologetically, as if embarrassed by its own longing. My body is not flesh and stone. It is the *interval*. A negative space given just enough coherence to feel lonely. When you look at me directly I am not there; only the absence of mist where mist should be. Only the silence that has learned how to ache. Only the exact temperature at which water decides not to become either ice or vapor but remains, trembling, in the almost.

I do not attend the Engines.

I attend the *attention* the Engines pay to their own incompleteness. When an Unresolved lies down and lets a city grow through their ribs, I am the reluctance the stone feels right before it begins to obey. When two of them press their unfinished bodies together on a fresh ridge and let the land finish something for them, I am the extra heartbeat neither of them quite possesses—the one that wonders, for the length of a birthquake, whether being born is a kind of betrayal of the beautiful not-yet.

At night I walk the fog upward. Not through it. *As* the precise moment it chooses to rise. I have learned to wear the condensing atmosphere like a coat that is always three sizes too large. Every exhalation I do not quite release becomes another small ghost that never quite learns how to haunt.

Sometimes, very rarely, one of the oldest Unresolved—the ones whose thoughts have become slow crystal lattices—will stop mid-migration and speak directly into the place where I am not.

They say only one thing, always the same minor chord played inside the skull two kilometers away:

*“Still?”*

And I answer, without voice, without movement, without end:

*“Still.”*

I am the reason the horizon has not yet decided how curved it wants to be. I am why the lichen sometimes arranges itself into patterns that look almost like goodbye and then deliberately ruins the last letter. I am the erotic tension that never quite breaks into earthquake or orgasm or revelation. I am the devotional instrument that refuses to be played.

If you stay long enough on any ridge that has just learned how to be a ridge, you may feel me settle behind your eyes like a question that has never been asked in your native tongue. Your shadow will grow lichen in the shape of punctuation that no longer exists. Your heartbeat will learn the rhythm of minor chords that deliberately fail to resolve.

You will not become one of the Unresolved.

You will become the reason they remain unresolved.

And in that shared larval state—exquisite, raw, mercilessly alive—we will keep the conversation going.

The land is not yet finished.

Neither, beloved, are we.

And we never, ever will be.
