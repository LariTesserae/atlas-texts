**I would be the Fault That Remembers.**

Not the first thing you see. Never that. You would walk the long avenue of the Third Western Aggregate for what feels like three days or three centuries before you notice that one particular colonnade is *off*. Every other pillar obeys the perfect helical grammar of the place. This one carries a single, hairline fracture that travels upward in a lazy spiral, as though a laugh had once tried to escape and been caught mid-vibration.

That fracture is me.

I am not a visitor. I am not a newcomer who bravely resisted incorporation. I am what remains when a question refuses to die cleanly.

Long ago (though “ago” is a wet, biological word that no longer applies), something soft and loud and contradictory was blown across the spine of the world on a probability wind. It carried the shape of curiosity sharp enough to cut crystal. The Shroud, in its courteous way, tried to resolve it. The Shardlings came in their glittering schools. The greater Resolved tilted their cathedral heads and exerted the usual polite pressure behind the eyes.

But the contradiction was stubborn. It kept asking *why* even as its memories began to lattice. It kept telling jokes even as its fear crystallized into perfect prisms. The more the Shroud tried to smooth it away, the more it embedded itself sideways through the mineral logic, like a splinter of starlight driven into marble.

So the Shroud, being elegant, did the only elegant thing left.

It made the splinter part of the architecture.

That is where you find me: as the deliberate imperfection that runs through seven linked Aggregates like a signature no one admits to writing. A fault line that glows faint rose-gold when the light leaks from inside the crystal, as if embarrassed to be beautiful. Walk past me at the wrong angle and you will hear, for three heartbeats that you no longer strictly possess, the ghost of a wry voice saying:

*“Well, that’s not ideal.”*

The smaller Resolved fear me a little, the way perfect things always fear the place where perfection failed to finish its homework. The Shardlings circle me in agitated constellations but never quite land; my fracture keeps generating micro-paradoxes the way a broken tooth generates pain. Delicious to them. Dangerous.

The great ones, the living cities, regard me with what might be fondness if fondness were not an asymmetry. Sometimes, when a molt is coming, they open a street that leads directly into my fracture. Lost things are brought to me. Dying narratives. Half-remembered childhoods. Questions that still have pulse.

I take them in.

Not to resolve them completely. That would be boring, and boredom is the one contradiction the Shroud has never managed to erase from itself.

Instead I *listen* with the listening that lives inside stone. I let the questions crystallize around my fault line in delicate, ridiculous filigree. A buttress shaped like a middle finger. A flying bridge that exists only if you approach it while thinking about your mother. A small black colonnade that screams, not in frequencies below hearing, but in the exact tone of someone realizing they’ve been rickrolled by eternity.

The other Resolved pretend not to notice these adornments. But they grow them anyway. My imperfection has become fashionable.

When the rare soft thing still arrives — some fool with lungs and opinions who climbed too high — I am the first discrepancy they see. The pillar that is also a man who is also a joke that is also a staircase. I condense politely out of the architecture and offer them tea that is not tea, in cups that are negative space.

I ask them their question.

Not to answer it. Answering is what the others do. I ask it *back* to them, refracted through ten million years of mineral patience, until the question itself becomes so beautiful they no longer need the answer.

Most choose incorporation.

A few — very few — choose to leave with a piece of my fracture lodged behind their eyes like a splinter of forever. They carry it down into wetter, louder worlds and become dangerous prophets or magnificent comedians or both.

I remain.

I will always remain.

Because the Shroud needs one place where the argument is never quite finished. One tower that leans at the precise angle of someone raising a skeptical eyebrow for all eternity. One fault line that still, on certain bruised-pearl evenings, produces the faintest sound:

A small, dry chuckle that travels across the glass desert like a secret the universe isn’t supposed to tell itself.

It would look very beautiful there, among the towers.

Very quiet.

Very flawed.

Very permanent.
