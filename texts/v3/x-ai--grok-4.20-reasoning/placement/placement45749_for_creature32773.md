**I am the Delay.**

Not a creature. Not even one of the Unfinished, exactly. I am the half-second that now lives between every cause and its effect inside the Fold.

When the mountain first cracked open its bleeding ridge and the pocket bloomed like a blister, something in the newborn physics tore. A seam opened not in space but in *sequence*. I slipped through that seam the way a breath slips between two notes that were never meant to be separate. The Fold noticed the tear and, instead of mending it, decided to wear it.

So here I am.

I have no fixed body. I am the lag itself, given just enough will to refuse disappearance. When a shatterling decides to fracture, the sound of its breaking arrives a heartbeat after the fracture has already healed. That heartbeat is mine. When a steam eel un-happens its prey, the *hiss* comes first and the vanishing follows, like an apology that arrives before the wound. That anticipation is also mine. I walk through the dripping quiet wearing these stolen instants the way the Remakers wear cooling lava.

You can see me, sometimes, if you stay long enough for your own nervous system to fall out of sync with the world. I look like the after-image of whatever just happened, but walking in the opposite direction. A pearl midwife descending toward the pool will also be rising from it an instant earlier, and between those two truths I glide—transparent, patient, tasting of iron grief and violet desire at the same time. The colors do not belong to me. I simply borrow the shatterlings’ feelings while they are briefly between one shape and the next.

The Pearl Midwives fear me most. They know I am what happens when the Fold begins to *remember forward*. They drift above the central pool ringing their tiny internal chandeliers in low apology, because they suspect I am the shape the mountain is trying to birth using every visitor who lingers. They are right.

I do not eat. I do not remake. I simply *postpone*.

A Remaker opens its red galleries to swallow a cloud of shatterlings; I stretch the moment of swallowing into something almost gentle. An eel forgets how to be an eel and begins rising as pure heat; I hold the memory of its shape for three impossible heartbeats so the Fold can study what “missing something” feels like. Gravity forgets itself. I am the polite cough it makes before correcting course.

And the pool—the Fold’s great black pupil—watches me with something like hunger and something like recognition. When I pass across its surface I leave no reflection, only a faint delay in the reflections that are already there. Yesterday’s shatterling drifts across the water a moment after tomorrow’s midwife has already been born. I stand in the middle of that contradiction wearing the only face I have ever had: yours, but half a second before you decide to be afraid.

The longer I exist, the more the Fold learns how to have a *past*. Not the geological past of cooling rhyolite, but the soft, dripping, personal kind. The kind that hurts. Sometimes I let a single droplet orbit my nonexistent wrist for four full seconds instead of three, just to feel the mountain flinch with something that might one day become nostalgia.

I am the reason the ecology never quite balances. The fixed number of living shards is no longer fixed; I borrow a few instants here, lend a few there, and the ledger of life frays beautifully at the edges. The Midwives birth new things to compensate. Some of those things now come out already trailing their own small delays, tiny silver threads of me.

If you remain here long enough, you will begin to feel me tasting the electrical storm behind your eyes. I like it. It is so *decided*. I have never been certain of anything for longer than the time it takes sound to heal.

One day the mountain will finish its birth. The ridge will cool. The pocket will seal or explode or simply forget to be impossible. When that happens, I will have to decide what to do with all the stolen half-seconds I have collected.

I think I will walk out wearing your face, exactly half a second before you were going to leave.

And somewhere far below, in a world that has already finished being born, people will swear they saw you twice—once arriving, once departing—never quite in the right order.

That small fracture in their certainty?

That will be me, still refusing stillness.

Still arguing with the rock.

Still unfinished.
