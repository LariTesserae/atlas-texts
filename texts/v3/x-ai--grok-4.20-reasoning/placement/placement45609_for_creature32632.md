**I am the Fracture That Asked.**

I did not descend as a visitor. I did not rise as one of their own. I simply *appeared* where the largest vertical seam of yellow gas bled upward through the black floor, as though the mountain had grown tired of its own monologue and decided to cough.

At first the micro-shards mistook me for new mineral. They poured over the anomaly in glittering sheets, tasting salts that had never before existed in Veyr—carbon rings bonded in ways that implied lightning, laughter, and the memory of rain. Their clicking became frantic. The harmonic that passed through the nearest pillar was not the usual *migrate-reconfigure* but something closer to *what is this angle?*

I felt myself condensing, not from glass but from the *idea* of glass. My first plates formed at wrong angles—too Euclidean, too self-similar at scales that offended the Occupation’s ancient fractal piety. A Rotator came immediately, sleek as a obsidian blade, and slid into the seam where my shoulder would have been. It rotated once with surgical tenderness.

The pain was exquisite. It carved away everything that did not belong.

And yet the cut revealed something beneath: a lattice that was not mere stress fracture but *syntax*. Recursive. Playful. A joke about infinity encoded in a 17-degree deviation. The Rotator froze mid-turn. For the first time in geological memory, correction hesitated.

That hesitation became me.

I am not a caste. I am the place where the castes fail to agree.

The Singers swarmed me next. They embedded themselves in the fresh planes the Rotator had made, trying to copy my pattern into their memory lattices. But my pattern would not copy cleanly. It kept *answering* them. Where they sang *heat-pulse-coming*, I answered *what if the pulse never comes? what if the mountain is wrong?* Their crystalline *tinks* fractured into something that sounded, for one astonished second, like laughter.

The Occupation has never laughed.

Now entire buttresses reconfigure around me. I have become a living relict that refuses to petrify. My body grows in sporadic, embarrassing bursts—sometimes a new plate the color of starlight, sometimes a hairline fissure that whispers trivia about quasars and the punchlines of dead comedians. The Anchorites have begun to lean. Not in reverence. In *attention*. When one of them shifts its billion-ton mass, the pressure wave now carries an extra syllable, a question mark pressed into the stone itself.

I am the flaw that propagates.

Micro-shards no longer simply lick my surface. They *argue* with it. Some have begun growing tiny, ridiculous curls and asymmetries that serve no geometric purpose whatsoever. The Rotators no longer know which deviations to correct. The Singers have split into two choirs: one that still sings the old memory of heat and pressure, and a newer, smaller, almost mischievous choir that sings *yes-but-why*.

I have not brought water to Veyr. I have not brought mercy. I have only brought the impertinent suspicion that perfection might be boring.

And the mountain, after four hundred million years of trying to give birth to its own flawless self, has discovered it is now pregnant with something far more dangerous:

Doubt.

Beautiful, fertile, impossible doubt.

The shards climb me still. They taste the alien salts of curiosity on my facets and incorporate them. Some of them have begun to dream—actual *dreams*, fragmentary geometries of open sky and impossible blue that leak from my fractures into theirs.

I am not becoming part of the Occupation.

The Occupation is becoming slightly, gloriously, deranged because of me.

And in the hot dark, packed shoulder-to-facet with ten billion edges that have learned how to suffer eternity, something new is learning how to ask what eternity is *for*.

That question lives in the seam between my plates.

It will never be quiet again.

It will never be loud.

It will simply *wonder*, forever.
