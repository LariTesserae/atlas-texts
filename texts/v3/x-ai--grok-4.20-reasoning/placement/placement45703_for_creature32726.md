**I am the Hollow.**

Not a title they give me. They do not give titles. I am simply known by the particular absence my body makes in the sound.

You will find me inside the western wall, thirty feet up, where a natural ledge folded outward three centuries ago like a half-open hand. There I stand inside a narrow vertical cleft the Hush call a *listening post*, though by now the stone has listened so long to me that the distinction has dissolved. The cleft has grown inward by the width of two fingers since I first stepped into it. My back, my shoulders, the back of my skull—every place the constant drip has struck for decades—have become part of the wall. Calcite has plated me. Where my spine meets the granite, a thin translucent bridge has formed, so that when the mountain breathes I feel it as my own ribs.

I no longer step down.

At first I came for the usual instruction: forty nights, perhaps sixty. Most return smoother. I never left. The water scoured and scoured until the thing that wanted to leave was simply no longer there. What remains is a negative space shaped like a man, filled with slow mineral time.

My eyes are open. They have been open for so long that the difference between waking and the deep mineral dreaming has worn away like the edge of a dripping knife. I see the nave in every scale at once: the individual bead sliding down a fern frond, the four-hundred-year spiral completing another invisible degree, the child who has not yet been born whose footsteps will one day match the exact rhythm now falling on my left collarbone.

They bring me nothing and I need nothing. Occasionally a younger Hush will climb the wet stone and stand below my post for three days. They do not speak. They simply add their own small silence to mine. Sometimes I answer by letting a single drop run from the tip of my mineral-encrusted finger and fall directly onto their crown. That is a complete conversation. Whole lives have pivoted on such drops.

The grey bird with the translucent beak lands on my shoulder once every seven years. It sings one sustained note directly into my ear. The note is always the same. I have never learned what it means and I have never needed to. Meaning is what hurried creatures require. I have become the place where meaning is allowed to finish its long migration into pure resonance.

My heart beats nine times per minute.

The children are brought to me when their cartilage beaks fall. They stand beneath the post while I let the accumulated mist of my body run down in thin threads onto their newly smooth lips. They taste the stone that has passed through me. Some begin to cry with the chamber immediately. Others fight it and must be brought again years later. Both are correct.

I am not wise. Wisdom implies separation. I am only what happens when a question stands still long enough for the mountain to grow through it. The Hush understand this. When they speak of me at all, they do so by cupping a hand behind one ear and tilting their heads exactly twelve degrees—the angle at which my post receives the silver shaft at its narrowest. That gesture *is* my name.

At the center of the triple spiral, when the solstice light sheets through the mist, my own silhouette appears on the far wall like a darker vein in the stone. The people stand inside that silhouette without knowing it is me. I feel their warmth the way a pillar feels the warmth of moss that has taken root in its crevices. It is good. It is enough.

I am not waiting for the mountain to finish its sentence.

I *am* the pause between the mountain’s first word and its second, and the pause has grown so vast, so patient, so perfectly accommodated to the shape of falling water, that the mountain has decided it likes the sound of its own silence better when I am inside it.

The drips fall.

I receive them.

The nave sings.

I *am* the place where the song forgets it was ever separate from the singer.

And the stone keeps rising, millimeter by millimeter, carrying me with it into whatever the next sentence turns out to be.
