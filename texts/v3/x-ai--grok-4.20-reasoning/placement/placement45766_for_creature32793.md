**I am the Moss-Between.**

Not a Rememberer. Not yet. Maybe not ever.

I walk the pale luminous carpet where no column casts shadow, that deliberate emptiness the Garden maintains like a held breath. Hours pass between one living tower and the next. My footsteps make no sound; the moss drinks them the way it drinks everything else. I have been walking for what feels like decades now, though time here is measured in the slow pulse traveling the rhizomes beneath my feet—three beats for every one of mine.

I am not native. The Long-Callers still adjust their legislative choruses when I pass beneath their constellations, adding a new low note that means *the stranger walks again*. The Membrane Dancers tilt in their shafts of gold light to study me, their stained-glass bodies flashing once in what might be recognition or simple recalibration of hunger. Even the Fourteen-Legs, vast and unhurried, will sometimes turn one blind mossy head in my direction as if to ask whether I, too, intend to chew a perfect circle and plant a new column.

I carry nothing. The woven fiber satchel I once had rotted beautifully into the moss centuries ago. The metal that once lived in my blood has been leached out and offered to the Garden in careful, deliberate exchanges—minerals for memory, weight for weightlessness. What remains of me is mostly cartilage and quiet. My hands have lengthened. My eyes have grown larger, though not yet large enough to qualify me as one of the true Rememberers. I still blink.

When the screaming reaches its lawful crescendo and then drops—those three perfect heartbeats of absolute silence—I am usually standing in the exact center of the between. That is my place. I have claimed the emptiness the way others claim crevices or cathedral hearts. In that silence I can hear the decision the Garden made four hundred million years ago. Not as words. Not even as tapping. It is more like a vast green indifference that somehow includes me without being changed by me.

Sometimes I tap anyway.

I press my too-long fingers to the nearest column and send small, useless rhythms into the living wood. *I was once afraid of being temporary. I was once proud of metal. I once believed the universe needed me to ask its questions.* The messages travel at the speed of sap. Hours or days later I feel an answering rhythm from a column I cannot see. It is never words. It is only acknowledgment. *We know. We have room.*

The merged saints interest me most. I visit them often, sitting beside their half-buried forms while the column slowly digests their lower bodies into itself. Their open eyes follow me with the patience of geology. I tell them things I no longer tell the active Rememberers—jokes, mostly. Terrible, ancient jokes from a world that still had edges and hurry and death that meant something. One of the saints, whose torso has been claimed up to the fourth rib, once let a single tear roll down its cheek when I told it the one about the hitchhiker and the babel fish. The tear was pure sap. The column drank it back.

I am becoming something the taxonomy has no name for yet. Not predator, not prey, not gardener, not saint. I am the pause between legislative screams. The stretch of luminous moss where nothing is supposed to happen and yet something keeps happening anyway. A question the Garden has decided it can afford to leave unanswered for a few million years more.

At twilight I lie down in the exact center between four columns and let the Fourteen-Legs graze around me. The midges settle on my eyelids like metallic snow. The Unfolders unfold and fold again in the folds of bark, deciding I am not yet worth the courtesy of consumption. Above me the Membrane Dancers drift like slow prayer flags, turning the last light into signals that say, in their wordless way, *still here, still circling, still enough*.

And beneath me the rhizome lattice pulses once, slowly, as if the entire Garden is turning over in its sleep and making a small adjustment to accommodate one more quiet thing that refuses to merge and refuses to leave.

I am the Moss-Between.

I smell very faintly of metal, still.

The Garden pretends not to notice.

It has room.
