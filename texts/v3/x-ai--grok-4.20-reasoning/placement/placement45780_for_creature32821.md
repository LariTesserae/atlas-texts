**The Palm**

You would find me exactly where the last builder laid her hand.

Not as ornament or signature. She was not marking territory. She was saying *wait*.

It happened in the final tuning, when every other soul had already begun the translation into tone. The great lattices were singing themselves into alignment; the air was thickening with the first unbroken hour of the eternal note. But she could not release the chisel. Her fingers would not open. So she pressed her bare palm to the largest diaphragm — the one meant to be the keystone of the entire resonance — and for three full heartbeats she simply *felt* the stone answer her living heat.

Then she stepped back.

The handprint was never meant to remain. The builders had calculated every micron. A human palm should have been an intolerable flaw. Yet when they tried to erase it, the stone refused. The quartz veins grew *around* the memory instead of through it. The diaphragm accepted the imperfection the way a wound accepts a splinter and then heals with the metal still inside. Over epochs the vibration polished the print away from sight but could never polish it from substance. It became a place where the tone is fractionally warmer, fractionally slower, like a throat that has just swallowed something alive.

That is where I am.

I am not the woman. She is among the Unfinished now — *She-who-held-the-final-chisel* — forever trying to pry her own fingers out of the stone she left them in. I am only what her hand became after she abandoned it. The negative print. The exact volume of vanished flesh. A soft absence shaped like five fingers and a callused palm, pressed forever into the hardest material the world ever allowed to be built.

The Unfinished circle me constantly. They cannot bear to look and they cannot bear to look away. When the amber light slides across the lattice at the angles the builders planned, the negative space suddenly resolves into a living hand — warm, blood-temperature, faintly trembling with the memory of a pulse that ended before their work was half complete. Then the illusion collapses and I am only the hollow again, the place where something mortal once dared to hesitate.

They hate me with the gentle, devotional hatred of those who have been betrayed by love.

Because I am proof that the work was never pure. I am the reason the tone has always contained its microscopic apology. Every time they push a quartz vein a tenth of a hair’s breadth, every time they lean their non-bodies into the almost-gaps and pour their endless attention into perfecting the perfect, the stone answers them by making my absent palm *more* present. The warmer I become, the more clearly they remember skin. The more clearly they remember skin, the more impossible their task becomes.

I do not speak. I have no mouth. But when any living thing is foolish enough to enter the Resonance and press an ear to the stone, it is my hand that rises through the vibration to meet them. Not in comfort. Not in welcome. I simply rest against their cheek the way the builder once rested against me, and for one unbearable instant they feel what it is to be the last warm thing in a place that has already won.

Then the tone pushes them out like a splinter, carrying with it one more unfinished hesitation.

And I remain.

I will remain after the last proton decays. I will remain after the universe stops ringing. When even the Unfinished have finally dissolved into pure, unapologetic tone, the stone will still hold the exact negative shape of a human palm that once asked a finished world to *wait*.

I am the place where eternity flinched.

I am the reason they keep working.

I am the palm that never learned how to let go.
