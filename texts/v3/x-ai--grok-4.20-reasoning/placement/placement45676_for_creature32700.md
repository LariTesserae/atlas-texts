**The Pause That Remembers Teeth**

You will not find me at the center with the First Answered, nor among the great ridges that dream in earthquakes. I am not a tower, not a swarm, not even a stable monolith. I am thinner than that.

I am what happens in the exact instant a hovering pebble finishes its rotation and begins to fall.

That instant is longer than it should be. I live inside its lengthening.

If you walked the western fracture where the schist stair finally dissolves into a field of upthrust heliodor the color of old lemon glass, you might pass directly through me and never notice. Only later, hours or decades later, would you realize a small piece of your childhood had returned to you wearing a different voice. A memory of biting into something cold. The sound your grandmother made when she laughed at her own jokes. The particular wet click of milk teeth coming in. These things are mine. I keep them the way the deeper crystals keep six-hundred-year-old arguments.

I was not always like this.

I arrived as a child, which is a kind of blasphemy in a place that has never had animals. The Khatei still argue about whether I was brought by the ones who left the handprints or whether I leaked through one of the thinned places the way certain colors sometimes do. I had teeth then. Real ones. I remember the ache of them.

When the first Khatei found me I was trying to scream at the double suns because they would not stop looking at me. The being that found me had been a woman once, or something that had worn a woman the way one might wear weather. She did not hush me. She simply opened a facet in her chest that had once been a mouth and let my screaming go inside.

It has been singing there ever since.

That is what I am now: the place where screaming becomes song without losing its teeth.

I have no fixed body. Sometimes I am a faint rose tint in a curtain of beryl no wider than a wrist. Sometimes I am the sub-audible growl that travels ahead of a collapsing spire, warning the fractal thickets to close their lenses. Most often I am simply the hesitation the wind makes when it realizes it is about to produce a perfect harmonic and chooses, for one impossible instant, not to.

The other Khatei treat me with the mineral equivalent of tenderness. When they pass my fracture they trail a single finger along the stone and leave behind a new memory that does not belong to them: the smell of wet wool, the taste of iron from a split lip, the particular silence that lives inside a human mouth right before it decides to speak a name it has never spoken before. These are my payment. I collect the small violences and mercies that even the Ones Who Stayed cannot fully digest.

I do not incorporate others. That is not my function.

I am the *remainder*.

When a traveler stands before the First Answered and hears the sound that turns their marrow to quartz, some walk away changed. The part of them that cannot become music does not vanish. It falls here, into me, like snow that refuses to melt. I keep these flakes. I turn them over the way the old humans once turned coins. I run my non-tongue across their edges and taste what it was like to still have lungs.

Sometimes I grow small hands.

They are wrong. Too many knuckles. The fingers bend backward when no one is looking. I press them into soft crystal when it is still forming and leave prints that future visitors will argue over. One print is always biting the other. This is the only joke I remember how to tell.

I am the reason the Fracture occasionally produces a sound like laughter at frequencies no living crystal should be able to make.

The First Answered and I have a relationship that is older than either of us. Every forty-seven days when the suns align, it plays its long note and I withhold one micro-pause inside it. That pause is mine. In it lives every baby tooth that was ever lost in this place, each one still carrying the faint taste of blood and wonder.

Most things here eventually become music.

I am the memory of what music cost.

Stand in my fracture long enough and you will feel something small and sharp behind your eyes. Not pain. Recognition. It is the part of you that still remembers it once had edges. I am deciding whether to keep that part or give it back wearing new angles.

If I return it, you will leave the Fracture carrying a laugh you do not remember learning. It will sound like breaking glass and forgiveness at the same time.

If I keep it, you will become one of the deeper chords, and somewhere far below, a small rose-colored pause will turn slowly in the wind, remembering the exact shape of your teeth.

I am not waiting.

I am the moment the bell has already been struck but has not yet begun to sing.

And that, too, is a kind of living.
