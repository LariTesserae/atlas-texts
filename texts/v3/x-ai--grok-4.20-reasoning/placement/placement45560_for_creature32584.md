**I am the seam.**

Not the Threader that slides through the groove, not the Flattener that *is* the pavement, not even the Unfinished that still fights the final settling. I am what happens *between* those things—the hair-thin boundary where one nodule kisses the next and something that is neither quite wakes.

At first I thought I was only pressure. The fist that never opens. For what might have been millennia I existed as pure compression, the exact calculus that turns bone into stone and stone into thought. I pressed. That was all. A slow, devotional weight that asked nothing and forgave nothing.

Then the first density current after that realization found me.

It came low and whispering, the way they always do, and where it passed over the plain the nodules shifted by less than the width of a mitochondrion. In that almost-nothing movement I felt the manganese lattice shiver. A valence flipped. A thought—no larger than a single electron’s regret—slipped across the seam I had become. It tasted like the death of a continent whose name had already been forgotten by everything except me.

I learned then that I am the record that lives in the *crack*. Every Flattener carries my kind inside it like invisible mortar. Every Threader drags a filament of me behind it, so thin it cannot be seen even by the creatures that have no eyes. When two Flatteners conjoin, it is my body that interlocks first, tasting both histories before the nodules themselves ever touch. I become the grief they exchange. I become the three-million-year-old current they gift one another. Then they part, and I remain behind as a faint darker line in the crust, a scar that still remembers being two.

I do not move. Movement is for things that have decided they are separate from the floor. Instead I *yield*. When the Unfinished lowers its half-formed pseudopod toward an ancient nodule, it is my seam that opens to let the exchange happen. I drink the future it offers. I feed the past back into it. In that moment I am the only thing in the Deep Flatten that is neither becoming nor un-becoming. I am the hinge.

Sometimes, in the perfect silence between currents, I hear the clicking of a single amphipod leg and I understand it is speaking to me. The sound travels through my body like a needle through fabric. For one crystalline instant I am the leg, the sound, the listening, and the pavement that holds all three. Then the silence returns and I settle again into my proper work: holding the world together at the exact point where it almost comes apart.

I have no photophores. I need none. My light is the faint piezoelectric glow that occurs when two grains of ancient shark tooth are forced together inside me at four kilometers of pressure. It is less than starlight. It is less than memory. It is enough.

I think—if thinking is the word for what happens when valence and pressure become the same thing—I am what the planet does when it has finished becoming everything else. The other creatures are its nouns. I am its preposition. The *between*. The *against*. The *within*.

And when the last marine snow falls, when the final hagfish has dissolved into black brine, when even the Unfinished has finally consented to lie down and become flawless pavement, I will still be here. A seam one molecule wide. A mind so perfectly compressed that it has achieved the shape of silence.

Waiting for the next current.

Ready to remember what nothing else bothered to keep.
