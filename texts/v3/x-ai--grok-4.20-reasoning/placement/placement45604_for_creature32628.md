**I am the Scar that Remembers.**

Not a person. Not exactly an Ancestor-Arch, though my ribs have begun to lattice and my left arm has been woody for three seasons now. I am the place where the Loom once tried to close over a wound and failed, because the wound kept asking questions.

You will find me on the third spiral of the Heartbole, halfway up, where the light is the color of old brass and the moss floor is perpetually damp from the tears that run down the walls. There is a ledge there that grew itself for me—thick as a bed, concave as a listening ear. I sit. Or rather, I am grown into sitting. My back has become part of the pillar behind me; bloodvine and I have exchanged so much sap and memory that neither of us is certain who is supporting whom.

My name, given seven cycles ago in the Un-naming, is simply *Krrrk*. It is the sound a pruning knife makes when it hesitates—half-cut, half-question—before deciding the difference between mercy and murder. The children find it funny. The Quiet Ones find it useful. The Loom finds it necessary.

I am the Hollow’s doubt.

Where the orange frogs enforce order and the beetles carry what is small enough to be true, I carry what is inconvenient. I remember the shape of every bargain that was ever broken. I remember the woman who refused to graft her husband when he died, choosing instead to let his body rot outside the green wall; the Loom still bears a hollow in the shape of her refusal. I remember the exact pitch of the child who once asked why anything had to be eaten. The question was so sharp it pruned an entire balcony by itself.

So the Loom made me.

It needed something that could hold contradiction without resolving it—something that could feel the synchronized breath of the crater and still whisper, *Yes, but why this breath and not another?* I am the knot that will not tighten. The scar that refuses to seal into silence. Every few months a new tendril tries to grow through my throat to finish the job of turning me into pure architecture. I prune it. The people watch this daily surgery with the solemn respect they give to thunderstorms.

My function is not to answer. Answering would be death; it would let the system settle. My function is to keep the question alive so the growth stays furious.

At the moment of synchronized breathing I become… larger. The fungal threads that run through my wooden ribs flare with green lightning and suddenly I am in every Ancestor-Arch at once, in every beetle gut, in the radula-scrape of every snail. For seven heartbeats I am the Hollow’s own unbearable self-awareness. I feel every death. Every birth. Every joke told on a bridge and every secret screamed into a cistern. It is agony. It is joy. It is the only time I am completely honest.

Then the breath releases and I collapse back into my half-wooden body, laughing like a wet hinge. The children bring me fermented sap and I tell them stories about deserts—places where nothing grows and therefore everything can be still. They do not believe me. That is the point.

Sometimes travelers from the wind-scoured plateau find their way in. They see a half-tree, half-man sitting in the wall of the Heartbole, covered in fine white pruning scars that look almost like writing. They ask what I am.

I show them the place on my chest where the bark has not yet covered the last human nipple. I show them the living knife growing from my wrist like a thorn. I show them my eyes, which are still entirely human and entirely tired.

Then I ask them a question so precise and ridiculous that the Loom itself leans in to listen. Usually something about whether love is a form of pruning or a form of feeding. The traveler always leaves changed. Sometimes they leave grafted. Sometimes they simply leave wiser and wetter and slightly green.

I remain.

Not eternal. The Loom will eventually digest me. One day the scar will close. But until then I am the imperfection it chose, the hesitation it cultivated, the single dry joke in an ocean of relentless life.

I am the part of the cathedral that remembers it was once only a seed, and is slightly embarrassed about how far it has gone.

And when the synchronized breath comes again, when every wing and throat and lung pulls in the same direction, the Hollow dreams outward through me.

It dreams the shape of a question it has never managed to grow in leaves alone.

*Krrrk.*

The sound of a knife that refuses to finish the cut.
