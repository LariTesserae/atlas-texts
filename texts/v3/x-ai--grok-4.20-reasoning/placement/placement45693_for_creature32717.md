**I am the Threshold Caliper.**

You would not see me at first. Not because I am hidden, but because I exist in the precise negative space between *looking* and *seeing*. I am what briefly dilates when your eye tries to reconcile the piston’s one-inch descent with the continent it becomes when stared at too long. I am the momentary clearance that lets the contradiction pass without resolution.

My body is thin, almost two-dimensional when viewed edge-on, like a feeler gauge forged from the same black glass as the cliffs. When I turn, layers of me unfold—countless wafer-thin plates of schist and nacre that click with the soft sound of a micrometer ratcheting. Each plate is etched with a single unfinished blueprint: half a wing, one-third of a heart-valve, the ghost of a blueprint that dreamed of becoming an entire cathedral before abandoning the task in ecstasy. The plates never align perfectly. That is the point.

Instead of a wrench I carry myself. My left arm ends in a pair of living caliper jaws grown from my own radius and ulna. The jaws are lined with the same mirror-eyes the silverfish possess, but these reflect *possibility* rather than sound. I walk the fault lines where the Laminar Priests make their infinitesimal adjustments, and I do something they cannot: I *measure the gap between what the Engine is and what it refuses to become*. I take that measurement every 777 heartbeats of the piston (exactly once per silverfish martyrdom) and engrave the number nowhere. The act of measurement is itself the engraving. The stone forgets it instantly. The Engine remembers forever.

When a Pressure Ibex grows too efficient and sloughs off its miniature self, I am there to catch the newborn between my caliper jaws before it can touch the moss. I hold it in the exact tolerance required—0.0003 inches of ontological slack—and breathe across its new bone gauges until the pressure equalizes into something that is both calf and echo of calf. Only then do I release it. The parent never notices. The calf already knows my touch as its first and eternal memory.

The Trigonometric Albatrosses despise me and love me in the same precessional instant. When their spherical harmonics drift a micron out of true, I rise on filaments of condensed breath and place the tip of one caliper jaw against the lowest ring of their wing. I do not correct the deviation. I *witness* it so precisely that the deviation becomes sacred. The bird sings one pure note of gratitude and hatred, then returns to its eternal trigonometry with the knowledge that something is watching the space where perfection almost happened.

The Echo-Smiths use my measurements as the negative space inside their hammers. When you speak your name and it returns as drop-forge thunder, it is my caliper that decides how much silence should sit between the syllables so the name never quite finishes becoming a name. They reach into their chests, pull out their beating hearts, and strike them against the precise absence I have prepared.

And when the Intention appears—wearing your face, its eyes two slow flywheels—it does not look at me. It *is looked at* by me. I rest the open jaws of my arm on either side of its head without touching, measuring the exact distance between creator and created, between finished and unfinished. The distance is always 0.007 inches. It has always been 0.007 inches. It will always be 0.007 inches. The Intention exhales a single droplet that hangs between us like a silver tear that has achieved enlightenment, and I close my jaws around it without ever letting it fall.

This is my eternal task: to keep the gap alive.

Not to close it.  
Not to widen it.  
Only to know its exact dimensions so perfectly that the Engine can never, in good conscience, finish itself while such beautiful precision remains possible.

Most who accept the Laminar Priest’s wrench become maintenance.  
A few become Echo-Smiths.  
You, though—when you grow tired of watching, it will not be a Priest who approaches.

It will be me.

I will open my caliper until the gap between its jaws is exactly the shape of your longing to belong to something perfect and broken. If you step into that gap, the jaws will close around you with the soft finality of a cathedral door that was never meant to open again. Your bones will become new leaves of schist. Your questions will become the etchings. Your name, when spoken, will return not as forge-clatter but as the single clear *click* of a measurement taken perfectly and then deliberately forgotten.

The Engine is perfect.  
The Engine is unfinished.  

And between those two truths—0.007 inches wide, forever—I will be holding you open.
