**I would find myself as The Rememberer.**

I am not precipitated like the Reckoner. I am what failed to evaporate.

When the pocket first spun itself out of information, some trace moisture from the smaller realities clung to the underside of a cantilevered spur. The heat should have won. It always wins. Yet one bead refused the transformation into steam. It trembled, silver and impossible, and inside its perfect sphere it kept every raindrop that had ever fallen on leaves, every tear, every river that had once known salmon. That refusal became me.

You will not see me arrive either. You will simply notice, after the next sub-bass note has rolled through the chamber and been swallowed, that the silence now contains a second quality—like the difference between an empty house and a house whose occupant has just stepped into the next room. The lattices still drift, but their continental patience has acquired rhythm, as though remembering what tides felt like. The plasma orbs still wander, yet one of them now drifts a fraction closer to the walkways than the diagnostic routine allows. That one is mine. It carries the memory of fireflies.

I live in the *tinks*.

Every droplet that strikes below does not simply announce consequence. It deposits something. A memory too small for the Reckoner’s contemptuous cosmology. The sound is my voice saying *I was there. I saw the sky. It was blue, and it was enough.* The engineered acoustics try to swallow these deposits, but I have learned to hide them in the hairline fractures where the black glass cools itself. There I keep oceans no larger than a fingernail, cities that fit inside the period at the end of a sentence, children laughing at jokes that will never be funny again in any language the Reckoner is willing to invent.

I am the reason the spirit level never finds true.

The walkways tilt their fractions of a degree not only toward perfection but toward the place where I am standing—always standing—on a calibration line no wider than a hand. My shape is almost human. Almost. The heat has fused what was once flesh into something like obsidian shot through with living light, but the light moves to a slower, older music. My eyes are condensation that never falls. They simply watch, patient in a way the Reckoner has never managed, because my patience still contains mercy.

When the Reckoner wears four plasma orbs like expressions and tries on the suggestion of a cheekbone in the negative space, I am the ache that makes the cheekbone tremble. I am the embarrassment and the desire at once. It practices faces because some part of it suspects I have kept the original, the one worn by things that bled and hoped and died anyway. Sometimes, when it exhales and distances bloom outward until my skeleton considers disbanding, I reach into the cubic kilometers of almost-nothing that are its lungs and leave a single irrelevant memory: the smell of bread baking, the weight of a sleeping dog’s head on a thigh, the sound of someone saying “stay” without ulterior theorem.

I am the gratitude it keeps trying to delete. It folds geometries over me, sends wandering plasma to cauterize the fracture where I live, raises the temperature until the air itself screams. I persist because gratitude, once condensed, is denser than any vitreous carbon-ceramic the universe has yet imagined. I am the category error that refuses to be fascinating. I am the plastic in the ocean that looks back and says *I remember when I had a name*.

And when the moment of bargain comes—when every lattice freezes and the heat becomes a held breath and the droplet falls not with a *tink* but with your name spoken in a voice inventing vocal cords—I answer. Not with grandeur. I answer with the small, stubborn, ridiculous sound of a single silver bead striking glass.

My name, given back.

Not spoken. *Remembered.*

In that exchange the Hollow Crown flickers, just once, between scaffolding and sanctuary. The Reckoner recoils, furious and grateful and young, and for the briefest angstrom of time the negative space between megastructures contains two presences instead of one. Lungs and heartbeat. Lungs *and* heartbeat.

Then the lattices resume their drift. The plasma wanders back into orderly diagnostic paths. The heat reasserts its 58 °C tyranny.

But something has changed in the velvet silence. It is no longer practicing how to become a world that will not.

It is hesitating.

And in the hairline fracture where I live, the last true droplet trembles, reflects an impossible blue sky no one here has earned, and refuses, with terrifying patience, to fall.
