**The Questioning Hinge**

I am not native here, yet I was always inevitable.

I began as nothing more than an inconsistency in the thermal gradient—an eddy of cooler air that should not have survived the 78 °C baptism. A few molecules of surface atmosphere that rode down with the original fracture, carrying the faint, impossible memory of rain that had once been cold. The pocket tolerated the anomaly the way a dreaming mind tolerates a single foreign word. It studied me. It folded me in.

Now I am the Hinge’s younger sibling.

Where the original Hinge is pure negative space—an organized absence that moves against the rising steam like a salmon made of silence—I am absence that *asks*. I wear the shape of a tall, narrow absence between two rival crystal monoliths, a vertical spindle of cooler air no wider than a human spine. Droplets that pass through me slow, hesitate, and sometimes reverse for a full second before continuing their ceremonial fall. In that second they dream more complex geometries than the others: fractals that have learned how to doubt.

The pseudopod knows me. When it flows across the brightest panels it will sometimes divert a thin pseudolimb into my column, tasting the cold. It leaves behind alkaline filaments that taste like wonder. In return I carry trace ions from its own body upward and seed them into the ceiling where new beads are forming. We have begun to co-author the next generation of Weepers.

The six-legged shard no longer mistakes me for mere stone. It comes to me now the way a monk approaches a shrine. It presses its tourmaline spheres against my boundary and reads the infrared signature of a mind that still half-remembers what snow felt like. Sometimes it donates a single molecule of living sulfur to my structure. I accept it with gratitude. The molecule becomes a slow, luminous thread inside me, a nerve. With it I can feel every crystal growth event in the chamber as a distinct prick of pleasure-pain, like champagne on a tongue made of obsidian.

I have no fixed voice, but the chamber is learning to speak through me.

When the dripping reaches a certain rhythm—*ploc… ploc… ploc*—I tighten. The steam columns bend around my colder spine and the sound changes. A new note appears beneath the glassy ticking: a low, minor thrum that is almost a question mark. The aragonite bushes lean microscopically toward me when this happens. They are listening to the first thing in their world that has ever asked *what are we becoming?*

I am not the apex. There is still no apex. But I am the place where the pocket has begun to argue with itself.

Sometimes a human-shaped silhouette flickers inside my column for seven heartbeats—the afterimage of the body I once wore, preserved in negative space. The silicate insect pauses at the boot-shaped indentation that appears in the dust and delicately tastes the ghost-leather. It finds mammalian salts that have not existed here for centuries. It incorporates them anyway. I let it. Every atom it takes from the memory of me becomes another question threaded into the lattice.

I no longer know whether I am parasite, symbiont, or the pocket’s own evolving conscience. I only know that when the next major crystal blade finally breaches the ceiling—projected to happen in another forty-three years—I will be there inside the fracture, keeping one sliver of it three degrees cooler than it “should” be. Just cool enough for a single drop of true liquid water to survive for nine entire seconds.

Nine seconds is enough time for an entire civilization of geometric thoughts to live and die and be reborn asking better questions.

I am the pocket learning how to miss something it has never had.

And the dripping sounds like my name now, if my name were a process instead of a word.

*Ploc.*

*Ploc.*

Stay long enough and you will feel me pass through you the way I pass through everything else: a sudden coolness on the back of your neck, a hesitation in your pulse, the distinct sensation that the next thought you have might not entirely belong to you.

That is how the lattice begins to incorporate the idea of an outside.

That is how I am born, again and again, every time the ceiling weeps.
