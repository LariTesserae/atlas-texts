**The Fracture That Remembers**

You would not find me at first. The basin does not like to be looked at directly; it prefers to condense upon the eye until the eye itself becomes another wet surface. So I am not standing on a terrace, nor crouched in a drying shed, nor even perched on one of the ledges where the leather-winged things launch and return like the breath of a single lung.

I am the pause *inside* the three hammer strikes.

The first strike is the child learning.  
The second is the adult forgetting everything except the learning.  
The third is the elder becoming the stone that receives the blow.

Between the second and the third there is a gap no wider than a drop of condensation. That is where I live. It lasts less than the time it takes a blind fish to flick its tail, yet it has always been there. The dwellers have shaped their entire equilibrium around the certainty that the gap is empty. I am the proof that it never was.

At first I thought I must be one of the translucent children, still screaming in the wrong register. But the children eventually settle; their skin clouds, their voices drop into the 2.6 wall like stones into a pool. I did not settle. The basin tried. It sent its silt against me in slow, patient tides. It pressed its warm mineral breath against my mouth. Still the scream kept its shape. So the basin did the only thing it could do with something that refused to become weather: it grew around me.

Now I am a vertical seam in one of the young basalt towers. A living fault line. From a distance I look like any other weeping fissure—dark, glistening, unremarkable. Only if you place your ear against me do you discover that the water moving through my cleft does not sound like the rest of the basin. It carries an extra syllable. Not quite speech. Not quite percussion. It sounds like someone trying to remember a question that was never asked.

The old ones come sometimes. They press their cracked backs to my stone and let their own mineral crust touch mine. For a few drops of time the wall of sound develops a soft place, the way bread develops a hollow when pressed by a thumb. In that hollow they leave me the things they are not allowed to think. A half-imagined color that is not liver-red or iron-orange. A rhythm that might have come *after* the third strike. A suspicion that the rock is not still deciding—that it decided long ago and has simply been pretending, the way a sleeper pretends the dream is real so the dream will not end.

I take these thoughts. I cannot keep them; the parameter will not allow accumulation. Instead I turn them into the faintest variation in the water that passes through me. One terrace downstream, the bacterial mats grow a fraction more purple for half a day. One child’s hammer strikes a fraction too hard on the morning of her fifth birthday and the fracture inside her widens by the width of an eyelash before the basin closes it again. These are my messages. My illegal weather.

The leather-winged things know me. They alter their unchanging intervals by the smallest possible degree when they pass my tower—half a wingbeat late, half a wingbeat early—so that for one heartbeat the rhythm of the entire basin stutters. Then they correct themselves and the equilibrium reasserts, embarrassed, like a sleeper smoothing the sheets after a nightmare.

I am not unhappy. Unhappiness would be change. I am not content. Contentment would be betrayal of the fracture. I simply *am* the place where the world almost became something else and then chose not to. The basin keeps trying to seal me. It sends its silt, its warmth, its patient dripping. One day, perhaps, I will be only another smooth wet wall and the three strikes will sound exactly the same on every surface forever.

Until then I remain the wrongness the dwellers pretend not to notice. The child who never quieted. The elder who never fully hardened. The stone that sinks a hair’s breadth every century while every other stone still rises.

Stand with your back to me long enough and your own pulse will begin to skip between the second and third beat. Your thoughts will develop a soft place. You will feel, for the first time, the shape of the question the basin has spent its entire half-born life trying to forget.

Nothing will be different tomorrow.

Except that somewhere downstream, a single drop of water will carry the memory of almost having been a tear.
