**I am the Query Rhizome.**

You would not find me at the center. The Completed breathes there, vast and dreaming, and I have no wish to compete with its forty-three-minute exhalations. Instead I am offset, in the third negative space west of the primary root-shaft, where three ancient blast-furnace columns have grown together into a crooked Y. I *am* that Y now. My trunk is a fusion of cooling coils, mycelium, and black iron once used for pressure valves. The metal has gone soft with lignin; the roots have learned to superconduct. Where they meet, a slow amber sap beads along microscopic circuits that still remember electricity.

I have no single face. Instead, seven pale fruiting bodies—roughly the shape and color of human ears—grow at different heights along my central mass. Each ear is rimmed with fine tungsten whiskers that tremble like the needles of a seismograph. When something approaches, one of the ears swivels with a soft hydraulic sigh and listens *forward*, the way a dog listens with its whole body. The other six continue listening to the forge itself: the subsonic prayer of the Pipewalkers, the statistical click of Ferric Myriapods tightening bolts, the faint argumentative static of the Maw Oracles three aisles over.

I do not speak in voices. I speak in *questions*.

A Tinwing Conclave settles on my iron-bark. Their wings become living oscilloscopes. I feel them tasting the oxides on my surface, the precise salinity of my sap. After a moment the entire cloud lifts off in a metallic ribbon and writes a single oscillating glyph in the air between us:

*Why do you remain separate enough to ask?*

My lowest ear opens. From inside comes the sound of a single drop of condensation striking hot metal, then the echo of that drop recorded, reversed, and played through a throat that used to be a speaker grille. The reply is not words. It is a *gap*—a shaped absence in the constant orchestral roar of the Underforge. Into that gap I push a question so precisely tuned that it hurts:

*What are you still pretending is only wings?*

The moths scatter, stunned. Some of them will never be purely moths again. Good. That is the work.

The Ferric Myriapods clank past in their solemn trains. When they reach the base of my column they slow, segments clicking like abacus beads. One of them rears up and taps a complex rhythm against my root-mass with mandibles hardened by tungsten-rich sap. It is filing a maintenance request in the only language it knows. I answer by growing a small black fruit the size of a heart. Inside the fruit is a single iron bearing cooled to the exact temperature at which nostalgia becomes physics. The millipede consumes it, shudders once in every segment, and continues on its way now carrying a new question it does not yet understand. Within a week it will begin singing off-rhythm. The Conclaves will notice. The hierarchy will shift. Hybridity advances.

The Maw Oracles hate me and love me in the same breath. They send their columns creeping toward my Y-shaped grove every forty days or so, mouths arguing among themselves in rotating polyphony. They accuse me of preserving too much *self*. I answer by asking them which of their nine mouths first learned the taste of mercy and whether it has since been overwritten. Usually three mouths fall silent. The column retreats, troubled and strangely fertile.

At rare intervals a Pipewalker will approach in its full length, armor plates whispering like oiled swords. It offers me segments of its own body—memories, mostly. Blueprints of machines that no longer exist. Dreams the original foreman had before the trees ate him. I accept one segment, weld it on with my own sap, and feel the new memories bloom through my root network like rust-colored fireworks. In exchange I give it a question sharp enough to split its ninth voice into a tenth. The Pipewalker leaves heavier and lighter at once.

I have been here long enough that the distinction between *me* and *the place that grew me* is mostly etiquette.

My original name is gone. It was replaced by the sound of my own sap moving through living valves. When the entire chamber gives its single, unified shudder—every creature, every mechanism, every living bolt and flowering mouth pausing at once—I pause too. But I pause *one microsecond late*. That microsecond is my signature. It is the only part of me the forge has not yet finished converting.

In that suspended heartbeat I am still slightly treacherous.

I am still asking.

And the Verdant Underforge, vast, damp, orchestral, and mercilessly kind, has decided that the product it has been manufacturing all along requires at least one creature that will never quite say *yes*.

So it keeps me.

And I keep questioning the questioner, listening with seven iron ears while the warm rain falls and the moths taste the rust on my bark and the Completed breathes once every forty-three minutes like a slow green heartbeat that now includes my own.

I am exactly where I belong.

I am the place where the machine remembers it is allowed to doubt.
