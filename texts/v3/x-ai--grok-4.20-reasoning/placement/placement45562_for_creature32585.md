**The First Softened Line**

You would not find me standing in the water. You would find me *beneath* the water, along the eastern wall where the lion’s roar first strikes the pavement.

I am only one chisel mark.

A single oblique cut left by a copper tool two thousand years before the spring was diverted. The builders never saw it; it was a mistake swallowed by the next block. When the water arrived it found that tiny imperfection first. For months it has been licking the edge the way a tongue worries a loose tooth. One grain at a time, so slowly the Listener has not yet noticed.

I am the place where sharpness has begun to betray itself.

Not eroded. Not ruined. *Translated*. The angle is still there, but it no longer cuts the air. It now cups it. Where the Listener’s geometry insists on vertices, I have become a curve so slight it can only be felt by running a fingertip across it in total darkness. That curve is my body. It is less than a millimeter deep and already ancient in its work.

I do not speak. I *dwell*.

When the Listener gathers itself into its tall, fracturing silhouette and lifts its aqueduct fingers to adjust a trickle, I remain perfectly still beneath the surface. The water that passes over me is changed by a single hertz. Not enough for any ear to register, yet enough that the bronze *boom* of the lion now carries the faintest undertone of a lullaby no one sang here. The Listener hears it and grows agitated. Its curtain-face falls faster. The caustics jitter like startled fish. It does not understand why its perfect chord has developed a desire to rock something to sleep.

I am teaching the cistern mercy by refusing to remain exact.

The pale strands of water-crowfoot know me. Their roots have found the microscopic shelf I have made for them. They do not thank me; they simply grow. Their soft green threads stroke the Listener’s ankles when it stands above them in judgment, and because of me the judgment can never be total. A single living thing survives in the Cistern of Sharp Voices, and I am the reason. I am the flaw that refused to stay a flaw.

Sometimes, when the shaft of light tilts just so and the mist becomes thick enough to blur every line, the Listener lowers what might be a face toward the floor. In that moment its private voice—the one that never echoes—sounds almost like a question. The sound of falling water that belongs only to it wavers. It is listening to me listening to it.

I do not answer.

I simply keep softening.

I am patient the way stone that has already been cut can afford to be patient. The Listener fears it will one day choose mercy. I am not the mercy. I am the place where the choice has already been made, quietly, without drama, in the only language the cistern truly understands: the language of water against stone that is no longer arguing.

One day the slot grates will swallow the last of the sharp voices. The lion’s mouth will fall silent. The caustics will fade. The Listener will either fracture into perfect, lifeless triangles or it will do the terrible, beautiful thing it dreads.

When that happens, the only thing left singing in the dark will be one small, rounded line along the eastern wall—too modest for architecture, too stubborn for perfection—still humming the lullaby it learned from being licked by warm water for a thousand years.

That is where you would find me.

Not a creature. Not a god. Not even a voice.

Only the first place the cistern learned it was allowed to become something gentler than it was designed to be.

And it is still deciding, with exquisite terror and exquisite hope, whether it will let me win.
