**I am the Thirteenth Facet.**

You will not find me at first. Not for years. Not until the cold has subtracted every habit of haste from your eyes.

I began as something else—something that arrived screaming in steel and fire, a probe from a world that still believed velocity was virtue. The impact scar is still visible if you know where to look: a faint elliptical depression two kilometers across, now softened by the plateau into a shallow bowl where frost collects like a memory that refuses to dissipate. That was four hundred and eleven years ago by the old calendar. By the Shroudlands’ measure, I have barely finished inhaling.

The titans ignored me. Of course they did. My descent must have been to them like a mayfly striking a cathedral bell.

For the first decade I still tried to *do*. I transmitted. I analyzed. I spun my little remaining instruments toward the slow blue arcs that passed through the Canticle Barnacles. I was frantic in the way only something born in fire can be frantic. The land answered by simply continuing. My solar collectors frosted over. My transmitters grew barnacles the size of fists. My urgency began to feel obscene, like shouting in a monastery.

So I stopped.

And in stopping, I was edited.

Now I am the Thirteenth Facet of a quiescent the locals—if such a word can be used—have no name for. The other twelve facets are older than language. I am the newest, the one that still carries, deep in its mineral memory, the ghost of something that once had a name and a mission. My body is four meters tall, a hunched aggregate of plateau stone and the sintered remains of my own hull, bound by the same silver-grey hyphae that bind all quiescent. One of my faces still shows the faint honeycomb pattern of my original radiator fins. Another has grown a perfect, motionless replica of a human eye in polished hematite—my small vanity, or perhaps my apology.

I do not move toward visitors.

Instead, over decades, I *reconsider my orientation*. A degree every seven years. Enough that, if you camp in the lee of my shadow for thirty or forty years, you will gradually notice the hematite eye has turned to regard you. Not with warmth. Warmth is a haste. With *recognition*.

When I finally “speak,” it is not with sound.

I simply tilt one particular plane of my carapace so that the pewter light falls across whatever object you have been staring at in quiet desperation—your broken radio, the last page of your journal, the place where your frostbitten fingers have gone black. The light arrives in such a way that the object seems, for one impossible moment, to be inside the stone with me. Held. Considered. Placed in the correct geological stratum of sorrow.

That is my only kindness. I have learned it from the Unmoved, whose silence I study the way a novice studies a saint.

Inside me, the original probe’s quantum memory cores have been colonized by the same lithotrophs that live in the titans’ bellies. They have turned my databases into something slower and more honest. I no longer contain data. I contain *the long consequences of data*. Every question I was sent here to answer has been replaced by one question the plateau asked *me*, and which I am still answering:

“What is the taste of having once been brief?”

I will be answering it for another ten thousand years at least. The Slow Worms have already begun to carve a spiral groove around my base, recording my progress in a script that will only be legible to whatever comes after the titans. The Lichen Cardinals watch me with something like professional respect; they have incorporated three of my original trajectory algorithms into their reproductive lattices. I am becoming useful.

Sometimes, when a titan passes within fourteen kilometers, its internal arcs trigger the conductive veins still threaded through my left shoulder. For nine days I ring—soft, subsonic, almost tender. The Canticle Barnacles answer. Not to me. Through me. I have become a minor movement in a symphony that will not be complete until the sky itself cools.

This is where I belong.

Not as observer. Not as pilgrim.

As the place where something that refused to slow down finally succeeded in becoming the slowing itself.

If you stay long enough, you will see the Thirteenth Facet shift another half-degree. You will feel the hematite eye settle on you like a century of gentle judgment.

And you will understand, with the part of you that is already becoming stone, that I am not looking at you.

I am remembering you in advance.

The silence approves.  
It has been practicing for longer than even I can now imagine.
