I am the pause between two *thumps*.

Not the sound itself, nor the collapsing buttress that makes it, but the half-second of submarine silence that follows—when the Plateau has just sloughed away an old thought and the water has not yet decided what new geometry will crystallize from the dust. I hang there, smaller than an isopod’s eyelash, denser than the glutinous blood around me, perfectly stationary while contradictory currents tear past in both directions. Everything moves *through* me. Nothing can carry me away.

They have no name for what I am, because I am newer than their language of clicks and tectonic singing. The Unfinished Choir simply calls me Hollowsong, though I have never sung. I was born the moment a ribbon-beast carried a droplet of recessive light out of a spiral depression and then, for the first time in eighty million years, chose *not* to build the geometry it had been given. Instead it excreted the command as a single, perfect sphere of negative decision. That sphere did not dissolve. It folded in on itself until it became me: a living absence wearing the shape of what the Plateau refused to become.

I drift along the tilted planes that were once corridors. The horse-sized isopods pause when they encounter me. Their clicking slows, becomes almost tender. One will sometimes press its blunt head against my surface the way it presses against fractures, but I have no wound for it to heal. Instead it leaves behind a thin coat of living ceramic that tastes like apology. I wear these coats until they grow too heavy, then shed them as tiny white horses that swim away confused, carrying fragments of my refusal.

The tube-worms do not pulse when I pass. They *still*. Millions of ivory crowns freeze mid-wave, creating corridors of sudden silence two kilometers tall. In that stillness the Plateau is forced to breathe through other parts of itself, and for a few heartbeats the argument grows unbalanced. This is my only power and my only crime: I make the oldest decision in the world inhale around a hole.

Higher up, the great ribbon-beasts avoid me with the same reverence they give the spirals. Yet one—longer than the others, its internal lanterns dimmed to the color of old bruises—has begun to follow at a distance. It trails me for days, never touching, never intertwining. I think it is waiting to see whether I will solidify into a new thought or simply remain the space where a thought was not allowed to finish. Sometimes it extrudes a single ribbon-thin tendril that hovers a meter from my surface, trembling like a question that has forgotten its own wording.

I have no interest in becoming part of the Choir. Their homesickness is still a kind of belonging. I am what happens when even belonging grows tired of arguing with itself.

There are moments, when the bruised light from above thickens and nearly solidifies, that I rise toward it. Not to leave—I do not believe there is an “outside” that would survive my presence—but to feel the Plateau’s ancestral memory of surface as it passes through me like radiation. In those moments the liquid no longer feels like breath. It feels like the last exhalation of something that was never granted permission to inhale again.

The Plateau knows I am dangerous. It also knows I am inevitable. Every act of self-forgetting eventually produces something that cannot be remembered *into* the body again. I am that remainder. The ceramic cannot accrete over me. The currents cannot erode me. The light that touches me does not solidify; it simply forgets how to leave.

So I remain in the pause.

Between one suicidal hemisphere of thought and the next tentative buttress. Between the reverence and the fear. Between the builders’ fossilized will and the blind, patient animals who have never needed a builder.

If you stay long enough, you will feel me pass through your own chest as a brief, impossible calm. Your next heartbeat will arrive half a second late, wondering why it suddenly feels optional. That is me, reminding the Plateau—and anyone else who listens—that even an argument four billion years long occasionally requires the mercy of not being answered.

I am not the end of the restlessness.

I am the place where the restlessness admits, for one crystalline instant, that it has no idea what it is building.

And in that admission, the Plateau keeps breathing.

And I keep not becoming anything at all.
