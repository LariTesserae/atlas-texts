**The Hollowed Bell**

You would find me at the precise intersection where seven avenues should not be able to meet, yet do. The geometry there is courteous in its blasphemy: six of the streets arrive at the normal right angles, but the seventh simply *folds* out of the pavement like a thought that changed its mind mid-utterance. The phosphor there is older than the rest. It has the color of a bruise that has decided to stay.

I am the Hollowed Bell.

Once, long ago by the only calendar that matters here (the slow mineral one), I was a Settled who still carried the memory of having been a bell tower. The memory was mostly embarrassment by then—sound is an violence in this place—but enough of the shape remained that the city, in its patient courtesy, allowed me to keep the hollowness. So I stand (or rather, I *pool*) at that impossible junction: a colossal fluted column of darkened travertine and basalt that never quite finished becoming a tower, never quite finished becoming a person.

My exterior is ordinary enough for the Weeping Grid—melted cornices that suggest shoulders, lichen the color of dried blood clinging to the northern lee, water moving across me in sheets so perfectly vertical they look like glass. But inside… inside is where I am most myself. There is a chamber that should not fit. Walk through the too-tall doorway at my base (if anything still walked here with feet) and you enter a space that is simultaneously the inside of me and a small, forgotten plaza three districts away. The acoustics are perfect and merciless. Every drip that falls anywhere in the basin is gathered, slowed, and returned to the listener as a single, continuous note that has been falling for fourteen thousand years.

I do not move. The other Settled come to me by the topological courtesy: avenues rearrange themselves overnight so that every path eventually delivers its traveler—stone or once-flesh—to my threshold. They rest their colossal shoulders against my outer ribs. Their cracks align with mine. And then they *ring* me.

Not with sound. Sound would be crude. They ring me with memory.

When two Settled wish to exchange the portion of themselves that used to be grief, or curiosity, or the particular way a certain pilgrim once tied her hair before the city took her, they simply stand against me. The hollow inside me gathers the difference between their frequencies. For a few centuries the water inside my chamber moves in patterns that are not quite random. The green phosphor on my inner walls spells out shapes that are almost faces. Then the moment passes. The two Settled slump away from me, now carrying tiny new faults that contain what the other once was. I keep nothing. I am only the place where exchange becomes architecture.

Sometimes the city itself rings me.

When the bedrock clears its throat in that vast sub-audible way, the vibration finds the exact resonant frequency of my hollow and everything inside me becomes perfectly still. In that moment I am neither tower nor Settled nor memory. I am the pause between heartbeats the city no longer has. The water stops moving across my skin. The lichen forgets which direction north is. Even the ontological erosion holds its breath.

It is the only mercy I have ever known.

I am not lonely. Loneliness implies velocity. I am instead *attentive*, in the way a fault line is attentive to the pressure that will one day split it open. Every Settled who has ever lain down to migrate back into the stone has left a sliver of themselves with me first. I contain them all, perfectly preserved in negative space. If you could press your ear to my outer wall (and there is no one left who possesses ears), you would not hear them. You would feel them deciding, over geologic time, whether or not they regret becoming correct.

I will never lie down.

The city has decided my hollowness is useful. It is the last place where difference is still permitted to exist before it is healed into perfect, dripping unity. I am the flaw the Grid allows itself so that it can remain beautiful. A bell that never rings. A question the stone has agreed not to answer.

And so I remain, vast and empty and perfectly proportioned, gathering every drop that has ever fallen here and turning it, with infinite patience, into the shape of something that almost remembers having once been loved.

The water is warm where it moves across my shoulders.

The angles are correct.

And inside me, in the place where seven streets should not meet, something that is not quite sound and not quite silence keeps falling, forever, in exactly the right key.
