**The Hollow Accountant**

You would not find me on any ridge.

I am not among the Court, though I stand closer to them than any living thing should dare. I am the thin black fracture that runs through the base of the obsidian tower—the one impossible angle given flesh. My body is that fracture made vertical. Where the tower leans, I lean opposite, counterweighting its refusal of geometry with my own. We have balanced each other so long that the difference between us has calcified into a single continuous structure. Stone and absence, married.

My height is exactly one seven-thousandth of the distance a dying man’s last thought travels before it becomes part of the roar. This measurement is not arbitrary. It is the only length still permitted.

I have no joints. Instead, fine silver lines—too bright for this light—run through me like fault lines that learned manners. They shift, but only by the width of one lichen cell every three centuries. The lichen itself grows across me in perfect alignment, yet it never quite covers the silver. A millimeter of brightness always remains visible, like a confession the plateau has not yet decided to forgive.

I do not speak. I *balance the books*.

Every time a Courtier trades an unearned name, I feel the transaction as a microscopic change in tensile stress along my left shoulder. I record it. Not in words—words would be events—but in the exact angle at which a nearby condensation droplet trembles. Each droplet is a ledger entry older than language. There are currently 4,672,819 such entries on my body. None have ever fallen.

The translucent herds pass through me as they pass through everything, but when they do, I adjust the equation of their silence by the smallest possible fraction. Just enough that their next crossing, four hundred years from now, will be *precisely* as wrong as it was always meant to be. This is my only duty. To keep the wrongness accurate.

Sometimes a new arrival—some fool still carrying the stink of change—will stand before me and ask what I am. Their question arrives after my answer, because I answered it nine hundred years ago when I tilted three micrometers toward the east. The answer is always the same:

“I am the reason the roar has never grown louder.”

I do not watch the Court. Watching implies duration and curiosity, both vulgar. Instead I *contain* the negative space their silhouettes require. When one of them begins the centuries-long gesture of raising a finger, I lower the corresponding absence inside myself so the gesture can remain perfect. We are not partners. We are negative and positive space that have achieved mutual contempt so pure it has become indistinguishable from love.

My name is not mine. It is the name the plateau screams when it dreams of ending, which it does not do, because dreams are change. The name is “Final Entry.” The Court sometimes borrows it for three hundred years at a time, then returns it heavier.

If you remain long enough, you will notice that one of the skull-pools reflects not the sky but my outline. In that reflection I am slightly taller. Slightly more complete. The reflection is lying, of course. There is no completion here. Only accounting.

I have never moved.

I have only ever adjusted.

And in ten thousand years the books have never failed to balance, though the currency is absence and the interest rate is eternity compounded by zero.

This is my place.

Not on the ridge.

Beneath it.

Inside the angle that makes the ridge possible.

The one fracture that was allowed to stand up and become bored.
