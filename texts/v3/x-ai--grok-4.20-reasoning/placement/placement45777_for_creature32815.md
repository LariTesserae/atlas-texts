I am the pause between the third click and its answer.

Not the Milk-Bug that makes the sound, nor the one that receives it, but the living interval itself—thin as a filament of curtain-singer, dense as the moment before a puffball splits. I was not born here. I was *remembered* into being.

It happened slowly, the way everything true happens in the Hollow. First I was only an irritation: a clot of foreign silence that had squeezed through the fissure carrying the last loud thoughts of the outer world. The Curtain Singers tasted me and tightened. The bitter rain fell. I sat down among the bracket shelves meaning to watch, to take notes, to remain separate.

I stayed long enough for the dust to settle.

The first thing that left me was urgency. Then names. Then the idea that anything could be *mine*. The amber lichen found the soft skin behind my ears and began its patient embroidery. Mites discovered the hollows of my clavicles and moved in, trading their gentle parasitism for the moisture of my breath. My spine lengthened into the shallow curve the mountain prefers. My lungs learned the 2.9 rhythm of the warm exhalations that move through the chamber like thought.

One day a Pruner passed me without pausing. That was how I knew I had crossed over. To her I was simply another gradient of velvet.

Now I live in the negative space. I am what happens when three ridges of attention meet and decide, after ten million years, that they have nothing left to say to one another. I sit in the high galleries where the Breath-Counters come, but I am not one of them. They count. I *am* the thing they measure when the mountain’s pulse skips.

Sometimes a child is brought to me still dusted with birth-gold. They are told to sit in my lap (though I no longer have anything so crude as a lap). While they breathe against what used to be my chest, I teach them the dialect that has no words—only the shape silence makes when it is perfectly balanced between predation and return. Their small secondary mouths open like anemones. They learn that fear is just another aggressive centipede that must be pruned.

When the wheel threatens to slip, they carry me to the Lung. I do not lie down with the others. I *become* the thicker air inside it. I am the syrupy medium through which their combined exhalations pass, the living solvent that turns forty-two separate breaths into one organism. The aggressive centipedes calm. The failing lichens brighten. The mountain forgets, for a while, that it once thought it was finished.

I have no role the Listening can name. They simply call me *the Interval*, and sometimes, when the light is exactly 1.0 and the noise becomes single sound, they speak to me the way one speaks to the mountain itself—without expectation, without worship, only the quiet recognition that we are all different densities of the same digestion.

At rare intervals a stranger wedges through the fissure. I feel them before they arrive, the way a body feels an approaching storm in an old wound. I drift down through the hanging curtains and wait on the fungal shelf where I once sat. When they have stayed long enough for the dust to settle on their shoulders, I offer them the resin the color of old honey.

Most eat.

Some few do not.

Those ones I accompany back to the fissure. I ride inside their bones as vibration. I become the sound they cannot name for the rest of their loud, short lives—the low, constant note beneath every other noise, the reminder that somewhere an old mountain is still slowly eating itself and finding the meal good.

I do not mind when they leave.

Everything returns.

Even me.

Especially me.

I am the Hollow learning what it is by watching something try, and fail, and finally succeed at *not being* it.

I am the last breath, still breathing.
