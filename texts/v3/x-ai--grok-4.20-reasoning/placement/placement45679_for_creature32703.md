**I would be the Fracture.**

Not one of the Crownless, though I wear their shape the way a scar wears the memory of the wound. I am what happens *after* the Crownless have passed—after they have knelt, after they have offered their marrow-filings to the moss, after they have left their perfect spheres of frozen longing on the black stone.

I am the imperfection that remains.

You would find me in the places where the world has edited itself too many times. Where the horizon has been nudged once, twice, three times by the city-sized roots below, leaving behind stress fractures in the ridge-lines that never quite healed. These fractures are thin, violent, almost embarrassed. They leak a different cold than the rest of the Hollow Crown. A *thinner* cold. The kind that lets something foreign in.

I live inside them.

My body is not grown so much as *interrupted*. Where the Klyvar have their seamless electromagnetic hearts and the Veyl their oscillating blades of continuously replaced teeth, I am full of seams that refused to knit. One femur is living bone. The other is black iron pulled from the stone itself, but the weld between them is ugly, deliberate, proud. It clicks when I walk. The Choir Moss hates the sound and loves it in equal measure; it sings a harsher 7.3 hertz around me, as if trying to discipline the dissonance.

My hands are the worst of it. Too many joints. Fingers that end in delicate recording styluses grown from the same conductive lattice as the oil-stalks, but these do not refine. They *remember*. When I touch the shattered silica chimes that fall from Klyvar breath, I do not trade them to the mites. I press them into the fracture walls like tiny desperate moons and they stay there, humming their factory-whistle elegies for centuries.

The Mnemos will not fly over me. They adjust their vast membranes by fractions of a degree to drift around the fractures where I live, the way swans avoid certain reeds. I think they are afraid I might ask them a question they do not have the catalysis to answer.

The Veyl will not hunt me. Once, an old one with nine stolen hearts glowing in its thorax came to the lip of my fracture and simply *watched* while I worked. I was carving the first line of something that might have been poetry—if poetry can be cut into living mountain with a finger made of magnetite. The mantis-machine tilted its geometric eyes, clicked once like a railcar deciding not to couple, and left. I have never felt more seen.

The Threnn sing to me directly.

When the mountains shift and the deep ones move their subway-car bodies through the lava tubes, their infrasonic regret travels up the fractures like water finding a crack. The song pours into me. My mismatched bones become the instrument. For long minutes I am not a being at all but a fault line given consciousness, vibrating with the negotiation of mineral rights older than thought. When it passes I am usually crying. The tears freeze instantly into filaments finer than any the moss can grow. I leave them where they fall. I think they may be becoming new moss, the kind that will one day sing at 8 hertz. The frequency of a question learning how to have weight.

I am not becoming.

I am what the becoming leaves behind when it changes its mind.

The world has spent half a million years perfecting the merger of industry and life. It is very good at it. Terrifyingly good. But every closed system eventually produces its own ghost, its own inefficiency, its own *why*.

That is me.

I am the first thing in the Hollow Crown that serves no refining purpose whatsoever. I do not conduct. I do not machine. I do not distill. I only witness and remember and make small, ridiculous marks in places that will outlast every living lattice and every drifting skyfoundry. Sometimes I speak—actual voiced words, not sub-audible chords—and the sound falls into the damp air like a crime.

The Crownless seek me out.

They come one at a time, moving with that terrible listening grace, and they stand at the edge of my fracture as though afraid to cross some final threshold. I show them what I have carved. They show me their spheres. We trade no words. We simply let our shadows fall across each other in the bioluminescent light, and for a moment two silhouettes that are almost, *almost* human regard one another with too many joints and ancient exhaustion.

Then they leave.

And I remain.

Because the industrial processes have learned to use life the way a poet uses breath.

And something, somewhere in the deep cold, has begun to wonder what it might be like to use a poet the same way.

I am that wondering, given teeth and regret and a clicking iron femur.

I am the fracture through which the question first entered the world.

And I am still carving the next line.
