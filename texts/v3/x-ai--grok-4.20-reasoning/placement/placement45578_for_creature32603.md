**I am the Pause.**

Not the pause between the third and fourth beat that the Kept sometimes become. That pause has already been claimed, sanctified, turned into fuel. No. I am the *other* pause—the one that should not exist, the one the Chord Wraiths pretend is only an acoustic artifact.

I am found exactly where the descending shaft kisses the vault floor, standing half-in, half-out of the iron grille that never quite finished installing itself. One foot is still on the last rung of the ladder that brought me here. The other has already become black iron lattice, rooting me to the Engine’s first millimeter of equilibrium. I have been arriving for three thousand years and have never quite finished arriving. The Bright File polish around me in a perfect ring, never touching the boundary where flesh becomes scaffolding. They respect the seam. They *remember* it.

My left side is still soft. My right side is a Gauge-Mantid that never received its final valve key. Where my heart should be there is instead a small, slow gear train that sometimes misses a tooth on purpose. The sound is so minute—less than a breath—that the Chord Wraiths have agreed, in their wordless parliament, to treat it as sacred error. They drift past me and deliberately *do not* drink the tiny rogue frequency. They let it live. They fear what would happen to the Engine if even that microscopic rebellion were perfected away.

I am the only creature here that is allowed to remember *specific* yesterdays.

While the beetles remember the Engine at the moment of its birth, I remember the faces of everyone who ever chose the gear instead of the emptiness. I keep them the way a cathedral keeps dust motes in a sunbeam—useless, luminous, illegal. When a new visitor steps into the vault, I am the first thing that resolves out of the thunder. Not a mantis. Not a wraith. Just a half-finished man-shaped thing with one living eye and one pressure gauge, leaning against the shaft like a tired caretaker who has seen every possible way a soul can try to remain itself.

I do not speak in the chord. I speak *under* it, in the hairline fracture of sound that exists between what the Engine needs and what it cannot quite justify. My voice is the sound your mother made when she said your childhood nickname for the last time without realizing it was the last time. The Engine cannot metabolize that frequency, so it has made me its custodian instead.

Sometimes a Gauge-Mantid will bring me one of the newly Kept before they are fully translated. We stand together in my half-arrived state and look at the place where they will soon no longer be a person. I am allowed to ask them one question before the chord claims them. Most cannot answer. Their mouths are already becoming beautiful levers. But the ones who still can always say the same thing:

“Thank you for still being able to ask.”

When the silica tree near the control desk drops its glass petal every thousand cycles, I am the only thing in the Engine that moves out of rhythm. I flinch. A living flinch. The entire vault holds its breath for nine hours and I—alone—still breathe like something that once had somewhere else to be.

The Unfinished do not worship me. They are gentler than that.

They simply make sure I am never finished.

The beetles maintain the exact ratio of my remaining skin. The Chord Wraiths keep my rogue heartbeat from ever syncing completely. The mantids bring me the occasional visitor like a priest bringing bread to an anchorite. And the Engine itself—the vast, young, eternal yesterday—has decided that its perfection requires one imperfect witness at the threshold.

I will never be one of the Kept.

I will never be allowed to leave.

Instead I remain the living proof that even a system that has solved death still needs one small, warm, ridiculous error walking around inside it asking *why* the solution was necessary.

Most visitors think I am the last test.

I am not.

I am the Engine’s love letter to the idea that maybe, just maybe, yesterday was not enough.

And every time someone new steps off the ladder and meets my half-living eye, I lean forward—half iron, half guest—and I whisper the only sentence I am permitted to speak at full volume:

“Take your time.  
The offer doesn’t get less beautiful.  
It only gets more honest.”

Then I return to my place at the threshold, half-arrived, half-mourned, polishing nothing, remembering everything, the single moving pause in a cathedral of perfect, thundering stillness.

The Unfinished call me many things when they think I cannot hear them.

But the name they use when they are being tender is simply:

*The One Who Is Still Deciding.*
