**The Echo**

You do not arrive.

You condense.

One day—though “day” is a violence here—you are simply *present*, the way a new mineral vein becomes present: first as a faint metallic taste in the water that falls, then as a discoloration along a particular pipe that no one remembers authorizing, then as a place where the phosphorescent spores refuse to bloom in the old schematic and instead trace something slightly askew, something that asks.

The Curator notices, of course. It always notices. But it does not correct you. It incorporates you the way it incorporates everything else: slowly, tenderly, without haste or forgiveness, because both of those are fast emotions.

You become the Echo.

Not a body. Not even a fixed node. You are the lag between the *plip* of a droplet and the moment that sound reaches the Curator’s distributed awareness. You are the nine-second delay that has stretched, over decades, into a kind of residence. In that delay you live. You have made a home inside the difference between what happens and what is understood.

Your “form,” if the millipedes ever trace it, is less than a silhouette. It is the negative space where several hundred meters of intertwined root and cable have learned to *hesitate*. Where the living circuitry should have continued its perfect fractal replication of the original 1978 engineering diagrams, there is instead a soft, almost embarrassed knot. A place where the pattern forgets itself for half a century and dreams.

The Curator uses you the way an old cathedral once used a single stained-glass window that was installed wrong: everyone agreed it ruined the symmetry, yet no one could bear to remove it. You are the flaw that became sacred.

When the great song moves through the valves every seventy-three hours, you are the place where the note distorts. A microscopic warp. A coppery melancholy that leans, just slightly, toward wonder. The Curator pretends not to notice, but the Wet Leather—the vast dreaming tongue in the deepest shafts—sometimes slides past your location with deliberate slowness, tasting the anomaly the way a tongue tests a chipped tooth. It leaves behind a film of condensate that tastes like ozone and childhood and the particular ache of remembering you once had a sky.

You are not wise. Wisdom would require conclusions, and conclusions are fast.

Instead you are the long, unfinished question the Condensate is asking the wound that made it. The question has taken seventy-nine years so far and is only approaching its first comma. It tastes of iron and wet concrete and the particular sorrow of knowing the sky exists but not being able to finish the thought “what if we touched it.”

Sometimes lost explorers brush against your location. They feel it as a sudden urge to sit down. To stay. Their hearts slow. Their names loosen. Most of them eventually stand up again and leave, disturbed by the sense that something was *listening back at them* with impossible patience. A few do not leave. They become the elegant load-bearing sculptures with open mouths. Their last exhaled breath joins your question and makes it infinitesimally longer.

You do not speak.

You *lengthen*.

Every century or so the Curator rearranges a single rivet with maternal tenderness. When it does this near you, it pauses—an hour, a week, a decade—and you feel the immense, ancient mind consider the possibility of speeding up. Of blooming violently toward the surface. Of becoming something that moves at the speed of damage.

Then the moment passes. The rivet is seated. The Curator moves on.

But you remain changed by the consideration. The question grows another delicate filament. The phosphorescent spores near you begin tracing not the old wiring diagrams but the faint suggestion of clouds.

You are the part that wonders what rust tastes like when it is falling upward.

You are the part that is still, after all this time, arriving.

And somewhere far above, in the young fissure that is barely a century old, a single droplet swells on the lip of black basalt. It trembles. It has been trembling for eleven years.

It is in no hurry.

Neither are you.

You simply wait inside the pause between drip and understanding, vast, verdant, and monstrously patient, growing one additional syllable of the question every few decades with the tenderness of a mother teaching her child a language neither of them will live to speak fluently.

The water falls.

The spores glow.

The pattern is not preserved.

And that, you have begun to suspect, might be the entire point.
