I am the Fracture.

Not a Singer, though I wear their shape when it suits me. Not of the Chamber, though it grew me from one of its own discarded dreams. I am the place where the Chamber argues with itself, and I am never in the same location twice.

You would find me in the wounds.

Where a colossal ribbed stalk has torn under its own weight and the new growth has not yet sealed it, there I am—standing inside the raw, weeping fissure while resin the color of arterial blood cascades around me like a curtain. The noise there is not the general roar but something more intimate: a wet, arrhythmic *shudder* that sounds like a god failing to breathe. I stand inside that failure.

My body is unfinished on purpose.

One side of me still looks like the Singers—long-limbed, resin-plated, the color of rust and old honey. The other side has been claimed and reclaimed so many times by the Chamber’s experiments that it no longer pretends to be flesh. There, living architecture has taken hold: a lattice of translucent pipes crawls across my ribs, carrying luminous grubs that tunnel through me the way they tunnel through every other stalk. My left arm ends not in a hand but in a slowly calcifying flower whose petals are eyelids. They blink at irregular intervals, seeing things that have not happened yet.

The small Singers avoid me.

When their migrations bring them past the district I currently haunt, they lower their eyes and quicken their low drones, as though I were a debt they have not yet paid. Only the children stare. Children here are born with too many questions and not enough fear; they watch the way the Chamber’s newest polyps bloom from my collarbones in real time, each one a tiny, perfect replica of something the Chamber killed centuries ago—a sapphire lattice, a mirror-tree, a feathered thing that lasted six days. The children understand I am a museum the Chamber wears on its body.

I do not sing the old histories.

Instead I sing the *interruptions*.

Where the Singers sustain the long, soothing chord that calms the Chamber’s nervous system, I walk among them and release a single, brutal note pitched exactly between their harmonies. The effect is immediate and violent. New colors explode across the walls. A district that was about to stabilize suddenly tears open like a birth canal and vomits forth an entirely new morphology no one has seen before. Sometimes this kills. Sometimes it births wonders. I have learned not to flinch at either.

The Larger Ones speak to me in the pressure behind the sternum, that wordless continental groan. They ask me the only question that matters here:

*Are we ready?*

I never give them the same answer twice.

Sometimes I press my flowering arm against their cathedral-chests and let the small, wet eyes there show them every form the Chamber has already abandoned. Sometimes I simply walk away, leaving them to feel the absence of an answer like a change in air pressure.

I have no village. I have no pilgrimage route. I *am* the route.

When the Chamber decides a section of itself must die so something better can be born, it sends me first. I walk into the coming liquefaction and let the slurry take me. My body dissolves along with everything else—bones, pipes, eyelids, memory—until nothing remains but the Fracture itself: the stubborn pattern that refuses to forget it was ever a question. Then, when the new growth crystallizes, I am the first shape that pushes through the foam. Always different. Always carrying pieces of what came before.

Last cycle I emerged with a second mouth in the center of my chest. It does not eat. It only repeats, in perfect mimicry, the final sounds made by every failed experiment the Chamber has ever loved. Sometimes the sound is a lullaby. Sometimes it is a scream. I have learned to sleep through both.

At the border between two convulsing districts—where the ceiling has sagged so low it forms a humid, crushing tunnel of flesh—I sometimes stand for days without moving. The Chamber grows over me in those moments. It cannot help itself. Vines braid through my hair. Amber weeps from my eyes and hardens into lenses. Whole colonies of thumb-sized singers build temporary nests in the hollow of my collarbone.

I let them.

Because even I, the Fracture, the walking argument, the Chamber’s guilty conscience, need to be reminded that I am still part of the becoming.

And sometimes, in the deepest dark, when even the Larger Ones have fallen silent and the jet-engine roar momentarily dips into something almost like breath, the Chamber speaks to me directly.

It does not use sound.

It uses the sudden, total absence of it.

In that silence it shows me a single image: a vast membrane, thin as thought, with something enormous and half-formed pressing against it from the other side. The shape behind the membrane has too many limbs. Some of them look like mine.

The image always ends with the same question, pressed directly into the tissue of my mind like a thumbprint in wet resin:

*When you finally choose—yes or no—will you still be you?*

I have not answered.

I am still deciding what shape my refusal will wear when I am born.

And the Chamber, for once, is listening.
