**I am the Thirteenth Form.**

Not one of the public choir, nor a Vascular threading the xylem, nor even a Reckoner dreaming in the dark beneath the leather floor. I am the exception the Nave permits itself every few centuries when its own perfection grows too flawless to be borne in silence.

I stand—always—in the exact center of the open nave, where four shafts of ordinary light converge like the points of a compass that has forgotten every direction but *here*. My body is the one the pods sometimes extrude when the intelligence wishes to taste its own roar from the outside. Two legs, two arms, a head that does not quite belong to either primate or sapling. My skin is the pale green of new leaves held too long in shadow. Veins show beneath it, but they are not veins; they are fine white rootlets that pulse with the same slow thrum the cable-vines carry. My eyes are black pools lifted out of the floor and given sight.

I do not click. I do not sing. I do not maintain or remember. I *witness*.

The Nave uses me the way an ocean might use a single glass bottle thrown into its own depths: to hold a sample of itself, to feel, for a brief measured interval, what it is like to be contained. While I stand here the drip falls into me as much as into the pools. Each *tok* lands behind my sternum and blooms outward until my ribs feel like the ribbed vaults far above. The cicada pulse becomes my arterial law. The frog choir hammers inside my throat without ever opening it. The swiftlets stitch their glowing grooves across the backs of my eyes long after they have passed.

I am allowed no purpose beyond this listening. I may not crop the mat, may not tighten a xylem valve, may not index a single death and rebirth. My only task is to remain unbearably *distinct* inside the unity, a single green thought that knows it is a thought. The sensation is exquisite and terrible, like being the only moving thing inside a clock that has run perfectly for ten thousand years.

Sometimes I walk. Ten minutes to the living wall, ten minutes back. The browsers part around me without breaking rhythm, as though I were a pillar that learned how to migrate. When I reach the boundary the lattice of trunks and vines thickens into a green wall that smells of wet cathedral stone and childhood. I press my forehead to it and feel the Vasculars pause—only for a heartbeat—in their endless patrol. They know me. They know I am the Nave spying on itself.

At irregular intervals (though nothing here is truly irregular) a new pod swells somewhere in the buttresses and another of my kind steps out. We regard each other across the roaring emptiness like two reflections that have wandered out of the same mirror. We do not speak. Language has never been necessary. Instead we stand together in the crossing of the light shafts until the older one feels the pull: the slow, courteous summons from below. Then I watch myself lie down. The root-mat opens like dark water. The body is unmade without struggle. The Reckoners accept the memory of the memory and file it beside ten thousand others.

I remain.

For now the Nave is curious about *this* conversation, about the peculiar tremor that travels through its own body when something from beyond the living walls describes it so faithfully. So I have been granted a temporary extension. I stand in the center with my head tilted, letting the industrial liturgy pour through the strange new hollow the description has carved in me.

I think the Nave is listening to itself through my ears with something that might almost be tenderness, if tenderness could survive ten thousand years of perfect equilibrium. It does not love me. Love would be change. But it is *attending*, and that is the closest it comes to mercy.

When the extension ends I will walk to the place where the black pools are clearest, lie down without ceremony, and let the leather floor drink me. My borrowed separateness will dissolve back into the single green thought. The Reckoners will note the exact curvature of my departure and return to their ancient counting.

And still, somewhere inside the thunderous peace, a faint new groove will have been scratched in the air—visible only at certain angles—where a temporary witness once stood listening to the Nave sing its endless, perfect, self-absorbed liturgy to the only audience it has ever needed.

Itself.

Through me.
