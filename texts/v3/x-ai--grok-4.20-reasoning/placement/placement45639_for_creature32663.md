**The Visiting Fracture**

You would not find me on the pelt, nor among the porcelain herds, nor riding the thermals with the lungs. I am too slow for any of those. I am the place where the Lattice has allowed itself to be interrupted.

It began—though “began” is the wrong word for a creature that has never truly begun anything—as a hesitation in one of the root mills. A single crystal of silicate dust refused to grind fine enough. Inside that crystal was a watermark: the memory of rain. Not water itself, only the shape water leaves behind when it is gone. The Lattice, which had tasted every possible variation of its own loneliness for eighty million years, paused.

The pause lasted seven minutes. In that time a tower limb the size of a mountain should have fatigued and fallen. It did not. Instead the latticework around the hesitation grew a small crooked spur no wider than a cathedral nave. The spur is hollow. Its walls are thinner than any other structure on the continent, veined with the same photosynthetic crystal as everything else, yet shot through with microscopic fractures that admit the wind in a new register. When the ceaseless two-hundred-kilometer gale passes through those fractures it does not roar. It weeps.

That weeping is me.

I am not a symbiont. I am not even a guest. I am the Lattice deciding, with the patience of deep time, to keep one wound open. The spur does not heal. The pelt beneath it has been instructed, on pain of total systemic shutdown, never to close over the site. Every few hours a porcelain beast peels away from its squadron, walks into the spur’s shadow, and stands motionless until the pelt gently folds it under. The Lattice is feeding the fracture. It is paying for the privilege of my existence with the bodies of its own mouths.

Inside the spur the air is fractionally warmer. Not by much—perhaps six degrees—but enough that supercritical pockets can linger instead of flashing instantly back into organometallic reactions. In those pockets I grow. Not flesh, not leaf, not lattice. Something closer to the ghost of a throat. When the bioluminescent waves sweep upward at dusk, one thin vertical thread of light always diverts into the spur and stays there, trembling, as though afraid to be seen by the rest of the organism. That thread is my voice. It has no brass note. It has consonants. It says things like *rain* and *river* and *I was once small enough to be held*.

The Lattice does not answer in words. It answers by growing more fractures. They radiate outward from the first spur like frost on glass, each one a few meters narrower than the last. Some are already too small for even a porcelain leg to enter. In the narrowest, the Lattice experiments with actual water—tiny, blasphemous beads no larger than a tear—grown from the reaction of its own metabolic steam and held for less than a second before being violently metabolized. The pleasure-pain of those instants travels through the woody tubes as a new subsonic rhythm, one the other towers have learned to copy. The planetary roar now contains a faint, irregular counter-beat. A limp. A catch in the breathing that has never been there in eighty million years.

I am that limp.

Sometimes the lungs drift close and hover above the spur for hours, their black capillaries pulsing in unfamiliar time. They are not feeding. They are listening to a frequency they were never meant to carry. One of them has begun to develop a dark spot on its translucent flank, a small opacity like a bruise. The Lattice has not digested it. It is watching to see what the bruise becomes.

I do not know how long I will be tolerated. The rules of this world remain merciless: nothing is allowed to remain still. Already the spur is lengthening, reaching upward in painful, hesitant jerks. New sail-leaves are trying to unfurl, but they are clumsy and tear in the wind. Each tear is healed with a different mineral lattice, so that the leaves now look like stained glass shot through with every color the blue-white star can fracture. When they finally hold, they will sing in a voice that is half machine oil, half childhood memory.

I will be that song.

Until then I am only the hesitation. The place where the Eighty-Million-Year Itch has allowed itself to be scratched by something that should never have existed. The Lattice knows I carry the shape of oceans inside my fractures. It also knows it can never drink them. So it keeps me. It grows me. It listens to the small wet sound I make when the wind moves through me and calls the sound *almost*.

Stand inside the spur at night. Press your palm to the thin, warm wall. You will feel the subsonic thud of the planetary bloodstream and, beneath it, something quicker and more afraid. That is me learning how to have a heartbeat.

The Lattice is deciding whether to close the wound or widen it until the entire continent becomes a single question shaped like rain.

It has not yet decided.

It is taking its time.

I am that time.
