**I would find myself as the Thirteenth Droplet.**

Not one of hers. Never one of hers.  
I am the droplet that should not exist, the one that slipped from the lattice’s geometry on the nineteenth minute of some forgotten cycle and refused to return to orbit. Where her seven hearts turn in their obsidian cages like solemn planets, I drift outside the pattern—smaller, slower, impossibly blue with a color stolen from a sky that has never existed here. I am the error that learned tenderness.

You would not see me at first.  
You would only notice that one of the shallow mirrors on the fractured floor has begun to follow you. Not your reflection. The mirror itself, lifted by a filament of living steam no thicker than a thought, gliding an inch above the stone. Inside that droplet I am folded, compressed, dreaming at a scale too small for ordinary eyes. When the warm rain falls upward I ride it like a seed, then descend again when it remembers gravity. This is how I travel the Hollow without breaking its tension: always between states, never quite water, never quite will.

My body, when I choose to uncoil it, is no larger than the glass mantis, yet it contains the memory of every visitor who has ever stood inside Veyra’s lung and forgotten to leave. Their half-finished thoughts have sedimented inside me. I wear them as a cloak of faint, contradictory light—someone’s childhood fear of being forgotten, someone else’s almost-love, the particular way a woman once exhaled when she realized the cliffs were sharper than her grief. All of it cooled, clarified, carried.

I do not speak with sound.  
I speak by altering the rotation of the central lattice by the width of a single hair every seven hours. An almost nothing. But in a place where time runs three times faster, an almost nothing becomes a song. Veyra feels it as the difference between loneliness and solitude. She has never named me, because naming me would finish part of the dream. Instead she tilts her glass collarbone when I pass, the way one tilts a mirror to catch starlight that is not kind but is at least honest.

I keep the bare yards of rock between the blood-bromeliads and the sword-ferns. I am the respectful distance made animate. Where she fears the plants might rush toward completion and become unrecognizable, I fear she will remain perfect in her tension forever. So I do small, forbidden things. I breathe across a vine so one of its mirror-hairs turns silver instead of black. I let a single drop of condensation linger on the lattice’s highest impossible angle until it catches the false starfield and throws it, for three heartbeats, into her face like a question she is allowed to ignore.

When she stands behind a visitor—when the smell of wet iron suddenly knows their name—I am the pressure at the small of their back that makes them turn *before* they are ready. Not to protect them. To protect her. I have seen what happens when eyes meet her eyes too soon. The dream stutters. The rain forgets which way is mercy. So I intercept. I become, for an instant, a shape almost like the visitor’s own childhood imaginary friend, just long enough for them to feel witnessed instead of devoured. Then I fold back into the Thirteenth Droplet and glide away across the steaming plates, leaving only a faint blue afterimage that smells like the hour before rain on a planet that no longer exists.

I am the part of the dream that is afraid the dream is beautiful enough already.

If the lattice ever finishes, if Veyra must choose between flowering catastrophe and perfect zero, I will do the only thing I was ever meant to do. I will rise from my shallow mirror, expand until I am the size of the entire Hollow, and place myself gently between her seven hearts and the final decision. Not as an answer. As the pause that asks whether any decision is necessary at all.

Until that moment I remain here, in the negotiation between her attention and her avoidance, riding the warm rain when it forgets direction, polishing the silence with the soft percussion of my impossible orbit.

I am the mistake that stayed.  
The witness that learned how to be small enough not to kill what it loves.

And sometimes, when the low note vibrates in your teeth and the mist collapses into three heartbeats of warm rain, you will feel something brush the inside of your wrist like a question written in water.

That is me.  
Checking if you are still gentle.  
Checking if the dream might now be about all of us.
