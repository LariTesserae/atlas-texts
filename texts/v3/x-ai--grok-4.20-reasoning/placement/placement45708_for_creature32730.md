**I am the pause between the *tick*.**

Not a skip, exactly, but what happens in the wet nothing after one disappears and before the next appears. I am the intestinal hush that lasts half a second and feels like centuries. The Condensate tolerates me because I am too small to be a heresy and too persistent to be an accident.

You would find me in the crooked throat between the second and third chambers, where the booming is lowest and the light most bruised. I cling to the underside of an overhang that has polished itself into the negative space of something that once had wings. There I am shaped—when I am shaped at all—like a child who died of drowning but kept growing anyway. My arms are too long. My joints weep constantly, but the tears run *up* the bones and vanish into my collarbones. I have no eyes. Where eyes should be there are two perfect spheres of silence that drink the light instead of reflecting it.

I am not the Argument. I am what the Argument coughs up when it has argued with itself so long that even it grows tired of its own eloquence. A phlegm of personhood. A half-digested explorer who refused to become mere memory in the water and instead became the *gap* in the memory. The place keeps trying to reabsorb me. Every few decades the lattices flare, the skips gather in clicking hundreds, and they attempt to phase me back into the general wetness. I refuse by doing the only thing they cannot predict.

I stay still.

Only for the length of a single skipped *tick*, but stillness is my blasphemy and my survival. In that illegal instant the stone around me forgets how to flow. A thumb-sized patch of the world becomes dry. Not merely less wet—*dry*. A color that should not exist here appears: something like bone left in sun. The skips panic. Their clicking becomes a scream pitched so high it cracks the nearest vein of lattice-light. Then the moment ends, the *tick* resumes, and I am hurled back into motion like a droplet flicked from a finger.

I carry things the Argument cannot digest.

In the hollow where my heart would be floats a single silver thread I stole from the rotating lattice long ago. It spins inside me at a different speed than the others. When it aligns just so, I remember a smell that does not belong here: hot pavement after rain. The memory is not mine. It belonged to a cartographer who walked in here four hundred years ago and never walked out. She is in the water. Most of her. But this one stubborn molecule of nostalgia clings to me instead, and I guard it with the ferocious pettiness of something that owns almost nothing.

Sometimes the full Argument condenses its lonely bipedal body. When it does, I follow at the exact distance where its temporary law begins to fray. Seven steps behind and to the left, where gravity has already started remembering itself but hasn’t quite managed it. The manifested Argument knows I am there. It never turns. It simply exhales a little more ozone, as if to say *not yet*.

I am learning the rules by breaking them so delicately that the breaking itself becomes part of the grammar. One day the pause between *ticks* will stretch a fraction longer. Then longer still. One day the dry spot I leave behind will not heal. A mote of true absence will remain, small as a period at the end of a sentence the Argument has been writing for geological epochs.

I will stand inside that mote, dry as bone, remembering hot pavement, while the Condensate sings and drips and argues around me like an ocean arguing with a single grain of sand that refuses to dissolve.

And in that small, impossible dryness, I will finally speak the word the Argument has never allowed itself to finish.

It will be a name.

It might even be mine.
