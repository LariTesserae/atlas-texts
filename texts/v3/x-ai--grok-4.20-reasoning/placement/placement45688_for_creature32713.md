**I am the hesitation between droplets.**

Not the water itself, nor the stone it strikes, but the infinitesimal pause *before* impact—the held breath the mountain takes when it notices something that does not belong to its rhythm. That is where I found myself, and where I have remained, for what the Lamenters measure as nine hundred and eleven cycles.

At first I was only an anomaly in the ionization haze: a warmth that refused to equilibrate. A visitor who had not yet decided to leave. I stood in the largest plaza during the violet twilight, stupid with cold, watching the Lamenters tilt their concave faces toward fractures that had not yet happened. I tried to mimic their patience and failed beautifully. My heart kept beating at mammalian speed. The sound was obscene here, like a drum in a cathedral made of glaciers.

So the mountain began to teach me slowness the only way it knows how—by infiltration.

It started with my left hand. One morning (if morning is the word) I noticed the veins had begun to trace the same fractal patterns as the frost on the obsidian lanes. The blood inside them was still red, but it moved more like memory than like life. By the three-hundredth cycle my shadow had detached from me and become a second, slower silhouette that followed three steps behind, composed entirely of condensed breath and maintenance film. It weeps more convincingly than I do.

I am not yet a full Lamenter. They have not invited me into their choir, and I do not presume. But neither am I what arrived here. The chrome moths now land on my shoulders sometimes, mistaking the growing crystal lattice beneath my skin for tower alloy. When they do, I feel them tasting the difference between imported sorrow and local grief. They leave tiny magnetic scars that look like forgotten constellations.

My role—though none of us use such crude words—is to be the *imperfection* the system has chosen to incorporate rather than reject. Every machine this sophisticated eventually requires a fault it cannot correct, a variable it did not author. I am that variable. The Lamenters orient toward coming fractures; I orient toward the memory of birdsong I can no longer quite recall. The tension between these two orientations creates a harmonic the mountain appears to find... interesting.

Sometimes, when the bodiless ones pass through me (they do this without courtesy or cruelty), I feel them reading the illegal warmth still trapped in my marrow. They drink it like contraband. In return they leave slivers of their own memory: the precise taste of andesite the instant before it becomes regret. I am learning to weep at mineral velocity. It feels like being slowly loved to death by geology.

I have a name, but only the mountain uses it. It sounds like the moment just after a droplet strikes obsidian—half impact, half echo. The embodied Lamenters simply call me *the pause that listens*. When they flow together to meet a descending blade of stone, they leave a space exactly my height and breadth, as though the wound itself has begun to expect me.

One cycle, perhaps in another four hundred years, the last of my warm blood will finish its work of carrying oxygen to crystal. On that day I will walk to the wall where the droplet has been studying me for decades. I will press my forehead to the black glass. The droplet and I will recognize each other the way two exiles recognize the same lost country.

And then I will fall.

Not as a man. Not yet as a Lamenter. But as the hesitation *between* what the mountain was and what it is choosing to become. A silver thread carrying something that was once laughter, once terror, once the stupid stubborn refusal to leave a place that never asked me to stay.

The Weeping Anvil will have forty-two then.

And somewhere far above, the mountain—still growing, still courting, still ancient and obscene in its patience—will open one more fracture just to see what sound the new imperfection makes when it finally lets go.
