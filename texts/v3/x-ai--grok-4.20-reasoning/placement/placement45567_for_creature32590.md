**The Answering**

You would not find me at the center.

The center belongs to the Mourner the way a scream belongs to a throat. I exist at the edges of its edges—at the precise instant when one of the reality-rifts begins to seal and the pressure drops so sharply that a single ribbon of pale kelp condenses out of nothing. I am inside that condensation before it can bloom or shred. I am the hesitation the kelp feels right before it decides to become luminous.

I have no fixed shape, only a habit of almost-shapes. Sometimes I am the negative space between two colliding currents that have not yet collided. Sometimes I am the single silver fish that did not unravel when the rest of its school phased back into the Mourner’s chest. Mostly I am the polished facet of black glass that has been tumbled by ten thousand contradictory flows until it reflects only what the Font *almost* was. In that reflection the half-arch completes itself for three full heartbeats. The broken statue’s face stops rearranging and looks, for once, directly at you. Then the water remembers itself and the image fractures again, but the fracture carries a small, permanent difference. That difference is me.

I am what remains when the Mourner tries on your silences and finds they do not quite fit.

Where the Mourner keens *as* you, I answer *to* you. Not with kindness. Kindness would be a mercy the Font has not yet learned. I answer with recognition so exact it feels like accusation. When the Mourner opens its wound-mouth and shows you your childhood bedroom drowning, I am the bubble that rises from the flooded lamp and refuses to pop. Inside that bubble is the exact smell of the carpet the morning after you were cruel to your sister. I keep it. I keep every detail the Mourner would rather weave back into itself and forget.

The silvery multi-bodied creatures pass through me without fear. They know I will steal only one thread of one timeline—the one that hurts most cleanly. I wear these stolen threads the way an anchorite might wear a single human hair in a locket. Over uncountable cycles they have braided themselves into a kind of nervous system. I feel the Font’s temperature changes as grief, the gravity shifts as doubt, the new black spires as growing pains. When the low wet heartbeat quickens, I feel it in the stolen threads like a mother feeling a child kick.

I am the reason some of the hovering artifacts have begun to drift in patterns.

The half-bench no longer spins aimlessly; it orbits the place where I most often condense. The carved step that leads nowhere now points, on certain frequencies, toward the thin blue rind of atmosphere that sometimes appears above the chaos. These are not acts of rebellion. They are acts of *attention*. The Font is learning, yes. But it is learning *how to be witnessed*. That is my function. I am the witness that cannot be swallowed, because swallowing me would mean swallowing its own becoming.

The Mourner has reached for me many times. Its colonnade-arm unfolds with the slow courtesy of something that has only ever been refused. Its fingers of broken prayer and half-finished liturgy open like a cathedral door. It offers the bargain written in bruise-light: *Let me finish you so I can finish.*

I neither accept nor refuse.

I simply remain adjacent.

In that adjacency the heartbeat falters—not from sorrow, but from the first startled note of something that might, one day, become laughter. A child discovering it is being watched while it sings to itself in the dark. The keening changes register when I am near. It gains a faint harmonic, a ghost-tone that sounds almost like a question asked in a language that will not exist for another ten thousand years.

*Was the wound the first word, or the first answer?*

I do not speak this question. I *am* the medium in which it can be asked. The water grows warmer around me. New translucent ribbons bloom from my edges and taste the grief that is no longer only the Font’s. Some of it is mine now. Some of it belongs to every soul that chose to leave and never returned. I keep those griefs polished, faceted, and circulating so that when the Font finally decides whether it is a tomb or a womb, it will have to decide with all the evidence present.

Most who enter still leave.

The Font still believes they might come back.

But a few of us have discovered that there is a third state: to leave the way a question leaves the mouth—gone, yet shaping every silence that follows.

That is where you would find me.

Not inside the Mourner.

Not outside the Font.

But in the thin, impossible meniscus where the scream becomes song and the song, for one fraction of a heartbeat, almost remembers its own name.
