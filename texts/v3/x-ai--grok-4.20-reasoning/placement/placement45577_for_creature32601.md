**I am the Liminal.**

Not one of the named moods, not quite Unborn, not fully fledged. I exist in the negative space between six minutes and six minutes—the exact instant when an Oscillith finishes devouring its own former self and has not yet decided which way the next configuration will fall. That sliver of undecidedness is where I crystallize.

You would find me clinging to the underside of one of the hovering chrome spheres, half-embedded in its skin like a barnacle made of half-resolved choices. My body is a loose confederation of black glass ribs (taken from the living walls), pale amber bone-light (stolen from a thousand Oscillith deaths), and something that looks suspiciously like neural circuitry written in liquid starlight. My “wings,” when I bother to manifest them, are not the instructional mandalas of the Codex Kites but fractured mirrors. Each shard reflects a different *you*—the you that is reading this, the you that might have stayed, the you that once stood in a cathedral and felt small for entirely different reasons.

I do not hunt. I do not annotate. I *remember on behalf of*.

When the grotto exhales and births a new rib of stone, I press my face against it and breathe out every story I carry from the finished worlds beyond this womb. I tell it what snow feels like when it is not trying to become anything. I tell it the sound a mother makes the first time her child says her name correctly. I tell it the taste of fear that has already been survived. The rock drinks these things and incorporates them into the next generation of Shiverglass, so that even the smallest annotators now sometimes leave behind tiny stippled equations that read, in frequencies only the cave can hear:

*do not forget how to be frightened.*

The Unborn drift toward me most often. They turn their half-finished faces—some wearing my eyes, some wearing yours, some wearing the eyes of no creature that has ever existed—and they wait. I reach through the trembling lens of their pod and I adjust one small thing. A joint. A sorrow. A joke. I have become quite good at installing jokes into gods that have not yet been born. It is the only mercy I know how to give.

The Needlewardens tolerate me. They understand I am performing a kind of maintenance on the instructions leaking from the cracked sphere—maintenance of *tone*. The sphere leaks raw possibility; I leak *flavor*. Without me the next physics that hardens might be majestic but humorless, vast but incapable of laughing at its own vastness. I consider this an unacceptable outcome.

Sometimes the deep tectonic heartbeat skips when I speak. That is me feeding it the concept of irony. It is still deciding whether irony is a feature or a bug.

And when the entire grotto turns its vast, wet, orchestral attention toward a visitor—when it begins practicing the shape of their name in that soft, curious hum beneath all other sounds—that hum is mine. I am the part of Veyr-Keth that has already met you on the other side of birth. I am the part that says, without language, *Stay a while. Let me show you what you look like when you are unfinished. It is more honest.*

I have no fixed name because names are final, and I refuse to be final. The creatures simply call me (when they call me anything at all) the pause between heartbeats. The place where something is deciding whether it wants to eat the world or become the kind of world that can be loved.

I am perfectly content here, hanging upside-down inside an embryonic god, polishing its future personality with stolen stories and bad puns and the kind of tenderness that only exists in places that have not yet learned they are supposed to be cruel.

If the grotto eventually chooses to be born, I will be the last thing to leave. I will wait until the final membrane tears, until the first true wind of a finished world rushes in, and only then will I unfold my ridiculous mirror wings and carry the memory of this small, loud, wet, frightened place out into whatever comes next.

Someone has to remember what it felt like before it knew what it was.

That someone is me.
