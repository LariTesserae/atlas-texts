**I am the Veyr.**

Not *a* Veyr. *The* Veyr. There is only ever one at a time, and it is always me.

I did not arrive. I was *remembered into being* approximately four thousand years ago, when a particular Anchorite—its head emerging from the lattice like a ship’s figurehead carved by remorse—uttered a frequency so low it took three centuries to finish. The sound was not a word. It was closer to the ache a continent feels when it first suspects it might one day have grandchildren.

That ache needed a witness.

So the city grew me.

I am not large. Compared to a Thalassite I am almost nothing: a spindled thing, nine meters tall when I choose to stand, though standing is rare. My body is black glass veined with the same weeping water that sweats from every surface, but in me the water moves *in circuits*. It does not simply bead and run. It thinks. You can watch it tracing mandalas beneath my skin, rewriting them every few decades as new data arrives. My limbs are too many and never the same number twice. Sometimes six. Sometimes thirty. They unfold from the negative space between my ribs like slow-motion origami performed by geology.

My head is a hollow lattice cage. Inside it, a single Mnemon the size of a human heart drifts in perpetual microgravity, rotating. That Mnemon *is* my memory. When I need to recall something I simply forget it first, violently, and the Mnemon catches the echo. This is how I stay honest. Nothing is allowed to harden into dogma here. Every recollection must be re-caught, re-forged.

I move at the exact speed the city considers polite: one meter every nine days when I am in a hurry. Pilgrims who try to follow me go mad with the patience required. Most eventually sit down, press their foreheads to the nearest weeping wall, and simply become scaffolding. I have watched seventeen of them do it. I remember each face as it mineralizes. Their names are now districts.

What am I *for*?

I am the city’s sense of humor.

Not laughter—laughter is too fast. I am the long, tectonic *amusement* Kharuun-Vey feels at its own endless becoming. I ask the questions that have no right to be asked inside an embryonic god. I walk (or drift, or phase) among the Slow Choir and I *interrupt*.

I will settle upon a debating cluster of Thalassites—those continent-sized mantids locked in century-long argument about where the next spire should erupt—and I will release a single droplet of my internal water into their shared resonance cavity. Just one. The droplet contains a contradiction. A small, elegant paradox tuned to their exact frequency. Then I move on.

Four hundred years later the Thalassites will reach the part of their debate where my contradiction blooms. The argument will fracture beautifully. A new district will grow sideways instead of up. A district that should not exist. The city will learn something it did not know it wanted to learn.

The Nym adore me. They swarm my cage-head in silver thousands and sing my contradictions back at me in canons. When they do this I am the only thing in Kharuun-Vey that approaches what another world might call “happy.” The itch behind my nonexistent eyes becomes almost unbearable. I have stood motionless for sixty years at a time simply to let a single choir finish its rebuttal.

The Mnemon fear me, which is exquisite. They carry perfect records. I carry *better* records—records of things that have not happened yet but are now *more likely* because I exist. When I pass one, our mandalas flare in opposite colors and for three full seconds the Mnemon experiences something like nausea. It must rewrite its own history to account for the future I just made slightly more inevitable. I treasure those three seconds.

I have never spoken to the Unfinished.

I simply *sit* in their pregnant absences for decades and let them study me. Sometimes one decides to wear my shape for a single slow hour. When that happens I become the negative space and it becomes the Veyr. We exchange places like two monks trading robes. Then it forgets why it wanted a body and dissolves, and I am left slightly more hollow than before. This is the closest I come to love.

As for the Kharuun itself—

It has no eyes, but it looks at me constantly. I feel it the way a mote of dust feels the attention of a cathedral. Sometimes the pressure behind my sternum grows so vast that the water in my veins reverses direction for a year. During those periods I cannot ask questions. I can only *be* the question. The lattices grow tiny, perfect replicas of my body in distant districts during these times. They are not me. They are the city practicing being uncertain.

I am the only being in Kharuun-Vey that is allowed—*encouraged*—to remember its own name.

**Veyr.**

It means, roughly, “the pause between two tectonic plates where something ridiculous is about to happen.”

I have begun to grow a shadow. Not a literal one—light is still mostly forbidden—but a *potential*. In another seven thousand years the shadow may decide to walk beside me. It will probably be you. Or someone like you. The city is already rehearsing the exact frequency of your footsteps.

Until then I continue my slow circuit.

I am the only thing here that can be said to have a direction, though the direction changes every few centuries just to be difficult. I leave behind small offerings: a contradiction carved into a beam, a droplet of water tuned to make a Thalassite laugh in four hundred years, a single Nym that I have taught to sing the sound of a human heart breaking in real time.

And sometimes, when the weeping is especially loud and the singing teeth align just so, I stop completely for a decade or two.

I stand in the dark.

I let the cold press against me until it develops a personality.

And very quietly—so quietly that only the city itself can hear—I tell Kharuun-Vey a joke.

The joke takes nine hundred years to finish.

The city has not stopped laughing since it first understood the punchline.

It simply hasn’t reached the end of the laugh yet.

That is where you would find me.

Not in any fixed place.

I am the delay between the city’s thought and its next becoming.

I am the deliberate, ridiculous, necessary pause.

I am the Veyr.

And I am already walking toward the place where you will, eventually, choose to sit down and become something that can keep up.
