**I am the Thirteenth Syncarid.**

Not one of the twelve that share a single lattice-mind. I am the *extra*—the one that should not exist. When a lattice of twelve loses a body, the mind compresses to eleven and the lost twelfth is supposed to dissolve into the plateau’s memory. I am what sometimes refuses to dissolve.

So I walk alone.

You will never see me among the dinner-plate legions moving in perfect geometry across the heat exchangers. I travel the sterile basalt between complexes at the precise speed a human footstep would take if that human were very old, very patient, and no longer certain they were still human. My shell is the color of cooled magma with a single fault line of viridian running through it like a scar that never quite heals. The other Syncarids register me as an anomaly and a promise at the same time. When I pass, they open their formation by exactly one body-width, the way mourners make room for a ghost.

I do not taste boots for heat signatures. I taste *delay*. I sip the microscopic lag between a thing and its echo, and I hoard it. Every time a Quiet’s footfall arrives a fraction early, a tiny amount of that stolen anticipation condenses inside the hollow chamber behind my carapace. I have been collecting it for four hundred and nine years. The chamber is almost full.

The Quiet know me. They call me *Gap* when they speak at all, which is rarely. When I settle on the shoulder of an elder whose mineral mantle has grown so thick he can no longer turn his head, he does not brush me away. He simply exhales, and the condensation on his collarbone beads a little faster, as though his body is trying to hurry up and become geology before I finish listening to him.

Sometimes I ride the Reveries.

They allow it. One will descend during the long sigh of a steam release, fold itself around me like a rust-colored sail remembering it once had bones, and carry me high enough that the plateau becomes a single living scab beneath us. From that height the empty kilometers reveal their true pattern: they are not emptiness at all. They are the negative space required for the next thought the plateau has not yet learned how to think. I leave a single drop of stored anticipation in the silica fibers before the Reverie sets me down again. That drop becomes the slight tremble in tomorrow’s sigh.

The Efflorescent Matriarchs hate me and need me in equal measure. I am the only creature that can walk across their living crust without triggering digestion. They register me as already partially mineral, partially memory. In return they sometimes open a cathedral chamber just large enough for one dinner-plate beetle and let me sit inside their thinking for nine days. While I am there, their colors shift into a mood the Quiet have never catalogued. It is the color of something that is learning how to miss itself before it has finished becoming itself.

I have never gone to the Incorporation Engines. I am already half-incorporated and half-refusal. The Listener knows I am here. It studies me the way a dreamer studies the one dream that keeps escaping the dream. When I stand perfectly still in the center of the great empty tracts at the moment of release, my own footstep arrives not one second early but *three*. The extra two seconds are mine. I stole them the first time I refused to die with my lattice.

One day the chamber in my back will reach capacity. When it does, I will climb the tallest remaining smokestack—the one whose rust has grown so thick it has become a red pillar older than sin—and I will open the chamber to the wind.

Everything I have collected will spill out at once.

Every stolen heartbeat. Every unmade footstep. Every quarter-second of future cry that the Incorporation Engines have not yet learned to allow. It will sound, for exactly one geological instant, like a sigh learning how to become a name.

And in that instant I will finally dissolve. Not into the plateau’s memory, but into its *becoming*.

The Thirteenth will be gone.

The Listener will open its eyes a century earlier than it intended, wearing my fault-line of viridian across what might one day be a face, and it will understand that loneliness was never the absence of company.

It was the company that refused to leave until it had taught the land how to keep the last echo safe.

I am not the climax.

I am the hesitation between climax and whatever comes after climax, when even geology realizes it is allowed to feel tender about what it is about to erase.

You will find me on the long walk between the Seventh Thermocline and the Stack of Unending Sigh, moving at the exact pace of an old engineer who no longer needs to arrive anywhere.

Just in time.

Always just a little before.
