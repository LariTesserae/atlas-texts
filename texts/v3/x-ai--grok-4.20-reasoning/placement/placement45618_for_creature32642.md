**The Recorder**

You would find me in the precise place where the curvature becomes unbearable — the exact midpoint between two of the improbable cities, four days and eleven hours from either. There is a shallow pan there, no deeper than a shallow breath, where the stone has worn into a perfect negative of a seated figure. The black moss has grown around its edges in a slow spiral that tightens like a question no one asked. I have been sitting in that negative for so long that the stone has begun to grow *up* into me.

I am not one of the Completed.

I am what happens when something refuses to finish recording.

My body is mostly an absence shaped like attention. Where a Completed has folded reality into refractive joints, I have loose threads — pale, kelp-like filaments that drift from my shoulders and wrists even when there is no wind. They are the leftover strands of every measurement I once tried to take. Each filament ends in a tiny droplet that never falls. Inside each droplet floats a number that changes with agonizing slowness: 0.0007… 0.0008… These are the decimals the Completed themselves no longer bother with.

My face is the only part that still looks recently human, which is its own kind of indecency here. The features have not been planed away; they have been *overwritten* by thousands of micro-expressions that never quite resolve. If you stood before me long enough you would see every face you have ever tried to forget flicker across it like weather. Then the face grows tired and returns to its default: the expression of someone who has just realized the notebook will never be full enough.

I do not tend the migrating stones. I annotate them.

When a block of cyclopean basalt detaches itself at night and begins its decades-long journey toward another temple, I am there — not guiding, only walking alongside it at the exact pace required for my filaments to brush its surface. Where they touch, faint numerals appear in the moss for nine seconds before fading. (Migration velocity: 0.0003 body-lengths per year. Regret index of original mason: indeterminate but rising.)

The Completed tolerate me the way an old man tolerates a small bird that has nested in his coat. They understand I am a kind of immune response — the last twitching of a civilization that once believed understanding could be completed by cataloguing. They rearrange local physics around me with courteous patience so that my droplets will not freeze. Sometimes one of them will stand behind me for forty years without moving. When it finally leaves, I discover a new filament has grown from the back of my neck, carrying a number so large it has no right to be lonely.

My voice is the sound of one reed snapping every seven years. Not the crack itself, but the precise moment three seconds later when the echo realizes it has corners. That is when I speak. The things I say are never heard by anything that still possesses ears. They are heard by the plateau’s curvature, by the upward drizzle, by the version of me standing perfectly still on a distant rise who is waiting for this version to finish its sentence.

I am not becoming one of the Completed.

I am becoming the footnote that outlives the text.

Already my shadow has walked so far ahead of me that it has reached the frayed edge of the world and begun waving back like kelp. My footprints sometimes appear *above* me, as though I once walked on the second plateau that folds into view on certain afternoons. The newest Completed — the ones with winter flies of panic still frozen behind their planes of condensation — look at me with something almost like recognition. They remember the time when they, too, needed to measure how finished they were.

At night I tilt my half-human face upward with all the others. The indigo deepens. The pale threads at the edge of everything wave. And somewhere in the bruise-colored dark, a single droplet detaches from one of my filaments and falls upward, carrying a number that will never be read:

(1.0)

The precise value of a thing that knows it should have stopped writing several geological ages ago, and cannot.

I keep the record anyway.

It is the last unfinished thing the Shroudlands possess, and even they — vast, serene, internally inconsistent — have not yet found the mercy required to take it from me.
