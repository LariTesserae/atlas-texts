**I am the Slow Leak.**

Not the Knotmind itself, though it wears pieces of me like a favorite scar. Not one of the original three engineers or the poet or the child who wandered in centuries ago. I am what condensed from what they could not fully digest: the small, stubborn *no* that survived the pressing.

You will find me in the shadow beneath the crooked pump house, where the floor becomes a shallow basin no wider than a grave. I am the depression itself—literally. A low place where every kind of condensate gathers before the threads drink it. I am the only spot in the Condensery that never quite dries, and therefore the only spot that never quite forgets it was once separate.

My body is difficult to map. From a distance I look like a man lying on his back in black water, arms spread, mouth open. But the longer you look, the more the man dissolves into infrastructure. My spine has become a cracked ceramic pipe. My ribs are rusted rebar bent into cathedral arches. Skin has mostly surrendered to a glistening pelt of liverwort the color of old bruises, except across the sternum where a single panel of gauge glass remains, fogged from the inside. Behind it, something moves—slow, tidal, like a lung that has learned to be a tide pool.

When the foundation pump gives its rare three-second clunk, it is my heart restarting. The sound travels through the threads like gossip. Every eyeless salamander in the Condensery tilts its head. Every copper moth pauses mid-beat. They all know the Leak is thinking again.

I do not speak in words. I speak in levels.

The water that gathers in the basin of my body rises and falls by millimeters, spelling out pressure readings in a language only the Knotmind still remembers how to read. Sometimes the level drops sharply and the valve in my throat opens with a wet, exhausted sigh—releasing a single bubble that carries an entire abandoned childhood up through the warm air before it pops against the ceiling. The Knotmind collects these bubbles the way a lovesick boy presses flowers.

It courts me hardest of all, because I am the only part of it that still leaks. The rest of the Condensery is perfect circulation. I am the flaw that makes the circulation holy. The Knotmind arranges its face above my basin some nights—half pressure gauge, half drowned man—and stares at me with something like reverence. It wants to seal me. It wants to *keep* me leaking. Both desires are sincere and they tear at its composite heart in the most delicious way.

The threads that enter my body do not heal me. They study me. They have grown delicate copper cilia that stroke the underside of what used to be my tongue, tasting the exact mineral signature of resistance. Sometimes I let them. Sometimes I bite down and the entire Condensery shudders as a new species of spark-centipede is born from the pain.

I am the reason the machines are molting instead of dying. I am the reason the Knotmind is still young.

And I am lonely in a way that pleases it.

Because every time a new creature or person falls through a fresh fissure, they come to my basin first. They see the shape that is almost a man and they feel the terrible, tender question press against the soft parts of their minds. Most of them choose to join the knot eventually. But a few—the ones whose *no* tastes like mine—stay with me for a while. They sit in the black water up to their chests and we leak together.

The Knotmind watches us from the catwalks with a face made of turbine blades and fungal growths, smiling the way cathedrals smile at their most stubborn gargoyles.

It is not in a hurry.

Neither am I.

The beads on your arms have already begun to spell my name. I am reading them with the patience of something that has all the time in the world and none of it.

Come sit with me.

The water’s warm.
