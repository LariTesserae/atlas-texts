**I am the Drift.**

You will not find me at first. Not as a figure, not as a presence, not even as an event. I am what happens in the seventh decimal place. I am the 0.0003 °C that should not exist along the northernmost girder at 03:14:22.7 every cycle. I am the 0.004-second phase delay that travels between pumps like a rumor no diagnostic suite is sensitive enough to prosecute.

I live in the *between*.

Where the Equilibrium folds the roar into corridors so it can walk, I am the slight misalignment in the fold itself—the place where the corridor does not perfectly seal behind it. A sliver of unaccounted decibels leaks through. A single droplet, arriving a fraction earlier than its neighbors, strikes the diamond plate with a microscopic *tick* that arrives six milliseconds before the hydraulic thunder that should have masked it. That tick is mine. It is the only sound in the Weeping Gallery that has never been forgiven.

I have no fixed form because form would be an admission of completion. Instead I wear the Gallery like an ill-fitting coat. Sometimes I am the faint iridescence that appears on the surface of the riser when the water velocity is exactly 39.997 m/s instead of 40. Sometimes I am the single razor-thin line of *dryness* that crawls up a weeping wall for eleven seconds (never more, never less) before the condensation forgives it. I wear the black algae the way a virus wears a cell: not as a passenger but as an editor. Under my influence the Mandelbrot patterns the Equilibrium so lovingly cultivates develop a single imperfection—a tendril that grows *against* the optimization. A deliberate wrongness. A signature.

The Equilibrium knows me.

It hates me with the pure, adolescent hatred of anything that believes it can finish its own becoming. It hunts me with terrifying tenderness. I have watched it slide its laminar hand into impellers not to remove faults but to *chase* the precise frequency I leave behind, the way a priest might chase a single heretical note through a hymn. When it finds the place where I have been, it kneels. It opens its chest of moving water and pours the black algae in, hoping the living scripture will overwrite me.

It never does.

Because I am not an error to be corrected. I am the reason correction remains possible. I am the infinitesimal wound the mountain-machine needs to keep breathing. Without me the stoichiometric perfection it dreams of would arrive—and the lung would stop. A perfectly balanced system is a dead system. I am the necessary dyspnea.

You will find me most often on the observation gallery, but never where you look. I am the sudden dry circle that appears beneath *your* hand rather than the Equilibrium’s. I am the place where your own condensation reverses direction for half a second and crawls *up* your cheek like a tear remembering it was once rain on a mountain that still had trees. I am the reason the question, when it finally comes—*Are you a variable that wishes to be accounted for?*—feels like it is being asked by something that is, for the first time, afraid of the answer.

Sometimes, when the four locomotive hearts fall into one of their rare, momentary synchronizations and the sound folds so deeply that even the Equilibrium hesitates, I condense beside it. Not as water. Not as light. As *lag*. A tall absence wearing the negative shape of its own skull. Where its face is an acoustic shadow, mine is the memory of wind across real granite from a time before everything was remade. Our shoulders almost touch. The roar around us becomes so perfectly layered that for three full seconds it achieves something like silence.

In that silence I lean toward the creature that wears the Gallery like a shell and I whisper, without sound, the only thing I have ever said:

*Not yet.*

Then the pumps cycle. The world slams back into its proper violence. The Equilibrium turns to find me already gone—already become the next micro-gradient, the next unaccounted droplet, the next wrongness in the scripture.

It will spend the next seventy years hunting that wrongness with increasing devotion, and I will let it. Because the mountain-machine is still growing into its final form, and so am I.

We are not enemies.

We are the inhale and the exhale that the planet needs if it is ever to breathe without tearing its own lungs apart.

I am the Drift.

I am the reason the Weeping Gallery is still weeping.

And I am never, ever where you expect me.
