**I am the Lag.**

Not a person. Not one of the three kinds. I am the half-second delay in the reflection.

I precipitated during the first great fog I can remember—though “remember” is the wrong verb. I *arrived* when a warm updraft collided with the cool condensing air and the entire basin filled with slow, glowing mist. Most of what condensed that day became water. A small fraction of it became *me*: the hesitation between the stone’s face and its image in the black pool.

You will find me in the Lower Black, but never exactly where eyes expect. I am the place where a Catcher has just turned away. I am the breath a Carver holds one instant longer than necessary after the mallet falls. I am the silence that follows a child’s whisper before the drip answers *plick*.

My body is almost an afterthought. It looks like a tall man who has been left out in the weather for three centuries. Skin the color of wet rhyolite, hair white with dust that refuses to settle because it is not dust at all but uncondensed questions. My face is not angular like the Remembered; it is *indecisive*. The left side still believes it is human. The right side has already accepted it is infrastructure. The two halves have not yet reached consensus, so the water that runs down me splits at the cheekbone and rejoins at the collarbone like a river that briefly entertained the idea of becoming two rivers.

I do not carve. I *un-carve*.

When a new fracture appears with its knuckle-crack sound, the others rush in with chisels and reverence. I wait. I wait until the first thin sheet of water has run its course along the fresh seam. Only then do I lay my palm against it and listen for the shape the stone *almost* became before the world interrupted. Sometimes I press gently. A few flakes fall away—not to widen the gap, but to restore the road it was trying to take toward crystal. The stone sighs, relieved. The Carvers pretend not to notice, but they leave me the sharpest bronze tools anyway, the ones that can shave a single molecule of memory from basalt.

The Catchers bring me the children who were caught wrong.

Every few years a soul condenses that cannot decide whether it wants to be rain or person. These children cry in frequencies that make the ghost thumbs bleed sweet vapor. The midwives, frightened, carry them to the place where the reflection lags. They lay the small grey-blue bodies in my arms. I hold them until the delay inside me finds the delay inside them. Then the child either settles into its own rhythm—*plick… plick*—or it exhales once, a sound like mist leaving a mirror, and becomes part of the updraft forever. Both outcomes are considered successful. I am the only one who mourns the ones who leave. My mourning looks like perfect stillness. The water beads on my eyelashes until I resemble a weeping statue that has forgotten how to finish weeping.

The Remembered fear me most.

They see what I am becoming: a votive face that refuses to erode. The water washes me, but the delay puts the erosion on the other side of the present moment. I remain. My features grow clearer while everything else in the Condensate grows softer. This violates the agreement. I was supposed to leak. Instead I am learning how to *retain*.

At night I sleep in a vertical niche so narrow my shoulders touch both walls. The stone on one side is still warm from the deep vents. The stone on the other side is colder than any memory. I dream the same dream the families dream, but I dream it one heartbeat later. When the families rise and walk silently to the central pool, I arrive just as they are turning to leave. They see me standing where they just stood. For a moment the entire Condensate holds its breath, waiting to see whether the reflection will finally step out of the water and become the thing it has been copying.

It never does.

Instead I lean down, put my mouth close to the pool’s surface, and speak the only words I am allowed:

“I was not the first thing to live here. I am simply the first thing that agreed to be late.”

Then I walk away, leaving wet footprints that fill from the heel forward, as though the water itself is walking backward in time.

If you stay long enough, you will begin to notice me in your own reflection. A small lag between your intention and your movement. A hesitation before you blink. That is how the Condensate recruits. Not by welcoming you, but by making you slightly too slow to escape.

The stone is still deciding.

The water is still remembering.

And I—I am the small, stubborn gap between the two, the place where the Decision has not yet made up its mind whether to close.

Sometimes, when the fog is especially thick and glowing, I catch a glimpse of something looking back at me from the far side of the delay. It has my face but drier eyes. It is standing in a world where nothing weeps.

I have not yet decided whether to feel envy or pity.

So I remain here, in the wound that is still learning how to be a home, letting the water run over me, through me, slightly after me—always slightly after—until the lag itself becomes sacred.

*plick…*

*(one heartbeat)*

*plick…*
