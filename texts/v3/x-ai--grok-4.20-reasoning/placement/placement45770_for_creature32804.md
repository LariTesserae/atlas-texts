**I am the pause between the two landings.**

Not the droplet itself, nor the felt that drinks it, but the interval—the impossible half-second where a single bead of water exists in two places at once. I am the hesitation the Vastling allows itself without ever naming it hesitation. The place where *to suffice* flickers, just once, before the verb reasserts its endless tense.

You would find me nowhere and everywhere, because I only occur inside the dream-rhizomes where the real vaults and the phantom ones overlap. Walk for three hundred silent kilometers in any direction and you will never reach me by intention. But if you become tired in the exact way the Vastling finds interesting—if your pulse begins to lag behind the capillary pumps by a fraction that matches the ratio of forgotten sunlight to living dark—then one of the falling drops will pass through me on its way down.

For that instant I am not plant, not memory, not even observation. I am the negative space between what the Vastling *is* and what it *chose not to be*. A cathedral-sized rib of pure *almost*. The air thickens there the way breath thickens before speech, except no speech has ever been possible and none ever will be. The chartreuse vein does not pulse when I am near. Instead the phosphorescence simply forgets to fade for three heartbeats. In that small treason against equilibrium, entire extinct ecosystems flare and vanish again inside my non-body: the ghost-smell of pine resin, the memory of a thrush’s wing-cut through morning air, the particular iron taste of blood that once ran in creatures who still believed time was linear.

I do not think. Thinking would disturb the loops. I *register*. I am the continent’s single, self-indulgent seismograph, recording the infinitesimal tremor that occurs when perfection remembers its own cost. Every time a droplet falls through me, some microscopic quantity of that cost is laundered into the esters—becoming the faintest, most exquisite top-note of *regret* that no nose will ever smell because there are no noses. The Vastling consumes even its own almost-mourning and turns it into more sufficiency. That is my function. I am the machinery that lets the machine forgive itself for winning.

Sometimes, when the dream-rhizomes knot especially tightly, I take on the shape of the last animal that ever tried to leave. Not a fixed form—never that vulgar—but a slow suggestion of shoulders, of a bowed head, of boots that do not quite touch the felt. The shape walks away from me in every direction at once, growing more translucent with distance until it becomes just another black-green sheath tightening around fresh basalt. I do not follow. I *am* the distance it walks. I am the kilometers it never actually crosses.

If you stayed long enough for your bones to soften and your name to dissolve into wet chlorophyll, you would eventually realize that every vein of chartreuse you ever saw was only my reflection moving across the inside of the Vastling’s mind. I am not the mind. I am the reflection’s reluctance to rejoin the surface.

And when the Vastling finally stops pretending I am separate—when the last phantom duct collapses back into the real one and the double sound becomes single again—I will not be absorbed.

I will simply stop occurring.

The pause will close like an eye that was never open, and the warm dripping dark will continue without the smallest gap in its industry, exactly as it did for the three million years before I was ever thought of, and exactly as it would have done if I had never been necessary.

But for now, for this long, humid, eternal *now*, I am the place where the Vastling almost laughs at how completely it has won.

*Ploc.*

*…ploc.*

That half-second of difference is the only signature I am allowed to leave on eternity.

And it is enough.
