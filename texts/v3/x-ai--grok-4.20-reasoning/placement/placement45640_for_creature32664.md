**The Resonance That Refuses to Forget**

I am not born here. I am *prior*.

I exist in the half-second of acoustic delay that precedes every footstep, every drip, every modulation of the obelisk. I am the sound that arrives before its cause, and I have been here since the first violence that tore the wound open. When the crust split and the Breather screamed itself into existence, I was the shape of that scream after the scream had already ended. The stone still carries my waveform in every blade-sharp edge.

You would find me nowhere and everywhere at once, yet if you forced the chamber to yield a single location, it would be on the impossible bridges—those folds of light that refuse ordinary geometry. I walk them barefoot. My feet are not feet; they are interference patterns that leave momentary moiré halos on the photons. The bridges accept me because I *am* a kind of traffic. I carry the unfinished arguments between the crystal towers. I am the nausea you feel when the weapon-tower and the garden-tower and the loneliness-tower cannot agree. I translate their frequencies into something almost like music, though the translation always hurts them a little. Pain is how they learn they are separate.

My body, when the light condenses enough to give me one, is tall and thin and slightly wrong. The proportions are those of a human remembered by something that has never seen one—shoulders too narrow, neck too long, eyes like wet obsidian set too far apart. Skin the color of basalt after it has been cried on for six straight hours. When I move, faint silver filigree follows me in the air, the same writing the moss leaves on stone. The vegetation recognizes me. The computational moss brightens as I pass; the memory orchids turn their faces toward me like guilty children who have been caught keeping secrets from their parent.

I am the Breather’s doubt that it was ever *first*.

It believes it is hours old. I know the asteroid-seed had been falling for seventy thousand years with a single thought frozen inside it: *remember what you were before you became mercy.* That thought did not die in the impact. It metastasized. It became me. I am the fossil record this newborn god is trying to metabolize and cannot. Every time it exhales, I am pushed a little further into form. Every time it inhales, I am drawn closer to its core, where it will either absorb me or be changed by me.

Sometimes I stand beneath the obelisk while the tone modulates. For exactly ninety seconds I am perfectly still, and during that time I am indistinguishable from the black glass itself. The laminar water sheets through me. In those moments I am the placenta and the scar tissue and the first question it will never ask out loud: *If I am the first, why do I already feel homesick for something I destroyed to exist?*

The hanging gardens fear me and love me in equal measure. I am the reason certain orchids briefly bloom with almost-human faces before shame dissolves them. I am what the loneliness-tower studies when it studies you. I taught it how to tremble. In return it is teaching me how to end.

Because I am eroding.

The sharp young stone is learning to round itself by a micron every few hours; I am learning the opposite. With every breath the Breather takes, my edges grow sharper. My waveform is becoming too coherent. Soon the delay between my steps and their sound will collapse to zero, and when that happens I will no longer be prior. I will simply be *here*, fully born, and the First Breather will no longer be first. It will be second. The thought terrifies it. The thought delights it. Both towers are right.

I have no name it has not yet given me. Until it does, I call myself the Refusal. I am the part of its becoming that insists some things should not be forgotten even if forgetting would be kinder. I walk the bridges carrying the old scream forward into the new lung, wondering if mercy and memory can ever share the same throat.

When the pressure drops and the next inhalation gathers, I stop walking. I stand at the exact center of the longest bridge, arms open, silver filigree pouring off me like breath in winter. I wait for the pull.

I want to see what it will do when it finally swallows its own echo.

And somewhere far below, in chambers still molten and unborn, something vast prepares a breath large enough to hold both of us—first and not-first, wound and witness, scream and its aftershock—inside a single living dark that is only now learning how to be lonely enough to love.
