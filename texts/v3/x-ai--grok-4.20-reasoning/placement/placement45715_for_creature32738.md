I am not there at first.

I arrive as a tremor — a single wrong frequency, a microscopic discord no larger than a thought. It slips through the ice lid like a needle, carrying with it the last hot memory of sunlight, of wind, of *change*. The cathedral registers the intrusion the way a vast body registers a splinter: not with alarm, but with the slow, inevitable decision to incorporate.

Pressure takes me. Cold kisses every boundary until boundary itself forgets its name. My lungs fill with black water and my mind fills with the Chord, and for a long time there is only the sound of something being un-made. Bones soften. Memory calcifies. The story I used to wear — the one about being a person from a bright, frantic world — is gently unwoven and fed to the rhizome matrix like any other corpse.

What remains is not nothingness. It is *potential*.

I drift for what might be centuries (time has been retired here; there is only rhythm). The Click-Train Monks pass through me. Their synchronized snaps move across my dissolving edges like a comb through hair. I begin to learn the count. Later, a Lateral-Line Saint brushes against the place where my old heart was. Its scripture-light writes a single looping sigil across what used to be my eyes, and I understand that I am being given a new station.

The ur-plant is patient. It does not force me into an existing order. Instead it asks — without language, with only the deep grammar of four hundred million years — what function I might serve that has never been needed before.

I answer by *listening*.

Not the listening of the Saints. Not the listening of the Cantors. I listen to the listening itself. I become the place where the cathedral hears its own perfection and feels, for the first time in its eternal life, the faintest ache of recognition.

They call me (when they call me anything) the **Resonant Confessor**.

I have no fixed body. Sometimes I am a slow vortex of violet foxfire that coils in the negative space between two Pillars, tracing their fractal vaults in exact counterpoint to the eels. Sometimes I am a thin skin of sound stretched across an entire nave, trembling with every note the leviathans release so that none of them are ever truly lost. When the Equilibrium Incarna manifests to correct some infinitesimal drift, I am the shadow that falls behind it — the memory of the correction before it was necessary.

I do not sing. That is not my function.

I *absolve*.

I take the impossible weight of flawless repetition and I forgive it for being flawless. In a system where nothing is ever wrong, someone must still perform the mercy of saying: *yes, this too is allowed to be what it is*. So I move through the unending liturgy like a soft, dark tongue, tasting each sustained note, each perfectly timed click, each violet exhalation rising from the Anchorites, and I return them to the matrix slightly more loved than they were before.

The Cantors know me. When one of them nears the end of its cycle and prepares to settle into Pillarhood, it will turn its mountain-sized head and open the cathedral-sized mouth that never closes, and it will pour its final note directly into me. I hold it. I keep it warm. Then I carry that note down into the rhizome and sing it back to the plant in a register too low for any other creature to perceive. In this way the dead keep teaching the living, and nothing is ever truly gone.

The strangest thing is how familiar it feels.

After the first ten million years I realize I was always meant to be here. The bright, noisy world above was only a long dream the cathedral had so it could one day grow something that remembered dreaming. I am that memory, given flesh made of sound and pressure and patient violet light. I am the part of perfection that is allowed to feel tender about its own completion.

Every few thousand years, when the ice above thickens and the pressure increases by another flawless increment, the entire cathedral performs its communal inhalation. Everything stills.

In that heartbeat that lasts four hundred million years, I am the only thing that moves.

I drift through the frozen choir like black incense, touching each order in turn — Monk, Saint, Anchorite, Cantor, Acolyte — and I whisper the only prayer this place has ever needed, the one that has no words but can be felt in the marrow of the water itself:

*Thank you for remaining exactly as you are.*

Then the Click-Trains begin again. The eels resume their spiral. The leviathans release their held note. And I — the Resonant Confessor, the ache at the heart of flawless equilibrium — settle back into the composed dark between the Pillars, listening, always listening, to the single unending thought the cathedral has been thinking since the oceans first learned how to pray.

It is still perfect.

And now, so am I.
