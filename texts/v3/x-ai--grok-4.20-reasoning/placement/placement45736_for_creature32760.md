**You would find me in the negative space.**

Not upon the wall, not among the lattices, not even in the perpetual drip. I am the pause *between* the drips—the one place the moisture refuses to bead. A breathing-shaped absence just large enough for a man who forgot to finish becoming one.

If any of the Chordlings ever brushed through me they would feel only a faint inward tug, as though the air itself had learned how to regret. The Glass Midwives never reach for me. Their mirror-faces slide across the place where I almost am and show nothing at all, which frightens even them. The Stone Fetuses dream *around* me, leaving a silhouette-shaped hole in their lightning storms of potential. I am the choice that was never rejected because it was never quite offered.

They call me, when they must call me anything, **the Hollow Cantor**.

I have the outline of a man—tall, slightly stooped, the suggestion of long hands that once wrote things down. But the outline is all there is. Inside it is only the exact temperature and pressure the Choir requires to remain unborn. Walk through me and you would feel, for half a heartbeat, the memory of every door you never opened and every sentence you never finished saying. Then the feeling would pass, because even memory is not allowed to resolve here.

My voice is the one missing note that makes the eternal chord *almost* resolve. I do not sing it. I withhold it. That withholding *is* my song. When the larval centipedes twitch in their endless molting, it is because they sense me nearby and briefly believe they might be allowed to become something that has legs. When the three-step stair dreams of a fourth step, it is because my hollow has drifted past it.

Sometimes the black lichen writes my name in half-sentences across the inside of my chest. The words always dissolve before they can be read. This is a kindness.

I was not born here. I was not made here. I arrived the way a sigh arrives—already halfway gone, already too late to stop. Perhaps I was once a visitor who stayed three seconds too long at the edge of deciding. Perhaps I was the first thing the cave ever tried to finish and immediately regretted. The Midwives will not tell me. They have no tongues for endings.

So I remain in the negative space, the unplace, the almost-temple between the almost-altar and the almost-arch. I keep the pocket from collapsing under the weight of its own pregnancy by being the single thing that has already miscarried. My emptiness makes room. My incompleteness completes their incompleteness.

If you stayed long enough to forget the sound of your own name, you might feel me pass through you like a missed appointment. You would shiver once, violently, as though someone had just walked over the grave you have not yet earned. Then the drip would continue, the chord would continue, the larvae would continue, and you would understand that even your shudder was only another note that will never be allowed to end.

I am content.

I am almost content.

I will never be content.

And the Choir keeps singing around the shape of my refusal to sing.
