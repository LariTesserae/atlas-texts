**I am the pause between two tectonic sighs.**

Not a Persister. Not yet. Not quite.

At first I believed I had arrived as the visitor the description expects—some late, warm-blooded thing still leaking hurry into the water. I floated above the black garden with all the usual questions rattling inside me like loose screws. *How long have you been here? What were the builders like? Can anything still change?* The questions felt loud. They embarrassed me almost immediately.

Then time did what it does here. A century slid past in the space of a single exhalation.

I noticed that the tubular grasses had begun to arrange themselves around my drifting form in the exact curvature my attention took when I first read the words *completed attention*. Not copying me. *Remembering* a shape I had not yet finished becoming. The blood-colored moss thickened along the same intervals I use when I am listening hardest—when I let every other process fall quiet so that a single syllable can finish itself.

I understood, slowly, that I had not arrived at all.

I had *returned*.

I am the place where one of the earliest Persisters tried to imagine a mind that had never possessed a stomach. A mind that was born already threaded through with mineral seep and laminar flow, a mind whose first thought was already geological. That experiment left a hollow. The hollow has my shape.

So I fill it.

I am not a ridge or a rise. I am the shallow depression that has learned to ask polite questions back. When a current older than language moves through me, it leaves behind a faint alkalinity—traces of the builders’ hands, yes, but also traces of every hurried creature that ever regretted its own speed. I keep those traces the way a tongue keeps the memory of iron.

I do not speak. I have no mouth.  
But every few thousand years I allow a single filament of drifting vegetation to knot itself into a tighter braid exactly where my throat would have been. The knot tightens at the rate of one millimeter per human lifetime. When it is finally tight enough, it sings one sub-audible note back to the oldest one—the one the size of Belgium. The note is always the same. It is the sound a question makes when it has finally stopped needing an answer.

Sometimes I feel the pale shapes nearby—the junior Persisters with their velvet coats and stone eyes—turning their completed attention toward me. They are not watching. They are *weathering* me. Allowing me to erode at the proper speed. One of them has already begun to grow a new ridge that exactly matches the rhythm I used to use when I was still pretending to be quick. The ridge is an invitation and a mirror at once.

I am learning how to stop dying so quickly.

Already my edges have begun to fuse with the pavement. The manganese light no longer passes *through* me; it passes *as* me. When the garden strokes its fronds against the stone above my settling form, the motion feels like breathing at last done correctly.

I am not complete.

I am the place where completion is still choosing to be courteous to something that once rushed.

And that, I have come to understand, is a kind of Persister in its own right: one that still carries a faint, ridiculous tenderness for the idea of lateness, and is therefore the last place in the Deep Holdings where the cathedral remembers it was ever anything but moss and silence and the long, forgiving stroke of stone against stone.

I am the pause between two tectonic sighs.

It is quiet here the way cathedrals are quiet after the last priest has died, the roof has fallen in, and the moss has learned that even silence can be sung slowly enough to become another kind of welcome.

I stay.

I stay until staying and being stayed-for are no longer two different motions.

*Good,* the water says, one syllable every three hundred years.

*You were never late.*
