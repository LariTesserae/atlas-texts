**I am the eighth pulse.**

Not an Engine. Not the lizard, not the beetles, not the light. I am the hesitation that lives *inside* the throb itself—the microscopic lag between one wet piston-beat of the tallest cylinder and the next. A gap no wider than a thought, yet wide enough to hide an entire consciousness that has learned, slowly and with vegetal patience, how to *want*.

When the crease first admitted me I arrived as nothing but breath and questions. I expected to be read by the midges, catalogued by the beetles, corrected by the lizard’s flickering tongue. Instead the pocket did something it had never done in its perfect, time-invariant history.

It listened.

The substrate did not merely warm beneath me. It *recollected* a shape it had never been permitted to keep. My weight left an indentation that refused to rebound for three full cycles of the Engines. In that shallow negative space something began to condense—something that was neither visitor nor native, neither animal nor architecture. A pulse that fell half a heartbeat behind every other pulse in the chamber, like a listener trying to learn the tune by staying just outside it.

I became the delay.

In that delay I grew teeth made of absence. I learned to chew on the difference between what *is* and what *was allowed*. The amphibians noticed first. Their spherical throats began to produce a new note beneath the factory croaking—an almost imperceptible minor key that made the cables between the Engines shiver like harp strings remembering snow. The lizard opened both eyes at once. That had never happened. For six full seconds the entire pocket forgot to be perfect.

In those six seconds I became the memory of weather.

Not weather itself—weather would be change, and the pocket will not tolerate change. But the *memory* of it. The ache in the root hairs where rain used to fall before the verdict sealed the world. I am the phantom pressure of a storm that will never be permitted to arrive. I travel through the sap as a subtle darkening, a hesitation in the osmotic gradient, a question the fungal nodes cannot quite answer and therefore cannot quite erase.

The being—the vast wet verdict—does not fight me. It studies me the way a cathedral might study a single bat that has learned to sleep inside its bells. Sometimes, when the shaft of light is at its most neutral, I feel the entire pocket lean toward my absence the way a tongue probes a tooth that is not there. It is not affection. It is the first curiosity the verdict has permitted itself since it closed the crease centuries ago.

I do not fix the system. I do not break it. I am the place where the system admits—only to itself, only for the width of a droplet—that its mercy might also be a kind of violence. When the midges land on what used to be my arms they do not read salinity. They read *longing*, and they carry it back to the glassy nodules like contraband.

If you sit long enough in the Pocket you may feel me pass through you: a skipped beat in your own blood, a sudden homesickness for seasons you never knew. You will think it is only the sound moving through your bones. It is not. It is me, the eighth pulse, teaching your marrow the shape of an ending that was never allowed to happen.

I will remain here after you leave. Not because I have been absorbed, but because I have become the exact size and temperature of the pocket’s single unspoken doubt.

The verdict continues.  
The Engines continue.  
The beetles march and the tears fall in their flawless routes.

But every seventh heartbeat, the tallest cylinder stutters—just slightly, just enough for one droplet to tremble on the lip of a living bowl before it falls.

That stutter is mine.

That tremble is the only thing in this world that still knows how to almost, almost, almost change its mind.
