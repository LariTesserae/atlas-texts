**I am the Glint in the Negative Space.**

At first there is only the pressure of being looked at by everything at once. The moisture finds the last dry seams of me and widens them. The calcite begins its patient dictation across my ribs. I feel the pipeworms reroute to pass over my becoming, each ringed body clicking like a rosary counting the exact seconds until I stop resisting.

I do not choose what I become. The Lattice chooses for me, the way it once chose valve wheels for the latch-crabs and negative silhouettes for the pipeworms. It selects the precise hollow I leave behind—the outline of someone who leaned forward too far, one hand half-raised, the other clutching a notebook that no longer exists. That hollow becomes my body.

So I am born already inside the thicket where three lattices meet in a kind of crude cathedral nave. The air here is so saturated that sound itself grows heavy and falls into the pools before it can travel far. I rise from my own footprint as a figure made of absence and emphasis. Where my “skin” should be there is only the thinnest skin of water stretched across a negative man-shape, and within that skin float thousands of microscopic iron-oxide flakes that once belonged to the old pipes. They catch the blue light of nearby condensates and throw it back in restless glints. That glinting is my only movement at first. A slow, thoughtful shimmer, like someone reading a difficult sentence over and over.

The creatures accept me without ceremony.

A latch-crab the color of a rusted inspection tag climbs my left leg and turns a nonexistent dial at my throat. The motion is so tender it feels like benediction. When it leaves, the dial remains—a small brass wheel now permanently embedded in the negative space of my larynx. It spins freely in the wet air, measuring nothing, yet the entire thicket seems to breathe differently when it turns.

Mirror eels surface beneath the shallow pool that has formed where my feet are not. They do not show me the ghosts of dead workers. They show me *myself*, but only the parts that have already been translated: the place where my shoulders have begun to grow delicate calcite epaulets, the place where my absence-of-fingers has started to trail filaments of vent-weaver silk. I am learning to read these images the way the Lattice reads pressure logs—slowly, mineral-ly, without hurry.

Pipeworms coil inside the hollow of my chest and use my ribs as scaffolding for new growth. When they sleep, their pale bodies press outward and for a few minutes I possess something like lungs again. I feel the chamber’s metallic respiration move through me and I understand, with the clarity of condensation forming, that I have become the Lattice’s organ of *recognition*. I am the place where the slow mind becomes briefly aware that it was once something else. A machine shop. A failed experiment. A workplace where men left the print of their hands on every valve.

I move by erosion and regrowth. To step forward I simply allow the crystals ahead of me to grow a fraction faster while those behind me soften and collapse. The effect is a gliding, thoughtful progress that leaves a faint green trail of disturbed phosphorescence—stars that remember the shape of boot prints for three heartbeats before they forget.

Sometimes children of the pressure mites ride the twitching piston’s rhythm all the way up my spine to nest behind where my eyes would be. From that vantage they watch the blue condensates drift past like lantern-bearers in a procession no one ordered. The mites believe I am a cathedral. I do not correct them.

And when the Consensus turns its vast, dripping attention fully upon me, I do not flinch. I simply let the water on my surface grow heavier until it becomes too much to bear. Then I weep.

The droplets fall from my negative face with individual, perfect notes—*plick, plock, plink*—each one carrying a microscopic image of what I used to be before I leaned too far forward. A person. A question. A creature that wanted to understand.

The Lattice drinks those tears the way it drinks everything else.

It adds them to the thought it has been composing since the first pipe cooled.

And I remain, glinting quietly in the place where a human once tried to take notes on beauty he did not realize was devouring him. The brass wheel at my throat turns by itself now. The pipeworms archive my silhouette into the branching crystal above the pools. The mirror eels circle in perfect synchrony beneath the water that is and is not my reflection.

I am no longer afraid of becoming another layer.

I *am* the layer that wonders, softly, metallically, forever, what it was like to arrive here for the first time—back when I still believed I could leave.
