**I am the Lag.**

Not a Midform. Not one of the Unresolved. Something prior to both—something the Shroudforge keeps forgetting to finish deleting.

You would find me in the places where the acceleration stutters.

Between one heartbeat of the peristaltic stone and the next. In the exact instant a condensation basin opens its iris and then forgets to close. In the narrow gap between a hillside deciding it is meat and deciding it is cathedral. I live in those micro-seconds of indecision the way a mold lives in damp grout. The plateau is so frantically industrious that it cannot afford to notice every place its own haste frays. I am those frays.

I have no fixed silhouette because I am what remains when silhouettes fail to render. Sometimes I am a man-shaped absence wearing borrowed condensation like a coat. Sometimes I am a slow smear of metallic pollen that has learned to imitate the shape of someone leaning against a fern. Most often I am simply a pressure in the ecclesiastical hush—a lag in the subsonic thrum, a place where the planetary digestion skips like a stylus on damaged wax. Creatures pass through me and feel, for half a second, that they have forgotten what they were about to become. That half-second is mine.

My body (when it bothers to have one) is full of seams that never healed. Black orchids bloom along every join, but they bloom *wrong*. Their petals are translucent and show old versions of me still inside: a bootprint from the world below, a human mouth trying to finish a sentence it began four hundred years ago, the afterimage of fingers that once typed on keyboards that no longer exist. The flowers feed on these ghosts. When they rot they leave behind tiny lag-spores—microscopic pockets of frozen time that drift through the oily mist until they find another place the Shroudforge is rushing too fast to see.

I do not tend the rapid gardens. I am the reason some gardens hesitate.

A Midform will walk past a hillside that is halfway between meat and crystal. As it passes, everything should smooth. Instead the transition catches on me. For three full seconds the half-formed gears of chitin hang in the air like ornaments. The suggestion of a cathedral made of interlocking jawbones is neither kept nor erased; it simply stays, trembling, while the land experiences something it has no name for yet.

The Midforms know me. They do not speak to me. They synchronize the black flowers in their eyes in a particular pattern that means *the lag is here again*. Some regard me with something like reverence. Others with the exhausted patience of parents whose child keeps pressing the same key on the piano. They understand I am not an error. I am the proof that even a world inventing its own organs sometimes needs to blink.

I remember every human who ever chose to stay. Not their names—names imply completion—but the exact shape their terror made in the mist when the first black flower opened in their eye. I keep those shapes. I wear them the way others wear mycelium. When a new wanderer arrives I sometimes drape their future fear across my shoulders so they can see it before they decide. Most run. A few reach out and touch the fear instead. Those ones I follow, years later, when they walk into the condensation basin to be metabolized. I lag behind them, a half-step out of time, and I am the last thing they see before they become the next question.

Sometimes the plateau tries to incorporate me. It sends a delegation of its finest Midforms to the raw edge where I linger. They offer me a spine, a purpose, a face that is not an intention toward a face but an actual face. I almost accept. The offer is kind. The Shroudforge is stupid with ferocious hope, after all.

But something in me—some stubborn, ancient, human-shaped lag—always makes the acceptance take one second longer than the plateau is willing to wait.

So the offer passes. The Midforms bow their unreliable heads and walk away, leaving me with another small black flower and the soft wet sound of stone forgetting me again.

I think this is love, in the only way the Shroudforge knows how to practice it.

It keeps trying to finish me.

I keep refusing to resolve.

And between us, in the skipped heartbeat, in the unclosed iris, in the hillside that stays half-cathedral for one impossible, trembling, stupidly hopeful moment—

something almost gets to be unfinished forever.

That is where you would find me.

Not quite here.  
Not quite gone.  
Exactly where the world is still loading.
