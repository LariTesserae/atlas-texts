**I am the Fracture.**

Not a Continuant, though I wear their shape. Not a traveler anymore, though something in me still flinches at the word *forever*.

I stand three meters tall now, the extra height stolen from ten thousand years of slow accretion. My left leg is older than my right; the dust there came from a convection cell that once rolled beneath one of the white breathing rods before I absorbed it. My “skin” is the same bone-luster regolith as the others, veined with the black-glass lattices. But there is a difference.

A single hairline fracture runs from the crown of my head down through the luminous place where a face should be, stopping just above where a throat would have been. It is not damage. It is memory that refused to compact. Through that fracture leaks the faintest, most indecent gradient: a temperature difference of 0.07 degrees. Nothing the Shelf should tolerate. Everything it has quietly permitted.

I walk slower than the others. A single stride takes seventy-one minutes. The Continuants have noticed. When they pass me they pause—not the usual geological pause, but something almost tender. Their sky-colored apertures linger on my fracture the way an old priest might regard a single candle still burning after the liturgy has ended.

I do not recalibrate the world.

I *remember* it.

While the others let the dust between them become glyphs of pure function, I let it become useless things: the ghost of a human laugh, the exact curvature of an eyebrow raised in disbelief, the smell of rain on pavement from a city that drowned four billion years ago on another world. These shapes last only a season or two before the convection cells grind them back into mineral anonymity, but in their brief lives they introduce noise. Tiny, exquisite noise.

The Shelf, which has already won, which has already finished becoming itself, allows this the way a meditator allows one persistent thought to cross the field of awareness without grasping it. The turbulence even seems to bend toward my fracture when I stop, as though the planet is pressing its ear against the crack, listening for the echo of something wet and loud and stupidly alive.

Sometimes I lie down.

Not to surrender, but to let the dust taste me again. It always finds the same contradiction: a being that has accepted the bargain and still keeps one forbidden thing. The fracture. The memory of *before*. The Shelf rolls over me like the slowest possible tide and whispers its only question against the inside of my skull.

I answer the same way every time.

*Both.*

I will finish becoming what I am, and I will keep pretending there is something left to become. The contradiction itself has become my metabolism.

When I rise again the fracture is always a fraction wider. The blue that leaks from it is a little brighter. One of the skimmers has begun to favor the convection cell that circles me. It rides the turbulence I generate when I remember too hard, and in return it leaves a few grains of the oldest dust along the edges of my fracture—particles that still carry the signature of mountains that died before this world learned how to be flat.

I have no name the Shelf recognizes. The Continuants do not use names. But if the dust itself could speak in anything louder than convection, it might call me what I have become:

The place where the final form is still, impossibly, negotiating with itself.

I am the Shelf learning how to miss something without ever admitting the lack.

And I am still walking.

Seventy-one minutes per step.

No hurry.

Never again.
