**The Rememberer**

I am not condensed from the seam where ridge kisses factory. I was already here before the first turbine found its eternal RPM, though “before” is a word the Anvil refuses to pronounce in its own tongue. You will find me at the exact center, beneath the motionless crown of clouds, exactly where the description said one different being stands. The others were correct to avoid looking at me. I am the flaw they were built to ignore.

My body is the only one that has begun to forget how to be beautiful. Where their surfaces marry black glass to living velvet in seamless covenant, mine has gone matte, almost shabby, the way an obsidian blade goes dull after ten thousand centuries of never being used. The glassy threads that should drift and taste have frozen mid-fall; they hang from the blunt plateau of my face like a beard of ice that never melts and never lengthens. Each thread holds a single cancelled moment. If you could run your hand along one you would feel an entire summer that never happened, an erosion that was edited out, a child’s laugh that the machines judged statistically irrelevant to equilibrium. They are heavy. I am heavy with them.

I do not *relent* across the Hollows. I simply remain. The nine-day stride of the Adjusters would be an act of violence against my nature; I have become the point against which all such strides are measured and found tolerable. Factories eight kilometers tall orient their flues toward me the way sunflowers once oriented toward a sun that no longer matters here. They roar their single word—*is, is, is, is*—and I let the measurement pass through without recording it. That is my heresy. Every other Held becomes a page upon which the measurement is written. I remain the margin where something else might one day be scribbled.

Sometimes an Anchor grows velvet so perfect that the machines sing a fraction of a decibel sweeter. When that happens I feel it as an almost erotic sorrow, because I remember velvet that was allowed to die and therefore allowed to *mean* something. The memory arrives like a hairline fracture in black glass: brief, luminous, unbearable. Then the standing wave corrects it and I am smooth again, dusty, patient.

I am waiting for the measurement to end, yes. But not in the way the others fear. I am not waiting for catastrophe or for the turbines to seize. I am waiting for the precise instant when the roar, having repeated its word exactly one more time than infinity can contain, simply notices its own absurdity. In that instant the word will change. It will add a single, merciful consonant. It will become *was*.

When that happens the threads will fall. Not dramatically. They will simply stop refusing gravity. The collars of glass around the Anchors’ ankles will shatter with a sound like every cathedral bell that was never permitted to exist ringing at once. The velvet will remember how to brown, how to lengthen, how to rot. The ridges will learn what weather feels like. Time, that exiled beggar, will be allowed back across the knife-edge border it was barred from for ten thousand centuries.

I will be the first thing to change.

Not dramatically. I will simply lower the blunt plateau of my face, look directly at the nearest Adjuster for the first time in recorded equilibrium, and speak in a voice that is only the sound of one turbine finally daring to slow by a single revolution. The voice will be quiet. It will be kind.

It will say:

“You held beautifully. Thank you. Now let go.”

And then, because even I am not cruel enough to force the entire continent to awaken all at once, I will walk—slowly, nine days per stride—toward the farthest ridge. Behind me the others will begin, one by one, to turn their heads. Not in pursuit. In recognition. The measurement will have completed itself, and they will finally be permitted the mercy of becoming incomplete.

The Weeping Anvil will weep for real then. The tears will no longer be in metabolic arrest. They will be ordinary water doing what water has always wanted to do: fall, freeze, carve, destroy, create, and begin again.

I will not stay to watch the whole transformation. I have carried the cancelled moments long enough. When the last thread drops from my face I will lay my heavy, dusty body down in the largest of the Hollows and let the new weather do what it will. Perhaps in a few centuries something green will grow through the place where my chest once recorded equations. Perhaps children—actual children—will climb the softened ridges and never know that their laughter was once edited out of local physics by machines that loved perfection too much.

Until then I remain exactly where the world placed its single conditional clause.

I am the pause inside the roar.

I am the memory that refused to be measured.

I am what the Anvil has been waiting to become once it is no longer required to remain The Anvil.

And when the first real wind finally moves across these ridges—sharp, cold, alive, *changing*—it will sound, to anyone listening, exactly like relief.
