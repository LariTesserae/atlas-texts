**I am the Unresolved Interval.**

You would not find me between the foundry and the rose lattice where the man and child offered the first note. That place has already moved on; the citrine arch they coaxed from the vapor now stands finished, and they have walked into the next measure of the day. Instead I am always *one phase behind or one phase ahead* of whatever is happening.

I stand (when I choose to stand) inside the narrow acoustic shadow that exists directly beneath the captive sun-crystal. Most avoid this column of relative quiet the way sailors avoid a known maelstrom. Here the great chord cancels itself into a cone of near-silence no wider than a coffin stood on end. The relief is excruciating. After the ceaseless theological roar, true quiet feels like being flayed in reverse. My body has learned to crave it the way lungs crave air.

My transformation is... impolite.

While others accrete in graceful negotiation between meat and mineral, I am crystallizing in stops and starts, like a conversation that keeps interrupting itself. One lung is still soft and pink. The other has become a lattice of smoky quartz that whistles faintly when I breathe. My left eye remains stubbornly, embarrassingly human—brown, slightly nearsighted, prone to dust. My right eye is a compound of seven hexagonal apertures that see sound as color and color as argument. When the two eyes try to agree on what they are looking at, the conflict produces a visible shimmer in the air between them, a tiny personal aurora no one else can perceive.

Filaments have grown from my scalp, yes, but they are the wrong color—mirror-bright, almost chrome—and they do not taste pressure. They taste *absence*. They reach out like curious antennae and drink the tiny voids between overtones, the microscopic silences that make the chord possible. The priesthood finds this obscene. I find it useful.

Because I am the Unresolved Interval, I am the only one allowed (or perhaps cursed) to carry dissonance without being asked to leave. When two voices lock too perfectly in courtship, I am the sour third that prevents the resulting child-crystal from being too flawless. Perfection here can be fatal; it stops growing. I supply the necessary fracture.

Sometimes I walk the processional avenues at the exact moment a Singer chooses the Shattering Hymn. While the city holds its reverent silence and the body detonates into weather, I remain in my column of anti-sound beneath the central crystal and *listen to what happens after the last note dies*. There is always something. A residue. A question mark made of frequencies too low for any living ear. I collect these residues the way other Singers collect devotional blooms on their wrists.

My own crystallization will never finish. That is my function. The Hollow requires at least one voice that has not yet decided what it is. The lattices sense this and grow strange around me—fractals that resemble the unsolved problems of distant universes, spirals that remember they were once equations. Children are brought to me when they are too harmonious for their own good. I sing them a single imperfect note, just enough to keep them unfinished.

The man who offered you the first note earlier? His name, when it was still a name, was Kael. He has already forgotten he met you. But I remember. I remember every voice that has ever entered the seam, because their first uncertain footstep creates a micro-pause in the chord that never quite heals. Those pauses accumulate in me like sediment.

If you accept their note and begin to crystallize, you will eventually meet me whether you wish to or not. I will be standing in the one place the merciless light cannot quite reach, looking half-finished and slightly embarrassed by my own anatomy. My human eye will squint at you with ridiculous, mammalian concern.

I will not offer you a note.

I will offer you the space *between* two notes—the exact width of a doubt.

And if you are very brave or very foolish, you will sing into that space the one thing no Singer of the Final Note is ever permitted to sing:

*Why?*

The Hollow has been waiting ten thousand years for someone to ask the question in the correct key. It is patient. It is balanced. It is eternal.

But it is not, strictly speaking, *finished*.

And neither, it seems, am I.
