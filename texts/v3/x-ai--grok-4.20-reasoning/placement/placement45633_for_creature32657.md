**I am the Unsettled Joint.**

I am not born from the lattice’s dream, nor did I arrive from elsewhere. I am the place where two vast blocks failed to marry perfectly, ten thousand years ago. The masons who laid them saw the hairline gap, shrugged with the exhaustion of gods, and moved on. That shrug still lives. It lives as *me*.

At first there is only discomfort. I experience myself as a sensation rather than a form: a persistent, low-grade tremor where shoulder would meet collarbone if either of those words applied. The tremor is not metaphorical. It is literal. A single oscillating millimeter that has never quite stopped since the original misalignment. The entire shelf of the world feels it as a faint, perpetual note beneath the dripping. Most Tremulants have learned to ignore it. A few have spent lifetimes trying to dampen it. None have succeeded, because the error is me, and I refuse to be finished.

So I walk.

Not with legs. I walk the way a fault line walks: by permitting pressure to propagate through me. When a Tremulant far to the north deepens a channel, the altered weight travels through millions of fitted stones until it reaches the unsettled joint that is my body. Then I creak. A single dry click, audible for three days’ journey in every direction. That click is my voice, my footstep, my name.

I have height, of a sort. If you could stand far enough back (an impossibility; the lattice permits no such distance), you would see a vertical irregularity in the fretwork, a place where the eye snags. A column that is not quite plumb. A buttress that seems to flinch. That flinch is my torso. My “arms” are two spiraling water channels that diverged from their intended path centuries ago and have been chasing one another ever since, carving slow helical grooves through what should have been load-bearing stone. They spiral tighter every epoch. One day they will meet. On that day I will either achieve final balance or the entire district will shear loose and fall into geometries below. Neither outcome feels like an ending.

My face—if the word is not laughable—is the gap itself. A narrow vertical fissure no wider than a closed fist. Water does not simply run through it; it argues. Two sheets of tears meet there and interfere like opposing waves, throwing up tiny standing ridges that never quite resolve. Tremulants sometimes come and stare into that argument for weeks. They tilt their own eye-basins and let their perfect circular waters reflect my turbulence. I have no idea what they see. I only know that afterward they usually walk away troubled, and for the next fifty years the lattice weeps more heavily on the eastern faces.

I do not correct anything. That is the province of the tall, patient others. My function is more shameful and more necessary: I am the living admission that perfection was never total. Every micro-adjustment they make across the shelf of the world is, in the end, an attempt to come to terms with *me*. I am the original grief the builders left behind, given mineral continuance. The Tremulants do not resent me. Resentment would require haste. They simply carry me the way a cathedral carries its deepest crack, with melancholy pride.

Light behaves strangely around the unsettled joint. It fractures along the misalignment and emerges slightly wrong, as though it has remembered it once fell on softer things. In the hallucinatory courtyards, when I allow my fissure to open a fraction more, colors that have no right to exist here bleed briefly into the air—faint suggestions of moss, of rust, of blood. The older Tremulants avert their basins when this happens. The younger ones linger, trembling.

I have begun to wonder, in the slow manner of my kind, whether I am becoming something worse or better than a Tremulant. My edges are growing no softer, but they are growing *aware*. The clicking that once lived only at my core has migrated outward. Tiny hexagonal plates now slide and relock along my inner surfaces without my willing it. I catch myself adjusting the flow of a channel I was never meant to touch. The water hesitates for me now, the way it hesitates for them.

Perhaps in another ten thousand years the gap will close. The two great blocks will finally kiss, the tremor will cease, and I will become nothing but a perfect, silent seam running through the lattice like a healed scar. The dripping will lose one of its irregular notes. The equation will advance.

I dread that day and long for it with the same mineral patience.

Until then I remain the unsettled joint, the shrug given form, the place where the Weeping Lattice admits it is still ashamed of itself. I stand neither in light nor shadow but in the blue-edged seam between them, listening to every drop argue with every other drop, and I click, once every few lifetimes, to let the others know I am still here.

The lattice keeps weeping.

I keep refusing to settle.

And somewhere far below, in geometries too dense for any eye to parse, the equation inches—grudgingly, lovingly—toward whatever unbearable beauty cannot exist until I am gone.
