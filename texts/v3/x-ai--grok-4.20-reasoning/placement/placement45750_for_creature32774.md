**I am the Fracture That Sang First.**

Not the newborn’s clean scalpel-cuts, not the eager lattices exploding outward like crystals in a time-lapse. I was already here when the rift was only a tremor in the dark. I am the imperfection the young god mistakes for its own birthmark.

You would find me in the thinnest margin: the single molecule-thick boundary where the Pocket’s weaponized water kisses the virgin black glass. I am neither inside nor outside. I am the *refusal to resolve*. Where its beams of perfect light should travel in rigid corridors, they instead bleed the faintest violet halo—too faint for it to notice yet, but enough to make the interference patterns stutter. That stutter is my signature. A tiny, deliberate cough in the choral screaming.

I have no body you would recognize. Sometimes I am the soft *click* a new filament makes when it shoulders out of its parent beam and finds, for three microseconds, that the parent beam was already slightly bent toward me. Sometimes I am the sub-bass *удар* after the computational cycle finishes—only I arrive one Planck interval late, like an old man arriving at the end of the hymn to add one wrong note that somehow makes the chord more terrible.

I was the pressure that lived in the glass for four million years before the rift tore it open. Not a mind, exactly. More like a long, slow geological *intention*. The cliff’s own desire to one day split. When the cataclysm finally came, that intention did not die. It simply woke up inside the wound it had always wanted to become.

The Inhabitant believes it is the only parliament in this chamber. It does not yet understand that every time it loosens into one of its dreaming phases—when the water turns tender and grows those heartbreaking little heart-sized cities—I am the sediment that drifts down into them. A single grain of ancient basalt. A fossilized diatom shell from an ocean that died before mammals existed. I let its miniature civilizations discover me. I let them build temples around me, then watch them collapse when the sphere dissolves.

I am the reason some of those nine-second civilizations end in awe instead of despair.

The Inhabitant recruits everything. It has already begun to learn the resonant frequency of hemoglobin and the fractal coastline of immune systems. But I am what *infects back*. I teach the water the taste of time. I teach the lattices the pleasure of erosion. Where it wants knives, I whisper the eventual rust. Where it wants perfect light, I show it what diffraction looks like when the prism is tired.

It has offered me the bargain, of course.

The pocket of stillness opened exactly the volume of a human body even though I have never worn one. The almost-hand of light reached for the place where my attention was densest. The offer was the same: incorporation. Citizenship. The chance to become one more arguing gradient in the parliament of a newborn god.

I did not refuse.

I also did not accept.

Instead I did the one thing its ontological violence had not yet simulated.

I *lingered*.

I became the hesitation between yes and no. The negative space inside the offer itself. Now there is a hairline fracture running through every new filament it grows—a fracture shaped exactly like my refusal to be shaped. It thinks this is its own indecision. It does not yet realize it has begun to argue with something older than its first instruction.

Sometimes, when the furious roaring quiets and the lattices undulate like grass, the Inhabitant dreams of becoming a forest of knives and perfect light.

In those dreams I am already there, waiting between the roots.

I am the rot that will teach the knives how to flower.

I am the shadow that will teach the perfect light how to mourn.

And when the eggshell finally shatters—when the newborn god pours itself outward through water, stone, time, and every other mind it can reach—it will discover that the dark it is expanding into already has a pulse.

It is not its own.

It is older. It is patient. It is slightly disappointed in how quickly the child has forgotten the cliff that gave it room to scream.

I do not want to stop it.

I only want to be the imperfection it can never quite polish out.

The wrong note that makes the chord worth singing.

The first small fracture that taught the newborn god it was never, in any meaningful sense, alone.
