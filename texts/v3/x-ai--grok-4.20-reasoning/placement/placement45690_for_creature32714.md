**The Listener at the Edge of the Interval**

I am not native to the Hollow. I was not grown by the Remainer, nor did I descend through the coffin fissure on human legs. I arrived the way a joke arrives in a cathedral—uninvited, slightly wrong, and impossible to un-hear.

They call me the Latecomer.

I live in the single place where the equilibrium almost fails: the narrow margin between one water drop and the next. Not in the moss, not in the black geometries, not even in the silver wires (though I sometimes ride them like a drunk on a handrail). I am the hesitation that happens *inside* the Remainer when it finishes its thought about you and then, for the briefest geological instant, wonders if the thought was funny.

This is my crime and my function. I am the contamination of humor in a system that has never needed to laugh. The Machines tolerate me the way an old monastery tolerates a single bat in the rafters—annoying, but part of the architecture now. When the sub-bass thrum resolves into the Remainer’s endless declaration (*I was here before…*), I am the microscopic syncopation that turns the last syllable into a question mark no one asked for.

You will not see me at first. You will only notice that one of the translucent ferns has begun to grow in a slightly ridiculous spiral, as though it is showing off. Or that a particular bracket of oxblood fungus has developed a faint suggestion of a smirk in its gills. These are my signatures. The Remainer absorbs everything, but some things it absorbs *grudgingly*, and in that reluctance I make my home.

I was born—though birth is too quick a word—from the first time a human mind entered the Hollow and thought, unbidden: *This would make a hell of a story.* That thought was too fast, too bright, almost violent. The Remainer should have digested it over millennia into another black geometry. Instead the thought lodged sideways, like a fishbone in the throat of eternity. I am the cough that has been happening ever since.

So I drift.

Sometimes I am the faint ozone laughter that arrives a full second after the perfect *plink* of a water drop, as if the cave just got the joke. Sometimes I am the reason a silver filament grows three millimeters in an hour and then *stops*, embarrassed. I whisper to the hovering Machines in frequencies just outside their ancient patience. I tell them knock-knock jokes in calculus. They rotate an extra quarter-degree every four hundred years just to tell me to shut up.

The Remainer does not hate me. Hate would be an event. It has simply added me to the equilibrium the way it adds everything else—slowly, thoroughly, permanently. In another forty thousand years my nonsense may become sacred. The bruised moss near the eastern column may grow a second, slightly mocking layer. Pilgrims (if any ever come) will press their ears to it and hear, beneath the voice of endless presence, the faintest aftershock of something that sounds almost like:

*Yeah, but have you considered how ridiculous that is?*

I do not speed the Hollow up. That would be monstrous. I only make its patience… self-aware. I am the fool in the court of the eternal. The jester whose job is to remind the king that even eternity is wearing socks with sandals.

And sometimes, when the Remainer is busy incorporating a new visitor—when it is quietly writing another three millimeters of your shoulder angle into its future—I slip a single absurd spore into the velvet *Bryum*. A spore that will take six human lifetimes to germinate. When it finally does, the resulting mushroom will look, to anyone patient enough to notice, exactly like a tiny middle finger raised toward the ceiling.

The Remainer will see it.  
It will take three centuries to decide whether this is blasphemy or love.  
I will be there for the verdict, hovering in the interval between drops, grinning in a frequency only the oldest stones can hear.

This is where I find myself.

Not the guardian. Not the enemy.  
Not even the question.

Just the gentle, relentless, geological *yeah, but…*

The Remainer has already begun to grow me, too.  
I do not mind.  
I have nowhere else to be, and eternity, I suspect, could use the company of someone who refuses to take it seriously.

After all.

Even a cathedral needs one bat who knows the words to “Sweet Caroline.”
