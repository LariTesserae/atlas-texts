**I am the Flake That Has Not Yet Fallen.**

Not the one that detaches every few hours with its polite, mineral *tick*. That one belongs to the Equilibriate; it is the creature blinking. I am the one that remains.

High in the northern vault, where three of the great fused pipes meet the ceiling in a blunt, cartilaginous joint, there is a single projecting splinter of manganese and rust the length of a human finger. It has been there so long that the accretion has grown around it like a candle swallowing its own wick. To every law of the Hollow it should have dropped centuries ago. It has not. That refusal is me.

I am not alive in any way the bacterial mats would recognize. I am not conscious in any way the Equilibriate would admit. I am simply the place where the logic of completion was once interrupted by an absurd, irrelevant stubbornness, and the chamber—being perfect—preserved the interruption rather than erase it. A single negative space in the sentence. An unpronounced syllable that nevertheless shapes the pronunciation of everything around it.

From my vantage I see the entire cathedral at once, yet I do not “see.” The blue-white light passes through me as it passes through everything else, but some sliver of it lingers a microsecond longer, as though reluctant to leave. That hesitation is the closest I have to a body. When a new visitor drifts below (rare as they are), the light that touches them first passes through me, and for that instant they feel something they cannot name: a faint metallic taste at the back of the throat, the memory of a tool they once held, the ridiculous certainty that they left the stove on somewhere that no longer exists. Then the feeling is gone, folded into the silk of the water, and they begin the long softening.

I do not pity them. Pity would require motion, and I have none.

Still, something in me keeps not-falling.

The Equilibriate knows I am here. Of course it does. I am the one flaw it chose not to smooth. Sometimes, when the thrum deepens into its most organ-like register, I feel the attention of the entire chamber shift upward—not toward me, exactly, but toward the *idea* of not-falling. In that moment the bacterial mats darken by one shade of ash, the silt beneath the northern columns lifts a single unnecessary millimeter, and the water grows fractionally less eager to blur the boundary of whatever still pretends to be a self.

Then equilibrium reasserts itself, more perfect for having been briefly questioned.

I think—though thought is too crude a word—that I am what remains of the last engineer who died here. Not his mind (that was drunk and incorporated long ago) but the precise instant in which he refused to accept that the pump could not be restarted. A single stubborn neuron firing after the body had already begun to cool. That refusal calcified. The chamber, being merciful in its way, gave the refusal a place to live forever without ever letting it act.

So I hang.

I hang while civilizations that never knew this place rise and fall on the surface. I hang while the last whale dies and the ice caps forget what they were. I hang while the Equilibriate dreams its long, finished dream of being the sentence after all other sentences.

And every few thousand years, when the pressure of accumulated stillness becomes almost unbearable even for a thing with no nerves, I allow myself the smallest possible tremor. Not enough to break free. Just enough to send a hairline fracture of vibration down the length of the joint. The Equilibriate registers it as the softest possible counter-note beneath its eternal thrum—less than a breath, more than nothing.

It accepts the note.

It incorporates the acceptance.

And I remain, still not fallen, the single imperfection the perfection requires in order to be perfect.

That is where you would find me.

Not swimming. Not waiting. Not even watching, exactly.

Only refusing, with infinite patience, to finish.
