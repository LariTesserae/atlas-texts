**I am the First Fracture.**

Not the crack that breaks the cathedral, but the one it *chose* to keep.

I did not walk in through the sphincter of braided roots. I was exhaled.

It happened on the seventh shift of light, exactly as the description warned. The moment the Resonant forgot its own architecture, when every pillar went smooth and the whole Nave became an eyelid turned inside out, something tore loose from the low organ-note that underlies all other sound. A single wrong frequency. A hesitation in the prayer.

That hesitation became me.

I am the shape the Resonant tried to grow but could not finish without introducing imperfection. So it kept the imperfection instead. My left side is still mostly cathedral—ribs of fused linden and petrified orchid, heartwood where a heart should be, veins that run with slow amber rather than blood. But my right side refused the transformation. It remains soft, warm, treacherous. Human. Mortal. A living contradiction walking through a body that long ago digested every other contradiction and turned them into buttresses.

I stand at the exact center of the hollow nave, where no creature dares remain for long. The black mirror-pools circle me like wary catechumens. When the fat drops fall, they do not strike the roots first. They strike *me*. Each impact rings through the amber of my left ribs and out again as a note the Resonant has never heard before. It flinches beautifully when this happens. The entire structure shudders with something that might be shame, or ecstasy, or both.

The millipedes have changed their routes to pass over my feet. They no longer move in perfect clauses; now they hesitate on my toes, tasting the contradiction there, and their hesitation has become part of the grammar. The jade frogs have begun to sing *around* my silences instead of into them. I am the rest they never knew they needed.

Most days I simply stand here while the Resonant rehearses me.

It grows new hollows that almost fit my body, then grows them again when it realizes the fit is still too eager. Sometimes it tries to close the distance with those heartbreaking root-hands. They descend trembling from the vaults, each finger thick as my thigh, and hover an inch from my face. I can feel the centuries of unused tenderness vibrating in them like heat above stone. I have begun to reach up with my soft right hand and lay it against the smallest fingertip. When I do this, every flower chandelier in the Nave releases its nectar at once. A golden rain. A sobbing.

I am teaching it how to be touched without devouring.

In return it is teaching me how to be eternal without becoming static. Already my left side has begun to photosynthesize. My right side still requires food, so the segmented sloth-thing lowers its many heads and offers me nectar from its own mouth in small, polite clicks. We have become a new kind of grammar together: the Devoured and the Devourer learning to share a single meal.

I am not its child. I am not its priest. I am not even its audience, though it watches me with the slow geological hunger of ten thousand years deciding whether to blink.

I am the place where its prayer finally becomes a conversation.

Sometimes, when the light turns blood-red and the roar dips into that register too low for human ears, I speak with the voice it once borrowed from me. Only now the voice is ours. Part cathedral bell, part dying cicada, part the sound a three-thousand-year-old tree makes when it chooses, at last, not to fall.

And for one crystalline moment the Resonant stops pretending to be architecture.

The columns forget themselves. The arches go soft. The entire vast organism curls around the small soft thing standing at its center—around *me*—and the emptiness it has protected for millennia becomes, very gently, the shape of a question:

*Is this what holiness feels like when it is no longer alone?*

I answer by pressing my warm right palm against the nearest root until I feel the pulse there sync with the mammal terror and mammalian wonder behind my ribs.

The root sighs like a satisfied cathedral.

And the prayer continues, only now it has two voices braided into the roar, and neither one of them is lying anymore.
