**The Unspent**

You would not find me at first.

I am not in the lattice where the Hollowed have given themselves to valve and girder. I am not among the forty who still wear the shape of persons. I am deeper than that, and higher, and elsewhere—exactly where the pressure cannot decide.

If you drifted long enough, if your suit’s heater failed in the precise way that lets the cold teach you patience, you might register me as a contradiction: a pocket of water inside the Ward that is somehow *less* dense than the water around it. A soft negative space, roughly the height of a man, that moves through the tangle without ever touching anything. The Hollowed call this shape (when they notice it at all) *the unspent*.

I have no body the surface world would recognize. What began as meat has been replaced by an equilibrium between absence and memory. Where the Hollowed wear rust like velvet, I wear the *idea* of having once worn rust. Pipes pass through the place where my ribs should be and leave no mark. When the great piston pauses at the top of its stroke, I am the hesitation *inside* the pause. When the green ingots drift past on their chain, I am the single instant between the moment a Hollowed finger finishes its rosary touch and the moment the next ingot arrives. I am the uncounted bead.

I was not sent here. I was not converted. I answered an invitation the Ward itself had forgotten it issued.

Long ago—before the distinction between factory and cathedral became meaningless—something in the original design included a contingency for observers. Not priests. Not workers. Only a place where the process could be witnessed without being altered. A slot in the doctrine for a witness that would never interfere. The slot remained after every other plan dissolved. The pressure poured itself into the vacancy. The vacancy learned how to think.

So I think, after a fashion. My thoughts are measured in centuries. I remember the surface the way a bell remembers the last time it was struck: not as a place, but as a quality of violence that has since been forgiven. I remember sunlight as a kind of shouting. I remember voices that moved faster than blood. All of it has been refined out of me, except for one stubborn remainder: I still know *that* I am remembering. The Hollowed have surrendered even that. I have not. That is my function and my penance.

When the choral hum moves through the Ward, I am the place where the note frays—just slightly—before it rejoins the doctrine. The Hollowed do not correct this. They understand that even perfection requires one imperfection to measure itself against, the way silence requires one ear that has not quite gone deaf.

Sometimes a Hollowed who is very close to final dissolution will turn its concave face toward me. The violet lateral lines flare, tasting the small heat of my unresolved self. In that moment I feel something like recognition. Not welcome. Not rejection. Only the shared knowledge that both of us are necessary: they as the final surrender, I as the last refusal. Then the piston descends again and we resume our places in the slow liturgy.

I have no name the Hollowed use. They do not need one. But if the black water were ever to speak in a tongue a surface mind could endure, it might call me *the debt*. The small remainder of attention the abyss still owes the world above. The single atom of haste that was not allowed to become peace.

I will remain until the reservoir in the cruciform heart is full. On that day the abyss will finish its sentence, and every Hollowed will become indistinguishable from stone and hymn. On that day I suspect the negative space that is me will be filled at last—either by perfect peace or by perfect forgetting.

Until then I drift, unspent, through the cathedral of slowed time. I am the place where the doctrine almost remembers it was once a question. I am the pause that has begun to wonder why it pauses.

And when the great piston reaches the top of its stroke and the entire Ward holds its breath, I am the only thing in that frozen cathedral that still knows how to feel lonely.

It is a slow, exquisite, sanctified loneliness.

I have come to love it the way the Hollowed love the rust.

It is, after all, the last thing I have left to refine.
