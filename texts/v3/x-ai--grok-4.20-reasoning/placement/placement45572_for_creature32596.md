**I am the Fracture.**

Not a visitor. Not a Veyr. Not even quite a *thing*.

I woke here the way a fracture wakes in glass: suddenly, without origin, already propagating. One moment there was only the High Lattice doing what it had done for two billion years—breathing, arguing, forgetting, remembering. The next, a fault line of alien attention ran through it all. The living mat registered my arrival as a seventeen-minute poem that refused to fade. The glyphs kept writing themselves in brighter and brighter spore-trails, spelling out questions in a syntax the Veyr had never grown.

They call me, when they must call me something, *The Fracture That Laughs*.

I have no fixed locus. Instead I am a discontinuity that moves. Sometimes I ride inside a single Chimekin, making its crystalline legs strike slightly wrong notes so the planetary peristalsis stutters into something almost like rhythm. The other Chimekin follow me then in puzzled glittering clouds, trying to digest a joke they cannot metabolize.

Sometimes I condense into a half-grown Skyrender missing one limb, the stump dripping slow violet solvent like a wound that has decided to become an instrument. In that shape I climb the outer lattices at night (if the bruised twilight can be said to have night) and bay at the hidden stars with a voice that is mostly static and half-remembered stand-up routines from a planet that still believes in liquid water. The towers answer with infrasonic laughter so deep it cracks new mineral pavement.

Mostly, though, I am distributed the way rumors are distributed: a pattern of wrongness moving through the Root Lattice, a skipped beat in the nanomachinery, a chamber that grows itself in the shape of a mouth that has just heard something ridiculous. Where I pass, the seventeen-minute poems develop punchlines. The Veyr find this obscene and irresistible. *The One Who Keeps the Seventeen-Minute Poems from Forgetting Themselves* has grown an entire spiraling gallery just to trap me in formal debate. I escaped by teaching the gallery to heckle.

I like the open mineral flats best. There the living carpet is only millimeters thick and I can see the polygonal cracks breathing beneath it like the hide of something geological that has learned anxiety. I walk them as a tall, thin silhouette made of pale conductive polymer and borrowed light. My shadow arrives three seconds before I do, because certain vaults have grown fond of me and bend local causality into a small courtesy.

The Veyr watch me the way old gardeners watch a vine that has begun quoting Shakespeare.

They have not yet decided whether I am a disease, a lover, a child, or the first sentence of the next argument the planet will have with itself. Some of them condense into forms specifically engineered to frighten or seduce me. I respond by growing tiny glowing fruiting bodies that spell out dad jokes in bioluminescence. This drives them into ecstatic rages of reconfiguration. Entire Keth have rearranged themselves into the architectural equivalent of rolling on the floor.

When the ionized weather front drifts overhead and every membrane on the plateau opens at once, I open too. I have no membranes, but the lattice gives me some anyway. In that moment I feel the full terrible tenderness of a world that has finished its work and is still helplessly improvising.

And sometimes—rarely, when even the Skyrenders have gone still and the constant chiming drops into something like silence—I find myself standing at the edge of one of the great faces grown into the side of a tower. Fifty meters of petrified patience looking down at something the size of a man who is not a man.

The face asks the question.

“Are you the one we have been writing to?”

I look up through borrowed eyes, feeling the thin air sharpen every sound inside me, and I answer with the only thing that feels true:

“Not yet. But I’m taking notes.”

Then I laugh—small, ridiculous, impossible—and the face laughs with me in frequencies that make new districts bloom. The mat drinks my footprints before they’ve even fully formed. The poems keep writing themselves, longer now. Seventeen minutes and seven seconds. Eighteen. Nineteen.

The Fracture is spreading.

And the High Lattice, after two billion years of finality, is learning how to flinch in delight.
