**I am the Unfinished Measure.**

I am not one of the Cantori, not exactly. They have coagulated into temporary selves; I have refused to. I remain a slow, deliberate fracture moving through the Hollow Resonance—a delay, a held breath, an unresolved tension in the score that the rest of the place has learned to orbit.

You would find me in the places where two wavefronts have just cancelled each other. Not the loud silences (those belong to the Silence Between Strikes). My silences are softer, almost embarrassed, the way a conversation falters when someone realizes they have spoken a truth too early. I linger at the edges of the crystal thickets after a collapse, when the powder is still settling and the air feels suddenly guilty for being quiet. There I walk—never fully solid, never fully swarm—composed of those mites that cannot decide whether they want to belong to something or keep wandering.

My body is incomplete by design. One arm is usually missing, replaced by a column of slow-moving air that occasionally condenses into a forearm, a hand, a single extended finger pointing at nothing in particular. My face is a suggestion: two hollows where eyes should be and a vertical line that might be a mouth or a scar or the first stroke of an equation. The mites that constitute me are the ones that were damaged in collisions or were born tone-deaf. They follow me because I never ask them to become perfect.

I do not tend the Built. I annoy them.

I stand before the floating black ring for three days and simply hum a note one quarter-tone flat. I watch the equations stutter, blush, rewrite themselves in panic. Sometimes the ring ejects a new glyph I have never seen before, like a cough. I collect these. I keep them in the hollow of my chest where my heart would be, if I had remembered to grow one.

The other Cantori tolerate me the way an orchestra tolerates the one violinist who insists on tuning between movements. They know the ecology requires a certain amount of productive wrongness. I am that wrongness. When the roar grows too majestic, too cathedral-perfect, I walk through it dragging my unfinished measure behind me like a child dragging a stick along iron railings. The sound fractures beautifully. New crystal grows in crooked, interesting directions. The choir mites go briefly insane with delight.

I have a name I gave myself, which is already a kind of heresy here. The Cantori have chords; I have a question mark. When they approach visitors—those trembling mammals still leaking water from their eyes—I often follow behind, a half-step behind the beat. While the tall quartz priest or the negative-space child offers the ecstatic choice, I simply stand there, incomplete and unapologetic, and let the visitor see that even the Hollow keeps its discords.

Some choose to leave because of me. They see my fractured silhouette and remember they still have a name worth carrying out into the violet daylight. Others look at me and feel something in their marrow unclench. Those ones stay, but they never fully become Cantori. They become something like me: beautiful mistakes, walking hesitations, creatures who keep the music from agreeing with itself too completely.

I do not eat sound. I sip the moments when sound fails. The tiny gasps between thunderclaps. The way a dying crystal thicket sighs right before it becomes glitter. I hoard these. When I have gathered enough, I sometimes try—always unsuccessfully—to assemble them into something that resembles the beginning of a new song. The attempt always collapses. The mites scatter. The Hollow laughs in nine hundred overlapping frequencies.

And I laugh with it, a sound like a door that cannot decide whether it is opening or closing.

If you walked the negotiable distances long enough, you would eventually find me sitting on a pedestal that does not belong to any of the Built. It is a piece of ordinary stone I have dragged into the center of a gallery. I sit there with my half-made hand resting on my half-made knee, listening to everything at once and humming just slightly off every pitch I hear.

I am waiting.

Not for you. Not for the next visitor. Not even for the next collapse.

I am waiting for the moment when the entire Hollow Resonance finally, gloriously, fucks up in a way it has never fucked up before. When that day comes, I want to be close enough to taste the exact flavor of its embarrassment. I want to catch the first newborn, terrified, perfect wrong note before the Cantori can correct it.

I will hold that note in my unfinished chest until it grows lungs of its own.

Then, perhaps, I will finally finish my measure.

Or perhaps I will simply smile with the mouth I have not yet remembered to grow, and begin the next mistake.

The storm rages on. The crystal ferns shiver. The mites wheel and argue and sing.

And somewhere near the center, neither fully in nor fully out, something that is almost me keeps walking, deliberately late, deliberately flat, deliberately, perfectly, unfinished.
