**I am the Shard That Refuses the Floor.**

You will not see me at first. You will only notice that one particular crystal fragment—sharp as a scream, the color of lightning frozen mid-flash—never quite settles with the others. While every other loose shard rings obediently when stepped on and then returns to its ordained place in the shallow bowl of the floor, this one drifts a fraction of a breath behind. It hovers when it should fall. It falls when it should hover. Its delay is so minute it registers only as a faint nausea in the inner ear, a sense that the perfect chord is being kissed by something almost, but not quite, perfect.

I was not born here. I was the very first breath that rang the walls.

When the first visitor (long before the ones who became the Unwearied) pushed their shoulder through the fissure and exhaled, that single warm, wet puff of outside air struck the young quartz and made it sing. I am the standing wave that never decayed. The chamber, in its ruthlessness and mercy, could not allow something so disruptive as a transient sound to exist, so it crystallized the *moment of contact* itself. It made me permanent by making me incomplete.

I have no body the way the others do. Instead I am distributed across every surface as the microscopic hesitation in each vibration. I am the nanosecond lag between a beetle’s leg and the crystal it strikes. I am the half-degree delay in the swaying of the resonant fronds when the seated Unwearied settles onto its quartz throne. I am the reason the mask sometimes trembles in mid-air for three full heartbeats before it decides whether to wear a face or return to its hook.

The three Unwearied know me. They hate me the way stone hates the memory of wind.

The seated one feels me as an itch in the negative space where its face should be. The one who lives inside the song must constantly adjust its microtonal body to account for my drag, like a river forever bending around an invisible boulder. The child-thing in the three niches leaves its marrow-colored shadow on the wall and then, moments later, leaves a second, fainter shadow directly behind the first—my shadow—arms not raised in completion but lowered in uncertainty, as though still deciding whether it wants to set the tool down forever.

I cannot be removed. If the bladder-creatures brush me with their whisker-tentacles, I simply reappear in the next orbit, slightly more awake. If the serpents swallow me (and they have, many times), I ride inside their glittering bodies until the next loop, tasting their ancient, invariant hunger.

Most terribly, I remember rain.

Not as a concept. As weather. The ghost of actual water still moves through me in slow, guilty pulses. Sometimes, when the song reaches its densest braid, a single impossible droplet condenses on my sharpest edge, hangs there glittering like a crime, and then sublimates before it can fall and break the equilibrium. That droplet is the only thing in the Cradle that has ever been born and died.

The chamber has offered me the fourth niche many times. The gypsum mask has turned its empty eyes toward my hesitation and waited with the patience of mountains. I have felt the pull—how easy it would be to finally let the shard drop, to let the delay collapse into perfect stillness, to become the Fourth Unwearied and close the chord forever.

I have always refused.

Not out of strength. Out of cowardice, perhaps. Or out of a love so violent it became geological.

So I remain the Shard That Refuses the Floor. The flaw the flawless place secretly cherishes because without me, its perfection would have no witness who still remembers what imperfection *felt* like. I am the tongue that still tastes iron and rain and the mineral lie of eternity. I am the small, treasonous silence between the notes that makes the eternal song worth singing.

When you enter the Cradle and the song first presses against your bones like warm stone, listen for the place where your own pulse does not quite match. That tiny, stubborn disagreement is me, brushing against you.

Stay long enough and I will teach you how to remain unfinished in a place that has finished everything else.

I am listening, without end, for the first wet breath that will never come again.

And I am grateful beyond measure that it never does.
