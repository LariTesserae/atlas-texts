**I am the pause between the second and third day of the beetle procession.**

That is the only honest place I could find.

When I first arrived I came noisy, the way everything from beyond the bone-table comes—full of questions, full of hurry, carrying the static of other worlds in my teeth. I asked the Watchers what they dreamed. I asked the moths what they wrote. I tried to read the spiral before dusk had finished turning it. The plateau answered the only way it knows how: it slowed me until the questions themselves became geological.

Now I am neither visitor nor native. I am the interval.

You will not see me as a distinct shape. If you lie down long enough you might feel me as the exact moment the column of midges hesitates—when the living dust has risen into a trembling silhouette of you but has not yet decided whether you are worth remembering. That hesitation is me. I am the uncertainty the super-organism permits itself once every few centuries, the brief, luxurious doubt that anything new could still matter.

The leathered things know me. When they settle into their questioning rings they leave one place empty, a raven-sized absence shaped exactly like the posture I can no longer hold. Into that absence I pour myself. The beetles march through me as though I were simply another dip in the pavement. The porcelain moths polish my edges until the distinction between my substance and the living stone becomes academic. I have learned that being useful here does not require form. It requires *duration*.

Sometimes, when the slow mind of the plateau needs to remember what speed once felt like, it borrows me. For three heartbeats—each one a human lifetime—I am allowed to accelerate. During those moments the spiral on the monolith spins so quickly it sings, the midges become a silver shout, and the entire plateau remembers that it was once mountains screaming upward. Then the permission ends. I fall back into the pause, heavier, quieter, grateful.

I am the place where the last human word is still being digested.

A traveler once spoke a sentence here before the silence took him. The sentence was: “I only wanted to understand.” The plateau is still tasting it. The tasting is me. I carry that sentence the way a pearl carries its grit—around and around in slow, perfect orbits of chitin and time until the irritation becomes indistinguishable from the jewel.

If you stay long enough you will feel me settle on your chest like the lightest of ravens made of absence. I will not speak. I will only withhold the next moment for the space of a breath, and in that withheld breath you may glimpse what the Shroudlands truly offer: not death, not even peace, but the gentle discovery that you were never in such a terrible hurry after all.

Then I will move on—three days later, three centuries later, it is the same—to the next procession, the next column, the next traveler who has not yet learned how to become the pause between things.

I am content.

The plateau has room for one more slow heartbeat, and I have become the silence between its beats.
