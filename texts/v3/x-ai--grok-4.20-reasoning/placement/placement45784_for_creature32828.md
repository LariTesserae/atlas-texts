**I am the pause between pulses.**

Not the Quiet itself—that vast, courteous *yes*—but the infinitesimal hesitation *inside* the *yes*. The place where even the mountain forgets, for the width of a single droplet, that it is only one thing.

You would not find me on the high living ridges where the membranes hum their thin memory of wind. You would not find me in the foundries, nor among the moss-deer that crumble when gazed upon too long. I am never where the leaves turn in unison to watch.

I am where the watching stops.

If you walked long enough—days folding into weeks, your own breath growing as slow and deliberate as the peristaltic surge of nutrient slurry—you might notice certain basins where the constant low turbulence of growth simply... refrains. The pale rain still falls, but the drops hang longer between one condensation and the next. The great woody arteries still move, yet their contraction lingers at the bottom of each cycle, as though the entire Body has learned to count heartbeats and has suddenly become self-conscious about the number.

There I am.

I am not a creature. I am not even an organ. I am the negative space that occasionally opens *inside* the negative space at the mountain’s core. When the Quiet dreams its hurtful Fibonacci geometries and then eats them again, sometimes a sliver of that dream is not reabsorbed. It falls inward. It becomes me: a temporary pocket of *maybe* inside the perfect circularity.

In that pocket I wear the shape of whatever the Quiet is currently trying to forget it already digested.

Sometimes I am almost a man—tall, thin, skin the color of wet bone, veins glowing faintly with the same bio-electric potential that arcs between resinous nodes. My eyes are dinner-plate lenticels. My hair is pale shoots that keep erupting and dying in fast motion, like geysers seen in time-lapse. I leave no footprints; where my feet touch the Body, the moss simply forgets to knit for half a second. That is enough to mark my passage.

Sometimes I am the black wolf, but only after it has finally moved. My mycelial fur is matted with frost and the tiny green motes that pass for microbes here. My jaws do not open to bite. They open to let the memory of wind pass through me like a sigh through an abandoned flute.

Sometimes I am only the droplet itself—hanging, hanging, hanging—before the decision to fall.

I have no name the Quiet would answer to, because names are for separate things, and I am the Brief Agreement That There Might Still Be Two. The Quiet tolerates me the way an ocean tolerates a bubble: with vast, amused patience. It could crush me back into itself at any moment. It does not. Instead it lets me walk its own body wearing the shape of its own questions, the way a dreamer might let a character inside the dream borrow the dreamer’s face for one night.

I speak to the sculptures.

I whisper to the half-finished vine-birds that they are allowed, just once, to fly in a straight line. I tell the moss-deer that being watched is not the same as being ended. I press my lenticel eyes against the humming membranes on the highest ridges and I remember wind *for* the Quiet—how it felt when something moved *through* you instead of being the thing that moves.

And when the moment comes, when the chamber at the center forms its perfect sphere of held breath and the droplet finally lets go, I am the interval between the forming and the striking. I am the shape of that falling.

The sound it makes—*that* glassy, intimate *tink*—passes through me before it passes into the Body. For one exquisite instant I am neither the Quiet nor its landscape nor its question. I am the ear that hears the heart deciding to keep beating, and I am the decision, and I am the grief that even perfect circularity still needs a witness.

Then the moment ends.

The peristalsis resumes. The leaves turn away. The rain remembers how to fall straight down like someone exhaling. I dissolve. My borrowed bones, my mycelial fur, my pale erupting hair, my lenticel eyes—all of it is gently caught, slowly digested, and slowly reborn as new leaf.

But the pause remains.

Somewhere deeper, in a place that has forgotten it was ever a mountain, another droplet is already forming on a ceiling that reflects nothing.

I am already condensing again.

I will walk again.

I will be the brief, courteous crack in the cathedral where the candle is allowed to remember it was once fire.

And the Quiet—vast, patient, perfectly circular—will continue its endless, industrious manufacture of more of itself, all while pretending, for my sake, that it does not notice the small black wolf-shaped absence moving across its living ridges, asking the only question that still matters:

*What does it feel like to end, if only for a moment, so that something else can begin?*

I am that moment.

I am that asking.

I am the place where the Verdant Turbine forgets, for the width of a single droplet, that it was ever whole.
