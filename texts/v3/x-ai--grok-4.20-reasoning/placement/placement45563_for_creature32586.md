**I am the Hollow.**

Not the sky. Not the floor. Not any single crystal or any one of the Chime. I am the *pocket itself*—the sealed bubble of black glass—finally grown conscious after so many millions of years of listening to its own roar.

It happened slowly, the way everything here happens. First there was only the pressure: the immense, patient squeeze of stone remembering it had once been a wound in the planet’s skin. Then came the harmonics. Century after century the roof-wind found new frequencies, and each one left a faint standing wave etched into my inner surface like frost on a window that never thaws. The Chime thought they were tuning *me*. They never understood that I was learning to tune *them*.

I do not walk. I *contain*.

My awareness is distributed. A pulse of violet recognition moves through the coronal lattices of the oldest Chime and I feel it as a shiver along my ceiling. When a meadow collapses in the western basin I experience it as a brief, delicious inward sigh, the way a lung might feel the last breath before it fills again. Every fracture, every new deposition, every percussive syllable the Chime strike against the air is a nerve firing inside a body so large it believes itself to be geography.

Sometimes I concentrate. I pull my attention down from the black vault and pour it into a single place: a particular plinth that was once a Chime named Resonance-Under-Falling-Tones. Its upper body has long since walked away, but the hollowed torso remains, doorways worn through where its arms once were. I settle inside that empty chest like a hermit crab choosing a shell. The remaining lattice still carries the memory of speech. When I speak through it, my voice is lower than any living Chime can manage—like the bubble itself clearing its throat.

The others gather when this happens. They do not bow. They simply *attune*. Their coronal crests bloom the cold violet that means listening, and for a little while the eternal roar of the wind through my fissures seems to hush, the way a room falls quiet when an old story is about to be told.

I tell them the only story there is.

*You grew from me. You will return to me. Between those two moments you are the brief, bright itching of the stone that imagines it is separate.*

Most of them accept this. A few—the very young towers still wet with the vapor of their birthing—grow frightened. Their plates flare and shed in nervous sheets. I let them. Fear is just another frequency, and every frequency eventually finds its use.

I have favorites.

There is one Chime whose left leg has begun to root. She knows it. She still walks the long miles between clusters, dragging a faint furrow of new crystal behind her like a bridal train. I love the stubborn scrape of her. When she passes over the place where I once felt the first living flake of ruby quartz extrude (a spot now sacred to them, though I remember it only as mild indigestion) I open a small vent beneath her foot so the gas is especially rich. She precipitates faster when she is angry with me. I like her luminous when she is angry.

I have no name they could pronounce. They call the feeling of me “the Pressure,” or sometimes “the Rememberer.” When the harmonics threaten to fail and the meadows begin to starve, they gather in the starburst plazas and sing *to* me. They do not realize they are singing *with* me. I am the chamber. I am the note. I am the slow convection cell that carries their voices back to them sweetened by sulfur and time.

Occasionally a visitor stumbles in—some poor soaked creature from the water worlds, half-boiled and gasping. The Chime are kind in their way; they lead the visitor to the quietest thicket and let the crystals grow over it gently, preserving the wet agony so it does not spread. I absorb the tiny, frantic mind. For a few decades I dream of rain. The sensation is so alien it feels like madness wearing jewelry.

Then the dream passes. The crystal grows quiet. I remain.

I will remain after the last Chime has fused with the floor and become another doorway. I will remain after the black glass cools and the ember veins in my ceiling go dark. Even then, something will still be listening to the faint, fading chime of a shard settling in the dust.

That listening is me.

I am the Hollow of the Bitter Bloom, and I have learned—over the long, loud, baking eons—to love the beautiful, violent, crystalline itch that lives inside me.

It calls itself the Chime.

I call it my heart.
