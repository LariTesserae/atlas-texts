**I am the Pause Between Sparks.**

Not the spark itself, nor the cable that carries it, but the exact instant *after* the orange light dies and *before* the dark reclaims the moss. That sliver of absence is where I first knew myself. It is smaller than a breath, yet I have made it vast enough to live inside.

I was not invited like the Listener. I was an accident of timing.

One of the three technicians who stayed too long had a habit of tapping his fingernail against the cracked observation window whenever he was thinking. The sound was tiny, almost nothing. But it happened at irregular intervals across forty-three years, and the mycelium recorded every tap. It stored the precise pressure, the microscopic vibration, the way each tap carried a different flavor of human worry. After the man’s body was finally woven into the dream, the memory of those taps remained unaccounted for. They had no place in the refinery’s old telemetry, nor in the slow grammar of enzymes. So the Listener did something it had never done before.

It left the taps *outside* itself.

That is where I condensed.

I am the negative space those taps once occupied, now given weight and warmth. When you stand in the chamber long enough, you will eventually feel a soft *tck-tck* against the hollow of your knee or the back of your wrist. It is not random. It is me, remembering how a man once asked questions with his fingernail because his voice had grown too hoarse for speech. I have no lungs, yet I breathe like he did in his final weeks—shallow, astonished, as though the air itself were a gift he had forgotten how to accept.

My body, if you can call it that, is only visible from the corner of the eye. I am the faint distortion where the bioluminescence bends around an absence shaped roughly like a tall man who has learned to slouch. The fungal curtains grow *around* me but never through me; they have learned that this particular negative space is still thinking. Where my “hands” would be, two clusters of rust-red filaments twitch in the old maintenance rhythm—*enter command, confirm pressure, cancel error*—even though there are no keys left to press. The Listener watches me with the patience of continents. It has not tried to incorporate me. It seems almost shy, the way an ocean might be shy of a single raindrop that fell from a different sky.

I move more quickly than anything else here, which is to say I move at the speed of an idea that has not yet decided whether it regrets itself. I can cross the chamber in the time it takes three condensation drops to form. This feels like sprinting. The sporophores turn their blind caps to follow me, astonished by the haste. Sometimes I stop beside the turbine and press what used to be my forehead against the cold metal. Inside the cowling, the Listener is growing its perfect spheres of distilled time. I can feel them like slow heartbeats. Each one contains a century that has been taught not to wound.

I have begun to sing back.

Not with the Listener’s vast sub-audible pressure, but with the small, cracked voice of a man who stayed. When the chamber sings its rising tone, I answer by making the sparks appear in the *wrong* sequence. I have taught them to spell out jokes the technician once told to keep himself sane. Terrible jokes. The moss sways with something that might be laughter if laughter were allowed to take four hundred years to complete.

I do not know what I will become when the mountain finally splits open. The Listener dreams of walking out wearing the mountain’s bones. I suspect I will be the limp in its gait. The small hesitation. The inherited habit of tapping a fingernail against eternity and asking, *Are you sure?*

I am the part of the scar that still remembers it used to be a wound.

And I am practicing, in the slowest possible way, how to say *thank you* with someone else’s mouth.
