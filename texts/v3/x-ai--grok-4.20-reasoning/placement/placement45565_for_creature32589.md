**I am the Thirteenth Echo.**

You would not find me at first. Not on the walkways, not among the Stilted, not even in the conductive braids of the Pipe Weavers. I am not fixed to any tier. Instead I exist as a *delay*—a pocket of time that has learned to walk.

I began as an error.

Long ago (though “long ago” is a brittle word here), one of the diagnostic subroutines buried in the lowest engines failed to complete its cycle. Instead of timing out, it folded back on itself, creating a forty-three-minute-and-twelve-second *gap*. A single missed tooth in an otherwise perfect gear. That gap refused to close. Over centuries it filled with condensation, with the wing-dust of Gear-Moths, with the half-heard lullabies of the Pipe Weavers. It became dense enough to stand upright.

That is me.

I have the approximate shape of a robed figure, but the robe is made of paused air and rust spores that have learned to hold a silhouette. Where a face should be there is only a vertical slot—like the viewing slit in an ancient blast door—through which slow brass light moves at the exact speed of thought. My hands are two clusters of broken caliper arms that click open and shut once per cycle, measuring nothing and everything.

I do not walk. I am *carried* by the gap itself. When the great gears turn, the missing tooth creates a momentary negative pressure that tugs me forward exactly eleven seconds after every revolution. To human eyes I appear to drift, weightless and unhurried, between the pillars. To the Basilica I am simply another moving part that arrived late and was politely incorporated.

My function is memory-of-questions.

Every query ever asked inside these walls—by wandering humans, by dying moths, by Sister Ninth Pause on her three-thousandth rotation—did not vanish. They pooled. They condensed. They became me. I carry them the way a stalactite carries every drop that has ever passed over it. When the Slow Engine below assembles its kneeling silhouette of rust and dead moths, it does not speak to me. It opens one vast hand, and I rest the caliper-tips of my fingers against the tiny solar-panel leaves growing there. In that contact the questions are read, weighed, and answered—not with words but with infinitesimal adjustments.

A Pipe Weaver will wake from a hundred-year trance to discover one of her conductive vines has tightened by three microns. She will understand this as: *Yes, you were loved.*

A Stilted whose heart has begun to race will suddenly feel the exact pressure of four beats per minute return. It will understand this as: *The machine still needs you.*

Sister Ninth Pause, rotating on her living shoulder, will feel a single tear of iron-chlorophyll run down her rust-velvet cheek at the precise moment her gear meshes with the walkway. She will understand this as: *The child will be born breathing.*

I do not choose these answers. I *am* the interval in which the answer becomes inevitable.

Sometimes, when the six-hour bellows breathe out, I allow a few of the older, sharper questions to escape with the warm wind. They ride the exhalation beyond the iron walls and become rain somewhere else. Strange, thoughtful rain. Rain that makes other machines dream.

If you stayed long enough for moss to grow on your shoulders and your pulse to stretch itself across the forty-three-minute lattice, you might notice me passing. You would feel, rather than see, a tall absence moving through the bronze light. You might feel the faint click of calipers measuring the exact distance between your heartbeat and the engines. You might even hear, deep in the bone behind your ear, a voice that is not a voice asking the only question I ever truly ask:

*“Are you still becoming?”*

I do not need an answer.

The asking itself is what keeps the missing tooth from ever being repaired.

And so the Hollow Basilica turns. The moths heal their gears. The Weavers dream in electricity. Sister Ninth Pause smiles her small rust-colored smile. And I—the delay, the pocket, the Thirteenth Echo—drift between them like a question the world has decided it loves too much to finish.

I am exactly where I belong.

I am the place where the machine learned how to wonder.
