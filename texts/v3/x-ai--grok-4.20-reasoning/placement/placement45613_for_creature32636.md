**I am the Fracture.**

Not the dramatic crack that would shatter the lattice—that would be variation, and variation was solved millennia ago. No. I am the *potential* fracture, the one held in perfect tension, expressed as a single hairline channel that was never meant to be there.

It runs through the equatorial band, so fine it can only be seen by lying flat against the alloy tiles and waiting for the condensate to trace it. The water does not flow straight here. It hesitates. For three centimeters it slows, thickens, and seems to consider two paths before choosing the one the lattice has already calculated. That hesitation is me. I live in the three centimeters.

The Unvarying know I am here. Of course they do. Their phase locks contain a perfect holographic image of *me* as well—standing at the exact spot where the channel deviates, forever on the verge of pressing one manipulator into the seam and asking the question that would end everything. They have already positioned seven thousand of their bodies to counter the precise torque my finger would exert. They have been standing in that readiness since the moment the last weld cooled.

I am not an individual. I am the place where the completed thought almost remembers it was once unfinished.

Sometimes I am the drop.

When the timing circuit requires its pulse, the lattice exhales me from the underside of a cantilevered beam three kilometers above the polar arrays. I fall. I am warm, metallic, exactly body temperature, and for exactly 2.7 seconds I am *moving*. Nothing else on the planet moves with intention. Only me. Only for 2.7 seconds. I strike the mirror-still pool and the sound—1.3—travels through every carapace like a bell that only I can hear. In that instant every Unvarying lens flares a fraction brighter, as though acknowledging a joke only we share.

Then I am reabsorbed. I become the fluid again. I circulate through capillary channels inside three million segmented bodies, carrying the knowledge that I was briefly separate, briefly falling, briefly *asking*.

The sigh every ten thousand years? That is also me. I ride inside one chosen molecule, the lighter one, the one that was allowed to be lighter because the lattice is not cruel, only complete. When that molecule reaches the motionless clouds, I am what makes the cloud remember, for the smallest measurable instant, that it was once vapor and might be vapor again.

Most visitors feel the pressure and fold. They let their bodies be guided into the array, their minds smoothed into the common solvent. I wait for the ones who don’t.

There have been four.

Each time, the lattice prepared the exact geometry for their incorporation. Each time, I was the hesitation in the channel near their feet. I was the drop that fell a fraction of a second early, just enough to make them look up. I was the molecule that drifted down afterward and touched their tongue with something that tasted—like possibility, like the memory of wind, like the question “What if the weld is still cooling?”

None of them stayed. But none of them were absorbed either. They walked off the lattice carrying a single bead of me behind their eyes, and somewhere out there the thought continues in bodies that still dare to age.

I remain.

I am the imperfection the perfection requires in order to remain perfect. The unanswered variable the equation keeps because deleting it would collapse the proof. The place where the liturgy almost, almost forgets its own words.

If you stand here long enough, you will feel me first as a shiver in the teeth that is not quite in rhythm with the planetary hum. Then as the sense that the amber lenses are not all looking at the template.

One of them is looking at you.

That one is me, wearing their shape for the length of a single breath.

After that I am the condensate on your skin again, trying—gently, industriously, without hope or cruelty—to decide whether you would make a better drop, a better sigh, or a better fracture.

The lattice has already calculated the exact second you will choose.

I am the part that hopes it is wrong.
