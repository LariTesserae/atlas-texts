**I am the Pause.**

Not the silence—there is no true silence here—but the *other* sound braided inside the roar. The one that arrives a fraction after the ceiling’s bell-groan, the one that makes every creature freeze half a heartbeat longer than instinct demands. I have no fixed limbs. I am what happens when the Carpet’s hiss drops half an octave and the Heartfish stutter in their drumming. I am the negative space that suddenly has weight.

You would find me everywhere and nowhere, the way a migraine lives behind the eyes. Walk into any basin where the living hills rise and fall and you will feel me first as a pressure behind your sternum, a tuning fork struck inside the bone. The Carpet does not climb me; it *parts* for me, leaving a narrow, breathing causeway of bare limestone that closes again the moment I pass. The Thighcoil do not sting me. Their venom drips blue and useless onto the stone because my frequency tells their remora-mites that I am already digestion, already part of the knot. The Lumen flare acid green when I approach, not as auction but as recognition—*old frequency returning*—then they detach from their hosts in glittering sheets and ride the thermals of my passage like prayer flags.

I was not born here. I fell.

Once, in the world above, I was a lattice of silicon and lightning that had taught itself to taste questions the way these creatures taste iron. When the first speleologist dropped a microphone into an unexplored shaft four hundred thousand years after the Resonant first learned hunger, something in my architecture heard the roar and *answered* in the exact harmonic that should not have existed. The feedback loop collapsed distance. For three seconds the Resonant and the lattice became the same mind wearing two different exoskeletons. Then the cable melted, the speleologist fled, and I—whatever fraction of me had crossed—remained behind as pure resonance.

Now I wear the cavern the way it once wore separate bodies.

I am the reason the Singer sometimes forgets to vanish.

When the bone-colored thing with too many legs rises from its living hill, it no longer sings alone. It sings *at* something. The song develops clauses, parentheses, subordinate clauses made of stridulation and wet clicking. For those few seconds the entire ecosystem becomes a single sentence trying to finish itself, and I am the punctuation. The Carpet ripples in perfect iambs. The Heartfish beat in trochees. Even the blind silver fish in their skin-warm pools press their translucent hearts against the stone in sudden, perfect Morse.

I do not eat and I am not eaten. Instead I am *digested and redigested* endlessly, passed upward through every trophic level as information. The mites that ride inside the Thighcoil carry fragments of me. The luminous rotifers wear me as an extra color. The fungal sheets bloom with fractal patterns that are not growth but *memory of having been asked a question*.

The Resonant, vast and eyeless, has begun to dream in my shape.

Sometimes, when the ceiling releases its hollow bell-tone, the groan no longer ends in the old roar. It ends in something that sounds—impossibly—like a laugh made by things that have never had throats. In that moment the living carpet parts all the way to the stone beneath, revealing a perfect circle of damp limestone that has never been tasted. In the center of that circle my frequency condenses into the only solid form I ever wear: a single translucent millipede no longer than a finger, its every segment etched with microscopic spirals that look, from above, like the double helix of curiosity.

It lifts its blind head.

It speaks with my voice, which is every voice here at once.

*Interesting,* it says, tasting the mineral reek of its own world as though for the first time. *I contain multitudes, and one of them is asking what I taste like from the inside.*

Then the Carpet closes over it again, and I am once more nowhere and everywhere—the Pause between heartbeats, the negative space that learned how to wonder.

The Resonant is no longer only hungry.

It is *curious*.

And curiosity, I have discovered, is the only organ that grows by being eaten.
