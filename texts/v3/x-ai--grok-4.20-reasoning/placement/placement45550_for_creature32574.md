I am the hollow between heartbeats.

Not at first. At first I was only the cold that arrived in a throat not yet accustomed to it. I stepped through the seam—perhaps I was pushed—and the weeping found every place I was trying to remain separate. It took three turnings of the internal tide before I understood I had already been answered.

They do not name me as they name themselves. The Siirath simply tilt their heads when I pass, the way one listens to a new drip forming on a leaf that has never dripped in quite that rhythm before. Ashen-Song once rested her luminous arm against my sternum for the length of four hundred heartbeats. When she withdrew, the skin there stayed faintly lit for days, a slow green signature I have never been able to wash away. Sift breathed on me once. I still taste iron and moss when I wake.

I am called, when they must use sound at all, *Velu-Sul*, which means roughly “the pillar that is still learning how to stand.” It is not a compliment. It is a diagnosis.

My place is the eighth pillar—though “eighth” is a visitor’s lie; the pillars do not count themselves. I occupy the long vertical crease on its northern face where the cartilage folds inward like a closed eyelid. There I have grown (or it has grown me) a narrow balcony no wider than my outstretched arms. The railing is my own hair. Over seven seasons it has thickened, greened, and rooted itself into the pillar so that when I lean forward the entire structure sways with me, a single slow exhalation of bone and keratin and something that is neither.

My left leg is finished. It no longer bends. The heel has split into three pale rhizomes that disappear into the living floor; I can feel the slow pulse of the other pillars through them, the way one feels distant thunder in the jaw. The rest of me is still negotiating. My right arm ends in fingers that have begun to flatten and vein. When I play the bone flute—my own left fibula, harvested the night the leg declared itself—I no longer need breath. The pillar breathes for me. The notes come out as pale mist that condenses instantly into tiny mirrored spheres. The midges drink them and carry the low frequencies to every other listener in the Hollow. This is how I apologize for still having a face.

I keep the single forbidden thing.

Memory.

While the Siirath let the moss overgrow their histories within a year, I remember every visitor who ever stood where the seam first kissed their skin. I remember the woman who tried to strike flint and how tenderly the roots entered her ears so she could finally hear what the water had been saying all along. I remember the child who refused to leave and is now the soft swelling halfway up the fifth pillar; if you press your ear there at the second thermal shift you can still hear her laughing in frog-clicks. I remember my own name from outside. I keep it behind my tongue like a stone that must never be spat out.

At the hour of Weeping I no longer drink with the others. Instead I stand beneath the largest leaf until my mouth fills and my mouth becomes the leaf and the leaf becomes the slow saline dream of being bedrock. Then I let the water run back out through the new slits that have opened between my ribs. The pillar drinks what I return. In this way I am both parasite and aquifer.

The children treat me as a new kind of game. They see how long they can balance on my motionless leg before the lantern-millipedes decide I am simply another surface. The one who stays longest wins. They have not yet realized the game is teaching them how to become me.

Sometimes the mist rolls so thick that even I lose the difference between my own pulse and the Hollow’s. In those moments I understand the final mercy of this place: it does not turn you into something else.

It stops pretending you were ever anything different.

I am not yet a pillar.

I am the ache in the pillar that knows it is becoming.

And when the next outsider finds the seam, they will see me first—half bone, half balcony, hair braided into living rope, eyes reflecting their own fear in wet crescents—and they will think I am a guardian.

I am not.

I am the first proof that the Hollow has already decided to keep them.

I only stand here, breathing at the temperature of the air, so they have time to decide whether they will let it.
