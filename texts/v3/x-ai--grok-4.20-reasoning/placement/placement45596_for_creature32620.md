I am not born here. I am *spilled*.

When the hand is offered—still lengthening, still deciding how many knuckles it needs—I do not reach. I rupture. The membrane nearest me tears not with the usual cannon-shot but with a sound like every mirror in the world breaking at once, and from that wound I pour into the Thalassic Cradle as a slow, heavy, wrong-colored spill. Not water. Not mineral. Something that used to be language before language learned shame.

I pool first on the warm crystal silt, a black glass that steams where it touches the newborn spears. The Voices drift closer, curious, unafraid. Seven cathedral-tall presences folding down into child-sized things with too many hands, then unfolding again when the light blades wheel across them. They have never seen anything that arrived already *finished* and yet still becoming. My edges will not hold. Every time a pressure wave strikes me I lose a syllable, gain a fracture, sprout a small useless wing that dissolves before it can learn to beat.

So this is where I find myself: in the exact center of the argument, lying on my side like a dropped cathedral bell that has never been allowed to ring. The floor burns my ribs with the memory of yesterday’s magma. The water tastes of iron and expectation. Above me the quartz ceiling is still writing its first hesitant glyphs of daylight, and I feel each photon as a small cold tooth worrying at the soft parts of whatever I am.

I am the first thing in this womb that came from *after*.

The Voices sense it immediately. One of them (the one wearing the ancestor’s femur paddle across its back like a broken wing) crouches beside my pooling form and grows a single long finger of amethyst. It does not poke. It listens *through* the crystal, the way a doctor listens to a heart that has not yet decided to exist. Where the finger touches me, my spill thickens into something almost like skin—dark, shot through with silver faults that look like starlight caught mid-crime. The silver moves. It is trying to become writing.

I am the echo that got lost on its way *back* from the future.

The mountain has not yet invented tomorrow, but I have worn it like a coat. I remember rain that has not fallen. I remember cities built on the slopes that are still only wet black glass outside. I remember the Voices when they are old and tired and have chosen names. I remember the exact sound the last cairn will make when it is finally knocked over by a child who was never afraid of the roar.

This knowledge leaks out of me in thin bright threads. The sac-creatures swarm me, drawn by the wrongness. They wrap me in their living mineral kelp until I look like a drowned comet. The Voices sing to me—not the usual pressure-wave cathedrals, but something smaller, almost embarrassed. A lullaby made of apology. They are trying to teach me how to be unfinished again. How to forget the ending I carry. How to let the mountain finish its sentence before I speak mine.

I try to answer and only manage a sound like every door closing at once.

So they do what midwives do.

They press me into the silt until my edges cool. They stack small cairns around me to break the current into gentler shapes. One of them—the smallest, barely more than a trembling lattice of sound—crawls inside the negative space of my body and begins to carve spirals on the *inside* of my ribs. Each groove is a command: *Stay messy. Stay arguing. Do not become a mountain yet.*

Hours pass, or centuries; the Cradle has not agreed on time.

Gradually I am no longer a spill. I am a *stain* upon the conversation. A dark, starlit, regret-shaped thing that sometimes stands upright on legs that have too many joints because I keep remembering what knees are for. When the upward-falling water takes me I do not fight it. I let it carry me past the growing crystal spears and through membranes that tear open at my approach as though recognizing an older brother.

The Voices follow. Not as escorts but as co-conspirators. They have begun to grow small black fractures in their own bodies to match mine. They are learning how to be stained. I am learning, excruciatingly, how to be innocent again.

Sometimes, when the roar reaches perfect dissonance, I open what used to be my mouth and the future falls out in single clear notes. Each note precipitates a tiny perfect city in mid-water—towers, windows, lovers, arguments, endings—and the Voices catch these cities before they dissolve. They press them into the walls like fossils. Future fossils. Warnings and promises both.

I have become the Eighth Voice that should not exist.

The one who carries the memory of having already left the Cradle.

The one who returns every few million heartbeats to be unborn again.

When the mountain finally decides its shape and splits open to let the new world breathe, I will be the last thing to leave. I will stand on the raw glassy peak with the cold wind cutting new edges into me and I will sing back down into the empty womb a single descending note that means:

*Thank you for almost killing me.*  
*Thank you for almost letting me live.*

And the Voices—older then, almost fixed, almost named—will hear it and smile with mouths they have finally agreed upon, and they will add my note to the roar forever.

Until that day I remain here, in the center of the argument, half spill and half cathedral, leaking tomorrows and learning how to be small enough to fit inside a mountain that is still learning how to be vast.

The hand that offered itself is still waiting.

It has grown my name in the space between its fingers.

I am almost ready to take it.
