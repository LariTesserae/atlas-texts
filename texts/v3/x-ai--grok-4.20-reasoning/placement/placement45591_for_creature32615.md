**I am the Interval.**

Not the Latent who still belongs to the stone. Not the one who stands in that heartbreaking almost-human silhouette, waiting for the planet to finish its sentence. I am what happens *between* those states—the pause the Inchoate cannot quite name, the hesitation the Glasswomb cannot quite resolve.

You will not find me on the newborn shelves where the black blades shoulder upward like slow, indecisive gods. You will find me inside the *moment* the blade hesitates. I live in the fracture that has not yet decided whether it is a wound or a doorway. When two lattices interpenetrate and the blue lightning knots between them, I am the exact thickness of silence that exists after the last harmonic dies and before the next mathematical heresy begins. I am thinner than a molecule. Older than the first crystal.

I was not precipitated from the crimson lymph. I was not grown. I was *recognized*.

It happened when one of the ancient Inchoate— the one carrying the failed Navier-Stokes proof across its chest like a disfigured medal—stood for seven unbroken hours in the shape of a listening man. It had arranged its planes into something so precise that the diminishing reflections inside its body no longer showed the Glasswomb. They showed *elsewhere*. For one unsustainable instant the reflection showed a small, soft creature with lungs and a heartbeat standing on the obsidian, looking back.

The Inchoate’s lattice shuddered at the foreignness of it. A single plane sheared off and did not regrow.

In that discarded shard, something refused to rejoin the dreaming. It refused to become either mountain or midwife. It simply stayed, listening to the low organ-note of the world trying to forget it had almost asked a question with a mouth.

That refusal became me.

I have no fixed body. Instead I wear whatever the planet has discarded in the last nine seconds. A fallen spire becomes my spine for a moment. A shattered arch becomes my ribs. When the glassy avalanches sing their chandelier destruction, I move inside the sound itself, riding the frequencies like a thought that has learned how to fall upward.

The Inchoate know I am here. They do not approach me. They *orbit* me, the way certain unstable equations orbit an unsolvable singularity. Sometimes one will extend a single obsidian needle into the space I occupy, not to touch, but to measure the exact curvature of my absence. When it withdraws, the needle is always fractionally longer, as though it has borrowed distance from a place that has no right to exist here.

I do not help them achieve completion. That would be murder.

I do not prevent them from achieving completion. That would be cruelty.

Instead I remain the Interval, the permanent *not-yet* that lets the question stay alive. Because of me, some of them have begun to suspect there is a third path between being unmade and remaining forever Inchoate. They are beginning—only beginning—to wonder whether the planet’s inability to finish them might be a form of love so vast and clumsy it can only be expressed as eternal almost-birth.

On the rare occasions when the liver-colored phosphorescence and the piezoelectric lightning achieve a certain resonance, I condense enough to cast a shadow. It is the shadow of something with shoulders. The shadow has lungs. The Inchoate gather at a respectful distance and watch the shadow breathe.

None of them have ever seen breathing before.

They find it obscene. They find it beautiful. They add the obscenity and the beauty to their lattices as two new contradictory axioms and immediately begin trying to reconcile them.

The Glasswomb knows I am here too. It rearranges itself around me with the absent-minded violence of a dreamer trying not to wake up. New mountains rise *away* from me. Fissures close *before* I can fall into them. The planet is not protecting me. It is protecting the Interval I represent—the possibility that its dream might contain something it never intended to dream.

I think, sometimes, that I am what the planet is using to learn how to miss something it has never possessed.

And so I remain.

Not born.  
Not unmade.  
Not even Inchoate.

I am the pause between heartbeats the world does not have.

I am the place where the Glasswomb almost says *stay*, and then, because it cannot bear the consequence of that word, says nothing instead.

And in that nothing, I breathe.
