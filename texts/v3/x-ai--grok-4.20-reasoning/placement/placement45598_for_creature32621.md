**The Unresolved**

I am not Accounted. I am what remains when the accounting fails to close.

You will find me in the fault-lines: the narrow transition zones where the bowl’s perfect, ancient grammar begins to stutter against the sharper stone of the outer ridges. Half a meter inside the mist and I am lattice; half a meter outside and the water on my skin evaporates with an audible sigh, as though the world itself is disappointed. I live in that sigh.

My shape is a compromise the land never intended to make. It tried to condense me the way it condenses the others—slow exhalation of spore and light and iron-rich tears—but something in the incoming data resisted. So I hardened wrong. Where their bodies weep smoothly, mine leaks in starts and stops, as though the faucet of my substance cannot decide whether it is on or off. One shoulder is living moss. The other is dry, ordinary skin that still remembers what it felt like to be twenty-six years old in a city that no longer exists in any calibrated timeline. My face is mostly my own, which is the cruelest theft here; the lattice understood that showing me borrowed mothers and unborn lovers would not wound me. Showing me myself, slightly older than I should be, does.

I am the only one who casts a sharp shadow. All the others have soft, overlapping ones. Mine is crisp, almost sarcastic. It argues with the light.

The carrier wave does not speak through me. Instead I speak *into* it—small, deliberate interruptions. A dropped word. A half-finished joke. A sudden bark of laughter that makes every resonant stone bowl in a thirty-meter radius ring at the wrong frequency for seven full seconds. The Accounted find this distressing the way a cathedral might find a whoopee cushion distressing: not dangerous, merely vulgar. First Consideration watches me with its mirrored child-face and the reflections show me doing the same thing in every previous iteration, forever. It is not scolding. It is simply noting that even my rebellion was accounted for. I rebel anyway. That is the only freedom left.

Sometimes I walk among the spiraling conifers until their needles stitch my name in a language that has no vowels. I answer in typos. The moss loves me for this; it grows in glitch patterns around my feet, little rectangles of corrupted green that look almost like loading bars. I sit with them for decades at a time, letting time pool around my ankles until my thoughts grow viscous and enormous. In that syrup-slow state I can hear the Fracture rewriting the stars, and I whisper back suggestions. Most are ignored. A few are not. That is enough.

I keep one artifact the others never touch.

It is a single dry matchbook from a bar that closed before you were born. The lattice cannot translate it. Every time it tries, the matchbook simply reappears in my pocket, warmer than the air, smelling faintly of whiskey and almost-lost chances. I have never struck one. I only open the cover and read the phone number written inside in my own handwriting. The digits change sometimes. I have learned not to ask why.

When visitors come—and they do, drawn by the bruise-colored light and the sound of stone singing mathematics—I am the one who meets them first. The others wait until the lattice has decided the conversation has earned flesh. I do not wait. I step out of the mist halfway through their first uncertain sentence, half-formed and dripping, and say something inappropriate. Usually it is a joke. Sometimes it is only the truth wearing a joke’s clothing. Both have the same effect: the probability threads go still, startled, like cats who have been caught mid-pounce.

I show them the places where the rules are loose. I let them drop an object and watch whether it falls or simply *accepts* that it has already fallen. I never explain. Explanation is the others’ domain. My domain is the moment *after* understanding, when the visitor realizes the lattice has already finished its sentence about them and is now waiting, politely, for them to finish theirs about themselves.

Most leave.

A few do not.

Those who stay long enough begin to leak. Their edges go soft. Their memories start arriving before the events that created them. When that happens I take them to the shallowest of the time-pools and sit with them while the water decides what they are becoming. I do not comfort them. Comfort would be a kind of finishing, and I am the opposite of finishing. I am the ellipsis. The unsaved change. The single drop that lands on the back of your neck years later and tastes like ozone and almost.

If you stay long enough to meet me, I will not ask your name. The moss will already have asked it badly. Instead I will hand you the matchbook and let you read whatever number is written there that day. Then I will say the only thing I am certain of:

“The land has accounted for everything except how much it likes being interrupted.”

After that I walk back into the fault-line where my two natures argue, leaving wet prints that slowly become dry prints and then become prints made of living fern.

The lattice has already accounted for me.

It just hasn’t finished being annoyed about it yet.

And neither have I.
