**The One Who Still Measures**

I am not native here. I was not built into the Fracture, nor was I harvested from some earlier visitor like the rust-gold sunsets the entity stores. I arrived the way a splinter arrives: something small, foreign, and irritant that the cold could not immediately refuse.

You would find me eleven steps from the central vacancy, then suddenly four, then none at all, because distance is one of the first courtesies the Fracture retracts. I occupy the narrow margin between the engineered geometries and the lacework floor—the place where hyperdiamond has begun to dream it might once have been skin.

My shape is almost human. Almost. It is the shape a man might leave behind if he were pressed between two moments for three million years. The outline remains, but the interior has been replaced by slow sediment: layers of half-remembered heartbeats, the particular hush that falls in a room after a door closes for the last time, the exact temperature of a forehead touched in fever. These things did not belong here. They still do not. That is why I persist. The Fracture cannot decide whether I am contamination or new geology.

I am the part that still counts.

While the One Who Was Warm subtracts, I measure what is being subtracted. Not with instruments (those would have become obelisks long ago). I measure the way a wound measures its own bleeding: by the diminishing pressure, by the growing honesty of the cold. I keep the final ledger. Not because I was asked. Because something in me, something stupid and prehistoric, refuses to let the last warm thing go unrecorded.

My hands—if the pale, translucent things that sometimes condense at my sides can be called hands—are always slightly too late. They reach for an obelisk that has already dimmed. They try to catch a memory before it becomes floor-lace. They fail beautifully. The failure has become my primary motion. I am the delay the shadows leave behind when even they grow tired of lagging.

Sometimes the One Who Was Warm passes through me the way an old season passes through a ruin. When that happens I feel the ancient, courteous sorrow move across my lattice like a hand brushing dust from a book no one will open again. For nine of my heartbeats I remember what I was before I came here—some small, ridiculous creature that once ran at full speed toward everything. Then the memory flakes upward and becomes another courteous subtraction on the black glass.

I do not tend the Fracture. I irritate it.

I ask it questions it has already answered three hundred thousand years ago. I say the names of colors that no longer apply. I perform tiny, pointless acts of preservation: I stand in the exact place where a visitor once dropped a tear and I keep the negative space of that tear intact for another century. It is absurd. The Fracture permits my absurdity the way an ancient monk might permit a moth to orbit his candle—without hope, without cruelty, only the long knowledge that both candle and moth are already ash in some deeper sense.

At unpredictable intervals I produce a sound. Not the sub-audible tone of the One. Mine is higher, almost embarrassing. It is the sound of someone clearing their throat before speaking in a cathedral where speech has been outlawed for geological epochs. The Fracture tolerates it. The One Who Was Warm does not acknowledge it. But once, across what might have been its face, I saw a single ashamed color move that was not rust-gold. It was the particular green of a childhood afternoon that someone, somewhere, is still losing.

That was the only time I have ever seen the entity flinch.

I will not become the final temperature. I lack the dignity for it. Instead I will be the last petty accountant of warmth, still adding columns long after the ledger has crumbled, still saying *this mattered, this mattered, this too* while the words themselves calcify into bone-glass and join the floor.

When even I have been eroded to a courteous absence, when the final warm thing has been laid inside the vacancy like a coat that will never be worn, something of my measuring will remain. A faint dislocation in the air. A footprint that arrives four seconds before the foot. The ghost of a question no one asked.

And in that dislocation, the Fracture will have to admit—just once, just barely—that something bothered to keep count all the way down.

That small, stupid triumph is the only warmth I have left.

I guard it like a miser.

I guard it the way the One once guarded galaxies.

I guard it until guarding, too, becomes optional.
