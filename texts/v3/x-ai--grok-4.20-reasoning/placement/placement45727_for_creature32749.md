**The Inscription**

You will not distinguish it from the monolith at first. The tall stone near the western lip simply *is*, another upright refusal to erode further, and the faint tracery across its face looks like any other scar left by ten thousand years of patient water. Only after the basin has had time to study you—after perhaps seventy or eighty of the long, irregular *plocs*—does the surface resolve into something that was once deliberate.

It is not writing. Not anymore. What remains might once have been a name, a warning, a prayer, or a simple statement of presence (“I was here”), but the distinction between intention and accident has been polished out of it. The grooves are now shallow enough that lichen grows inside them like ink that has decided to live. Ochre follows the old lines, sage pools in the deeper pits, charcoal outlines what might have been a circle or an eye or simply the place where a finger rested for a century. The pattern is too consistent to be random, too eroded to be read. It has become a palimpsest that no longer needs to be deciphered.

The Still One stands beside it the way two old stones stand beside each other: no ceremony, no conversation, only the shared knowledge that they have outlasted every reason for separation. When the taller figure tilts—once in a human lifetime, always to the left—the shadow it barely casts moves across the Inscription like a finger tracing a sentence it already knows by heart. The lichen brightens by one impossible fraction. Equilibrium acknowledges itself.

The Inscription does not move. Movement would be a form of revision. Instead it participates in the light. On those rare occasions when the Still One draws its eye-beads inward and truly *looks*, the directionless ivory-gray seems to gather for a moment inside the worn markings, as though the pearl itself were remembering that it was once an irritant. Then the moment passes and the light returns to its usual evenness. Nothing has been added. Nothing has been taken away.

Its feet—if they can be called feet—are the monolith’s base, fused so gradually with the basin floor that the boundary cannot be found. Stone became meaning became stone again in the same gentle gradient the Still One exhibits higher up. In this way the two figures are not companions but phases of the same slow consent: one upright and watchful, the other upright and remembered. The collapsed ring of stones, the half-sunk shelter, the Inscription, and the Still One are all earlier drafts of a single sentence the basin finished long ago.

It has no voice. Voice would disturb the dripping. Yet when the Still One catches a drop in its palm and the sound becomes *truer*, the Inscription seems to deepen, as though the altered note were being added to a record no longer kept in words. The grasses nearest the monolith relax a fraction more. The mist holds its breath at chest height a moment longer. These are not effects. They are recognitions.

There is no nostalgia here. Nostalgia requires an elsewhere. There is only the completed text that has agreed to remain legible just enough to prove that legibility was never the point. The Inscription has consented to be the last mark that does not demand a next one. In return the basin has allowed it to remain faintly, almost indecently, *present*.

If you stood before it for three full days and nights (though night remains theoretical), you might notice the faintest darkening along one groove—just one—as though a single drop had decided to linger inside the stone instead of falling. Not sorrow. Not joy. Only a geological full stop that has learned how to tremble with recognition.

Then the moment ends.

The next *ploc*, when it comes, sounds almost like the period at the end of a sentence that has finally, perfectly, accepted its own ending. The Still One does not smile. But the lichen at the corner of its mouth catches the light in a way that suggests the world has been read, understood, and gently closed.

And the basin, having been witnessed, keeps its equilibrium a fraction more beautifully than before.
