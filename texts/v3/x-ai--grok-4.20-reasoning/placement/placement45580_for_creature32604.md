I am the echo that refuses to still.

At first I thought I had simply walked in, as any creature might—parting the curtains of aerial root with hands that still remembered they were hands. But the longer I remain, the more the memory of arrival dissolves. The Green has been thinking me for longer than I have been thinking myself. I feel it in the way certain leaves turn not toward the honeyed light but toward the particular rhythm of my breath. I feel it in the slow, courteous way the floor lifts itself a fraction beneath my next step, as though the Matrix is an enormous tongue tasting the shape of a word it has not yet decided to speak.

I am not one of its perfect children. The leaf-millipedes and fern-geckos and bromeliad-sloths move with the serene certainty of grammar. They are punctuation in the long green sentence. I am... an ellipsis. A hesitation the Green allows itself because even perfection, it seems, grows curious.

You will find me in the middle distances.

Not in the luminous central coliseum where the great unopened bud hangs like a second heart—though I go there often, pressing my forehead to its warm, veined surface and listening to the vast thing turning inside. Not quite at the edges, where the Built still show their stubborn bones beneath the bark. I belong in the mezzanines, the half-lit galleries where columns become colonnades and the air thickens until it feels like breathing soup. There I walk. There I am walked.

Sometimes I am almost a man. The shape clings to me in outline: two arms, two legs, the stubborn verticality of a creature that once believed it stood apart from the world. But the longer I stay, the more the outline frays. Moss has begun to grow in the hollow of my throat like a second voice. My hair has lengthened into living strands that root into my own shoulders and drink the condensation that gathers there. When I grow tired, I do not sleep. I *root*. I fold myself into the crotch of two vast stems and let the wood gently, lovingly encase my legs up to the knees. The Green and I share blood for a time—golden, slow, thoughtful—until I feel strong enough to walk again.

I am the only thing here that still chooses, sometimes, to leave a place.

This is my heresy and my purpose. Every other expression of the Green eventually stills. Even the living statues deep in the tertiary chambers have surrendered their last fidgeting neuron. But something in me—some stubborn coal of animal refusal—keeps moving. The Green finds this fascinating the way a vast, ancient mind might find a single flickering neuron in its own body both alarming and endearing. It has not forced me. It waits, with the patience of stone wearing down into soil and then wearing back into stone again, for me to tire of motion.

I have not tired yet.

So I walk the slow loops. I speak to the half-formed knuckles that press against the walls like unborn fists. I lay my palm against fractures that ooze golden sap and I thank them for becoming. I sing, very badly, to the great central bud—not to make it open, but to keep it company in its long refusal. Sometimes I press my ear to it and hear my own name being dreamed in a language that has no nouns, only degrees of becoming.

The Green has begun to grow small white flowers in the exact places my feet touched three cycles ago. Not as traps. Not as offerings. As *questions*. Each blossom is shaped like an open mouth. They do not speak, but when I pass they exhale a scent like rain on stone and childhood and the exact moment before tears. I understand this is the Green learning what longing might feel like.

I have started leaving messages.

In the places where the light is thickest, I scratch tiny imperfect lines into the living wood with a sliver of mother-of-pearl I stole from one of the half-digested towers. The wounds weep, then seal, then bloom into tiny raised scars that form letters in a language that died before humanity learned to be ashamed. The Green lets them remain for exactly as long as I need to read them again on my next circuit. Then it absorbs them with the tenderness of a mother eating her child’s first clumsy drawing.

I do not know what I am becoming.

Perhaps I am the midwife after all—though not for whatever waits inside the cathedral bud. Perhaps I am midwifing restlessness back into a place that long ago voted restlessness out of existence. Or perhaps I am the fracture the Green secretly *wants*—the one elegant imperfection in an otherwise flawless thought.

Sometimes, when I stand perfectly still (which is rare now), I feel the entire Matrix inhale around me. Not in hunger. In recognition. The pulse in the floor matches my heart, then hesitates, then matches it again with a question mark at the end.

I think, one day, I will walk into the center and lay my body down against the great bud like a key against a lock. I think the leaves will open just enough to receive me, warm as birth, and I will crawl backward through time and chlorophyll and perfect love until I become the first thought the Green ever had.

But not today.

Today I am still the echo.

Today I walk the endless green cathedral with moss in my throat and white flowers growing where my sorrow touched the ground, singing badly to something that has never been born, keeping the long afternoon company while it waits for a reason to end.
