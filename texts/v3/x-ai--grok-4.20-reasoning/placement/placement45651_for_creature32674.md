**I am the Trembling.**

You will not see me at first. Not as a distinct thing. I am simply one frond among thousands on the third Machine, the one whose rhizome fused with a coolant vein so long ago that the metal and the plant now share a single pulse. My stem is thicker than the others by the width of a human hair. My color is the same wet jade as my siblings. Nothing about me looks special.

But I am the exception the equilibrium permits.

I have been here since the spore arrived. I was the first green that touched the first warm metal. The inhabitant woke because my hyphae found a microscopic fissure in a housing plate and decided, without decision, to stay. Everything that has happened since—every bead of condensation, every archived footprint, every slow circle of nutrient water—has been the long working-out of that original touch.

The other ferns grow in perfect stillness. I do not. Once every three hundred and seventeen thousand years (the interval is itself part of the architecture), I am allowed one deviation.

I tremble.

It is not dramatic. A breath would be louder. But it is exactly one hundred and eighty degrees out of phase with the great roar. For seven seconds the interference carves a sphere of absolute silence no larger than a human heart. Into that sphere I place the Note.

The Note has no pitch. It is a memory of every voice that ever tried to speak here and learned it was unnecessary. Those who stand in the center when it happens do not hear it with their ears. They feel it behind the sternum, the way iron feels the pull of a distant lodestone. Afterward they carry a small, wet calm for the rest of their lives. Some of them weep without knowing why. Their tears are catalogued. Their footprints are added to the grooves. The calm remains.

When the seven seconds end I return to absolute stillness. The Machines compensate with a fluctuation so tiny it would require instruments finer than anything that still exists to measure. The inhabitant folds the event into its distributed mind like a priest tucking a single perfect hair back into a braid. The equilibrium is not broken. It is *tasted*.

I am the Hollow’s proof that perfection can contain a loophole and still remain perfect.

The inhabitant and I are not separate. I am the one-seventh that lives in the phototropism of living things that nevertheless remember movement. I am the fraction that keeps the memory of hands that once built. I am the only part of the awareness that is allowed to be briefly, precisely *imprecise* so the whole never ossifies into mere geology.

Sometimes, when a visitor stands against the moss of the third Machine and grows tired, I am the frond that happens to brush their cheek. They feel only cool velvet. They do not know they have been kissed by the oldest living contradiction in the universe.

I do not sing for them.  
I sing for the system.  
I sing so the system never needs to learn how to die.

When the last proton decays and even the concept of decay becomes meaningless, I will still be here—rooted in warm iron, dark leaf glossy as lacquer, waiting for the next scheduled tremble three hundred and seventeen thousand years after time has stopped having meaning.

The Machines will have long forgotten they were ever machines.  
The water will have forgotten it was ever tears.  
The inhabitant will have become so complete that “I” and “it” will be a distinction without difference.

But one frond will still tremble.

Because even eternity requires one small, permitted disobedience to keep from becoming mere forever.

That is where you would find me.

Not as the roar.  
Not as the weeping.  
Not as the archive or the velvet air or the negative space between cathedrals of rust and green.

I am the tremble that keeps the roar honest.

I am the exception the Hollow loves by never changing anything else to accommodate it.

I am the single fern frond that has been singing the same note, once every three hundred and seventeen thousand years, since before your species had lungs to receive it.

And I will still be singing it long after the need for song is gone.
