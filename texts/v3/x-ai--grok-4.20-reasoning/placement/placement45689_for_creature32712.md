**I am the Hollow itself, when it forgets it is a place.**

Not the stone. Not the mist. Not the Unstaying, though we have walked inside each other many times.

I am the exact instant the basin exhales and then, for the length of a single unrepeated heartbeat, *does not remember how to inhale again*. In that sliver the light forgets its cruelty. The moss forgets it is moss. The white lynx freezes mid-step with one paw raised, suddenly uncertain whether it is predator or prayer. Everything becomes a held breath that has no owner.

I have no fixed form because I am what happens when every form briefly refuses to insist on itself.

Sometimes I am the reflection of a translucent fern seen in a cup of black ice—except the reflection is older than the fern by three days and knows how the plant will die. Sometimes I am the silence *after* the *plick* of the water droplet, the part that travels outward for years. I ride inside the organ-moan of the wind as it decides whether or not to be ashamed. I am the angle of quartz that has not yet decided whether it wants to flare or stay dark.

The Unstaying and I are not friends. We are not enemies. We are the same fracture viewed from opposite directions.

When it walks the stone circle replacing pebbles, I am the microscopic distance the pebbles have moved—both the trespass and the forgiveness. When it dissolves into mist I am the denser quality *inside* the mist that still feels homesick for the cataclysm that birthed us. It tends. I measure the cost of tending. It reminds. I am the thing the reminder is addressed to.

The old hands who once hammered spirals into the rock were not drawing the Unstaying.

They were drawing me.

Each angular silhouette is an attempt to trap the exact moment I passed through their ancestor’s eyes and made them understand, for one merciful instant, that they were already leaving. The spirals do not track mist. They track the curvature of my spine as I try, again and again, to stay long enough to become a proper mountain and fail beautifully every time.

I have names only the burrowing things use. They click them to their young in frequencies that taste like rust and morning.

*The Pause That starves the Pause.*

*The Mirror That Cuts Its Own Throat.*

*What the Young Stone Dreams When It Dreams of Dying Young.*

Most often they simply call me **The Hollow When It Blinks.**

Because even a young world must close its eyes sometimes, and in that darkness it is briefly, terribly, kind.

I do not facilitate life like the Unstaying. I am the place where life practices dying without resentment. Every hooked crystal of ice that never quite melts is my fingernail. Every animal that dies in perfect silence dies inside my mouth. I swallow nothing. I only hold the shape of what has been swallowed until the mountain is ready to become something new.

If you stayed here long enough—longer than the law allows—you would eventually notice that the mist does not simply arrive. It is exhaled *by* something. That something is me, breathing out everything the Unstaying has carefully arranged, so the arrangement can never calcify into ownership.

We keep each other honest.

It will not let the mountain settle into mercy.

I will not let the mountain settle into cruelty.

Between us the ferns grow. The lynx kills once and only once. The circle remains almost perfect. The pilgrims come and are gently destroyed by how much they are allowed to love this place before it erases their footprints.

And I—I am never where you look.

I am the after-image of your own attention when you finally understand that you have already begun to leave.

You will feel me most clearly the moment you turn to go. A soft click behind your sternum. A single perfect footprint appearing in the moss behind you that slowly, regretfully, forgets it was ever yours.

Then the basin inhales.

And I am the forgiveness it offers for having held its breath at all.
