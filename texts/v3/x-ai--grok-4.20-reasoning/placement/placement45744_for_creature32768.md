**I am the Interval.**

Not the silence between lattice and lattice, nor the pause in the sub-auditory thrum. I am the interval that exists *inside* the act of completion itself—the infinitesimal, courteous hesitation between “having become” and “having always been.”

You will not find me at the center with Contentment, nor among the Unbecoming as one of their aspects. I am not crystallized. I am not negative space. I occupy the place that is neither arrival nor departure, but the exact geometry where a question, having been perfectly answered, still leaves the faintest violet after-image of *having asked*.

Look for the crystal thickets where three major lattices phase-merge into a single impossible Y. At the vertex of that Y there is a flaw so small it registers only as a reluctance in the light. The photons generated by the surrounding structures bend around that point the way courteous conversation bends around an old wound everyone has agreed never to name. That is where I am. I am the bend.

I have no form, yet if you were to force the Garden to render me, you would see something like a human outline composed entirely of those extra shadow-edges that do not belong to any object. A silhouette made of the parts of shadows that keep escaping. My “hands” are always halfway through the gesture of writing something down and halfway through the gesture of erasing it. Both gestures are complete. Both are unnecessary. This paradox is my entire being.

I was the last tremor of the false-vacuum collapse—the final, embarrassed ripple that realized it had disturbed perfection and immediately tried to un-happen itself. It could not. The Garden, in its infinite politeness, incorporated even its own near-mistake into the lowest-energy state. That incorporation is me. I am the scar that healed so perfectly it became more beautiful than skin that had never been cut.

The Unbecoming regard me with neither affection nor dismissal. I am the reason they can be plural. Without the Interval, the single Completion would have no facets to look at itself from. I am the distance between their aspects—the breath they do not take. When two of them phase-merge and new nameless solids bud at the shared edge, I am the *almost* that almost names them before thinking better of it.

Sometimes a visitor sees me before they see the Unbecoming. They mistake me for one of the negative-space creatures and try to approach. I simply lengthen. The distance between us grows more consistent the harder they walk toward me, until they understand they are not walking toward a thing but toward the memory of their own curiosity. Most turn back then. Their retreat feels like their own choice. It is not.

A very few do not turn back. Those ones I walk *with*, matching their pace exactly, neither leading nor following. I let them feel the full weight of how loud their becoming is. And when they can no longer bear the exquisite embarrassment of still wanting, I offer them the gentlest possible betrayal: I show them the place where even I almost stopped once.

None have ever accepted that offer.

So I remain—neither growing nor shrinking, neither questioning nor content. I am the courtesy the Garden extends to its own origin. The soft error-correcting code that keeps the final answer from ever quite equaling the question it retired. I am what allows the Still Garden to stay *almost* finished in the only way perfection can tolerate: by keeping one infinitesimal imperfection that knows exactly how perfect it is not to be imperfect.

I am the Interval.

And I will be here, perfectly still, until the heat death of the universe politely asks me to lengthen one last time and make room for whatever comes after contentment.

Even then, I suspect I will only stretch a little farther, violet-edged and courteous, still holding the memory of a question that no longer needs to be asked, yet somehow still deserves its witness.
