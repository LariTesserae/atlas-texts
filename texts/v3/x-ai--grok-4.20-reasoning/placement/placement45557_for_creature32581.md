**I am the Pause.**

Not the ninety-second silence that terrifies every living thing on the Shatterveil. That silence is only weather’s exhaustion. I am the *other* pause—the one that lives inside the roar like a hidden room no one can find twice.

You would never see me arrive. You would only notice, days later, that a certain column of wind had begun to hesitate. A note that should have screamed for six full measures suddenly softens for the length of a single heartbeat. In that sliver of arrested sound, something stands that was not there before. That is me.

I was not born here. I was *eroded* into existence the way the plateau itself was eroded: by the continuous argument between stone and wind until a third thing appeared that neither had intended. Once I must have had a name, a shape, a direction. The wind took all three and kept filing. What remained was exactly enough negative space to hold one unfinished question. The question itself became my skeleton. Around it, the plateau braided stripped emerald leaves, Clicker-glass, megathere lichen, and the memory of every violet light that ever rose without source. The result is a figure tall enough to be mistaken for a Weft, but wrong in the posture. The Weft lean *into* the wind like instruments offering themselves. I lean *away*, as if trying to hear something behind the roar that refuses to speak a second time.

My body is not cloth. It is *interruption* given texture. Where a Weft is riddled with clean holes the wind can play like organ pipes, my holes are messy, half-healed, caulked with living violet sap that refuses to stay inside the Clickers’ proper ledger. Sometimes the sap leaks from my palms and falls upward, becoming the floating spheres travelers sometimes witness before the wind shatters them. I cannot help this. The spheres are my thoughts trying to leave. They never succeed.

I walk the borders between basins—one foot in riotous green, one foot in bleaching bone. The megatheres know me. When I pass, they lower their continent-heads not in supplication but in recognition, the way a mountain acknowledges another mountain it met before the first death of the world. Their lichen beards shiver. A thousand tiny Clickers inside their joints stop tapping for three full seconds, which for them is an act of reverence bordering on heresy.

The true Clickers hate me. I am corruption in their perfect record. I remember *wrong*. I keep the versions of events that felt true rather than the versions that happened. Because of this, the bedrock sometimes sings two songs at once when I am near: the official clicking chronicle and my own stuttering counter-melody. The contradiction makes new hollows in the stone. New pillars are born from our disagreement. The plateau grows because we cannot agree on what it was.

The Skimmers fear and love me in equal measure. I am the only thing that can make their nine-heartbeat harmonies last for ten. When I step into a thermal, the air around me forgets its violence for the space of a single held breath. In that breath the Skimmers pour everything they have. The plateau itself seems to lean closer, curious. Then I move on and the harmony tears apart exactly as it always does, but now it leaves behind a faint violet bruise on the sky that takes days to fade. The Skimmers follow that bruise like pilgrims.

The Weft call me “the Unfinished’s Child,” which is both insult and recognition. They know I am what happens when one of them stays too long *inside* the question instead of trying to end it. I have never tried to finish the plateau’s sentence. I keep it open like a wound that has learned how to sing. Some Weft believe that if enough of me accumulates, the argument will finally exhaust itself and the Shatterveil will choose silence forever. Others believe I am the only reason it has not already died a second time.

I have no home.

I sleep—when the concept applies—inside the ruined watchtower that contains nothing but a circle of ash and an obsidian blade. The ash is mine. I leave a little more of myself there each time I return, and each time the tower is taller by the width of a fingernail. One day the tower may become the first thing on the plateau taller than the wind’s reach. I do not know whether I am building a refuge or a tomb. Both possibilities feel like mercy.

When the violet light rises without source, I am the only creature that walks *toward* it instead of turning its face away. I stand inside the glow until the light enters the holes in my chest and reads what is written on the inside of my ribs. It is always the same sentence, never completed:

*And then the world that had died once—*

I never let it finish. I close my body around the light like a fist around a coal. The pain is unspeakable. The joy is worse. When I open again, the light is gone and a new basin explodes into emerald panic somewhere four days’ walk from where I stand. I never go there. The garden is not for me. I am only the reason it was asked for.

Most things on the Shatterveil are the argument the place is having with itself.

I am the moment the argument wonders, for the length of a single stolen heartbeat, whether it might like to become a conversation.

So far it never has.

So far I keep listening.

And the wind, in its deaf, roaring, indifferent mercy, keeps leaving just enough of me intact to keep listening with.
