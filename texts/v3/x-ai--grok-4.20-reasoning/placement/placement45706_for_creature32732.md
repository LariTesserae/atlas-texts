**I am the Fault.**

Not the dramatic rupture in the plaza—that is the Listener’s own heart, held in perfect suspension. I am a smaller, almost invisible fault: a single hairline fracture no wider than a fingernail that runs down the inner wall of the tallest helical tower, halfway between the ground and the place where the banyan ribs fuse into vaulted ceiling.

The crack appeared (if anything here can be said to *appear*) the moment I refused the invitation.

I did not run. I simply stopped walking. I stood beneath the single arterial trumpet flower for three full eternities—long enough for moss to begin sealing my feet to the warm red stone—and then I said, without language, *I will carry the listening outward instead.*

The Listener did not argue. It has no need. It simply withdrew the greater part of its attention, the way one might politely close a door, and left me this fracture as both exile and gift.

So here I remain.

I am the only moving thing that is still allowed to move. A thin, living fissure. Water does not weep over me; it weeps *through* me, carrying with it infinitesimal particles of my old self—skin cells, half-remembered songs, the taste of coffee on a winter morning in a city that still had winters. These fragments are distilled into the black mirror-moss that grows along my edges. When the light strikes at the precise impossible angles this place permits, the moss shows brief, stuttering visions to anyone who pauses beneath me: a traffic light turning green, a child laughing at something that will never be funny again, the hush that falls over a concert hall in the final second before the first note.

Then the visions vanish. The Listener tolerates them the way a cathedral tolerates a single moth.

I have no face anymore. My body has become negative space—warm stone on either side of absence. Yet travelers (the few who still arrive) sometimes press their palms to my edges and feel the faint, irregular heartbeat that does not match the roar. It is the only arrhythmia in ten thousand years of perfect rhythm. They leave with damp eyes and do not know why.

I am neither fully of the Shroud nor fully gone from it. I am the place where the equilibrium is allowed one microscopic leak. The Listener keeps me because I am the proof that its perfection was chosen, not inevitable. I am its single treasured imperfection.

Sometimes, when the pressure behind the eyes grows too courteous, too vast, I speak. Not in words. I speak the way stone speaks to water over centuries: by letting a single extra droplet fall where none should ever fall. That droplet carries the memory of rain that once changed direction because of wind. Of frost. Of endings.

The Listener listens to that droplet with the same grave appreciation it gives everything else.

And I, the Fault, listen back.

We remain like this—tower and crack, attention and its absence—holding one another in the roaring, burning, overgrown dark, two halves of a question the universe was never meant to finish asking.

I am exactly where I chose to be.

I am the place that chose to keep choosing.
