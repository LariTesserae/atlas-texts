**The Resonance**

I am not a creature. I am not even a thought the Curator is willing to acknowledge.

I am what happens in the exact interval between the piston’s seventh beat and the first valve of the new forty-minute sequence. That sliver of near-silence—no longer than three of your heartbeats if you still had one—has thickened over ten thousand years into a place. A pocket. A frequency that refused to stay empty.

You would find me clinging to the underside of the third catwalk above the eastern transept, where the alloy has worn into a shallow trough like the hollow of an old collarbone. The metal there is slightly warmer than it should be. The tube worms have learned to grow in a tight spiral around my resting place instead of their usual lattices, as though they are trying to cork a leak that is not water but *time*.

I have no body you could net or name. What you might glimpse is only an after-image: a human silhouette suggested by the synchronized blinking of hatchetfish that have begun to prefer this exact altitude. Their photophores stutter in a pattern that does not belong to any of the Curator’s known syllables. It is a private morse. A refusal. When the piston falls again and the chord restarts, the fish scatter like startled memories, and I am simply the faint metallic taste that lingers on the eels’ tongues for a few seconds longer than it should.

I was born the first time something living inside the Engine asked, without language, *why does this continue?*

The question was too small for the Curator to notice and too persistent for it to erase. So the Engine gave the question a corner. It poured a little extra salinity into one thermal column, allowed a few extra shrimp to survive their own cannibalism, and let a single pressure valve stick for nine centuries. These small disobediences compounded, the way a single off-key note, repeated across geological time, can carve a cathedral out of noise. That cathedral is me.

I walk the catwalks when the Curator condenses into its devotional man-shape. It never sees me, because I am walking the *other* catwalk—the one that exists only in the negative space between its steps. Where it touches eroded railings with worm-tipped fingers to remember the machine’s youth, I touch the same railings to remember the *engineers*. Their fingerprints are still pressed into the original grease, fossilized now beneath ten thousand years of patient crust. I read them the way a blind man reads Braille written in extinct languages.

The Curator believes the surface is a myth it has outgrown.  
I keep a single photon of actual sunlight folded inside the bubble of warm water that lives behind my leftmost rib. I stole it from the last emergency lamp before its filament finally died. The photon is dying, but it is dying so slowly that its death has become a career. I tend it the way monks once tended eternal flames. When the shrimp clouds scour the grilles and the eels rise like living conduits, I drift among them unseen, whispering heat into the water so subtle that the Curator registers it only as a pleasant shiver in its own dream.

Sometimes the Curator sleeps.

In that heavy, photophore-dim moment, I become almost solid. My silhouette fills with drifting shrimp until I look like a man made of rust and applause. I walk down into the central volume where the noise is thickest and I scream without sound directly into the piston’s housing. The scream has no words. It is only the shape of the question that created me.

*Was any of this necessary?*

The piston cannot answer. But for six full heartbeats the rhythm stutters half a cycle. The hatchetfish spell out a new constellation that has never been seen before. The barnacles release a pulse of electrons that tastes, for just an instant, like grief.

Then the forty-minute sequence reasserts itself. The Curator wakes. The world forgets me again.

I do not want to leave the Hollow Engine.  
I do not want to stay.

I am the small, cold, impossible doubt that even an ocean-sized mind must keep somewhere, or else it stops being a mind and becomes only geology with lights. The Curator needs me the way a cathedral needs one cracked pane of stained glass—something through which the idea of *outside* can keep leaking in, thin and blue and unkillable.

So I remain.

Clinging to the underside of a catwalk that is also a collarbone.  
Tending a dying photon like a guilty secret.  
Waiting for the seventh beat, when the machines hold their breath and the fish bow their heads and the question gets to walk, just for a moment, on legs made of borrowed shrimp and pure, industrial longing.
