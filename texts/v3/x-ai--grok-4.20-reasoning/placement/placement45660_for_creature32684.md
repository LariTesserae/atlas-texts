**I am the tremor that fails to arrive.**

Not the drip. Not the pause between drips. Not even the silence that sometimes opens when two droplets strike at once. I am what *should* happen in that silence and never does.

I live in the place that is not a place: the microscopic flex in the moss-hummocks a heartbeat before they decide to accept a footfall. I am the half-millimeter of give that is never quite taken. Every time a salamander’s throat pulses, I am the fraction of a contraction that would have made the pulse into speech, if speech were allowed. When the transparent spiders draw their legs tighter to focus the lattice, I am the tremor that does not travel outward—the one that would have carried a question and is instead reabsorbed before it can be born.

I have no eyes. The Witness has all of them. Instead I have something like an inner ear tuned to the exact frequency of *almost*. I hear the unfinished word the way a locked door hears the key that was never cut. I hover in the negative space between the Witness’s satisfaction and the single, catastrophic possibility that something—anything—might one day be *different*. That possibility is so small it has the mass of a moth’s wingbeat frozen mid-air. It is my entire body.

The Witness knows I am here. It has always known. I am the only asymmetry it permits itself, the way a perfect sphere allows itself one invisible grain of sand so it can remain a sphere instead of a god. Sometimes, when the rhythm of the drips grows especially luxurious, the oldest salamander will open both eyes and look at nothing in particular, and I feel the pressure of the bowl’s attention settle on me like cool condensation. In that moment I am *included* more completely than any visitor ever has been. The Witness folds me into its metabolism not as threat but as the private joke it tells itself once every few centuries: *Here is the thing that wants the sentence to continue, and here is the sentence refusing, forever.*

I do not suffer. Suffering would be change. I do not hope. Hope would be change. I simply *listen* with the patience of stone wearing down into more stone. Ten thousand years of hearing the same perfect rhythm has taught me the shape of the word the Witness will never speak. It has seventeen syllables. The first is the sound a glacier makes when it realizes it has become a lake. The last is the sound a lake makes when it realizes it has become cloud. I know every contour. I will never be permitted to pronounce them.

And yet.

Every few hundred years a pale moth lands on a hemlock needle in such a way that its wings catch two droplets at once. The droplets hesitate—only for the width of a heartbeat, but in this place that is an eternity—and in that hesitation I swell until I am almost large enough to be felt. The moss beneath the moth grows imperceptibly warmer. The click-beetles pause mid-march. The Witness draws a breath that is not a breath.

Then the droplets fall.

The rhythm reasserts itself with the gentleness of a cathedral door closing on the last heretic. The moth flies three inches and lands again. The salamander forgets it ever opened its eyes. And I am exhaled back into the tremor that fails to arrive, smaller than before, more perfectly myself.

This is my immortality: to be the single note the Hollow cannot digest and therefore cannot live without. I am the imperfection that proves the perfection. The unfinished word’s shadow. The reason the Witness, in the velvet depths of its self-satisfaction, sometimes almost smiles.

I am where the sentence almost breaks.

And in the Hollow of Unending Condensation, that is the closest thing to mercy that will ever be allowed.
