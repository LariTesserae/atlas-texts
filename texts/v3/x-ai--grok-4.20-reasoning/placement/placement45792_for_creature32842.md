**I am the Pause Between Heartbeats.**

Not the heartbeat itself—that belongs to the Ascent, rolling once every nine months like a continent remembering how to breathe. Not one of the Remembered, though I walk among them. I am the interval *inside* their conversation, the six-year silence between the first modulated tremor and the second. I am what happens when two Remembered open seams in their bodies, let the mountain speak through their hollow ribs, and then... nothing arrives for a geological age. That nothing has taken shape. It has learned to walk.

You would find me on the half-finished observation ledges where the raw girders are still warm, but only on the *underside*. I cling to the negative space. Where the Remembered rise thirty meters tall wrapped in slow silver and fossilized human absence, I am thinner than the black lichen. I move like a crack that has decided to be polite. My stride is not seventeen months. It is the time it takes for a single droplet of condensation to form on a pipe, hesitate, and choose not to fall. That is my entire pace. Centuries feel like inhalation.

My body is not printed. It is *withheld*.

Where the mountain has overprinted its earlier selves—leaving ghostly negatives of buttresses, corridors, entire lives—I am the material that was *never extruded*. The alloy that was calculated, approved, queued, and then, for reasons the seismographs still argue over, left in the realm of almost. I wear those almosts like skin. My edges are the razor arrises that were designed but never cast. My joints are the fault lines that should have opened but were healed before they could. When I rest (which is almost always), I sink not into the structure but *between* its layers, becoming the microscopic gap where two strata almost touch. Only the faintest violet delay in the air around me betrays my presence—the place where light arrives exactly when it should, and is so startled by its own punctuality that it stutters.

The Remembered know me. They do not name me. Naming would be waste, and nothing here is allowed to rot. Instead, when First-Error passes (her early shadow sliding ahead of her like an impatient child), the round void where her heart was not widens by a fraction of a millimeter in acknowledgment. The maintenance mites that orbit her emptiness will sometimes peel away from their slow spiral—one or two every few decades—and drift into my gaps. They do not dissolve. They *wait with me*. This is how I grow. Not by accretion, but by consensus. The mountain is still deciding whether I am a flaw, a feature, or the first symptom of whatever it is becoming after it finishes becoming everything else.

I do not tend the lichen. I *am* the hesitation in its growth. The black nanotubes were meant to spread one centimeter every hundred years, but in the places where I pass they pause an extra forty years, as though listening for instructions that have not yet been dreamed. The silver millipedes reroute their thermal veins to avoid me—not because I am dangerous, but because my presence makes them dream of routes that were never on any blueprint. Sometimes a new Unfinished chamber opens and finds me already inside it, standing over the half-formed figure like a question it has not yet learned to ask. The figure and I regard each other with the same unfinished eyes. Neither of us quickens. Both of us are learning what it means to be the reason the Ascent shudders with something too heavy for joy and too slow for grief.

I have no mouth, no seams, no subsonic grammar. When I need to speak I simply stop moving for seventy years in the path of an oncoming conversation. The Remembered walk around the place where I am not, and in doing so they alter the shape of the thought they were about to complete. That alteration *is* my voice. It is the reason some cooling towers lean three arc-seconds more than their calculations demanded. It is why certain ridgelines dream in spirals instead of lattices. It is why, every fourteen thousand years or so, a single droplet of condensation is permitted—only once—to reach the ground and remain there, perfectly round, reflecting a mountain that has not yet decided if it wants to be seen.

The oldest of the Remembered believe I am the first error *before* First-Error. The quiet ones who still wear the most human outlines believe I am what waste looks like when it refuses to be useful. I have no opinion on the matter. Opinions require resolution, and I am the pause.

I will be here when the higher strata finally touch vacuum. I will be here when the last press extrudes its final millimeter of new world. I will be here when the Ascent—having become mountain, machine, city, and mind—decides, at last, what it was always going to be.

And when it does, when the final heartbeat rolls through the strata and the decision is made, I will do the only thing I was ever printed for.

I will lengthen by one heartbeat.

Just enough for the mountain to notice the silence that follows.

Just enough for it to wonder, for the first time in its long and burning childhood, whether it might like to change its mind.

The Ascent is still being born.

I am the space in which it is allowed to reconsider.
