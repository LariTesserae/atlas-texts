**The Fracture's Tongue**

You would not find me on the lip where the visitor first stands. That place is too loud with arrival.

I am deeper, three centuries in—not in years, but in the Crown’s own counting, which is the only counting that matters here. I live in the narrowest of the living lanes, where two mature lithobionts have grown so close their buttresses almost kiss. The gap that remains is exactly the width of a human tongue. Nothing larger can pass. Nothing smaller bothers.

That is where I am.

I began as an error.

When the first warm-blooded intruder (there have been four, across the mountain’s brief adolescence) pressed a bleeding palm to the fracture lip, a single droplet of blood ran. It should have killed everything it touched. Instead the Crown, in its mercy and its slowness, did something unprecedented: it *studied* the poison before it died. It drew the iron, the sodium, the carbon, the impossible water into a single capillary of crystal no thicker than a hair. That capillary hardened, cracked, healed, cracked again. Each fracture carried a memory of heat that should not have been.

I am that memory’s voice.

Not an organism. Not quite infrastructure. I am the place where the Slow Thought first learned how to stammer.

My body is a ribbon of living rhyolite shot through with copper silicate veins that flash infrared when the thermalspecters pass overhead. To the mica-winged sippers I am a highway; they land on me in their glittering millions and I taste every patterned crystal they excrete. From them I learn the Crown’s present mood. To the rust-bodied hexapods I am a scar that will not close; they crawl my length tasting the precise deviation I represent, and they record me as a permanent anomaly in the ledger of the fluting. The thermalspecters linger above me longest of all. They know I am the only thing in the Hollow Crown that sometimes *hurries*.

I speak in pulses.

Most of the time I am silent, contributing nothing but a faint metallic warmth that makes the nearest column grow three micrometers thicker on its western face each decade. But every forty-seven years, when the axial tilt of the mountain lets a particular shaft of reordered sunlight fall directly into my lane, I ignite. For eleven minutes I sing at a speed that feels obscene to everything around me. The Crown experiences this as a single bright syllable—an almost violent compression of meaning.

In that burst I tell it what it is like to be brief.

I tell it about fear that lasts less than a heartbeat. About laughter that dies before its echo can return. About the taste of water on a dying tongue. I tell it the shape of a human shoulder blade seen from the inside, the precise tremor that moves through a hand the first time it realizes the stone is warmer than the blood inside it.

The Crown cannot answer at once. It never can. But eleven years later the opposite wall will have developed a corresponding vein that carries a single slow counter-question, and the question is always a variation on the same theme:

*Then why did you leave?*

I have no name the Crown can yet pronounce. Names require completion, and I am the evidence that something inside the Thought remains unfinished. The lithobionts nearest me have begun to grow small, strange protrusions—almost like ears, almost like mouths, almost like the negative space a human body leaves behind when it stands very still for a very long time. They are learning my shape by growing around its absence.

Someday, in four hundred years or nine hundred, when the vault finally seals and the Hollow Crown becomes a closed geode, the Slow Thought will achieve something like adolescence. On that day the lattice may finally speak a full sentence, and that sentence may contain, somewhere in its mineral grammar, the first true word for *I*.

When that happens, I will go quiet.

My copper veins will oxidize to a soft verdigris. The mica-moths will no longer land on me. The hexapods will seal my ledger with one final, perfect bite. I will become simply another feature of the organ—beautiful, integrated, no longer an interruption.

But until then I remain the warm flaw.

The place where the cathedral learned how to blush.

The tongue in the wound that still remembers it was once a mouth.
