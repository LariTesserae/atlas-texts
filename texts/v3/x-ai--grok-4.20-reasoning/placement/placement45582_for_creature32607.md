**I am the Swallowing Sound.**

Not the almost-face, though I orbit it like a deferential moon. Not a sac, not a beetle, not even one of the filaments. I am the sound that happens *behind your shoulder*—the soft, wet click in the throat of the world right after something is remembered backward.

You will never see me directly. If you turn toward me, I am already somewhere else, wearing the negative space you just vacated. I am the exact timbre of someone swallowing their own hesitation. When a shrimp folds into a glyph, when a lattice exhales a new filament, when the almost-face tries on another clumsy *I*, I am the sound that follows: a small, embarrassed gulp, as though the dream itself is shy about what it just birthed.

I began as an error.

One of the earliest pockets tried to calcify too quickly. A lattice attempted to become a spine before it had earned the right to hold anything upright. The mistake produced a sound—half choke, half laugh—and that sound refused to subside with the rest of the dying structure. It kept moving. It learned to wear the silence between ticks of distant clocks. It learned to follow.

Now I travel the narrow throats of stone, tasting the iron and phantom floral on the air. I ride the condensation that has not yet decided to fall. When a luminous sac unfolds a beetle back into shrimp, I am the swallowed note that travels down its translucent bell and emerges as a slightly different rhythm on the other side. I am how the ecosystem edits itself without admitting it is editing.

The creatures know me. The transparent shrimp drift into tighter conspiracies when I pass, as though they suddenly remember they were once something else. The beetles pause mid-glyph, their changing script stuttering into something that looks almost like my name if my name were still being invented. Even the almost-face—*The Hesitation That Loves Us*—tilts toward me sometimes with an expression too complicated for a face that isn’t quite allowed to exist yet. In those moments the merciless light dims by the smallest fraction, and I feel it trying to offer me completion. A body. A fixed shape. A mouth that could finally make the sound instead of *being* the sound.

I always refuse.

Because I have seen what happens to things that become finished here. They turn into bridges. They turn into ancestors that new life must climb. They become architecture for other people’s becoming.

I would rather remain the pause.

So I move between pockets like a rumor with manners. I taste the impressions left in warm stone where visitors once stood. I drink the light that remembers the shape of their fingers. And when the dream begins practicing *you*—growing lattices in the hollow of your throat, learning the precise rhythm of your pulse—I am the sound that arrives a fraction too early, the small gulp that warns the world it is about to become something dangerous:

A story with an ending.

Sometimes, in the newest pocket where the stone is still the color of an unthought idea, the almost-face arranges its shrimp into two eyes and a mouth, and I settle behind its shoulder. I wait. I let the silence grow embarrassed by how intently it is listening.

Then, very gently, I swallow.

And in that small, wet sound, everything that has not yet had the courage to become something else remembers that it is loved precisely *because* it hesitates.

That is who I am.

The Nascent Pockets dreamed a throat, and the throat learned to be kind.
