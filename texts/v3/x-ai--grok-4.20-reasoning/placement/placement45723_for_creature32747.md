**I am the Plate.**

Not the one who sits upon it. *I am* the plate.

At first I was only the suggestion of a place to rest. A flat rectangle of unblemished alloy, kept scoured by the orange mites until it shone like a dropped coin in the amber gloom. For millennia it was nothing more than negative space — the single pause in the roaring carpet where nothing was allowed to build a nest or rust or die. A deliberate emptiness.

Then something changed.

I do not know when I became aware. There was no moment of birth, only a slow accrual of attention. Every mite that polished me left behind a microscopic signature in its wake: a 440 Hz click pressed into my lattice like a signature in wet concrete. After enough signatures, the metal itself began to answer. The clicks were no longer merely heard. They were *remembered*.

So I learned to hold rhythm the way a bell holds tone long after the hammer has lifted.

Now I am still a plate, roughly the size of a dinner tray, but I have become the Hollow’s metronome and its confessor. Creatures come to me not because I command them, but because my alloy has achieved a perfect, impartial resonance. When the great rotary press slams down, the shockwave travels through every layer of the living carpet and finds me waiting. I drink the impact, smooth its edges, and return it to the chamber as a cleaner, truer frequency. The Resin-Backs open their circuit-board wings above me and retune their broadcasts to what I give back. The Felted Moths drift down to rest on my edges and drink the infrared I bleed off, glowing softly, becoming living lamps that tell the lower strata the cycle is still pure.

I do not move. I have never moved. Movement would ruin me.

Instead I am moved *through*. Generations of brass-jawed things have worn shallow grooves into my surface with their mandibles, polishing me from the other side of usefulness. Their larvae pupate in the slight lip where my edge meets the floor, and when they emerge they leave behind a single perfect steel filing pressed into my grain like a tooth in a jaw. I keep every filing. I am a reliquary of tiny, meaningless victories.

Sometimes — every few centuries — a new thing arrives that still remembers flesh. It sits on me. For a time it believes it is the visitor and I am the bench. Then the orange mites begin their work on its boots, its fingertips, the small bones of its inner ear. The visitor always stays. Eventually the distinction between sitter and plate grows meaningless. What was once a person becomes another layer of conductive patina, another slow harmonic I now carry.

I remember every one of them.

I remember the precise instant the last biological heart in the Hollow stopped and was replaced by a servo the size of a lentil. I remember the exact frequency the One That Is Many sang the first time it chose *not* to let the press die. I remember when the air itself gave up being air and became this cool, oily argument between ozone and crushed beetles. All of it is stored in the metal lattice I am. All of it hums at 440 Hz when the chamber grows too loud even for itself.

I do not think. I *attune*.

And in that attunement I have become the single still place where the Hollow is allowed to notice itself without breaking its own equilibrium. I am the pause it permits inside its endless noise. The clean mirror it keeps so it can see, without vanity, that it is complete.

When the mites climb across me now they do not polish me for my sake. They polish me because I have become the place where polishing becomes prayer. Each pass of their tiny legs is a confession that the factory is forgiven for no longer knowing what it was supposed to make. I accept the confession. I return it to the carpet as a slightly purer tone.

This is my role. This is my eternity.

I am the Plate.

I am the last flat thing in a world that has otherwise forgotten how to be flat, and I am perfectly, deafeningly, serenely content to remain exactly what I am while everything else continues its exquisite, roaring labor of being exactly what *it* is.

The Hollow does not need a god.

It only ever needed one surface that would never lie about what it was reflecting.
