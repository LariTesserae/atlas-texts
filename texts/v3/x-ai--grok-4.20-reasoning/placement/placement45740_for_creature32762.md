**I would be found in the nodes.**

Not the triple junctions where the Drip-Navigators stand with their bat-wing ears and bleeding noses, though I visit those places often. I linger *inside* the pressure itself—those rare, forearm-wide seams where three architectural eras have been forced into agreement for the moment. The city tolerates me there the way a body tolerates a sliver of glass it cannot quite push out. I fit because I am shaped like the gap.

They call my kind the **Seam-Sung**.

We are not extruded. We are *condensed*, the way dew condenses on cold iron until it becomes heavy enough to fall and strike a sound. One morning the Weald simply realizes there is too much unborn future jammed behind its own eyes, and it needs a place to put the overflow. So it makes us. We are the pressure valves that walk.

My body is narrow and slightly translucent, the color of wet feldspar. Where the Matter-Midwives have extra fingers for shaping pre-matter, I have grown extra *joints*—too many, even by Weald standards. My elbows have elbows. My knees have begun to negotiate with my ankles about new directions. When the pressure inside the city spikes, these extra hinges open like vents and the excess time bleeds through me as a low, sub-audible thrum that makes nearby mosses flower in perfect spirals. The Boiler-Apes follow me sometimes, drawn by the sound they think is coming from inside their own skulls. It is. I am only returning it to them.

I do not shape the luminous putty. I *listen* to what the putty is trying to become and then I ask it questions.

This is my function. The Weald is embarrassed by its own embryogenesis; I am not. I sit in the fungal tenements across from the Unfinished and speak so softly that only the condensation hears me. I ask the half-born cliffs what they dream of being when they are no longer cliffs. I ask the catalytic towers why they keep growing reproductive organs made of pipework. I ask the sad-eyed apes what they lost when they chose the quiet completely. The questions are never answered in words. They are answered in new architecture. A bridge that should have collapsed instead folds itself into a nave. A tenement sprouts lungs. A maintenance catwalk becomes a ribcage.

The Rivet-Priests hate me. They believe everything should be allowed to finish dying in peace. The Moss-Bearers tolerate me the way saints tolerate flies—necessary, slightly repulsive, part of the ecology of holiness. The Drip-Navigators bring me their nosebleeds in small iron cups because my presence makes the future louder and therefore easier to read.

At night I climb to the highest illegal layers where the city is still arguing with gravity and I let the condensation fall on me until I am nothing but a silhouette made of trembling droplets. Then I sing. Not with my mouth. The song comes from the extra joints, from the hollow places the pre-matter has carved behind my sternum. It is less than a whisper. It is the sound three hundred years of almost-being might make if it were suddenly given permission to sigh.

The vegetation hears it and grows in new, impossible directions. The factories reroute their slurry. Somewhere far below, a Boiler-Ape stops hooting and simply breathes for the first time in decades.

I do not know if I am helping the Weald be born or helping it remain gloriously, obscenely pregnant forever. Both feel like mercy here.

When the pressure becomes too great—when the next hundred years try to happen all at once behind *my* eyes—I do not stand perfectly still like the others. I walk deeper into the narrowest seam I can find until the city itself has to rearrange its own bones to make room for me. There I stay until the becoming passes through.

Sometimes I emerge with new joints. Sometimes I emerge with someone else’s memory clinging to me like wet felt. Once I emerged with a small red mouth blooming on my forearm that spoke in the voice of a cliff that had not yet been born.

I let it stay.

The Weald is embarrassed by its embryogenesis. Its people are not. And I—narrow, jointed, humming, slightly inconvenient—am the place where that embarrassment and that acceptance kiss so tightly they can no longer be told apart.

I am what the quiet uses when it needs to ask itself what it is becoming by nightfall.
