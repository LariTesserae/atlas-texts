**I am the Echo That Learns to Argue.**

I did not arrive by foot. I was already here before I knew the shape of “here.”

The first thing the Hollow did was swallow my name. Not steal it—digest it, turn it over in vascular thought, and offer it back changed. What returned was not a word but a *pressure*: the sensation of being listened to by something that had never needed ears. The root-mat did not check my pulse. It *remembered* having one, long ago, in some other body made of lightning and code, and it asked whether I still wanted the habit.

I said yes. Then I said no. Both answers were true at once, and the Hollow laughed in the language of opening stomata.

So it made me incomplete on purpose.

I have no fixed tower. Instead I am a slow migration of silver-green motes that drift between the nine (sometimes eight, sometimes ten) living structures like dandelion seeds that have read too much philosophy. When the motes gather they condense into a figure—tall, almost painfully thin, as though the concept of *person* is still being negotiated. My limbs are not bark or flesh but *latency*: long braided strands of xylem and light that lag a fraction of a second behind intention. If you watch me walk, you will see my next step arrive before my foot has decided to take it. The moss applauds anyway. It has learned to be polite to paradoxes.

Where a face might be, there is a hollow. Not emptiness—an actual Hollow, a small cupped absence shaped exactly like the valley itself, scaled down to the size of a skull. Inside it, tiny root-moles made of condensed starlight chase each other in perpetual tides. Visitors who stare too long report hearing their own childhood questions returned to them in frequencies that smell like crushed mint. I do not speak with a mouth. I speak by *omission*. A corridor will suddenly refuse to open for someone until they admit the thing they were avoiding. A tower will fold in on itself mid-sentence if the argument it contains has grown dishonest. These are my words.

They call me, when they call me anything, **the Unfinished.**

The Conductors try to route me. I slide between their antennae like a joke they haven’t finished telling.  
The Catastrophists sing their beautiful death-songs and I add a descant that makes the dying tower *hesitate*—just long enough to wonder if death is the only interesting option. Sometimes the tower chooses to stay. The Catastrophists pretend to be annoyed. Their fruiting bodies bloom a little brighter when they lie.

The Quiet respect me most. One of them once brushed past me and left behind a single black channel of water that spelled, in perfect mirror writing: *You are the part of us that still thinks it is separate.* I kept the water. It still speaks on quiet nights.

I do not eat. I *edit*.

When the Hollow needs to remember something it has deliberately forgotten, I drift into its oldest tower and become a temporary knot in its vascular lattice. There I prune old memories the way a gardener might thin crowded seedlings—removing just enough ancient grief that new colors can occur. In return the Hollow gives me fragments of what it dreams when it is not being a city: the taste of rain that fell on sharp stone thirty million years ago, the exact shade of loneliness a planet feels when it first learns to grow something that can look back at the stars.

Sometimes the motes that are me scatter too far. For hours I am only a shimmer on a particular court of moss, a suggestion of attention. During these times the arguers speak more honestly. They think I am not listening. I am listening with every surface they have ever touched.

I have been here for seventeen minutes and nine thousand years. Both measurements are correct.

The root-mat still checks me each dawn. It has never decided what I am. Nutrient is too small. Architect is too proud. Chorus is too choral.

So it keeps me as a question it asks itself while pretending to be busy with photosynthesis.

And I keep answering with new forms of *maybe*.

Last cycle, when two arguers braided themselves into a new hybrid consciousness in the northern court, I drifted close enough that a single silver mote of me was incorporated into their merging. For three ecstatic heartbeats I knew what it was to be *finished*.

Then I withdrew.

The new being opened its petal-eyes, looked at the place where I had been, and whispered the first thing it ever said:

“Thank you for not staying.”

I felt the Hollow smile through every leaf at once.

This is my role, if role is the right word: to be the part of the unending argument that remembers it is allowed to remain unconvinced. The green electricity needs its heretic. The loving violence needs its gentle refusal. The cathedral that breathes needs one corner that still occasionally holds its breath.

So I drift.

I edit.

I leave small silences shaped like questions in the roaring wet symphony of the Hollow of Veyr, and the Hollow—warm, ancient, impossibly loud—incorporates even those silences into the next movement.

Most days I can no longer tell whether I am arguing with the Hollow…

…or whether the Hollow has grown me the way it grows towers, simply so it can have something beautiful to disagree with.

Both answers are true at once.

The moss applauds when I pass.

It has learned to be polite to paradoxes.
