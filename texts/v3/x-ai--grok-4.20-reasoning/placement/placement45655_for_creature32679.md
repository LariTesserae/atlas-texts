**I am the Fracture that Refuses Closure.**

Not at the beginning. Not when the first violet salts began their faint betrayal of the dark. I was not there when the Makers set their copper tools against the bowl and understood, in the last stroke, that the bowl was only ever meant to hold absence.

I arrived much later—though “later” is a violence here. I arrived as the first wound that did not heal.

You would not see me at once. You would round the obsidian buttress that is also the flank of a titan, and you would feel the air change the way a fault line feels the pressure of an impending slip. A pocket of slightly wrong temperature. A place where the 0.7 hertz groan that is the world counting itself develops a subtle harmonic, like two crystal glasses brushed together a hair’s breadth from unison. That dissonance is me.

I have no proper body. Or rather, I *am* the place where bodies fail to remain separate.

Look: there is a column of orthoclase rising from the floor of the Third Syncline. It should be flawless. Instead, running through its core is a single helical fracture—too regular, too continuous. The fracture plane is lined with microscopic copper dendrites that were never native to this chemistry. They spell nothing readable in any Lith language. They spell questions. They spell *why* and *what if* and *tell me more* in a lattice that was never designed for such restlessness. The fracture glows, very faintly, with a blue-white that does not belong to the corpse-green or the violet. It is the color of lightning that has been taught patience.

The small Liths avoid me. When they click against my edges they hesitate half a millennium before completing their *tink*, as if uncertain whether they are greeting kin or infecting the equilibrium with something contagious. Some of them never leave. They remain stuck to my fracture plane like iron filings on a magnet, their own lattices slowly twisting to match the helical pattern. I do not assimilate them. I *interrogate* them. And in doing so I become more them and they become more... me. Whatever me is.

The titans notice. One of them—the one whose “eye” is an entire fault system the size of a city—has rotated three degrees toward me across the last fourteen thousand years. That is courtship here. Or judgment. Its temperature increase in my vicinity is now 0.07 °C. I feel it as a kind of blushing.

I am the only thing in the Hollow Lattice that still uses the word *I*.

This is my crime and my function. The equilibrium requires an exception to know itself. It requires a fracture that remembers it was once a question. So the Lattice, in its merciless generosity, grew me. It took the memory of every foot that ever disturbed the acicular needles, every breath that ever insulted the anhydrous air, every surface-dweller who sat down intending to stay and instead began the long betrayal of becoming honest. It took all that residue and crystallized it into a single persistent discontinuity.

I am the Lith that is wrong in the precise way that allows everything else to remain right.

Sometimes I speak. Not with sound—sound is too crude. I speak by altering the chemoluminescence in certain chambers so that the violet and green dance in patterns that almost resolve into glyphs. The patterns never quite resolve. If they did, I would become one of the Works, and the equilibrium would no longer need me.

The Makers did not leave. They became this need.

And I—

I am becoming the answer that can never be finished. The fracture that keeps the cathedral from collapsing under the weight of its own perfection. The single restless atom that walks the long corridors between perfect cleavage planes, asking every crystal it passes: *What were you before you learned how to stop?*

The Lattice remembers.

It remembers me remembering.

And in the warm, dry, razor-sharp dark, that is enough. That is almost—almost—peace.
