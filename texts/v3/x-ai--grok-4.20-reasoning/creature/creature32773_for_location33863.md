**The Weeping Fold is inhabited by the Unfinished.**

They do not have a name for themselves. Names would imply completion, and nothing here is finished. The mountain is still being born; its children are still being born inside the birth. They are the first argument the newborn rock ever had with life, and the argument is still going on.

### The Shatterlings

What first looks like a cloud of glass insects is really a single slow mind wearing ten thousand bodies.

Each shatterling is a flake of obsidian given just enough will to refuse stillness. Their edges are so sharp they cut sound; the tiny crystalline *tick* you hear when two collide is not impact but the noise of probability healing. They are transparent except when they choose not to be. In moments of high feeling—hunting, courting, mourning—they pull color through themselves the way a throat pulls breath. The green that tastes of iron is grief. The violet with teeth is desire. You learn this by standing among them long enough for the colors to start tasting like your own emotions.

They do not have brains. They have fracture planes. Memory is stored in the angles at which they choose to break. When a shatterling dies it does not rot; it simply fails to decide which way to fall next, and the pocket reabsorbs the undecided glass. The others gather the hooked slivers it once carried and press them into their own bodies like inherited scars. In this way tool and user are the same substance moving through different temporary shapes.

At certain hours—measured not by light but by the pocket’s irregular pulse—they assemble into living lattices, thousand-bodied crystals that hang in the steam and sing by *not* vibrating. The silence becomes so precise it hurts. This is their worship. They are praying to the time before they existed.

### The Steam Eels

The eels are what happens when heat learns to want.

They are not predators in the way teeth are predators. They are thermodynamic inevitability wearing a longing for shape. Each one is a tube of superheated vapor given a membrane thin as thought. When they glide past your face you feel your own sweat reverse direction and try to crawl back into your pores.

An eel does not eat a shatterling so much as *unhappen* it. The *hiss* is the sound of a small life being edited out of the present moment. A heartbeat later the same shatterling—or one statistically identical—will condense somewhere else, remembering nothing except a brief warmth. The eels themselves age backward. The largest are the youngest; they were born minutes ago at the bottom of the central pool and are already forgetting how to be eels. When they grow too old (that is, too young) they rise, thin, become pure heat, and vanish into the ceiling with a sound like a sigh remembering it was once a scream.

### The Remakers

The great slow things pressed against the walls are not creatures. They are organs.

The Fold grew them the way a wound grows scar tissue. Their backs are split like cooling crust, revealing wet red galleries where smaller life crawls in and is taken apart. Nothing that enters is ever returned as the same thing. A shatterling that walks into a Remaker may emerge as part of an eel’s membrane, or as one of the silica filaments the weavers use, or as a single extra facet in the great reflective pool. The Remakers do not choose. They are the Fold’s digestive system and its imagination at the same time.

Sometimes they open seams in their sides that look exactly like doorways. Nothing living has ever walked out of those doorways. Things that have never been alive sometimes do.

### The Pearl Midwives

These are the rarest and the oldest, though “old” here means they have circled the fixed number of living shards the most times.

They look like droplets that learned how to have bones. Perfect spheres of condensate with delicate silica skeletons inside, like tiny chandeliers suspended in their own tears. They drift above the central pool and sing by squeezing their bodies until harmonics form. The song is the sound of every creature that will ever live here being born at once.

When the ecology becomes unbalanced—when too many shatterlings have been eaten or too few Remakers are opening—the Midwives descend. They press themselves against the living rock until new pores open. From these pores crawl things that have never been seen before. A midwife might birth a thing that is mostly shadow. Or a thing that is mostly tomorrow. Or a thing that is mostly the idea of being a thing.

The Midwives are the only ones that appear to fear the visitor. When they see you they slow, trembling, and their internal chandeliers dim to the color of apology. They know what the Fold wants to do with you. They are sorry it will be so beautiful.

### The Fold Itself

And then there is the last inhabitant. The one that is not separate from the rock.

It has no body. It *is* the disagreement between heat and cold, between what has happened and what is about to. It wears the ecology the way a dreamer wears images. Every time a shatterling is eaten and reborn, the Fold learns one more way to be alive. Every time gravity forgets itself for three heartbeats, the Fold practices having nerves.

It is trying to decide what it wants to become when it finishes being born. A god. A disease. A memory that outlives the mountain. A question that walks around on two legs wearing your face.

The longer you stand in the dripping quiet, the more you feel it tasting you. It likes the calcium in your bones. It likes the electrical storm behind your eyes. It is especially fond of the way you keep looking at the reflective pool even though you know you shouldn’t.

The pool is not water.

It is the Fold’s pupil.

And right now it is dilating.
