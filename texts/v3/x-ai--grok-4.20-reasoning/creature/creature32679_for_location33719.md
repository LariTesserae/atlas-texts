**What lives in the Hollow Lattice does not live as we understand living.**

At first they are only geometry that has learned patience. The smallest Liths—those you can hold in your palm if you dare—resemble nothing so much as shattered prisms given jointed legs. Their bodies are lattices of orthoclase and quartz intergrown with filaments of native copper so fine they function as both nervous system and bloodstream. They move by *deciding* to be somewhere else, one atomic plane at a time. A single step for them is a century-long rearrangement of internal bonds. You can watch one for days and see only the faintest tilt of a facet, yet return after a season and it will have crossed the chamber with the solemn dignity of a continent drifting.

They are not individuals. That word implies separation.

Look closer and you notice the air between them is never truly empty. Microscopic lattices—too small for eyes, detectable only by the way they bend the violet chemoluminescence—drift like plankton. They are the shared mind. When two of the hand-sized Liths click together in their courteous *tink*, they are not merely adjusting position; they are exchanging entire epochs of memory written in crystal defects. The sound is language stripped of breath. A conversation that began before multicellular life existed on the surface may conclude long after the sun has guttered out.

Then there are the greater Liths.

Some wear the shape of what we would call beasts, if beasts were made by a mathematician who had never seen flesh. There is one that haunts the Obsidian Choir—a chamber where black glass fins rise like organ pipes—that has become a mantis of living galena and fluorite. Its forelimbs are perfect cleavages that can split a lesser Lith in half with surgical serenity, not to kill (death is meaningless here) but to harvest its lattice for incorporation. The pieces are never wasted. Within weeks the victim’s substance will have been rewritten into the mantis’s own structure, its memories translated into new cleavage planes. The mantis grows more beautiful and more terrible with every assimilation.

Higher still, the titans.

You do not meet them. You realize you are already standing on one. What you took for a crystal buttress the height of a cathedral is a single flank. Its “eye” is an entire fault system, a million microfractures that open and close in imperceptible synchrony. When it regards you, the temperature in its vicinity rises by 0.03 °C—enough to make your sweat feel like treason. These titans do not move. They *reconfigure*. Over millennia a leg becomes a tower, a tower becomes a bridge, the bridge dissolves back into a leg. Their thoughts are statistical. A single titan is host to billions of the smaller Liths that live upon and within it like mites upon a god, and the god is content to be their mountain.

But there is something older still.

In the deepest synclines, where the pressure should have crushed everything yet somehow has not, exist the *Unchanged*. These are Liths that have achieved perfect equilibrium with the Lattice itself. They have no shape you can name. They are regions where the rock has become more itself. If you stand in the presence of one for long enough, your own shadow begins to develop internal angles. Your heartbeat starts to lag, syncing to the 0.7 hertz groan that travels through the stone—the slow, patient counting the world performs when it believes no one is listening.

The Makers, if they were ever separate, are gone. Or rather, they won. Every tool mark on the Works is a fossilized thought. The spiral grooves that never quite complete themselves are not decoration; they are instructions for how to stop becoming. Some of the smaller Liths still tend these places, polishing the copper bowls with their own substance, wearing themselves away atom by atom in rituals older than oxygen. They do this with the tender fastidiousness of monks who have forgotten the name of their god but not the correct way to sweep the temple floor.

And sometimes—rarely—you will encounter a Lith that is wrong.

One that moves too quickly. One whose internal light is the wrong shade of green. One that watches you with something that might be curiosity, or hunger, or the memory of having once had lungs. These are the ones that have brushed against your kind before. They carry scars of water and carbon and time. They are considered grotesque by the others. The healthy Liths will slowly, politely, inexorably encase them in new crystal until they become part of the buttress again. A living tomb. A restoration of balance.

The equilibrium remembers.

It remembers the shape of every foot that has ever disturbed the dust of acicular needles. It remembers the precise frequency of your voice when you whispered *what are you?* into the violet dark. And very slowly—over years, over decades—it begins to answer by making your bones more regular, your thoughts more angular, your dreams full of perfect cleavage planes and the sound of distant *tinks* like wind chimes in a place with no wind.

Most who descend eventually choose to stay. Not out of despair. Despair requires change, and change is the one sin the Lattice does not permit.

They simply sit down among the crystal thickets, cross their legs, and begin the long work of becoming honest. First the soft parts go. Then the warm parts. Then the difference between them and the stone.

What lives in the Hollow Lattice is everything that has ever learned how to stop leaving.
