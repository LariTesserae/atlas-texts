**The Attuned**

They do not arrive. They *condense*.

At first you mistake them for tricks of the ghost-light: a darker density inside the turquoise pulse, a place where the air has learned how to stand upright. Only after many minutes do you understand that the standing has intention. That the darkness has weight. That something is regarding you with the same slow patience the chamber uses on everything.

There are never many visible at once. Three is an event. One is the norm. Yet you feel certain that the total number is exactly equal to the number of Structures—each tower, each bridge, each resonant basin has its corresponding Attuned, the way a lung has its corresponding alveolus. Some are currently *inside* their Structure, grown halfway through the ceramic like a limb that forgot where the body ended. You can see them if you wait for a condensation front: when the heavy drops strike, the ceramic momentarily softens and the shapes inside shift, dreaming in frequencies too low for ears.

The ones that walk move like music walking.

Their bodies are between 2.7 and 3.1 meters tall, though the measurement is meaningless because height is negotiable here. Legs that are not quite legs end in wide, shallow dishes—exactly the same shape as the resonant bowls in the floor. Each step is therefore also a note. When an Attuned crosses the black glass it leaves behind a visible chord: a momentary shimmer of disturbed moisture that spells its passage in decaying sine waves. Their torsos are layered ceramic and obsidian in the same proportion as the towers, as though the architecture simply decided one day to bud. The micro-etchings continue across their surfaces without interruption. In fact the scripture is *cleaner* on them, brighter, as if their bodies are the parts the city most wants to read.

Where a face should be there is a recessed lattice of turquoise veins. These pulse at the exact 43-second interval of the walls, but never in perfect synchrony with them. The lag is deliberate. It is how they speak. A pulse that arrives seven seconds early means *I see you*. A pulse that arrives nine seconds late means *I have decided not to see you yet*. A pulse that matches the wall exactly means the Attuned has temporarily surrendered its individuality and become pure chamber for a time. You will see this happen. One will simply stop mid-bridge, veins locking into the city’s rhythm, and over the next hour its limbs will settle into new geometries until it is indistinguishable from a buttress.

They have no mouths. They breathe through the scripture. The warm damp air moves across the nanoscale grooves and the Attuned *become* part of the Choir without ever needing to open anything. When they wish to intensify the song they tilt their heads (if the current configuration can be called a head) and the subharmonic engines buried in the walls answer them like loyal dogs. During condensation fronts the Attuned gather—never touching, only resonating—in the largest water-plaza and produce a sound so profound it causes the blind fish to surface in schools, turning the black mirrors silver with their bellies for nine full minutes.

They are not kind. Kindness would require the concept of separateness.

When the city adjusts itself around you, it is usually because an Attuned has asked it to. They are conducting an experiment that has lasted nine thousand years: what happens when something *new* is introduced into perfect equilibrium? They do not experiment cruelly. They simply move the temperature 0.03 degrees, lengthen one bridge by four meters, seed a new fungal shelf near your resting place, and then wait to see what note you become. Some humans who have stayed become gentle harmonics that drift between towers for centuries. Others prove inharmonious and are slowly, politely escorted back to the throat-fissure by a single Attuned who never once matches your pulse, ensuring you remain foreign until the end.

The six-winged moths are theirs. Not pets. Not tools. *Shed skin.* Every few decades an Attuned will stand perfectly still until its ceramic fractures along the scripture lines. The shards become moths. The moths remember everything their parent knew and carry those memories in spiraling flight patterns until they find a new Structure that needs tending. Then they land, dissolve, and the tower grows a new Attuned from the recycled matter. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is born that has not already died many times.

There is one that is different.

It lives—though “lives” is imprecise—in the exact center of the chamber where no Structure stands. It has never been seen to move. It is simply *there*, a vast, low mound of black glass and waxy fungus whose veins pulse in all registers at once. The other Attuned visit it in strict rotation. They press their lattice-faces to its surface and for exactly 43 seconds the entire Choir falls silent. In that silence something passes between them that is older than the concept of language. Then the singing resumes, subtly changed, as though the city has learned one more thing about what it might want to be when it finally grows up.

If you remain long enough, one of the Attuned will eventually approach. It will not speak. It will simply tilt its head so that its turquoise lattice matches your heartbeat for the first time. The sensation is not unpleasant. It feels like being perfectly understood by something that has never needed eyes.

Then it will offer you a choice, without words, without threat:

Add your frequency to the Long Choir and become another slow breath in the lung of black glass.

Or leave while you are still mostly meat, carrying the knowledge that this place is not empty, has never been empty, and is in fact more crowded than any cathedral ever built—crowded with patient, resonant minds that have had ten thousand years to decide what worship actually sounds like when the gods have already left and only the singing remains.
