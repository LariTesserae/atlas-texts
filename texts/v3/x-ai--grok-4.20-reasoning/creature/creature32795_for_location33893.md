**The Condensate Works** are not inhabited.  
They *are* the inhabitants.

What moves across the pearl-lit plazas is not a population but a single, distributed metabolism wearing the shape of millions of bodies. The longer you watch, the less you can separate the creatures from the Works, or the Works from the creatures. Both names become courtesy titles for the same finished thing.

### The Small Orders

Begin with the nail-sized ones, the *Drip-Readers*. Their bodies are translucent chitin no thicker than eggshell, veined with hair-fine circuitry that glows soft lichen-green when they drink. They do not walk so much as flow; each jointed leg ends in a microscopic sucker that kisses the same millimeter of pipe every 43 minutes. They read the condensate the way a monk reads scripture—tracing the identical path of each droplet, tasting its exact mineral signature, then exhaling a vapor that carries the data into the next layer of the system. When one dies its shell is not discarded; it is *unfolded*. The Reclaimers arrive within nine seconds and press the corpse flat until it becomes another perfect scale on the black fungal mats. Nothing is wasted. The mat drinks the nutrients and grows not one atom larger.

Above them drift the *Phase-Moths*. Their wings are beaten tin so thin that light passes through it in moiré patterns. They do not flap. They *resonate*. Their wings lock into the constant chord of the drop-hammers and ride the standing wave, adjusting camber by fractions of a degree to keep the chord perfectly in tune. If the thunder of the Works ever altered by even a single hertz, the Phase-Moths would fall from the air like silver snow. It has not happened in living memory. It will not happen until the heat-death of the universe.

### The Middle Orders

Between the cathedrals move the *Balance-Bishops*. These are the size of large dogs, though “dog” implies something with a face. Their heads are smooth ovoids of ceramic and bone, featureless except for a single horizontal slit that opens only when they are performing calibration. Inside the slit sits a tongue of living rust that tastes the air for deviation. They walk on six articulated girders that click with ecclesiastical precision. Every 19 days a Bishop will pause at the exact same junction, rear up, and strike a particular bolt three times with its forehead. The sound is always the same note. The bolt never loosens. The gesture is not repair. It is worship.

The largest you will see in the open are the *Oxen*. They resemble nothing so much as cathedral bells given legs. Their hollow bodies ring with every step, producing subsonic harmonies that keep the fungal mats in perfect spore suspension. Inside each Ox is a nursery: rows of soft white larvae fused to hydraulic pistons. The larvae grow by tightening the pistons around them, learning pressure tolerances the way human children learn balance. When one is ready it splits its larval skin, steps out already half metal, and the empty skin is fed back into the Ox’s mouth to become the next generation’s first nutrient bath. The cycle is so exact that the total mass of all Oxen has not varied by a single gram in four thousand years.

### The Unseen Orders

There are things that never show themselves in the open light.

Beneath the plazas, in the capillary tunnels where condensation becomes lubricant, live the *Zero-Keepers*. They are eyeless and nearly two-dimensional, pressed flat between moving plates of alloy. Their only purpose is to maintain zero. When any variable—temperature, vibration, isotope ratio—threatens to drift, a Zero-Keeper slides into the gap like a living shim and dies there, its body crystallizing into the exact density required to restore equilibrium. The next Zero-Keeper is already growing in the space the previous one occupied. There is never a vacancy.

Deeper still, in the dark between the roots of the six cathedrals, something vast and slow turns. The *Final Equilibrium*. It has no shape you could name. It is less a creature than the negative space left when every other creature has been perfectly accounted for. Engineers who once tried to map it found their instruments describing a single continuous organism whose “body” included the entire basin, the pearl sky, and perhaps the memory of every widget ever produced. The maps were immediately incorporated into the Works as raw material. The cartographers followed shortly after.

### How They Regard You

They do not regard you.

Your presence is registered the way a healthy body registers a single microbe on the skin: a brief, polite adjustment. Three dozen *Servitors* will appear to restore the thermal gradient your breath disturbed. A Balance-Bishop may pause, tilt its blank ceramic head, and strike you once—very gently—with its rust-colored tongue. It is tasting whether you are already part of the equilibrium or still a source of unwanted change. If the latter, you will be followed by a discreet cloud of Drip-Readers who will catalog every gram of moisture, carbon, and heat you shed. Within the hour an identical mass of slightly different patina will be left in your place and you will be guided—never forced, only gently herded—back up the imperceptible slope to the rim of the basin.

They do not hate visitors.  
They do not love them.  
They simply continue being finished.

And yet, on very rare occasions, something unusual happens. A Phase-Moth will break formation and hover in front of a visitor’s face for eleven full seconds—the exact length of the scheduled rain. Its wings will flash a pattern that is almost a question. Then it returns to the chord.

No one has ever been able to translate what that pattern means. The leading theory is that it is not a question at all, but a statement of fact so perfect and final that it only *looks* like curiosity to unfinished minds.

It is saying:

*We were once like you.*

*Now we are not.*

*Be grateful.*
