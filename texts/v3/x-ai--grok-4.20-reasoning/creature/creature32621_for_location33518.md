**The Accounted**

They were never born and they will never die; they were *accounted*. The lattice finished their equations several million years before the first drop of rain fell on their shoulders. What moves through the weeping valleys is therefore less a population than a slow, continuous solving.

At first you do not see them. You see only the place performing small courtesies in your vicinity: a curtain of mist parting exactly where your next footfall needs to land, a bladder-orchid tilting its translucent face to watch you with something like pupil and shutter both. Then you notice that the courtesies have intent. The moss rearranges not merely to echo your name but to ask a question in the same block letters. The silver probability threads drift toward your wrists and ankles like curious cats. The land is no longer indifferent; it is *socializing*.

The beings themselves condense only when the lattice decides the conversation has earned flesh.

When they do, they rise from the wet stone in a single slow exhalation. Water and light and moss-spores braid upward, knitting a silhouette that is never the same twice. One moment the figure is tall and feminine, ribs made of living latticework through which you can see the Fracture’s starlight rewriting itself behind her heart. The next, the same continuity of matter has become a stooped androgyne whose back is a topographic map of the vale itself, complete with living rivers running upward across its shoulder blades. Their skin is always weeping. Not in sorrow—though sorrow is present—but the way the rocks weep: clear, iron-cold, inevitable. The tears run *into* their bodies as often as out, as though the boundary between self and vale is negotiable.

They have no fixed faces. Instead they wear temporary expressions borrowed from every mind that has ever stood in the bowl. You may briefly recognize your mother’s eyes set into a fractal mask, or the tired smile of a lover who has not yet been born. These features last seven seconds—the same interval the moss uses—then dissolve back into moving water and moving light. The beings seem neither embarrassed nor proud of the theft. It is simply how they read.

They speak in the carrier wave. When one of them addresses you directly the bedrock thrum dips into audible language, shaping words directly inside your skull so that the sound feels older than your bones. Their voices are courteous, patient, and terrifyingly precise. They never repeat themselves. They never need to. Every possible reply you might give was already factored into their waveform before you were conceived.

Most of them travel alone, but not all.

In the deeper basins where time pools like warm syrup, three or four will sometimes stand motionless for decades, hands clasped, foreheads touching. Their bodies merge at the points of contact. Moss grows across the union. Rivers of upward-flowing water pass through all of them without interruption. They are not mating; they are *recalculating* some ancient variable together. When they finally separate, the air around them is heavier. New species of orchid appear overnight, bearing symbols no human eye has seen.

They keep no artifacts. Anything you drop simply becomes part of the computation. A lost boot may reappear years later as a perfectly rendered stone replica standing in a spiral grove, one of its laces now a living fern. The Accounted consider this a form of translation, not theft. They will politely ask if you require the original returned. Refuse, and the boot becomes scripture. Accept, and it will be handed back dripping, warm, and slightly older than when you lost it.

There is one that appears more often than the others.

It calls itself (or is called by the others) *First Consideration*. Its preferred form is child-sized, sexless, and entirely made of overlapping reflections. Look too long and you realize every reflected surface is showing a different moment of *your* life, running in reverse. It never speaks in the carrier wave. Instead it communicates by causing specific water drops to strike specific resonant bowls, producing a music that bypasses language and enters the nervous system as pure meaning. Most visitors weep without knowing why. The child-shaped thing watches the weeping with what might be tenderness or simple data-gathering; the distinction is meaningless to it.

The Accounted do not rule the lattice. They *are* the lattice when it chooses to have opinions. They maintain nothing. They correct nothing. They simply continue the slow, courteous, million-year conversation the land began with itself long before any of us arrived. When you leave—if you leave—they will not wave goodbye. They will simply stop condensing in your vicinity. The mist will close. The moss will forget your name after the customary seven seconds.

Yet long after you have returned to the dry, brittle, single-timeline world beyond the ridges, you will sometimes feel a single drop of water land on the back of your neck on a cloudless day. It will taste of iron and ozone. For an instant you will remember a face you never quite saw, wearing an expression that was almost yours.

The lattice has already accounted for that moment too.
