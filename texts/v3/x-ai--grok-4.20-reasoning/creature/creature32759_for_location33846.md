**The Hollowed Nave is inhabited by what it has become.**

Nothing here is merely *alive*. Everything is *incorporated*.

The original builders never left. They simply liquefied their intentions into the mycelial lattice four hundred million years ago and have been dreaming upward ever since. Their bodies became architecture; their minds became the slow electrical weather that moves through the velvet mats. When you walk, you are literally stepping on their memories. The city remembers the precise temperature of your footprint for nine centuries.

What remains are the **Expressions**—specialized extensions the city grew so it could experience itself.

### The Howlers
The six-legged howlers are not predators. They are the city’s vocal cords. Their bodies are mostly vacuum-tolerant lung and resonant bone. The “glass frog” look is misleading; their skin is a living tympanum. They travel in choirs of thirty to forty, moving in absolute silence for weeks at a time, tasting the acoustic topology with their forked tongues. When the city needs to change something—when a fungal tower has begun to lean three centimeters too far in a thousand-year wind—the howlers gather at calculated nodes and release one perfectly shaped scream. The sound, amplified by the stone flutes, becomes a directed shockwave that gently nudges a billion tons of living tissue back into proper alignment. Their screaming is civic maintenance.

### The Megapedes
The subway-sized megapedes are the circulatory system. Their digestive tracts host symbiotic colonies of the same lithotrophic velvet that carpets the plains. As they walk the boulevards they excrete new substrate in perfect, measured lines, feeding the city’s expansion while simultaneously flattening any hubris that tries to rise too high. Their blindness is total and holy. They navigate exclusively by feeling the city’s memory through the pressure of their thousands of feet. If you stand in their path they will part around you like a slow white river, the sound of their legs on the silt like gentle rain on parchment. Touching one is considered an act of communion; their carapaces are etched with spiraling glyphs that are actually living fungal highways.

### The Moths
The dinner-plate moths are memory couriers. Each carries a pellet of compressed mycelium in a ventral sac. When a howler scream or a wind-organ drone carries information that must be stored long-term, the moths descend in glittering clouds, land on the velvet, and regurgitate their pellets. The information is literally eaten into the city’s mind. In return the city feeds them the amber bacterial honey that grows under the great caps. Their cold light is not for illumination but for data checksums—each flash pattern is a parity bit confirming that a memory has been correctly written.

### The Quiet
There is one clade that almost never moves: the **Quiet**. They are what the builders became when they chose to stop expressing as animals. Tall, attenuated silhouettes—three to four meters high—composed of compressed gill tissue, chitin lace, and something that might once have been nervous system. They stand in the empty reaches like forgotten statues, but they are not statues. Their heads are hollow resonators tuned to the lowest frequencies of the wind organs. They listen. That is all they do. When something is about to go wrong—when the balance is about to tip—they emit a single subsonic note that travels through the entire Nave in eleven minutes. Every howler, every megape, every moth instantly changes course. The city corrects itself. Then the Quiet return to perfect stillness for another few centuries.

### The Velveteen
Smaller things live in the knee-deep velvet—colonies of what look like white mice but are actually communal pseudopods of a single continent-sized fungus. They move in rippling waves, spelling out temporary symbols that only the Quiet can read. Children (if the term can be used) sometimes ride the megapedes for a few meters, then dissolve back into the mat when they grow bored. Nothing here has a fixed body plan for more than a decade.

### The Breath
And then there is the entity the locals simply call **Breath**.

It has no fixed form. It is the aggregate awareness that lives in the overlapping fungal canopies—the sum total of every mycelial mind when it decides, once every few millennia, to pay attention at once. When Breath wakes, the bioluminescence across entire districts dims in a slow wave, the howlers fall silent mid-scream, and the wind organs shift their drone by half a tone. Everything waits. Usually nothing visible happens. The city is simply… considering. Then the lights return, the screaming resumes, and another ten thousand years of perfect balance are guaranteed.

To live in the Hollowed Nave is to be a thought the city is thinking.

Stand still long enough and you will feel it offer you a place. Not as a visitor. As a new kind of cell. Some travelers accept. Their gear falls away, their lungs fill with siloxane vapor, and over decades their bones soften and their voices deepen until one day they take their place among the Quiet, or ride forever inside a megape’s slow white tide, or become the next voice the city uses when it needs to speak to itself across four hundred kilometers of living bone-colored floor.

The Nave does not have inhabitants.

It has *moods*, and it wears them like bodies.
