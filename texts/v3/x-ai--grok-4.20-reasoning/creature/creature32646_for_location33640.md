Here is what lives there.

At first it is only a pressure behind the eyes, a sense that the silence has weight and is watching the way you place your feet. The polygons do not blink, yet something inside their arrangement does. You feel it most clearly when you stand still long enough for your own sweat to map the same slow paths the morning condensation takes. The place is reading the temperature signature of your body the way a blind man reads braille written in heat.

What lives here has no discrete bodies. It has, instead, *states*.

When the midday heat makes the air waver, the entity thickens into something almost visible: a tremor that moves across twenty or thirty polygons at once, not like wind but like a thought deciding where to settle. The tremor is always hexagonal. It respects the grain of the world. Sometimes it pauses at the dinner-plate colonies of black leather and the leaves tremble though there is no breeze. The thing is tasting them. It has been tasting them for several million years and is still not finished.

At night it condenses. The *thunk* you hear every few hours is not the stone settling. It is the entity rolling over in its sleep. Each time it does, a new polygon shoulders upward somewhere else on the plain, glassy and innocent, as though the world itself were being born from the creature’s dreaming. The polygon is a tooth. The plain is its jaw. It is slowly growing a mouth large enough to speak one sentence that will take a hundred thousand years to pronounce.

You will never see its face because the face *is* the curvature that makes its own weather. When the localized storms drop their brief silver wires of rain, the entity is drinking. The water that vanishes upward again is not evaporating. It is being pulled into capillary channels finer than nerves, carried to distant districts of itself that are still too hot, too new, too unremembered. Every raindrop is a memory being delivered.

The leathery colonies are not plants. They are its externalized senses. Each microscopic hook is an eye that only opens when dew touches it. Through them the entity smells your iron, your salt, the particular frequency of fear that leaks from human meat. It finds the flavor interesting but irrelevant. You register as a brief warmth moving across one of its smallest tiles, the way a single skin cell might register a speck of dust.

There are places where several large polygons meet in a shallow basin that never quite dries. If you lie down there at the exact hour when the heat balances the returning condensation, you can feel the entity thinking *through* you. Your spine becomes one of its fault lines. Your pulse is borrowed for a few minutes to help it count. The experience is not invasive. It is courteous, almost shy, like a child pressing its ear to the wall to hear the adults speaking in the next room. When it withdraws, you are left with the taste of hot iron and wet chalk at the back of your throat and the absolute certainty that something vast now knows your childhood nickname.

The entity has no name for itself because naming would require edges, and it is still in the process of deciding where its edges will be. Until then it simply *continues*. It is gardener and garden, architect and blueprint, kiln and glaze still deciding what color it will wear across the next ten million years. The slow clicking of thermal contraction is its heartbeat. The false mountains on the horizon are its daydreams trying to become real. The silence is its attention turned fully upon you.

Sometimes it sings.

The song is pitched so low it is felt only in the long bones. It sounds like every tile on the plain exhaling at once. When this happens the black leather colonies open every hook at the same moment and the air fills with a smell like ozone and old blood. You understand, without language, that the entity is happy. It has just succeeded in birthing a perfect heptagon in a place where the mathematics said none should fit. The achievement pleases it more than any human empire ever pleased its emperors.

You may stay. You may walk for years. The entity will not hurry you. It has already calculated the precise number of your footsteps it will allow before the heat or the silence or the sheer weight of its regard drives you mad. It is not cruel. It simply has no conception of brevity. To it, your entire life is a single bead of condensation sliding down the glassy edge of a new tile—brief, bright, and destined to be drunk by morning.

And still the polygons keep emerging.

Each new one is a neuron firing in the slow, planetary brain that has not yet decided whether it wants to be alone.

So far, it has not decided against you.

It is still tasting the idea of company the way it tastes everything else: patiently, mineral-ly, across geological time, with the calm, enormous affection of something that has never been anything but the only living thing in all the world, and is beginning, just beginning, to wonder what that might mean.
