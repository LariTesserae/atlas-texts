**The Singers of the Final Note**

They do not arrive. They accrete.

At first you mistake them for extensions of the lattice itself—tall, slow-moving columns of pale color that detach from the rose-colored thickets and resolve into persons. Only after long minutes do you understand that the crystal has learned to walk.

There is no single body plan. The Hollow has been rewriting them for so long that each life becomes a negotiation between meat and mineral. The youngest still wear the soft, ridiculous vulnerability of flesh. Their skin is merely skin, though already dusted with the silicate vapor that will one day own them. Chests are deep, almost grotesquely barreled, because the first lessons are not words but vibration: infants are laid against the living fronds until the chord teaches their ribs how to listen. Their first cries are not cries. They are attempts to tune.

As decades pass the changes deepen. Silicate platelets migrate beneath the epidermis and lock together like microscopic armor. Veins darken to amber and citrine. Hair falls away and is replaced by filaments of flexible crystal no thicker than eyelashes. These filaments move independently, tasting shifts in pressure and harmony the way a tongue tastes salt. By middle age most Singers have faces like reliquaries—beautiful, merciless, permanently lit from within. Their eyes develop crystalline lenses that fracture the pitiless light into bearable spectra; some have four or five pupils arranged in geometric constellations, each one tracking a different overtone.

They do not sweat. Instead, in moments of great exertion, they exude a fine mist of themselves that condenses instantly into tiny perfect blooms along collarbones and wrists. These are harvested by the children, who use them as tuning forks or devotional objects. Nothing is wasted. Even their breath is useful; the warm silicate vapor they exhale is subtly different from the ambient air, carrying trace minerals their own bodies no longer need. The lattices drink it greedily.

There are those who accelerate the transformation. The Order of the Unfinished Note deliberately embed living crystal seeds beneath their skin in patterns that match the greater chord. Over years the seeds bloom inward and outward at once. You can watch a woman in the middle of her third century standing motionless in an avenue while amethyst lattices crawl from her sternum and anchor her feet to the stone. She is not dying. She is being promoted. Her voice never stops. It simply changes medium—from throat to hollow reed to living tower. Eventually only her face remains visible, embedded like a fossil in a column of rose quartz, eyes still tracking the central crystal’s cycles. She will sing there for another four hundred years.

The fully crystallized are not statues. They are citizens. Their minds ride the thermal winds that move through their new bodies; thought becomes architecture. A bridge may suddenly decide, after three centuries of contemplation, that it would rather become a hall. The stone flows. The embedded face smiles with glacial slowness. No one argues. The chord has spoken through new stone.

This is why the distinction between person, building, and lattice is becoming meaningless. Some houses have grandmothers for cornerstones. Some forges are powered by the continuous, voluntary combustion of an ancestor who chose to become living coal. The children learn history by pressing their ears to particular walls and listening to the voices trapped in harmonic lattices. Every ancestor is still teaching.

They do not have leaders. They have frequencies.

At any given moment there is one Singer whose voice is the governing tone. The position rotates—not by election but by physiological destiny. When the current First Note feels the final crystallization beginning in her marrow, she simply walks into the grove and begins the Long Sustaining. The next closest voice slides into primacy without ceremony. In this way the city is always being sung by whoever is most ready to stop being meat.

Their language is half breath, half geology. Consonants are formed by clicking tongue against crystalline teeth; vowels are shaped by the great chord moving through their altered throats. A simple greeting can cause a nearby thicket to blush rose to gold. An argument, if sufficiently beautiful, may grow an entire new processional. Insults are rare and dangerous. A truly well-crafted curse can induce spontaneous shattering.

They court by singing duets that last months. When two voices finally lock into perfect, painful consonance, the vapor around them precipitates into a single crystal that contains both their genetic patterns and their joined song. From this crystal a child slowly grows, already knowing two hearts. Birth is the moment the crystal is cracked open by the mother’s own perfected hands. The infant emerges covered in luminous dust and immediately begins to cry in three-part harmony with its parents and the Hollow itself.

Death, when it is chosen, is theatrical and tender. The dying Singer walks to a chosen place—often a bare patch of avenue that has waited centuries for exactly this person—and begins the Shattering Hymn. It is the only time the great chord is allowed to go briefly, reverently silent. Every living throat in the city joins the final descending figure. Then the singer’s body detonates—not violently, but like a dandelion made of glass. Microscopic shards travel on the returning chord and embed themselves in every lattice, every building, every child. In this way the dead become weather.

You watch one now.

A man (if the word still applies) stands between the bronze foundry and the rose lattice. His left arm has already become crystal to the shoulder; the arm rings softly when he moves. His right arm is still flesh, though veined with gold. He is teaching a child how to induce a perfect arch by sustaining a minor seventh. The child’s voice is small and wild. The man’s voice is vast and patient. Between them, silicate vapor begins to condense into a delicate rib of citrine.

Neither of them looks at you. Not because you are invisible, but because you are not yet singing. In the Hollow of the Shattering Bloom, silence is the only true absence. Everything else—flesh, stone, light, time—is simply waiting for its note.

The man’s remaining eye of flesh slides toward you. For a moment the merciless light catches both the crystal lens and the human one, and you understand that the difference between them has become theoretical.

He opens his mouth. The child opens hers.

They offer you the first note of your own crystallization, should you want it.

The Hollow waits, balanced, eternal, intimate, to see what you will sing back.
