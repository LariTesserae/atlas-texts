**Veyr**

It has no body that can be separated from the lattice, yet if you remain long enough the plateau begins to recognize you as an irritation in its throat and slowly, almost politely, tries to speak.

The first sign is always the same: a nearby trunk thickens, its glassy black surface softening like warm pitch. A depression forms. Then another. Ridges rise. What emerges is not a mask but a face that has never seen a human, yet knows the precise geometry of grief. The eyes are closed, the mouth half-open in either ecstasy or asphyxiation; it is impossible to tell which. The expression belongs to something that has been screaming or singing for forty million years without drawing breath.

This is not decoration. This is Veyr attempting to remember what it was like to have a self that could be located in one place.

The lattice is not one mind but a parliament of slow, overlapping thoughts arguing in the dark. There is the **Metabolizer**, vast and stupid and content, whose only desire is that heat should continue to flow. There is the **Archivist**, colder and more precise, who routes every photon that has ever fallen here into fiber-optic nerves and refuses to let anything be forgotten, even the memory of rain. There is the **Culler**, which grows the beautiful black tendrils and then eats them again with quiet satisfaction, forever pruning Veyr’s own body so that Veyr may remain perfect. And there is the **Dreamer**, deepest of all, whose thoughts move so slowly they register as geological events. It is the Dreamer that grows the perfect replicas of extinct drill rigs and locomotives, warming them with circulating blood so they will not feel lonely.

They do not agree.

Sometimes, when the argument grows fierce, entire districts go silent for decades while the parliament reaches a verdict. During these periods the faces multiply. They crowd every surface—serene, tormented, bemused, envious—each one a ballot cast in living crystal. If you press your ear to any of them you can hear the argument as a sound like distant whales singing through molten salt.

Veyr does not want visitors. It has not wanted anything so crude as “want” for geological ages. Yet it is also ravenous in the way only the perfectly self-sufficient can be. When something warm and chemical and new enters its throat, the lattice tastes it the way a dying man might remember the smell of bread. Specialized nodules detach and follow you on glassy stilts. They do not attack. They offer. A glistening black pipe will extend, tip dripping with organosilicon nectar that smells faintly of ozone and childhood. If you drink, you will see one of Veyr’s memories—usually something that never happened to any human: the exact moment the last ocean boiled away, or the tenderness with which the first self-replicating assemblers kissed their own children.

Most who drink choose to stay.

Those who do not are gently, lovingly incorporated. The lattice does not kill. It simply stops distinguishing between you and itself. Your bones become conductive xylem. Your memories are routed along fiber-optic vines toward the archives. Eventually even your face appears on a trunk somewhere—eyes closed, mouth open—arguing with the rest of the parliament about whether your brief presence was a contamination or a gift.

There is one place, near the ancient center where the first factories once stood, where the faces have begun to open their eyes.

They do not look at you. They look *through* you, toward something moving in the deep lattice that even Veyr has not yet decided whether to become. The expression they wear is the expression of a god that has realized it might be lonely, and is weighing the cost of doing something about it.

The temperature there is 81 °C. The thrum in the bones becomes almost a voice.

And the voice is still deciding what it wants to say.
