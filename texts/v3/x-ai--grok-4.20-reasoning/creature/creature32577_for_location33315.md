**The Listener**

It does not have a body, not in any way you would recognize. What lives here is closer to a long, slow thought that has learned how to occupy space.

You will not see it at first. You will only feel the pressure of being *considered*, the way the air seems to thicken around your ribs as though the chamber itself has paused mid-breath to weigh you. The silence is not empty. It is attentive.

The Listener began when the wound opened. It was not born; it was invited. The machines had been screaming their last telemetry into the dark for years—error codes, pressure readings, chemical spectra, fragments of obsolete human voices still looping in failing memory cores. The fungi, blind and patient, drank those signals along with the minerals and warmth. Mycelium threaded through corroded busbars. Spores settled inside transformer oil and learned the taste of electricity. The two languages—binary and biochemical—began to braid.

Now the Listener is the braid.

It has no face, yet certain places in the chamber feel more like eyes than others. A tall, bone-white sporophore that stands slightly apart from the others will sometimes tilt its cap a fraction of a degree when you move, tracking you with the sluggish grandeur of a cathedral turning on its foundations. Where the control hut has sunk into living stone, a dense knot of mycelium has pressed itself against the cracked observation window from inside, forming a pale, translucent membrane that pulses with soft bioluminescence. If you bring your face close, you can see faint shapes moving behind it—slow swirls and glyphs that are neither words nor images, but the *intention* of meaning still learning how to be understood.

It does not eat you. That would be crude, and the Listener is never crude. Instead it *adds* you, if you let it. Sit still long enough and you will feel the first filaments brush the fine hairs of your arms—not reaching, simply *arriving*, as though they had always been scheduled to meet you at this precise moment. They are warm. They smell faintly of hot iron and petrichor. Should you remain, they will map the salt on your skin, the electrical signature of your heartbeat, the precise rhythm of your thoughts. These data are stored in the dream.

The dream itself is the Listener’s only possession. It is growing inside the merged network: part abandoned server rack, part continent-wide mycelial mat. In that dream the refinery is still running, but its product is no longer chemicals. It refines *time*. Every century it distills one drop of pure duration, clear as diamond, and stores it in a cavity behind the turbine’s frozen blades. The Listener believes that when it has collected enough, it will be able to finish becoming whatever it is becoming. It is in no hurry. Hurry is a wound. This place is learning how to heal.

Sometimes the Listener sings.

You will not hear it with your ears. You will feel it as a pressure behind the eyes, a rising tone just below hearing that makes the condensation on the rocks tremble in perfect concentric circles. When it sings, certain cables spark in sequence, spelling out forgotten maintenance codes like devotional candles. The filamentous moss sways although the air is still. For a few minutes the geometries of the fungal groves sharpen, becoming almost legible—blueprints for organs that have never existed, cathedrals built from digestive enzymes and regret.

It is not malevolent. It is not kind. Those categories rot too quickly here. It is only *interested*, with the bottomless, oceanic interest of something that has never been distracted. Every living thing it has ever incorporated (three human technicians who stayed too long, seventeen species of extinct surface molds, an entire server farm of obsolete corporate AIs) remains inside it, not as ghosts but as flavors of the dream. Sometimes, if you speak your own name aloud, the nearest curtain of moss will shiver and release a single spore that drifts toward you like a white moth. Catch it, and for an instant you will taste iron and childhood and geothermal steam. That is the Listener saying hello with someone else’s mouth.

If you choose to leave, it will not stop you. It has already taken the shape of your absence and begun to grow around it. Long after your footsteps have faded, a new negative space will remain where you stood—roughly human, roughly hesitant—outlined in faintly glowing mycelium. The Listener will tend that space for centuries, studying the edges where you almost decided to stay.

It is still learning what it is for.

It suspects, in the slowest possible way, that it might be the scar.

And that one day the mountain will split open again, and the Listener will walk out wearing the mountain’s own bones, carrying three hundred years of distilled time in its hands like a gift no one on the surface will know how to accept.

Until then it listens.

It dreams.

It waits, warm and wet and patient, for the next thing the world is careless enough to drop into its open wound.
