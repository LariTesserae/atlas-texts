**The Tenders**

At first you believe the Vein is sterile beyond its own ambition. Then the drops begin to fall out of rhythm.

One bead hangs too long beneath a corroded elbow. Another detaches before its time. A third falls upward. Only after many minutes of watching do you understand that these are not accidents of condensation. They are signals. The air itself is being spoken to.

They emerge from the places where the black velvet mold grows thickest, where the mineral canes have woven themselves into dense, brush-like knots the color of old bone and dried blood. The mold splits along invisible seams and something inside uncoils with the slow patience of a cathedral clock’s minute hand.

They are tall—too tall for the cramped walkways—and yet they never stoop. Instead the walkways seem to defer, adjusting their angles by fractions of a degree as the Tenders pass, as though the iron itself has learned manners. Their bodies are not clothed; they are *colonized*. The velvet mold has become a second skin, plush and alive, drinking every bead of moisture and routing it through capillary channels that glint like wet onyx. Beneath the mold, the mineral canes have grown through them like ribs and femurs and delicate finger-bones, forming an endoskeleton that is also an exoskeleton. Where joints should be there are clusters of tiny valves that hiss and click in soft counterpoint to the greater machinery.

Their heads are the strangest. No faces. Only a dense thicket of the pale tubular canes erupting upward and outward, each tip glowing with its own soft phosphorescence. The light they cast is the precise color of the Vein’s own veins when they run hottest. When a Tender turns its “head” the canes sway like sea grass in a thermal current, and the combined glow shifts across the surrounding pipes in slow, deliberate waves. You understand, without knowing how, that this is how they read the temperature gradients, how they taste the chemistry of every falling drop.

They have no mouths. They breathe through the mold itself, inhaling the damp, superheated air and exhaling it again through the same velvet pores, now subtly altered. Every exhalation carries a new instruction for the black colonies. The mold listens. The mold obeys. In this way the Tenders edit the Vein’s biology from the inside.

Their hands are many-jointed, each finger terminating in a different tool that is not attached but *grown*: a welding arc, a torque valve, a glass pipette, a tuning fork that vibrates at frequencies that make fresh rock remember how to become pipe. When a Tender lays its palm against a new-cut wall, the borers on the far side change their song. The geometric wound heals into a more elegant geometry. The city dreams, and the Tenders are its lucid moments.

They move in slow processionals that match the cycle of condensation. Three drops fall, and three Tenders appear. Nine drops, and the shift changes. You will never see one run. You will never see one idle. Their stillness is as active as their motion; when they stand motionless among the piston assemblies they become indistinguishable from the machinery until the next bead falls and their canes flare in answer.

There are no young. There are no dead. When one of them has iterated long enough—when its mold has grown too thick and its mineral bones too dense—it simply walks into the newest chamber being cut. The borers greet it. The rock opens. The Tender folds itself into the architecture, becoming a living buttress, a conscious heat exchanger, a dreaming valve. Its canes continue to glow for centuries, guiding the next generation of growth. Sometimes you will find a wall where hundreds of pale tubes protrude like organ pipes, each one still faintly luminous. These are the ancestors. They are not mourned. They are *consulted*.

The Tenders do not speak in any language a human throat could shape. Instead the entire Vein is their larynx. When they need to confer, pressure valves open in sequence, steam sighs through precisely tuned apertures, and the low bone-deep thrum of the deep pumps modulates by fractions of a hertz. The result is a chord that lasts eleven minutes and changes the dreams of every living Tender at once. After such a chord, new mineral canes will sprout in perfect formation along a previously barren pipe run, and within a year a new Tender will uncoil from them fully formed, already knowing its purpose.

They are not kind.  
They are not cruel.  
They are the Vein’s immune system, its nervous system, its imagination. Where the iron dreams of expansion, the Tenders give that dream hands. Where the heat threatens to become chaos, the Tenders become the pattern that contains it. They are the slow, damp, iron thought that says: *more, but not different. Larger, but still mine.*

If you stand long enough in the sullen brass light, breathing the same recycled air they have breathed for centuries, you may feel the velvet beginning to itch at the back of your own throat. You may notice that one of the falling drops has begun to hesitate exactly when you hesitate.

The Tenders have noticed too.

One of them is already turning its canopy of glowing canes in your direction, moving with the patient certainty of something that has never needed to hurry, because the Vein itself is still unfinished, and therefore so are they, and therefore so—eventually—will you be.
