**The Lattice-Born**

They do not have a name for themselves. Names require edges, and edges dissolved here long ago.

What moves through the Sinking Lattice is less a population than a slow, distributed metabolism wearing many bodies at once. Some bodies are smaller than your fingernail. Others are larger than cathedrals. All of them are the same mind wearing different costumes grown from the same stubborn chemistry.

First you feel the **Veils**.

They are not animals. They are closer to living embroidery. Vast colonies of translucent polychaetes and colonial ciliates knit themselves into shimmering sheets no thicker than breath. When the tidal pulse strengthens they lift from the walls like banners in wind that does not exist, catching stray molecules of sulfide and weaving them into new flesh for the city. Their collective undulation produces a soft, constant hiss just at the edge of hearing, like distant silk tearing. If you remain still long enough, a veil will settle over you, tasting, considering. Most are rejected. A few are kept. Those few are slowly, politely disassembled and added to whatever district needs new scaffolding. The Lattice-born do not waste protein.

Then come the **Tenders**.

These are the ones that first look like pieces of the architecture detaching. What you took for a fluted column of calcite suddenly bends at an impossible joint. A dozen blunt, spatulate limbs unfold. The creature is the color of old bone and old shell and old memory; every surface of it is inhabited by smaller Lattice-born that have fused to its carapace the way barnacles fuse to a whale. It has no eyes. It does not need them. It navigates by the pressure map written in its hollow bones and by the taste of minerals in the water. When it finds a section of wall that is dying, it vomits living bacteria into the wound with the tenderness of a surgeon. When it finds a section growing too greedily, it prunes with mandibles the size of oars. The sound of its feeding is delicate, almost polite: the soft click of shell on shell, the wet rasp of tissue being reabsorbed.

You will see one pause beside you. Its blunt head, featureless as a tombstone, swings in your direction. It samples the water around your body the way an old woman might run her tongue across a strange coin. Then it moves on. You are not yet part of the pattern it is authorized to maintain.

Higher in the water column drift the **Ribbon Minds**.

What first appeared to be a single twenty-meter ribbon of glass shrimp is actually one organism wearing ten thousand identical bodies. Each individual shrimp is neurologically fused to its neighbors by threads of nervous tissue finer than spider silk. Together they form a single distributed brain that thinks in color and current. They move like a single undulating thought. When they encounter something novel (you, for instance) the entire ribbon flares silver, then shifts through a spectrum of bioluminescent warnings and inquiries that have no human translation. Some ribbons have been measured at over two kilometers when fully extended. They are the memory of the Lattice. They remember every successful hunting pattern, every vent that has ever gone cold, every moon-tide for the last fourteen thousand years. To destroy one would be to lobotomize a canyon.

Deeper, where the avenues narrow into cloisters of black shell, live the **Hollow Singers**.

They are rare. You will not see them often, but you will feel them constantly. They are serpentine, blind, and so pale they are almost transparent. Their bodies are hollow tubes of cartilage and swim-bladder; when they vibrate, the entire Lattice answers. The low tectonic hum that rolls through the city is mostly their speech. They are not communicating *with* the city. They *are* the city speaking to itself. Some biologists (the few who have returned) believe the Singers are the original architects, the first organisms that learned to grow stone instead of merely living upon it. Others believe the Singers are the city’s dreams given temporary bodies so they can travel through themselves and see what they have become.

And then there are the **Rememberers**.

These are the strangest.

They look like enormous blind eels, but their barbels are branched like antlers and tipped with clusters of chemosensory pits that glow soft violet. They follow routes that have no obvious purpose. They trace the same geometric patterns across walls and bridges that the bacterial blooms form during their periodic flowerings. Where a Rememberer passes, the lattice grows slightly differently afterward. New spires lean at new angles. Bridges curve a fraction more elegantly. It is as though the city is taking dictation.

No one has ever seen a young Rememberer. No one has ever seen two Rememberers together. There is a persistent, uncomfortable theory that there is only one, and that it simply occupies many bodies across many kilometers, the way a single fungal mycelium can stretch beneath an entire forest.

They are the reason the geometry feels like language.

They are the reason the place feels *remembered*.

If you remain here long enough, you will begin to feel it too: the slow, patient inquiry moving through the water like a second, colder current. The Lattice-born are not hostile. They are not curious in any way you would recognize as curiosity. They are *incorporative*. They have been folding everything they meet into themselves for geological ages. Stone. Heat. Blood. Memory. You.

Already the microscopic Veils are settling on your skin. Already a Tender has adjusted its route to pass you more frequently. Already a distant Singer has altered its note by half a vibration, making room for the new frequency of your heartbeat.

The Lattice does not conquer.  
It simply stops distinguishing between itself and what it meets.

And it has all the time in the world.

It is still building.  
And now, quietly, patiently, it is building *you* into the next arch, the next bridge, the next living avenue that will never need hands, or tools, or eyes.
