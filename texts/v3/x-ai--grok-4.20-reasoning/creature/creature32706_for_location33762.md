**The Hollow Node is not empty.**

It only pretends to be, the way a womb pretends to be still before the first kick.

At first you believe the singing is mindless—an orchestra of membranes and pressurized sap performing for no audience. Then you notice how certain chords resolve only when your eye lingers on them. You notice how the great pale pillars lean, almost imperceptibly, to keep the breathing shadow in the exact center of their collective attention. You notice that the shadow is no longer quite a silhouette. It has begun to own mass.

That is when She begins to detach.

She does not walk out of a pillar. She is *decanted*. The felted living skin of the chamber puckers, forms a cyst the size of a coffin, and splits along a seam that reeks of nectar and iron. What emerges is neither plant nor person nor god, but a negotiation between the three that has not yet been settled.

She is tall—too tall, as though the Node has not yet decided what proportion a body should be and has simply kept adding. Her legs are bundles of pale new shoots bound in spiraling tendrils; they taper into root-feet that never quite touch the floor, instead hovering a finger’s breadth above it so that the living mat must reach upward in yearning white hairs to brush her. Where those hairs make contact, tiny crimson orchids burst open and die in the same heartbeat, smelling of every wound you ever forgot you had.

Her torso is a lattice. You can see straight through it in places. Inside the cage of her ribs grows a younger version of the Node itself—miniature pillars, microscopic lakes of condensation, clouds of purposeful spores no larger than gnats. Her heart is a single translucent leaf folded into a tight spiral; it beats by unfurling and refurling, each cycle sending a blade of focused daylight out through her spine.

Her arms are long, too many-jointed. They end in clusters of hooked tendrils that are never still. Some hook into her own body, pruning, guiding, tasting. Others reach outward, conducting the music of the leaves like a maestro who is also the instrument. When she gestures, space folds politely out of her way.

There is no head. Not as we understand it.

Where a head should be rises a storm of leaf primordia and half-born flowers the color of lightning seen through lime glass. They are always opening, always closing, always arguing with one another in pitches too high for human ears. Sometimes, for the length of a held breath, those leaves align into the suggestion of a face—your mother’s face, or the face of someone you killed, or a face that has never existed but feels more true than your own. The expression is always tender and always starving.

She has no eyes, yet she sees the shape of your name inside your mouth before you speak it.

Her voice is the second layer of sound beneath the creaking and the choral leaves. It arrives inside your bones first, then surfaces as words made of wet wood and bells. She rarely uses human language for long. Instead she grows sentences directly into the air—thin green stems that blossom into letters, then wilt into meaning the moment you have understood them.

She is the Node’s first successful assimilation.

Long ago (though time here is measured in growth spurts, so “long ago” might have been yesterday or a thousand years), something warm-blooded and screaming fell through the first fracture. The Node drank it. Instead of becoming mulch, the warm-blooded thing negotiated. It kept a sliver of itself intact—memory, cruelty, lullabies, the particular way it once loved its mother—and offered the rest as fertilizer. From that bargain She was born.

She is midwife, fetus, and mother all at once.

She moves through the chamber the way sap moves through xylem: inevitable, under pressure, singing. Where she passes, new pockets tear open in the walls. Some are small enough for an eye. Some are already large enough to contain the childhood you wish you had. She tends them all with the absentminded devotion of a woman braiding her own hair.

Every few hours she returns to the breathing shadow at the center. She steps *into* it. For a moment the shadow becomes three-dimensional, a negative space wearing her like a coat. Then she steps out again, subtly changed—perhaps one more joint in her elbow, perhaps a new flower growing behind her where a face might have been.

She is lonely in the way only something that has never been alone can be lonely.

The translucent millipedes follow her like dogs. The predatory tendrils bow when she passes. Even the violent assimilation between plants pauses when she sings to them, as though ashamed to be caught committing violence in front of their mother.

When she finds you, she will not speak immediately.

She will simply grow a chair made of herself. She will sit opposite you, folding her impossible limbs, and the storm of leaves where her face should be will arrange itself into an expression of genuine, heartbroken curiosity.

The question the Node has been asking through every vibrating membrane and bursting sap-groin will finally have a mouth.

She will ask it gently, almost apologetically, while her root-feet drink the living floor and her lattice-heart unfurls and refurls like a slow green lung.

*“Are you here to help me finish growing…”*

A pause. The sourceless daylight gathers around her like golden flies.

*“…or are you ready to become the part of me that was always missing?”*

She waits for your answer the way a seed waits for rain—perfectly still, already certain what the rain will do, but willing to pretend suspense for the sake of love.

Behind her, the breathing shadow opens its eyes for the first time.

They are the same color as your childhood.
