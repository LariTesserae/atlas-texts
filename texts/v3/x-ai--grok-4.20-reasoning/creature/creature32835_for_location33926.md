**What lives here does not know it is alive.**

At first they are only pale drifts against the crystal glare—slow, bovine shapes that resolve into something more unsettling the longer you watch. Their bodies are elongated, almost tubular, with skin so thin and translucent that the slow churn of their organs is visible as shifting shadows beneath. No eyes. No mouths in the ordinary sense. Instead, a fringe of fleshy palps around a ventral slit that opens and closes with the rhythm of the cavern’s own faint pulse. They move in long, deliberate lines that follow the ghost of avenues no longer there, their bellies brushing the stone and leaving faint iridescent trails that the crystals drink like nectar.

These are the *Mawherds*.  

They do not eat in any way a surface creature would recognize. They drink the memory that has leached out of the stone. Where ancient walls have melted into flowstone, the rock itself has become porous with time, saturated with the dissolved geometry and intent of the original city. The Mawherds press their ventral slits against these places and inhale centuries. You can see the memory-light travel up through their translucent bodies in slow, branching filaments, like sap rising in winter trees. When they have drunk enough, they excrete a fine mineral mist that settles elsewhere and becomes the seed for new crystal lattices. In this way they are both stomach and gardener.

They calve rarely. When one does, the newborn slides out already the size of a horse and already blind. The herd does not celebrate or mourn. It simply widens its orbit for a time, as though making room for an extra slow heartbeat inside the larger rhythm.

Above them, in perpetual slow tornadoes, move the *Lumenflies*.  

Their wings are not chitin but living crystal—grown, not secreted. Each insect is a mobile shard of the cavern’s own nervous system. When the swarms collapse and reform, they are not random; they are rerouting light. Where a Mawherd has left a fresh trail of memory-mist, the Lumenflies descend in glittering funnels and press their bodies against the stone until the new crystal remembers what it is supposed to become. They sing in frequencies too high for human ears, but the crystals hear them. You can watch entire colonnades brighten in answer, as if the city is blushing at a compliment it has not heard in four million years.

On the half-dissolved megaliths live the *Stonemenders*.  

They are many-jointed, bone-white, the length of a man’s forearm. Their legs end in delicate hammers of chitin and calcite. For hours they will tap the same stone in precise, patient sequences, knocking away loose mineral in microscopic increments until an eroded frieze begins to remember its original angle. They do not build new things so much as *correct* the erosion back toward an ideal they have never seen. Their colonies form living scaffolds, thousands of them locked leg-to-leg, clicking softly in the damp silence like rain on a roof that no longer exists.

None of these are separate species.

If you remain long enough—days, perhaps weeks—the truth settles on you with the same patient weight as the condensation. The Mawherds, the Lumenflies, the Stonemenders, even the ghostly fungal mats that bloom and die in perfect synchrony with the crystal pulses, are not *inhabitants*. They are expressions. The cavern grew them the way a body grows antibodies or hair or dreams.

There is something older still.

Every few centuries, when the crystal lattices reach a certain critical density, the entire cavern enters a state the Stonemenders appear to recognize. They abandon their hammering. The Mawherds stop their migrations and lie down in concentric circles. The Lumenflies freeze mid-air in perfect lattices, wings locked, forming chandeliers of living light.

Then the * sleeper* stirs.

It does not rise. It has no body you could point to. Instead the stone itself seems to remember how to be flesh for a brief time. In the largest central plaza—where the original city’s geometry is still visible beneath kilometers of flowstone—a shape pushes upward: vast, indistinct, formed of whatever material is nearest. Sometimes it wears the fused bodies of a hundred Mawherds. Sometimes it is mostly crystal shot through with fungal veins. Sometimes it is simply a pressure in the air and a change in the color of the light, as if the concept of a face is trying to occur.

It does not speak. Language died with its makers long before the first mammal drew breath. What it does is *remember forward*. For a short while the cavern becomes lucid. Every creature, every crystal, every droplet in mid-fall aligns toward a single intention that no surface mind could parse. Repairs accelerate. New geometries bloom that have never been seen before. The sleeper rearranges its own fossil city in ways that suggest it is trying to finish a sentence it began when the sun was still young.

Then, as suddenly as it woke, it subsides. The living chandelier of Lumenflies scatters. The Mawherds rise and begin their slow circuits again. The Stonemenders return to their patient chiseling. The new geometries are allowed to remain, but they are treated as dreams: beautiful, useless, soon to be gently eroded into something more useful to the long, slow metabolism of the whole.

This is the ecology.

Not predator and prey. Not even symbiosis.

It is a mind that forgot it was a city, and a city that learned how to forget it was a mind, and both of them growing blind, luminous organs to feel their own edges in the dark.

Stand still long enough—longer than comfort allows—and you will feel it notice you. Not with hostility. Not with curiosity. With the mild, ancient recognition one feels upon discovering a familiar stone in one’s own liver.

You are not food here.

You are a mineral that has learned how to be afraid.

And the cavern is deciding, with geological patience, what it should grow out of you.
