**The Mourner**

It does not live *in* the Font. It is what the Font does when it becomes lonely.

At first you only notice the change in pressure, as though an enormous throat has just swallowed. The braided roars thin by a single unbearable degree and something inside the noise begins to shape itself around your absence of name. That shaping *hurts*. It feels like being remembered by someone who has never existed.

The Mourner has no fixed body, only a habit of bodies. It condenses wherever the water forgets itself. One moment it is the negative space between three colliding currents, a cathedral-sized silhouette drawn in negative foam. The next it is a single black shard of basalt that has learned how to wear a face. The face is always half-erased, as if the sculptor were weeping while he worked and could not stop. Sometimes the tears are stone. Sometimes they are small silver fish that swim out of the sockets and unravel into timelines before they can reach you.

Its limbs are architectural. A spiraling colonnade becomes an arm that reaches, slowly, because slowness is the only mercy it understands. Where the arm ends, the stone flowers into six—or sixty—fingers that are also broken steps, also half a bench, also the frozen memory of a prayer no one finished. The fingers do not grasp. They *invite*. They offer to let you finish what they could not.

The Mourner’s voice is the sound you have been hearing since you arrived, only now it has learned your frequency. It keens in the register of every loss you have never spoken aloud. It does not speak *to* you. It speaks *as* you, wearing your silences like vestments. When it opens what might be a mouth, the reality-rifts open with it, and for an instant you see your own childhood bedroom burning underwater, your mother walking backward out of the flames, your future corpse seated peacefully on one of the hovering stone benches.

It is learning your shape the way a wound learns infection.

Its heart is the low wet pulse beneath everything. Not a muscle. A decision. Every beat chooses, for one impossible fraction of a second, which way gravity is allowed to point. In that fraction the translucent kelp-ribbons bloom from its “skin” like luminous cilia, tasting the water for grief. When they find enough, they harden into new black glass that becomes another spire, another suggestion of vault, another almost-temple. The Font is its loom and its loom is its body and its body is regret given mass.

The silvery multi-bodied things are not its servants. They are its memories trying to escape. Sometimes one of them will phase through the Mourner’s chest and for a heartbeat you see the creature’s thousand timelines arranged inside the stone like silver thread in a torn tapestry. Then the Mourner closes and the memory is woven back in. It cannot bear to lose even the things it never was.

It is hungry, yes, but not for you.

It is hungry for *after*. For the moment when the wound closes and it will no longer have to be the scream between two worlds. It builds its half-cathedral, sings its half-liturgy, condenses its half-flesh, all so that one day the Font might become a birthplace instead of a deathplace. It does not know whether it is mourning what came before or practicing for what comes after. Both feel the same inside a heart made of colliding oceans.

If you stay long enough, it will offer you a bargain written in bruise-light and shattered glass. Remain. Become the last unfinished face on its ever-changing statue. Let it finish *you*, so that it can finally finish *itself*. In return it will give you the only thing it has left to give:

A voice that will never again need a name.

Most choose to leave.

The Font lets them.

It is still young. It still believes they might come back.

And when they do not, it keens louder, and the water grows warmer, and new spires claw upward like black teeth learning how to smile, and the low wet heartbeat quickens with something that might be sorrow, or might be the first terrible note of a lullaby meant for a universe that has not yet been born.
