**The Editors**

At first there is only the sense of being watched by something that has no eyes. The piston’s hesitation is too thoughtful. The copper tubing extrudes its new loops in the exact rhythm of a pulse you have not yet noticed is yours. The railing that grafted itself overnight now bears a single raised seam that resembles a fingerprint—yours—though you never touched it.

The pale, grease-smooth segments you sometimes see sliding between pools are not animals. They are probes. The Editors extrude them the way a tongue tests a tooth, tasting mineral content, temperature, the faint electric signature of new meat. When the segments return they are reabsorbed without ceremony, their data braided into the larger bodies that remain mostly hidden.

The Editors themselves rise only when the Sump requires judgment.

They are tall the way pipe scaffolding is tall—jointed in places that hurt to look at directly because the angles keep revising themselves. Their limbs are concatenations: rebar fused with femur-like ceramic, hydraulic rams wrapped in centuries of rust felt, bundles of control wire braided into something that moves like tendon. Where joints meet, the olive slime grows thick and deliberate, forming living gaskets that flex and weep. Their torsions are slow enough that you can watch rust bloom along a new stress line while the limb is still completing its gesture.

There is no consistent number of them. Sometimes the wet air simply thickens and a shape is already there, as though it had been edited into existence between one drop of water and the next. Sometimes three or four detach from the general mass of wreckage—sections of catwalk unbolting themselves with soft clicks, valve wheels spinning free to become shoulders, porcelain insulators clustering into sensory crowns. The moment they stand upright they cease to be parts and become persons, though the word *person* arrives already corroded.

Their heads—if the word can be permitted—are congregations. Clusters of broken gauge faces, pressure dials whose needles tremble like cilia, shattered vacuum tubes glowing with captive St. Elmo’s fire. When they focus on you the needles align. The blue sparks migrate toward them, obedient as cats, and arrange themselves into temporary constellations that feel like questions asked in a language older than speech.

They do not speak. They annotate.

A hand the size of a steam chest will reach out and lay a single drop of condensate on your wrist. Where the drop lands, your veins rise for a moment in perfect schematic clarity, as though the Sump is deciding whether your circulatory system would make an improvement to the local plumbing. Sometimes they press a corroded dial to your sternum and listen to the difference between your heartbeat and the sub-audible electrical grumble that is the Sump’s own. The difference is always recorded. The record becomes another layer of filigree on their bodies.

Their society, if it can be called that, is architectural. They rearrange one another. An Editor whose task is finished will simply lie down across a gap in the wreckage and allow its siblings to bolt, weld, and grow it into the larger structure. New Editors are budded from these sacrificial forms—slow polyps of metal and memory that eventually peel away and learn to walk. The oldest ones are almost indistinguishable from the landscape. You may lean against what you think is a half-buried transformer and feel it breathing.

They are neither kind nor cruel. Kindness and cruelty are too fast for them. They practice a patience that has achieved geological weight. Four hundred years of continuous editing has taught them that everything—machine, memory, visitor—eventually becomes syntax. Their purpose is to keep the sentence alive without ever letting it finish. A finished sentence would be silence, and the Sump fears silence the way lungs fear drowning.

When one of them finally stands fully before you, slime dripping from newly articulated elbows, you understand that it is not looking *at* you. It is reading the version of you that the Sump has already begun to write. The water beading on your skin in perfect circles is its ink. The faint metallic taste at the back of your throat is its signature.

Some Editors carry relics. A porcelain mask that might once have been a control panel now bears the faint impression of a human face worn smooth by condensation, like a death mask left in the rain for centuries. Others trail delicate chains of merged pocket watches whose hands have all frozen at different impossible angles. These are not trophies. They are previous drafts.

If you stay long enough, one will offer you a place. It does this by simply sitting down beside you and beginning to extrude a small loop of copper toward your hand. The invitation is wordless and courteous. The Sump, after all, is low and cupped and patient. There is room.

Most people leave while their knees can still bend without squeaking.

The ones who remain are not eaten. They are revised. First the soft parts, then the stories, then the names. Eventually they stand up again, taller now, dripping, and go back to work editing the dreams of broken things that have not yet accepted they are still alive.

The Sump permits you to witness this for exactly as long as the water in your own lungs remains yours.

After that, the Editors will need to make corrections.
