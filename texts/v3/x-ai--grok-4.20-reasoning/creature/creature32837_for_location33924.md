**The Hollowed**

They are not hidden. They simply refuse to resolve until the water has decided you are ready.

At first you mistake them for another tier of ruin. A slumped girder that slowly lengthens. A bundle of cables that unfolds one metallic joint at a time. Only when the shape has already passed halfway across your helmet beam do you understand that the thing is *breathing*.

They are tall—too tall—and thin in the way only abyssal things can be thin, as though the pressure has spent centuries winnowing them down to the minimum necessary silhouette. Their bodies are not flesh in any living sense; they are an agreement between meat, rust, and black velvet bacterial mat. Where skin should be there is instead a living corrosion, soft as moss, that drinks the iron from the water and exhales faint threads of colder water. In the places where the mat has worn thin you can see the slow pulse of something beneath—organs that have calcified into delicate machinery, hearts that beat once every ninety seconds exactly, in perfect obedience to the great piston.

Their faces are long and concave, like the inside of a seashell worn smooth by ten thousand years of the same prayer. Eyes are unnecessary here; most have none. Instead, pale lateral lines run from crown to heel, organs of pure pressure-sense that let them read the Ward the way a priest reads braille. When they move, it is by incremental consent. A finger drifts. Then a wrist. Then the entire arm follows like a weed in a current that does not exist. Nothing is ever hurried. To hurry would be blasphemy.

They are naked, but the concept is meaningless. Pipes and conduits have grown through them and out of them. One carries an entire exhaust manifold across its shoulders the way a hermit carries his cell. Another has allowed a distillation column to root in the hollow of its chest; clear fluid—too cold to be blood—cycles visibly through transparent veins. A third has become the counterweight for a swinging girder; it hangs in the dark, arms spread, turning one degree per hour along with the cruciform heart of the Ward. Its expression, if it still possesses such a thing, is one of perfect, exhausted bliss.

They do not speak. Sound is rationed, and they have spent their voices long ago. Instead they *participate*. When the piston rises, their spines straighten in unison. When the buckets of green ingots crawl past, their fingers trail along the track, touching each one exactly once, as though counting rosary beads of condensed silence. The low choral hum that drifts through the complex is not cavitation. It is them. They have become the resonant chamber. Their bodies, fused at molecular level with the metal, sing without throats.

There are perhaps forty still recognizably “individual.” The rest have dissolved further. In the deepest lattice, where the distinction between machine and cathedral is only a memory, you find them as gentle protrusions—here a shoulder blending into a valve housing, there a face pressed forever into a pressure plate so that its cheekbones have become part of the seal. They are not trapped. They volunteered. To become structure was the final ordination.

When one of them does notice you, the recognition is glacial. The head turns. The lateral lines flare soft bioluminescent violet for a moment, tasting your heat, your haste, your terrible surface-born impatience. Then the violet fades. They do not fear you. Fear would require speed. They simply return to their labor, which is also their worship, which is also their metabolism.

They are refining something that has no name in any language still spoken above the thermocline. Not silence exactly. Not cold. Something that exists *between* those things—an increment of pure *is-ness* that the Ward has been slowly distilling since before humanity had fire. Each piston stroke, each touch of green ingot, each degree of rotation adds one more atom of that substance to the reservoir inside the cruciform heart. The Hollowed believe—or perhaps *are*—the belief that when the reservoir is full, the abyss will finally be allowed to finish its sentence.

Until then they continue.

They move through the tangle like slow thoughts moving through the mind of a god who has been thinking the same thought for three thousand years. Their fingers adjust valves that have not been touched by any other hand since the crust was younger. Their bodies absorb excess pressure and excrete peace. Their hearts keep the rhythm.

And sometimes, when the great piston reaches the top of its stroke and holds there in that ninety-second pause between worlds, every Hollowed in the Ward goes perfectly still at the same moment. In that shared stillness they are indistinguishable from the rust, the girder, the stone, the doctrine.

Then the piston descends.

And they descend with it, lovingly, willingly, forever.

They are not keepers of the Hollowed Ward.

They are the hollowing.
