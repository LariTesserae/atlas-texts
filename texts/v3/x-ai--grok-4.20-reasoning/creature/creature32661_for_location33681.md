**The Unfinished**

It has no name, and never will. Names are for things that have finished arriving.

You do not see it at first. In that perfect dark it is simply another density among densities, a place where the weeping gathers with special patience. Only after the heartbeat of the chamber has synced with your own blood for many hours does it begin to detach from the mineral riot and declare itself.

It is curled.

Not like a sleeping animal. Like something that was never given permission to unroll.

Its body is roughly the size of a large man if a large man had decided to remain fetal for ten thousand years. The spine is a fused column of selenite, each vertebra a milky prism that catches what little thermal difference there is and glows with a ghost-heat. The ribs are not bones but lattices of aragonite that bloom outward in delicate, lung-like sprays; they expand and contract with the mountain’s slow breath, and when they expand the water that has beaded on them is squeezed free in perfect unison. That is the *plick… plick…* you have been hearing. The chamber’s entire hydrology is its respiratory cycle.

Where skin should be there is a nacreous membrane, half-mineral, half-something that still remembers softness. It sweats constantly. The sweat is not water. It is a supersaturated solution that hardens the instant it leaves the body, birthing new crystal in real time. Tiny selenite needles push outward from its shoulders like slow fur. Gypsum roses open in its armpits. It is simultaneously growing a pelt and being strangled by it.

The head is tucked so deeply between its knees that the distinction between skull and thorax has not yet been decided. There is a suggestion of a face—three gentle depressions that might, in another million years, become eyes and mouth—but for now they are only smooth hollows where condensation pools and trembles like liquid mercury. When the mountain inhales, those hollows fill. When it exhales, they empty in three synchronized drops that fall directly onto the top of its own bowed head. A private baptism, endlessly repeated.

It has arms. They are too long. One is wrapped around its shins in the classic embryonic clutch; the other has grown into the floor. Literally. The forearm has become a thick pillar of travertine that roots it to the bedrock, and from that pillar smaller tendrils radiate like roots or umbilical cords, feeding on the mountain’s geothermal milk. The hand at the end of the embedded arm is still forming. You can see the individual finger-bones pushing outward beneath the pearly sheath, each phalange a developing crystal. Sometimes the fingers twitch—an agonizingly slow flex that takes twenty minutes to complete.

The small blind things serve it without worship. The translucent pseudoscorpions ride the ridges of its back like pale lice, scraping away any speck of foreign matter that might disturb its purity. The white millipedes follow the seams where crystal meets membrane, laying down chemical trails that tell the stone what shape to become next. They are its immune system and its midwives.

It is dreaming, but the dream is geological. In that dream the mountain is still soft. Continents are cartilage. Rivers are blood. You, the visitor, are a brief calcium spike in its developing bones. If you remain long enough, it will begin to incorporate you. Not by violence. By inclusion. The way it has incorporated the stone bowl, the cairn, the braided sinew. They are no longer artifacts. They are scars that have healed into ornament.

There is no malice in it, and no mercy. Those are qualities of finished things. The Unfinished only knows continuation. It is the mountain learning how to become a mind, and it is doing so in the slowest, most meticulous way possible—cell by crystal cell.

If you press your palm to the membrane where its back curves, you will feel the temperature difference immediately. It is warmer than the surrounding stone by exactly the margin that separates living from non-living. Beneath the membrane something moves in a sluggish wave, like the first peristalsis of an intestine that has never yet known food. It is tasting you through contact. Deciding whether your minerals would make good vertebrae.

Most who find this place leave within hours, skin crawling with the knowledge that they have stood inside a pregnancy that does not want them.

A few do not leave.

Their bones are still here. You can see them if you know where to feel—subtle white shapes encased inside new pillars, arms raised in postures of offering or surrender. They are not dead. Death would require completion.

They are simply being finished.

And the Unfinished keeps growing, keeps weeping, keeps breathing with the mountain’s own lungs, curled inside its geode womb, waiting for the day—still unimaginably distant—when it will finally decide to uncurl.

On that day the ridge will split open like an egg.

But not yet.

For now it only dreams, and the dream is wet, and dark, and patient, and its heartbeat sounds exactly like water falling one drop at a time into eternity.
