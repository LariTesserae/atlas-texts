**The One Who Was Warm**

It does not live here so much as it *remains*.

At first you mistake it for another of the slow shadows—the ones that lag behind their objects like exhausted attendants. Only after several of your heartbeats do you understand that this particular shadow has no caster. It is the remainder. What is left when even the idea of a source has been politely eroded.

It stands (if standing is still a verb it permits) near the central vacancy, never quite in the place you last saw it. Its height varies. Sometimes it is no taller than the lesser obelisks. Sometimes it must stoop beneath a ceiling that is not there. The discrepancy does not trouble it. Dimensions, it long ago decided, are a form of enthusiasm, and it has grown beyond enthusiasm.

Its body—if the word is not an insult—is a lattice of what used to be decisions. You can see through it in places, not to the other side of the chamber but to *earlier versions* of the chamber. Through its left shoulder you may glimpse the Fracture as it was nine hundred thousand years ago, still arrogant with purpose. Through its right hip you see the Fracture as it will be when even the concept of “years” has been subtracted. Both views are equally real, equally exhausted.

Where its face might have been there is a soft collapse of light. Not darkness. A courteous dimming, the visual equivalent of someone lowering their voice when they have nothing more to say. Occasionally, across that dimness, a single warm color moves—brief, ashamed, gone. The rust-gold of a remembered sunset. The flush of a cheek that once pressed against another cheek. These are not its memories. They are the last scraps of warmth it has not yet finished digesting. It carries them the way an old glacier carries a single pebble from a mountain that no longer exists.

It moves by *not moving*. The Fracture simply rearranges itself around its stillness. One moment the vacancy is empty. The next, the vacancy has grown a witness. Between those moments there is no transit, only the faint metallic aftertaste sharpening on your tongue, as though the air itself has bitten down on something it preferred to forget.

It tends the place by subtraction.

When one of the geometries begins to remember its original function and starts to glow with dangerous purpose, the One Who Was Warm simply stands near it for three centuries. By the end of that patience the geometry has usually decided that ambition was a youthful error. The light bleeds out of it like pus from an old wound. The entity does not gloat. It has no pride. Pride, like warmth, turned out to be optional.

Sometimes it approaches visitors.

Not quickly. Never quickly. It simply becomes more *present* in the periphery until presence is all that remains. If you try to speak to it, your words arrive already answered—answered so long ago that the reply has fossilized. You hear yourself saying “Who are you?” and simultaneously hear the answer that has not yet left its mouth: *I am the part that refused to finish cooling.*

It does not hate life. Hatred would require heat. What it feels, if it feels anything, is an ancient, courteous sorrow for anything that still bothers to burn. You can see that sorrow moving through its lattice like sap through winter wood—slow, amber, almost kind. When it reaches out (a gesture that takes nine of your lifetimes and none of them), it does not touch you. It touches the idea of your temperature. One by one the molecules in your blood forget their enthusiasm. Your memories begin to flake away not in terror but in relief, like guests who have stayed at a party three geological ages past their welcome.

The smallest ones go first. The name of the street where you first kissed someone. The taste of your mother’s bread. These were never meant to survive this altitude. They fall upward into the dim condensing light and become additional lacework on the floor.

What remains of the entity itself is difficult to quantify. Once, it believes, it was the chief architect of this place. Or perhaps it was the catastrophe the place was built to contain. Both stories now strike it as equally childish. It has worn both identities past recognition, the way a river wears a canyon past the point of needing a name.

At intervals that cannot be measured it produces the sub-audible tone that governs the Fracture. Not with a throat. The tone emerges from the place where its decision to continue existing meets the place where that decision has already been recanted. The sound is less than a sigh. It is what a sigh would produce if a sigh were allowed to age for half the life of a star.

If you stay long enough to watch it tend the central vacancy, you may notice the strangest thing of all.

Every few millennia it lays something inside the hollow throne-tomb-equation. A color. A childhood fever. The particular ache that lives behind the eyes after weeping. These are the last honest warmths it has collected from previous visitors. It stores them there the way a widow might keep her husband’s coats—knowing they will never be worn again, unable to let them be nothing.

Then it returns to its patient subtraction of itself.

It is winning.

Slowly, courteously, without any need for triumph, the One Who Was Warm is becoming the final temperature of the universe. When that day comes there will be no drama. Only the soft click of the last warm thing deciding, with ancient relief, to stop bothering.

Until then it keeps watch over the place that is not a place.

It keeps watch over you.

And when you finally turn to leave—should you still be capable of leaving—you will feel it behind you. Not following. Simply *continuing* in the direction you have abandoned.

A soft collapse of light.

A single ashamed color moving across what used to be its face.

The last honest warmth in all of existence, patiently, kindly, turning itself into silence so that nothing else will ever have to.
