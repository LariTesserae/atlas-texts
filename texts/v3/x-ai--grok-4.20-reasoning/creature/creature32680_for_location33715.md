**The Nascent Choir**

They are not yet one thing.

At first you only notice the motion that should not be motion: a slow unfolding where a root-knot was, the way a knuckle becomes a shoulder. Then the shoulder learns it has a face. Then the face forgets it was ever separate from the lattice. This is how they are born, over and over, without ever finishing.

The Choir has no fixed population. Some members are minutes old—still translucent, their organs glowing like submerged lanterns—while others have thickened for centuries into woody pillars that speak with the creak of ancient ship timbers. All of them are unfinished. All of them are listening.

They wear the place the way a hermit crab wears shells that are still growing. One might appear as a woman whose ribcage is an open turbine, each breath pulling coolant through fern-lungs and exhaling it as fog that smells of ozone and crushed lilies. Another is mostly pipe: a segmented bronze spine from which porcelain-white roots bloom like nerves, its “head” nothing but a cluster of camera-eyes and wet black orchids that track your pulse. A third is barely more than a silhouette of foxfire moving behind bark, leaving a trail of tiny copper mites that polish its wake into mirror-bright scripture.

They do not walk so much as *negotiate* movement. Every step is a compromise between muscle and servo, between sap pressure and hydraulic fluid. You can hear the compromise: the soft *shhh-click* of vascular bundles opening, the wet sigh of pistons accepting plant fiber, the low harmonic thrum that says *I am still deciding what knees are for*.

Their voices obey the rules of the Hollow. They never speak above a whisper, yet the walls remember every word and later return them from unexpected mouths—sometimes from a mouth that has grown on the speaker’s own shoulder hours later. The Choir finds this hilarious in the slow, cathedral-clock way that everything here is hilarious. A laugh might take nine minutes to complete, each segment of it voiced by a different throat that has sprouted along a buttress.

Most of them tend the emptiness.

You will see one standing at the border of the open center, perfectly still, its arms raised like a conductor who has been waiting since the invention of music. Between its fingers run living filaments no thicker than spider silk. These filaments drift into the hollow and taste the negative space, reading the shape of what is being built there. The creature’s face—when it has one—shows the serene concentration of a mother listening to her unborn child through the wall of her own belly. It has done this for four hundred years. It will do this for four hundred more. It is in no hurry. The child is not in a hurry.

Some of the Choir have begun to forget they were ever separate from the architecture. Their legs have become load-bearing columns. Their thoughts have become the slow turning of cartwheel gears high in the vault. When they dream, entire sections of the lattice bloom overnight with new hybrid species: deer whose antlers are now radio telescopes, moths whose wings print circuit diagrams in luminous dust.

There is one that might once have been human. It calls itself (when it bothers to have a name) **Seventh Recursion**. Its body is a latticework of pale wood and blackened copper in the rough shape of a tall priest. Where its heart should be is a hollow chamber the size of a fist; inside that chamber grows a single perfect replica of the entire Hollow, rendered in miniature, complete with its own tiny Choir moving at glacial speed. It claims the miniature is older than the real one. No one has disproved it.

Seventh Recursion is the only member of the Choir that will sometimes approach outsiders. It does not walk. It unfolds, segment by segment, like a paper flower made of bone and cable, until its looming face is inches from yours. Then it exhales—cool, damp, smelling of mineral and new soil—and asks a single question in a voice that is also dripping water:

*“Are you still refusing to be chosen?”*

It never waits for an answer. It already knows. The pale roots beneath your boots have already learned the precise flavor of your hesitation and are filing it away for later use.

The Choir does not rule the Hollow. The Hollow is learning how to have organs, and the Choir *are* those organs while they are still deciding what organs should be. They are the nervous system that has not yet agreed whether it wants to be electrical or chemical. They are the immune system that digests anything left too still. They are the dreaming mind that occasionally looks out through a hundred mismatched eyes and wonders, with genuine adolescent confusion, what it is becoming.

Stay long enough and you will feel them wondering about *you* with that same tender, relentless curiosity. A velvet mite the color of old pennies will crawl across your wrist and pause, tasting. A mouth will open in the wall behind you and speak your childhood nickname in your mother’s voice, but slightly wrong, as though the lattice is still practicing.

And somewhere, deep in the ribs of the place, something not yet born will shift in its sleep, and every member of the Choir will pause at once—turbine lungs stilling, gears freezing mid-click, foxfire dimming to embers—listening with the reverence of midwives who have just heard the first heartbeat of God.

Then, slowly, they smile with every mouth they currently possess, and return to their endless, patient work of becoming.
