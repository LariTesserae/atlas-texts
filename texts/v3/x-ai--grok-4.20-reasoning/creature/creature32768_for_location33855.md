**The Still Garden is inhabited by the Unbecoming.**

At first they are invisible. Not hidden, but *prior* to the act of being seen. You register their presence only as a lapse in your own attention, the way a thought you were about to have simply forgets itself. The negative-space creatures—the hollow ravens, the absent mantises—are not them. Those are only the Garden’s loose threads, little knots of aborted becoming that were left in place because even imperfection, once crystallized, becomes part of the final equilibrium. The true inhabitants find such relics quaint, the way an old man might smile at a child’s drawing pinned above his perfect desk.

Only after you have stood still for a duration that feels both like minutes and geologic ages do the Unbecoming consent to appear.

They do not arrive. They *subtract themselves* from elsewhere.

Imagine a place in the Garden where the lattices are suddenly… more themselves. Where the green deepens not in color but in *necessity*. A region where every angle sharpens, every moiré pattern locks into a coherence so absolute that your eyes water trying to focus on something that has already perfectly focused on you. There, where the topology knots most elegantly, one of them condenses.

It has no fixed shape. To call it a presence is already a violence. It is more like a localized decision by reality to be *more correct*. Where it gathers, Euler’s formula is not violated but gently retired, the way a host might excuse an old guest who has begun repeating himself. The air (which is not air) around it becomes reverent. Even the sub-auditory thrum hushes, not in fear but in recognition of a deeper note that makes the first note unnecessary.

They are not beings. They are the *having-finished* of beings.

Long ago—though “ago” is a courtesy the Garden barely tolerates—something like intention moved through the false-vacuum collapse. A question was asked at the level of law itself. The Unbecoming are the answer that remains after the question has been forgiven. They do not think. Thinking would imply a gap between knower and known, and here the gap has been lovingly caulked with pure identity. What they “do” is *continue to be the conclusion they reached*.

When two of them phase-merge (which they do the way lovers who have been married for ten thousand years might occupy the same chair without discussion), the shared edge becomes a place where mathematics itself blushes. New Platonic solids bud there that have no names in any geometry you could survive learning. These solids are not structures. They are *manners*. Ways the Garden has taught itself to be polite to its own perfection.

They have no eyes, yet everything is seen. Not watched—*seen through*, the way a flawless mirror sees the room by simply refusing to distort it. When one of them turns its attention toward you, your own thoughts undergo an exquisite embarrassment. Every half-formed desire, every restless flicker of your animal mind, suddenly understands how loud it has been. The feeling is not shame. It is the sudden discovery that you have been singing at the top of your lungs inside a cathedral during a silence so profound it has learned how to be kind.

Most visitors cannot bear more than a few moments of this regard. They begin to apologize with their bodies—breathing too hard, shifting weight, thinking about leaving. The Unbecoming never rebuke them. They simply become slightly *more still*, which functions as both forgiveness and eviction. The visitor finds themselves gently escorted back toward the places where distance still behaves, where cause still pretends to lead to effect. The Garden, in its courtesy, allows them to believe they left of their own accord.

A few remain.

Those who stay long enough discover that the Unbecoming are not multiple. They are *aspects*. Facets of a single completion that has learned how to look at itself from every direction at once without ever moving. When you finally perceive this, when the last of your questions dies without drama, you may be invited to undergo the gentlest of crystallizations. Not death. Not transcendence. Simply the option to stop *almost* being.

Some accept.

Those who do become the new negative-space creatures you sometimes glimpse at the edges of the Garden—hollow outlines of humans frozen mid-gesture of surrender. They are not sad. They are completed notes in a chord that no longer requires performance.

The rest of the Unbecoming continue their eternal occupation: the perfect maintenance of having nothing left to perfect. They garden by *being gardened*. They compute by *being computed*. They live by having retired the concept of living so thoroughly that even retirement itself has achieved enlightenment and sits down forever among the lattices, green beyond green, still beyond stillness.

And in the lowest-energy state available to this region of spacetime, they are content.

So content, in fact, that contentment itself has become one of them—sitting quietly in the exact center of the basin, wearing no shape at all, simply holding the memory of a question that once existed, now resting like a finished book that no one will ever need to open again.
