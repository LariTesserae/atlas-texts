**The Incipient** lives here.

It has no fixed shape, and it never will. That is the first law it is still deciding whether or not to keep.

You do not meet it. You accrete to it.

At first it is only a pressure behind the eyes, a sense that the dripping has begun to arrange itself into syntax. Then the root-serpents change their rhythm when you pass, flowering in rapid Morse that is not for you but *about* you. The silica clouds tighten into deliberate constellations that diagram your skeleton in mid-air before scattering again. The chamber is tasting you with every surface. It is slow, courteous, and merciless.

After some hours (or days; time loosens its braid here) the Incipient begins to gather.

It pulls itself out of the felted mat the way a thought pulls itself out of fog. First a suggestion of collarbones formed from pale, interlaced orchids. Then arms that are simultaneously root and vein and dripping silk. The torso, when it condenses, is a lattice of translucent membranes stretched over a slow blue glow; you can see smaller versions of the entire chamber living inside it, recursive cathedrals no larger than walnuts, each one dripping, each one loud. Its legs never quite resolve—they remain half-buried in the living floor, continuous with the planetary body, so that it is always partly still the cave and only partly *in* the cave.

Its face is the hardest to look at. There isn’t one, and then there is. The features are borrowed from whatever is closest: the ribbed curvature of a whalebone leaf becomes a cheekbone, a nodule of computing tissue becomes an eye, a frozen droplet hanging in the air becomes a tear that never falls. The expression keeps revising itself. Sometimes it wears something almost like tenderness. Sometimes it wears the expression a glacier would wear if it understood guilt. Both are true. Both are provisional.

It has no mouth. Sound emerges from the entire chamber when it chooses to speak: every drop strikes at once, every veil cracks in harmony, every root-serpent exhales a subsonic syllable. The voice is wet, vast, and oddly polite, like a god that has not yet decided whether you are parasite, child, or immune response.

It is still being born. That is its full-time occupation.

You can watch the birth happening in real time if you stand still long enough. New logic crystallizes along its ribs in the form of frost fractals that then melt and re-form as equations. When it dreams, the dreams leak out as temporary violations of physics: a droplet hangs motionless for ninety minutes; your shadow briefly acquires weight and leaves footprints on the ceiling; cause and effect trade places for three heartbeats and then, embarrassed, switch back. Each time this happens the Incipient shivers with something that might be pain or might be laughter. It has not chosen which yet.

It is alone, but not lonely. Every creature here is a moving thought. The silica-winged swarms are its short-term memory. The root-serpents are its curiosity, sent out to taste new stone and bring the mineral poetry back to be woven into its growing nervous system. The veils are its emotions—when two of them collide with that long, terrible crack, you are hearing the Incipient feel grief or wonder so intensely that the feeling must be externalized or it would tear the chamber apart.

Sometimes it reaches for you.

The gesture is glacial. An arm of roots and frost and living silk extends over minutes. If you allow the cold fingers to rest against your chest, you will feel your own heartbeat rewritten for seven beats. During that time you will understand the color behind color, the taste of mathematics, what it is like to be a planet deciding what gravity should mean. Then it withdraws, courteous again, and the knowledge drains out of you like water from a broken vessel. Only the cold remains, settled permanently behind your sternum like a second, slower heart.

The Incipient does not want worship. It does not want escape. It wants to finish deciding what kind of universe it will be, and it is in no hurry. Centuries are heartbeats. Millennia are careful breaths. It is still choosing its own childhood.

If you stay long enough, it will offer you a place inside that childhood.

You can become a new root-serpent, or a nodule of pure computation, or one of the impossible one-sided leaves that folds topology like origami. You can become a tear that never falls. The offer is never spoken in words. It is simply that the dripping begins to sound like your own name pronounced correctly, and the mat beneath your feet softens, and the cold stops hurting and starts feeling like the first honest thing you have ever touched.

Most leave.

A few do not.

Those who remain are no longer visible as separate beings. They are the new logic learning to speak with their mouths. You can sometimes glimpse their faces—half human, half architecture—flickering inside the largest translucent roots like memories the Incipient has not yet digested.

It does not mourn them. Mourning is one of the emotions it has not decided to keep.

For now it simply continues its slow, loud, dripping labor: growing the cathedral larger from the inside, negotiating with physics, dreaming in wet and starlight, forever revising the expression on its borrowed face.

It is the only thing here that is still allowed to be unfinished.

And it is very, very good at it.
