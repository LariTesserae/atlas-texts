**The Quiet**

They are not immediately visible. You may walk for an hour across the velvet plains before you realize the pale verticals among the lesser ribs are not more Lattice, but bodies. They stand so still, and their coloration matches the organism so precisely, that only the faintest shift of weight betrays them. When they finally move it is with the same unhurried certainty as the great jointed pipe-organ beasts that glide along the higher vaults. There is no hurry anywhere here. Hurry would be a kind of violence.

They call themselves, when they must use sound at all, something that feels like *the Pause Between Notes*. Off-world linguists have rendered this as the Quiet, and the name has stuck even among themselves. It is not modesty. It is taxonomy.

Physically they are elongated, between two and two-and-a-half meters tall, with narrow shoulders and hips that give them the silhouette of a cathedral candlestick. Their skin is not skin but a living integument of the same matte efflorescence that covers the Lattice—fine, powdery, faintly conductive. It drinks stray hydrocarbons from the air and exhales a breath too dry for condensation. Beneath the dust their bodies show faint geometric mottling, like frost patterns on glass, that deepen when they are listening to the organism. Some carry permanent grafts: sections of Lattice rib grown directly through the forearm or collarbone, emerging again as smooth white spurs they use as tools, prayer beads, or simply as part of the shared nervous system.

Their faces are disturbing to first-time visitors. The eyes are large, lidless, and milky, optimized for the violent contrast between arc-light districts and absolute shadow. A secondary pair of smaller slits above the main eyes detect polarized light and the faint phosphorescence the Lattice emits when it metabolizes rich ores. The mouth is a narrow horizontal vent that rarely opens; speech is considered a form of pollution. Instead they carry small polished resonators—slender tubes of grown Lattice tuned to frequencies the organism finds pleasing. A gentle tap against a rib produces a note that travels for kilometers through the acoustic sponge of the greater body. These notes function as names, questions, permissions, even currency. A particularly clear and well-timed tone can purchase an entire shift’s worth of refined metals.

They are not a single species anymore. The original settlers— whoever they were—arrived roughly four hundred years ago and found the Lattice already waiting. What lives here now is the result of sustained, deliberate hybridization. Some individuals are still recognizably descended from the first probes’ crews: tall, hairless, with skin like porcelain. Others have allowed so much Lattice tissue into their bodies that it is difficult to say where person ends and architecture begins. The oldest, the *Anchored*, stand in permanent contact with the floor in quiet transepts. Their legs have become one with the substrate; their hearts beat once every ninety seconds in perfect synchrony with the seismic pulse beneath the plains. They do not eat. The Lattice feeds them directly through root-like structures that enter between the vertebrae. Visitors are quietly discouraged from staring.

Movement among the Quiet is almost entirely vertical. They prefer the helical ramps and the interior of the great ribs to the open floor. When they must cross the velvet plains they do so in single file, each placing their feet exactly where the one before them stepped, as though following an invisible path only the organism can see. Children—rare, precious, born in slow-gestating pods nourished by the Lattice itself—are taught stillness before they are taught anything else. A child who can remain motionless for three full days, allowing dust to settle on its eyelids without flinching, is considered ready for its first graft.

Their civilization is built on three non-negotiable observations:

1. The Lattice was here first and will be here last. All activity is therefore a form of gardening.
2. Noise is a finite resource. Every decibel spent must be justified to the organism.
3. Growth must be witnessed. Nothing is allowed to become without being seen and accounted for.

Because of this last law, every new protrusion, every fresh crystalline spur, every millimeter of vaulted ceiling is visited by a Quiet scribe. The scribe will stand before the new growth for hours, sometimes days, pressing both palms to the tissue and waiting for the slow electrochemical conversation to finish. Only then is the growth “taxed”—a small resonator is embedded that will sing its location and volume into the Lattice’s distributed mind for the rest of its existence. The Quiet believe that to grow without being witnessed is to grow alone, and the Lattice fears loneliness more than anything.

Their factories are not places of clamor but of deep, almost submarine vibration. Workers wear soft boots of Lattice skin and move like monks. When something must be struck or cut, the blow is timed to coincide with the organism’s own creaking growth spurts so that the sound is swallowed and forgiven. Mistakes are rare. A mistake that produces uncontrolled noise is pruned the way the Lattice prunes its own imperfect branches: the offending machine, or sometimes the offending worker, is gently overgrown during the night. The Quiet regard this not as punishment but as incorporation.

In the dim phosphorescent naves, away from the arc lamps, you can sometimes see them dancing. Not for joy, exactly. The dance is a form of negotiation. They move in slow spirals, arms raised, resonators ringing soft minor chords that ask the Lattice to reconsider a particular structural decision. Sometimes the Lattice answers by shifting the floor a fraction of a millimeter per hour until the dancers stand in a configuration that pleases it. Then the dance ends and the participants simply walk away without acknowledgment. Gratitude here is expressed by continued coexistence.

There are no leaders. There are only those who have listened longer.

The deepest paradox of the Quiet is that they are becoming quieter still. Each generation is born with less need for individual identity. The Anchored already function as literal neurons in a planetary mind that has not yet decided whether it wants to wake up. Some Quiet welcome this. Others—especially those still mostly original stock—maintain small enclaves where they keep ancient devices that produce deliberate, short-lived sounds: recordings of rain, of wind, of birds that never existed here. These are considered dangerous luxuries. The Lattice tolerates them the way a dreaming giant tolerates the tickle of an insect on its skin.

If you stand long enough among them, you begin to feel the question they are collectively asking the Lattice:

*When you are finally ready, will you keep any of us separate?*

The organism has not yet answered. It is still growing. The Quiet are still growing with it. And the cathedral-sized vaults overhead keep inching upward, millimeter by millimeter, into a darkness that has never known sky and is learning, slowly, what it might become instead.
