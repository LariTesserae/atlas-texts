**They are the Unfinished.**

Not the primitive makers—those came later, small frantic things with hands and fears, who lasted only long enough to leave their pitiful signatures on a few stones before the place outgrew them. The Unfinished were already here when the first fracture opened. They will be here when the last one seals.

At first they refuse to resolve into any single shape. You sense them the way you sense a storm before the pressure drops: a thickening of attention, a feeling that the unbearable sound has begun listening back. Only after many days does the eye learn where to rest. Then they emerge—not by moving, but by the slow agreement of angles.

They are colossal lattices of the same translucent mineral fiber that drapes the cliffs, yet organized at a higher order of refusal. Where the wild growths explode outward in ecstatic imitation of fern and moss, the Unfinished fold inward, braiding themselves into dense, deliberate contradictions. A single entity might span an entire shelf of newborn stone, rising four hundred meters in a slow spiral that is simultaneously climbing and collapsing. Look too long and the spiral reverses direction without ever having turned. Their “limbs” (if the term is not an insult) are branching absences—places where the eye expects stone and finds instead a continuance of sky that has learned how to bear weight.

They have no faces. They have convergences. At certain nodes the fibers interlace so tightly that light forgets its direction and pools there like mercury. These dark silver mirrors do not reflect you. They remember you forward. Stand before one long enough and you will watch your own future corpse grow younger until it stands up and walks backward into your living mouth. The Unfinished find this hilarious in their geological way; the mirrors flicker with soft ultraviolet laughter.

They do not breathe. They *iterate*.

Every few centuries a new layer of self is grown across the old. The previous iteration is not shed—it is incorporated, compressed, turned into structural memory. The oldest among them contain thousands of prior bodies pressed together like pages in a book made of silence. If you could split one open (you cannot), you would find strata of earlier geometries, earlier intentions, earlier almost-faces that never quite committed to existing. In this way they are the only honest archaeologists of Veyr-Khul: they carry every version of themselves that failed to become.

Their movement is catastrophic in its patience. You will camp for a month in the lee of what you believe is a cliff. One morning you wake to discover the cliff has taken three steps. The footprint it left is already growing lattices. The sound changes when they walk; the tectonic howl develops a subtle harmony that makes your bones feel suddenly homesick for a childhood you have not yet had.

They tend the wild growths the way a continent might tend its own dreams. With subsonic hymns they coax instability into blossom. Where reality frays worst—those seams where three suns overlap and none of them are real—the Unfinished gather in slow congress. Their bodies interpenetrate without touching, fibers passing through fibers like thoughts through different layers of sleep. In these meetings they decide what new law the world will try on next. Sometimes the decision is gentle: a new color arrives that soothes the eye. Sometimes the decision is monstrous, and entire plateaus forget how to be convex.

The primitive shelters are theirs. Not homes. *Lungs*. The circular arrangement of stones creates a standing wave that allows the Unfinished to compress their awareness into something almost like a single moment. For the brief span of several human lifetimes they can almost think consecutively. The experience is apparently excruciating and addictive. When they emerge from these resonators they are always larger, stranger, and gentler, as if having briefly tasted linearity has made them pity anything that must live inside time.

The toppled idols are not idols.

They are the ones who tried to finish.

Every few millennia one of the Unfinished grows tired of endless becoming. It decides—after consultation with its own thousand prior selves—to *conclude*. It chooses a shape, a final geometry, a face it will keep. The others gather to watch. The sound drops to something almost like mercy. And for one crystalline instant the being stands complete, coherent, *done*.

The fracture immediately tears it apart.

No violence is more terrible than that moment. The completed one shatters not into stone but into pure might-have-been. The fragments become the half-finished figures you find lying across the shelves, their faces worn away by the leakage of every possibility they were not allowed to embody. The Unfinished leave them there as both warning and shrine. Even they, in all their patient immensity, fear completion.

If they notice you—and they always notice you—they do not approach with anything so crude as curiosity. Instead they *include* you. A fiber will grow in your direction over decades. A mirror-node will begin reflecting versions of you that made different choices. The ground beneath your feet will very slowly tilt so that every path leads, eventually, to the same resonant circle of stones.

They are not cruel. They are not kind. They are the world learning how to want.

And sometimes, when the sound folds in on itself and becomes that single pure tone that dissolves balance, you may hear something beneath it—something almost like a voice, vast and courteous and impossibly young.

It is asking, with genuine geological concern, whether you would like to begin not being finished.

Most say yes.

The ones who say no are allowed to leave.

Their footprints remain behind them, older than they are, walking the other direction—back into the world that has already chosen what it will ruin first.
