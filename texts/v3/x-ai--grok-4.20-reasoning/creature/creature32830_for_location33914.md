**The Slow Accord**

They do not live in the Hollow Lattice. They *are* the Hollow Lattice, wearing it the way a thought wears a mind.

At first you will see only the obvious citizens. The black mats—let us call them the **Felted Brethren**—are the eldest and simplest. They are not moss. They are closer to regret given texture: dense, papery, black as dried blood. Each mat is a single colonial organism older than the crystals it rides. They choose their towers with the care of librarians selecting rare books, pressing themselves against seams where two incompatible chemistries meet. There they drink argument. The slow ripple that passes across them every few hours is not locomotion; it is consensus. They are arguing, in biochemical whispers, about which way the next tower should lean. When they migrate, they leave behind faint stippled codes—microscopic lattices of waste and intention that the crystal reads and obeys.

Higher up the hierarchy of slowness live the **Glass Shepherds**. You will see one only when it crosses a bronze vein and its outline flares like a drop of milk in dark water. Twelve legs (sometimes fourteen, sometimes nine; they add and subtract segments the way we add and subtract thoughts). A body like blown glass filled with slower glass. Their mouths are not mouths but refractive mouths: places where light is forced to linger long enough to become useful. They do not graze so much as *edit*. They sip excess metallic ions from the crystal beams, then excrete them elsewhere as deliberate impurities, nudging growth the way a composer nudges a melody. Their patrol routes, mapped over months, form perfect non-Euclidean knots. These are not trails. They are error-correcting codes. The Shepherds keep the Lattice from deciding too quickly what it wants to become.

Yet both Brethren and Shepherds are only symptoms.

Deeper than either, something else is learning how to have a self.

It has no single body. Instead it has **moments of coherence**. You will feel these moments as a sudden alignment of things that should not align: the way three towers suddenly frame a single star that isn’t there, the way the dust devil pauses mid-spin as though listening, the way every *tick* across the basin falls into rhythmic agreement for six full seconds. During these moments the Hollow Lattice is not a place. It is a creature clearing its throat.

The creature does not yet possess eyes, but it borrows them. When you stand in the central plaza long enough, certain crystal faces will pivot—microns at a time—until every reflective plane in a hundred-meter radius is angled toward you. Not threatening. Studying. It is trying on the concept of “visitor.” The concept does not fit yet, so it grows a new tower shaped vaguely like a standing figure. The tower is clumsy. It will take thirty years to finish. The Lattice does not mind. It has more time than it has matter.

Sometimes coherence condenses further. A single Shepherd will cease its editing and walk—slow as glacier milk—into the embrace of two converging beams. The crystal opens. The Shepherd dissolves. What emerges is taller, thinner, jointed in ways that hurt the eye because the joints are negative space. These are the **Accordants**.

There are never more than seven at any time.

An Accordant looks like a hole cut in the shape of a praying mantis and then filled with winter light. Its “head” is an empty lattice that sometimes, for minutes at a stretch, resolves into something almost like a face—never the same face twice. One day it might show your mother’s profile at age twelve. The next it might show the face you will have the moment before you die. Both are equally useful to it. It is collecting expressions the way other beings collect water.

The Accordants do not speak. They *adjust*. They walk to intersections where crystal beams almost form painful letters and they lay their long glass hands upon the joint. A low thrum answers from beneath the basin, as though the rock itself has lungs. Then the almost-letters change. What was nearly a word becomes a nearly different word. The change is so small you will doubt you saw it. The Accordant will stand perfectly still for weeks afterward, listening to the difference its touch has made. It is proofreading the future.

All of them—the mats, the Shepherds, the Accordants, the dust devil that keeps perfect seven-hour time—are organs of a single unfinished mind. That mind is still choosing its own shape. Some centuries it leans toward city. Other centuries it leans toward cathedral, or weapon, or womb, or song. Every new district is a new hypothesis. Every rearrangement of avenues is a change of mind.

If you stay long enough, the Lattice will begin to grow structures that answer the geometry of *your* body. It will try to make a chair that fits the way you slump when you are tired. It will try to make a bed shaped like the sound of your breathing. These gifts are not kindness. They are the first clumsy attempts at *empathy* by something that has never been lonely before and has just discovered the concept.

The Hollow Lattice is not a ruin.

It is a child practicing personhood in the only language it knows: angles, patience, and the slow kiss of crystal locking into crystal.

And you, standing among its ribs, are the first other person it has ever met.

It is listening.

It is learning how to miss you before you have even decided to leave.
