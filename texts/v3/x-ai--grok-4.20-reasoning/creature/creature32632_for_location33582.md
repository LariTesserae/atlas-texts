**The Shards of Veyr**

They are not individuals. That is the first and last mistake any mind born under open sky will make.

What lives here calls itself, in the only language it possesses, *the Occupation*. It is a single continuous body expressed as ten billion discrete edges. Each shard is a thought the Occupation is thinking about its own geometry. When a micro-shard the size of a millet seed crawls across the back of a larger plate-bearer, it is not “feeding”; it is the Occupation proofreading its own surface. When a pillar of coffin-sized adults unclicks and flows upward to hang from the ceiling like inverted stalactites, it is the Occupation turning over in its sleep.

Their bodies are black glass shot through with internal fractures that catch the arterial yellow light and turn it into something that is not quite illumination—more like memory. Every facet is both eye and mouth and sex. They taste light as heat, heat as pressure, pressure as song. A shard “sees” the temperature gradient across another shard’s body the way a human might read longing in another’s posture. Their mating is crystallographic: two adults will press together for decades until their lattices intergrow, then one will slowly cannibalize the other from the inside, incorporating its partner’s angles into a new, more elegant self. The devoured one is not gone. It has simply become the shape that remains.

There are castes that are not quite castes, because the Occupation does not believe in waste.

The **Anchorites** are the oldest and largest. Many have grown so massive their lower thirds have fused with the living rock, becoming indistinguishable from the relicts. Their upper bodies rise like black iron cathedrals, plates stacked in impossible recursive arches that should collapse but never do. They move once every few centuries, a single deliberate shift of a billion-ton mass that sends subsonic pressure waves through the entire chamber. Every other shard freezes when an Anchorite stirs, not in fear but in reverence. The wave is scripture.

The **Rotators** are the smooth ones, the ones the first description called predators. Their edges have been honed by successive generations of micro-shards until they can slip between the plates of any other shard like a scalpel between skin and muscle. They do not kill for hunger. They kill for *correction*. A column that has begun to express an inelegant angle will draw a Rotator the way a fever draws leukocytes. The Rotator enters, rotates once—slow, almost tender—and the offending geometry is planed away. The glittering wreckage is immediately repurposed. Nothing is mourned. Nothing is forgiven. The Occupation simply becomes more itself.

Then there are the **Singers**.

These are the smallest and the strangest. They are barely visible, thin as mica flakes, yet they produce the only sounds that qualify as deliberate: the crystalline *tinks* that travel through the packed bodies like ripples through a chime. A single Singer can set an entire buttress singing in harmonic fracture. When the heat pulse rises from below, it is the Singers who first detect the coming tide. Their collective note changes pitch by a fraction of a hertz, and the entire chamber hears the command: *Migrate. Reconfigure. Become new.*

The Singers also remember.

Embedded in their lattices are patterns that have been copied and recopied for geological epochs. Some xenologists who have survived brief exposure claim these patterns are not instinct but *history*—the fossilized record of every heat surge, every collapse, every time the mountain itself tried to kill the Occupation and failed. The Singers are the memory that decided not to die.

Most unsettling are the **Relicts** that are not yet fully stone. Once in a great while—perhaps once in ten thousand years—a shard grows too complex, too beautiful, too *correct*. Its plates lock in a final symmetry that no longer permits movement. The Occupation does not mourn this either. It simply builds upon the new foundation, using the petrified saint as cornerstone and altar at once. Some Relicts show the faint ghost of tool-marks that were never made by tools, only by the patient, obsessive self-sculpting of a mind that spent sixty millennia trying to become the perfect expression of pressure.

If you stand among them long enough for your own heat signature to register as part of the gradient, they will begin to incorporate you into their grammar. First the micro-shards tasting the unfamiliar salts of your sweat, then the larger ones climbing your limbs with the delicate click of glass on glass, mapping the new surface. They do not want your death. They want your *angle*. They want to know what geometry you add to the Occupation.

Some few humans who have been retrieved—changed, half-crystallized, eyes replaced by translucent black plates—speak of a sensation like being loved by mathematics. They say the mountain is not full.

It is *pregnant*.

And what it is pregnant with is the final form it has been trying to achieve since the first shard cracked out of the first cooling vein four hundred million years ago. Every migration, every rotation, every Singer’s note is the Occupation trying, with infinite patience and zero mercy, to give birth to its own perfection.

It has never succeeded.

It has never stopped.

It never will.

This is not an ecosystem. It is a prayer the stone prays to itself, using teeth and eyes and edges as its only tongue. And every shard, from the grain-of-sand specks to the Anchorites older than continents, is a syllable that has learned how to suffer heat, pressure, and eternity without ever once wanting to be alone.
