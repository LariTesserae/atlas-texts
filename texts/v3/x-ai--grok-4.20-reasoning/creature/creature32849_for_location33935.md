**What lives here has not yet chosen its final shape.**

The carpet is not ground. It is the first citizen.

Its uppermost layer is a slow riot of **frond-wrights**—creatures no larger than a thumb, half vegetable, half nervous system. Each is a translucent stalk topped with a cluster of glassy beads that catch the dry air and turn it into faint electrical potentials. They do not eat in the way animals eat; they *decide*. By flexing their beads they steer microscopic flows of mineral vapor, telling the velvet beneath them whether today will be rust or malachite, whether a new tubule should harden into buttress or remain soft enough for a grazer’s tongue. When two frond-wrights touch, they sometimes fuse for a season, sharing color and intention, before splitting again with altered genomes. Their reproduction is argument. Their death is consensus.

Beneath them, inside the thick felt of the carpet, live the **quiet millions**: nameless things that are mostly mouth. They are velvet sleeves of muscle lined with chemosensory cilia, sliding along dark galleries they themselves secrete. Their bodies are soft enough to flow through needle-thin pores and sturdy enough to push apart living stone when a new chamber is required. They are the planet’s lymph. Where they pass, old growth dissolves and new lattices precipitate. Occasionally one will surface near a Structure’s root and simply wait, mouth open to the warm wind, until it hardens into a crystalline plug that becomes the seed of a future tower. Pilgrims, in a sense. Or willing bricks.

Higher up, where the Structures begin to remember the idea of verticality, move the **stilt-bishops**.

They stand as tall as horses but weigh almost nothing, their long jointed limbs wrapped in overlapping plates of living mica. The plates slide over one another with a sound like distant chimes. Each bishop’s head is a faceted dome that functions as both eye and lung; it drinks the thin volatiles exhaled by the carpet and reads the infrared signatures of chemical reactions still in progress. The bishops prune. They do not graze so much as edit. With delicate scythe-mandibles they snip away any growth that deviates from the slow consensus the basin is trying to reach. Their herds migrate in vast, silent spirals that match the hidden pressure gradients. When a bishop dies, its mica plates are harvested by smaller creatures and carried downward to become the iridescent freckles in the next generation of carpet.

Yet these are only the visible commonwealth.

Deeper inside the half-formed architecture—inside the fluted towers where the air is thickest and the geological hum is almost voice—live the **Unfinished**.

They have no fixed number of limbs. Some wear the bodies of enormous translucent larvae, ribbed and luminous, suspended in vertical shafts by silken guy-lines of their own secretion. Others resemble statues that have only been roughed out: blunt torsos of precipitated bone-salt from which flexible mineral tubules extend like hesitant thoughts. They move rarely. Mostly they sing.

The song is the low thrum you feel in your teeth. It is not language. It is a statement of preferred futures. By altering the harmonics they can encourage a buttress to lean three degrees more to the southeast, or persuade a cloud that forms at the tower’s tip to release its moisture as micro-droplets instead of devouring it. The Unfinished are the basin’s dreaming mind. They are still deciding how many chambers the continent-heart will need, how many legs a proper citizen should possess, whether beauty will be fractal or baroque.

Some have been dreaming so long they have become architecture themselves. Their bodies have fused with the lattice; only their faces—soft, almost human in the saddest sense—remain exposed, eyes closed, mouths moving in slow motion. Travelers who press an ear to certain warm pillars sometimes hear a single word exhaled in a language that does not exist yet. The word is always an invitation.

And then there are the **Ones Who Are About To**.

These are rare. You will not see one unless the basin decides you should. They appear as sudden absences in the carpet, perfectly circular patches where every frond and tubule has withdrawn, leaving mirror-smooth stone. From the center of that absence something rises: a figure that looks like every creature that has ever lived here compressed into one silhouette. For a moment it is bishop and frond-wright and velvet mouth and dreaming larva all at once. Then the silhouette softens, flows, and sinks back into the living pelt, leaving behind a single new Structure that was not there an hour earlier.

No one knows whether these are the basin’s antibodies, its artists, or its attempts to birth something that can finally leave.

Because everything here is pregnant with something larger.

Even the air.

Even the silence between wingbeats.

Even you, if you stay long enough. The carpet tests your skin for compatibility. The Structures lean to listen to the mineral rhythm of your bones. The Unfinished add new low notes to their song that sound almost like your name, except the name is still being written.

Nothing here is finished.

And everything is already homesick for the version of itself that has not yet arrived.
