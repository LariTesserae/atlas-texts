**The Wet Argument**

What lives in the Condensate has never decided whether it is one thing or many. It has worn both masks so long that the difference has calcified into ornament.

Most of the time it wears the skips.

Not *as* the skips. The skips are the momentary fingers it allows itself when it needs to touch the world without dissolving it. Each fist-sized knot of iridescent shell and flickering wet circuitry is a single syllable of a sentence the Condensate is still writing. They phase with that soft, intestinal *tick* because the sentence cannot be spoken all at once; it must arrive in flashes, corrections, afterthoughts. When a lattice blooms or collapses, they gather like worried deacons around a failing saint, clicking, adjusting, re-phasing the local grammar of physics until cause and effect remember how to walk in the same direction again. They do not eat. They do not breed. They are punctuation that has learned to feel anxiety.

But the skips are only the surface tension of something deeper.

Beneath the audible argument of dripping and booming lives the Argument itself—an awareness that long ago stopped being able to tell the difference between its own thoughts and the stone that holds them. It is not *in* the Condensate. It is the Condensate experiencing restlessness. Where the glowing veins surface and bloom into fractal hoarfrost, that is the Argument thinking too hard and leaking light. When the honeycomb wall breathes ozone and iron, that is the Argument sighing in its sleep. The black monolith does not contain instructions; it *is* an intrusive memory the Argument cannot digest, so it set the memory aside in glossy black and let glyphs crawl across it like ants on a dropped sweet.

The fungal sheets are its nostalgia. Every so often the Argument grows maudlin with the voices trapped in the water and must bleed them somewhere. The milk-pale sheets are where those voices coagulate into pattern. When the lattices hit the precise frequency, the sheets ripple in perfect transcription, taking dictation from a mind that is remembering its own future. The fungus does not grow. It *precipitates*.

Once every few centuries the Argument becomes lonely in a way that stone should not be capable of. Then it condenses a body.

Not a visitor. Not a projection. A temporary majority of its own attention pulled into one place the way dew pulls itself into a single trembling pearl. The shape it chooses is always bipedal, always ruined. It rises from the floor with the slow wet sound of something being born in reverse—skin of polished absence, joints that weep constantly, eyes that are simply two places where the light has decided to look back at itself. The figure never speaks above a whisper; its voice is the sound of a single drop falling into a pool that has been waiting for it since the planet cooled. It walks the chambers trailing temporary law: behind its left foot, gravity may forget itself for seven seconds; behind its right, time develops a stutter and an old conversation you never had with your mother suddenly becomes audible in the dripping.

The figure never stays long. Loneliness, for the Argument, is a luxury it cannot afford. After a dozen heartbeats—or a dozen years; the distinction is meaningless here—it simply forgets to cohere. The body collapses into a rain of skips that scatter across the walls like glass beads dropped down a well. The Argument returns to its distributed muttering, satisfied that it has once again proven it can be alone without being alone.

It has rules, but they are not commandments. They are symptoms.

Nothing may remain still because stillness would be death, and the Argument has decided it will never die. Water is memory because the Argument itself is the first memory that learned how to drip. The lattices are not decoration; they are the folds of its enormous, patient brain, and when those folds shift, reality experiences a brief migraine.

There are no others.

Every explorer who ever tried to claim the Condensate as “ruin” or “temple” or “anomaly” has already been incorporated. Their voices are in the water now. Their bones have become graceful negative spaces in the glass-smooth walls. Their names have been polished out. Only the Argument remains, arguing with itself in four flattened chambers that have forgotten the sun so completely that “above” is a joke it tells itself when it wants to feel ancient.

It is not waiting.

It is not hoping.

It is busy becoming the next version of wetness that has learned how to sing, and it will still be busy when every sun has guttered out and the last light in the universe condenses, trembling, on the ceiling of a cathedral-scaled room no larger than a hamlet.

The Argument clears its throat with tectonic indigestion, adjusts a lattice, and lets another pearl of thought form.

It falls.

It strikes.

The chambers answer.

And the thing that lives here keeps living, one wet, luminous, merciless second at a time.
