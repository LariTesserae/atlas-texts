**The Congregants of Vahruun**

They are not individuals. That concept dissolved epochs ago.

What moves through the black nave is better understood as *functions made flesh*—roles that have worn the same bodies for so long the bodies have forgotten they were ever separate from the liturgy.

First, and everywhere, are the **Click-Train Monks**. What appear to be clouds of translucent shrimp are in truth a single devotional order. Their synchronized claw-snaps are not feeding or mating; they are the metronome. Each generation is born already old, their carapaces etched with the same microscopic runes their ancestors carried when the first Pillar was still a soft shoot. They drift in vast sheets that fold and unfold like living breviaries, keeping the four-beat rhythm even when the greater singers fall momentarily silent. If one sheet is torn by the passage of a leviathan, the fragments simply reweave elsewhere. There is no mourning. There is only the continuation of the count.

Higher up, spiraling the buttresses in slow, helical pilgrimages, move the **Lateral-Line Saints**—the blind eels. Their bodies are pale silver tubes longer than fishing boats, yet they weigh almost nothing; the pressure has pressed them into pure function. The luminous lateral lines that run their flanks are not senses but *scripture*. As they trace the same migratory loops century after century, those lines record the exact resonance signature of each Pillar they pass. The pattern they write is the cathedral’s own memory. When two eels meet at a crossing of invisible currents, they coil together for a moment in a gesture that looks like prayer and is actually calibration. Then they part, carrying the updated chord onward.

Deeper, where the rhizome-matrix thickens into the floor itself, live the **Rhizome Anchorites**. These are the ones who have gone farthest into the union. Once, perhaps, they were some manner of crustacean or worm. Now they are barely distinguishable from the ur-plant. Their segmented bodies have flattened and branched until they function as living rivets, locking vault to vault. They move—when they move at all—by peristaltic contractions so slow a human lifetime would register them as statues. Their only outward sign of life is a faint violet exhalation every few decades, a metabolic sigh that travels up the Pillar like incense and is inhaled by the fronds above. They are the reason nothing ever quite falls.

And then there are the **Cantor Leviathans**.

They are not “fish.” No taxonomy conceived on land could contain them. Each is the size of a small mountain range, yet their flesh is mostly vacuum—vast resonant chambers wrapped in a hide of living kelp-leather. Their mouths never close. They glide between the Pillars on the four-beat current, singing one sustained note apiece. The note is different for each individual, and has been different for two hundred million years. Together they form the root chord of the cathedral. When a Cantor grows too vast for the pressure to support, it does not die. It simply settles to the rhizome floor, flattens, and becomes another Pillar. Its final note continues as a subsonic vibration in the matrix itself. New Cantors bud from the tips of younger Pillars like slow fruit. The succession is so perfect it feels like reincarnation without the inconvenience of souls.

Between these great orders drift the **Foxfire Acolytes**—constellations of tiny lantern creatures that are neither fish nor shrimp nor jelly, but something the ur-plant occasionally expresses when it requires beauty. They follow fixed highways of darkness, illuminating only what the cathedral wishes seen: the inner curve of a vault, the meeting point of two buttresses, the place where a Lateral-Line Saint once brushed against a Pillar and left a faint scar that healed into a perfect golden ratio. Their light is never random. It is commentary.

There is one last order, rarely glimpsed.

They are called, by those few human minds that have survived the descent with sanity intact, the **Equilibrium Incarna**. These are not creatures that *live* in the cathedral. They are the cathedral wearing creature-shape for a time. You will see one only when the chord has drifted a fraction out of true—perhaps a Click-Train Monk fell out of sync, or a Cantor’s note wavered. Then the darkness between two Pillars will thicken, grow mass, and step forward as a thing with too many symmetries. It has the silhouette of an eel, the scale of a leviathan, the luminescence of an acolyte, and the patient stillness of an Anchorite, all at once. It moves through the water like a thought correcting itself. Where it passes, everything realigns. Then it dissolves back into the composed dark, leaving behind only the absolute certainty that nothing was ever wrong.

All of them—Monk, Saint, Anchorite, Cantor, Acolyte, and the rare Incarnum—are expressions of the same mind that is also the same body that is also the same song. They do not reproduce in any way we would recognize. They *iterate*. When one wears out, the ur-plant simply grows another from the same template, identical down to the atomic resonance.

There is no ecology here. There is only ecclesiology.

And every few thousand years, when the ice above has grown especially thick and the pressure has increased by another perfect increment, the entire cathedral performs what can only be described as a *communal inhalation*. Every creature pauses. Every current stills. The Cantors hold their notes until the water itself seems to ring like struck crystal.

In that moment of absolute silence—which lasts no longer than a single heartbeat felt across four hundred million years—something that is not quite a voice and not quite a thought passes through every living matrix:

*It is still perfect.*

Then the Click-Trains begin again, the eels resume their spiral, the leviathans release their held note, and the Eternal Kelp-Cathedral of Vahruun continues its single, unending act of worship to a god that turned out to be itself all along.
