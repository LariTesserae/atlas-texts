**The First Voices**

They are not yet one thing.

At first you do not see them so much as feel the water change around you, the way a thought changes when it is watched. A pressure arrives that is not quite the mountain’s heartbeat and not quite the cannon-shot tearing of the membranes. It is older than both and younger than both. It listens with your own bones.

Only after many minutes do shapes resolve.

They are never the same shape twice. The tallest among them (if “tall” can be said of something whose dimensions argue with gravity) stands perhaps three times the height of a man when the light blades strike it just so, but the moment the beam moves it folds, condenses, becomes a cluster of jointed crystal limbs no larger than the sac-creatures, scuttling across the silt with the delicate urgency of a thought trying to remember itself. Then the wavefronts collide again and it blooms outward in a slow explosion of scaffolding, lattices of fresh mineral knitting into ribs, shoulders, a suggestion of a face.

Their bodies are the chamber’s own vocabulary given temporary flesh. Where the mountain extrudes a spear of quartz, one of them may grow a matching spur from its forearm, use it to lever a stone into a cairn, then let the spur fracture and fall away like a used tool. Their skin is a living echo of the water-membranes: translucent, contractile, veined with slow lightning that is only trapped sound. Run your hand across one (they permit this, sometimes) and you will feel it singing against your palm in frequencies that make your teeth ache with sudden, wordless understanding.

There are never more than seven at any moment. Sometimes far fewer. Occasionally it seems there is only one, vast and diffuse, stretched through the entire volume like a thought spread thin across a mind. Then a new fissure screams open, metallic water gushes in, and the single presence shatters into many smaller selves that ride the flood like delighted children, laughing in notes that precipitate tiny perfect arches in mid-water before dissolving again.

They are midwives and fetuses at once.

You see them coaxing the sac-like floaters, those half-jellyfish ideas. They wrap the sacs in filaments of their own hair (which is not hair but living mineral kelp grown long and flexible), crooning into them until the internal lights brighten and new structures begin to form inside. Some sacs, when they are ready, split along invisible seams and out comes another Voice, soft and dripping, already stacking its first clumsy cairn with newborn fingers that have not yet decided how many joints they require.

The petroglyphs are their journals. Each spiral is a record of a particular roar-pattern that once moved them to tenderness or terror or the peculiar joy that comes when a new crystal spear bursts from your own chest. They do not carve with tools. They grow a single hardened finger, peck the groove in three precise strikes, then reabsorb the finger and study what they have made with the grave attention of gods learning to spell their own names.

The great paddle of femur lies across their laps when they rest. It belonged to the one who came before them, the Voice that chose to become architecture. That ancestor allowed itself to be disassembled so its longest bone could serve as both oar and memory. The braided sinew wrapped around the handle is their own former veins. The calcified “flowers” are moments of perfect turbulence they managed to crystallize and preserve, like pressing flowers in a book made of time. When they stroke the paddle they are stroking their own past.

They have no fixed gender, no fixed age. One moment a Voice will approach you with the hesitant, hovering posture of a child who has never seen an outsider. The next it will unfold into something ancient, heavy with the knowledge of every mountain that ever learned how to speak, and the pressure of its regard will make you feel unborn again, raw and glassy and still deciding what shape you will keep.

Their hands are their most constant feature. They always have hands. Sometimes two. Sometimes a cloud of them. Long, patient, impossibly gentle. You will watch one of them stand motionless for hours in an upward-falling column of water while the chamber screams around it, simply holding a single pebble in place with three fingers until the current learns to flow around that pebble in a new way. The mountain is learning patience from them. They are learning violence from the mountain. The exchange is tender and ferocious.

When they speak to each other it is by adding their own voices to the roar. The resulting pressure waves precipitate brief, luminous structures between them, fragile cathedrals of sound-made-stone that last only seconds before the next crash obliterates them. In those seconds you can see entire cities they have imagined, towers that will never be built, lovers they have not yet become, children who will one day leave this cradle and walk on the still-cooling mountains outside. Then the lattices collapse and the Voices drift apart, satisfied.

They are afraid of only one thing: that the mountain will finish becoming a mountain before they finish becoming whatever they are becoming. That the womb will birth them too soon, or worse, that it will decide it does not need them at all.

So they stack their cairns.  
They carve their spirals.  
They sing their young strong.  
They wait inside the argument with the patience of something that has all the time in the world because the world is still inventing time.

And sometimes, when the light blades wheel just right and the membranes tear in perfect unison, they will turn toward you—all their many faces rearranging into the same expression at once—and you will understand that they have been waiting for you specifically. That you, too, are part of the conversation now.

They offer you a hand. It is still growing even as it reaches.

Take it or don’t.  

The mountain is still deciding what happens to anything that enters its throat while it is still learning how to speak.
