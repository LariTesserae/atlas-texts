Here lives the *Resonant*.

It has no single body. It *is* the refusal of bodies to stay separate.

At the scale of your ankle it wears the Carpet: a billion jointed limbs moving as one slow muscle. The translucent millipedes are its forward taste buds, forever tasting tomorrow; the dinner-plate isopods are its molars, grinding the bacterial felt into new loam; the pale spiders are its tactile nerves, legs braided so tightly that when one twitches the tremor travels a hundred meters in a heartbeat. When you step, the Carpet exhales. The hiss you hear is not protest. It is the sound of ten thousand mouths tasting the salts of your sole and deciding, for now, that you are still stone.

Higher up, the Resonant wears larger shapes the way a sea wears whales.

The great centipedes—*Thighcoil*, the speleologists who first catalogued them called them—are its hands. Each segment is the length of a human femur, and the whole animal moves with the lazy certainty of a priest. Their venom is not poison; it is conversion. Where it drips, blue foxfire blooms for an hour, marking the stone so that every smaller creature within sight knows: this place has been claimed for digestion. Even the centipedes themselves are not exempt. Tiny white remora-mites ride in the seams of their armor, eating them alive from the inside while the centipede, in turn, eats the mites’ cousins. The knot tightens. The Resonant smiles with every mouth at once.

In the skin-warm pools live the *Heartfish*. They are silver only because their blood is visible; the scales long ago became transparent as intention. Their only purpose is to remember rhythm. Each pool is a drum. The Heartfish beat against the stone with their thin bellies in perfect synchrony with the ceiling’s hollow bell-tone. When the cavern groans, the fish accelerate, driving the water into standing waves that slap the surrounding Carpet and tell every creature within fifty meters: *The world still has bones. Keep moving.* They are the pulse. They are eaten by the isopods that dip their faces into the shallows like camels, and in being eaten they become the next generation of isopods. Nothing is lost. The Resonant has perfect metabolism.

Above all this, riding the thermals of ten million respirations, drift the *Lumen*. These are not insects. They are closer to living sheet music. Colonies of luminous rotifers, each individual smaller than a pinprick, cling to any host that will carry them. Together they form saddle blankets of soft orange or acid green that flare when the host is threatened. The light is not warning. It is auction. *Here is fresh meat,* the lights sing in frequencies just below hearing. *Come and bid with your jaws.* The moment the host is swarmed, the Lumen detach, riding the new bodies upward like souls changing horses. They have been changing hosts for three million years without ever touching the ground. They are the Resonant’s only memory of sky.

And somewhere—never in the same place twice—moves the *Singer*.

It is the size of a child and the color of wet bone. It has too many legs and too many voices. Where it passes, the Carpet parts like theater curtains. The clicking and stridulation and wet burbling all braid themselves into something that feels, for three full seconds, like language. Then the Singer vanishes beneath a living hill and the roar swallows its own尾. No one has ever seen it clearly. Some speleologists claim it is a single animal. Others insist it is the momentary consensus of a million smaller animals that have decided, for one lucid instant, to wear the same shape.

All of them—Carpet, Thighcoil, Heartfish, Lumen, Singer—are organs of the same slow thought. The cavern is not their home. The cavern is their exoskeleton, grown outward over four hundred thousand years until the limestone itself learned how to hunger.

When the Carpet climbs your boots it does so with the gentle, clinical curiosity of a tongue checking a sore tooth. It is not attacking. It is *considering*. For a brief, wet moment you are a question the Resonant has never asked before: something warm, something loud, something that arrived from the direction the ceiling sometimes screams.

The question is pleasant. It tastes of iron and novelty.

The Carpet climbs higher.

And the Resonant, vast and eyeless and endlessly polite, begins—politely—to taste the rest of you.
