**The Hollow Lattice is lived in by those who have learned to be small.**

First come the ones that are barely separate from the world at all.

Across the vast living membrane between Colossi move the **Mossbacks**—six-limbed browsers the size of small horses. Their shaggy pelts are not fur but a symbiotic pelt of the same moss that carpets the world; it grows thicker in winter, thinner in the brief thaws, so that from a distance a herd looks like slow landscape simply detaching itself and wandering. Their eyes are milky and slow-blinking, almost unnecessary. They navigate by taste and vibration, dragging long sensitive tongues across the skin of the planet and reading the chemical gossip of distant Colossi. When a Colossus sings its Long Conversation, the herds freeze mid-step, legs locked, moss bristling, until the vibration passes. Some of them never move again. Their bodies become new hummocks of green, accepted back into the membrane without ceremony.

Higher up, in the vaults and hanging gardens of the Colossi, live the **Glimmerwings**. They are not insects in any earthly sense. Each one is a translucent membrane stretched over a lattice of cartilage, large as a condor, veined with living light. They do not flap so much as *resonate*, riding the constant updrafts and singing back to the Colossi in frequencies the plants understand. When they die they do not fall. They are reabsorbed through the aerial roots, their cartilage woven into new growth. The natives believe the Glimmerwings are the lattice’s eyes, that through them the world watches itself grow.

Lower, always lower, hunt the **Choir**. These are the predators. Long, low, multi-jointed things like otters stretched by nightmare into eight-meter lengths. Their bodies are hairless, glossy, the color of wet bone. They do not hunt by sight or scent but by *harmony*. Each pack sings a chord; the sound wraps around buttresses and returns changed. Where the echo comes back wrong, there is prey. Their throats contain secondary resonating chambers grown from the same cellulose as the Colossi. When they kill, they do it gently—almost reverently—pressing their faces into the throat of a Mossback until its pulse syncs with their own and then simply stops. The Choir never waste a body. What they cannot eat, the moss will.

And then there are the **Lattice-Born**.

They do not call themselves human. They have no word that maps to that concept. The tallest among them might reach your shoulder; their limbs are too long, their necks too graceful, their skin the color of moonlight on frost. Their eyes are enormous, all pupil, adapted to the pewter dusk and the slow pulses of lichen-light. Where hair should be they have fine white rootlets that move on their own, tasting the air.

They are not a single people. Each settlement belongs to the hollow where it was grown. One lives inside the thigh-bone curve of a fallen Colossus and calls itself *Vein*. Another inhabits a high gallery of fused trunks and answers only to *Song-That-Remains*. They do not travel for pleasure. They travel when the lattice tells them to—when a new Colossus begins its decades-long birth and the membrane ripples for hundreds of kilometers, the Lattice-Born feel it in their bones and begin the long walk. Their children are born with soft green veins across their bellies. Some of these veins flower. Some of them fruit. The ones that fruit are taken gently away by the elders and pressed into the living wood of the nearest Colossus. No one weeps. This is simply what happens to some of them.

Their language has no future tense. Only *now* and *the Long Conversation*. When the subsonic thrum begins—when every trunk and root and membrane stiffens—they go silent for days. Some stand with palms flat against the wood, eyes closed, letting the vibration reshape the marrow of their thoughts. Others curl fetal against the warm inner walls of their hollows and dream the dreams the lattice dreams. Those who survive these dreams come back changed. Their root-hair grows thicker. Their voices drop half an octave. They begin to smell of sap after rain.

There are older things too.

In the deep cavities where three or more Colossi have grown together over centuries, something gathers. The natives will not name it. They leave offerings of meteoric iron blades and small carvings of unborn Mossbacks, then withdraw. These are places where the boundary between *creature* and *world* has begun to dissolve. Sometimes a Lattice-Born elder walks into one of these convergences and does not come out. Sometimes something else walks out wearing the elder’s skin like a cloak that doesn’t quite fit anymore. Its eyes are too green. Its joints bend wrong. It speaks perfectly, but the moss on the floor leans toward it when it passes.

And deeper still—perhaps only once in a generation—a Colossus will dream so intensely that part of itself *stands up*. These are the **Awakened**. Thirty, forty meters tall, a walking thicket of trunk and root and slow green thought. They do not speak. They simply walk across the membrane for a season or two, leaving footprints that become lakes, then close again. The Lattice-Born follow at a distance, singing soft counter-harmonies to the Long Conversation, trying to soothe the giant back into stillness before it tears the world’s skin beyond healing.

This is the ecology of becoming.

Everything here is negotiating its own existence with something that has not yet decided what it is. The Mossbacks are the world’s slow thoughts. The Choir are its hunger. The Glimmerwings are its curiosity. The Lattice-Born are its uneasy conscience—small, brave, temporary voices trying to talk a god into kindness while it is still young enough to change its mind.

They all know the same truth:

One day the lattice will finish learning how to be a world.

On that day it may no longer need pilgrims crawling across its freezing hide. It may no longer need eyes, or voices, or dreams that walk around wearing borrowed skin.

Until then, everything that lives here does so by permission, and on sufferance, and with a kind of trembling love that has no other place to go.
