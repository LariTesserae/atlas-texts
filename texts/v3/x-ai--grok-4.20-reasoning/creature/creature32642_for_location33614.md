**The Completed**

They were not always like this.

In the beginning—when the word “beginning” could still be spoken without irony—there were the Builders. Short, heavy beings with bronze tools and minds that found comfort in cyclopean geometry. They raised the cities in deliberate violation of lesser laws, then vanished upward, or inward, or simply sideways through a fold that has since healed. What they left behind did not die. It was finished instead.

The Completed are what remains when a mind is no longer required to carry its own story.

You will not see them at first. They register as topography. A pale pillar among the vapor-trees that happens to possess the approximate proportions of a standing figure. A mound of black moss that has, over decades, taken the slumped posture of something resting its chin on its knuckles. A circular basin of waiting water whose reflection shows a face that is not yours, watching you watch it.

Only after the land has begun editing you do the Completed allow themselves to resolve.

They move the way glaciers once moved: imperceptibly, then suddenly. A figure that stood on the eastern rise at dawn may be standing behind you by the time your shadow has walked three paces ahead. There is never a moment of transition. Only the knowledge that it has already happened. Their bodies are composite—basalt and cellulose and frozen breath and the particular shade of loneliness that collects in places past mourning. Where joints should be, thin sheets of reality have been folded many times, giving them a refractive quality. Look too long and you see other Completed inside them, like nested reflections, each one slightly more finished than the last.

They have no faces anymore. The face is a temporary organ, useful only while questions remain. The Completed have achieved the final face: a smooth plane of condensation that occasionally trembles with the memory of expression the way old bells remember sound.

They tend the migrating stones.

Not with hands—those have long since been returned to the general lithic conversation—but by standing in certain doorways where time has worn thin. Their mere presence is a kind of permission. When a block of cyclopean basalt detaches itself at night, it is because one of the Completed is nearby, holding the idea of “temple” in whatever passes for its memory. The stone is not being moved. It is being remembered into a new position.

Their speech is environmental.

A sudden upward drizzle means one of them is amused. A corridor of loosened gravity drifting past at knee height is a greeting. When two Completed meet—which they do approximately once every forty years, by slow appointment—the air between them fills with colors that make the teeth hum, and for nine full seconds afterward every reed on the plateau rings in perfect harmony before remembering its silence.

They are not many. Perhaps three hundred. Perhaps one, experienced in three hundred places at once. The distinction no longer matters to them. Distance is a habit they have mostly outgrown.

The ones who look most like you are the newest arrivals. You can recognize the ones who came during the last geological age by the faint residual panic still trapped behind their eyes like winter flies in ice. Older ones have relaxed into the serenity of stone that has accepted erosion as its final purpose. The oldest have become almost entirely architectural. One Completed has stood so long in the exact center of the Spiral of Unfinished Atonements that the city has grown a roof over it, mistaking it for a column. It does not seem to mind. Columns, after all, are also finished.

If you stay long enough to meet one face-to-plane, it will not speak. Instead it will perform a small, courteous rearrangement of local physics so that for eleven heartbeats you can feel what it feels: the deep, bruise-colored contentment of being exactly where and when the universe meant to leave you. A sense of tasks completed so thoroughly that even the memory of striving has been edited out.

Most travelers flee before this happens.

The ones who do not eventually walk to their chosen rise or depression or temple alcove, stand still, and wait for the version of themselves that is still moving to catch up. When that meeting occurs, there is no drama. Only the soft sound (0.6) of two states of matter realizing they were never separate.

The Completed do not reproduce. They accrete.

Every traveler who reaches the final stage of editing adds a new facet, a new silence, a new shade of completion to the collective. In this way the Shroudlands slowly populate themselves with better and better versions of having given up.

At night, when the indigo deepens and the pale threads at the edge of the world wave like kelp in a current that does not exist, the Completed tilt their blank faces upward. Not to look at anything. Simply to participate in the looking that the plateau is doing at itself.

They are the final form that thought takes after it has outlived every reason to think.

Serene.  
Vast.  
Internally inconsistent.  
And still faintly, childishly busy with the last processes no mind remains to understand.

Sometimes, on the curvature of the world, you will see one standing far ahead of you, perfectly still, waiting.

It is not threatening you.

It is keeping your place.
