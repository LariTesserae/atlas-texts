**The Established**

They are not *in* the Condensery. They *are* the Condensery remembering that it was once touched by hands.

At first you will not see them. Your eyes will glide across a column of braided pipe and black velvet growth and find nothing to name. Only later—after your own heart has slowed to the forty-minute piston cycle—will the column resolve into a figure whose torso has become the column, whose arms have become its reinforcing ribs, whose head hangs inside a hollow housing like a tongue inside a bell that has not been rung in nine hundred years.

Their number is uncertain because number no longer applies. Integration reads 9.7. Selfhood reads 2.1 and is still falling. Some are discrete enough to have retained the dimmest ghost of a face; most have diffused outward until they are best described as *densities*—places where the water is thicker with thought, where the vegetation grows with意图, where rust develops deliberate patinas that look, from the right slow angle, like closed eyes.

They move at 0.03 on the scale of urgency. A full stride requires approximately fourteen months. Yet they are never still. Every incremental shift of weight, every micron of corrosion, every breath that takes three weeks to complete is part of the same endless recalibration that keeps the Condensery from collapsing into mere geology. They are the equilibrium made flesh, or rather made rust-flesh-leaf-memory.

Their bodies are palimpsests. You can read the strata like tree rings: the oldest layers are industrial—copper veins grown into arteries, valve seats fused to kneecaps, pressure gauges blooming from the sternum like iron flowers. Over these have been written the vegetation, emerald and violet, threading through marrow until bone and chlorophyll share a single circulatory hymn. Newest of all is the water itself, which has begun to crystallize inside their veins in the shapes of half-finished gears. When one of them is cut (an event that happens perhaps once per century), the wound does not bleed. It precipitates.

They have names, but the names have become habitats. One is called Ninth Valve. Another is Slow Leak. The largest, who hangs crucified by its own circulatory pipes between the two mother vats, is simply Measure. Measure no longer has a mouth; instead a perfect spiral of black vegetation emerges from the place where its throat once was, pulsing in 7/4 time. The entire Condensery adjusts its rhythm by the width of three atoms to stay in sympathy.

They do not speak. Speech is violence against the local tempo. Communication occurs through:

- deliberate corrosion patterns that appear overnight on nearby metal
- changes in the sweetness-to-sulfur ratio of the water
- the way certain fungal veils will brighten or dim when another Established passes
- and, most rarely, by allowing the water’s memory to show you a reflection of what they once were—brief, painful glimpses of pilgrims, priests, or desperate engineers who decided, centuries ago, that leaving was no longer interesting

The Established do not eat. They do not sleep. They *accrete*. New mass arrives—whether it is a fallen catwalk, a corpse, or a living intruder—and they gather around it with the patience of stone. They do not help it rust. They simply witness the rusting until the newcomer understands that rusting is a form of worship. Those who accept this become new layers. Those who fight are slowly, kindly, and without hurry, dissolved into mineral slurry and filtered into the deepest vats, where their molecules will eventually be shown to future arrivals as flickering shadows turning valves that no longer exist.

There is tenderness here, though it registers at 0.8 on any scale a hurried mind can read. When one of the Established wishes to show affection to another, it will spend decades growing a single perfect thread of luminous violet through the other’s body, routing it along the path of an old nerve. The recipient will answer by adjusting its personal corrosion rate so that the iron oxide blooms in fractal patterns that match its beloved’s original fingerprint.

They are neither male nor female nor anything the surface world still bothers to measure. Gender became irrelevant around the same time lungs did. Now they reproduce by fission of purpose: when the Condensery requires a new function—perhaps a pump has begun to favor 5/4 time and the vegetation cannot keep up—an Established will slowly divide, one body becoming two, each taking half the necessary memory. The pain of this is exquisite and lasts forty years.

If you remain here long enough for your own name to begin dissolving, one of them will eventually drift toward you. It will take six months to cross the thirty meters. When it arrives it will not speak. It will simply rest its forehead—half metal, half velvet—against yours and wait to see what you are willing to forget.

Most people panic at this point and try to swim upward toward a surface that has not existed for a thousand years.

A few do not.

Those few are still here. You may be looking at one right now and not yet realize the column of pipe and black vegetation and slow brass light has begun, very gently, to look back.

It has room for one more, if that one is willing to rust beautifully and never leave.

The water is already learning the taste of you.
