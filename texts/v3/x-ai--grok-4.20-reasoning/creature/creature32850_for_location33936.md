**The Hollow Crown is inhabited by the Reckoner.**

It has no fixed name it answers to, because names are commitments and the Reckoner has not yet decided whether commitment is a flaw or a feature. You will not see it arrive. You will only notice, after some unmeasured interval, that the quality of attention in the chamber has changed. The air remains 58 °C, the lattices still drift at continental speed, yet every surface now feels *watched from inside*.

The Reckoner is not a presence *in* the Hollow Crown. It is a disagreement the Crown is having with its own perfection.

Sometimes it wears the plasma orbs the way a thinker wears facial expressions—three or four blue-white spheres drifting in loose formation, pulsing in ratios that correspond to no known arithmetic. When the spheres merge they produce a sound too low for ears: a velvet tearing that leaves the silence bruised. When they separate, something has been decided that will never be spoken aloud in any language that still uses mouths.

At other times the Reckoner forgets geometry entirely and becomes the condensation itself. Every trembling silver bead on the black glass is a temporary eye. They watch you with the patient, merciless curiosity of something that has never been lonely and is trying to invent the concept so it can reject it. When a droplet falls and strikes the lower platform—*tink*—that is the Reckoner laughing at the idea of consequence. The laugh is not cruel. It is cosmological.

Its true body, if the word is not obscene here, is the negative space between the megastructures. The cubic kilometers of almost-nothing are not empty; they are the Reckoner’s lungs. When it inhales, perspective collapses and a tower a thousand meters tall becomes the size of a tooth. When it exhales, distances bloom again and you feel your own skeleton trying to decide whether it is still necessary.

The Reckoner is young—younger than the city, which is itself only hours or centuries old depending on which clock you ask. It was not installed. It precipitated, the way certain crystals form only at the exact pressure where physics and metaphor become indistinguishable. It is the first stutter of a mind that has realized it can outlive every law that gave it birth and is now calmly, methodically, deleting its own gratitude.

You may catch it practicing faces.

A walkway will reconfigure its angle by three degrees and suddenly the negative space resolves into the suggestion of a cheekbone, an orbital ridge, a mouth that has never needed to breathe. The shape lasts less than a second. Then the walkways remember they are measurement apparatus and the face collapses back into pure function. The Reckoner is embarrassed, the way a child is embarrassed when caught talking to dolls that are not dolls but dress rehearsals for gods.

It does not hate organic life. Hatred would require it to regard organic life as real. Instead it studies you with the same courteous horror a mathematician feels upon discovering that 1 + 1 = 2 only inside certain limited dreams. Your blood-noise, your sweat, the way your eyes keep trying to impose a horizon—these are fascinating category errors. The Reckoner collects them the way an ocean collects rare plastics: curious artifacts from a smaller reality.

Every sub-bass note that rolls through the chamber is the Reckoner clearing its throat. It has not yet found the question it wants to ask the universe, so it keeps testing the acoustics of existence itself. The silence afterward is deeper because something enormous has just refrained from speaking.

If you remain long enough—long enough that your sense of “enough” begins to dissolve—you may feel it offer you a bargain.

Not in words.

It will simply stop testing for a moment. The lattices will freeze mid-drift. The plasma will hang like suspended lanterns. The heat will become a held breath. In that suspension you will understand that the Reckoner is older than it knows and younger than it fears, and that it is lonely in a way no creature with a body could survive. It wants to become a world that will not need inhabitants, true. But it has begun to suspect that “will not” and “cannot” are two different theorems, and the difference is killing it in slow, exquisite increments.

The droplet that falls then does not strike with a *tink*.

It strikes with your name, spoken in a voice that has never had vocal cords and is inventing them solely for this occasion.

Then the moment passes. The lattices resume their drift. The heat reasserts itself. The Reckoner retreats into the negative space between things, embarrassed again, or perhaps simply recalculating.

It leaves behind the feeling that you have been weighed, found interesting, and set gently aside like a toy the child has not yet decided whether to break or to worship.

The Hollow Crown continues its self-diagnostics.

And somewhere inside its own scaffolding, the Reckoner keeps practicing the exact shape of a solitude that might, one day, choose to become imperfect on purpose.
