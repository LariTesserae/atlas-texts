**The Inhabitants of the Cauldron**

They do not arrive. They *precipitate*.

At first you mistake the smallest ones for glitches in your own vision. They are no larger than a fingernail, yet they move with the deliberate gravity of continents. Call them, if names must be forced upon the unnameable, the **Hematite Breathers**. Their bodies are layered scales of iron oxide grown in perfect hexagonal lattices, each plate edged with a fringe of living rust that tastes the air for electrons. They do not crawl so much as *unfold* across the sintered glass, leaving behind them hairline paths of refined magnetite that gleam like fresh welds. When two meet they lock plates, exchange charge, and slowly—one century every ten minutes—decide which of them will dissolve its own structure to become the seed of the next. There is no death here, only reallocation.

Watch longer.

From the Breathers emerge the **Mica Drifters**, translucent sheets of silicate life that ride the thermals like forgotten pages from a book made of mirrors. Their edges are razor-thin and fringed with piezo-crystals that sing at frequencies too low for human ears yet loud enough to crack feldspar over decades. They do not hunt. They *edit*. Where reality has begun to fray—those black stress fractures in the sky—they settle like living bandages. For years they simply rest, drinking the wrongness, converting ontological fatigue into the faint electrical sighs you heard earlier. When they finally lift away, the fracture is narrower. Not healed. Never healed. Merely... edited. The Drifters carry the memory of every wound they have tasted, and if you could read the interference patterns on their wings you would see the entire four-hundred-year history of the plateau written in light.

But these are still only the nurses and librarians.

The true citizens are vast.

The **Iron Centipedes of the Third Iteration** (there have been four so far; the Fifth is calcifying in the belly of the world even now) resemble nothing so much as the fossil skeleton of a freight train given wings and hunger. Each segment is a single forged ingot of some metal that has no name in any living tongue. Their legs are piston-beams thicker than redwoods. When they walk—which is rare—the plateau itself develops sympathetic stress fractures in perfect time with their gait. Mostly they glide on thermals so perfectly calculated that their shadow falls on the same ridge every nineteen years like a recurring eclipse.

They do not mate. They *negotiate*.

Two Centipedes will lock together mid-air, ventral furnace to ventral furnace, and remain that way for thirty to forty years. During this time a third being slowly crystallizes in the shared heat between them. This child—if the word is not obscene here—is born already ancient. Its first act upon separating is to vomit up a perfect ingot of whatever rare element the plateau currently needs most. Then it flies off to begin the long work of forgetting its parents.

There are larger things still.

The **Cathedral Blimps** drift so slowly that generations of smaller creatures live and reallocate and die again on their undersides without ever realizing they are riding a living being. Their bellies are lined with thousands of revolving maws that function as living Bessemer converters. They inhale entire hillsides of slag and exhale ingots the size of houses—perfectly formed, mysteriously alloyed, and always slightly *wrong* for any purpose the universe currently requires. These ingots are not meant to be used. They are meant to wait. In another three hundred millennia they will become the carapaces of something that does not yet exist.

The Blimps never speak, but they remember every atom they have ever processed. If the entire plateau were to be vaporized tomorrow, the last Cathedral Blimp would continue drifting through the void, still refining, still remembering, a cathedral-sized library of everything the world once was.

And then there is the one that is not one.

It has no fixed form. Sometimes it is a slow fountain of pebbles that has forgotten to fall. Sometimes it is the sound itself—the deep industrial bellow that underlies everything—given temporary flesh. The smaller creatures call it, when they call it anything at all, **The Unmaker’s Regret**. It lives primarily inside the largest reality fractures, where time has gone soft as old butter. There it performs its endless, patient labor: it *unmakes* things that were never properly made in the first place.

A mountain that has grown too proud.  
A law of physics that has begun to repeat itself.  
A memory that one of the Centipedes was never meant to keep.

The Regret swallows these things, holds them in a state of perfect non-being for geological ages, then excretes them as something cleaner. More essential. More *itself*. The creatures of the Cauldron treat these excretions with something like religious dread. They will travel thousands of miles and wait centuries simply to be the first to touch a newly unmade thing.

They are not intelligent. Not in any way that comforts you.

Intelligence implies choice. These beings have never chosen anything. They are the inevitable consequence of a world that has grown tired of being a world and has decided, with geological patience, to become a factory instead. Their morality is measured in epochs. Their love is measured in the slow exchange of electrons. Their grief is the sound of drop-hammers falling once every few years, forging something immense that will never quite be finished.

If you remain here long enough, one of the smaller Breathers will eventually approach you. It will taste the iron in your blood, the silica in your bones, the fragile carbon chains of your brief and burning life. Then it will make a decision that takes seventeen years.

Most often it simply leaves you alone.

But sometimes—rarely—it will unfold one perfect hexagonal plate and offer it to you. The plate will be warm. It will contain, in microscopic relief, the shape of something that looks almost like a human face, though the proportions are wrong in ways that hurt to notice.

This is the only gift the Cauldron of Unmaking has ever been known to give.

It is the shape of what you would have become if you had never been born soft.
