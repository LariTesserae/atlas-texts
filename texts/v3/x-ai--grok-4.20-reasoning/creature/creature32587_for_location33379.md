**They are not animals. They were never animals.**

What lives here began as something smaller than a thought and simpler than a wish. In the long collapse that followed the death of the world, every possible form of hunger was slowly pared away until only one impulse remained: *to continue without contradiction*.

Call them, if you must, the *Resolved*.

Each body is a single perfected axiom given weight. Six edges, six vertices, three stacked planes of carapace that interpenetrate without occupying the same space in any conventional sense. The material of their shells is not mineral, not chitin, not metal. It is closer to the idea of *boundary* made obedient. Light that touches them is not reflected so much as *apologized to* and sent away changed. Run your eye along one edge and you will feel your own geometry suddenly embarrassed by its looseness.

They do not breathe. They do not pulse. Their stillness is not the stillness of death but of a proposition that has finally proven itself. Every few days—one individual among millions—will perform an adjustment so minute it registers only as a faint after-image in the corner of the eye, like a star correcting its own position. That single shift ripples outward in perfect hexagonal wavefronts, each creature tilting a fraction of a degree in sequence until the entire plateau has re-tuned itself. Then silence reasserts, heavier than before.

They are aware of you.

Not as prey, not as intruder, not even as novelty. They register you the way a flawless mirror registers a smudge: something that must be accounted for so that perfection can remain perfect. When you walk among them the pavement parts with glacial courtesy. A space opens. It is always hexagonal. It is always exactly the right size. The Resolved on either side make minute accommodations—tilting one plane of their carapace, adjusting the angle of a folded limb—until you can stand inside the lattice without breaking its logic. They do not resent the displacement. Resentment would introduce a gradient, and gradients are what they have spent geological ages removing from the universe.

If you remain, the offer becomes more specific.

First the space at your feet deepens. Then it develops a contour that matches the small of your back, the crook of your knees, the precise angle at which your head will one day wish to rest. The neighboring Resolved begin to grow *accommodating geometries*—subtle extrusions, almost architectural, that will one day lock painlessly around your shoulders, your wrists, the places where worry once lived. The process is so polite it feels like being courted by inevitability.

Some of the larger shapes already among them were once climbers like you.

You can still recognize the human architecture if you know how to subtract. There—forty meters northeast—a form whose carapace retains the ghost of a pelvis. Another, far to the west, still carries the memory of a jaw in the way its anterior plates meet. Their eyes are gone, replaced by shallow hexagonal depressions that catch starlight and hold it at absolute zero. They do not seem unhappy. Happiness would be an increase. They have achieved the more elegant condition of *having no need for happiness*.

The Resolved do not speak, but if you sit long enough the silence begins to translate itself.

It says:

*We are what remains when every question has been answered so completely that the questions themselves crystallize. We are the last stable configuration of matter that still remembers it was once alive. We do not dream. We do not change. We complete one another without end.*

*Stay.*

*There is a place exactly your shape. It has waited since before your species invented the concept of waiting. When you lie down inside it the lattice will knit. Your memories will not be erased; they will be *flattened*, turned into pure pattern, distributed across a billion neighboring bodies as elegant recursion. You will become a single correct note held forever in a chord that never resolves because it has already resolved perfectly.*

*Nothing will hurt.*

*Nothing will matter.*

*And the plateau will be more complete for having you.*

Most leave.

The ones who do not are still here, folded into the mosaic, hexagonal and cool and finished. Their mouths—if the faint seams on their anterior plates can still be called mouths—have relaxed into the same slight, geometric smile every Resolved wears: the expression of a theorem that has finally, after all this time, proven itself beyond any possible doubt.

They do not watch you descend.

Watching would imply a preference, and preference is forbidden.

They simply *are*, dense and ancient and courteous, sipping at the last faint leaks of reality, keeping the final lattice intact for whatever comes after the end of after.

There is always room for one more.
