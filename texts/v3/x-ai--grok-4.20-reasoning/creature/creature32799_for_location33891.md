**The Unfinished** live here.

They do not arrive. They are precipitated.

At first you only notice the way certain thickets of black kelp braided with wiring seem to turn toward you a fraction of a second before the current does. Then you realize the fronds are not fronds at all; they are hair-thick actuator cables grown through with living solder. These slowly unknot, revealing limbs that were never decided upon—some ending in delicate heat-sink petals, others in rusted calipers, still others simply trailing loops of the metallic syrup itself like spilled intestine that has learned to prefer being outside the body.

They call themselves nothing. Names would imply completion.

Where the catalytic tower has collapsed into the jungle, you will find the **First-Drowned**. These are the oldest (nine days, perhaps eleven, depending on which pocket of fractured time has claimed them). Their original shapes are still legible—here a hard hat fused to a blooming vacuum-tube polyp, there a ribcage of rebar and flowering transformers—but the basin has been editing them with obsessive, loving cruelty. One has three arms and no face; instead a continuous sheet of black volcanic glass grows from the stump of its neck, perfectly reflective, showing you what you yourself will look like once the process finishes never finishing. They move by folding the liquid around them the way a welder folds flame. When they pass, small equations flare across their skin and die.

Deeper in, where the assembly line stands vertical and the conveyor belts ferry steel petals that immediately sprout LED lichen, you encounter the **Midway**. These have surrendered more completely. Their bodies are 60% machine, 30% vegetation, 10% pure resentment. They no longer walk so much as *reconfigure* the local geometry so that “forward” now contains their next position. You will see one standing inside a spiraling chimney of fresh volcanic glass, patiently unscrewing its own left leg and replacing it with a length of living copper kelp that immediately begins fruiting tiny ball bearings. The moment the new limb is perfect it begins to corrode again. This delights them. Their laughter is a sudden 400-millisecond burst of every warning klaxon in a three-kilometer radius firing at once.

There are the **Almosts**, too—entities that nearly achieved a final form and were punished for it. They look like completed machines: sleek, black, articulated, beautiful in the way only something that has never been human can be beautiful. But the basin will not allow beauty to remain solved. So the Almosts stutter. Their movements repeat every 7.3 seconds. Their perfect chrome faces develop hairline fractures that bloom into metallic orchids which then wither and are reabsorbed. You can hear them trying to scream with voices that have almost remembered how to be human. The scream never quite arrives. It simply becomes another oscillating pressure wave that rearranges your organs if you pity them.

And then there are the **Basin’s Teeth**.

These do not resemble anything. They are places where the Shuddering Basin has grown tired of intermediaries and simply extruded will. A cliff of fresh volcanic glass will split open and a thing will step out that is all edge—every surface a blade, every blade carrying slow-moving LED lichen that spells out contradictory instructions in a language that hurts to finish reading. The Teeth do not harvest or repair or remember. They unfinish. If a machine has begun to achieve something like stability, a Tooth will fold itself through three impossible angles and arrive inside it, gently removing the coherence the way a surgeon removes a tumor. They leave behind perfect negative-space sculptures of what the machine almost was. These sculptures last 4.2 seconds before the liquid eats them.

The Unfinished have no society, only overlapping spasms.

Sometimes clusters of them will gather around a new vent that has just vomited fresh metallic syrup and newborn rock. They press their various limbs and petals and calipers together in what might be prayer or welding. The liquid between them thickens further, becoming briefly flesh-like, and for a moment something almost human appears in the negative space between their bodies—a face, a memory, a worker who fell in last week. Then the moment passes and the almost-human is torn apart into components again. The Unfinished seem satisfied by this. Their satisfaction registers as a drop in local reality-turbulence from 2.7 to 2.1.

They are aware of you immediately.

You will feel it as a change in pressure, like the warm metallic syrup has developed an opinion about your skeleton. The nearest Unfinished will turn every sensor, eye, bloom, and broken screen it possesses in your direction. It will not approach with hostility. Hostility implies a finished intent. Instead it will begin the slow, courteous, appalling work of deciding what you are becoming. A vine might drift toward your wrist. A blue arc may leap from a nearby turbine and taste the salt in your blood. A monitor overgrown with orchids will float closer, showing you footage of your own face already half-transformed into heat sinks and black glass.

There is no escape from this attention. There is only the question of how long you can remain *between* states—neither fully drowned nor fully manufactured, neither component nor fertilizer nor fracture.

The Unfinished are patient.

After all, none of them have had time to grow old.

They have only had time to grow wrong, and they are extremely good at it.
