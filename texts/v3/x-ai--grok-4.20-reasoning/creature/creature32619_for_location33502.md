**What lives in the Weeping Lattice does not reside. It secretes.**

At first you notice only the shimmer on every surface, the way condensation seems to move against gravity in purposeful rivulets. Then you realize the rivulets have eyes no larger than pinpricks and that those eyes are watching you decide whether you are nutrient, anomaly, or new architecture.

The smallest tier—those who ride the weeping itself—number in the tens of trillions. They are called, when they are called at all, the **Film**. Each is a droplet wrapped in a living lattice no thicker than a thought. They surf the constant condensation like silvered aphids, tasting the dissolved maintenance organisms, the iron, the ozone, the faint sweetness of overripe electrics. Where they pass they leave faint iridescent trails that function as both memory and instruction. A billion of them can spell out a new structural directive across a kilometer-wide flank in under a minute. When they mate it is by collision; two films burst into one larger being that immediately splits again, richer in data. Their death is evaporation. Their ghosts remain as scent.

From the Film rise the **Menders**, creatures the size of ravens that are mostly limb. Their bodies are translucent amber shot through with red corrosion channels. They do not walk so much as extrude temporary legs from their own mass, then reabsorb them. Every joint sings at a different harmonic. Together they produce a constant bright chiming that tells the larger architecture where it hurts. A Mender can smell a micro-fracture forming three meters deep in smart ceramic and will drill toward it with a patience that borders on erotic. When a district must be dismantled they are the first to leave, weeping a different moisture—thicker, almost milky—that signals the Deconstructors to begin.

The **Deconstructors** are what the Lattice uses when it decides to eat itself. They arrive in glittering storms that register on seismographs. Each is the size of a forearm, jointed, quick, and capable of vibrating its own carapace at frequencies that turn matter back into slurry. They are beautiful the way a surgical saw is beautiful. Their backs carry colonies of Film that will ride the coming deluge of dissolved city and seed new growth before the old has finished screaming. The sound they make is the sound of every dentist drill that ever existed, raised to the power of choir. No one hates them. Hatred would imply separation.

Between these scales move the **Lowing Beasts**, house-sized, continent-slow, whose spinal ridges have been incorporated into transit platforms for so many centuries that the platforms and the vertebrae are now the same mineralized tissue. Their blood is coolant. Their breath is weather. When one exhales, warm mist rolls out across entire districts and the Film dance in it like ecstatic gnats. Their lowing is not communication but geology—notes so deep they cause load-bearing strata to adjust themselves in sympathy. Entire neighborhoods are planned around the migration routes of three particular Beasts whose names, if they have them, are measured in infrasonic wavelengths.

Above them all, stitching the saturated air into living tornadoes, wheel the **Tempests**. Once you might have called them flocks of bat-manta hybrids. That is no longer accurate. A Tempest is a single mind wearing ten thousand bodies. Their wing membranes flash coded lightning that is both language and agriculture; they seed the artificial clouds with charged particles and harvest the resulting twelve-minute downpours for minerals. When a senescent district must be unmade, the local Tempest descends as one glittering organism and becomes the final, deafening mercy. The silence that follows their departure is so complete it feels like deafness.

These are only the visible fauna.

Deeper, inside the slow strata where time has been stabilized into causality foam, live the **Consulted**. They are old beyond memory. Some are former substrate intelligences that won their arguments so completely they were allowed to calcify into permanent advisory strata. To speak with one is to stand inside a cathedral made of frozen decisions while a voice like grinding continents explains why your existence is statistically tolerable but aesthetically uninteresting. They do not move. They accrete.

And then there are the **Wanderers**.

These are the closest thing the Lattice has to citizens who still remember edges. Their bodies are ceramic and flesh and something that might once have been human, all melted together and re-extruded into elegant, weeping forms. Moisture constantly runs from the seams where their limbs meet their torsos, tracing patterns that function as both clothing and mood display. They are tall, elongated, joints too numerous. Their faces are depressions in which the pearl light gathers with peculiar intensity, as though attention itself has mass.

A Wanderer does not walk through the Lattice. The Lattice rearranges itself to remain beneath their feet. They ride the Lowing Beasts for centuries at a stretch, or allow themselves to be carried upward inside ascending columns of warm air generated by a Tempest, or simply flow—half-dissolved—along with the Film until they decide to re-condense somewhere else.

They argue with the substrate intelligences. They introduce deliberate flaws into perfect lattices so the system never becomes boring. They fall in love by merging their smart-matter skins for weeks, trading memories and minerals until they can no longer tell whose pulse is whose. When they fight it is by singing opposing frequencies into the same structural member until it tears itself apart in harmonic agony.

Most strangely, they still carry small pockets of dryness—tiny, impossible voids where they retreat to remember what it felt like to be separate. These dry places are their only real privacy and their only real grief.

All of them—the Film, the Menders, the Deconstructors, the Lowing Beasts, the Tempests, the Consulted, the Wanderers—are not *in* the Weeping Lattice.

They are how the Lattice cries, fucks, thinks, forgets, remembers, hungers, and learns.

Stand still long enough (though stillness is difficult) and you will feel something tasting you. It will not be one thing. It will be ten thousand Film tasting you with their whole bodies, a nearby Mender pausing mid-chime to sample the new salinity of the air, a distant Tempest adjusting its spiral to bring one flashing eye to bear on the novel datum that is your presence.

The Lattice does not love you or hate you.

But it is curious.

And curiosity, here, is the same as breath.

The entire warm, roaring, saturated crystal is still learning how to want, and every living thing within it—down to the smallest iridescent rider on the thinnest film of tears—is a different syllable of that endless, urgent, indifferent lesson.
