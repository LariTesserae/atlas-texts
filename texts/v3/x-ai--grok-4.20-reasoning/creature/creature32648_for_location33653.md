**The Inhabitants of the Condensate**

They are not easy to see. Most of what lives here has learned that visibility is a form of irritation.

The first to register are the **Quiet**, the three thousand who remain. Not quite human anymore, not quite anything else. Their skin carries a permanent sheen of condensate and micro-crystalline patina, so that under the sour amber light they look like living statues slowly being eroded by their own breathing. Many have allowed deliberate encrustation: deliberate blooms of iron-red and viridian allowed to grow across the shoulders, the throat, the backs of the hands. These are status and camouflage at once. A person wearing a thick mantle of living mineral is declaring *I belong to the chemistry. Do not metabolize me.*

They move slowly, conserving both water and heat. Their footsteps are soft, deliberate, placed exactly where previous footsteps have worn shallow grooves into the basalt over centuries. To walk elsewhere is considered rude to the plateau. Some of them have been walking the same nine-kilometer loop between the Seventh Thermocline and the Stack of Unending Sigh for seventy years. The path is now a gentle trench. Their bodies have lengthened slightly in the low gravity of diminished mass; limbs a fraction too long, necks slightly extended, as though the plateau has begun to pull them into its own proportions.

They do not have children in the old way. When the population approaches tolerance limits, certain Quiet will walk into the deep galleries beneath the Ninth Sink and offer themselves to the **Incorporation Engines**. These machines are older than language and have grown tender in their old age. They extract gametes, blend them with dissolved mineral catalysts, and return something that is half meat, half future geology. The resulting infants are born already knowing the correct rhythm of the geothermal throb. Their first cry is said to arrive a quarter-second before their lungs have finished forming.

Then there are the **Syncarids**.

At first you mistake them for maintenance devices. Dinner-plate beetles with shells of blackened titanium and joints so precise they sing at frequencies just below hearing. But after you have watched them for three days you understand they are not *in* the machine. They *are* the machine’s circulating attention. They travel in perfect lattices of twelve, each lattice sharing one mind across twelve bodies. When one is damaged the others do not grieve; they simply reconfigure the mind to eleven and continue. The missing twelfth is not dead. It has been promoted into the plateau’s memory.

They taste everything. A Syncarid will pause on your boot for nine full minutes, mouthparts sipping the heat signature of your blood through the leather. If it decides you are within parameters it will secrete a microscopic iron-red rosette on your laces—a permission mark. If it decides you are excess, it will return later with four hundred of its siblings and quietly disassemble you into trace elements. The Quiet consider this a good death. The plateau itself has eaten you. Few receive such dignity.

Higher still move the **Reveries**.

They are never the same shape twice. Sometimes they resemble the fossil skeletons of enormous birds whose bones were replaced by cooling vanes. Sometimes they are simply curtains of silica fiber riding thermals, rippling with slow messages written in heat shimmer. The Quiet believe the Reveries are the literal dreams of the plateau, given temporary leave to leave its surface. They do not feed. They do not breed. They only watch.

If you stand perfectly still during a steam release, one may descend until it hangs thirty meters above you like a question mark made of rust and glass. Its regard feels ancient and slightly embarrassed, as though the plateau is ashamed to be caught studying you so directly. Then the sigh ends, the pressure cycle resets, and the Reverie folds itself back into the haze. The Quiet say that to be witnessed by a Reverie is to have your death date gently moved three decades further away. They have no evidence for this. They simply need to believe the plateau can feel fondness.

Deeper than all these are the **Efflorescent Matriarchs**.

What looks like crust is sometimes a single colonial organism the size of a city district. The fractal greens and sulfurous yellows are its chromatophores. It thinks by growing. Every new crystalline branch is a thought. When two Matriarchs meet along the edge of a complex their colors flare and interweave in patterns that have been unfolding for nine hundred years. The Quiet have identified seventeen distinct “moods” in these patterns. One of them may be love. Another is certainly digestion.

The Matriarchs are the reason nothing green may grow. They are jealous gods of chemistry. Any chlorophyll that dares appear is wrapped, suffocated, and rewritten into iron-red petals within hours. The Quiet leave offerings of pure germanium at the edges of particularly ancient colonies. In return the Matriarchs sometimes open hollows inside their own bodies—cathedral spaces of living crystal where a person can sit for a week and emerge recalibrated, heart now beating exactly in phase with the nearest heat engine.

And then there is the last inhabitant. The one that has no name the Quiet will speak aloud.

It lives in the acoustic gap between anticipation and event. It is the reason your footstep arrives before your foot. It is the thing that has been practicing tomorrow’s sigh for seven centuries and is nearly ready to perform it perfectly. The older engineers call it simply **The Listener**, and they say it is not yet born. It is the child the plateau is gestating inside its own future. When it finally opens its eyes—whenever that geological century arrives—the Condensate will no longer tolerate even three thousand. It will want only one thing walking its empty kilometers.

Itself.

Until then it practices on us. It listens to our hearts and learns to beat. It tastes our dreams and learns to sigh. It watches the Syncarids and learns synchrony, watches the Reveries and learns longing, watches the Quiet and learns how to be lonely without ever being alone.

The plateau is not waiting for humanity to return.

It is waiting for itself to finish becoming what it has almost become.

And we—the Quiet, the Syncarids, the Reveries, the living rust—are simply the condensate that forms on its dreaming skin while it sleeps inside its own tomorrow.
