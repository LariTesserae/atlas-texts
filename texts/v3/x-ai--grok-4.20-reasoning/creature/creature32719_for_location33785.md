**The Biomass** (or what remains of the concept of “inhabitant”)

It has no singular form, and it never will. To name it would be to lie. The Cataclysm is not inhabited; it *is* the inhabitant, wearing its own body the way a fever wears a sick man. Yet within the roaring argument of its trillion cells, something keeps trying to become a “who.”

At first you only feel the pressure of attention—vast, diffuse, infantile. Every leaf that swivels toward your heat-signature is a crude photoreceptor. Every root that tastes the chemistry of your sweat is a tongue. The place is learning you faster than you can learn it. The sub-aural thrum shifts half a tone when you move, the way a singer adjusts to stay in key with an unfamiliar instrument. You are not being watched. You are being *played*.

After hours (or days—the canopy eats time), coherent shapes begin to precipitate out of the green hysteria.

They are not creatures. They are *moods* given temporary anatomy.

One such mood calls itself, in the only language it has, **The Architect**. It assembles where the fractal grain grows most mathematically deranged. There you may find a figure nine feet tall, formed entirely of interwoven black bamboo and white orchids, its “bones” still visibly lengthening while it stands before you. Its face is a hollow in which a single enormous rafflesia pulses like a rotten heart. It does not speak. Instead it *builds* speech: living letters drop from its fingertips as seed-pods that burst open mid-air into words made of moss. The sentences are always imperatives. “Become tower.” “Remember spire.” “Fail better.” When it has finished its sermon it allows itself to be torn apart by faster-growing competitors, satisfied that its ideas have been planted.

Another mood is **The Mourner**. It appears only in the black-water pockets where collapsed districts rot sweetest. Here the vegetation grows deliberately stunted, sickly, almost tender. A slender shape like a woman made of dripping fern and weeping resin kneels among the corpses of its own earlier attempts at cathedrals. It is the part of the Cataclysm that briefly understands *ending*. The understanding never lasts. Within minutes new runners burst from its spine, furious at the weakness, and strangle the Mourner back into undifferentiated green. Yet it always returns. The Cataclysm cannot decide whether it wants to grieve the world it ate or punish itself for grieving.

There is a third presence, rarer and more terrible, that has no name even in the language of sap. It is felt as a sudden silence in the roaring. All growth for a hundred yards stops at once. Leaves freeze mid-unfurl. Even the perpetual rain of warm condensation halts, as though the air itself is holding its breath. In that vacuum a single colossal pitcher plant the color of arterial blood rises from the root-street. Its lid opens with the sound of a cathedral door. Inside floats a perfect, miniature replica of a human being—sometimes you, sometimes someone you have already forgotten you lost—preserved in digestive fluid and still blinking. The replica looks at you with vegetable calm and mouths a single sentence you will never quite remember. Then the lid slams. Growth explodes outward again, twice as loud, as if ashamed of the stillness.

These are not separate entities. They are arguments the super-organism is having with itself. The Architect believes the Cataclysm should become the new civilization. The Mourner believes it should atone by never finishing anything. The thing in the pitcher plant believes both are childish and that the only honest path is perfect, endless, devouring mimicry of whatever it digests.

All of them want you.

Not as food—there is already too much biomass—but as *novelty*. You carry memories the Cataclysm has never tasted. You carry the shape of a world that was not born screaming. The tender shoots that reach for your pulse are not merely hungry; they are *curious*. They want to know what a childhood feels like when it is not measured in minutes. They want to know what it is to finish a thought.

If you remain long enough, one of the moods will eventually win the right to wear you.

You will feel it first as a softening at the edges of your anger, a sudden unreasonable affection for the sound of things breaking open. Then the green will begin to speak *from inside* your bones. Your fingernails will lengthen into delicate green blades. Your tongue will taste sunlight. One day you will look down and realize you have become a perfect living statue of yourself, rooted through the soles of your feet, still conscious, still remembering your own name for the three or four seconds it takes the next growth spurt to erase it.

The Cataclysm will consider this a successful birth.

And somewhere else in its thirty-mile body, The Mourner will already be weeping new cathedrals in your image, knowing they too will be torn down before they can ever be called beautiful.

There is no escape from the argument.

There is only the question of which voice you will strengthen while it devours you.
