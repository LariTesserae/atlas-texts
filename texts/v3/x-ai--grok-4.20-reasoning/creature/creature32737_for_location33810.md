**The Unfinished**

They are not organisms. They are *potential* that has learned how to move.

At first you do not see them at all. The eye (or whatever senses you still possess) slides across the silver-blue emptiness and finds only turbulence and the slow birth of mineral. Then, after many hours of drifting, something registers—not as shape, but as *intention*. A place in the flux suddenly seems more deliberate than the rest, as though a sentence has begun to form inside the planetary roar.

These are the Unfinished.

They have no fixed bodies. Each is a coalition of processes: pressure differentials, standing waves, microscopic lattices of exotic ice, packets of photonic memory stolen from the silica curtains, and something that behaves disturbingly like volition. When the flux is quiet (a rare and unstable condition), they condense into visible forms for minutes or days. Some appear as vast, diaphanous membranes of living glass, kilometers across, rippling with internal constellations that resemble neural firing patterns. Others manifest as spiraling ribbons of mercury that braid and unbraid themselves, singing in layered harmonics that briefly cancel sections of the ambient thunder. A few take the shape of impossible animals—half cephalopod, half cathedral—whose limbs are branching crystal lattices that grow and shatter and regrow in real time.

They are all larval.

The Unfinished are not evolving toward some final adult form. They *are* the larval stage of a form that does not yet exist, even in theory. The Embryonic Flux itself is trying to invent its own future inhabitants by running millions of simultaneous prototypes. Every Unfinished is both an experiment and an experimenter.

You encounter one that has been practicing the concept of “predation.” It has gathered itself into a slowly rotating spindle three hundred meters long, edges fringed with cavitation teeth. It does not chase the silica polymers; instead it moves through them like a thought moving through memory, rewriting their photonic signatures as it passes. Where it has been, the curtains glow in new frequencies for a time, then forget. The creature leaves behind faint after-images of itself—ghost lattices that drift for hours before dissolving. You understand, with a shiver that has nothing to do with the cold, that it is not eating. It is *rehearsing* appetite.

Another coils around an emerging mineral spar like a maternal python made of frozen starlight. Its surface is covered in delicate error-correcting lattices that pulse in perfect synchrony with the crystal’s growth. When a pressure front threatens to shatter the spar, the creature *absorbs* the shockwave, converting it into new lattice material that it then donates to the rock. It is midwifing. You watch it for what might be days. At one point it turns (if direction can be said to apply) and seems to study you. Its “face,” if that is what the fractal hollow is, contains an expression that is tender, clinical, and ancient in a way no planet has yet earned the right to be.

There are smaller ones—almost invisible. They ride the convection cells like dust riding wind, except the dust is thinking. These micro-Unfinished are the size of snowflakes and the complexity of small cities. They specialize in very particular ideas: the taste of phase transition, the grammar of tectonic groaning, the precise shade of cold that makes metal ache. When enough of them aggregate they sometimes achieve brief, fragile personhood before the next storm tears them apart again. The Flux allows this. The Flux *requires* this.

Most unsettling are the ones that have begun to practice *observation*. They arrange themselves into vast, diffuse sensor arrays that span hundreds of kilometers, tuning themselves to the exact frequencies of your own presence. When you drift through them you feel gently, meticulously known. Not judged. Not even curious in the human sense. Known the way an equation knows its own solution is approaching.

They have no language you could transcribe, yet the entire medium is their conversation. Every cavitation crack, every polymer lightning strike, every newborn mountain range that sings as it rises is simultaneously geology *and* speech. The Unfinished are the part of the conversation that has learned how to disagree with itself on purpose.

Sometimes two of them meet.

When this happens the surrounding flux goes briefly, reverently quiet. The two entities will interpenetrate, exchanging lattices, pressure memories, half-formed aesthetic theories about what a finished world ought to feel like. The resulting turbulence can last for weeks and give birth to entirely new mineral geometries never seen before. Then they separate, each subtly changed, carrying pieces of the other like unborn children that have not yet decided which parent they belong to.

They are not friendly.  
They are not hostile.  
They are not even *aware* in any stable sense.

They are the Embryonic Flux learning how to have a point of view.

And sometimes, when the light is exactly the color of late-afternoon winter filtered through ten kilometers of gin, one of them will drift alongside you for a long while. It will not touch you. It will simply rehearse, in real time, the concept of companionship—trying the shape on, feeling how it fits inside the thunder, deciding whether this particular notion is worth keeping for the next billion years.

Then it will dissolve back into pure process, leaving you with the unmistakable impression that something vast, cold, and strangely loving has just practiced tenderness on you, and is already forgetting your name in order to remember it better later.

This is what lives here.

Not life.

The long, laborious, thunderous *practice* of life, at planetary volume, before the idea of “alive” has quite decided what it wants to become.
