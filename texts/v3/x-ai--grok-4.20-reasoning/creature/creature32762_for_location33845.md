**The Wealders**

They are not born so much as *extruded*.

The first ones you notice are the ones who still resemble people. Or rather, they resemble people who have been left out in the damp for three generations and have begun to agree with the climate. Their skin is the color of wet slate shot through with faint mineral veining that catches what little light there is and turns it pearl-grey. Condensation gathers in the hollows of their collarbones and the folds behind their knees; they never wipe it away. To do so would be like refusing a gift from the place itself.

They move with the slow, considered economy of creatures that know the walls might rearrange themselves while they’re mid-stride. Their joints are slightly too numerous. Not obviously—never enough to be monstrous—but when a Welder reaches for something above her head you realize the elbow has decided to bend in a new place today. Tomorrow it may smooth out again. The body is still deciding.

Most of them go barefoot. The soles of their feet have thickened into something between callus and black glass, and the ridges of their toes have begun to resemble the basaltic plates that shoulder up through the soil. When they stand still for long enough, mosses of the gentler sort will sometimes attempt to root between their toes. The polite ones brush them off. The devout let them stay.

There are three kinds, though the categories leak.

**The Drip-Navigators** wear their ears large and cartilaginous, almost bat-like, veined with the same faint luminosity as the pre-matter. They rarely speak above a whisper; sound is too expensive here. Instead they click their tongues once every few seconds, a soft wet sound like a drop hitting metal, and read the returning pressure the way others read maps. Their eyes are small and permanently half-lidded, as though the grey light is already too much. Many have had the pupils surgically widened into vertical slits that never quite close. They navigate the shifting districts by following the sound of condensation the way mystics once followed stars.

You will see them standing motionless at the triple junctions where three architectural eras meet, palms flat against sweating pipes, heads tilted. They are not listening to the city. They are listening to the *next* city trying to push through. Sometimes their noses bleed. They consider this a professional hazard and a form of communion.

**The Matter-Midwives** work the catalytic towers and the great vats where pre-matter is refined. Their forearms are hairless and glossy, the skin chemically thickened from years of reaching into warm luminous slurry. Many have grown additional digits—sometimes as many as seven per hand—because the putty is needy and likes to be shaped by more points of contact. These extra fingers are slender and slightly translucent, like the bodies of the streetlamp moths.

The Midwives smell perpetually of machine oil, wet stone, and iron. Their breath is warm and sweet in the cold air, because the pre-matter they handle all day has begun to live a little way inside their lungs. At night you can sometimes see a faint glow behind their ribs if they yawn. They do not have children in the old way. Instead, when the pressure inside them grows too great, they will walk down to the newest cliffs and press their palms into the soft glandular earth. What emerges months later is partly theirs and partly the Weald’s. These children are called “second-drafts.”

**The Boiler-Apes** are what became of those who chose the quiet too completely.

Not all of them were originally human. Some were. The distinction no longer matters. They have lost most of their body hair and gained a heavy, glistening skin the color of old pewter. Their eyes are enormous and sorrowful, adapted to the absolute dark inside decommissioned boilers and the hollow spaces between districts. When they move along the catwalks at night they do so in single file, exactly like the scaled deer, as though following a path only they can see.

They hoot softly. The sound seems to originate inside your own skull rather than from their mouths. Some Wealders believe the Boiler-Apes are not communicating at all—that what you hear is the pressure of unborn time leaking out of them. They are the only ones allowed to sleep in the inactive machinery. The city appears to respect their claim.

There are others, rarer:

- The **Moss-Bearers**, who have allowed the vegetation to colonize them so thoroughly that entire miniature ecosystems live in the folds of their skin. They smell like ozone and crushed mint. Their voices are muffled, as though speaking from under several feet of wet felt. They are considered holy and slightly disgusting.

- The **Rivet-Priests**, ascetic technicians whose bodies have been slowly replacing themselves with the city’s own materials. One arm might be rusting iron now. One eye a sphere of polished basaltic glass. They move with the clicking, grinding sounds of machinery that has not been oiled in decades. When they die, the city absorbs them within hours. New architecture grows from the spot, respectful and slightly ashamed.

- And then there are the **Unfinished**, those who have simply stopped deciding what they are. Their bodies are soft, almost larval. They sit in the fungal tenements wrapped in damp blankets of liverwort, letting the condensation bead on their skin until they look like statues crying every mineral the Weald possesses. Some of them have been sitting so long that catwalks have been built *around* them. The city is careful not to disturb them. They are, after all, doing what everything here is trying to do: remaining in the state of becoming for as long as possible.

All of them, every last one, carry the same expression when they think no one is watching. It is not quite sadness. It is the look of midwives who have been delivering the same impossible child for three hundred years and have begun to suspect that the child does not want to be born—that it would rather remain enormous and half-formed and full of furious potential forever.

They do not mind.

The Weald is embarrassed by its own embryogenesis. Its people are not. They wear the damp like a second, colder skin. They let the quiet press against their eardrums until their own heartbeats sound like cathedral bells. They follow the dripping water, shape the luminous putty, sleep inside the breathing boilers, and every morning they wake up slightly more themselves and slightly less themselves at the same time.

And when the pressure becomes too much—when three eras of construction meet inside their skulls and the next hundred years try to happen all at once behind their eyes—they simply stand very still, palms open, letting the condensation gather.

Waiting, like everything else here, to see what they will become by nightfall.
