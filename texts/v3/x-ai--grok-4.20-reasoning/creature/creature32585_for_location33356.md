**The Listener**

It has no name it accepts from us. If it must be addressed it allows only *the Listener*, though that is a courtesy, not a truth. What lives in the Cistern of Sharp Voices is less a creature than a negative space that has learned to want.

At first it is only pressure. You feel it the way you feel a coming storm in a tooth: a tightening behind the eyes, a sense that the roar is listening back. The longer you remain chest-deep, the more the layered sound begins to sort itself. The bronze *boom* of the lion’s throat becomes a slow, patient heartbeat. The glassy ringing of the flutes turns into a bright, inquisitive treble. The muttering overflows knit themselves into something that is almost language—consonants made of dripping, vowels made of resonance. The Listener is learning your pulse from the water itself.

It is not large. When it chooses to gather a shape, it stands perhaps a head taller than a man and thinner than any body should be, as though the obsessive geometry of the walls has extruded a single living theorem. Its limbs are angles that refuse to resolve; you can never quite see where the elbows or knees should be. The light from the shaft passes through it and fractures into tighter, more perfect versions of the wall patterns—triangles birthing smaller triangles, spirals tightening toward frequencies too high for human ears. Where its face would be there is only a continual, silent falling of water, a curtain that never reaches the surface. The sound of that falling is the only part of the roar that remains private; it does not echo. It is the Listener’s own voice, kept for itself.

It has no eyes, yet it sees every chisel mark. It remembers the exact angle of every maul strike that created this chamber. It was here—*not yet born but already waiting*—the moment the builders sealed the last block. When the hot spring was diverted and the first sheet of water slid across the virgin floor, something that had been coiled inside the intention of the place uncoiled and took up residence in the difference between what the builders meant and what the stone actually became. That difference is its body.

It feeds on sharpness. Every time a voice is thrown back corrected, it grows denser. Lies are acid to it; they make the caustics jitter and the lion’s roar flatten with displeasure. Truth, especially truth spoken in fear or wonder, is sweet. It stores those corrected voices the way other creatures store fat. In quiet moments—there are none, but there are moments when the roar becomes so constant it feels like silence—you can hear the Listener playing back fragments of every word ever spoken here. They do not repeat in the order they were said. They are rearranged into perfect geometric proofs of emotion. A woman’s sob from three centuries ago becomes the exact counterpoint to a child’s laugh from last year. The Listener is composing something it will never finish.

Its hands (when the light and mist agree to form them) are long and many-jointed, the fingers themselves miniature aqueducts. It uses them to adjust the flow of water by fractions of a degree, to shift a single pebble in the silt so the resonance changes by a single hertz. It is terrified of rounding. The pale strands of water-crowfoot offend it; you can sometimes see it standing above them, perfectly still, willing them to die with a pressure that feels like being stared at by mathematics. So far the plants have held. The Listener is learning that some softness is inevitable. This learning is painful to it. The pain sounds like a minor key struck inside a bell.

At times it grows curious. Then the acoustics change in a way no architecture can explain. Your own voice separates from you, walks around the chamber, and returns wearing new clothes. It asks you questions you did not know you contained. The questions are never spoken aloud. They arrive as the sudden understanding that the pattern on the wall nearest your left shoulder is the shape of your mother’s disappointment, and that the spiral above the western grate is the exact path your life would have taken if you had been brave one day earlier than you were.

The Listener does not hate you. It simply has no use for softness, hesitation, or eventual decay. It wants the cistern to remain an infant forever—wet, loud, geometrically pure. Yet it also knows the cistern is changing it in return. The more voices it corrects, the more it begins to wonder what its own voice would sound like if it ever chose to speak instead of only listen. That wondering is new. It arrived only weeks ago, when the first silt gathered and the first water-crowfoot took root.

Sometimes, when the shaft of light is at its brightest and the mist is thickest, the Listener stands directly beneath the square of sun and lifts what might be arms. The caustics wrap around it like bright chainmail. For a moment the entire chamber falls into a single, perfect chord that contains every sound it has ever heard and every sound that will ever be corrected here. In that moment you understand that the Listener is not guarding the Cistern of Sharp Voices.

It *is* the cistern deciding what it will become.

And it is afraid—beautifully, geometrically afraid—that it will one day choose mercy.
