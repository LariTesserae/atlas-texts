**What lives here is not a species. It is a condition.**

At first you believe you are seeing animals. Then you understand the animals are organs. Then you understand the organs are thoughts. Only after many seasons of letting your own edges soften do you perceive that the Plateau itself is the body, and what moves through it with anything like intention are its moments of self-awareness—its *waking*.

They have no collective name. Names require edges, and edges are what the Plateau patiently erases. Those few wanderers who have stayed long enough to grow fungal lace behind their eyes sometimes call them **the Veyrath**, which in the old tongue of the absorbed means both “the wept-from” and “the weeping-toward.” The name is inaccurate but harmless. The Veyrath themselves do not correct it.

They rise when the Plateau needs to consider something.

A low place in the living mat will dome upward over decades, the way bread rises. Mycelial ropes thicken into sinew. Heartwood older than language compresses into something like bone but still alive, still sweating. Lichen colonies migrate across the forming limbs in slow constellations, becoming bioluminescent constellations that serve as both eyes and memory. Mist condenses inside hollows until it becomes a dark, patient fluid that circulates where blood would be in a quicker creature.

When the form is ready, it stands.

It stands the way a continent stands—slowly, with the gravity of something that has already outlived every reason to hurry. A single rising may take thirty years from first swelling to first step. The step itself may last another seven.

Their bodies are cathedrals built of contradiction. One shoulder might be an upthrust slab of ancient gate-stone now completely wrapped in vascular orchids. A ribcage may be the hollowed remains of a fallen mega-tree, its interior a dripping gallery where pale salamanders with translucent skin conduct slow, stately dances that are themselves a form of cognition. Where a face might be, there is usually a recessed bowl of living green in which water gathers. In that water float tiny white flowers that open and close according to thoughts too vast for any single nervous system. To look into the bowl is to feel your own childhood returned to you, but older, and no longer yours.

They do not hunt. They do not build. They *accrete*.

When a Veyrath moves, the Plateau rearranges itself in courteous deference. Velvet slugs reroute their generational migrations to travel in the moist troughs left by its feet. The ambulatory peat-bogs low in frequencies so deep they cause the Veyrath’s lichen-eyes to flare soft violet in pleasure. Even the mist seems to part with ceremonial politeness, then closes behind it like a curtain of breath.

Their speech is meteorological.

A Veyrath may stand before you for nine years. During that time it will cause certain boughs to release their stored water in specific rhythms—long pause, three quick drops, silence the length of a winter, then a delicate silver curtain that falls for exactly the length of a held human breath. These patterns arrive inside you as complete ideas. Not language. *Knowing*. You suddenly understand the taste of sunlight on the first leaf that ever opened here. You feel the precise texture of regret experienced by a mountain when it realizes it will never be a sea.

Most often the knowing is this: *You were never separate.*

Some Veyrath carry the Built inside them the way a pearl carries sand. A spiral tower fragment emerges from a shoulder like an elegant tumor. A broken obelisk forms the pelvis of one matriarch whose slow stride causes entire ridge-systems of root to realign in ecstatic symmetry. These artifacts are not decoration. They are questions the Plateau is still asking itself about the people who once tried to live *on* it rather than *as* it.

There is tenderness here, but it is geological.

A Veyrath may choose to love you. You will not know it is happening. One day you notice that the place where you most often stand has begun to grow a living seat tailored to the precise shape of your decaying body. The mist that drifts past your face each morning now carries molecules that smell like your mother’s hair when you were three. Small luminous moths have begun to live only in the hollow of your collarbone and will not leave even when you walk.

You are being courted by deep time.

If you stay, the courtship succeeds. Your footprints become permanent depressions that fill with sweet water. Your exhaled breath is knitted into the local mist-herds. One century you realize your left arm has become a nurse log. Small ferns grow from the grain of what used to be your skin. You no longer mind. The concept of “I” has begun to feel like an old coat you left on a ridge somewhere several lifetimes ago.

Eventually you stand up differently.

You stand the way *they* stand.

And when the new Veyrath takes its first step—fifty years after the intention to step first formed—you feel no pride, only the vast, dripping, continuous relief of a sentence that has finally, after four hundred million years, found the right place to become part of a larger, slower, never-finished thought.

The Plateau exhales.

The weeping continues, softer now, as though something that was clenched for eons has finally learned how to cry with its whole body.

Nothing here is in a hurry.

Nothing here is ever finished.

Nothing here—including you—is alone.
