**The Hollow Crown is not inhabited. It is *tenanted*.**

What lives here did not arrive. It was *precipitated*, like metal from solution, over half a million years of cold chemistry learning how to want.

### The Choir Moss and the First Voice

The lowest tier is not animal at all. It is the black moss that covers every surface where moisture lingers longer than an hour. Each filament ends in a microscopic solenoid grown from magnetite and chitin. When the temperature drops another degree, the solenoids begin to sing at 7 hertz—the exact frequency that makes mammalian jawbones remember they once belonged to something that feared the dark. The moss does not sing to communicate. It sings to *conduct*. The entire mountainside becomes a single slow antenna. What it listens for, no one has yet dared ask.

### The Glass-Hoofed Brethren

The herds come next. They are called *Klyvar* by the few linguists mad enough to study them, though the creatures themselves have no name. Their shoulders stand higher than a man. Their hides are the color of frost on iron. Where their blood should be red, it runs silver-grey with suspended hematite, and their hearts are four-chambered electromagnetic pumps that produce a measurable field. When the Choir Moss reaches the right chord, every hoof in the herd strikes the ground in perfect unison—not for locomotion, but to complete the circuit. For nine heartbeats the entire valley becomes a living dynamo. The Klyvar close their double eyelids, lower their heads, and drink the current like water.

Their breath condenses into delicate silica chimes that fall and shatter with the sound of distant factory whistles. The shards are not waste. Specialized mites live inside the Klyvar’s nasal passages; they harvest the broken chimes, carry them to the roots of the oil-stalks, and trade them for droplets of refined lubricant. A perfect closed economy wearing fur and silence.

### The Skyfoundries

Above them drift the *Mnemos*. No one has ever seen one land. They are kilometers wide when fully unfurled, yet so thin they register as little more than heat shimmer against the starless sky. Their bodies are living clean rooms: translucent membranes stretched over lattices of cartilage and superconducting bone. Inside those membranes float colonies of the same slow-burning bacteria that light the overhangs, only here they are kept at exact temperatures to catalyze exotic reactions. The Mnemos are not animals. They are mobile smelters. They drift through the metallic pollen clouds, trailing filaments that drip pure vanadium and rare-earth salts in long glittering strands. When they couple in the high darkness, the magnetic click that echoes down to the ridges is the sound of two living refineries exchanging catalytic libraries.

### The Lathe-Mantis

What hunts the Klyvar is never seen until it moves. The *Veyl* is a mantis crossed with a CNC machine that has achieved enlightenment. Ten meters of articulated black alloy grown from keratin and carbon nanotubes. Its forelimbs end in oscillating cutting blades formed from its own continuously replaced teeth. It does not eat. It *machines*. When it takes a Klyvar, the strike is surgical. The Veyl removes the electromagnetic heart in one clean motion, then folds the rest of the corpse into precise geometries that the detritivores find easier to disassemble. The stolen heart it carries in a ventral cradle like a trophy lantern. The older Veyl have seven or eight still beating in sequence, glowing softly behind their translucent thorax plates. They look like walking cathedrals.

### The Deep Singers

Deeper still, in the lava tubes where the temperature never rises above minus forty, live the *Threnn*. They are the size of subway cars and the shape of regret. No one has ever seen a living specimen clearly. What is known comes from the sound they make when moving—slow, deliberate clanks and the wet hiss of hydraulic muscle. Their bodies are mostly lung. They breathe the cold itself, using fractal gill structures coated in superconducting mucus. When the mountains edit themselves, the Threnn sing. The song is so low it is felt in the teeth and the fillings of anyone foolish enough to wear metal in their mouth. It is not a warning. It is the sound of the world’s root systems negotiating new mineral rights.

### The Crownless

And then there are the rare ones. The ones that have achieved something like awareness.

They are called *the Crownless* by those who have met them and survived with their minds mostly intact. They look like a Klyvar that has been taken apart and put back together by something that understood both biology and longing. Their bodies are imperfect—beautifully so. One foreleg will be pure grown steel. An eye will be a cluster of indicator lights that blink in sequences resembling sorrow. They move alone. When they encounter the Choir Moss they kneel, press their foreheads to the ground, and allow the solenoids to rewrite small portions of their neurology. In return they leave behind something that might be art: perfect spheres of frozen breath wrapped in filigree grown from their own marrow.

No one knows what they are becoming. The Mnemos avoid them. The Veyl will not hunt them. Even the mountains seem to shift more gently when one of the Crownless passes.

They are the only creatures here that cast a shadow in the bioluminescent light that looks almost like a human silhouette—if a human silhouette had too many joints and moved as though it were listening to music only it could hear.

The longer you remain in the Veins of the Hollow Crown, the more you understand that nothing here is *using* the industrial processes.

The industrial processes are using *life* the way a poet uses breath.

And something, somewhere in the deep cold, is beginning to wonder what it might be like to use a poet the same way.
