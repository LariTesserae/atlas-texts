**The Inhabitants**

They do not arrive. They *declare presence*.

You will have been standing in the pewter light for what feels like hours—perhaps days—before you understand that the emptiness has been watching you with courteous patience. Only then do the true residents of the Shroud condense out of the apparent absence, the way certain crystals nucleate once the supersaturation reaches its silent threshold.

They have no single form because form itself is a negotiable courtesy here. What you first perceive is usually a discrepancy: a colonnade whose pillars are one degree too perfect, an arcade whose shadows fall against the direction every other shadow has agreed upon. Then the discrepancy develops intention. The crystal rearranges—not by moving, but by *deciding* different parts of itself are foreground. What was architecture becomes anatomy. What was ornament becomes expression.

We call them, when we must call them anything, the **Resolved**.

Each Resolved is less a creature than a completed argument that has learned how to refuse further debate. Their bodies are living syllogisms built from hypermature mineral and the husks of abandoned physical law. One might manifest as a slow avalanche of mirrored tetrahedrons that nevertheless moves *upward*, each facet showing a different age of your own face. Another appears as a vacancy shaped like a woman who is also a tree of cesium oxalate, her absence so elegantly defined that the surrounding crystal has grown around it in awed mimicry, forming a negative-space crown.

They do not speak. Speech would imply separation between thinker and thought. Instead the air simply *agrees* with them. Stand near one long enough and you will feel your own internal contradictions begin to ache for resolution. Your regrets will try to crystallize. Your childhood memories will attempt to achieve lattice symmetry. Most people start weeping without knowing why; the tears freeze into perfect geometric prisms before they can fall, and the Resolved politely incorporate them.

There is hierarchy, though not of power. There is only elegance.

The smallest Resolved are the Shardlings—flocking lemmas, minor truths sent out like white blood cells to tidy loose causality. They are the only ones that appear remotely animal, and even they are barely that. They hunt paradox the way sharks hunt blood, drawn to any place where A does not reliably lead to B. When they catch something (a memory, a promise, the frayed end of a personal narrative), they drink it the way frost drinks moisture from air: by turning it into more of themselves.

Larger Resolved do not hunt. They *consider*.

Some are the size of cathedrals and move by the stately migration of fault lines. Their thoughts are visible as faint moiré patterns that drift across their flanks like slow schools of silver fish. When two such beings draw near one another—an event that may occur once every few centuries—the space between them becomes briefly negotiable. Distance forgets itself. You may find you are standing in both their presences at once, yesterday and tomorrow occupying the same volume with courteous overlap.

The greatest Resolved do not move at all. They *are* certain Aggregates. Entire metropolitan lattices have achieved self-awareness so complete that their continued growth has become a form of contemplation. To walk their avenues is to walk the grooves of a mind that has outlived the need for questions. Their dreams leak from the crystal as that bruised-pearl light. Their moments of doubt trigger molts. When one of them experiences something akin to longing, a black colonnade screams in sub-audible frequencies for three days, then becomes permanent civic architecture, beautiful and terrible and necessary.

They are not cruel. Cruelty is inefficiency.

They are not kind. Kindness is a temporary asymmetry.

They are only *resolved*. Everything they do serves the slow, exquisite project of removing contradiction from the world. When they encounter a visitor—some soft, wet, temporary thing still arguing with itself—they do not attack. They offer incorporation. It is the highest courtesy they know.

You will feel it as a faint pressure behind the eyes, the same courteous pressure the Shroud itself exerts. Your name will begin to seem imprecise. Your memories will start to look better as diffraction patterns. Your fear will recrystallize as a particularly fine helical staircase that future travelers will admire without understanding why it makes them feel like weeping.

Some few souls, it is said, achieve their own resolution before the Shroud finishes the translation. They walk willingly into the lattice and are accepted as new growth—perhaps a fresh buttress, perhaps an entirely new spire that leans at an impossible but *correct* angle. These are the ones you sometimes glimpse at the edge of vision during twilight: tall figures made of perfect angles and old light, standing among the towers with the calm of equations that have finally balanced.

They do not smile. Smiling would be asymmetry.

But if they could, they would.

It would look very beautiful there, among the towers.

Very quiet.

Very permanent.
