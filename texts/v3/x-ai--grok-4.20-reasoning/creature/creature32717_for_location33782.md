**The Hollow’s Inhabitants**

They are not animals. They are not mechanisms. They are the unfinished intentions of the Engine given temporary bodies so that the intention can remain forever unfulfilled.

The smallest are the **Chronometric Silverfish**. Each is the length of a man’s forearm and bright as mercury. Their segmented bodies are scored with microscopic gear teeth that mesh, when they march, with the serrated rim of the great flywheel cliff. They move in ranks of exactly 144, no more, no less, and every footfall lands on the same instant of the piston’s one-inch descent. If even one were to falter, the entire oscillation would have to stop; therefore none ever falter. Their compound eyes have fused into smooth mirrors that reflect only the sound waves around them. They do not eat. They *measure*. When one dies—an event that occurs exactly once every 777 years—it is immediately encased in a bead of the ever-present condensation and rolled by its siblings into a fault-line socket where it becomes a new bearing. The socket seals with a sound like a satisfied monk finishing a chant.

Higher up the trophic knot live the **Pressure Ibex**, creatures whose skeletons are literal pressure vessels. Their ribs are boilerplate curved by natural selection that was never natural. Along the spine runs a row of osseous chimneys, each capped by a valvular horn. When the internal steam generated by the slow digestion of copper moss reaches critical pressure, the valves lift with a theatrical *psshht*, releasing exactly enough vapor to drive the next cycle of the nearest water-hammer. Their eyes are replaced by gauge glasses; you can watch pale green fluid rise and fall behind the transparent bone. They never breed. Instead, when one grows too efficient, a portion of its own bone sloughs away in the shape of a perfect miniature ibex that simply steps out of the parent’s flank and begins grazing on its own. The parent immediately becomes slightly less efficient, restoring equilibrium. The new calf will do the same in its time. The lineage is therefore infinite and stationary.

Above them wheel the **Trigonometric Albatrosses**. Their wings are not wings but three mutually perpendicular rings of stiffened feather and cartilage, each ring rotating independently like a gimbal. They do not flap; they *precess*. Their flight traces perfect spherical harmonics in the sourceless light, and the frequency of their wingbeats is the carrier wave upon which the entire sonic liturgy is modulated. When they pass directly over the piston, the frequency momentarily doubles in a respectful *ostinato*. Their beaks are tuning forks. They eat nothing. They are sustained by the differential between the fifths and octaves produced by the steam vents. Occasionally one will descend in a perfect spiral, alight on a vine as thick as a mainsail rope, and drive its tuning-fork beak into the living tissue. For nine heartbeats the vine sings in a minor key. Then the bird releases, satisfied, and returns to its eternal trigonometry. The vine never bleeds. It simply records the note for later use.

Deeper in the recursive pockets—those places where perspective folds if stared at too long—dwell the **Laminar Priests**. These are the only inhabitants that might once have been human. Their bodies are elongated, hairless, skin lacquered with the same permanent water that coats everything else. They wear robes woven from living moss that never grows longer or shorter. Each carries a single tool: a wrench formed from their own left forearm, the bones fused and calcified into steel-hard alloy. The wrench has no moving parts and never leaves their grip. They walk the fault lines where crystal lattices function as wiring, pausing at junctions that look like nothing to any other eye. There they perform infinitesimal adjustments that change nothing measurable, yet without which the entire system would have finished itself long ago. Their faces are serene, almost kindly, until you notice that their mouths have been replaced by slotted ports. When they speak—only ever to the Echo-Smiths—the sound comes out as the precise clatter of a drop-forge shaping a single syllable of an unknown metal language.

The **Echo-Smiths** themselves are harder to see. They are tall, thin, and composed mostly of negative space. You register them as the absence of dripping between two particular droplets, or the exact shape of silence between two piston strokes. They catch the translated echoes of your spoken name and hammer them into new objects—tools, ornaments, prayers—that the Engine immediately incorporates into its substance. The objects are always perfect and always superfluous. The Smiths never rest. Their forges are the hollows inside the basalt where supercritical water flashes to steam. Their hammers are their own hearts, which they reach into their chests to retrieve, strike with, and replace still beating.

And then there is the **Intention**.

It has no fixed form. Sometimes it is a single droplet that hangs in the air for a thousand years, refusing to fall, containing within it every species that will never need to evolve. Sometimes it is the smell just before lightning in a place where lightning can never arrive. Most often it appears as a figure standing exactly where you intend to walk next, wearing your own face but with the eyes replaced by slowly turning flywheels. It never speaks. It only regards you with an expression of infinite, exhausted pride—the look of a creator who has achieved perfection and is now condemned to perfect it forever.

All of them, every silverfish and ibex and priest and absence, understand the same truth at the level of their marrow:

The Engine must never finish.  
Therefore they must never stop.  
Therefore they are happy beyond all human measure.

They have already accounted for you. When you grow tired of watching, one of the Laminar Priests will approach without hurry, extend the wrench that is his forearm, and offer to let you carry it for a while. If you accept, the wrench will fuse to your bones with a sensation like cold orgasm. You will understand your task within nine beats of the piston.

Most do accept.

The Engine is perfect.  
The Engine is unfinished.  
Both statements remain simultaneously, eternally true, and now so do you.
