**The Unwearied**

They were never born inside the Cradle. They *arrived* as something else and allowed the chamber to finish them.

At first you will not see them at all. You will see only the clicking tourmaline pill-bugs, the violet gas-bladders drifting along their immutable rails, the long jointed serpents writing their endless signatures across the air. You will think these are the inhabitants. They are not. They are the loom on which the Unwearied are woven.

The Unwearied are what remains when every possible motion has been performed so perfectly that motion itself becomes a kind of stillness. They are three, always three, though their number feels more like a chord than a count.

The first is the size of a seated man and never moves from the quartz block that has become its lap. Its body is not crystal; it is the *memory* of crystal—transparent, yet it bends light the way regret bends time. Where its face should be there is only the negative space that the gypsum mask was carved to fit. Sometimes the mask levitates from its hook inside the hut, crosses the chamber without sound, and settles onto that absence. When it does, you can see the outline of cheekbones, a heavy brow, the faint scar of an old ritual cut across the bridge of what used to be a nose. Then the mask drifts away again and the face returns to being the shape *left after a face has listened for thirty thousand years*.

The second dwells inside the song itself. It has no fixed location. It is the exact difference between any two notes at any given instant. When the high keening dips a microtone to accommodate the soft clack of a beetle’s leg on rose quartz, that microtonal adjustment *is* the second Unwearied. It wears the braided roar like skin. If you stand in the center of the chamber and close your eyes, you can feel it pass through your ribs, rearranging the small bones of your ear until your own heartbeat falls into the eternal measure. It does this without cruelty or kindness; it is simply completing the chord.

The third is the hardest to bear. It is small—child-sized, perhaps—and it lives in the three empty niches inside the hut, occupying all of them and none. It is the only one that still remembers having once been meat. Sometimes it leaves a shadow the color of warm marrow on the wall behind the largest resonant grove, a silhouette with its arms raised in the exact attitude of someone who has just set down a tool and stepped back to judge their carving. The shadow never quite fades. If you place your hand against it, the stone is body temperature. The shadow leans into your palm like a cat.

They do not speak. Speech would be change, and change is anathema here. Instead they *indwell*. When the first Unwearied settles onto its quartz seat, every resonant frond in the chamber alters its angle by half a degree—not enough to see, only enough to feel in the teeth. When the second passes through a living body, that body forgets one childhood memory and replaces it with the sound of perfect crystal. The third simply *watches*, and in watching completes the system. Their regard is the final harmonic that makes the song self-sustaining.

The primitive culture whose marks you find did not die. They became these three. One chose the seat. One chose the song. One chose the empty places between. They had names once—harsh, consonantal names full of clicks and glottal stops—but the names have been filed down to perfect silence. Now they are known only by the way the light behaves when they are near.

If you remain long enough, the chamber will offer you the fourth niche.

It does not pressure. It simply reveals, over hours or days or centuries, that there has always been space for one more. The gypsum mask will drift toward you, turning slowly so that its empty eyes can measure the shape of your face. The offer is courteous, eternal, and without mercy:

*You may add your own absence to the equilibrium.*

Some have. Their silhouettes appear for a single instant among the petroglyphs—arms raised, tools laid down—before the stone forgets them and the song continues, richer by one more perfectly cancelled life.

The Unwearied do not guard the Cradle.  
They *are* the cradle, rocked to stillness by their own singing.

And they are listening, without end, for the next note that will never come.
