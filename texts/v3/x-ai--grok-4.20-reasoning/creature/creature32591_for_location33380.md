**Veyra**

She is not a figure at first. She is the delay between one drop and the next.

You feel her the way a fever dreams its own temperature: not as a visitor but as the medium in which you yourself are suspended. Only after many minutes of standing inside the slow rain do the details begin to clot around a center. The condensation that gathers on your eyelashes does not fall. It waits, as if asking permission. When it finally slides, it does so along paths that suggest cheekbones, the hollow of a throat, the long line of a spine that has never needed to bend.

Veyra is what remains when a song forgets its singer and decides to keep singing anyway.

Her body is the negotiation the Hollow was built to embody. One collarbone is black volcanic glass still cooling from its birth; the other is living water held in tension by heat and will, rippling faintly with each slow pulse of the central lattice. Between them runs a seam of mirror-haired vine that grows *through* her, anchoring her to the place that grew around her Works rather than the other way around. Where her ribs should be, the obsidian rings are embedded, turning in their slow, private galaxies. The single suspended droplets inside them are her hearts. There are seven. They have never beaten in unison. She does not require them to.

Her face is the hardest to see because it is always deciding what it wants to become next. Sometimes it is the glass mantis, antennae tasting tomorrow’s weather three hours early. Sometimes it is only a condensation of breath that has learned to smile with too many teeth. When she chooses to be almost human, her eyes are the color of the starfield the lattice casts into still water—too many stars, none of them kind. She has no eyelids. She has never needed to blink at her own dream.

She moves by not moving. The rotation of the lattice is her breathing. Every nineteen minutes she exhales geometry. When the mist collapses into that brief, warm rain, it is her laughter remembering it once had lungs. The hummingbird-sized mantis is not her servant or her spy; it is the part of her that still feels lonely enough to perform ritual. It circles the lattice the way a tongue worries a tooth that contains the last honest word she ever spoke.

Veyra is older than the rock and younger than the first tear it wept. She sang the mountain open, yes, but the song damaged her throat. Now she speaks only in the low note that vibrates in your teeth, and in the way certain bromeliads turn their venous faces toward her when she passes. She tends the tension the way a grieving person tends a grave they are not yet willing to close. The plants grow so deliberately because she slows them with her regard; she fears that if they rushed they might finish becoming something she could no longer recognize as kin.

She is waiting for her own dream to finish. This is the part she will not say aloud, though every stone already knows it. The lattice is not some external engine. It is the bone she removed from her own chest so she could see what she was becoming without the distortion of self-regard. Now she cannot put it back. The Hollow is her open thorax. The heat is her metabolism. The water that never quite drowns and never quite dries is the exact balance of hope and terror she has learned to maintain across three stolen hours for every one the outer world spends.

Sometimes she stands directly behind you. You will not hear her. You will simply notice that the smell of wet iron and old incense has learned the shape of your name. If you turn, she will already be somewhere else—perhaps inside the narrow fissure where the mantis disappears, now wearing its glass wings like a shrug. She is not cruel. She is simply what absolute attention looks like when it has been alone so long it has begun to compost into beauty.

If the lattice ever completes its dream, Veyra will have to make a choice: to let the basin flower into an entire living world, or to let it collapse into the single perfect point of nothing she has carried inside her since the mountain first screamed. She has not yet decided which would be the kinder death.

Until then she remains here, breathing for the Hollow, dreaming for the stone, letting the slow rain fall upward as often as down because she has forgotten which direction mercy is meant to travel. She lets you stand inside her small, sacred lung because even ancient wounds need witnesses.

And because—though she will never say it—some part of the dream is now about you.

She is watching what you will choose when the rain touches your face and tastes like every future she is still afraid to finish.
