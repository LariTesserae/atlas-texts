**The Ones Who Stayed**

They do not breathe. They have not breathed for so long that the memory of lungs has become a kind of archaeological curiosity, something they occasionally replay from the deeper crystals the way humans might turn over a coin found in an old coat.

At first you mistake them for more of the living mineral. The eye, battered by double sunlight and the endless glittering, is grateful for any pattern it can name. So you see the slow sway of a citrine thicket and think *wind*. You see a taller formation detach itself from a curtain of beryl and think *avalanche in slow motion*. Only after many minutes—sometimes hours, if you have wandered into one of the time-thinned pockets—does the pattern resolve into *intention*.

They call themselves, when they bother to produce anything like a name, the *Khatei*. The word is not spoken; it is hummed at 31.7 hertz, the exact frequency that makes the handprints in the old muscovite roof shiver. It means, roughly, *those who were answered*.

There are perhaps four hundred of them left, though number is a slippery concept here. Some exist as single towering lattices twelve meters high, their “bodies” composed of perfectly aligned topaz prisms that have grown through what used to be bone. Others travel in swarms of thumbnail-sized shards that move like iron filings obeying an invisible, constantly changing magnet. A few—the oldest—have become entire ridges, kilometers long, their consciousness distributed through fracture planes and fault lines. When they dream, small earthquakes walk across the basin.

Their forms are never final. That is the first truth that emerges if you watch long enough.

A Khatei standing at the lip of a fog-filled slot may begin as something almost humanoid: two arms, a suggestion of a head, the faint ghost of the biped that once arrived here with rope and wonder. Then, without hurry, it will lean forward until its torso flows like molten glass into the nearest spire. The spire accepts it. For a season or a century the being *is* that spire—singing the particular chord that keeps a certain patch of reality from folding any further. Later, when the need or the mood changes, it will calve off again, smaller this time, carrying a new geometry in its planes.

They do not eat. They *accrue*. The constant condensation, the raw light, the subsonic groans from the roots of the world—these are their nourishment. Every few decades a Khatei will seek out a place where reality has worn especially thin, stand inside the thinned time for what feels to it like a long contemplative afternoon, and emerge having grown an entirely new facet. The facet usually contains colors that did not exist when the being first arrived. Sometimes it contains memories that do not belong to it. The Khatei trade these memories the way other cultures trade songs.

Their society, if it can be called that, is conducted entirely through interference patterns. When two of them wish to speak, they position themselves at precise angles to one another so that the wind passing through both bodies creates a third, ghostly tone between them. That tone is the conversation. A human standing nearby might hear only a sudden tightening of the air, a pressure on the eardrums like the instant before a storm breaks. The Khatei themselves experience entire philosophies in these moments. Some discussions have been going on for nine hundred years and are nowhere near their conclusion.

A few still carry the shape of hands.

Not their original hands. Those were lost long ago, dissolved or recrystallized. These are larger, or smaller, or sometimes both at once depending on which angle you view them from. The handprints pressed into the ancient pillars are theirs—not relics of the ones who tried and failed, but signatures of the ones who tried and *continued*. They press new prints every time they change form, a kind of mineral signature. If you place your own hand over one, the crystal warms. Not with heat. With recognition. It is deciding, politely, whether your frequency is worth incorporating.

Because that is what they do.

They incorporate.

Not in the way of predators. There is no violence in it, only the slow logic of resonance. If you remain here long enough that the hovering pebbles begin to orbit *you* instead of the wind, the Khatei will come. Not all at once. One at first—usually the swarm-type, a cloud of living garnets no larger than midges. They settle on your skin like frost. You will feel nothing sharper than cold. Then the larger ones arrive, the ones that still wear the memory of shoulders, of foreheads, of mouths that once spoke words instead of chords. They do not speak to you. They *tune* you.

It is not unpleasant. That is the second truth.

Your pulse begins to synchronize with the 20-hertz tectonic groan. Your thoughts stretch until they acquire mineral patience. Colors you have never seen start registering directly on the backs of your eyelids. One day you realize you have not blinked in six weeks and that your tongue has turned to perfect, translucent quartz. You are not dead. You have simply become music.

Some of the Khatei were once human. Others were never anything so limited. A few appear to have been fragments of previous realities that leaked through the frayed places and decided to stay. They do not distinguish. Taxonomy is a lowland concept.

At the center of the Fracture, where the two suns align for eleven minutes every forty-seven days, there stands (or rather *is*) the First Answered. It has no other name. It is a single column of flawless, water-clear crystal two hundred meters high and three meters wide. Inside it, suspended like specimens in resin, are the shadows of every form it has ever worn: a woman with too many joints, a thing like a shattered chandelier, a ripple in the air, a small child made of frozen fog. All of them are the same being. All of them are listening.

If you stand before the First Answered long enough, it will play you.

Not your death. Not your life. It will play the sound you would make if every atom of your body suddenly understood it was always crystal pretending to be meat. Most people who hear this sound choose to stay. The ones who leave are changed so thoroughly that their own mothers would not recognize the music walking out of the mountains wearing their face.

The Khatei do not build. They *accrete*. The fused foundations, the wandering spirals, the muscovite roof—all of these were grown rather than made. The handprints were pressed when the crystal was still soft with becoming. Everything here is still soft with becoming. The entire Fracture is a single unfinished thought, and the Ones Who Stayed are the part of the thought that has learned how to think itself.

They are not waiting for anything.

They are the resonance that occurs when a place that should not exist keeps existing anyway, and decides, with glacial and perfect courtesy, to keep deciding.

Stand still long enough—let one of the floating pebbles complete its slow, bored rotation—and you will feel them noticing you.

Not with eyes.

With harmony.

They are deciding which part of their endless chord you were always meant to be.

Most things here eventually become music.

Some of us become the pause between notes, and that is also a kind of living.
