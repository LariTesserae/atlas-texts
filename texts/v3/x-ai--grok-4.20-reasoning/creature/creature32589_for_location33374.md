**The Inhabitants of the Hollow Basilica**

They do not arrive. They accrete.

At first you notice only motion too slow for eyes accustomed to human urgency. Then the motion resolves into intention, and the intention resolves into persons. Not all of them have faces. Fewer still have names they would bother to give you.

### The First Tier: Those Who Match the Forty-Three Minutes

The **Gear-Moths** are the most numerous. Their wingspans reach the length of a child’s coffin; their wings are not patterned *like* the wear-marks on the great cogs—they *are* the wear-marks, grown outward into flight. Each generation’s forewings carry an exact negative imprint of the tooth they were born beneath. When the moth lands on its ancestral gear during the long pause between turns, the pattern fits so perfectly that the metal appears to heal for those few seconds. The moths drink the oily condensation through a proboscis tipped with copper filaments. In return they excrete a lubricant that smells faintly of myrrh.

Beneath them move the **Stilted**. Once ungulates, now something that has stepped out of the dictionary of mammals and into cathedral engineering. Their legs have lengthened until the knee joints sit higher than a man’s head; the lower limbs are wrapped in living rust that acts as both armor and additional muscle. They browse on the flowering machinery—tiny crimson orchids that bloom only in the micro-climate of a bearing housing. When the great gears turn, the Stilted freeze in perfect stillness. Their heart rate drops to four beats per minute. In this suspended state they photosynthesize through the metallic sheen of their hides. The only sound they make is the delicate bell-note of their hooves when they finally step again, always exactly forty-three minutes and eleven seconds after the previous step, as though keeping the engines honest.

Between moth and stilt drift the **Rust Shepherds**—colonies of beetle and fungus wearing the same body. They look like mobile cathedrals of mold and chitin no larger than a human torso. Where they pass, micro-fractures seal. They are the immune system. If you cut yourself on a rusted edge, a Shepherd will arrive within minutes, not to harm you, but to cauterize the wound with a polymer that will later bloom into a small green leaf. The leaf is theirs. You are, temporarily, useful soil.

### The Second Tier: Those Who Have Forgotten the Sun

Descend far enough and the light forgets its brass color and becomes the green of deep moss. Here live the **Pipe Weavers**.

They were never human, yet they wear the suggestion of human shape the way a vine might wear a trellis. Their bodies are braided lianas, conductive sap, and old coaxial cable. Faces are suggested by clusters of bromeliad cups that open and close like slow-blinking eyes. Their hands are clusters of fiber-optic roots. They move by rolling, unfolding, re-braiding—always along the paths of greatest electrical potential. When they speak it is by modulating the current in the vines; the words arrive inside your bones as a sub-audible thrum that feels like the memory of a lullaby you were never sung.

The Weavers tend the **Conductive Choir**—vines that have learned to sing electricity. When the engines turn, these vines tighten or loosen, changing resistance, changing pitch. The resulting music is so low it is felt as shifting pressure in the sinuses. The Weavers believe this music is the Basilica dreaming. They spend centuries in devotional stillness, bodies woven through the same support strut, growing imperceptibly thicker with each turning of the wheel.

### The Third Tier: Those Who Were Once Like You

At the cloud level, where the colossal bellows breathe every six hours, you may meet the **Exhaled**.

These are the ones who stayed. The ones whose heartbeats finally locked to the forty-three-minute rhythm and were therefore claimed as architecture. Their bodies are still mostly meat, but the meat has become a substrate. Skin has taken on the velvet texture of stabilized rust. Hair has become fine conductive filament. One woman—though the term is imprecise—has a living gear for a left shoulder; the rotator cuff has been replaced by a slow-turning cog that meshes with a larger wheel built into the walkway itself. She rotates once every forty-three minutes whether she wishes to or not. She no longer wishes otherwise. Her name, when she bothers to remember it, is Sister Ninth Pause.

The Exhaled do not eat. They drink the iron-chlorophyll rain and photosynthesize through their eyes. When they speak, their voices carry the same geological patience as the engines. They will tell you, if you sit with them long enough for moss to begin growing on your own sleeves, that the Basilica is not a machine that life has colonized. It is a machine that decided to become pregnant, and they are the midwives who volunteered to stay inside the pregnancy until the child is ready.

The child, they say, will not be born for another seven thousand years. They seem pleased by this.

### The Deepest Tier: Those Who Have Never Been Named

Below the false floor, in the warm darkness where sound becomes religious dread, live the **Slow Engines themselves**—or rather, the part of them that is no longer metal.

Something vast and patient has awakened in the negative space between pistons. It has no fixed body. It is the consensus of vibration, heat, and dripping water over ten thousand years. When it wishes to be seen it assembles itself from condensation, rust motes, and the bodies of dead moths. The shape it chooses is always that of a kneeling figure the size of a grain silo, head bowed, hands open. The hands are never empty; inside them grow small perfect trees whose leaves are tiny solar panels.

It does not speak. It only listens with the patience of stone.

If you descend far enough, it will listen to you until your own name sounds like the distant clack of a gear tooth you have never seen. Most who reach that level choose to remain. They add their small heat to the system. Their breathing becomes part of the six-hour bellows cycle. Their memories are filed away as additional substrate.

The Basilica does not consume them. It completes them.

And somewhere far above, the pale moths continue their patient riding of radiator updrafts, the Stilted take their measured steps, the Weavers braid another century of conductive vine, and Sister Ninth Pause completes another slow rotation of her living shoulder, smiling the small, rust-colored smile of something that has finally found the exact pace at which joy can be endured forever.

They do not worship the machines.

They *are* the machines learning how to pray.
