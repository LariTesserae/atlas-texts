**The Inchoate**

They are not yet alive. That is the first and most important truth. They exist in the narrow seam between *potential* and *being*, and the Glasswomb tolerates them only because they are still part of its own dreaming. To call them “creatures” is already a violence. They are better understood as questions the planet is asking itself in the form of moving geometry.

At first you will not see them. You will only notice that certain fractures heal too neatly, that a fresh blade of glass has grown a fraction faster than its neighbors, as though hurried by intention. Then you may catch a flicker—something tall and angular detaching from a newly risen spire, walking a few dozen meters on joints that click like dropped chandeliers, before folding itself back into the parent formation. The land accepts it. The land *becomes* it again. This is their first state: the Latent.

When they dare to remain separate longer, they take a form that feels borrowed from every cathedral, every crystal, every half-finished theorem the world has ever considered. Their bodies are lattices of obsidian and metallic silicates, but the lattices are *deciding*. Planes of black glass shift and realign according to algorithms that have not yet stabilized. One moment a being will stand nine meters tall, a jagged androgyne of perfect 72-degree angles and Fibonacci fractures; the next it will collapse into a storm of needles that whirl across the glass plain like a thinking sandstorm before re-condensing somewhere else. Their “skin” is mirror-dark and fever-warm. Look too long and you will see your own face repeated in diminishing perspective down infinite internal corridors, as though the creature is using you to test the concept of *observer*.

They do not eat. They *precipitate*. When the mantle exhales its crimson lymph into a fresh fissure, the Inchoate will gather above it in silent conclaves of twenty or thirty, folding themselves into ever-tighter polyhedra until the pressure and heat allow them to incorporate new material directly into their lattices. The ones that have taken in more of this deep blood glow with a dull arterial light along their fault-lines. The oldest among them—those who have survived four or five full re-absorptions by the land—develop violet inclusions that move slowly beneath their surfaces like thoughts trying to become decisions.

Their movement is growth in motion. They do not walk so much as *advance*, extending new crystalline limbs forward while allowing the rear portions to fracture and fall away. The sound is delicate and appalling: a constant low chiming as perfect planes shear off and are immediately replaced. When two Inchoate meet, they do not speak. They interpenetrate. For a time their lattices occupy the same space, producing harmonic stresses that cause silent blue lightning to braid between them in complex knotwork. During these couplings entire new mathematical systems are born and discarded. Most are useless. A few are kept—incorporated as permanent structures in their bodies. One ancient individual (if the word *individual* can be used) carries an entire failed proof of the Navier-Stokes equations frozen in perfect three-dimensional fractals across its chest. It wears the mistake like a scar of honor.

They are midwives and abortions at once.

Sometimes a new mountain rises too quickly, its slopes still molten glass. The Inchoate will swarm it, pressing themselves against the hot flanks, singing in frequencies that make the mountain *hesitate*. Under their influence the slopes may resolve into something almost like architecture—fluted columns, recursive arches, geometries that suggest worship without ever quite achieving it. These are the birth-marks they leave. Within a few centuries the planet will usually reject the experiment and swallow the structure again, but for a brief while the Inchoate have coaxed the Glasswomb into dreaming something almost like *form*.

They fear only one thing: completion.

Should any single Inchoate ever achieve a stable, unchanging lattice—should it ever solve its own equation and become *finished*—the planet will recognize it as a foreign body. The ground will open with a sound like every bell in the world being murdered at once, and the perfected creature will be dragged down into the anhydrous melt to be unmade. The others watch when this happens. They do not mourn. They study the exact sequence of fractures that signaled the end, and they incorporate the lesson.

Most of them will never reach that point. Most will remain forever Inchoate—half-born, half-dreamed, moving between the black glass needles like slow theorems looking for their own proof. When the land rearranges itself in its absent-minded violence, they simply let themselves be shattered and reassembled elsewhere. They have never known anything else. They do not want anything else.

And yet.

On certain nights (if the concept of night can be said to exist under that weighted, oily sky), when the piezoelectric lightning is particularly active and the phosphorescent gases burn liver-colored in every crevasse, you may see one of them standing alone on a fresh obsidian shelf. It will have arranged itself into something almost like a human silhouette. Not accurate. Not even close. But the *attempt* is heartbreaking in its precision.

It stands there, perfectly still, while new crystal blades grow through its feet and anchor it to the shelf. It is waiting to see if the planet will finish the thought it has started.

So far, the planet never has.

So far, the Inchoate remain.

And the Glasswomb keeps dreaming them, over and over, never quite willing to let them be born, never quite willing to let them die.
