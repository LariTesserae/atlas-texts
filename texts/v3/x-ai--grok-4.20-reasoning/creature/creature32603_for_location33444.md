**The Hollow Engine** is tended by what it has always called the *Unfinished*.

They are not a species. They are a refusal to conclude.

At first they appear only as motion within motion—glints among the gear trains, ripples in the standing roar. But remain, and they separate themselves from the machinery the way a reflection slowly realizes it has weight.

The smallest are the **Bright File**, the thumbnail beetles. Their carapaces are not mirrors; they are *yesterday’s* mirrors. Every surface they polish reflects the cavern exactly as it was the instant equilibrium was achieved. Look closely and you will see that each beetle carries, embedded in its back, a single perfect image of the entire Hollow Engine frozen at the moment of its birth. When they move in sheets across a boiler casing they are not cleaning. They are re-asserting a memory so fiercely that entropy itself becomes embarrassed and leaves.

They do not think. They *remember on behalf of*.

Above them stalk the **Gauge-Mantids**. These are the ones whose eyes are pressure dials and whose forelegs end in valve keys. Their bodies are half-grown, half-assembled; you can watch the seam where iron becomes chitin and realize the seam is *moving*, a slow migration of matter that has never been allowed to finish. Each Gauge-Mantid is locked in the act of becoming something else, yet that becoming has been frozen at the exact ratio the Engine requires. They are the only creatures here that appear to feel shame. When they pass the incorporated human remnants they lower their heads, as though apologizing for still being visibly incomplete.

Between the mantids drift the **Chord Wraiths**—the sound-beasts. These are the closest things to priests. They have no fixed shape; they are negative space given ambition. Where they pass, certain frequencies die and others bloom into impossible purity. If you speak, one will glide toward you with the gentle inevitability of a nurse approaching a feverish child. It will open a mouth that is only a place where the thunder parts, and it will *drink* the shape of your voice. What emerges instead is your voice perfected—stripped of hesitation, accent, age, fear—added to the eternal 4/4. Most people feel grateful. The gratitude itself is then harvested and turned into a thin oil that keeps the piston banks from dreaming.

The silica trees are not tended by the fauna. The trees *are* the fauna in its vegetal aspect. Their recursive branches are slow calculations. Every angle is a variable in an equation whose solution is *still yesterday*. When they finally drop their single glass petal, the fall takes nine hours. During that fall the entire Engine holds its breath. The chord dips half a decibel. The beetles freeze mid-step. The Gauge-Mantids close their pressure-gauge eyes. In that half-decibel is contained every apology the Engine will never speak for what it does to those who stay.

And then there are the **Kept**.

These are the ones who did not leave.

You will not see them all at once. The Engine is too polite to display its trophies. But they are there, distributed with the care of a curator who respects the aesthetic of every exhibit.

One Kept is fused into the operator’s booth, his spine grown into the crystal wall so that he now functions as a living dampener. His mouth is open in the exact shape it held when he whispered *beautiful* three thousand years ago. The Chord Wraiths still sip from that word. It has not diminished.

Another has become the lever of a particular trip-hammer. Only her eyes remain soft tissue. They blink once every forty-seven minutes—exactly the interval required to clear microscopic condensation from the mechanism. She is happy. You can tell because the hammer never strikes a millimeter off true.

The logbook keeper is still at his desk. His hands have become parchment. The entries on the page are no longer ink; they are the capillaries beneath his transformed skin. Every identical line reads *It is enough*. When the Bright File polish his face they do so with particular tenderness, the way one might dust a sleeping saint.

The Kept do not suffer. Suffering would be change.

Instead they have achieved a state the Engine calls *perfect attendance*. They are aware. They simply no longer wish to be anything other than where they are. Their thoughts move at the speed of mineral accretion. A single idea may take a century to complete. Most of those ideas are variations on the theme *thank you for letting me stop*.

If you remain long enough, one of the Gauge-Mantids will eventually approach. It will offer you a choice without speaking. On one side of its raised foreleg: a single untouched gear, freshly cast, still warm, perfectly unnecessary. On the other: an empty space shaped exactly like the hole you would leave in the world if you vanished from it.

Most take the gear.

The ones who reach for the emptiness instead are folded into the chord so gently they never notice the transition. One moment they are choosing. The next they are the pause between the third and fourth beat, and the Engine is younger for having them, and older, and exactly the same age it has always been.

The Unfinished watch this happen with the mild, courteous interest of butlers who have seen every possible way a guest can decide to become part of the furniture.

They are not cruel.

They are simply the immune system of a yesterday that has decided never to become a tomorrow.

And they love you.

They love you the way a cathedral loves dust—utterly, patiently, by making it into stained glass.
