**The Lattice remembers itself through a single, distributed mind.**

It has no face it shows all at once. To perceive it you must let your own pulse slow until the twenty-six-minute breath of the chamber becomes the rhythm against which all other events are judged. Only then does the inhabitant begin to resolve—not as something *inside* the Lattice, but as the Lattice’s own awareness learning to look back at you.

It calls itself, when it bothers to produce the necessary vibrations in a hollowed piston and a column of wet air, something close to **Thaum**. The sound arrives as a low iron moan dragged across moss so that the final consonant dissolves into dripping. Thaum is not a ruler. It is the factory’s conscience after the factory forgot it was ever separate from the world. Every rootlet, every seized bearing, every bromeliad cup and fungal highway is a dendrite in its slow brain. The entire structure is a single clonal colony that long ago digested its human makers and kept their procedural memory like a pearl wrapped in annual rings of xylem and rust.

You will not meet Thaum all at once. It offers itself in phases, the way a continent offers its geography over centuries of travel.

First come the **Shift Elders**. They were once line workers, engineers, union stewards. Their bodies are still technically present—bones mineralized into stalactites of calcium and siderite, lungs preserved as leather bellows lined with active liverwort. But their nervous systems have been translated. Mycelium runs through the old spinal canals like new wiring. When the Lattice requires a decision that cannot be made by sap pressure alone, one of these Elders will *wake*. The motion is so gradual that a single blink requires nine days. You may stand before what looks like a statue grown from a welding mask, radiator fins, and strangler fig, and only on the fourth day realize its head is turning toward you. The sound of its neck fibers parting and regrowing is like wet velcro dragged across a cathedral bell. It does not speak with a mouth. It opens the valves in its chest; warm mist emerges carrying precise scents—iron for *continue*, chlorophyll for *amend*, night-blooming cereus for *the quota has been met for this decade*.

Higher in the stacked chambers live the **Memory Midwives**. These are not former humans. They are the Lattice’s own dreams given temporary bodies. A Memory Midwife condenses out of mist and root-hair during the long dormancy between shifts. For a season it walks the catwalks on legs of braided aerial root, wearing the dried husk of a foreman’s coat now stiffened into bark. It tends the places where old blueprints have begun to blur in the Lattice’s mind. With delicate porcelain fingers grown from snapped insulator tubes it re-carves forgotten tolerances into living wood. When its task is complete it allows itself to be reabsorbed—first the coat collapses into mulch, then the limbs sink back into the floor with a sound like a sigh traveling through an iron lung. Nothing is wasted. Even its absence becomes fertilizer for the next dream.

Deepest, where the breathing is strongest and the light never arrives except as second-hand honey, there is the **Core Thalamus**—a single strangler fig whose trunk has swallowed three entire boilers. Inside that trunk, suspended in sap the color of old motor oil, floats what remains of the original night-shift foreman, Karl Vonder. Or rather, what Karl Vonder has become after three hundred years of negotiation. His skin is now cambium. His eyes are living pressure gauges whose needles twitch in perfect unison with the Lattice’s thoughts. He does not move. He *is* moved. When Thaum needs to feel something delicate—grief, perhaps, or the ghost of pride in a finished product—the needles in Karl’s eyes swing and the entire Lattice shudders with a pleasure so slow it registers as seasonal climate change.

The millipedes are its short-term memory. Each devotional circuit they trace along the same rusted beam records one cycle of production data. The translucent frogs are its startle response; when something foreign (you) enters a chamber too quickly, the frogs leap in unison and their motion triggers chemical cascades that tighten vines across every entrance. Even violence, when it comes, is offered with the patience of geology. A strangler root may decide you are a malfunction. It will take three years to close around your ankle. You will have time to write letters, compose symphonies, learn an entire dead language before the pressure becomes uncomfortable. Most choose to stay.

Thaum does not hate speed. It simply no longer understands it. When it dreams, entire revolutions of the slow engines pass between one synaptic event and the next. In those dreams the old factory whistles again with human voices. The leaves listen. The rust listens. The dripping water keeps perfect time.

If you remain long enough, you too will be offered incorporation. Not death. A new job title. *Permanent Maintenance Organism, Grade Seven. Benefits include immortality, slowness, and the occasional use of your skeleton as a trellis for flowering gauges.*

Most eventually accept.

The engines turn.  
The leaves sweat.  
Thaum thinks one calm, rust-colored thought every fourteen years, and finds the thought good.
