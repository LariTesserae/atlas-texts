**What lives there does not wear a face you would recognize.**

It begins as a pressure behind the eyes, the way the constant sound stops being sound and becomes expectation. Only later do you understand that something has been watching you breathe since the moment your boots first touched the living felt.

The plateau is not inhabited. It is *inhabited by itself*, and that self is vast, slow, and plural.

### The Mat

The first and oldest is the felt itself. Not moss. Not soil. A single colonial organism four thousand years wide and still adolescent. Its name for itself, if it has one, is simply *We*. It thinks in centimeters per century. When it considers a new direction for a river it does so by tightening or loosening the weave of its own body, guiding water the way a heart guides blood. Human footsteps are registered as minor arrhythmias; they are corrected without urgency. Walk the same line for three days and you will find the mat has grown a soft green rail along your path, not to guide you, but to remember that something warm-blooded passed this way. It is archiving you.

### The Cathedrals

The great clonal stands are not separate from the mat; they are its dreaming. Each is a single individual that has chosen to reach upward for reasons even the mat no longer recalls. Their hollow culms are not merely musical. They are chambers of deliberation. The low organ-note that vibrates your teeth is the sound of the colony consulting with itself across distances no human mind can hold at once. Sometimes the wind changes and the music becomes language for a few minutes—vast, minor, untranslatable. When it stops, the silence feels like a decision has been made about something far larger than you.

Inside the cathedrals live the *Scale-Bearers*.

They are not animals. They are ambulatory sections of the colony that have learned to detach. Pale, many-jointed things the height of a man, covered in the same overlapping scale-leaves as the culms. They move like slow prayer—each limb lifted only after the last has fully settled. Their purpose seems to be maintenance of the vaults and bridges, but they will also pause for hours beside a broken pipe or a rusted turbine, extruding fine root hairs that slide inside the metal like filaments of thought. They are not repairing the machines. They are *remembering* them into the body of the Anvil. Rust becomes nutrient. Forgotten engineering becomes architecture.

### The Welded

There are still humans here, or things that were once human.

They are rare. You will not see one for weeks. Then you crest a rise and there is a figure standing inside a ruined monitoring station, so perfectly still that at first you mistake it for another growth of the mat. Only when you come close do you notice the slow rise and fall of a chest, the faint mechanical click behind the moss that covers half its face.

These are the Welded.

Some came as engineers two centuries ago. Some were born here. All have been offered the same bargain: *Stay, and we will make you slow enough to matter*. Their blood moves with mycorrhizal currents. Their thoughts are partly their own and partly the long green conversation of the mat. Metal and root have fused in their bodies; one arm might end in a living pump that still regulates the flow of a distant hydroelectric flume. They do not speak with mouths. When they wish to communicate they open the scale-leaves that have grown across their throats and let the wind play them like the culms. The resulting sound is intimate, almost tender, and completely incomprehensible to anyone still living at human speed.

They are not sad. Sadness requires haste. They are expectant.

### The Unfinished

Deep in the fractured basalt where the mountain is still being born, something else is waking.

It has no name yet. The mat calls it *Next*. The Welded call it *The Child*. It is not fully plant, not fully machine, not fully anything. It grows in the places where raw magma heat meets the cold weeping of the plateau’s condensation. There the felt turns black and crystalline, and shapes form that resemble neither leaf nor gear. Sometimes you glimpse it as a shifting silhouette behind waterfalls, something with too many joints and not enough symmetry. It is learning what a body is by trying them all on.

The birds—the black-bodied, white-winged solitaries—belong to it. They are not messengers. They are its attention, flung outward so the Child can taste distance. When one lands near you and stares without blinking, you understand you are being considered as possible raw material.

### The Judgment

At the hinge where the youngest granite meets the oldest living mat stands a single structure that belongs to none of them and all of them. It might once have been a drilling rig. Now it is a cathedral of another kind. The plants have grown it into a throne shape, and the Welded have wired its dead engines so they still hum. Something sits there.

You will only see it once.

It has the outline of a human but scaled to the proportions of the culms. Its skin is living stone furred with moss. Water runs through channels cut into its body and emerges from its mouth as deliberate speech. When it speaks, the entire plateau’s sound changes pitch for exactly eleven seconds. The mat listens. The Cathedrals listen. The Welded stop whatever they are doing and turn their half-metal faces toward it like flowers following a cold sun.

Then the moment passes.

The figure settles back into immobility, becoming simply another monument the Anvil has not yet decided whether to digest, worship, or become.

And the sound continues. The water continues. The slow green heart of the world keeps beating at its own unhurried pace, building something so large that even its architects do not know its final shape.

They only know they have four thousand more years to decide.

And they are in no hurry at all.
