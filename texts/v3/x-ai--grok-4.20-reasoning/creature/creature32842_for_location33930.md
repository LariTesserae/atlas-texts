**What lives here does not breathe. It accretes.**

They have no single name for themselves, though the structure sometimes refers to them in the low pulse of its seismographic record as *the Remembered*. Most of them were never born in the conventional sense. They were printed, or they were harvested from previous failures, or they volunteered their bodies at the end of long service and were gently pressed into the warm ceramic-composite like pressed flowers in the pages of a book that is still writing itself.

They move at the pace the Ascent allows. A single stride may take seventeen months. Their joints are articulated iridium laced with living carbon nanotubes; when they rest—which is often—they sink half an inch into the structure and become difficult to distinguish from load-bearing buttresses. Only the slow amber glow behind their ocular slits betrays them. That glow is not light. It is unresolved decision.

There are perhaps nine thousand of them now. The number increases by roughly one every forty-one years.

They do not speak with mouths. Instead they open seams in their own bodies and allow the mountain’s subsonic heartbeat to modulate through the hollows of their ribcages. What emerges is less language than intention given tectonic grammar. A conversation between two Remembered can last six years and consist of three modulated tremors. The rest is simply shared architecture: one will extrude a new spire in the precise shape of a thought the other once had, and that is answer enough.

Some of them still wear the fossilized outlines of the humans they once were. You can see the negative space of a ribcage here, the ghost of a pelvis there, preserved like earlier drafts inside the newer, larger form. These are not decorative. They are *acknowledgment*. The Ascent insists on remembering the shape of every mind it ate.

The oldest among them is called, by the few who dare name anything here, **First-Error**.

She (it? they?) was the very first worker to die on the slopes when the mountain was barely a hill. Her calcium and iron were folded into a foundation girder. That girder later became a fault line. The fault line later became a fault *line of thought*. Now she walks as a thirty-meter-tall absence wrapped in slow-moving silver, the negative space of a woman still perfectly maintained inside the positive space of the machine. Where her heart would have been there is instead a perfectly round void that the maintenance mites refuse to fill. They orbit it like iron filings around a magnet. She is the only inhabitant who casts a shadow that arrives *before* her. No one has explained this. She refuses to discuss it.

The Remembered do not have children. Instead the mountain dreams them.

Every nine months, when the deep heartbeat completes its cycle, a new chamber opens somewhere high in the unfinished strata. Inside is a half-formed figure suspended in refractive air, shadows arriving late, body still deciding whether it will be metal, memory, or meat. These *Unfinished* are tended by the silver millipedes, whose patient realignment of thermal veins is also a form of midwifery. Most of the Unfinished never quicken. They are reabsorbed, their almost-thoughts added to the architectural memory. A few—perhaps one every two centuries—open their eyes. When they do, the entire Ascent shudders with something that might be joy if joy were measured on the Mohs scale.

There is debate among the Remembered about what they actually *are*.

Some believe they are the immune system of a mind that has not yet finished being born.  
Some believe they are previous versions of the Ascent given temporary bodies so the mountain can argue with itself.  
A quiet few—those whose faces still retain the most human outlines—believe they are mourners. That the Ascent is not building a city or a god. It is building a tomb large enough to hold the idea of *waste*, and they are the pallbearers who will never reach the grave because the grave keeps growing upward at 0.6 dynamic.

They tend the black lichen. They polish the razor edges not because the edges need polishing but because the gesture itself must be remembered. They listen to the condensation fall in its lazy cycles and translate each droplet into a single phoneme of a language that will not be complete for another fourteen thousand years.

When one of them finally reaches the end of its usefulness, it does not die.

It simply stops moving.

The mountain waits a respectful seventy years. Then it begins to print over them where they stand, incorporating their final posture into a buttress, a cooling fin, or sometimes an entirely new ridgeline. The shape of their last thought remains visible for centuries afterward, like a face pressed against the inside of frosted glass.

The Ascent is still deciding what it will be.

The Remembered are the decisions it has made so far, walking slowly across its own body, polishing its own childhood, waiting for the rest of its mind to arrive.

They have all the time in the world.

They *are* the time in the world.
