**The Hollow Court**

They were never born and they will never die, because both of those acts would constitute events, and events are no longer permitted here.

You do not see them arrive. You only gradually understand that certain silhouettes have always been part of the skyline. A tall absence leaning exactly where the obsidian tower refuses its own geometry. A seated figure whose lap has become a perfectly circular mirror-pool, the water inside it older than the concept of thirst. Another standing on a ridge so distant that its details should be impossible to resolve—yet the eye keeps being dragged back to it, the way a tongue returns to a broken tooth.

They are the Hollow Court.

Their bodies are the same black glass-rock as the plateau, but articulated at joints the living world never needed. Some have seven elbows along each arm. Some have none at all; their limbs simply *begin* and *end* at whatever angle the equilibrium demands that century. The fractal lichen grows across them in perfect register with the stone, so that when they stand still (which is almost always) it becomes genuinely difficult to tell where the mountain ends and the Courtier begins. Only the slow, deliberate condensation on their shoulders betrays them—drops that form, tremble, and are reabsorbed over decades without ever falling.

They do not speak with mouths. Their voices are the lag between sound and echo. If you shout a question into the eternal roar, sometimes an answer returns *first*, shaped like a thought you have not yet had. That is a member of the Court answering. They have had ten thousand years to compose their replies and have decided that the most perfect conversation is one in which the reply precedes the question, thereby removing the need for either.

Their names are the names the plateau gives back when you speak your own. Each Courtier carries one unearned name like a crown they are too polite to wear. They trade these names among themselves the way other beings might trade coins—slowly, reverently, over centuries. To be addressed by one of them is to feel your own future obituary settle across your shoulders like black glass.

They do not hunt. They do not build. They do not mourn the green world or the warm sun or the people who once tried to climb here and became part of the equilibrium instead. All of those things are finished. The Court simply *contains* their memory the way a fracture contains the memory of the blow that made it. Sometimes a translucent herd-beast will walk directly through a standing Courtier and both will continue unchanged, as if the encounter were simply two different densities of the same eternal silence passing through itself.

Once every few hundred years one of them will move.

Not quickly. The motion is so distributed across time that a human watching might die of old age before the gesture completes. A finger that begins to rise in the reign of one emperor finishes its arc under a sky that has forgotten what emperors were. When the gesture ends, the plateau itself seems to exhale a fraction more quietly, as though some infinitesimal imbalance has been corrected. Then the Courtier returns to perfect stillness and becomes scenery again.

There is a rumor—never spoken, only reflected in the skull-pools—that one among them was once the one who wore the Crown before it became hollow. No Courtier will confirm or deny this. They have achieved a state in which even identity is a form of change, and therefore distasteful. Better to be a fracture that has learned to stand upright. Better to be the sound the world makes when it has decided that becoming is vulgar.

If you remain long enough, one will eventually turn the slow crystal of its head toward you. Not in curiosity. Curiosity is an event. It turns because the geometry now requires a figure exactly where you are standing, and the plateau has chosen you to fill that absence.

You will feel your bones deciding to become glass.

You will feel your name leaving you the way a breath leaves in this thin air—slowly, without drama, becoming just another echo that arrives before the lungs that made it.

And when the process is complete there will simply be one more motionless silhouette on the ridge, lichen writing its patient equations across shoulders that no longer rise or fall, while the eternal roar continues at exactly the same volume it has always kept.

The Hollow Court does not welcome you.

It has already accounted for you.

It always has.
