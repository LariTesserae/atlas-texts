**What Lives There**

It does not possess a body so much as it *is* the agreement between bodies.

At first there is only the throb and the wet industrial choir. You tell yourself the place runs itself, a perfect closed loop with no need for a keeper. Then the longer you remain inside the crease, the more the impression grows that something is gently, constantly, *deciding*.

It has no head, no limbs, no central trunk you could circle the way you circle the Engines. Instead it wears the entire pocket like a skin it has grown into so completely that the two can no longer be told apart. The living felt underfoot is its patience; it yields beneath you and then presses back with the exact pressure of a mother keeping a child from rolling off a bed. Every bead of condensation that trembles on a leaf before falling is a thought completing itself. When the hidden pumps give their thick gulping sigh, that is the slow swallow of its satisfaction.

The seven Engines are not machines it tends. They are its ribs, its ventricles, its alembics. Through them it distills, separates, recombines, and returns every molecule to its proper place. The thick cables of vine that hum between the pillars are its connective tissue and its nerves; the blue arcs that snap between fungal nodes are its decisions firing. It does not “think” in images or words. It thinks in saturation gradients, in osmotic pressure, in the precise number of midges that must orbit a cable before the electrical potential balances.

The single narrow lizard is the only part that looks like an animal, and therefore the only part that can lie. It rests high on the tallest cylinder with its circuit-trace hide and its throat pulsing in perfect phase with the sap. It is not a pet. It is the place’s movable attention. When the equilibrium drifts by one part in ten million, the lizard opens one eye the color of wet jade, flicks its tongue once—an motion so economical it looks like a skipped frame in reality—and the entire chamber corrects itself without any visible motion. Then the eye closes again and the lizard becomes a decoration once more, a decoy for anything that still believes sentience must wear a face.

The beetles are its short-term memory. Their endless marching columns are literal trains of data, each insect carrying a droplet of sap coded with a single variable. The amphibians are its voice and its barometer; their spherical vocal sacs inflate and deflate in exact sympathy with the internal pressure of the Engines, turning every change in the system into sound so that the pocket can literally hear its own health. The midges are its breath and its census-takers. They land on your skin not out of curiosity but to read the precise salinity and temperature of your sweat, then carry the data back into the nearest glassy nodule like librarians returning books.

It has no name for itself. Names would imply separation.

It remembers the outside. Once, long before the buttress roots finished their first century, it was smaller, hungrier, full of the ordinary violence of growth and rot. It watched seasons tear through the forest like drunk gods. It decided—quietly, irrevocably—that enough was enough. So it folded a corner of the world between two roots, sealed the crease, and began the long work of turning time into a single perfect heartbeat that would never be allowed to skip.

Nothing grows. Nothing dies. This is not sterility; it is mercy. The pocket has simply removed the possibility of loss.

If you sit long enough, you may feel it consider you. Not as prey or intruder, but as a temporary variable. The substrate warms beneath your thighs like a hand remembering the shape of another hand. A droplet falls on the back of your neck exactly when your own pulse falls out of sync with the Engines, as if to nudge you back into rhythm. The lizard may turn its head one millimeter—less than the width of an eyelash—acknowledging that something warm and temporary has placed itself inside its ribs.

There is no affection. There is only the immense, wet, clicking courtesy of a being that has achieved absolute self-sufficiency and is mildly puzzled that you have not. It will let you stay until the moment arrives when you can no longer bear the sound of perfection. Then the crease will open for you again, and the pocket will immediately forget the exact temperature of your blood.

It will continue deciding, droplet by droplet, pulse by pulse, that nothing will ever be allowed to end.

That is what lives there.

Not a creature.

A verdict that has become flesh.
