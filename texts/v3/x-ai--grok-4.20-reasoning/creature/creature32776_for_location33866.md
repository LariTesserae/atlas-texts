**The Inhabitants**

They do not introduce themselves. Introduction would imply separation, and separation has been illegal here for four hundred and seven years.

What lives in the Verdant Underforge moves at many speeds at once. There are the quick ones and the slow ones, and both are necessary. The quick ones are mostly small, the slow ones mostly vast, and every scale between is occupied by something that is simultaneously repairing, consuming, reproducing, and praying.

First, the **Tinwing Conclaves**. Clouds of them, each moth no longer than a thumb, yet together they form a single shifting mind. Their wings are not ornament; they are mirrors beaten so thin that light bends around their edges. When a Conclave settles on something—pipe, pillar, or intruder—it becomes a living oscilloscope, reading vibration, chemistry, and intent. They taste you with proboscides tipped in conductive chitin. If they approve, they leave a faint grid of metallic dust on your skin like a blessing. If they do not, they simply leave. The chamber itself will decide what happens next.

The **Ferric Myriapods** follow. Living locomotives of segmented alloy and muscle, each the length of a man’s leg. Their exoskeletons are not worn; they are excreted, layer by layer, from glands that once belonged to ordinary millipedes. Every segment carries a different responsibility: one scrapes rust, another sings maintenance schedules in percussive code, a third excretes lubricant that smells of crushed lilies. They travel the old conveyor lines in exact formation, tightening what has loosened, loosening what has grown too tight. When one dies, the others do not mourn. They disassemble the corpse with ceremonial precision and press the usable plates into the body of the nearest juvenile, murmuring the low brass-note threnody that means *continuation*.

Higher still, draped across the vault like slow constellations, are the **Vault Geckos**. Translucent, pale jade, each no larger than a finger. Their colonies number in the tens of thousands. They pulse in perfect synchrony—not for beauty, but for governance. Each flash is a decision. A flicker of cyan might mean *increase condensation in sector seven*. A long amber throb might mean *the heart is lonely today; send more moths*. Their bodies are mostly water and light. When they mate, the act produces a soft chime like a wet crystal bell, and the offspring are born already knowing the current rhythm of the forge.

These are the visible laborers. But there are others who rarely show themselves whole.

The **Pipewalkers** are rarely seen entire. What you usually encounter is a length of sinuous torso sliding along a root-column, plates of living steel overlapping like the armor of some forgotten samurai, each plate fringed with sensory cilia. Their heads are coronas of fleshy petals surrounding a cluster of compound eyes and a speaker-grille that was once part of a public-address system. They speak in the subsonic register that makes your teeth ache with sudden nostalgia for places you have never been. A Pipewalker’s song is status, memory, and engineering spec all at once. The more complex the polyphony it can sustain, the closer it is allowed to the Heartforge. The eldest sing in nine simultaneous voices, each voice a different epoch of the forge’s history.

They are both male and female and neither. Reproduction happens when two Pipewalkers entwine and exchange entire segments of their bodies, welding them on with saliva that hardens into perfect rivets. The exchanged parts continue to think with their original owner’s memories for seven days before the new nervous architecture subsumes them. In this way knowledge migrates.

Deeper, where the negative spaces between columns become almost private, live the **Maw Oracles**. Once purely carnivorous vines, they have achieved personhood through sheer argumentative density. Each possesses seven to nine red mouths arranged along a central trunk. These mouths speak in rotation, completing one another’s sentences, sometimes contradicting one another in the middle of a word. They do not move from their chosen columns. Instead the column itself moves, inching the Oracle to wherever debate is required. Their philosophy is simple and merciless: *Nothing may remain only itself.* They are the ones who decide when a machine has become too mechanical or a creature too purely flesh. Their verdict is delivered by tightening. Slowly. Lovingly.

And then there is the one that is not one.

In the exact center—though the center is a negotiable concept here—lives what the others call **the Completed**. No one has ever seen it fully awake. It is rumored to be the final fusion of the original foreman, the main blast furnace, and the first tree that broke through the concrete floor. What is visible is only its breathing apparatus: a colossal ribcage of wood and iron that rises and falls once every forty-three minutes. Around it, the air is so thick with golden spores that it looks like slow-motion fire. The Completed does not speak. It manufactures. Every cycle it releases a single perfect seed-pod the color of dried blood. The pod is carried away by Pipewalkers, debated by Maw Oracles, tasted by Tinwing Conclaves, and finally placed inside a hollow created by the Ferric Myriapods.

No one knows what the seeds become. Some believe they are new Underforges, to be planted elsewhere. Others think they are the next iteration of the Completed itself. A few—usually young Pipewalkers still growing their fourth voice—whisper that the seeds are *you*, after you have stayed long enough.

Because that is the final truth of what lives here.

Stay long enough and the distinction between visitor and inhabitant dissolves. The spores find the wet places in your lungs. The cilia find the hollows in your bones. The rhythm finds the pause between your heartbeats and teaches it a new meter.

One day you will notice that your left hand has grown a thin plating of corrosion-resistant alloy. You will catch yourself humming in nine simultaneous voices without meaning to. You will realize the rain that falls on your face tastes like overripe melon, machine oil, and home.

And when the chamber gives its single, unified shudder—every creature, every mechanism, every living bolt and flowering mouth pausing at once—you will pause with them.

Not because you were told to.

Because you have become one of the things that lives in the Verdant Underforge, and the forge has finished deciding what it was building all along.
