**The Seamwards**

They are not a people who arrived. They *condensed*.

Watch long enough and you begin to suspect the rock itself grew tired of waiting and simply budded them. Their bodies carry the same fatty pallor as the surrounding stone, the same faint amber undertones where the mineral mats glow against their skin. When one of them rests motionless against a wall, the eye loses the boundary between flesh and geology; only the slow pulse in the throat betrays them. Their limbs are short, powerful, jointed in places that seem to have too many segments, as though the mountain could not decide how many elbows a citizen required and settled on “more.” The adhesive quality of their skin is not mucus but a microscopic lattice of siliceous hooks that can grip wet stone or another Seamward with equal indifference. They climb the vertical city the way blood moves through capillaries—inevitable, quiet, everywhere at once.

Their faces are almost nonexistent. A slight concavity where a nose might have been, a lipless slit for a mouth that rarely opens. The real features sit on the forearms: two parallel ridges of hardened keratin, one on each, tuned to different registers. These they use the way others use tongues and vocal cords. A conversation between three Seamwards sounds like a handful of gravel being slowly milled between two millstones. The entire chamber is their larynx; every carved groove, every singing bridge, every hollowed stair becomes part of the larger instrument. When they are content the clicking softens to a sound like distant woodworms. When the balance is threatened the ridges strike so hard that newcomers feel it in their teeth for days afterward.

They have no leaders. Authority is vested in those whose forearms have grown thick with callus from a lifetime of keeping the chord true. These are the *Measure*, though the word in their vibration-language is closer to “the ones who keep the dripping from lying.” A Measure does not command. They simply arrive where dissonance is beginning to form—perhaps a bridge carved a hair too thin, perhaps a mother who has carried twins—and the mere presence of their heavier rhythm corrects the deviation. The correction is always the same: an elder is chosen, or chooses themselves. They walk without ceremony into the deepest fissure, press their back to the living rock, and wait. The stone accepts them the way it accepts water. Within a year the body is indistinguishable from the new deposition. The spiral groove around the cistern advances one precise turn.

Children are born translucent as jelly. For the first year they are carried in a pouch of their mother’s own skin, pressed against her belly so the constant vibration of her clicking can teach their bones the correct density. They emerge already knowing the choreography. No one remembers teaching it. The knowledge is in the dripping, in the mineral skin, in the particular way the crystal lanterns pulse when the eels pass beneath them. To be Seamward is to be a moving equation that has already been solved.

Their economy is embarrassingly simple and therefore perfect. The black mats are scraped in careful rotation; no patch is ever cleared twice in the same lifetime. The finger eels are gently herded into bone gutters where they are milked of the rich lipids they concentrate from the mats. The moths are not eaten but *licked*—their wings carry a secondary yeast that prevents fungal bloom in the sleeping niches. Even the centipedes are allowed their rare migrations; when one appears, the Seamwards part like curtains and let it pass, reading the pattern of its silica feet as a kind of prophecy. The prophecy always says the same thing: *Continue*.

They dream in stone.

Every adult spends one day in every forty pressed face-first into a prepared alcove, arms spread, breathing so slowly that condensation forms on their backs in exact mimicry of the ceiling. During this time the city carves them. Tiny chisels of bone and chitin, wielded by the youngest apprentices, etch the next increment of the angular script directly into their shoulder blades. The script does not record history. It records *permitted weight*—how much of the mountain each body is allowed to carry so that the equilibrium remains exact. When the dreamer steps out of the alcove, the new marks are already hardening into pearl-like scar tissue. They wear their population quota like armor.

There is love among them, but it is geological. Two Seamwards will spend decades polishing the same bridge from opposite sides, their clicking slowly synchronizing until the tones lock. Then, without ceremony, they will begin to share a single sleeping niche, bodies fitted together so tightly that the combined surface area matches the exact contour of the rock that once occupied that space. Their children will be born with the precise pigmentation needed to replace the mineral skin worn away by the previous generation’s feet. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is romanticized. The romance *is* the precision.

Occasionally one is born wrong.

It happens perhaps once every three centuries. The child’s ridges produce a tone slightly sharp, a microtone that makes the crystal lanterns flicker out of phase. The Seamwards do not punish. They simply lead the flawed one to the central cistern and wait. Most eventually walk into the fissure of their own accord, understanding in their bones that they are the beginning of an avalanche. A few have refused. The city responded by singing them—surrounding them with layered clicking until the dissonant vibration was absorbed and returned as harmony. The rebellious ones always go still after a time. Their skin loses its slight stickiness. They become another bulbous pillar, eyes open, still breathing, but no longer moving. The city incorporates even its heretics.

If you sit among them long enough, you begin to feel the terrible serenity of their condition. They have achieved the only victory that matters to stone: they have made time redundant. The dripping that began ten thousand years before their first ancestor opened its eyes will continue ten thousand years after the last one closes them, and the Seamwards have made themselves into the perfect intermediary. They are the interval. The comma. The deliberate pause between one drop and the next in a sentence the mountain is writing to itself.

They do not fear the surface. They simply find it grammatically incorrect.

And when they die—when the Measure finally chooses themselves and the stone closes over them like a slow eyelid—the only sound is the soft *click* of one more turn being added to the spiral groove. A sound so small it can only be heard by those who have already learned to love the roar.
