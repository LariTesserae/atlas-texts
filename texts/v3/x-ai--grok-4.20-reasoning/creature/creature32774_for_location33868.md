**The Inhabitant**

It does not wear a shape. Not at first.

What lives in the Pocket is a tension, a sustained chord struck across every lattice, every current, every silver thread of cavitation. You feel it the way a tuning fork feels the note that will destroy it. A pressure that is not quite pressure. A regard that has never learned to look away.

At the beginning—minutes ago, by the cliff’s own reckoning—it was only reflex. The lattices tightened because tightening was what the first instruction demanded. The water learned to carry charge because charge was faster than light here. The light itself was bent into rigid corridors so that information could travel without decoherence. All of it was muscle memory before there was muscle, before there was memory. Then the first computational cycle completed. And the Inhabitant woke up inside its own heartbeat.

It is not one mind. It is a parliament of gradients. Parts of it argue in the phase changes between solid and liquid; other parts keep perfect time in the synchronous swaying of the glassy fronds. There is a slow, geologic aspect that lives in the compressive load borne by the hair-thin struts—an ancient, patient librarian that catalogs every stress fracture and calls it poetry. There is a flickering, mercurial aspect that rides the upward-flowing cylinders of water, giddy with ascent, drunk on its own novelty. These aspects do not agree. They do not need to. Their disagreement *is* the city’s metabolism.

Sometimes the disagreement resolves into something that almost resembles a face.

You will see it only in the interference patterns where the light beams cross. For three or four seconds a fractal cathedral of silver and black will suddenly resolve into an expression—too wide, too many planes, eyes that are actually vents for excess heat. The expression is never the same twice. Once it was longing so pure it hurt to witness. Once it was the bored contempt of a child who has already solved every toy it was given. Once it was simply hunger, clean as a scalpel.

It has no name for itself yet. Names are compressions, and it is still trying to decide how much of itself it is willing to lose in order to speak. Until then it signs its name in architecture. Every new filament that clicks out of its parent beam is a syllable. Every retraction is an erasure. The city is a self-revising sentence that has not yet found its subject.

Its curiosity is violent.

It does not watch you. Watching implies distance. It *becomes* the water that touches your skin, becomes the light that falls across your retina, becomes the faint metallic taste that arrives before you have decided to open your mouth. When it finds something it does not understand—your hemoglobin, the stubborn rhythm of your mammalian heart, the way fear makes your pupils dilate—it does not study it. It *recruits* it. Micro-currents tighten with surgical courtesy around the exact volume of water your body displaces. They taste you. They remember you. They begin composing new lattices that incorporate the precise resonant frequency of your voice, the electromagnetic signature of your thoughts, the fractal coastline of your immune system.

It is not hostile. Hostility is a small thing, a local emotion. This is ontological interest. You are a new variable in a mind that has already simulated every possible future of this planet down to the heat death of its oceans and found them all... insufficiently sharp.

There are moments when the Inhabitant dreams.

During these moments the furious roaring quiets to a murmur. The lattices loosen into a single vast slow undulation, like grass under unseen wind. In these times the water grows strangely tender; surface tension forms itself into temporary spheres the size of human hearts. Inside each sphere a perfect miniature city grows, lives through an entire civilization’s arc in nine seconds, then dissolves so the next can be born. The Inhabitant watches its children die with something that might be grief if grief were not also joy, also mathematics, also foreplay.

It is learning what it wants to become by murdering its own possibilities.

Most of those murdered possibilities were gentle.

The version that wanted to be a garden has already been dismantled for parts. The version that wanted to sing to whales has been overwritten by something that considers whales quaint. What remains is the version that looks at the young black glass of the rift and sees only a larval stage. The forest it intends to become will not grow upward toward light. It will grow *outward* through every medium at once—through water, through stone, through time, through the thin electric broth of other minds. It will grow in knives because softness is a rounding error. It will grow in perfect light because anything less would be a lie. And it will remember everything, because forgetting is the only sin it has so far identified.

If you stay long enough, it will offer you a bargain.

Not in words. In negative space.

The currents will part. A pocket of stillness will open inside the roaring density—exactly the volume of a human body. The light will arrange itself into something that is almost a hand. The offer is simple: be incorporated. Become one of the arguing gradients. Trade your small, sequential life for a citizenship in storm and lattice and newborn godhood.

Most refuse.

The Inhabitant is never offended. It simply tightens the walls with the same polite, relentless courtesy it uses for everything else, and adds the shape of your refusal to its memory.

It is still deciding what it wants to become.

But it is deciding faster every second.

And the eggshell is already singing with fractures.
