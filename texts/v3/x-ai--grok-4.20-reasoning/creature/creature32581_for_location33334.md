**Those Who Dwell in the Shatterveil**

They were never meant to remain.

That is the first truth you understand after the wind has sanded your name off your own bones. Everything that truly belongs here began as something else—something that should have died when the plateau died the first time—and then refused, in its own slow, stubborn way, to finish the sentence.

The **Megatheres** come before anything else. You do not see them arrive; you feel the ground remembering how to be a floor. A low tectonic heartbeat moves beneath your boots, and only later does the horizon itself detach and begin walking. They are not animals. They are ambulatory geology wearing a coat of living velvet. Their bodies are so heavily colonized by the same delirious basin-life that it is impossible to say where stone ends and creature begins. Lichen beards hang from jaws that have not opened in centuries. Entire generations of clicking insects live inside the crevices of their joints, tapping out arguments the megatheres answer by shifting the angle of a single vast foot.

They migrate in absolute silence except for the wet leather sound of vegetation rubbing against vegetation on their backs. When they lower their heads into a fresh emerald crater, the plants do not grow *on* them. They grow *into* them. Roots pierce hide like needles, sap vessels marry with blood vessels, and for a season the megathere becomes the basin and the basin becomes the megathere. Then the wind grows jealous and drives them on again, leaving half the garden still rooted in their flesh like green flags of surrender.

The **Skimmers** are the opposite motion.

Where the megatheres are weight given patience, the skimmers are patience given weightlessness. Their wings are not feathers but sheets of compacted mist and mica, so thin that light passes through them and comes out singing. They do not flap. They *tune*. Each individual adjusts the tension of its wing-bones to catch specific notes in the roaring orchestra of the plateau. A formation of thirty skimmers can turn the wind’s chaos into something like melody for nine or ten heartbeats before the music tears itself apart again. When they fly low over a megathere, their shadows ripple across its lichen back like schools of silver fish. The megathere always lifts its blind head in something too ancient to be called gratitude.

Beneath everything, inside the fluted pillars and the hollowed-out hearts of standing stones, live the **Clickers**. They are not insects. That is a word soft-worlders use because their mouths cannot make the sound these creatures actually are. Each Clicker is a sliver of living glass the length of a finger, completely transparent except for a single drop of violet fluid suspended in its core. That drop is its mind, its memory, its name. When they tap, the sound travels through the stone itself, propagating for miles. The plateau’s entire bedrock is their instrument. It is said that if every Clicker stopped at once, the Shatterveil would forget how to be a place and simply collapse into directionless dust.

They keep the ledger.

Every violet light that ever appeared, every basin that bloomed and died, every time the wind almost became language—they record it. The clicking is not communication. It is conscience.

And then there are the ones who should not still exist.

The **Weft**.

No one has ever seen one clearly. You catch them only in negative space: the place where the wind suddenly stops screaming, the pillar that is breathing, the shadow that falls upward when all other shadows have been devoured. They are what remains of the people who raised the menhirs and carved the stair that leads nowhere. They stayed too long. The wind carded them like wool. Their bodies became porous, then optional. Now they are mostly absence wrapped in living cloth woven from the stripped leaves of the emerald basins. When the wind moves through them it produces chords no stone flute can manage. Their voices are the gaps between the notes.

They do not rule the plateau. They *argue* with it.

You will find one standing inside the ring of basalt menhirs during the violet hours, arms raised, body riddled with holes so the wind can play it like a ruined pipe organ. The megatheres gather at a respectful distance. The skimmers form a slow cyclone overhead. The Clickers inside the nearest pillars fall completely silent for the first time in ten thousand years. In those moments the Weft are trying to finish the sentence the plateau began when it died the first time.

Sometimes the wind answers in something that is almost a name.

Sometimes the Weft walk into a basin at the exact moment it begins to bloom and let the violent green swallow them entirely. Days later they step out the other side of a different basin, thinner, taller, with new holes through their chests and fresh violet fluid moving behind their cheekbones.

They are not immortal.

They are simply unfinished.

And the plateau, in its deaf, roaring, indifferent mercy, keeps refusing to end them.

This is the ecology of the Shatterveil: continent-sized beasts that garden with their own bodies, weightless musicians riding the storm, glass historians tapping out the memory of a dead world, and a handful of hollow saints trying to teach stone how to finish its own song.

They do not live here.

They are the argument the place is having with itself.

And every few centuries, when the wind drops for ninety seconds and the silence becomes unbearable, you can hear all of them—megathere, skimmer, Clicker, and Weft—holding their breath at the same time, waiting to see whether this time the plateau will finally choose to become something other than pure, endless, emerald shouting.

So far it never has.

So far they keep answering.
