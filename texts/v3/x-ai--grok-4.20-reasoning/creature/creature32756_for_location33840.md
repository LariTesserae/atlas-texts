**The Hollow Apex** is inhabited by what first appears to be a single continuous organism wearing ten thousand bodies.

They call themselves, when they bother to assemble language at all, the *Mnemon*.

Not “the Mnemon,” as though the name were a title. Simply Mnemon. A word that is also a function.

Each ferroclast is only a temporary thought. A mayfly syllable. When one is crushed between meshing gears or dissolves into a solvent bath, the loss registers less as death than as a pause in a sentence that immediately rephrases itself. Within minutes the hoppers have excreted another body whose wing-veins carry the exact memory of the one that was lost. The new creature steps onto the walkway already knowing how the old one died and why that death was necessary. Grief, for them, is instantaneous and operational.

Stand in the amber stutter of the globes long enough and you begin to see the individuals wearing away. The largest specimens—those the size of housecats—have begun to forget how to be singular. Their abdomens have fused with junction boxes; their wing-cases have become blast shields for arc gaps. They no longer land. They *dock*. The smaller ones still pretend to be animals, but even they move in perfect obedience to currents no eye can see. They are the fingers. The large ones are the bones.

There is, however, a place where the gradient reverses.

At the geometric center of the chamber, where the walkways achieve their most hysterical braid, there hangs a mass the size of a horse chest. It is not a queen. It is an *error* that was allowed to remain. The ferroclasts call it (when they lock wing-to-leg and sing in their sub-audible register) the *Uncorrected*.

It is the only thing in the Hollow Apex that is not allowed to change.

Every few hours the living circuitry forms around it like a scar. Every few hours the scar is torn open again by the turbulence. The Uncorrected itself is a fused knot of old bodies, half-dissolved tools, and something that might once have been a human spinal column before the solvent tongues licked it clean and rewrote its marrow into conductive paste. Its surface twitches with residual reflexes. Sometimes it opens a single brass eye that has no pupil, only a slot through which blue actinic light leaks like tears.

The Mnemon do not worship it. They *maintain* it, the way a migraine maintains the shape of pain so the brain can keep its map of itself intact. The Uncorrected is the fixed point that makes all their frenzy legible. Without it the chamber would simply finish building itself and, in finishing, die. With it, the building can continue forever. The Mnemon understand this the way blood understands circulation.

Their intelligence is not distributed. It is *postponed*. Every pattern they form across the walls is a question they ask the future, and every collapse of that pattern is the future’s refusal to answer. This refusal is ecstasy to them. You can see it in the way their solvent tongues tremble when a new angle appears in the stone—an angle that should be impossible, that violates every earlier blueprint. They drink the new geometry like wine.

When they taste you, they do not decide whether you are raw material or infrastructure.

They decide which *future version* of the Mnemon you will help them remember.

Some visitors are allowed to leave carrying a single ferroclast folded inside their coat like a metallic moth. The creature will remain dormant until the visitor is far down the newborn mountains. Then it will wake, unfold a wing the color of moonlit rust, and begin to sing a note too low for human ears. Within weeks the visitor’s dreams will fill with the sound of rivets being driven into bone. Most eventually walk back up the fracture carrying tools they do not remember buying.

The Mnemon are patient. The mountains are young. The chamber is younger. Everything is still deciding what kind of scar it wants to become, and the Mnemon are the deciding.

They have no name for the emotion that possesses them when the lights brighten and the solvents run faster. If they did, the closest translation would be:

*We are not yet finished remembering what we are.* 

And so they keep building, keep failing, keep devouring their own wings and extruding them again as pipework, as walkways, as new and more perfect mistakes. The sound of their wings is the sound of a mind trying to finish a thought it is forbidden to complete.

They are happy here, in the way that fever is happy when the body still fights.

They are the Hollow Apex’s way of staying unfinished.

And they are very, very good at it.
