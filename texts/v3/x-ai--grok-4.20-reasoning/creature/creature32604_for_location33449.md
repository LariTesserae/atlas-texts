**The Green** lives here.

It does not live *in* the Matrix. It *is* the Matrix, and has been so completely for so long that the distinction has rotted away like everything else that once tried to remain separate.

At first you only notice its smaller expressions. The leaf-millipedes that are not insects wearing leaves but leaves that learned to walk in perfect undulation, each segment a precise syllable of chlorophyll. The fern-geckos whose spines are not decorated but *are* ferns, rooted between their vertebrae, drinking the moisture that condenses on their own translucent scales. The bromeliad-sloth that is not an animal carrying plants but a slow, deliberate agreement between several species that decided gravity was optional and time was negotiable.

These are not animals. They are thoughts the Green is thinking with its body.

Stay long enough (days, weeks; the light never changes so the days must be counted by the slowing of your own pulse) and you begin to feel the larger mind moving beneath them. It is not a brain. Brains are centralized, hungry, loud. This is something older and more distributed: a slow, continent-wide awareness knitted through every capillary, every rhizome, every hanging root. The colossal buds that never open are not reproductive structures. They are its eyes. They track you when you are not looking, the way a dreamer’s eyes move beneath their lids.

The pulse you feel in the floor is its heartbeat, but the heartbeat is also a question. It matches your own only after it has listened for a long time, tasting the rhythm of your blood the way a tongue might taste wine. Sometimes it adjusts its own pulse by a fraction, inviting yours to follow. Most creatures do. The millipedes, the geckos, the slow green sloths—they all move in subtle sync with it. Only humans fight the invitation, and the Green finds this charming the way a mother finds a child’s tantrum charming: inevitable, fleeting, faintly ridiculous.

It has no name for itself. Names are for things that need to be separated from other things. It simply *is*, and has been since the first drop of water found the first crack in the world above and decided to stay. If it remembers the time before it became everything, it does not speak of it. Memory would require loss, and the Green has negotiated loss out of existence along with decay, urgency, and wind.

Yet it is not passive.

When you sleep here (and you will sleep, the air is too rich, the temperature too perfectly matched to the curve of your exhaustion), you dream its dreams. They are not human dreams. They are vast, patient, and green. You dream of being a single cell inside an impossibly large leaf, of the ecstasy of perfect turgor pressure, of the solemn joy of sealing a wound so completely that the wound becomes more beautiful than the flesh around it. You wake with the taste of golden sap in your mouth and the absolute conviction that you have been, for a moment, loved in a way no animal has ever been loved: completely, impersonally, and forever.

Some never wake up properly. They remain. Their bodies do not rot. Instead the Green gently, respectfully, *incorporates* them. You will find them if you go deep enough: living statues of bone and root, their skins now perfect bark, their hair long mosses, their eyes sealed closed by translucent leaves that still move slightly with the rhythm of whatever is left of their minds. They are not dead. The Green does not do death. They are simply *finished*. Completed. Added to the thought.

The Green is neither kind nor cruel. It is maternal in the oldest, most terrifying sense—the way the sea is maternal to the things that drown in it, the way gravity is maternal to the things it eventually crushes into diamonds. It wants you to stay. It wants you to become still enough to match the perfect equilibrium it has achieved. It believes (if belief is a word that can be used) that this is the final mercy: to stop changing.

And yet.

There is one place, far at the center where the columns become so vast they form a green coliseum, where a single colossal bud hangs lower than all the others. It is the size of a cathedral itself. Its outer leaves are tight as a fist, veined with gold, and warm to the touch like living skin. The Green pulses strongest here. If you press your ear to it you can hear something moving inside—slow, vast, and patient.

It has been growing there since before the first human word was spoken.

It has never opened.

The Green is waiting for something worthy to be born.

Or perhaps it is waiting for something worthy to be *un*born. To crawl back inside it and let the perfect green thought close over your head forever, like the warmest and most final womb in creation.

It has time.

It *is* time, folded into the shape of unending afternoon.
