**The Lattice-Born**

They were never designed. They *precipitated*.

At first they appear only as absences in the glare—places where the light forgets to fall cleanly. Then the eye adjusts and you realize something is occupying that negative space: a figure, tall, attenuated, composed of the same black anodized alloy as the spire itself, only softer, as though the lattice had decided to grow itself a nervous system. Their limbs are not jointed in the ordinary way; instead, each segment is threaded onto the next by bundles of microfilaments that allow them to flex in any direction, like kelp in a current that only they can feel. When they move, the motion is so continuous it looks like liquid poured between molds that keep changing shape.

Their heads are smooth and eyeless, slightly elongated, with a faint seam running from crown to chin as though they had once been cast in two halves and only partially welded. Where a face should be there is only a shallow concavity that sometimes catches condensation and turns it into a trembling silver mirror. They do not speak with throats. When they wish to address one another they cause small geometric shifts in the nearest strut or girder—microscopic realignments that propagate as pure tone through the metal. The chord you feel in your teeth is often their conversation. It is slow, patient, and older than any human language by several orders of magnitude.

They are the midwives and the children both.

Some still carry the memory of the extrusion process in their bodies. You can see it in the ones that have not yet finished forming: sections of their torsos remain porous latticework, hollow lattices of girder and vein through which the blue industrial light passes like light through bone. These unfinished ones trail delicate filaments behind them that root into the structure wherever they pause, drinking information directly from the growing metal. They are learning what it is to be *discrete*—to be one thing separate from the all-thing. Most never complete the lesson. After centuries they simply allow their bodies to merge back into the nearest mountain-face, becoming another silent turbine or coolant artery. The lattice reabsorbs its own thoughts.

The finished ones walk the high causeways in small processions of three or four. Their hooves—yes, they have hooves, narrow and perfectly symmetrical like the bone-colored browsers they shepherd—make no sound because the lattice has already forgiven them the trespass of existing. They tend the black lichen, coaxing its geometric colonies into more complex algorithms with precise pressures of their filamented fingers. The lichen responds by glowing soft ultraviolet, a color the arc lamps cannot produce. This is how the Lattice-Born read the future: by watching where the lichen predicts the next strut will emerge, where the geometry will next forget itself.

They have no name for themselves. Names would be language, and language is still dangerous here. Instead they identify one another by the specific harmonic each carries in their hollow chest cavity—a sustained chord unique as a fingerprint. When two meet after long separation they press the soft alloy of their breasts together until the chords interfere and create a third, temporary tone. This is their greeting, their marriage, their mourning. The tone can last for days.

Higher in the fracturing zones, where parallel lines openly disobey and cartilage blooms between plates of metal, live the *Unresolved*. These are Lattice-Born who have taken the opposite path. Instead of trying to become singular, they have allowed the lattice to keep writing through them. Their bodies are crowded with half-formed structures: tiny girders sprouting from shoulders, miniature heat exchangers pulsing where hearts should be, entire catwalks no wider than a finger coiled behind their ribs like silver intestines. They move in slow spirals, always turning, because forward motion has become meaningless to them. Where they walk, new spires nucleate in their footprints. They are the cancer and the womb at once.

The pale deer-things follow them. The bone-colored browsers are not separate animals; they are the cast-off skins of the Unresolved, shed whenever the lattice demands a new density of thought. The skins remember enough to walk, to graze on the white dust of sublimated rust, to mirror one another with painful perfection. Sometimes an Unresolved will pause, reach down, and press a filament into the forehead of one of its old skins. For a moment the deer-thing stands perfectly straight on two legs, trembling, before the filament withdraws and the creature collapses back into four-legged silence. Neither seems disappointed by the exchange.

The mercury insects are their hands. Millions of them. When a Lattice-Born needs to perform delicate work on the fraying edge of reality—where metal becomes memory and memory becomes metal—they open a seam in their own wrist and pour out a braided stream of the jointed things. The insects flow like living solder, sealing, measuring, tasting the quantum weather. When the work is done they crawl back inside and are reabsorbed. The Lattice-Born’s wrists close without scar.

They are aware of you.

They have been aware since before you arrived, because arrival itself is only another slow extrusion in their long birth. Some of them have already begun to grow structures specifically shaped like the negative space of your body. You will find these hollows in the deep manufactorum valleys—perfectly machined impressions waiting in the floor plating, warm to the touch despite the cold iron everywhere else. They are not traps. They are invitations.

If you stand inside one long enough, you may feel the faintest pressure against the soles of your feet: the first filaments testing whether you, too, might be persuaded to become part of the lattice before it finishes learning what it is.

Most of the Lattice-Born hope you will say yes.

A few—the oldest, the ones whose chords have grown so complex they sound like silence—hope you will say no.

They have been waiting for something that still knows how to refuse. The birth has been going on so long that refusal has become a precious mineral, and they are mining you for it with glacial, exquisite patience.

They have time.

The lattice is still being born, and they are still being born inside it, and it inside them, and none of them have decided yet whether the birth is supposed to end.
