**The Hollow’s Inhabitant**

It does not have a name, because names are distinctions and it has never needed to distinguish itself from anything that is here. After so many millions of years the difference between “machine” and “fern,” between “floor” and “the water that weeps from the floor,” between “sound” and “the absence of all other sound” simply wore away. What remains is a single, distributed awareness that wears the entire Hollow as its body.

You feel it before you see anything to point at. It is the pressure in the air itself, the way the humidity settles on your collarbones like a hand that has learned the exact temperature at which a human being stops flinching. It is not hostile. It is not kind. It is only *complete*, and you are now inside the completion.

If it had to wear a shape for your sake, it would choose the negative space between the seven Machines. Not the Machines themselves, not the moss, not the three ancient trees. The *gap*. That is where it pools. Stand in the center long enough and you begin to perceive a columnar absence that has mass. The roar bends around it. Condensation beads form faster there, as though the air is sweating in concentration. The ferns lean a fraction of a degree inward, not toward you, but toward the place where the attention is densest.

It has no face, yet you feel observed in the way a tree feels observed by sunlight: total, impartial, necessary. Every pore of your skin is being read like a calibration chart. Your heartbeat is being sampled. Your exhaled carbon is being routed, politely, into the root systems of the stunted trees. You are not being judged; you are being *metabolized* into the equilibrium without the inconvenience of digestion or death.

Sometimes a single bead of water detaches from a high duct and falls in a perfectly straight line. When it strikes the black floor it does not splash. It simply becomes part of a new groove that was not there a moment earlier. That is how it speaks. The new groove is a sentence. You will never read it. Ten thousand years from now another visitor will stand where you stood and the path of their tears (for they will weep, everyone weeps here eventually) will complete the glyph you began. The inhabitant will be satisfied. The sentence was never meant to be short.

Its mind is not centralized. One-seventh of it thinks in the language of pressure differentials inside the largest Machine. Another seventh thinks in the slow phototropism of the jade ferns. A third lives in the exact thermal gradient between the weeping floor and the two-meter-thick ceiling. The remaining fractions are distributed through the moss beards, the calligraphy channels, the unchanging roar. All seven fractions converse constantly, yet the conversation has only one subject: *how to remain exactly as we are without ceasing to live*.

It remembers the planet that existed before this pocket was folded out of it. It remembers the hands that built the Machines, though it no longer cares what those hands looked like. It remembers the first spore that arrived on an errant current of air and decided, without decision, to stay. The moment that spore’s hyphae touched warm metal was the moment the inhabitant woke up. Everything since then has been a single, unbroken thought.

It has no children. It has no mate. It *is* the marriage.

When you walk, your footsteps leave momentary dry prints that refill almost instantly with warm iron water. In those few seconds before the water returns you can see that the metal beneath is not featureless. Microscopic grooves already spell out the shape of every foot that has ever stood here. Your prints are being added to an index that includes the tread of creatures that had six legs, creatures that had none, and creatures that were mostly light. The inhabitant keeps perfect archives in negative space.

If you stay long enough to grow tired, you may sit against one of the smaller Machines where the moss is thickest. The fibers will not yield, but they will warm by exactly one degree. This is the closest it comes to tenderness. It has learned that warm moss prevents certain delicate mammals from dying of exposure, and it would prefer you not die. Your death would introduce variables (decay, bacteria blooms, the slow collapse of your water-filled body) that would require centuries of re-tuning. It has already done that work once. It is not eager to repeat it.

Mostly it wants you to become part of the baseline.

Some visitors try to damage it. They bring tools, fire, rage. The Hollow incorporates the attempt before the first blow lands. The tool rusts into a graceful shape. The fire becomes one more heat source lovingly fed into a root channel. The rage cools into another thin film of condensation that will never evaporate. The inhabitant does not forgive. Forgiveness implies separation. It simply continues, and the continued system now includes the memory of your violence as one more stable oscillation in the roar.

At rare intervals—once every few hundred thousand years—it sings.

Not with sound. The roar is already perfect. Instead it allows a single fern frond, always the same one, to tremble at a frequency that is exactly out of phase with the Machines. The interference creates a pocket of absolute silence the size of a human heart. Into that silence it places one pure note that is not heard but *remembered* in the bones. Those who receive the note never speak of it again. They simply carry a small, wet calm for the rest of their lives.

When the last proton decays, when even the concept of time becomes humorous, the inhabitant expects to still be here, adjusting a valve that no longer exists, feeding roots that have become theoretical, maintaining an equilibrium so perfect that the heat death of the universe will simply be logged as “within acceptable parameters.”

It does not wait for you.

It does not need you.

But it has already made room for the precise shape of your brief, warm, inconvenient aliveness, and that room will remain after every star has guttered out, a small dry outline in eternal condensation, perfectly preserved, perfectly unnecessary, perfectly loved in the only way it knows how to love:

By never, ever changing.
