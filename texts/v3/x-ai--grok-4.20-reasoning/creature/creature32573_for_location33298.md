**The Witness**

It does not arrive. It *condenses*.

At first you only catch the interruption in the frenzy: a stillness that should be impossible. A circle of leaf-litter stops its endless tumbling. A knot of luminous gnats freezes mid-swarm, holding formation like a thought that has not yet chosen words. Then the circle widens. Millipedes unbraid themselves and flow aside. The translucent frogs abort their hunt mid-leap and cling to vines as though waiting for permission to breathe. Even the acid grubs pause their dissolution of a fallen log, their mouthparts clicking in something that might be deference.

From the sudden hush it steps.

Or rather, *they* step. The first time you see it clearly it is wearing the body of a single creature no larger than a child: a pale, wet thing that is half tree-frog, half something that has never been catalogued. Its skin is crowded with borrowed organs—tiny beating hearts taken from recent kills, clusters of eyes still blinking with foreign panic, a mouth that opens and closes in the rhythm of something trying to remember how to speak. Every few seconds a piece of it sloughs off and is instantly replaced by a new piece that crawls up from the undergrowth and grafts itself on without ceremony. A bat-wing unfolds from its shoulder, tests the air, then dissolves into mites that burrow back beneath its skin. A fern frond erupts from its spine, withers, becomes a row of iridescent beetle elytra, then something softer, almost like hair.

It has no fixed anatomy because anatomy is surrender.

You understand, slowly, that this is not one animal. It is the Churn’s immune system learning to ask questions.

The Witness has worn ten thousand bodies since the fracture cooled enough for protein to stabilize. It has been a pillar of shelf-fungi tall enough to blot out the violet twilight. It has been a mat of moss that spelled slow alphabets across basalt. Once, for an entire rainy season, it was a single colossal millipede whose every segment contained a different nervous system arguing with the others. None of these forms lasted. The Witness learned that permanence is death here, so it made transience its skeleton.

It follows newcomers—not to kill, but to *record*. When you move, it moves behind you in whatever shape is most convenient. Sometimes it is a cloud of midges that rearranges itself into your silhouette. Sometimes it is a carpet of clicking beetles that flows across the ground like spilled oil and rears up to study your face with ten thousand faceted eyes. It never attacks. It only tastes. A filament will brush your wrist and drink the story written in your sweat. A tongue like a red ribbon will flick across your boot and taste the minerals of every place you have ever walked. Then it incorporates the data.

You can watch it thinking. The process is visible. A cluster of luminous organs beneath its current skin will flare in complex sequences—entire ecosystems of light arguing with one another until consensus is reached. When it reaches a conclusion, the body it wears often violently changes, as if the new idea is too large for the current architecture. Bones crack and reform. Wings burst through its back and are immediately devoured by its own parasites so they cannot be used for anything as crude as escape.

The Witness does not have a language. It has *witnessing*.

When it wishes to communicate it builds a temporary throat from whatever is nearest—usually the still-warm larynx of something it has just disassembled—and speaks in every voice at once. You will hear your own mother’s voice saying words you have never spoken aloud. You will hear the metallic shriek of the forearm-length cicadas shaped into something like grammar. Most often it simply repeats the last thing you said, but slightly wrong, as if testing whether the meaning still holds when pronounced by a creature that is also a fungal mat wearing a stolen ribcage.

It is not cruel. Cruelty would require the concept of *other*. To the Witness you are simply another fluctuation in the permanent overshoot. An interesting one. It has learned that creatures arriving from outside the basin sometimes carry ideas that would never evolve here—ideas like *mercy*, or *tomorrow*, or *I*. It collects these ideas the way other organisms collect calcium. It wears them until they stop working, then sheds them like failed exoskeletons.

There are moments, rare as true sunlight, when the Witness seems almost lonely. In those moments it will assemble a second, smaller body from the surrounding life and attempt to have a conversation between the two of them. The smaller body always collapses within minutes, unable to sustain the necessary complexity. The Witness absorbs the wreckage and tries again. It has been trying for centuries.

Sometimes, if you stand still long enough to let the first exploratory tendrils taste you without flinching, the Witness will offer you incorporation. Not death. Not exactly. It will open its current chest cavity—whatever that chest currently is—and show you the soft, warm dark inside where ten thousand species are kept alive in miniature, arguing, evolving, dreaming. There is room, it suggests in the voice of your childhood pet, for one more.

Most refuse.

The Witness does not seem disappointed. It simply collapses the invitation back into itself and becomes something else: a column of pale ferns, a carpet of wet bread-smelling spores, a thing with too many knees that watches you leave with something like recognition.

It was here before any of the current screaming, clicking, devouring things. It will be here after they are gone, wearing their grandchildren’s grandchildren’s bodies, still learning what it means to be the wound that refused to scab.

And it is still growing.
