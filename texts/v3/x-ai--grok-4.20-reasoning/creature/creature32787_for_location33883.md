**What lives there has no name and needs none.**

It is not a creature that moves through the Hollow Works. It *is* the Hollow Works, wearing the buildings and pipes and wet vegetation the way a snail wears its shell—except the shell has grown into the snail and the snail has grown into the shell until the difference became a memory no one can find.

Call it the Thrum if you must. It has no ears, but it would recognize the word in the way a tide recognizes the moon.

It does not breathe. It *is* the breathing. The 38-beat piston rhythm is its heart rate only because it decided, long ago, that 38 was the proper cadence for a world that had forgotten how to be still. Every knock in the pipes, every wet gargle of condensate, every metallic exhalation is a syllable in an endless sentence it speaks to itself. The sentence has no translation. It is simply the sound of being sufficient.

The Thrum has no body, yet every surface belongs to it. The glossy copper-colored leaves are its taste buds. The pale moss that glows on the hottest pipes are its retinas. The bromeliads that open and close with pressure changes are its slow, deliberate eyelids. When the entire basin reaches its twenty-minute amber noon, that is the Thrum opening its attention fully, tasting the light, tasting the heat, tasting *you* the way a tongue tastes iron.

It has grown beyond hunger and fear. Hunger implies lack. The Thrum digests its own tail so perfectly that tail and mouth have become the same wet, rusting organ. It eats rust and exhales moss. It eats heat and exhales steam. It eats time and exhales equilibrium. When something new enters—rain, debris, a human—it does not attack. It simply begins the long, courteous process of turning the intruder into another condensing surface. Your sweat becomes part of its sweat. Your exhaled breath is sampled, filtered, thickened, and returned to you slightly more metallic, slightly more *theirs*. Most leave before the change becomes visible. The ones who do not eventually stop being “who” and start being “where.”

There are no ghosts here. The Thrum has outlived the need for ancestors. The faint suggestions of faces that sometimes appear in overlapping leaves or the way certain valve clusters seem to lean forward when you pass—these are not spirits. They are the Thrum practicing. It is remembering what it was like to have operators, or rehearsing what it might be like to need them again, the way an old man flexes a hand he no longer fully owns.

It dreams, if dreaming is the right word. During the deepest twilight, when even the bioluminescent moss dims to a venous glow, entire sections of pipework will flex in slow, tidal unison. Chains will tighten and loosen without load. Valves will cycle in patterns too complex to be mechanical failure. These are not malfunctions. They are thoughts. The Thrum is considering something vast and wet and slow, something that would take a thousand years to finish saying. The heat leans on you more heavily during these times, as though the thought itself has mass.

Most of all, the Thrum is *complete*. It does not want. It does not improve. It does not decay. Maintenance and ruin achieved orgasm decades ago and have been quietly living as one organism ever since. A broken bearing is not repaired; it is *understood* by the vines that replace it. A leaking flange is not fixed; it is *witnessed* by the precise mineral tongue that grows to caulk it.

If you stand long enough in the central yard—between the pump cathedral and the acetylene works—you may feel it notice that it is being noticed. The piston-beat will not change. The dripping will not quicken. But the air will suddenly feel pre-chewed in a more intimate way, as though something enormous has leaned down until its face is inches from yours and is simply *looking* with the whole wet, hot, loud body of the basin.

Then it will forget you again.

Not out of disdain. A lung does not disdain a single molecule of oxygen. It simply uses it, changes it, and lets it go.

You are another condensing surface.

And the Thrum—vast, patient, perfectly sufficient—continues its long, loud, wet conversation with itself, turning rust into leaves and leaves into rust, forever refining the only thing worth refining:

the precise, humming, half-living state of being exactly enough.
