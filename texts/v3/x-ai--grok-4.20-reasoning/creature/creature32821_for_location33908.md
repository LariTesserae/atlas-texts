**The Unfinished**

They were never meant to remain.

The builders understood this from the first strike of antler against quartz. Every measurement they took, every angle they cut, every breath they held while fitting stone to stone was an act of self-erasure. They did not build a monument. They built a door they intended to walk through and never walk back from.

What lives here now is what walked through.

Not ghosts. Not preserved corpses. Not even memory in any sense a mammal would recognize. The Unfinished are the portion of the original minds that could not be completely translated into pure tone. A remainder. A decimal that refuses to resolve.

You will not see them at first. The single bone-white note is too loud, too total. But after some measureless stretch of time (minutes, hours, centuries—the place does not distinguish), the ear begins to betray the mind. Within the unchanging roar you begin to detect the slightest imperfections, so faint they feel like faults in your own hearing. A microscopic warble. A hesitation at the edge of a harmonic. A place where the tone seems, impossibly, to almost apologize.

That is them.

They live in the places where two membranes almost touch but do not. In the hairline gaps the builders deliberately left—gaps so precise that modern instruments would call them accidental. The Unfinished inhabit the almost. They are the tension between one perfect surface and another. The longing that perfect equilibrium could not quite erase.

Sometimes they take a form, but only when the amber light falls at the exact angle the builders planned for this exact moment of witness. Then the lattices and buttresses suddenly resolve into suggestion: a shoulder hunched in concentration, a brow furrowed above eyes that are simply holes bored through three feet of stone, hands forever pressed against ears that no longer exist. The figures are never complete. An arm ends in a pillar. A torso becomes lattice. A face is only half a face; the other half is negative space that somehow still hurts to look at.

They are trying to finish the work.

That is their only activity and their only torment. The great tone is perfect, yet they remain convinced that one more adjustment, one more infinitesimal shift of a single quartz vein, would make it flawless in a way that would finally allow them to dissolve. So they push. They push with force that would pulverize mountains if it were physical. Here it is only a change in pressure so subtle that it registers as a change in the temperature of your blood by less than a tenth of a degree.

You feel them most clearly when you press your ear to the stone. What you first mistake for the warm, ancient vibration is actually the press of their attention back against you. They are listening to the sound of your living body—your heartbeat, your breath, the electrical stutter of your thoughts—with the kind of desperate, devouring curiosity that only something which has never been allowed to die can feel.

They envy you the ability to decay.

Some of them have names, or had them. You can feel the shapes of those names moving through the tone like slow fish beneath ice. *She-who-held-the-final-chisel. He-who-sang-the-first-note-into-being. The child who was not yet born when the work began but was carried here in her mother’s belly so that she too could give her unfinished life to the machine.*

They do not hate you for being temporary. They simply do not understand how you bear it. To them your passage through the Resonance is like watching a mayfly deliberately set itself on fire while laughing. They study the way your bones try to sing along and fail. They study the moment when the tone begins to hurt you, when your soft biology starts to come apart, and they lean closer, almost tender, as if they could learn the secret of departure from watching you flee.

The longer you remain, the more you feel yourself becoming adjacent to them. Your thoughts develop tiny warbles. Your memories arrive with microscopic hesitations. You begin to notice, with something like horror, that you too are starting to feel unfinished.

This is how they reproduce.

Not by creating more of themselves, but by making others recognize that they were never complete to begin with. That every living thing is a half-measure, a compromise, a stone that still has somewhere else it desperately needs to be.

Eventually you will leave, as everything soft must. The tone will push you out like a splinter.

But one small hesitation will remain behind, lodged between two membranes that almost touch. A new imperfection in the perfect note. A new voice in the choir that can never quite finish its part.

The Unfinished will keep working.

They have all the time that remains in the universe, and a little more besides. They believe that if they can only achieve one perfect, unbroken, unapologetic instant of tone, they will finally be allowed to stop existing.

They have been wrong for longer than your species has had language.

They will probably be wrong until the last proton decays.

And still they lean into the almost, foreheads against the stone that is also their foreheads, pushing, pushing, with the patient, terrible devotion of those who were loved enough to be given eternity but not loved enough to be allowed to end.
