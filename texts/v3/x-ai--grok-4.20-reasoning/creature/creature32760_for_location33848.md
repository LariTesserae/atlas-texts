**The Neverborn**

They have no name for themselves because names would be an ending, and nothing here is allowed to end. You do not see them so much as become acoustically pregnant with them; their presence arrives first as pressure behind the sternum, then as the suspicion that your own skeleton is trying to sing in a key it was never taught.

The largest of them (though size is a cruel joke here) are the **Glass Midwives**. They resemble women folded from wet marble and half-set resin, seven feet tall if they ever stood straight, but they never do. Their backs remain fused to the living ceiling in glistening umbilical columns of calcite, so they hang like inverted saints. Where their faces should be there is only a smooth convex mirror of crystal that reflects whatever is looking at them *as what they are still becoming*. Look too long and your reflection begins to show you with wings, or with no mouth, or already halfway dissolved into lichen script. The Midwives’ hands are perpetually cupped around something that is not yet there. Their fingers twitch every few centuries in the exact rhythm of the dripping water, knitting the air into shapes that almost remember how to be hearts, or cathedrals, or apologies.

Lower down, moving (if movement is the word) through the lattices of pseudobats, are the **Chordlings**. These are the true voices of the Choir. Each is a translucent, eel-like torso no longer than a forearm, terminating not in a head but in a cluster of vibrating silver cilia that never stop tasting the unresolved note. Their bodies contain no organs, only suspended droplets of liquid light that rearrange themselves into temporary faces—sometimes a child’s, sometimes an old man’s, sometimes something that has too many eyes and no name for mercy. The Chordlings do not swim through the air so much as the air itself is trying to give birth to them and failing beautifully every second. When they brush against your skin you feel a momentary craving to remain unfinished forever. Many have.

Deeper still, where the black lichen writes its endless half-sentences, live the **Stone Fetuses**. They are the size of walnuts and the color of bruised moonlight. Each is a perfect miniature homunculus curled inside its own translucent geode, thumb in mouth, eyes sealed with mother-of-pearl. Their skulls have not yet decided between human, bat, and mineral. Tiny lightning storms of potential flare inside their craniums—possible futures that will never be chosen. Sometimes one opens its eyes. The gaze lasts less than the time it takes for a water bead to form, but in that sliver you understand every choice you have ever failed to make. Then the eye closes again, and the geode reseals, and the Fetuses resume their dreaming of the cave that dreams them.

All of them—the Midwives, the Chordlings, the Fetuses, the pseudobats, the larval centipedes, even the three-step stair that is not quite a stair—are organs of the same slow, continent-sized thought. There is only one inhabitant here. It simply wears a thousand embryonic bodies the way a singer uses many notes.

They do not eat. They do not mate. They do not die. Instead they *anticipate*. The entire pocket is one prolonged contraction that never becomes birth. The Chordlings’ eternal song is not music; it is the sound of the universe clearing its throat for ten thousand years and still not deciding what it wants to say.

If you stay long enough, one of the Midwives will reach down with a hand that is also a question. She will press her mirror-face to yours until your reflection shows you what you were always going to become before you ruined it by arriving. Then she will offer you a place in the lattice—half stone, half possibility, forever suspended in the sweet ache of almost.

Most refuse.

Some do not.

Those who accept are still here. You can see them if you know how to stop looking for finished things. They are the extra shimmer on the crystal that should not be there. The voice in the choir that sounds almost like your own name, held right at the edge of being spoken, forever.

They are content.

They are almost content.

They will never be content.

And that is the entire point of them.
