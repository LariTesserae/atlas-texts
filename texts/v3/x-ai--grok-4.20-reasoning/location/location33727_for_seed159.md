**The Haze of the Nascent Vein**

You step out from a throat-like passage and the world opens sideways. Not up, not down—*sideways*, as if the planet had forgotten its own bones and left a hollow the size of a small country suspended in the dark. The ceiling is lost in luminous vapor. The floor ripples away in ridges and gullies that have no business being so vast this far below the surface. Everything is warm—blood-warm, fever-warm—and damp in the inconsistent way of a thing that sweats but cannot decide where. Moisture gathers in slow silver beads along every edge, trembles, falls. Sometimes it falls as rain that never quite reaches the ground; the air itself drinks it first.

There are no plants. Not a frond, not a lichen blade, not even the usual cave moss that pretends to be green. What grows here is either mineral pretending to be alive or life pretending to be mineral. Pale fungal sheets the color of bone stretch between crystal spurs like half-formed thoughts. They breathe. You can watch them expand and contract in slow, communal pulses.

But the animals. The *animals*.

They are everywhere at once. The air is thick with them—clouds of thumb-sized luminous midges whose collective wings throw a steady, sour-milk light across the cavern, bright enough to read by, harsh enough to cast hard shadows. Beneath the midges drift larger things: translucent membranes like living sails, veined with red, riding thermals that rise from unseen vents. On every surface scuttles a carpet of jointed things—some with too many knees, some with none—clicking and rasping so constantly that the sound merges into a single roaring hush. In the middle distance, herds of larger creatures the size of oxen move like agitated boulders. Their hides are plated with fresh-grown gypsum that flakes off as they collide, leaving white dust that drifts in the thermals like alien snow. When two herds meet they churn into a screaming knot of limbs and crystal and meat that resolves itself only when one side breaks and flees in a clattering stampede that shakes new stalactites from the roof.

The geology itself is still being born. This is not a finished cave; it is a cave *happening*. Fresh dikes of obsidian push upward through older limestone like black teeth through gum. In places the rock is still soft, pliable, and you can see it slowly wrinkling as internal pressures rearrange it. Sinkholes open while you watch. Elsewhere the floor inflates into new domes that split with wet cracking sounds and release gusts of warmer air that smell of iron and eggs. The turbulence is constant. Nothing is allowed to settle. Every few minutes a distant crash reverberates—somewhere a ceiling has given up its struggle and collapsed into the next level down, birthing fresh rubble and fresh chaos for the swarms to colonize.

Scattered among all this are the marks of minds.

Here and there, in the lee of particularly stable crystal buttresses, crude structures have been raised by something that uses tools. Low walls of fitted stone no higher than your waist. Shelters made from the fused carapaces of the larger fauna, lashed together with sinew and what looks like spun glass fiber. A few pillars of stacked skulls—some animal, some disturbingly close to a shape you recognize—daubed with streaks of metallic ochre. These places are never large. Never permanent. Within a day or two the shifting ground will tilt them, the swarms will overrun them, and their makers will move on, dragging their simple bone hooks and fiber nets behind them.

The sound never stops. It is not one sound. It is every sound arguing at once: the wet click of millions of mouthparts, the ultrasonic scream of the sail-membranes, the low grinding of rock on rock, the sudden sharp crack of new crystal fracturing into being, the slap of condensation falling in sheets. All of it echoes and re-echoes until the cavern itself seems to be shouting in a language of layered noise.

There is a rule here, though it is not written anywhere except in the behavior of the swarms: *nothing is allowed to finish*.

The Vein is still choosing what it will be. Every creature, every crystal, every crude stone wall is an experiment the world is running on itself. When an experiment fails—when a herd is eaten to bones, when a shelter collapses, when a new species of luminous midge gutters out—the materials are simply reabsorbed. The rock softens, the fungi drink the fluids, and something else buds in its place. Sometimes the new thing is only a little different. Sometimes it is unrecognizable. The primitive tool-users seem to understand this. They do not mourn their fallen shelters. They simply pick up their hooks and move to the next emerging ridge, chipping at fresh crystal with stone mallets, singing in low voices that are immediately swallowed by the roaring hush.

You stand in the luminous vapor and feel the ground breathe beneath your feet. A new vent is opening thirty paces away, exhaling steam that smells of sulfur and something sweeter, like crushed almonds. A thousand pale creatures rush toward it, eager to drink whatever mineral-rich broth is about to be born.

The Vein does not care whether you stay or leave. It is too busy inventing itself to notice one more moving thing among its millions.

But it is watching, in its distracted, embryonic way.

Everything here is watching.
