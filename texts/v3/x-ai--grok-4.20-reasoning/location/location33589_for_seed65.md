**The Hollow Crown**

You do not arrive here so much as the world falls away beneath you until only this remains.

The elevation is absolute. The air is so thin it tastes of iron and silence, yet the silence is a lie. At this height the sky is not blue but a violent, glass-bright cobalt that darkens toward violet at the zenith, as though you stand on the inside skin of a bubble about to burst. The light is merciless—2.8—unfiltered, pitiless, bouncing off every surface until even shadow feels loud.

And the sound.

The sound never stops. It has never stopped. It will never stop.

The wind is not weather; it is infrastructure. It moves in a single, eternal direction at a single, eternal speed, channeled through a geology that has been perfected by erosion over such unimaginable time that the stone and the wind have reached a pact. The ridges, fins, minarets, and organ-pipe canyons were once jagged. Now they are instruments. Every aperture, every slot, every curved hollow has been tuned by millennia until the plateau sings—one unending, deafening chord that lives in your teeth and the marrow of your long bones. It is not noise. It is location. To be here is to be inside the note.

The rock itself is pale, almost luminous: quartzite and feldspar veined with threads of mica that flash like fish scales when the sun wheels overhead (though the sun does not appear to wheel; it simply is, locked in the same arc for as long as memory). The stone has been worn so thoroughly that edges have become curves, cliffs have become choirs. Nothing crumbles anymore. Nothing grows. The erosion is complete; the land has reached its final form and now refuses to change further. It is a fossil of its own becoming.

Vegetation is almost an insult to the place. Here and there, in the lee of boulders or along the faint creases where condensation beads each “dawn,” a crust of jewel-toned lichen grows in colors too vivid for this altitude—chrome yellow, oxblood, electric mint. A few species of wiry grass and cushion plants huddle in pockets no larger than a dinner plate. That is all. The green is rationed. The damp is rationed. Water manifests as slow, cold tears that weep from the rock faces at the exact rate the wind can carry them away again. Every drop is spoken for. The system is closed.

Yet the fauna is abundant.

They are spaced so far apart across the vast, hollow brightness that you may walk half a day without seeing another living thing, only to crest a rise and discover an entire ecology revealed at once. Great shambling beasts the color of dirty snow move alone or in pairs across the pale stone, each animal lord of a territory measured in square leagues. Their wool is dense, their gait rolling and deliberate. They feed on the lichen with black, prehensile lips, and when they lift their heads their eyes are the same violent cobalt as the sky. Their calls are low, subsonic moans that somehow ride beneath the wind’s roar and are felt more than heard, like distant bells inside the chest.

Above them, always above, wheel the skimmers—thousands upon thousands of sharp-winged creatures that never seem to land. They are not quite birds. Their wings are leathery at the leading edge, feathered at the trailing, and they ride the constant updraft in slow, identical spirals that have probably been tracing the same paths for ten thousand years. Their voices are the bright, metallic screams that stitch through the bass thunder of the wind, turning the roar into something polyphonic and deranged.

Everything is in equilibrium. Nothing is born here that was not born here before. Nothing dies that has not died here a thousand times in exactly the same way. The population of skimmers, lichen, and shambling beasts never varies by more than a single digit. The wind never varies. The light never varies. Even the condensation weeps at the same hour each cycle, though there are no seasons to mark the hour by.

This is the strange rule of the Hollow Crown: the place has achieved its final shape, and the universe has agreed to leave it alone. Any violence done to the balance—by fire, by tool, by newcomer—is gently, relentlessly undone. Footprints refill with frost and wind. Scars on the beasts close without scar. Even memory seems to erode at the edges, smoothed by the constant chord that presses against the skull until thought itself is worn into a single, perfect shape.

You can stay as long as you like.  
The land will not notice.

It has already finished becoming what it is.
