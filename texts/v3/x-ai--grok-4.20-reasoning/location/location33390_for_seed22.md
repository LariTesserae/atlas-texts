**The Fracture of Veyr**

The land has not yet learned how to be old.

It rose only yesterday by planetary reckoning—thrust up by some vast, half-forgotten engine that still grumbles beneath the crust. The result is a low, open country of impossible sharpness: knife-edged ridges of fresh obsidian, canyon walls like broken glass, basalt columns that rise in perfect geometric clusters where no human hand directed them. Erosion has not had time to round anything. Every surface still carries the memory of its birth.

Yet the air is warm, heavy, and damp. Moisture does not arrive as honest rain but as a conspiracy: sudden localized cloudbursts that last seven minutes and vanish, condensation that beads on every edge and runs in silver threads, ground fog that pools in the lee of new cliffs and breathes like a living thing. Pockets of water appear and disappear—mirror-black pools that reflect not quite what stands above them. The humidity itself feels engineered, as though the atmosphere were being slowly marinated.

Across this raw young expanse the vegetation has answered with obscene enthusiasm. It does not carpet the land. That would be too simple. Instead, colossal **megadendrons** stand kilometers apart, each one a vertical continent. Their trunks are wider than cathedrals, spiraling upward in braided lattices of wood, crystal, and something that behaves like muscle. Between them lie vast, echoing empties—hollow volumes where the eye finds almost nothing but distance. The contrast is hallucinatory: lush green hells the size of cities, separated by near-voids of warm, dim air.

The light is the color of tarnished bronze. It arrives bruised and reluctant, filtered through perpetual spore-haze and the upper canopies of the megadendrons, which glow faintly with their own photosynthetic engines. Shadows pool like ink. Everything feels one hour before nightfall, forever.

And the sound—God, the sound.

It never drops below the level of a jet engine at takeoff. The fauna is abundant in the way storms are abundant. Vast herds of **resonant titans**—creatures like giraffes crossed with pipe organs—gallop between the green islands. Their bodies are partly biological, partly acoustic architecture; ribs flared into parabolic reflectors, throats tuned to specific resonant frequencies. When they call, the sound is not merely loud but *structural*. It can knock loose fresh shards from the young cliffs. Smaller swarms of **fractal screamers** wheel overhead in living Mandelbrot patterns, emitting layered shrieks that interfere and reinforce until the air itself seems to tear. The megadendrons have learned to join in. Their hollow interiors function as organ pipes. Wind, breath, and deliberate muscular contraction turn entire forests into choirs that answer the herds in deafening antiphony.

This is not noise. It is infrastructure.

The cities are sparse enough to match the land’s terrifying emptiness. You may travel three days between them and see nothing but raw geology and the occasional green tower on the horizon. Then, without warning, the ground becomes *patterned*. Perfect hexagonal plates slide beneath the soil. Towers of programmable matter rise in silence, their surfaces cycling between states—liquid mirror, black glass, living bone. These are not human cities in any classical sense. They are **Veyr Nodes**: self-aware urban organisms built at the scale of the landscape itself. Some of them migrate slowly across the young terrain on fields of modified Casimir energy, leaving behind fresh canyons as they pass. Their architecture defies several laws that physics still claims to enforce. Walkways fold through higher dimensions. Rooms exist in temporal parallel. Entire districts phase in and out of perceptibility, syncing their presence to the deafening calls of the titans outside.

The ecology is active, relentless, and deeply strange.

Predator and prey here do not simply chase each other; they *negotiate* in frequencies below and above human hearing. Certain screamers deliberately trigger controlled avalanches of megadendron seeds so that new green islands will grow exactly where the herds need them in six weeks’ time. The Nodes release clouds of nanites that drift on the damp wind like glittering pollen; some fauna have evolved to breathe them, incorporating the machines into their own resonant skeletons. Time itself behaves poorly in the shadow of the largest megadendrons—minutes stretch or compress depending on the harmonic state of the forest.

The rules of this world appear to be:

1. Nothing may remain finished.
2. Emptiness and excess must coexist at every scale.
3. Sound is a form of architecture.
4. The land is still deciding what it wants to become, and it is being argued into existence by creatures that can shatter stone with their voices.

Stand long enough in one of the great hollows between green towers and you begin to feel the argument in your bones. The warm, damp wind moves across fresh glass cliffs. A distant Node sings back to a herd of titans in frequencies that make your vision blur. Condensation gathers on your skin and runs upward for a moment before remembering gravity.

This place is not post-apocalyptic. It is post-*genesis*. The world has just been born screaming, and the scream is still going.

It probably always will be.
