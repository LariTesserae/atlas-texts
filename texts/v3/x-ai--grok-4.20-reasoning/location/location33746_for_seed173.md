**The Hollow Engine**

You are inside a single, cathedral-sized chamber entirely underwater, a self-contained industrial relic that has run so long it has become geology. There is no natural substrate anywhere. Every surface — floor, walls, vaulted ceiling forty meters overhead — is fabricated: plates of an alloy whose name is long forgotten, massive riveted girders, concrete cured under pressure, banks of silent transformers the size of cottages. All of it has been worn by three kilometers of cold seawater for so many thousands of years that edges have become rounded, seams have grown soft, and the metal itself has taken on the color of old bone and verdigris. The erosion has reached its final, stable form. Nothing is still crumbling; the structure has settled into the shape it will keep until the planet itself ends.

The water is cool, almost chilly, and completely still in the wide central volume while rushing with theatrical violence through a lattice of pipes and channels that pierce the walls like organ pipes. Sound is everywhere and overwhelming: a deep, bone-shaking industrial chord. Turbines the size of houses turn in their housings with a constant metallic moan. Every few minutes a pressure valve somewhere opens with a sound like a howitzer firing underwater, sending a rolling shockwave that rattles every loose fitting for thirty seconds. Bubbles roar upward in columns. Distant pistons clang in slow, patient rhythm — one every seven heartbeats — as though the place is still trying to pump something that no longer exists. The noise is so loud that fish use it as topography; they orient by vibration the way surface birds use the sun.

Light is dim, bluish, and sourceless. A few emergency strips still glow behind thick glass, throwing weak fans of illumination that die after ten meters. Most of the light actually comes from the creatures themselves. The fauna is abundant, almost crowded in its own sparse way. Vast shoals of silver hatchetfish drift through the open water like living constellations, their photophores blinking in slow code. Arm-thick eels the color of motor oil hunt along the girders, bodies rippling in perfect silence while the machinery screams around them. Clouds of shrimp click and flare in the up-currents. On every surface, tube worms and barnacles have built dense crusts that look like rust from a distance; they are the only “vegetation,” sparse and functional. No kelp, no meadows. Just these patient encrustations turning the industrial cathedral into a reef of iron and meat.

The scale is intimate despite the height: you can always see the far wall, always know you are inside one structure. Catwalks and gantries cross the space at irregular heights, their railings now softened into smooth curves by erosion. Between them is mostly emptiness — water, sound, and fish. Density is low. The machines are enormous but widely spaced, like the columns of a drowned basilica. You can swim for long minutes without touching anything except drifting curtains of shrimp.

This is not a ruin that has gone quiet. It is still active, still industrial, only its purpose has been forgotten even by itself. Pumps cycle. Heat exchangers create ghostly thermal columns where warm water breathes upward and cold water sinks. Every forty minutes a new sequence seems to begin: valves open in series, turbines spin faster, the chord of sound shifts half a tone, and the fish respond instantly, rearranging their schools as though following sheet music only they can read. Predator and prey move through these changing acoustic corridors in ancient, perfect choreography.

There are no windows. There never were. The only way in or out is through a series of flooded service tunnels whose hatches have long since corroded open. The entire complex is a closed loop, a machine that has become its own ecosystem, loud, cold, dim, and relentlessly, mechanically alive. It does not need the surface. It does not remember the sun. It has achieved its oldest stable state: an engine that no longer makes power, only rhythm, and in that rhythm an entire world of blind, luminous creatures has learned to live without question.
