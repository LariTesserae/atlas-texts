**The Hollow Node**

You slip through a fracture no wider than a coffin. The rock is fresh-broken, edges still sharp enough to draw blood, as though the earth split open only days ago. Water beads on every surface—not flowing, simply *present*, a constant cool sweat that gathers, trembles, and drops in fat, slow pearls. The air is warm, thick, almost soupy. Each breath tastes of green rot and nectar.

Then the passage widens and the world forgets how caves are supposed to behave.

The chamber that opens around you is the size of a small theater, yet it feels infinite. The distance between one surface and another is elastic; stare too long and the far wall drifts away like a ship leaving harbor. This is the first fracture in reality: scale itself is negotiable. The pocket is both intimate and hollow, a single living room carved inside a root system the size of a mountain range that has never seen the sun.

There is no stone here anymore. Every surface—floor, walls, ceiling—is a single continuous organism. Not moss on rock. Not vines on soil. The substrate *is* plant. A dense, felted mat of microscopic root hairs, fungal filaments, and leaf primordia forms a breathing skin that covers every contour. From this living felt rise the macro-structures: colossal, pale shoots thick as redwoods, but fresh, raw, *young*. Their bark is still soft, translucent, veined with luminous sap that glows the color of new limes. These pillars do not merely stand; they pulse. You can watch them thicken while you breathe.

The light is wrong.

It is full, ordinary daylight—warm, slightly golden, the light of mid-morning in a meadow—yet it has no source. It simply exists inside the chamber, as though the concept of “day” leaked through a tear in physics and decided to stay. Where the light touches the living walls, colors bloom in reckless overdose: violets that hurt to look at, greens so saturated they leave afterimages like welder’s burns. Some leaves are transparent and act as lenses, focusing the sourceless light into moving blades that crawl across the floor.

Sound is constant and deafening.

The Node never stops shouting. A deep, wet creaking like wooden ships tearing themselves apart rolls through the air as the great shoots force themselves larger. Every few minutes a new lateral branch erupts from a pillar with a sound like a gunshot wrapped in wet meat. Sap geysers, then immediately coagulates into glistening amber ropes that hang and sway. Beneath that industrial roar is a second layer: the vegetation *sings*. A chorus of membrane-thin leaves vibrates in complex chords, producing something between whale song and temple bells. The echoes never die. They multiply. Sometimes they answer questions you have not asked yet.

Water behaves strangely. Condensation gathers on the ceiling-leaves until the drops grow too heavy. Instead of falling straight, many drift sideways or orbit tiny eddies of air before smashing against a pillar in explosive, fragrant bursts. These miniature rains happen in cycles, like slow lungs. After each one the air smells sharper, sweeter, almost alcoholic.

The ecology is almost purely vegetal. Fauna is vanishingly rare—only the occasional translucent millipede the length of your forearm, its every segment a tiny independent leaf, or a cloud of white spores that moves with intention, like a school of fish. Everything else is plant, yet the plants have learned predation. One species grows hooked tendrils that wait motionless for hours, then lash out to impale slower neighbors. The impaled plant does not die; it is *assimilated*. Its tissues soften, its colors bleed into the victor, and within a day the two have become a single organism wearing both patterns. Evolution here is loud, fast, and visible.

Reality itself is brittle.

Walk ten paces toward what looks like a distant flower the size of a house and you arrive instantly, as if space folded. Look away, then back, and the flower has moved behind you. Time is measured in growth spurts rather than seconds; if you sit still long enough you can feel your own hair growing in sympathy with the Node. Shadows sometimes remain after the light has moved on. One shadow in particular—a perfect silhouette of a human figure—stands in the exact center of the chamber and never aligns with any living thing. It appears to be breathing.

There are rules, though they feel more like habits the Node has not yet broken:

- Nothing manufactured survives. Tools rot into mulch within minutes. Metal turns brown, softens, and puts out rootlets.
- Sound is currency. Shout and the nearest pillar will bloom. Whisper and the leaves turn toward you like ears.
- If you bleed, the living floor drinks. Where your blood lands, a spiral of crimson-veined orchids erupts within seconds. They smell like your childhood.
- The Node is still forming. Every hour another fracture opens somewhere in the living walls and a new pocket bleeds into existence. Some of these pockets are only big enough for an eye. Some are big enough to swallow cities. All of them are still warm from creation.

This is not a cave.  
It is a seed that has already germinated, cracked the crust of the world, and is now growing *inward*—a green, loud, impossible womb that has not yet decided what it wants to birth.

Stay long enough and you begin to feel the question forming in the back of your mind, asked not by you but *through* you, in the voice of vibrating leaves and bursting sap:

*Are you here to help it grow, or are you the next thing it intends to assimilate?*

The pillars creak. The sourceless daylight shifts. Another drop of water falls upward.
