**The Hollow of Weeping Veils**

You discover it by accident, or perhaps it discovers you. A seam in the world no wider than a doorway, hidden behind a curtain of cold mist that clings to stone and skin alike. Step through and the temperature drops further, settling at the edge of what breath can tolerate. This is not a valley or a forest. It is a single breathing room, a pocket no more than forty paces across, embedded like a cyst within colder, barer rock. The ceiling is lost in darkness only two or three man-heights above your head, yet that darkness feels vast because it moves.

Everything here is wet.

Water does not fall so much as *arrive*. It condenses on every surface in perfect, trembling beads that grow too heavy and slide downward in slow silver threads. The rock walls (what little true rock remains) weep steadily. The air itself weeps, releasing its moisture onto your eyelashes, the backs of your hands, the inside of your throat. There are no streams, only a perpetual damp migration: droplets sliding along fungal ropes, pooling in leaf-bowls, dripping from the undersides of enormous pale leaves that arch like cathedral buttresses. The sound of water is the loudest thing here, yet it is never loud; a constant soft ticking and kissing, thousands of tiny impacts muffled by living tissue.

The vegetation has won so completely that it *is* the substrate. At 2.8 it has crossed the threshold from “covering” to “being.” There is no soil visible. You walk on a thick, resilient mat of interwoven mosses, liverworts, and pale rhizomes that flex gently underfoot like the hide of an enormous animal. From this living floor rise nine colossal pillars spaced with deliberate, almost architectural sparseness. Each pillar is a single organism (or perhaps one organism wearing nine bodies). They resemble neither tree nor fungus nor kelp but something ancestral to all three: smooth, cartilaginous trunks the color of bone, faintly luminous with slow green veins. Their surfaces are etched with natural whorls and folds that primitive hands have deepened into steps, handholds, and narrow balconies. The “city” is not built against the plants. The city is the plants, lightly edited.

The settlement numbers perhaps thirty living structures. Some are woven baskets of living vine grown into tight spirals and then hollowed. Others are tumor-like swellings in the pillars themselves, carefully encouraged by bone chisels and obsidian flakes until they form chambers large enough for a family. Narrow rope bridges of braided root fiber span the open air between pillars. The open spaces between these structures feel enormous because the density is only 1.2; the eye travels long distances through cold, misty emptiness before meeting the next soft vertical mass. Every surface is alive. Even the walkways regenerate if cut. The entire pocket is one slow, tidal organism pretending to be architecture.

It is very dark.

Light is almost forbidden here (0.1). What little exists comes from within. The veins in the pillars pulse with a cold, bacteriologic glow, the faintest teal and violet. Clouds of minute glowing midges drift like living dust, moving in slow constellations that the inhabitants read the way sailors once read stars. Some of the larger millipedes carry symbiotic lantern glands along their backs; when they cluster on a wall they create trembling maps of soft gold. The darkness is not empty. It is full of slow movement and quiet breathing.

And the animals.

Fauna at 3.0 is not an exaggeration. They are overwhelming in biomass and intimacy, yet strangely courteous to the ear. The sound parameter remains mostly quiet because almost nothing here has learned to scream. Instead the air is full of soft clicking, papery wing-rustle, and the constant low susurration of thousands of tiny feet. Translucent pale frogs the size of thumbnails move across the moss in slow living carpets, their throats pulsing. Long, feather-like centipedes drift in the mist on silken parachutes. Blind white moths with wingspans wider than your hand rest on the pillars in such numbers that the bone-colored surfaces appear faintly furred. When one of the small, velvet-furred predators (something between a shrew and a sloth) moves along a branch, every surface it passes over ripples with fleeing life, then settles again behind it like water closing.

The ecology is young (erosion 0.7). This pocket tore itself open perhaps only decades ago, a wound in the larger cold rock that filled instantly with spores. The pillars are still thickening; you can watch them grow if you sit still long enough. Their expansion is audible as a low creak, like ship timbers in a slow sea. Cracks in the surrounding stone are still sharp, unweathered. New fungal shelves appear overnight, delicate as lace, then thicken by morning into usable shelves or benches for the inhabitants.

The inhabitants themselves are few, small, and primitive. They wear garments of pressed moss and insect silk. Their tools are bone, obsidian, and the living plant itself. They speak rarely. Their language seems half made of touch; they press palms to the pillars and appear to listen. Some carry small bone flutes that produce notes too low for human ears; when blown, the pillars answer with subtle shifts in luminescence. Whether this is communication or coincidence is one of the strangenesses (1.6) that never quite resolves.

Rules of the world, as far as they can be read:

- Nothing may be killed that does not first bite. The balance is too tight.
- All waste must be returned to the moss floor within one turning of the slow internal “day.” The plants are hungry.
- Light beyond the permitted bioluminescence is forbidden. The last outsider who tried to strike flint was gently but relentlessly overgrown by morning.
- The water that weeps from the ceiling is slightly saline and carries dissolved minerals from the rock above. Drinking it too freely produces vivid, identical dreams of being a root.

The whole place is active (dynamic 2.1). You can feel the slow pulse of growth, the quiet predator-prey dramas playing out in miniature across every square inch, the condensation cycle that rises and falls with the unseen thermal tides of the stone above. Every few hours a mist so thick it feels like milk rolls through the chamber, hiding even the nearest pillar, and when it recedes the distribution of glowing insects has changed according to laws no one has yet deciphered.

It is cold enough that your breath becomes part of the weeping.

It is quiet enough that you begin to hear your own blood.

And it is so thoroughly, impossibly alive that after a time you can no longer tell whether you are a visitor inside the Hollow of Weeping Veils, or merely another of its temporary parasites, allowed to remain for as long as you cause no lasting harm to the single enormous organism that dreamed this pocket into being and is still dreaming it, slowly, gently, in the dark.
