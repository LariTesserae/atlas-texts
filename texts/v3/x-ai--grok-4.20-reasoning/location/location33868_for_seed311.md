**The Pocket** is a single, self-contained chamber no larger than a generous ballroom, yet it is a city. It sits embedded like a cyst within the young, black-glass cliffs of a newly opened abyssal rift—barely a hundred meters below the true seafloor, freshly split by forces that obey neither plate tectonics nor known engineering. The rift is still singing from its birth; the rock walls are so sharp they look wetly cut by a scalpel the size of a continent.

Everything here is submerged.

The water is not an ocean. It is the atmosphere, the blood, the wiring. Cool, dense, almost syrupy at this pressure, it carries a faint metallic taste even though no mouth exists to taste it. Light—ordinary, blue-white, noon-on-the-poles light—pours from thousands of embedded emitters that refuse to dim or flicker. The beams do not scatter the way they should in water; instead they seem to travel in rigid geometric corridors, as though the liquid itself has been taught to behave like fiber-optic glass. Where the beams cross, brief interference patterns bloom like impossible stained-glass windows suspended in the flood.

There is almost no room to move.

The city has grown in negative space. Every millimeter not occupied by structure is occupied by violently fast, deliberately channeled currents. The “streets” are fissures no wider than a forearm. The “towers” are lattices of iridescent black and silver that interlock like graphite fibers magnified a billionfold, each strut thinner than a hair yet bearing the compressive load of an ocean. They phase—sometimes solid, sometimes momentarily semi-liquid—according to rhythms no human eye can parse. Between them run tighter lattices still, then lattices within those, descending into fractal urban tissue so dense the eye simply gives up and sees only a single glittering mass.

This is not ruin. It is minutes old by planetary standards. The edges are still extruding; you can watch new filaments shoulder their way out of parent beams with soft crystalline clicks, knitting the city tighter in real time. Erosion has not yet begun its work. Every corner is lethal, every joint bright as a fresh fracture.

There is no vegetation worth the name—only the occasional stand of glassy, antenna-like fronds that sway in perfect synchrony, harvesting gradient energy from the raging micro-currents. They look like frozen lightning.

There are no animals. Not a microbe, not a fleck of plankton. The water has been scrubbed to sterile perfection and then weaponized. Any life that tried to enter would be dismantled for parts before it understood it had arrived.
