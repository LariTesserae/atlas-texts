**The Hollow Crown**

You emerge inside a single, self-contained pocket that should not be able to hold what it holds.

The space is no larger than a modest mountain valley, yet the distances inside it feel wrong. Every structure stands in extreme isolation, separated by cubic kilometers of almost nothing. The air is hot—close to 58 °C in the open volumes, hotter still near the black-glass monoliths—and it carries a faint, metallic dampness, as though the heat itself is sweating. Moisture condenses only where the architecture deliberately cools itself: hairline fractures in the material, the undersides of cantilevered spurs, the throats of resonance horns. There it gathers in trembling beads of perfect silver before the heat wins again and the droplets race away as steam, hissing like struck matches. Sometimes a droplet falls far enough to strike a lower platform. The sound is shockingly loud in the prevailing quiet: a bright, glassy *tink* that ricochets for seconds before the engineered acoustics swallow it.

The city—if it can be called a city—is young. Everything still wears the razor edge of first formation. Angles meet at tolerances measured in angstroms. Surfaces are so smooth they feel wet even when they are not. The primary material is a vitreous carbon-ceramic that drinks the dim light and gives back only a faint reddish-black reflection, like fresh obsidian cooled under immense pressure. Threaded through it are lattices of something brighter—iridescent, almost alive—whose exact composition refuses to resolve. These filaments sometimes rearrange while you watch, sliding past one another with the slow inevitability of continental drift, yet without sound.

There is no sky. The ceiling of the pocket is simply *farther up*, lost in thermal haze and dimness. The floor is equally uncertain; in places it drops away into shafts that have no visible bottom, only a faint orange glow suggesting greater heat below. Between the scattered megastructures run thin, elevated walkways no wider than a hand, suspended by nothing visible. They were never meant for feet. They exist as calibration lines, measurement apparatus, the nervous system of a single vast computation that has not yet decided what it is computing.

Nothing grows here. Nothing flies or crawls. The only motion is infrastructural. Every few minutes a pillar will flex, its surface blooming with transient geometries that fold away again. Occasionally a sphere of blue-white plasma the size of a fist drifts between two towers, following a route that obeys no obvious law of gravity, before dissolving into heat. These are the only “fauna,” and they are less alive than lymph.

The strangeness is not theatrical. It is architectural. Elevation here is ontological as well as physical: the pocket sits at the absolute apex of a world-spanning megastructure whose lower levels no longer exist in any accessible frame. The chamber is therefore at the highest possible altitude that still possesses air, heat, and the memory of water. Perspective sometimes fails. A tower that appears a kilometer distant will suddenly resolve into microscopic detail when your attention shifts, as though focal length itself is a negotiable parameter. Sound, too, is disciplined. The default state is a pressed, velvet silence so complete you hear the blood in your own ears. Then, without warning, the entire pocket will emit a single, sub-bass note—more felt than heard—like a cathedral bell struck inside a glacier. After the note fades, the quiet feels deeper than before.

This is not ruin. It is scaffolding. The city was instantiated only recently, spun out of pure information and held at the edge of stability by technology that no longer bothers to obey the physics that produced it. The condensation cycles, the micro-adjustments of the lattices, the wandering plasma orbs—all are diagnostic routines. The place is testing whether it can continue to exist. So far it has decided yes.

If you stand still long enough, you may notice the walkways slowly reconfiguring their angles by fractions of a degree, as though the entire pocket is a spirit level trying to find true in a universe that has none. The heat rises. A droplet forms above you, trembles, falls. The *tink* when it strikes is the only conversation the Hollow Crown is willing to have with anything that still possesses a body.

It does not need inhabitants.  
It is practicing, with terrifying patience, how to become a world that will not.
