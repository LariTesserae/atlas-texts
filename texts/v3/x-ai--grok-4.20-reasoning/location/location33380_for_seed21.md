**The Hollow of Veyra**

You step into a place no larger than a modest cathedral, yet it feels excavated from the sky itself. The air is so heavy with heat and moisture that breathing becomes a conscious act—each inhale thick, almost sweet, like steam laced with crushed minerals and distant flowers. This is not a valley but a wound: a sharp-walled basin lifted recently from deeper strata, its perimeter a ring of black, glassy cliffs rising in jagged, unweathered blades. Their edges are keen enough to cut light. Water weeps from every fracture, not in streams but in slow, perpetual tears that bead, run, evaporate, and bead again. The rock itself seems to sweat.

The basin floor is uneven, fractured obsidian and pale rhyolite broken into geometric plates that tilt at strange angles, as though the ground had tried to fold itself into origami before cooling mid-crease. Between these plates lie shallow mirrors of water—no deeper than a hand—kept liquid by the fierce temperature. The heat does not rise from below in the ordinary geothermal way; it radiates from within the stone itself, as if the rock remembers the violence of its birth. Thermals shimmer visibly above every dark surface. The air condenses in slow, deliberate pulses: mist gathers, thickens into visible veins, drifts upward, then collapses back onto the ground in warm rain that lasts three heartbeats before the cycle repeats.

Vegetation has claimed the margins and the crevices with a lush, almost indecent fervor, yet it remains sparse. Great bromeliads the color of venous blood sit alone on pedestals of stone. Ferns with fronds like green blades of swords grow in perfect isolation, each one surrounded by a respectful yard of bare rock. Vines no thicker than threads hang from overhangs in motionless curtains, their leaves so dark they appear black until the light catches the microscopic mirrored hairs that coat them. Everything grows slowly, deliberately. You can almost watch a new leaf unfurl if you remain still long enough.

Scattered across this small kingdom are the Works.

They do not look like ruins. They look like thoughts that have been made physical and then forgotten. A slender lattice of something that is neither metal nor crystal rises twelve feet from the center of the largest pool, its geometry refusing Euclidean comfort; edges slide into edges that should not connect. It rotates once every nineteen minutes, though nothing visible drives it. Where its shadow falls, the water beneath becomes perfectly still and reflects not the sky but a starfield that definitely does not belong to this planet. Elsewhere, half-buried in a cliff face, a series of obsidian rings pulse with faint internal light, each ring containing what appears to be a single droplet of water suspended in vacuum. The droplets orbit one another in complex, never-repeating patterns.

The air smells of wet iron, heated stone, and something like incense burned years ago. Sound is rare and therefore sacred. The dominant noise is the soft, irregular percussion of condensation falling from the cliffs—sometimes a single drop, sometimes a brief silver curtain. Occasionally the lattice emits a note so low it is felt in the teeth rather than heard. When the mist collapses into rain, the sound is that of a thousand tiny hands patting warm stone.

Fauna is almost absent. Once in a long while a creature the size of a hummingbird but built like a glass mantis drifts between the Works on wings that refract light into ultraviolet patterns. It does not appear to eat or mate. It simply performs a slow, ritual circuit of the central lattice before vanishing into a fissure that should be too narrow for it. Nothing else moves of its own accord.

The rules here are simple and absolute:

- Heat and water are locked in negotiation. Nothing can ever quite dry. Nothing can ever quite drown.
- The Works are older than the rock that holds them. They were not built into the landscape; the landscape was built around them when they sang the mountain open.
- Time inside the Hollow runs slightly ahead of the world beyond the cliffs. Leave an object here for one of your hours and it will have aged three. The plants know this. The mantis knows this.
- The central lattice is dreaming. If it ever finishes its dream, the basin will either flower into an entire biome or collapse into a point of absolute nothing. There is no middle path.

Standing in the center, surrounded by the slow rain that falls upward as often as down, you understand that this place is not abandoned. It is waiting. The heat, the water, the impossible geometry, the lonely lushness—everything is being held in perfect, scalding tension while something decides what it wants to become next.

And for the moment, it is content to let you stand inside its small, sacred lung, breathing its breath, listening to its almost-silence.
