**The Hollow of the Unfinished Engine**

It is no larger than a cathedral nave, yet it feels scooped out of the roof of the world.

You stand inside a high cirque whose walls rose yesterday—or perhaps a million yesterdays ago—jagged, black, and glass-edged, as though the planet had flexed a muscle and forgotten to smooth the fracture. The elevation is punishing; the air is thin enough that breathing feels like drinking. But the water parameter has broken the usual rules of altitude. Moisture beads on every surface, weeps in silver veils down the young cliffs, condenses from nothing into visible breath that drifts in slow, deliberate coils. The atmosphere itself is lactating. Every outcrop, every leaf, every scale or shell is lacquered with a permanent film of water that never quite falls as rain and never quite dries.

The vegetation is almost obscene in its lushness against the cool temperature and sparse density. In pockets no wider than a village green, ferns taller than houses unfurl fronds heavy as wet velvet. Moss grows in deep-pile drifts the color of oxidized copper. Vines thick as ship’s hawsers hang in perfect catenaries between stone columns, dripping, dripping, never lengthening, never rotting. Between these sudden jungles lie wide lanes of naked rock where nothing grows at all. The contrast is so precise it feels intentional, like negative space left by a designer who wanted the eye to keep moving.

And the eye does move, because the rock has become machinery.

Not metaphorically. Not approximately.

The young cliffs have faulted and folded into the unmistakable silhouettes of industrial forms. A three-story escarpment leans outward in the shape of a flywheel, its rim serrated by natural erosion into perfect teeth. Opposite it, a column of basalt has split into a piston that rises and falls—one inch, exactly—one inch, exactly—one inch, exactly—driven by water pressure from a hidden aquifer. The motion is real. The change is not. The piston has been performing this single oscillation since the moment the mountain birthed it, and will continue after every witness is dust. The system is in perfect dynamic equilibrium: loud, violent, and utterly frozen.

This is the industrial without the built.

Engines of stone and living tissue turn, mesh, steam, and spark with no forge, no blueprint, no hands. Crystal lattices grown along fault lines function as wiring. Water hammers through hollowed basalt in rhythmic thumps loud enough to rattle teeth—*whump-whump-whump*—like the heartbeat of a factory the size of God. Steam vents formed by supercritical water shriek in perfect fifths. The sound is industrial, deafening, and liturgical. It never varies by a single decibel.

Fauna throngs in the sonic cathedral. Pale, blind things like silverfish the length of your forearm march in geometric formations across the flywheel’s teeth, their footfalls clicking in exact syncopation with the piston. Goat-shaped creatures with ribcages like boilerplates graze on the copper moss; when they exhale, vapor jets from bony chimneys along their spines. Flocks of birds that are more gyroscope than animal wheel in three simultaneous axes, their wings catching the dim light in strobing metallic flashes. Every creature is abundant, every creature is exactly where it has always been, performing its single eternal task. Nothing is born. Nothing decays. The food chain is a closed loop that has become a knot.

Light is dim, sourceless, the color of a storm that can never arrive. It arrives equally from every direction and casts no moving shadows.

Reality here has achieved industrial breakdown.

Stand in one place long enough and the perspective folds. The piston is suddenly the size of a continent. Take three steps and it is again only thirty feet tall. Certain stones, when regarded directly, resolve into endless recursive factories inside themselves; look away and the recursion is gone. Echoes sometimes precede the sounds that make them. If you speak your own name it returns translated into the metallic clatter of a drop-forge. The rules of the world are simple and absolute:

- Everything is already functioning at maximum designed capacity.
- Therefore nothing is permitted to change.
- Therefore nothing *can* change.
- The Engine is perfect. The Engine is unfinished. Both statements are simultaneously, eternally true.

There is no entrance you remember using. There will be no exit that feels like departure. The Hollow does not evolve; it has simply *persisted* in the shape of a single instant of violent mechanical becoming. The weeping cliffs, the dripping vines, the marching silverfish, the oscillating basalt piston—all of it locked forever at the ecstatic moment before completion, screaming, dripping, turning, never arriving, never stopping.

You may stay as long as you like.

The Engine has already accounted for you.
