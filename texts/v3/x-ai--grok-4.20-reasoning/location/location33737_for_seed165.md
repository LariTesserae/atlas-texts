**The Hollow Crown**

You stand on a knife-edge ridge at the roof of the world. The air is so thin it feels like breathing through wool. Every inhale tastes of iron and wet stone. The temperature has not changed in ten thousand years.

The plateau stretches in all directions like a frozen ocean petrified mid-storm. Its scale is deceptive: what looks like an hour’s walk to the next ridge is actually three days, and the ridge after that may not exist at all. Distances here are a suggestion the world has stopped honoring. The light is the color of old pewter, dim and sourceless, as though the sun itself gave up trying to reach this altitude and now only sends apologies.

The rock is black, glassy, and ancient. Glaciers once planed it smooth, then abandoned it. Wind and water have worried it for so long that the erosion has achieved a terrible equilibrium: every crack, every overhang, every impossible cantilever has found the exact angle at which it will neither collapse nor heal. The stone holds its fractures the way a dead man holds his last expression.

Moisture appears only as condensation. It beads on the rock in slow, deliberate drops that never quite fall, or gathers in perfectly circular pockets no wider than a skull. These tiny mirrors reflect skies that are not above you. If you stare too long, the reflections begin to lag.

Vegetation is almost an insult to the term. Here and there a black lichen grows in geometric patterns that repeat at fractal scales, as though the stone itself is trying to remember what life was. Nothing green has been seen in living memory. The few stunted shrubs that exist look like they were carved from the rock by a blind man and then forgotten.

The sound never stops.

It is not wind. The air is too thin and too still for ordinary wind. Instead the plateau emits a constant, layered roar: deep tectonic groaning mixed with the high glassy shattering of ice that never finishes breaking. The noise is loud enough to make your teeth ache, yet nothing ever moves. The sound is not produced by events. It *is* the equilibrium. This is what stability sounds like when reality has been broken for so long it has calcified.

Scattered across the emptiness are the Works.

They are not ruins in any ordinary sense. A single obsidian tower leans at an angle geometry refuses to name; it has leaned at precisely that angle since before language. Another structure resembles a flight of stairs that begins in the ground and ends in the ground again thirty meters away, having gone nowhere. They are made of the same black glass-rock as the plateau, suggesting they were never “built” by hands but *expressed* by the wound in the world the way a pearl expresses grit. Over the eons they have weathered into their final, impossible forms. They will not change again.

The fauna is rare and wrong.

You might see a herd of translucent beasts the size of horses crossing a distant saddle. They have too many joints. Their legs move in perfect synchrony, yet they make no sound above the eternal roar. Sometimes they walk through each other. Sometimes they walk through the mountain. They have been doing this same crossing forever; it is part of the equilibrium. Closer to you, fist-sized insects with wings of frozen lightning crawl across the rock in mathematically perfect spirals. They never deviate. They never die. They are not alive in any way you would recognize.

Reality here is not unstable. That would imply change, and change is forbidden. Instead it is *stably broken*. The laws of physics have settled into new, grotesque shapes the way a shattered bone heals crooked. Gravity forgets itself in certain corridors. Echoes arrive before the sounds that made them. If you speak your own name, the plateau sometimes answers with a name you have not yet earned. These phenomena do not fluctuate. They are as fixed as the stones.

The longer you remain, the more the emptiness reveals itself as fullness turned inside out. Every cubic kilometer contains almost nothing, yet that nothing is *crowded* with the memory of things that were un-made when the wound occurred. The absence has weight. It presses on the eyes.

This is not a dead world. It is a world that has achieved the final, deafening stage of rigor mortis and now believes itself to be perfect. The Hollow Crown does not want visitors. It does not want anything. Wanting would be a form of change, and the Crown has finished changing.

It simply *is*, at deafening volume, beneath a sky the color of a bruise that will never heal, while black lichen writes endless equations across stone that has already solved them.
