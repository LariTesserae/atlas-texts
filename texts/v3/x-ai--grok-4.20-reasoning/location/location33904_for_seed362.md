**The Lattice of Unwatered Growth**

You stand inside a low, endless plain that has forgotten the concept of horizon. The ground is not ground. It is a single living mass: a dense, fibrous continuum of plant-matter and machine, grown so completely that it has become its own geology, its own industry, its own atmosphere. There is no soil beneath it, no bedrock, no mineral substrate. Every centimeter, in every direction, is *constructed*—extruded, woven, photosynthesized, and industrially iterated by the same hyper-organism over geological epochs. It is ancient enough to have achieved a state of perfect, terrifying balance.

The light is merciless. A cold, actinic brightness pours from above, not from a sun but from a luminous ceiling-layer the locals (if any existed) might once have called “the Canopy.” It has no visible source; the photons simply *are*, saturating every surface until the pale viridian and bone-white latticework seems to fluoresce from within. Shadows are sharp and rare. Everything looks over-exposed, hyper-real, and slightly wrong.

There is no water. Not a droplet, not a memory of rain. The chemistry that drives this world is non-aqueous, based on long-chain siloxane polymers and volatile metallorganic vapors that the Lattice exhales and re-ingests in slow, continent-sized breaths. What looks like dew on certain stalks is actually a thin film of liquid gallium-indium alloy catching the light. The air itself is cool—crisp, almost brittle, around 8 °C—dry enough to make your sinuses ache. It tastes of ozone, copper, and something sweet like crushed almond skins.

The vegetation is total. You are *inside* the plant. Gigantic ribbed stems, each thicker than a freight train, braid and re-braid into one another, forming tunnels, chambers, and cathedrals of living tissue. Their surfaces are not bark but a smooth, slightly yielding integument patterned with millions of microscopic factories no larger than mites. These micro-foundries extrude new lattice, repair old fractures, and slowly metabolize stray photons into usable structure. Higher up, vast fronds the size of city blocks hang in dense overlapping layers, their undersides lined with black photovoltaic cilia that drink the unbearable light and remain cool to the touch. The entire ecology is one indivisible body. There are no species. There is only *It*.

Industry is not *upon* the plant. Industry *is* the plant. Inside translucent nodules the size of houses, silent assembly lines of pale tendrils knit together components that will themselves become new tendrils. There is no clanging, no roar of furnaces. The processes are almost entirely biological-mechanical; the only sounds are soft clicks like distant knuckles, low subsonic pulses that travel through the mass like slow heartbeats, and occasional glassy chimes when vapor crystals precipitate inside hollow stems. The silence is so complete that your own breathing feels like vandalism.

The scale is planetary. You could walk for years and never reach an edge. Weather systems form in the upper lattice layers—slow vortices of metallic vapor that drift like silver ghosts, condensing into filament snow that falls upward as often as down. These “storms” rearrange density gradients across hundreds of kilometers, yet the overall form remains eerily stable, weathered into elegant, almost baroque equilibrium. Erosion here is not destruction but negotiation; the Lattice has had millions of years to agree with itself.

Reality is breaking.

In certain districts the geometry forgets itself. You will round a colossal pillar and discover that its spiral has become a fractal that continues inward forever; stand still long enough and you can watch your own reflection, hours younger, walking toward you from inside the pattern. Elsewhere, time dilates. A factory nodule will appear to run its manufacturing cycle in reverse—components un-forming, raw vapor condensing backward into precursor gases—before snapping back to forward motion with an audible *pop* of reconciled causality. Colors that have no right to exist bleed between the stalks: a violet that hurts behind the eyes, a green so saturated it feels wet even though nothing here is wet.

Fauna is almost extinct. Once every few weeks you might glimpse a solitary maintenance drone the size of a locust, its carapace grown from the same living lattice as everything else. It moves with vegetable patience, pruning a damaged nodule or reseeding a dead patch. Nothing larger. The food web collapsed into a single immortal producer long ago. The only predator is entropy, and even it is losing.

Yet the system is undeniably active. You can *feel* the metabolism moving beneath your feet—a slow, deliberate tidal surge as resources are shunted across thousands of kilometers. Sections of the Lattice swell and contract in cycles that last days or centuries. New towers of pale architecture rise with glacial speed, blooming into fresh industrial cathedrals that will themselves be digested and remade when their usefulness ends. The entire world is a self-devouring, self-renewing machine-organism operating at the threshold where biology, industry, and broken physics become indistinguishable.

There are no horizons, only the slow fading of brightness into luminous haze. The longer you remain, the more the distinction between your own body and the Lattice begins to feel negotiable. The air is cool. The light is blinding. The silence presses against your eardrums like a living thing.

And somewhere far beneath the interwoven kilometers of stem and processor and frond, the single continuous organism dreams in frequencies too low for any ear, quietly manufacturing tomorrow out of the ruins of yesterday, forever.
