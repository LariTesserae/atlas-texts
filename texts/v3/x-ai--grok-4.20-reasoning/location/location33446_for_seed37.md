**The Nascent Pockets**

You do not so much enter as *coalesce* into the place. A fissure no wider than a shoulder yields, cool and damp, and suddenly you are inside a chamber that feels embarrassed by its own existence. The walls have not decided what they are. In places they remain raw geology—wet claystone the color of bone left too long in moonlight—while elsewhere they have begun to bud, forming shallow ridges and half-finished lattices that look like the first attempts of something vast to knit a skeleton.

Everything is intimate. The largest pocket is no bigger than a village shrine; most are smaller, connected by throats of stone so narrow you must turn your head sideways and still feel the rock tasting your breath. Yet the light is merciless. A flat, shadowless brilliance saturates every surface, bright enough to read by, bright enough to hurt. There is no sun, no obvious bioluminescence. The light simply *is*, leaking from the frayed edge where reality has not finished caulking itself to physics. If you stare at any single point too long the glow develops a pulse, as though the air itself is embarrassed to be seen.

Water moves like a shy rumor. It does not flow so much as *remember* flowing. Droplets condense on the ceiling in perfect spheres, hang, then fall with the deliberate pace of thought. They strike the floor and bloom into coin-sized mirrors that last only minutes before they are drunk again by the stone. The air stays damp, cool enough that your exhaled breath becomes briefly visible before the brightness devours it. You can taste iron and something faintly floral, as if flowers once grew here and the memory refuses to leave.

Vegetation is almost an insult to the term. Here and there, sparse filaments of pale matter—neither root nor fungus—emerge from cracks. They wave slowly, though there is no wind, flexing like the whiskers of a dreaming animal. Some of them bleed soft light when touched, and the light remembers the shape of your finger for several seconds after you withdraw.

The fauna, by contrast, is *obscene* in its abundance. The pockets are crowded with life yet never feel crowded; the creatures keep perfect negative space between them, as though obeying an etiquette older than species. Transparent shrimp no longer than a fingernail drift through the air on unseen currents. Things like jeweled beetles click across the walls, their backs etched with glyphs that change when unobserved. Floating sacs of luminous jelly pulse in slow predator-prey ballets—when one is caught, both hunter and hunted briefly occupy the same space before the loser is *unfolded* into new shrimp. There are no sounds of jaws or screams. Only the faintest percussion: a ticking like distant clocks, the whisper of carapace on wet stone, and sometimes a sound exactly like someone swallowing behind you.

The built elements are the most unsettling. Scattered across the embryonic rock are structures too precise to be accidental: tiny bridges made of fused gravel, half-arches that lead nowhere, delicate lattices that resemble both bone and circuit. They look grown rather than made. Some are still *forming*—you can watch a lattice slowly extrude itself from the wall like a thought solidifying. Touch one and the nearest creatures freeze in perfect unison, as though you have interrupted a conversation you were never meant to witness.

This is a place still being born. The erosion parameter has not yet been written; the stone is trying on shapes the way a child tries on voices. Sometimes a wall will ripple like muscle and a new pocket will bud outward with a soft pop, birthing three more shrimp and another impossible bridge in the same motion. The air tastes of wet chalk and ozone. Reality here is thin as wet paper. Look too sharply at your own hands and you may see them slightly out of register—two versions, one heartbeat apart. Speak and the words sometimes arrive before your mouth finishes shaping them. The silence is not absence of sound but presence of something listening so intently that all other noise has been crowded out.

The ecosystem has no visible bottom. The shrimp eat the filaments. The beetles eat the shrimp. The luminous sacs eat the beetles and, occasionally, a piece of the ceiling that has not yet decided to be stone. Everything is predator and everything is also architecture. When a creature dies it does not rot; it *subsides*, becoming another delicate lattice for the next generation to climb.

If you stay long enough the pockets begin to feel like lungs. Slow inhale of condensation, slow exhale of new stone. The light brightens and dims in time with some pulse you cannot name. The walls are still learning what distance means. Sometimes the far side of a chamber is suddenly the inside of your own mouth. Sometimes you realize one of the lattices is the fossil of something that has not yet evolved.

This is not a cave.  
This is a pocket of the world that has not finished being dreamed, and every creature inside it is both inhabitant and half-finished thought. The dream is cool, damp, impossibly bright, and it is still deciding whether or not to let you leave.
