**The Hollow Apex**

You reach it only by following a fracture no wider than a shoulder in the newest teeth of the world.

The peaks here tore upward yesterday, geologically speaking—perhaps within the last few decades. Their flanks are glassy with conchoidal fracture, black and rust-red and the color of a blade held to light. No water has ever touched them. No snow clings despite the thin, cold air that tastes of iron and static. Elevation is absolute; the sky feels peeled back, violet-black even at what should be noon. The wind is too dry to howl. It hisses across edges that have not yet learned how to be mountains.

Inside the fracture the stone opens like a seedpod and you step into a place that should not fit.

The chamber is no larger than a theater, yet it has been built and overbuilt until the word “room” becomes a lie. Every cubic inch is claimed. Walkways the width of a hand spiral between vertical foundries; pipe nests braid so tightly that the air itself seems threaded. Brass, blackened steel, pale ceramic, and something like petrified engine grease form the only geology. All of it is new. Every rivet still carries the blueing of recent heat. Every cut surface glitters with the memory of the tool that made it.

There is no water. Chemistry here runs on anhydrous solvents that crawl through transparent veins like slow mercury. They smell of ozone and hot solder. Where they leak the inhabitants drink them immediately, the way desert creatures drink dew that never was.

Light is dim and chemical: sickly amber globes behind clouded glass, stuttering arcs behind wire cages, the occasional bloom of phosphorescence where a conduit has been scored by a thousand tiny mouths. Shadows are thicker than the things that cast them.

And the things that cast them are everywhere.

The fauna is a single rolling storm of creatures best called *ferroclasts*—moth-bodied, crab-shelled, the size of a man’s palm to the size of a cat. Their wings are sheets of beaten foil so thin they make no sound at all when they beat; the only noise they produce is the soft clicking of jointed metal legs and the wet-paper rustle of solvent tongues. They layer in the air like gnats above a lake that does not exist. They carpet every surface in shivering, living felt. They thread through the smallest gaps in the engines like thoughts. Their shells are the exact color of the machinery, so at first you believe the entire chamber is simply moving, breathing, reorganizing itself without need of biology.

They are not pests. They are the secondary nervous system.

The place is in permanent crisis.

What was an electroplating bath a minute ago is now a drop-forge. A catwalk that carried workers (there are no human workers) folds into a new stamping die while the old one is already being dismantled by a tide of ferroclasts. Parts are born, used, broken, and fed back into the hoppers in a single convulsive cycle. The entire pocket behaves like a restless, feverish machine that is simultaneously factory, warehouse, and immune system. Nothing is allowed to finish. Nothing is allowed to rest. The turbulence is devotional.

Yet the sound stays low, almost courteous. A constant sub-audible thrum sits at the edge of hearing, the sound of ten thousand well-oiled hearts turning at once. Occasional soft chimes when two solvent pipes kiss. The papery rustle of wings that do not disturb the air. A quiet so dense it becomes another form of pressure.

Every few hours the ferroclasts arrange themselves into living circuitry—thousands of them locking wing-to-leg until they form pulsing diagrams across the walls. For a moment the lights brighten, the solvents run faster, new mechanisms bloom from the floor like steel flowers. Then the configuration collapses under its own weight and the turbulence sweeps the pattern away, leaving only a snowfall of shed wing-flakes that are immediately gathered and re-smelted.

The geometry is learning.

Angles that began at perfect ninety degrees have already begun to drift. Corners tighten or open by fractions of a degree each day, as though the chamber is slowly remembering a different shape it was meant to become. The surrounding stone participates; fresh fissures appear and are immediately colonized by new pipework that grows straight out of the living rock. The pocket is not finished. It may never be finished. It is young the way a wound is young—raw, precise, still deciding what kind of scar it will leave.

There are rules here, though they are felt rather than spoken.

1. Nothing may remain unchanged for long.
2. Nothing may leave the chamber.
3. The ferroclasts are both maintenance and malfunction; to remove them would kill the engine, and to let them rest would also kill the engine.
4. The cold is absolute. Heat is a local event, quickly bled into the stone and the thin air above the peaks.

Stand still long enough and the living felt of creatures will climb your boots, tasting the oils in your skin, deciding whether you are raw material or new infrastructure. Most visitors choose to keep moving.

Outside, the young mountains keep rising. Inside, the Hollow Apex keeps building something it has never been told how to finish. Both processes feel like they began at the same violent moment, and both seem to be accelerating.

The longer you remain, the more you understand that the chamber is not *at* the top of the world.

It *is* the top of the world, folded small, sealed, and set vibrating at industrial frequency so the rest of the dry, mineral planet can pretend it is still asleep.
