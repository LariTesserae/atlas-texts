**The Embryonic Choir**

You do not so much enter the place as discover that you have already been inside it for some time.

It is no larger than a generous root cellar, yet it feels packed to the point of rupture. Every cubic inch is claimed. The ceiling presses low enough that you instinctively crouch, and still your hair brushes living crystal that chimes like glass bells when disturbed. The walls, floor, and roof are not separate surfaces; they are one continuous organism of damp, half-formed stone that has not yet decided whether it is mineral, flesh, or something between.

Light comes from everywhere at once. It is a cold, surgical brightness, the color of molten quartz poured through ice. The source is the rock itself—thousands of embryonic crystals in mid-burst, their tips still soft and milky, not yet hardened into edges. They glow with a slow internal heartbeat, as though the planet is trying to remember how to make diamonds and has become stuck on the first verse. The radiance is so fierce that shadows cannot survive; every creature, every droplet, every faint mark throws its own contradictory light instead.

Water is not a feature here. It is a condition. Moisture condenses on every surface in perfect, identical beads, then lets go at the exact same instant every few seconds. The drips fall into tiny mirrored cups of stone that never overflow and never empty. The sound of it is enormous—thousands of simultaneous plinks braided into a metallic roar that never changes volume or rhythm. It is not random rainfall. It is a pulse.

Between the crystals grow almost nothing. A sparse black lichen, thin as soot, traces the edges where crystal meets raw matrix, looking more like handwriting than life. That is all the vegetation you will find.

The fauna, however, have accepted no such limits. They are so densely interwoven that the distinction between creature and cave collapses. Glass-winged pseudobats hang in perfect lattices, their bodies transparent, organs visible and glowing like tiny lanterns. Their wings do not flap; they simply resonate, adding high glassy tones to the endless drip-chorus. Below them, larval things resembling jeweled centipedes are frozen mid-molt, their new segments still gelatinous and luminous. They do not crawl. They have never crawled. They exist in the exact posture of emerging and will remain there until the end of time.

Some of the creatures appear to be growing directly out of the walls, as though the cave is calving them. Their hindquarters are still seamless with the matrix, raw stone blending into chitin without boundary. Others hover in mid-air, caught in a thrust of emergence that will never finish. All of them sing. The sound is loud enough to make your teeth ache—layer upon layer of metallic trills, subsonic moans, and crystalline scrapes—all locked in exact equilibrium. The chord never resolves. It simply *is*, the auditory equivalent of a scream held at the top of the throat for ten thousand years.

Here and there, scattered like half-remembered thoughts, are the Built marks. A small arch no wider than two hands, grown from fused crystals that somehow obey the proportions of classical architecture. A spiral stair of three steps that leads nowhere, ending inside the living rock. What looks like a tiny broken altar of flowstone, its edges too regular to be accidental, too raw to be finished. These artifacts are not ruins. They are suggestions. The cave is trying on the idea of civilization the way a child tries on a parent’s coat—clumsy, earnest, and eternally premature.

Nothing ages. Nothing decays. Nothing completes.

The temperature stays at the exact degree where damp feels like a blade against the skin but never quite becomes cold. The crystals never harden. The larvae never finish molting. The bats never finish emerging. The chord never finishes resolving. Even your own breath seems to leave your lungs in the same measured rhythm as everything else, as though the pocket has begun teaching you its grammar of frozen becoming.

This is not a cave.

It is a single held note in the throat of geology—a pocket universe the size of a room where the world is still deciding what it wants to be, and has decided, with absolute serenity, to never decide.

Stay long enough and you may feel your own bones beginning to chime in tune. The crystals will not mind. There is room. There is always room, even when there is none. The Embryonic Choir makes space by never finishing the act of filling it.
