**The Verdant Lattice, or “Weepcage”**

You stand inside a high cirque no wider than a cathedral’s nave and transept combined, a tight bowl cupped between three fresh fracture ridges of black basalt. The ridges themselves are young—perhaps only decades old—knife-edged and raw, but they have already been swallowed. Every surface, every crack, every ledge has been claimed by a single clonal organism: a cold-loving, hyper-dense bryophyte-woody symbiont the locals (if any existed) might call *veilthorn*.

It is not “plants growing on rock.” The veilthorn *is* the substrate. It has built the entire hamlet-scale space out of itself.

### Form and Architecture
The growth obeys an obsessive geometry. From the basin floor rise hundreds of parallel pillars, each the girth of an ancient oak but formed entirely of layered moss heartwood and hollow capillary tubes. These pillars lean together at precise angles to create natural vaults and ogives, so the whole place reads as a single organic cathedral no larger than a village square. Between the pillars run narrow “lanes” — corridors no wider than a man’s shoulders — floored with a resilient, ankle-deep felt of liverwort and hair-cap moss that drinks every droplet and exhales cold mist.

Higher up, the pillars branch into fractal balconies and walkways that cross the lanes without ever touching the stone beneath. These living bridges are so densely interwoven that they form roofed arcades and spiraling staircases of living wood. The entire structure is multi-layered: you can descend through root-caverns below the felt floor, climb through trunk-chambers inside the pillars, or walk the upper galleries where the canopy has knitted itself into a laced dome thirty feet overhead. There is almost no open air. The space is saturated, packed, *occupied*.

### Water and Cold
Everything weeps.

The air is only a few degrees above freezing yet supersaturated; a permanent, slow-moving condensation fog drifts between the pillars like incense. Droplets nucleate on every surface — on the velvet moss, on the translucent vein-leaves, on the ice needles that grow outward like white fur. The droplets swell, freeze, thaw, swell again. They run in deliberate, audible rivulets down the pillars, following helical grooves the plant itself has grown to channel them. At the base of each pillar the water collects in small, skin-covered bladders the size of fists. When these bladders grow heavy they split with a soft *pop*, releasing a measured pulse of water that the moss floor drinks instantly.

At night the temperature drops below zero and the entire lattice rings with infinitesimal chimes as thousands of frozen droplets crack and fall.

### Light and Sound
Light reaches the Weepcage only as a dim, pearl-grey luminescence filtered through the living roof. On rare days when the mountain mist thins, pale shafts pierce the lattice and ignite hanging curtains of frost into brief, cathedral-glass constellations. Most of the time the interior remains in a state of perpetual nautical twilight.

Sound is intimate. The dominant voice is water: the steady, patient dripping, the whisper of mist condensing, the glassy *tink* of ice. Every few minutes a deeper *thock* travels through the wood as a heavy root settles or a new arch completes itself and locks into tension. Occasionally a small white moth the size of a fingernail flutters past; its wings make a sound like torn paper. That is almost the only fauna. The ecosystem is 99.7 % vegetable. What little animal life exists consists of microscopic rotifers in the water bladders, a few species of ice-boring beetle, and the rare alpine ptarmigan that mistakes the upper galleries for shelter and leaves in confusion.

### Biology and Rules

The veilthorn is one continuous organism. Every pillar, every bridge, every moss carpet shares the same root-mass threaded through the fractured basalt like silver wire. It grows with visible haste — you can watch a new tendril extend, thicken, and ossify into structural wood over the course of a single day. Its growth follows cryptic environmental gradients: moisture, faint light, and the slow exhalation of minerals from the young rock. The result is a self-organizing city. No mind directs it, yet the patterns are too consistent to be accidental. It builds redundancies, buttresses, even “windows” — thin membranes of translucent leaf-tissue that admit the dim light while retaining humidity.

The rules of the world here are simple and merciless:

- All space will be filled.
- All water will be caught, stored, and redistributed.
- Nothing that is not veilthorn may remain for long.

Any seed blown in from outside is swiftly overgrown and dissolved. Any larger animal that tries to nest is gently but inexorably walled out or absorbed. The lattice does not hate; it simply converts.

### Feeling of the Place

There is no true stillness. The Weepcage is *busy*. It is growing, drinking, freezing, thawing, reinforcing, expanding — all in ultra-slow motion. Standing in one of its narrow lanes, you feel like a microbe inside a living lung. The cold is constant and penetrating, yet the air smells vividly alive: green, mineral, faintly sweet, like crushed ice and wet stone and the inside of a church bell.

Above you, new arches are forming even now. Below you, unseen root pumps push bladder-water through capillary highways. Around you, every surface glistens, weeps, and breathes.

This is not a ruin. It is not even a forest.

It is a young city that happens to be made entirely of cold, wet, hungry life, and it is still in the first ecstatic stages of building itself.
