**The Hollow Lattice**

You stand inside a place that feels like the inside of a forgotten ribcage.

The basin is only a few hundred paces across — hamlet-sized, sharply delimited by a circular rim of fresh black rock that rises like broken pottery. The walls are young, their edges still viciously sharp, as though something heaved upward from below only decades ago and the world has not yet had time to blunt it. Everything here is low and contained, pressed beneath a flat, dim sky the color of pewter.

There is no water. The ground is a sterile grit of fractured silicate and anhydrous salts that drink moisture before it can even form. Your tongue feels dusty the moment you breathe. The air itself is cool (just cold enough for breath to fog, though the fog vanishes instantly, as if the atmosphere is jealous of any liquid).

Yet the basin is not empty.

From the mineral floor rise structures. Not ruins, not buildings in any human sense, but lattices — hundreds of them — arranged in what feels disturbingly like streets, plazas, and districts. They are made of translucent ivory-colored crystal shot through with veins of metallic blue and dull bronze. The beams and plates interlock in geometries too regular to be accidental, yet too strange to have been designed. Some towers rise thirty meters, branching like antlers frozen mid-growth. Others form wide, shallow arches that span the empty avenues like bridges to nowhere. The spaces between these constructions are vast. You can stand in the center of what feels like a thoroughfare and feel the loneliness of a single candle in a cathedral.

The crystals are young. Their edges catch what little light exists and cut it into bright, painful lines. Fresh fracture planes glitter like new bone. Here and there you see where growth is still happening: hairline seams where translucent fingers are slowly prying two plates apart with the patience of centuries compressed into visible motion. If you sit long enough in the silence, you can hear the faintest occasional *tick* as one crystal kisses another and locks.

Vegetation, if it can be called that, is almost an insult to the word. Sparse black mats — thick as felt, dry as paper — cling to the northern faces of certain towers. They are not plants. They are closer to extremophile films that feed on the faint chemical gradients where different crystal species meet. Every few hours one of the mats will ripple slowly, like the hide of an animal dreaming, and migrate a few centimeters toward a fresher fracture.

Fauna is rarer still. Once in a long while you glimpse something pale and many-legged scuttling along a crystal beam. It moves with the terrible slowness of cold-blooded deep-sea creatures. Its body is almost pure transparency; you only see it when it crosses a dark vein in the crystal and its silhouette briefly registers. It appears to be grazing on the black mats, or perhaps on the crystal itself. You never see more than one at a time. The predator-prey relationship here, if it exists, operates on geological schedules.

The light is dim and sourceless, as though the sun has been ground into dust and scattered evenly across the sky. The crystals phosphoresce very faintly in reply — a cold, greenish-blue ghost-light that makes the empty avenues feel both holy and deeply wrong.

Sound is almost extinct. Your footsteps in the grit are an obscenity. After a time you begin to walk more carefully, almost reverently, because the silence feels earned. The only noises are the crystal *ticks*, the whisper of a crystalline dust-devil that spins in the same plaza every seven hours like clockwork, and — very rarely — a low, sub-audible thrum that seems to come from beneath the basin, as if something vast beneath the rock is clearing its throat.

This is the strangeness at the heart of the place: the Lattice is clearly *in motion*. It is an ecosystem pretending to be architecture, or architecture that has learned how to grow. The organized density of the structures suggests city, suggests intention, yet there is no tool, no hand, no mind behind it — only chemistry and time and the young violence of recent emergence. The lattices are still deciding what they want to be. Every decade they add another district. Every century they rearrange a thoroughfare.

You feel watched, not by eyes, but by the slow pressure of becoming.

Stay long enough and you begin to notice that certain intersections of crystal almost form letters in an alphabet that hurts to recognize. The black mats seem to prefer certain towers over others, as if choosing favorites. The pale creatures follow routes that look, if you sketch them over days, like patrol patterns.

And always the quiet. The cool. The sense that this small, hollow theater of mineral and slow life is still rehearsing for some purpose it has not yet grown large enough to understand.

This is the Hollow Lattice. It has only just begun.
