**The Hollow of the Nascent Lattice**

You stand inside a space the size of a cathedral nave crossed with a small industrial yard—perhaps two hundred paces across at its widest—yet the volume feels immense because almost nothing occupies its center. The air is cool, just above the point where breath would steam, and damp in the inconsistent way of condensation environments: beads of moisture form on every surface, gather into trembling lenses, then fall in slow, irregular drips that strike the spongy floor with the soft *tok* of a finger tapping flesh. There is no true rain, only this endless quiet weeping from above.

The boundaries of the place are not walls but living masses. Vegetation is total. Every surface—floor, vaulting heights, what should be sky—is overgrown, interlaced, and structural. Great ribs of pale, woody growth arc overhead like the ribs of some half-formed beast, braided with black iron pipes that pulse faintly, as though sap and coolant have become the same fluid. Concrete buttresses have been swallowed and remade: their rebar now blooms outward as root lattices, their aggregate replaced by dense fungal felt. The “buildings” are not separate from the plants; they *are* the plants. A row of what might once have been factory halls now presents as colossal, hollow stems the width of townhouses, their bark patterned with the ghost-geometry of brickwork. Windows are lenticular gaps between leaf-layers. Doors open and close by the slow hydraulics of living wood.

This is an urban settlement at the scale of a single parish or company town, but it is still embryonic. The erosion parameter is “emerging”: the ground itself is raw and undecided. In places it is soft loam that dimples underfoot like new skin; a few paces away it becomes glossy black chitinous plates that have not yet decided whether they are obsidian or hardened resin. Geological time has been collapsed. Stalactites of root and cable descend from the vault and have not yet reached the floor; some hang only a meter above head height, still lengthening in visible increments if you are patient enough to watch. The entire chamber is under construction by its own biomass and machinery.

Reality here is breaking down at full intensity.

Perspective is negotiable. Walk ten paces toward what appears to be a distant colonnade of trunks and you arrive immediately at its base; turn around and the open center now seems kilometers wide, the far side lost in true dark. Certain angles simply fail to resolve. If you stare too long at a junction where vine meets copper busbar, the junction forgets which substance it is and becomes a hybrid tissue that is neither and both—green veins carrying voltage, insulation that grows in annual rings. Gravity is polite but inconsistent; droplets sometimes fall upward toward warmer fungal masses on the ceiling. Time is locally looped in pockets: a single leaf might unfurl, pause, and refurl itself every seventeen minutes like a slow mechanical bellows.

The light is almost absent. What little exists comes from within. Pale foxfire runs in circuit-board patterns beneath translucent bark. Clusters of insects—too many to count—drift in lazy constellations, their abdomens glowing soft amber or surgical blue. Every so often a larger spark jumps between two metal roots, illuminating for a heartbeat the filigree of mycelium that has replaced wiring. The darkness is not empty; it is saturated, velvety, alive with negative space.

Fauna is abundant and chimeric. Tiny velvet mites the color of oxidized copper move in rivers along structural beams, polishing rust into patina. Things that might be moths but have transformer wings drift between shafts of cooler air. In the open hollow, translucent deer-like silhouettes browse on nothing visible; their antlers are ferrite cores wrapped in living wire. They move in perfect silence until one is taken by a predator: a thing that is half jaguar, half assembly-arm, whose joints hiss gently as it drags its kill into a side-chamber that seals behind it like a closing flower. The ecosystem is active, predator-prey, but at the pace of cathedral clocks. Nothing hurries. Everything is being digested, rebuilt, and re-grown in the same continuous motion.

Sound is mostly quiet. The dominant noise is the irregular dripping—*tok… tok… tok*—and a sub-audible thrum that might be distant engines or a single vast heartbeat distributed through every root. Occasionally a gear the size of a cartwheel turns three precise clicks somewhere high in the vault, then stops for another hour. Leaves rustle, but only when no one is looking directly at them. The place sounds like a factory that has fallen asleep and is dreaming in plant metabolism.

There are rules here, though they are still forming:

- All matter is negotiating between kingdoms. Metal wishes to flower. Flesh wishes to conduct. Stone wishes to respire.
- Anything left stationary long enough will be incorporated. Tools, corpses, thoughts.
- The center must remain empty. The Lattice is building something in the open volume—something not yet born—and will not tolerate interference.
- If you speak above a whisper, the words come back later, spoken by a different mouth growing from a different wall.

This is not ruin. This is not overgrowth. This is a small, cool, dark, damp womb the size of a town, where industry and ecology have been folded together so tightly that neither can remember which was the seed and which the soil. It is still choosing what it will become, and it is choosing with every dripping second, every quiet turn of a living camshaft, every chimeric heartbeat.

Stay long enough and you may feel it choosing *you*, too. The pale roots beneath your boots are already learning the taste of your weight.
