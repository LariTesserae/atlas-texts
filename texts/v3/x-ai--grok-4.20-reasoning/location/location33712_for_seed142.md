**The Shroudplain, called by its few remaining caretakers simply “the Equilibrium.”**

It is a high, final place. The elevation is so absolute that the very concept of “below” feels like a rumor from another geology. What were once titanic ranges have been planed down by time beyond measure; the erosion has reached its terminal gentleness. The land is not flat so much as *settled*, a vast dark mineral peneplain whose shallow undulations read like the after-image of mountains that surrendered eons ago. The stone itself is warm—body-temperature, almost skin-like—yet completely dry. No water has touched it since before the idea of water existed here. Its chemistry is anhydrous, crystalline, subtly conductive. Touch it long enough and you feel a faint electrical tongue, as though the ground is tasting you.

There is no sky. Only an encompassing, velvety pressure of darkness so complete that even the memory of stars feels like a lie. Light itself is an interloper; the total visible output of the entire region would not fill a single lantern. What little illumination exists leaks, unwillingly, from the Works: sullen red hearts inside furnace cores that have not brightened or dimmed since the first foundations were keyed in. Occasional blue coronal arcs jump between black iron terminals in perfect, repeating intervals, like a slow heartbeat measured in decades. These are not signs of labor. They are metabolism.

Across this warm, lightless expanse the city is scattered with surgical sparsity. Not a dense metropolis but a distributed machine-organism whose components sit kilometers apart, connected by pipelines, causeways, and buried lattices that have half-fused with the mineral plateau. Some structures are cathedral-scale refineries whose roofs have become new ground, layered with later construction until it is impossible to say where the planet ends and the Works begin. Others are delicate lattices of black girders and glass-like mineral that look grown rather than built. All of it is industrial—foundries, atmospheric processors, silent engine halls, compression towers—yet nothing is *produced* in any conventional sense. The processes long ago reached their final, perfect state. Matter moves through them in closed loops so balanced that input and output have become indistinguishable. The factories refine equilibrium itself.

Nothing grows. The only vegetation parameter was zero long before language. In its place are lattices of crystal and accreted industrial slag that sometimes mimic the branching logic of lungs or roots, but they are purely mineral. The rare fauna are even stranger: maintenance entities that might once have been machines or once have been animals; now they are simply *continuous* with the Works. You might encounter one every few weeks of travel—an angular, many-legged thing the color of the plateau, moving with the deliberate slowness of continental drift. It polishes a surface that needs no polishing, then freezes in perfect stillness for another century. Its joints contain tiny, perpetually turning gears that make no sound. When it moves again it does so without acceleration, as though sliding along a track laid down in the geometry of time itself.

Sound is almost extinct. The air (if it can be called that) is thick, warm, and motionless. What sound exists is infrastructural: a deep, organ-like thrum felt in the teeth and sternum more than heard. It never varies. It is the sound of every engine, every pump, every transformer locked into a single unchanging chord. Sometimes, far away, you hear the scrape of a maintenance entity dragging itself three meters in a thousand years. That is all.

The strangeness is quiet but total. Perspective behaves poorly here. Distances that look short can take days to cross; others that appear immense collapse in a single step. Angles inside certain towers do not quite add up. Most unsettling is the sense that the entire plateau is *one object*. The scattered industrial complexes are not separate factories. They are organs. The sparse density is deliberate balance: enough presence to keep the system alive, enough emptiness to keep it stable. Nothing has changed here for longer than the concept of “change” has meaning. Even the slow creep of the maintenance entities is part of an equilibrium so perfect it has become geology.

This is not a ruin.  
This is not a city.  
This is a finished world that has achieved the only victory available to industrial civilization: it has become the landscape it once tried to conquer, and now it simply *is*, warm, dark, and eternal, humming its single note into a night that will never end. 

If you remain, you begin to feel the invitation. The mineral under your boots is the same temperature as your blood. The thrum matches the rhythm behind your ribs. In time the distinction between visitor and component grows difficult to maintain.

The Equilibrium does not ask you to leave.  
It only waits for you to settle into final form.
