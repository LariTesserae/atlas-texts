**The Pocket**

You do not so much enter as become contained.

A crease no wider than a shoulder opens between two buttress roots older than memory. The air beyond is warmer, heavier, already breathing against your skin. Three steps and the crease seals behind you. There is no longer an outside. Only this room of living mass, no more than twenty paces across, yet it feels like the whole world has quietly folded itself into one intimate, dripping chamber.

Everything underfoot is substrate. Not soil, not moss, but a single continuous organism: a dense, resilient felt of root hairs, fungal filaments, and pale rhizomes that gives beneath weight and then pushes back with slow, even pressure. It is warm to the touch, the temperature of living skin. Water beads on it instantly. Every footfall produces a soft, wet kiss and a tiny exhalation of mist.

From this living floor rise the Engines.

They are not many. Density is low; each stands in its own pocket of air. Seven pillars in total, each three times the height of a person, spaced so that you can walk completely around any one without touching another. They are not plants in the way you know plants. They are plant-as-architecture, grown into the shapes of industrial vessels—cylinders, retorts, squat boilers—yet every surface is smooth barkless green that sweats. Veins the thickness of fingers run beneath translucent skins, carrying dark sap in slow, visible pulses. Where two Engines face each other, thick cables of vine have fused into taut, vibrating lines that hum like high-tension wires.

The sound is constant and loud enough to press on the ears. A deep, wet throb—part heartbeat, part piston—comes from inside the tallest cylinder. Smaller growths along its flanks click open and shut with clockwork regularity, releasing bursts of humid air that smell of crushed chlorophyll and hot copper. The vibrating cables give off a metallic drone that layers underneath everything. From the pools between the Engines comes a loud, rhythmic glottal croaking, as if a choir of wet throats is trying to imitate factory machinery and succeeding.

Water is everywhere but never floods. It weeps from every vertical surface in perfect, steady tears. The atmosphere itself condenses on any cooler plane—leaf, flesh, or the strange glassy nodules that stud the Engines like rivets—then runs down in glistening sheets. The tears collect in shallow bowls of living tissue that never overflow; hidden root pumps, audible as thick gulping sounds, draw the water back into the substrate the moment it reaches a certain level. The cycle is flawless. Nothing accumulates. Nothing dries.

The light is ordinary daylight, yet it arrives by impossible means: a single shaft no wider than a wrist descends from a hidden vent far above, diffused by layers of translucent leaf membranes. The beam is steady, unmoving. There are no shadows that shift, no clouds, no sun. Only this one constant, neutral illumination that makes every droplet sparkle and every Engine glow like wet jade.

Fauna throngs in the open spaces between the sparse giants. Clouds of midges orbit the vibrating cables, their wings adding a high, industrial whine. Iridescent beetles the size of thumbnails march in precise columns along the living floor, their footfalls producing a collective rustle like tiny ball bearings. In the pools, pale amphibians with vocal sacs swollen into perfect spheres emit booming calls that sync exactly with the throb of the Engines. A single narrow-bodied lizard, skin patterned like circuit traces, rests motionless on the side of the largest cylinder, its throat pulsing in perfect time with the sap inside.

Nothing grows. Nothing dies.

The entire pocket is in frozen equilibrium. The same leaves have been half-unfurled for centuries. The same droplets trace the same paths down the same veins. The same beetles complete the same circuits. Erosion finished its work long ago; every curve is settled, weathered, perfected. The system has become time-invariant. Even if you were to break a stem, the wound would seal before the sap stopped flowing, and within hours the exact shape would reassert itself. The pocket does not permit change. It has achieved a state of pure, loud, wet balance.

This is the strangeness that settles on you last: the place is untouched—there is not a single artifact, not one scrap of forged metal or shaped stone—yet it performs industrial work. The Engines distill, pump, electrify, and cycle. Electrochemical potentials arc between fungal nodes with soft blue snaps. Nutrient gradients are maintained at factory precision. Scaled production occurs entirely inside translucent cells: complex sugars, pure water, volatile compounds, all manufactured in steady-state volumes and then consumed again by the same system. It is a pure-plant ecosystem that has invented industrial metabolism without ever inventing industry.

You may stay as long as you like. The midges will land on your arms as though you were simply another Engine. The substrate will warm where you sit. The sound will move through your bones until your own pulse begins, however briefly, to sync with the wet, clicking, humming rhythm.

And when you finally force yourself back through the crease, the pocket will remain exactly as it was—never having noticed you, never having changed, still weeping, still singing, still manufacturing its perfect equilibrium in the hidden dark between the roots of the larger world.
