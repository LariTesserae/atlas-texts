**The Lattice of Unvarying Condensate**

It stretches so far that the horizon itself seems an engineering decision. Low, planetary in girth, the entire surface has been replaced. There is no substrate that was not first designed, cast, and fitted. Every plate, girder, and flue was placed with the same ruthless precision that now keeps it eternally sharp. The erosion parameter reads young because nothing has been allowed to age; the world is held at the exact moment of completion, as though the last weld cooled only seconds ago and will never be permitted to cool further.

The ground is not ground. It is a continuous mosaic of blackened alloy tiles, each the size of a city block, locked edge-to-edge in an angular herringbone that never repeats yet never varies. Between the tiles run hairline channels no wider than a finger. These are the only “rivers.” Warm water—1.8 on the scale, neither rain nor groundwater but the slow, perpetual exhalation of the machine—condenses on every horizontal and slanted surface, beads, and slides downward in perfect, repeatable paths. The water is slightly metallic, slightly sweet, and exactly body temperature. It weeps from the undersides of cantilevered beams in unbroken silver threads that never thicken or thin. Where the threads meet the channels they form shallow, mirror-still pools that reflect the unchanging clouds above. The atmosphere itself is a condensing surface; moisture coalesces on your skin the moment you stand still, as if the air is trying to incorporate you into the same equilibrium.

There is no vegetation. Not a blade, not a lichen, not even a recognizable fungus. What little organic-looking matter exists is either corrosion bloom on the oldest joints (deliberately maintained at the same orange-brown patina) or the bodies of the fauna themselves.

And the fauna are overwhelming.

They coat every surface in layers three and four deep. They are not insects, not machines, not precisely animals. Each is a segmented thing the length of a forearm, carapace the color of oil on water, legs ending in delicate multi-tools. Their eyes—if they are eyes—are motionless amber lenses. They sit in perfect geometric arrays: ranks along pipe runs, spirals inside idle turbines, stacked shoulder-to-shoulder inside vast vent grilles like filings on magnetic paper. There are trillions. They fill the world the way grass once filled prairies, yet the total picture is static. No leg twitches. No antenna quivers. Their density is so high that from a distance the ground itself appears to have a nap, a living velvet that never shifts in the steady, normal light.

The light is sourceless and even, as though the sky is one enormous frosted pane. It casts no shadows longer than themselves; every edge remains crisp. The temperature sits at a constant 2.4, warm enough that the air feels thick in the lungs but not hot enough to drive convection. There is weather—vast, motionless banks of low cloud locked above the lattice like a second ceiling—but the clouds never move, never rain, never thin. They simply are, the same way the fauna simply are.

Sound is 1.3. Mostly quiet. What noise exists is infrastructural: a bass register so low it is felt in the teeth rather than heard, the idle murmur of continent-sized engines whose every cycle exactly cancels the entropy of the last. Every few minutes a single drop falls from a high catwalk into a pool below—always the same pitch, always the same interval, as though the滴 itself is part of a timing circuit. The fauna make no noise at all. Even their breathing (if they breathe) is synchronized into the planetary hum so perfectly that the sum is silence.

This is a fully constructed industrial environment that has solved its own purpose. Whatever it was built to produce, it now produces only its own continuation. Every factory hall the size of a mountain range runs at zero net output. Every drone-animal is exactly where it must be to maintain every other drone-animal. The system has reached frozen equilibrium so complete that time has been squeezed out of it. Nothing emerges. Nothing degrades. Nothing is born and nothing dies. The young, sharp edges remain young and sharp forever; the condensation continues its fixed routes like a diagram that has learned how to drip.

If you walk, your footsteps ring too loudly and then too softly as the fauna absorb the vibration without ever stirring. If you touch one, its surface is warm and faintly yielding, like heavy rubber over metal. It does not react. None of them ever will. The strangeness is not cosmic or grotesque; it is the quiet, industrial strangeness of a machine that has perfected stillness while remaining industrious, of a planet that has become a single, dense, wet, warm, perfectly balanced thought it will never need to finish thinking.

There are no horizons worth walking toward. Every direction is the same unchanging lattice, the same motionless legions, the same silver threads of water tracing the same paths they traced when the last bolt was driven home an uncountable age ago.

This is the place that solved change, and then kept the solution running at full industrial capacity so that the solution would never need to be solved again.
