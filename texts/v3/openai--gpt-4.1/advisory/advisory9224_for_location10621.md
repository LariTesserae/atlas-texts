**Preparation Guide for the Multiverse Traveler: “The Chitinous Dark”**

Welcome, explorer, to a world at the absolute edge of what we might call biosphere—an ancient ecology before plant, before sunlight as lord, before anything familiar to green or mammal vision. This is not merely “alien.” This is our own primordial unconscious, given tectonic scale: a place of nameless forms, ceaseless wetness, and crushing togetherness. *You will need more than boots and optimism.* Read carefully.

---

### Psychological Preparation

**1. Prepare for Overwhelm of the Senses:**  
- The sound will not abate—imagine thunder layered with coral reef cacophony, only thicker and closer and never-ending. White noise generators will not save you. Practice meditation techniques for withstanding relentless sensory input. Ear protection is essential, but even this will only muffle the vibratory onslaught.
- Visual adaptation is critical: the dark here is not just absence, but population. Expect to see nothing at all, then sudden constellations of dim glow, then confusion as gleaming shells and mineral blooms pulse. Night-vision devices may help, but be wary—too much light may attract attention, and too little means blindness.

**2. Prepare for Claustrophobia:**  
- This place presses in: physically, psychically. You will be jostled, crowded, wedged between masses of moving bodies if you stray from your base.
- Practice calm breathing in enclosed and tightly-packed spaces. For those with even mild claustrophobia or discomfort with insects/crustaceans, rigorous exposure therapy is recommended before departure.

**3. Abandon Expectations of Solitude or Open Space:**  
- There is nowhere unpopulated, nowhere purely “clean” or un-touched. You are always observed, if only passively, by the pulsing, shifting masses.  
- Prepare mentally for persistent feelings of “surveillance” or being enveloped.

---

### Physical Preparation

**1. Protective Attire:**
- **Full-seal, abrasion-resistant suits** with waterproofing *inside and out* are mandatory. Think advanced diver gear, but made for crawling, squeezing, and repelling biofilms. Standard exosuits as used in hostile marine environments are most effective.
    - *All interfaces—wrists, ankles, neck—must be pressurized and locked.*
    - Material should tolerate acid, enzyme, and constant friction.
- **Facial protection:** Panoramic, sealed visors with anti-fog and rapid self-cleaning mechanisms. Expect regular splatter.
- **Gloves:** Dexterity is less useful than impermeability here. Extra-wide finger shields may help “brush” small creatures aside.

**2. Respiratory Gear:**
- High-grade respirator or closed-cycle rebreather. The air is heavy, moist, and thick with microspores, organic decay, trace acids, possibly strange gaskin gases evolved from mineral cycles. You *cannot* rely on a simple air filter.

**3. Sensory Equipment:**
- *Heat-seeking or infrared overlays* will be confusing—the warmth comes from many undifferentiated, sliding shapes. However, vibration sensors may help map local body traffic, so travel with an array of seismic detectors and a tactile navigation grid.
- Plan to navigate by slow, cautious mapping and haptic sensors rather than sight or sound.

---

### General Survival Strategies

**1. Movement:**
- *Stay low*. Towering makes you a collision hazard; the animal forms do not yield to newcomers.
- **Never rush.** The ecosystem is a slow-moving mass—wait for gaps, move gently. Sudden interruption may trigger defensive or opportunistic attacks.
- Avoid placing your full weight on any surface without first scanning with pressure pads—some shells are thin and soft, others armored; some pools look like mineral slabs but are biological mouths.
- Be prepared for perpetual damp. Bring augmented traction gear (gecko pads or micro-spikes), because slipping is constant.

**2. Defensive Measures:**
- Carry a non-lethal repulsion field or vibration generator to create a comfort “bubble” in emergencies—strong enough to discourage contact, weak enough to avoid provoking aggression.
- *No bright lights.* Luminescence attracts both predators and swarmers; use very dim red or blue headlamps only when necessary.

**3. Nutrition and Hydration:**
- **Do not consume** any water or biological matter from this environment. Even mineral water likely harbors extremeophilic microbes with unknown chemistry.
- All food and water must be hermetically sealed and consumed inside suit or shelter.

**4. Shelter:**
- Portable environmental shade/tent is essential—make it sealed, anchored, and camouflaged (surface temperature and chemistry must match native rock).
- Ensure no open seams, as small life will seek entry for warmth, humidity, or food traces.

**5. Waste Disposal:**
- You must contain all bodily waste; local decomposers will swarm to any organic trace and may compromise your gear.

---

### Social/Contact Considerations

**1. Local Intelligences:**
- There is no evidence of “civilized” intelligence or culture—*but do not assume stupidity.* The fauna is perfectly adapted to this density war, and has senses foreign to us.
- Avoid mimicking or interfering with the local swarm-harmonies (especially not with vibration, song, or light—accidentally “joining” a mating or feeding call could be disastrous).

**2. Documentation and Collection:**
- All sampling gear should be triple-contained. Spores, secretions, or living hitchhikers could be catastrophic either here or on return.
- Document through non-invasive means—no flash, no invasive sampling. The local biome will resist extraction.

---

### Emergency Protocols

- *Egress protocols* must be digital, sealed, and redundant. Teleportation or rapid retreat is advised if swarmed or mired.
- If suit is breached, *do not attempt field repairs in place!* Retreat to shelter or away from dense crowds. Suit breaches may attract “cleaner” species.
- Schedule regular psychological check-ins: this environment nurtures a quick onset of anxiety, depression, or intrusive thoughts. Be aware!

---

### Mindset and Philosophy

**Respect the density.**  
You will not “conquer” or even traverse much of this world. You are not an observer here, but another fleeting protein film swept through by the tidal churn of life. Move softly. Be grateful for every breath in your own air. And remember—*this* is what most of the universe’s life probably looks like: sound, wet dark, pressure, countless bodies, no “room” except that which you insist upon for yourself.

Travel, observe, and return with humility.
