### Preparation Guide for Visiting the Forge-Mountains at the Threshold

---

#### Mindset & Psychological Preparation

**Understand the Mood:**  
You are stepping into a world at its rawest—perpetual beginnings, unhealed surfaces, a place where straight lines and stories have not yet been etched. There is no safety of settlement or sign; your psychological compass must accept flux, ambiguity, and indifference.  
**Key Attitude:** Humility before scale, readiness for solitude, and a comfort with silence so deep that your heartbeat will stand out.

---

#### Research & Contextual Understanding

- **Geology in Real-Time:**  
Expect tectonics as performance: mountain-building is not an ancient memory but an ongoing, sometimes audible phenomenon.  
- **Ecology of Scarcity:**  
Life is relentless but precarious; competition is subtle and ferocious, adapted for survival under the strictest constraints.  
- **Ambient Technology:**  
Industry is natural, not cultural—machinery grows from minerals, performs inscrutable geochemical business, and dies back as spent shells.

---

#### Physical Preparation & Gear

**Clothing:**  
- **Extreme Cold Protection:** Layered, insulated clothing; windproof outer shell.  
- **Moisture-Resistant:** All layers must handle near-freezing mist, persistent wetness, and rapid shifts from snow to drizzle.  
- **Gloves & Face Shielding:** Skin will not last long bare—thin atmosphere means sharp cold and biting wind.  
- **Sturdy, Waterproof Footwear:** Expect both snowfields and wet, sharp rock.

**Life Support:**  
- **Oxygen Support:** Air is thin; non-acclimatized lungs may struggle. Consider a rebreather or portable O² source.  
- **Hydration:** Water is everywhere but is nearly always frigid and potentially laden with unknown minerals or machine byproducts. Bring filtration or purification options.  
- **Nutritional Reserves:** Food must be high-calorie, compact, and easy to eat gloved and standing (there are few safe spots to rest).  
- **Heat Sources:** Compact, reliable stoves or heating packs; lighters may not work well given moisture and wind.

**Shelter:**  
- **Portable, Insulated Shelter:** Compact enough for sudden setup; wind-anchored. Permanent camps are unsafe—rockslides and sudden snow are a risk.  
- **Emergency Beacons:** If you lose your way, any electronic signal will be your only hope—no trails, no markers, no humans.

---

#### Tools & Safety

- **Ice Axe, Crampons, Ropes:** Needed for glacier and scree traversal.  
- **Multipurpose Tool:** Repairs, defense, sampling. Stones and odd machine fragments may jam delicate gears.  
- **Hazard Sensors:** Wearable meters for air toxicity (from mineral emissions), geiger counter (never assume radiation is absent in newborn geology), barometer.  
- **First Aid:** For cuts, hypothermia, altitude sickness, and minor animal bites.

---

#### Interacting with the Living and the Mechanisms

- **Observe from Distance:**  
Both the bio-creatures and the mechanical entities here are highly specialized. Any interaction is a potential risk for both you and them—do not feed, touch, or approach unless necessary.
    - *Amphibious colonies*: Keep to dry ground lest you disrupt breeding pools.
    - *Grazers & Rodents*: Will keep their distance, but sudden movements may provoke panic-escape, causing rockfalls.
    - *Predatory Birds*: Large, clever—avoid being isolated in the open.
    - *Metallic Beetles & Engines*: Absolutely do not try to collect or tamper; the chemistry is barely stable.

- **Sound Discipline:**  
Noise travels; your strange presence will stand out. Move quietly; avoid electronics that emit sustained noise.

---

#### Navigation

- **No Paths, Only Concepts:**  
GPS may be unreliable, as the electromagnetic fields could be disturbed by local mineral and machine activity. Use visible landmarks (knife-back ridges, unique rock outcrops) for orientation.
- **Visual Mapping:**  
Document the position of moving mineral veins or semi-regular geysers—they can serve as waypoints.

---

#### Psychological and Existential Notes

- **Enduring Flux:**  
Everything in this world is transitional. Places, creatures, even ‘landmarks’ might shift, vanish, or be created anew. Be ready to adjust not just your route, but your sense of place.
- **Indifference of the Land:**  
This world will not ‘notice’ you. There will be no shelter from fate except what you bring—no welcome in a doorway, no warning signs, no traditions or hospitality.

---

#### Environmental Ethics

- **Leave No Trace:**  
Even a footprint can last oddly long in certain new-formed snows or wet rocks. Do not disrupt the delicate balance of emerging life and machine here—watch, experience, but never alter.
- **Respect Processes in Progress:**  
Never interfere with vein-machines or their byproducts; you could trigger unpredictable releases or deprive a patch of life of its only nearby heat or nutrients.

---

#### Prepare for Awe and Vulnerability

This is a realm of emergence: everything you see will seem alien, unfinished, and momentous. Take notes, sketch, keep a record—you may not remember the specifics, only the feeling of standing at the genesis of geology, the root of ecosystem, the birth-throes of a world that is still deciding what it wants to become.

**In summary:**  
- Carry what you need to stay alive in cold, oxygen scarcity, and unpredictable moisture.  
- Plan for self-sufficiency, as there is no help or guidance.  
- Observe, never intrude—the world’s workings are both fragile and fierce.  
- Let yourself be changed; you walk not as a conqueror or scientist, but as a careful, respectful guest in a place where everything is being newly made.

---

*May you return with the hush and clarity of an uncarved stone, bearing the memory of night-light, half-sung emergence, and the patient industriousness of unhurried land.*
