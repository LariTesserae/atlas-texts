**PREPARATION GUIDE FOR THE UNFINISHED PLATEAU-CITY AT THE EDGE OF BECOMING**

*Overview:*
You, as an ordinary (if multiverse-savvy) human, are contemplating a visit to a nascent city on a young, uplifted plateau — a place liminal, half-made, wild not only in matter but in law. Traversing this locality will test your assumptions about cities, environments, navigation, and even selfhood. Preparation is less a matter of gear than of readiness to adapt, both in body and mind.

**1. Physical Preparation:**

- *Climate & Atmosphere:*  
  The air is cool, metallic, thick — damp, but not stifling. Permanent overcast, syrupy pewter light, intense mists and brief, drenching rains. Dress in layers: waterproof but breathable shell, insulating underlayers, quick-drying fabrics. Comfortable boots with hydrophobic tread for climbing and slipperiness — expect to encounter all textures from mossy grit to sweating alloys to unfinished, resonant stone.
  
- *Visibility & Light:*  
  The world is dim, perpetually misted, brightened here and there by electrical flashes or luminous vegetation. Bring a headlamp or a compact, broad-spectrum light — but expect pockets of electromagnetic static. Choose low-tech, chemical-based backup lights (cyalume sticks, cheat: arcane phosphorescent stones). Amber or bronze-tinted goggles might help your eyes adjust to the tarnished, inconsistent glow.

- *Air Quality:*  
  While breathable, the air is heavy with mineral and electric scent, possibly ionized. Consider a comfortable, reusable respirator (even a simple dust mask works) if sensitive to changing atmospheres: there may be moments of metal-tang or botanic spore.

**2. Navigation, Locomotion & Orientation:**

- *Shifting Geography:*  
  Nothing is quite finished. Directions and paths are contingent. Rely less on maps (they will quickly become obsolete) and more on instinct, line-of-sight, and local landmarks (anomalous towers, color or sound gradients). Bring a compass, but expect anomalies in magnetic field. Pre-download or learn orientation techniques for shifting architecture — such as rope trailing (for backtracking) or using colored chalk for temporary marks.

- *Bridges, Gaps & Verticals:*  
  Chasms, half-formed stairways, looped or ambiguous bridges abound. Carry a lightweight climbing harness and an extendable, friction-lock stick or line for testing dubious footing and assisting with ascents/descents. A grappling hook (old-fashioned or modern), collapsible pole, or an auto-retract winch could be lifesavers. Don’t trust apparent boundaries — verify visually *and* physically.

- *Local Transport:*  
  Rail lines and other semi-reliable forms of transit sometimes phase in and out or alter course. If invited aboard, check whether your passage is “real,” and if you emerge at the expected destination. Memorize the area’s major waypoints. Sometimes travel must be improvisational: ask residents for tips, and watch how the local creatures move.

**3. Interacting with the Environment:**

- *Material Hazards:*  
  Surfaces ‘sweat’ water and possibly low-amp current. Wear gloves (rubberized or at least non-conductive) for handrails, ladders, or metallic edges. Avoid pooling water near visible cables or machinery. In the event of a sudden discharge, retreat to dry, vegetated ground.

- *Living Ecosystem:*  
  The wildlife is abundant and often out-of-phase with familiar biology. For large slugs or carapace creatures, give a respectful berth. Their movements may seem bizarre, but they are generally not hostile unless cornered (or, in rare cases, electrically charged). Flocks of birds are fast and rarely interact with ground-walkers, but don’t look up with your mouth open during major flyovers (droppings, shed scales, and sometimes micro-phenomena).

- *Industrial Activity:*  
  Avoid standing too close to rapidly assembling (or disassembling) machinery. If machinery “notices” you — opt for a slow, non-panicked retreat. Don’t try to interpret the city’s deep-echoing, choirlike moans as warnings; treat them as environmental data.

**4. Social, Cultural, and Metaphysical Considerations:**

- *Residents:*  
  City-dwellers are as emergent as their habitat: not all are strictly human, and some may phase in and out, or communicate in bursts of gesture, color, or resonance. Be polite, respectful of personal (and spatial) boundaries, and do not expect permanence in interactions. Carry a small, universally recognizable token (e.g., a reflective disk, a simple drawing) for peaceful greetings.

- *Contingency of Rules:*  
  Time, space, and physical law are not always fixed. Practice patience and non-attachment. If you find yourself “stuck” in a loop, change your perspective (literal or metaphorical). Always test new paths for stability before committing, and don’t panic if a stairwell or bridge alters direction — these events are usually temporary.

- *Recording Experience:*  
  Digital devices may behave uncertainly. Prefer analog: waterproof notebooks, pencils, mechanical tickers, or record observations aloud for possible pickups by sympathetic infrastructure. Cameras may distort or “double-expose” reality; treat images as art, not testimony.

**5. Health & Safety:**

- *Hydration and Nutrition:*  
  Water is abundant but suspect: gather condensation if desperate, but purify through multiple means (filters, chemical tabs, UV). Local flora and fauna are not recommended for consumption — not merely unfamiliar, but possibly mutable in nutritional content (or physics). Bring your own high-calorie, long-life rations.

- *Exposure & Fatigue:*  
  The viscous atmosphere, inconsistent footing, and shifting gravity may cause rapid-onset tiredness or disorientation. Schedule rest breaks where the ground feels *firmly* real. Survey for pocket-warmth (heat vents, power runnels) in preference to wet, cold benches.

**6. Mental and Philosophical Preparation:**

- *Expect Uncertainty:*  
  This world is not finished. It will not yield to earthly logic or expectation. Try not to “solve” what you see; observe with humility. You’re bearing witness to becoming, not reminiscence.  
  Meditation practice, mindfulness, and an openness to paradox will serve you as well as any tool.

- *Leave No Trace, Take No Piece:*  
  To disturb or remove material here is to participate, possibly irreversibly, in the city’s unfolding. (You may feel tempted to “fix” a slab or rescue a lost cable. Resist unless genuinely invited.)*

**Summary Packing List:**

- Layered, waterproof and insulating clothing
- High-tread, water-resistant boots
- Gloves (nonconductive), headlamp, chemical light-sticks
- Compass, colored chalk/markers, backup navigation aids
- Climbing harness, retractable line or pole, grappling hook
- Analog notebook and pencils (digital equipment only as backup)
- Lightweight water filter, chemical purifier, calorie-dense food
- Respirator/mask
- Small, friendly “greeting” object
- Mindfulness/meditative discipline

**Mindset:**
Arrive as a guest of something much larger than you — a city that dreams and grows even as you walk it.
Document, but do not intervene (unless invited). Move gently, respectfully. Accept that you may see impossible things, and that much of what unfolds here will never be finished, or fully remembered. That, in itself, may be the world’s greatest invitation.

Welcome to the place of Becoming.
