**Preparation Guide for an Ordinary Human Multiverse Traveler: The Secret Crevice of Mineral Life**

---

### **1. Psychological Preparation**

- **Expect Profound Alienness:**  
  Prepare for near-total sensory unfamiliarity. The life here is not *carbon*—no roots, leaves, or animal movement; only mineral “plants” and inert, half-swallowed machines. It’s a world that feels wrong if you expect biology and right if you expect something else entirely.

- **Meditate Beforehand:**  
  The place is *tightly enclosed* and *unrelentingly strange.* Claustrophobia, awe, and a sense of being unwelcomed or out-of-place are natural reactions. Steady your nerves and practice calming breathing techniques.

- **Set Aside Habits of Motion:**  
  No animal, insect, breeze or weather. *Stillness* dominates, broken only by shivering mineral growths and the constant, unending machine hum. You’ll have to attune yourself to subtle movement and sound—a strategy of stillness and patience is best.

---

### **2. Equipment and Clothing**

- **Protective Suiting:**  
  - **Hard-exterior clothing** (composite, ceramic, or resilient polymer) to prevent skin lacerations from sharply-edged “leaves” and crystal formations.
  - **Reinforced boots** with traction suited to smooth, glassy, or jagged stone.
  - **Insulated underlayers**: the air is just above freezing.
  - **Full-coverage gloves and visor:** glassy crystals can shatter and are likely razor-sharp. Gloved hands and a hood or helmeted face shield are critical.

- **Environmental Gear:**
  - **Helmet with filtered air supply:**  
    The air tastes of ozone and rust—possible respiratory irritation. Bring a rebreather or at least a heavy-duty dust mask with mineral filters.
  - **Scraper or multi-tool:**  
    To gently move aside or cut through mineral “undergrowth.” *Do not bring chemically reactive metals or tools prone to corrosion.*
  - **Portable lighting:**  
    Headlamp and chest lamp with diffused, cold-spectrum light (to avoid triggering unknown photo-reactions in the “flora”). Never use strong heat sources, open flames, or strong UV here.

- **Mapping and Marking:**  
  - **Inert chalk or mineral sticks** (not tape or paint, which might interact weirdly): Mark your path as these mineral boulevards might be subtly shifting or difficult to retrace.
  - **Compact compass**—be aware, however, some magnetic anomalies may make it unreliable.

---

### **3. Provisions and Survival**

- **Bring Sufficient Water and Food:**  
  There is *no water,* and nothing is edible or even compatible with terrestrial biology.

- **Medical Supplies:**  
  - **Antiseptics**
  - **Wound sealant**
  - **Longer-term first aid for lacerations** from mineral edges, potential mineral dust inhalation remedies, and, perhaps, a mild sedative in case of psychological distress.

---

### **4. Navigation and Safety**

- **Watch for Entrapment:**  
  The crevice is narrow, crowded, and movement will be slow and awkward. Ensure you have a *buddy system* or a check-in plan. Never push, lean, or pull hard on plantlike structures—they may shatter, collapse, or, in some cases, trigger unknown reactions.

- **Avoid Disruption:**  
  Do *not* attempt to break or force your way past particularly dense thickets unless absolutely necessary. Damaging the lifeforms could trigger chemical reactions, electrical discharges, or structural collapses.

- **Do Not Tamper With Machinery:**  
  Half-buried generators and panels hum with unreadable, *dormant* potential. Do not touch, attempt to open, or connect any machinery to your own devices. The equilibrium here is ancient and very stable—disruption could be catastrophic or, at minimum, energetically unpredictable.

---

### **5. Sensory Adjustment**

- **Bring Sound Dampening Gear:**  
  The endless growl, pings, and sonorous metallic notes will *fill* your hearing—consider earplugs or soft noise-canceling headphones if you need respite.

- **Prepare for Low Light:**  
  You’ll be navigating in gloom interspersed with odd glints, half-light, and the rare artificial flicker. Your eyes will need to adjust; have a red-filter option for map reading or equipment checks.

---

### **6. Scientific and Experiential Recommendations**

- **No Biological Sampling:**  
  Don’t touch your face, mouth, or any open wound after direct contact with local materials. Even seemingly inert substances may have long-term irritant or allergenic effects.

- **Environmental Respect:**  
  Everyth\ing here is *timeless;* even your footprints and tool marks may last millennia. Do not introduce foreign substances—not even a dropped crumb or drop of sweat.

- **Sensory Journaling:**  
  Take notes—written, not spoken—on your sensations. Many travelers report non-visual or even non-auditory impressions: a “tingle” of deep time, odd moods, or a sensation of being watched by intelligence not present.

---

### **7. Exit and Decontamination**

- **Debrief and Cleanse:**  
  On leaving, remove outerwear and thoroughly clean all exposed skin. Carefully decontaminate boots and gloves (mineral dust may be both persistent and sharp-edged).

- **Psychological Processing:**  
  The changeless strangeness of the place can linger as a sense of being outside time, or induce nostalgic alienation. Make time to reconnect with familiar biological places—parks, gardens, forests.

---

### **Summary: What You Might Experience**

Visiting the crevice is like stepping into the lungs of a planet that never quite learned to live like Earth—beauty and order in complete alienness. You’ll feel the chill of endless stone, the wary awe that comes from touching something unconnected with humanity, and the hush of time held motionless. Walk carefully, breathe slow, *don’t touch what you don’t understand,* and let the deep, mathematical rhythm of non-carbon life fill you with respectful wonder. Most of all: carry out everything you carry in, and leave only your careful footprints behind.
