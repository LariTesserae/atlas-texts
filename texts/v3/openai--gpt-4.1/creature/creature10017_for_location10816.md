Who lives here?

The inhabitants of this place are legion, yet no one has ever given them precise names. They are both the citizens and the song, the only pulse, living forms shaped by the same impossibility that forged the city itself. No single form rules, nor is any variety greater than another; their diversity is the result of minute variations iterated across eons of eternal, rhythmic stasis.

**Visual Aspect and Structure:**  
Their bodies suggest machinery and crustacean in equal measure. Some are the size of a thrown stone, skittering with stubby, bristle-trailing limbs along rails or between wetted plates. Others unfurl in slow mechanical waves, cuttlefish-like, pancakes of matter sliding blindly over half-submerged pipes. Most carry marks of corrosion—an edge rendered translucent, flaking bands of mineral plating, a half-glass limb faded by ages of condensation and run-off.

The dominant class crawl in flowing masses. From above you’d see them as wet-lustered streams, flowing between obstacles, parting and merging with insect-consistency. Their ‘shells’ are not chitin, but segmented alloys—hammered glass, pitted pewter, fused mineral layers accreted in self-repairing envelopes. Where joints meet, droplets gather from the air, beading and running into carefully grooved pans on their underbellies, aiding in their locomotion and in the cooling of the frictional heat they constantly generate.

Others, smaller, exist almost as dust. A cloud of pale specks always hangs in the air, refracting the sterile fluorescence into spectral halos. These microforms cling to every surface, coat the air like an endless, shifting mist. You might think them spores, motes, or perhaps a form of plankton—but their movements, though graceless, are intentional: their passage across surfaces reduces the molecular roughness, smoothing and polishing as they roam. Their bodies are scant—mere shells of silicate, latticework suffused with electrostatic charge.

There are those that hang in the upper gloom, multi-winged or filamented, gliding in somnolent circuits near the muted lamps. Their wings are laminar, composed of stacked transparent scales, etched with circuitry-like veins of tarnish. When they beat, their drone is modulated, a binding thread in the city’s constant mechanical chorus.  

**Behavior:**  
None of them feed, in any sense you understand. They graze the air, not to harvest but to keep it moving, their passage helping vapor condense, droplets to gather and descend in the prescribed drops from above. Their scavenging is ritual rather than sustenance: a body passes over a patch of slow-gathering corrosion and, by virtue of their movement, wears it down in perfect measure, never eradicating it, never letting it grow. 

Breeding is unknown here. Population does not wax or wane. Instead, forms move in patterns—loops, whorls, circuits around the factory remains. If you could chart their migrations with time-lapse vision, you would see every route retraced, every action repeated, an endless dance written in micro-adjustments preserved across eternities.

Their communication is frictional—tapping, shivering, scouring. They send vibrations along rails, click together in brief clusters under sheltering gantries. The purpose of these exchanges is unclear: perhaps they coordinate, reaffirm each other’s presence, or simply echo themselves to resist true quiet.

**Intelligence and Awareness:**  
In this place, consciousness—if it exists—is a shared resonance: there is no individual sense of self, but rather a vast, ceaseless awareness of proximity, velocity, and force. They know each other through their impacts and the ever-renewed static fields that accompany their passage. Memory is carried not in minds but in the wear of flooring, the gloss of handrails, the reliable groove left by thousands of minute claws.

Are they constructs, products of the sleeping industry around them? Are they a mineral evolution’s response to forever-damp, sterile stasis? It is impossible to say. Any boundary between machine and animal, product and process, is as fogged and ambiguous as the mist on the rails.

**Essence:**  
Perhaps what lives here is not so much a population as a process—a choreography of half-alive, half-constructed bodies, eternally itinerant, maintaining the equilibrium the place demands. They are the restless cast of an unyielding theater: creatures of condensation and friction, whose ecology is not growth or predation, but the perfect keeping of a cycle that neither ends nor intensifies.

They are not eerie to themselves; they are not lost in the monotony. For them, the endless circuit is fulfillment: each repetition a note, every collision a measure, their collective movement the only meaning this world permits. It is only outside eyes that seek meaning in their labor. For them, the boundaries of sense are perfect and whole.

And so they endure—never aging, never beginning—living at speed and in stillness both, population and function wedded so tightly that neither need nor wish could ever arise. Their only hunger is for the next movement in the endless chorus; their only rest is the moment, held forever, between one clattering step and the next.
