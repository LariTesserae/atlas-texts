Whoever or whatever lives here is not easily named. A home shaped by forgotten human hands, claimed utterly by layered centuries of plant and water, attracts neither true wilderness nor the fully domesticated. The dwellers of this place are emergent—some part of what was, some part of what is, some part of what simply persists.

### The Palefolk

If any intelligence remains here, it is subtle, stooped low, and colored by the hush and microclimate of this dome. The Palefolk are not a people in the ordinary sense—no villages, no tools, no speech left that you could hear—but the lingering coalescence of those who first roofed this hollow and made its benches and pool. The Palefolk are not ghosts, though they might be mistaken for such by anyone who slips through the pane of mist-light: they are an event more than a form, agglomerations of moisture and memory. Sometimes, if the pool’s breathing slows, you sense a glimmer in the prismed air, as if something almost visible were shuffling slowly along the benches, hollow-boned, draped in cloaks hammered from wet moss, hair caught up in curling tendrils of liana. They do not touch or move the world the way a living body does, but their presence is manifest as a sudden pause in the motion of a vine, a thicker bead of water, a spiral gleam on stone just behind the corner of your gaze. Memory rendered animal, living on the condensation’s edge.

### The Quick-Retreaters

Visible life, meanwhile, avoids inheritance or dominion. There are always beetles—milk-pale, with translucent limbs—and their sudden dart and vanish is all nervous precision, like clockwork actors interrupted. Amphibians the size of dew-wet thumbprints flick from surface to surface, their gold-marbled eyes fixed with ancient patience. Each species seems tuned exactly to this haven: not quite like those outside—paler, rounder, marked with odd patterns recalling water stains on old paper. Some are so delicately translucent that if you press your hand down, leaving warmth for a minute, you may see them drawn silently in the after-shadow your palm leaves in the mist.

### The Unburdened

Among the rootwork, in caverns of moss, live the mouse-like mammals—soft, black-eyed with pelts of rain-matte velvet. They seem less like animals than sleepwalkers, moving without hunger or hurry, endlessly winding and rewinding their paths through leafy tunnels as if rearranging the maze of someone’s recollection rather than obeying the pressures of body or season. Their pups, if there are such, are neither found nor sought; no predator disturbs them, and no scavenger chews the remains. There is neither visible violence nor competition, just a choreography of side-by-side existences, tracing and retracing the blueprint of this echoing room.

### The Plantmind

But there is another presence here, not creature but collective. The plantlife that forms a single interwoven web of green does not merely take up old space; it *remembers*—with a slow, generational consensus—each break of light, each passage, each excess of moisture or drought. It recalls, in fiber and filament, the intent of those first crafters. New growth bends around a bench but never covers it entirely; long moss hangs like a drape but leaves an alcove open, as if waiting for a body to return and sit. There is a patience in the rhythm of its movement—the way fronds edge toward sunbeams, the way each leaf drinks mist but never drinks the pool dry. Nothing here crowds out or withers the rest. It is a parliament of green voices, negotiating its form so that the structure below—pavilion, sanctuary, memory-space—remains legible, if you know how to look.

### The Silence Itself

Above and around all things dwells the silence—almost an organism, as palpable as the chill air, as circumspect as any native beast. It feeds gently on all things that enter: a stray insect falls quiet, a wanderer’s footsteps slow, voices dissolve into air and are never echoed back. If this silence is alive, it expects nothing, wants for nothing, and keeps all things held—resilient, never stagnant, perpetually fresh as condensation itself.

### Who may enter, and who may stay?

There are no true intruders, not even you—only those who are briefly permitted to be part of the stillness. The boundary between dweller and guest is functionally nonexistent. To cross the space is to be absorbed, laced in by green, time-dampened, mapped as a slight, transient variation in the webbed equilibrium. The Palefolk hold only the softest of memories; the Quick-Retreaters make immediacy their home; the Unburdened move with neither plan nor regret; the Plantmind shepherds the space toward balance; the Silence waits.

A person passing through would leave little but might—if still enough, slow enough—find their self becoming entangled, their mind braided for an instant into the gentle stasis of this unforgotten hollow, and perhaps, for a moment, become another thing that lives here, neither whole nor erased, but held quiet and complete inside the bones of the place.
