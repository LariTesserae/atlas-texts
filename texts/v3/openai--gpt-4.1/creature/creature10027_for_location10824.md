**Who (or What) Lives Here**

Those who dwell here are shaped by, and in negotiation with, the strict, intricate order their bowl imposes. They are not merely inhabitants but careful inheritors: people whose habits are made of reverence and avoidance; creatures at one with a dance of spacing as much as of presence.

*The People:*

They call themselves little or nothing, their tongue sparing with labels. Their bodies move quietly: not so hunched as to cower, but always aware of an invisible margin—the rules are lived rather than known, unwritten but intuited in every footfall. Skin attuned to damp, hands broad-knuckled and clever, hair dark or pale but tendrilled with moss powder and occasional beads of vibrant lichen. Children, thinner and quieter than human children elsewhere, carry with them woven satchels, always two arm-lengths away from anyone but kin by necessity, their play silent, imagination flourishing in spaces unfilled by sound.

Their society is one shaped by spacing. These are settlers, yet not conquerors, their movement more like infiltration than possession. Perhaps survivors, perhaps explorers exiled by accident or design, their identities are less important here than their adaptation. Living memory rarely dwells on how they came; the elders keep only slanting stories, fragments of old laws that match the geometry of the cliffs, not of their ancestors’ old valleys. They speak in gesture, in bird-of-paradise postures, signaling across the open spaces with hands and eyes, voices rarely raised, laughter muffled under palms. Name-giving is a ceremony of distance: names spoken only at the hollow’s edge or in the thunder of rain, so the place and what dwells here might not overhear.

Inside each hut, the threshold is sacred—not merely a marker of inside and out, but a band preserved against intrusion, layered with crushed ferns and flickering stones to discourage anything that might trespass. Their dwellings are sparse within; warmth is managed by piled hides, no hearths that smoke, only stones heated and wrapped. There are no idols; belief here comes in patterns, in the deliberate setting aside of space, in food tithed to the shadows and the hollows, in what is not touched. Trade—when it happens—is done by leaving, not by taking: one places a crafted thing in the open space between houses, steps far back, waits as the intended recipient approaches, the object dissolving into their use with no word exchanged. To approach too close, to overlap, is taboo. Intimacy takes the form of choreographed proximity—sitting just out of arm’s reach, touching only through objects passed delicately, recognition expressed by mirrored posture, never by contact.

What governance exists is distributive: decisions ripple through gesture, a seated circle widely spaced in the moss-soft dusk, new rules or warnings mapped in twigs or pebbles, then erased so the ground returns to its preferred ambiguity. Every night a watch is kept, not for predators, but to mark the signals that move across the bowl: light patterns in the fungus, the rare click that flutters in the air. The people do not trespass on those signals; if a click echoes, everyone pauses, listening, remembering—a lost child must yield to a beetle’s path; a forager waits for moss to resettle before breaking off even a single frond.

*Other Presences:*

The animal multitude rules in number, their partitioned territories as careful as the humans’. The velvet-antlered watchers—creatures neither wholly mammal nor fungus—move fluidly between copses, never grazing an area already marked by another’s damp print. Their eyes, green-veined and slightly unfocused, notice the humans as just another absence, just another buffer to circumnavigate. Insect clouds pulse and whirl, the beat of their wings a kind of moving silence, their flight paths mapping invisible grids. Only at the springs—those geometries of water too strange for comfort—does emptiness concentrate, encircled by the stillness of all things, animal and human alike.

Even the plants perform agency. Ferns tilt their leaves to shade no path but their own, fungi withdraw color as footsteps near. Mosses coil and broadcast only to their kind, their convulsions met with respectful distance, never investigation. An ecology of constraints, evolved in parallel—no single species dominates, none ever blots out the others; survival here is a matter of never pressing too close, of thriving in the corridor of possibility left by every other life.

Something older abides, almost not alive—a presence betrayed by the bowl’s very shape, in the strictness of spaces, the echoes of that resonant click, the law that makes emptiness as vital as form. The people do not question it aloud, shaping their myth instead through vigilance: all prosperity here is a loan, measured by the space one leaves unoccupied.

*Summary:*

So—if you ask what lives here, it is not a who, but a choreography:

- The humans: careful, unassertive architects of the margin, practicing a culture of silent respect and deliberate exclusion.
- The animals: segmented, beautiful, their populations thrumming against each other but never mixing, surviving by obedience to invisible barriers, aware of the bowl’s wider pulse.
- The flora: exuberant but never invasive, arranged as if by rule, participating in the pattern of separation.
- And underneath, perhaps an ancient law, or a force living precisely in the emptiness between, that which governs but never speaks, attended by all, owned by none.

Here, life is vibrant, but it is the spaces *between* lives that let abundance persist. To dwell here is to become a student of absence, thriving in the hush where all presences—human, beast, and green thing—agree to keep within their measured bounds.
