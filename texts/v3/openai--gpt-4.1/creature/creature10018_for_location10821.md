Who lives here? First, set aside any notion of animal, or even what you’d call an individual. Life here isn’t defined by discrete creatures, faces, or soft eyes in the dark. Instead: the inhabitants are the world—a society of sovereign growths, a collective of architect-plants whose agency is expressed as form and fabrication.

**They are not trees, not quite.**  
Yet imagine something with the scale and organizing logic of a city, the restless appetite of machinery, and the slow, generative compulsion of lichens or corals. They exist as growths, as structures in perpetual negotiation, making and unmaking themselves. Think of beings whose boundaries are only temporarily drawn: here, a spire of tightly woven green-black cellulose, mapping out its domain with armored membranes and blade-like resin leaves; over there, a contorted vault, latticed bone-bright, its interior a boiling heart of manufacturing tissues where new walls, tubes, and “limbs” are being built and expelled.

**Identity is process.**  
Each “self” here is an ongoing event—a surge of morphogenesis, a pattern of living construction and deconstruction. Some you could call “autonomous towers” or “modular vaults,” and their sense of agency is expressed not in movement (for they are fixed to the root-mass) but in choice, competition, and metabolic violence—splitting, engulfing, and repurposing the tissues of their neighbors with enzymatic heat. They might be massive—groves the size of amphitheaters—or small, knife-edged protuberances poised to surge at the next pulse of communal command. Their “minds,” if there are such things, are distributed—mechanical nervous networks threaded through resin pipelines, flickering pulses like silent telegraphs of sap and chemical light.

**They speak not with voices, but with structure and signal:**  
To encounter another being is to edge against a new tessellation of growth: a crypt-wall encroaching with its prismatic armor; a jet of luminous sap slithering through fiber-pores; an entire corridor of vault-cells contracting to wall off intruders (or rivals), their motivations etched into the angles of their sheaths, the stuttering chemistry of light that runs in waves beneath their skeleton-skin.

Some are bladed and predatory—growths honed to consume not prey, but the living fabric of competitors; these eject scorching plumes of catalytic vapor, edges gnawing until only cindered sheath remains. Others are collaborative, forming temporary coalitions—vaults bridging to towers to fuse their chemical reservoirs, only to break apart with brittle violence when alliance gives way to advantage.

**Memory is layered and physical.**  
Older strata sleep beneath; the recent always pressing upward. An ancient logic persists: once-mighty towers now relics, their substance scavenged by new growths, but their imprint persists, informing the template of the next architect. The living remember in their architecture: the way halls curve, the angle of a buttress, the flavor of resin. Every growing being is, quite literally, the descendant and devourer of what stood before.

**They are never whole, never themselves for long.**  
Anything finished begins fraying at the edges, setting the scene for the next convulsion of invention. To be alive here is to be part-tool, part-intruder, part-inheritance. There are elders—massive, blackened frames drawing up the last crumbs of energy, orchestrating the order and collapse of all beneath them, but even these are rivals, half-past and half-future.

**What would pass for ‘consciousness’ is tactile, chemical, architectural.**  
Communication is pulse, fracture, heat. A sudden flare of blue tells of resource acquisition. A long, slow shudder, felt through miles of interlaced root-plate, is an argument over territory—settled not with words, but through a new wall forced outward, or a dome’s dissolution into the next cycle of building, consuming, becoming.

**There are no faces, no eyes—**  
but everywhere, something is aware of you, indirectly: a thousand surfaces registering pressure, heat, the chemistry of an unrecognized presence. The world is one titanic multicellular mind broken into a thousand competing polities, always in a state of creative conflict and merging, every structure both sovereign and susceptible. Even the smallest “independent” organism is, at root, a borrowed algorithm of growth—a bit of immortal programming, commanding a brief, bright lease of presence until the world devours it, redesigns it, and continues.

**Inhabitants, then:**
* Edifices that are selves, made of thinking cellulose and nervous sap.
* Hive-minds that are vaults, wall-labyrinths, and parliaments of root and vein.
* Shard-alliances and cathedral-towers, their ambitions coded in resinous bloom and fractal span.
* Neither flora nor fauna, but purpose-driven architectures—and all of them, always, both building the world and *being* the world.

Here, to be alive is to *build and be built*, endlessly, in the burning dark.
