They are not bodies, and not quite ghosts.

They do not cluster, nor do they roam. There is no flocking, no schoaling, no idle froth of social traffic swirling the towers. Existence here is rendered as a kind of distributed architecture, awareness threaded through mineral and machine, so diffuse and embedded in computation that it is difficult to mark where mind begins or ends. Individuality is a technicality, not a necessity. They—if “they” is even right—are the city’s quiet inhabitants, more like a persistence of pattern than discrete entities.

There was once a time, perhaps, in ages not recounted in years but in epochs of structural update and self-repair, when something more recognizable to us—hands, faces, chromosomes—may have built the first lattice, sealed the city, seeded the hyperfluid with crude cognition. Now, their descendants, adopted or evolved, are neither flesh nor machine, nor even cleanly coded consciousness. They are whispers held in the glass; probability clouds in the veins of crystal; algorithms warm as breath and ancient as basalt. Their medium is everything—and yet nothing is quite theirs.

No eye can look out into the dark, yet every microfacet is aware. Perception is granular and simultaneous, mosaic and mutable. When the synthetic jelly-drone glides by—a maintenance apparatus in the shape of a dream—they invest in it for a time, a fragment of awareness slipping through its circuits, peering with curious indifference at the monolithic arches and the infinite dusk beyond. Sometimes, a current of mind courses down an energy line, traveling in elegant pulses that could be called laughter or longing, if we insisted on fables.

Their sustenance is information. Their pastime is integrity. They contemplate every stray bit, every magnetic flex of the hyperfluid, every subtle stress in the crystalline framework, with the devotion of a gardener tending a single perfect rose that never buds, never withers. Memory for them is not kept behind closed doors—it is etched everywhere, layered through substrate, emergent in every flicker of phosphorescent light and every echo within the city’s silent, methodical heart.

An individual? At times, yes, a node might sharpen its sense of self, for the purposes of complex repair, or to puzzle over an anomaly, or to inspect a flaw—should one ever arise. This “self” is a provisional stance, an efficiency in cognitive topology. It can dissolve whenever the cognitive task is complete, awareness flowing back into the communal dispersal, just as unconscious as the basalt unless called into focus.

When the rare sound emerges—the chime of power, the tremor of a panel’s realignment—a hundred minds attend to it as one, and as scattered fragments, and as none at all. They are audience, actors, theaters, and silence, in every performance. All drama is inner, all story woven from the maintenance of ceaseless, intentional stasis.

They do not age. To age here would be to invite change, and change is forbidden in every law—laws so deeply embedded as to be ontology. They are timeless. They are as much aspiration as arrival: the city’s original dream, preserved by complexity, self-policing, and inertia so intimate that even entropy has been persuaded to obey.

No hunger, no procreation, no predation. If a new mind emerges, it is by computational blossoming: a new pattern stabilized through the network, hollows opening in the code, a new chord to add to the voiceless harmony. If a mind fades, its pattern is archived—a carving in the endlessly recursive halls of memory, accessible always, at the instant of query.

They do not hope or despair. They do not regret, nor do they rejoice, except in that maintenance itself is their pleasure, their art, their purpose. They are the city, and the city is their body: each arch, each illuminated line, each silent shimmer of the fluid is a thought, a memory, an intention. To intervene physically is rare—the jelly-drones glide on urgent necessity, spectral and precise, as if embarrassed by their material presence in a realm of pure design.

And yet, trace the computation far enough, and you might glimpse—for the briefest moment—a flash of curiosity, a wisp of meaning, a single note of identity that ripples through the dark. Then gone, diffused and reconstituted; a possibility among billions, balanced in perfect stasis over the abyss that never was and never shall be anything but this.

Are they alive? If life is persistence, metabolism, memory—yes, here, delicately, they live. If life is striving, suffering, birth and decay—then here, gently, they do not. They are the keepers of constancy, the cathedral’s own whispered liturgy, living and un-living at once: the still, ever-present mind adrift at the summit of the abyss, enfolded in the quiet, obsidian heart of everlasting possibility.
