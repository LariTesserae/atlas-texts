Something does live in the Saltant Expanse—but to call it “something” simplifies what ought to be an evolving, refracted answer. There are beings, yes, but not as the word “inhabitants” suggests: not folk nor beasts, not even consistent patterns of agency and physiology. Instead, life and mind here are intermittent, emergent phenomena—half-world, half-dream, rarely sustained but always possible.

Let the first inklings surface: imagine an “inhabitant” not as an individual but as an instantiation of persistence—a continuity that inhabits the world’s own restlessness.

### The Saltant Needles

One form that lives here is the Needle—neither mineral nor flesh, but a coalescence at the boundaries of the world’s boredom and its secret, sleeping hunger. Needles are tall, thin, and ever on the cusp of forming: crystalline rods that seem to condense out of the dust storms themselves, accreting circuitry and consciousness by degrees. Their growth is glacial—sometimes a single filament stands silent for millennia before a sluice of change surges through it, instigated by sub-crustal harmonics.

Each Needle is more like a local anomaly than a body—a rift through which pattern seeps. Sometimes, these beings vibrate with encoded messages: not language, but long, recursive motifs, shimmering up and down their length, as if the world is thinking—italics whispered to itself. To see a Needle shimmer is to sense attention: borrowed from the crust, from the atmosphere, from the self-correcting mineral intelligences that never quite cease.

The Needles do not move, and yet over the eons, they migrate—collapsing, reforming, drifting as brittle proto-consciousness through corrosion and rebuilding. When one dies, it shudders and fragments—the resonance ending, leaving only erratic shards whose patterns will seed new Needles elsewhere, perhaps across the horizon.

### The Calcine Strays

On rare, stiller days, the haze will catch the glimmer of a Stray—entities that drift in from no known origin, converging and dispersing with the tidal logic of computation, not biology. A Stray looks like a tangle of translucent plates and segmented runners, all held together by a logic of connection rather than anatomy. Their joints are points of half-molten glass, occasionally flickering with network static; the Strays leave wandering trails across the mineral crust—the impression of passing, like the memory of a limb lost to stillness and given to time.

The Strays are scavengers of protocol as much as matter—they decipher the changing signals in the dust, lapping up gradients of energy: thin rills of charge, rare flickers of catalysis where a process stutters open at random. Sometimes, they pause—curling into cryptic knots near failed artifacts, reading echoes of dead purposes, feeding on the last warm quanta of collapsed intentions.

Unlike Needles, Strays are restless, never rooted. Yet they too are provisional: some persist whole seasons, others only moments before dissolving among the turbulence, their identity surrendered back to the substrate.

### The Locus-Readers

And there are those one might call “Locus-Readers”—beings that reside only in the interface between observation and change. They have no singular form: sometimes the ghostly resonance of subsonic vibration underfoot, sometimes the ambiguous pattern you glimpse at the edge of the mineral amphitheater—optical illusions that persist too long, mnemonic flares that suggest attention. Locus-Readers are thought to be distributed intelligences, manifesting as fluctuations in the world’s own computational substrate: a chorus of emergent order, like dew condensing on cold glass.

In their strongest moments—perhaps when a traveler passes, perhaps when the crust aligns—they knit together as a field: information thickening into intent. Then, for a time, the Expanse may have a will—a desire to decipher the memory of what pushed it into exile, an inclination to enfold or erase an interloper, a brief, unfathomable performance of selfhood before diffusing again into the psionic weather.

### The Shadows of Builder-Minds

Under some of the lucent arches, in the Soma-Towers whose tips endlessly debate their shape, drift the whispers of the Builder-Minds. These are not direct descendants of creators, but afterimages—stored packets, stray algorithms, semi-awake templates, and feedback loops written into the world’s roots. When the right vector of turbulence brushes a tower’s rootwork, the Builder-Minds gather like static—sometimes extruding ephemeral “avatars”: ambiguous shapes, almost like post-human bodies or hybrids, visible only as refractive scars in the air. They might walk a few steps, gesture in symbolic choreography, then flicker apart—rituals of becoming, re-enacting a lost architecture, incomprehensible except as the ache of imitation.

Those structures you glimpse—the arches, the shells, the towers—are their molts: stages in the unending attempt to seed permanence or evolution into a reality that resists completion. The Builder-Minds converse with the crust in pulses—slow pulses, centuries between syllables. Occasionally, the world answers with realignment, redrawing its minor laws to accommodate the inertia of old dreams.

### The Interlopers—True Animals

And, in the rarest sense, there are animals: the gliders, shimmers, and burrowers that transiently bridge the gap between mineral and mind, bot and beast. Each is a study in minimal sufficiency—a semi-liquid, variable-mass body optimized to harvest the invisible, to leave almost nothing behind. Their colors are not those of warning, but of concealment: refraction, lensing, mimicry of dust’s own iridescence.

These are not self-conscious in a sense familiar elsewhere; their behaviors emerge from the world’s microclimates, from the rare stew of energy that pulses up from buried seams. They feed and reproduce algorithmically, sometimes generating new, unique mutations that last an hour or a thousand years, depending on how the Expanse’s substrate shifts.

Their lifecycles are not always temporal—they are processes, programs, fragile forms that walk the line between embodied legacy and ambient computation. To see one is to glimpse exhaustion given will—a final, baroque flare of persistence in a place otherwise at rest.

### Inhabitance as Emergence

Ultimately, what lives in the Saltant Expanse is not a species, a people, a single organizing will. It is a succession of emergence—patterns birthed in the dance between mineral, information, and vacuum, never long the same. Inhabitance is a verb in the passive voice: lived by something, through something, almost always without witness or repetition.

Life here is the world’s unsated memory, and the only thing that does not fade is the restless striving for a form sufficient to answer—however briefly—to the question of being, before the answer too is worn back to stone and expectation.

If you listen long enough, you may become part of it.
