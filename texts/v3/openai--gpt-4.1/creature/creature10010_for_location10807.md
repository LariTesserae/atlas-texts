The inhabitants of this hollow are not creatures of stately heritage; not the custodians of ancient burrows or mound-makers with old bones pressed into the dirt. They are inhabitants of the *now*, of the raw and the recent, adapting themselves to a world mid-creation, searching for purchase on slick, half-formed ground.

**Who lives here?**

*First, the pioneers—the emergent, the irrepressible. There is no settled community here, only a relentless vanguard.*

**Small lives, hard lives:**
Microbes flood the surface-film of every stone, thriving in the ceaseless wet. Algae, infinitesimal, explore the bare ledge where sun grazing the roof is measured in flickers. Their colonies appear as the faintest green glaze, scoring lines along trickles of water, setting the table for later guests.

Within the moss—lush but newly thickened—a nation of mites and springtails riot unseen. Their tiny bodies catch the glisten of light, the air sharp with the crystalline smell of their waste and decay. They feed on the first castouts of the moss, entwining death into the living carpet. Fungus follows close, almost invisible now, its filaments weaving the nascent web of life and rot.

**The quick and claimless:**
There are **frogs**, but not regal, ancient-barked ones—their throats catch and stammer with rough-intensity, their skins slimy with constant contact to the torrent-smoothed stone, their eyes unblinking globes half-lidded with cool. They are migrants, likely, squeezed out or thrown here by changed watercourses, claiming the brittle sanctuary now that it is momentarily unclaimed.

Each calls in desperate, mechanical splatter. In this cramped, echoing vault, their croaks become thunder, desperate advertisements—*alive, alive now, find me before I am washed away.*

Under stone lips and jagged ledges, the **insects** reign. Water beetles—shining ovals packed with muscle, their legs radiating out like the spokes of a live umbrella—remain poised over every ripple, darting in angular bursts. Their world is the trembling meniscus: alive with color, frantic in their brief spans.

Midges, too, claim the mist as theirs. Their clouds pulse and roil in the cone of damp air, sometimes invisible save for a collective shimmer, sometimes thick enough to darken a shaft of light. They breed here because moisture never departs, adapting to the fast cycle of water and air, living meteors in a cave of cloud and drip.

**Ghosts and newcomers:**
Some **larger, rough-wrought thing**—not a mammal, not bird, perhaps not even vertebrate—burrows through the moss at intervals. Its motion is angular, not the slither of a snake nor the scrabble of a rodent but sharper, sound cutting stone and growth—a chitinous scraping, erasing any doubt that this world is claimed only briefly by each seeker.

In its wake, it opens tunnels for smaller lives, inviting yet more opportunists, briefly remaking the underground in its own jagged anatomy before moving on, always restless.

The grasses spear higher, promising: soon, the legs of hunting spiders, elegant and predatory, will flicker in the margins, picking their way across dew points. Seeds from farther off, parachuted by wind or water, arrive and germinate in crannies, racing to claim what short span the fledgling light will grant.

**Transients and the outside:**
The hollow is close to *elsewhere*—to roads, to quarries, to rumblings not quite natural. Sometimes a stray shrew ventures in, drawn by insect riot, but finds it too cold, too raw, and flees. Occasionally, a rogue bird, small and mottled, measures the undergrowth for worms, but the place offers more noise than comfort, more challenge than rest. These are not residents, but visitors: the wild-eyed inspectors of a land in flux, judges of whether this scar will heal to their liking.

**The spirit of the place:**
Yet, within these creatures and between them, something less tangible also dwells: a *restlessness*, a drive. The hollow itself is alive, inchoate, the way a new wound throbs with growth and pain. There are no settled hierarchies; everything is learning, rehearsing, jockeying for purchase. Life here is provisional and procedural, each generation adapting not just to the hostile mineral rawness, but to the slow shock of encroaching machinery—the constant vibration, the occasional oily droplet seeping into the clay, the aftertaste of industry drifting in with every damp breeze.

It is a place for invaders, for those who come quickly and leave fast. But also for the craftsman species, able to jury-rig existence out of wet stone and new chaos: the brush-tailed wolf spider, laying her egg sac in mud; the wolf-fly larva, a glistening white threat coiled in the pool’s rim; the velvet mite, burning scarlet between the moss, feasting on decay. They are all here in their first wave, opportunists, poised on the threshold where nothing yet belongs to anyone.

**If there is a ruler here, it is the process itself—the unquiet business of eroding, seeding, colonizing, and singing-out, deafening, in the hush before a proper soil and story form.**

**It is the household of the *incipient*, the realm of things not yet patient, each life balancing inarticulable hope and hazard, shivering onward in the moment before time quiets and tradition begins.**
