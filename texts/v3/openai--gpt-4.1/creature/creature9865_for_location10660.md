They—if the word even holds meaning here—are not born, and they do not die as living things do. They emerge, accrue, dissolve, and are scavenged, built of and consumed by the ceaseless flux. One might try to picture them as a swarm, but the word fails, not vast enough, not intricate enough. What exists in the Fuming Hive-Fissure are populations, nations, perhaps even civilizations, but none with memory as we know it, none with story carried through time. There is no spoken word, no image carved into the stone, only the everlasting pressure of being one among millions in an intimacy that is not communal, but existential—sharing every surface, sensing every fracture.

**THE SMALL AND THE MANY**  
Most are small: faceted bodies, lengths varying from a finger to a wrist to an arm’s span, their outlines half-given in the shifting red and violet gloom. Imagine a beetle, yes, but fused from glassy plates, surfaces puckered from mineral growth and worn smooth by eons of friction. Each is jointed, segmented, built to interlock with others, to crawl, to press, to become a tessellation in motion. Many sport crystalline setae—hairlike sensors—probing for heat differentials, for chemical traces left on the creases of stones.   
Their undersides pulse light—a code of hunger or intent, coherent only to those that brush and pass it on. Occasionally one will split, right where segments meet: its mineral flesh crumbles, and new forms slip from the breach, quick to scuttle or slide or fuse into the nearest mass. Their “faces” are blank: perhaps an orifice or a cluster of pits, shiny with the promise of touch, of message, of exchange.

**THE HUNTERS**  
Among them sluice the predators—sleek, elongated, sheathed in bands of mirrored crystal so pure they seem carved from frozen lightning. These are solitary, never clustering, always moving with inexorable calm. They ripple through the world as pale veins in darkness, flattening to squeeze through razor cracks or unfurling into serpentine forms. When they find prey, they stop, arch, and press along the back of their chosen. There is a moment where two luminescences pulse together, a silent conflict, and then the hunter’s shell gleams brighter, soaking energy, while the unlucky dims, collapses, and is quickly disassembled by the crush of a million scavengers.

These hunters never leave marks but the absence of glow and the subtle sadness of rearranged, cooler stones.

**THE GREAT BODIES**  
Sometimes, within the towering hive-lattices or curled beneath domes of fused glass, one finds beings of immense size—bodies the size of bears, perhaps, but their dimensions seem distorted, endlessly prismatic, almost dimensional errors. They do not often move; they are nodes of persistent presence, in whom dozens, hundreds, sometimes thousands of smaller forms are embedded or clustered. They pulse with directed luminescence, arranging the flows of their kind around them, like architects or conductors—but they hold no consciousness that could be reached. Memory is held in the shape and rhythm of their encysted mass, memories made not of thought but of endlessly repeated movements, undulations, positions, and fusions.

Are these ancestors? Queens? Or simply slower, more ancient patterns—buildings made of life, drifting closer and closer to bodiless stone?

**THE CORALLINE BUILDERS**  
Occasionally, latticework emerges—brittle, crystalline walls and ridges, built through nothing but generations pressing bodies together until mineral traces accrete and fuse, then crumble as the flow of heat shifts. No builder ever plans these structures; they are an emergent product of millions' bodies aspiring to optimal warmth and safety—yet when the hive masses drift away, the walls remain, mapped anew by each oncoming tide.

**THE GHOSTS**  
Some forms are extinct but not entirely gone: transparent shells lodged forever in fractures, their patterns migrated into new flesh but remembered in the shape of passageways or the stilled dance of a silent chamber, never quite empty, always edged by the past’s presence.

**SENSING AND COMMUNICATION**  
They have no eyes. Instead, they “see” only what is pressed against or grazed—tiny eruptions of heat, shivers of chemical contact, light exchanged through phosphorescent streaks, making the world a crowded, tactile maze of desire, warning, and resonance. The entire population is a chain of signals sliding over signal, an orchestra of mute need and nameless identity.

**CONSCIOUSNESS—IF IT EXISTS**  
No one of them is an individual in the way we mean. They are moments in a continuum, slots in a logic circuit built of warmth and pattern, existing briefly, their existences summed only in swarm. Each is a node of a million-fold hive: each is replaceable, each is a memory held in touch. Their histories are written not in words, but in the changing architecture of passageways and heat-gatherings, in who pressed against whom, where, and when.

**IF YOU MET ONE**  
Were you to meet one—a form unfolded from the churning mass, trailing heat and faint light between its shell-seams—it would not see you, not know you, not even register you if you lacked warmth and mineral tang. If you touched it, it might pause, pulse color—transmit some message of question or warning—and move on. You, not built of stone and absence and orderly dissolution, would be neither threat nor meaning. You would be invisible, unthinkable.

**THEIR WORLD**  
They are the churn of life built from friction, contact, heat, and mineral form. Each is both inheritor and ancestor, maker and remnant—a glimmer in the endless, unceasing tumult. They are the keepers of the Fuming Hive-Fissure’s only memory, the substance of its silence, and the restless tenants of its never-sleeping, always crowded dark.
