Who lives here? It is an answer that flows and complicates itself with every breath a visitor might take, with every step risked onto the breathing, shifting mats of matter that coat the stony world-in-progress. Nothing here is named, not in the tongue of any aboveground life; everything is unnamed except by the work it does upon its neighbor and the marks it scars into stone or silence. Yet something can be said for the kinds of living that make this feverish realm their home.

Some are singular for only an instant, matters of mere seconds, birthed by a chemical marriage in a drip of condensation, unfurled from mineral gels in a shimmer of heat and slickness. These ephemerals—bodies made of bubbles rimmed with chitin-glass, or pure pushes of replicated signal that read as a temporary organism—come and go in the gap between the crumbling of a ledge and the closing of a fissure, their legacy only the noises they emit and the molecules they add to the next cycle. They swarm in plumes, dyeing the air with transient fluorescence and call, all impulse and response, brief as hope and gone.

Others have permanence—relatively speaking. These are the burrowers, the enduring. Some pass as creatures at a glance, but on closer examination reveal themselves to be colonial bodies: mineral-muscled tubes ribboning through cracks, sliding their crystal-tipped maws forward, extruding whorls of bristles along their flanks, bodies flexing in geometric locks as if each was still uncertain of its form. Where they move in numbers, the stone itself is reworked in their image—corridors lined with the glittering waste of their passage, exoskeletal leavings curling off like wood shavings beneath the blade of a rough sculptor. Their “faces” are indistinguishable from their ends; they sense by taste, by vibration, by the pressure of new air in a tunnel. They eat the heat; their flesh is laced with ore, their movement a slow, grinding hunger.

Clinging atop these are the opportunists, the grazers—carpets of creatures whose thin skins refract, whose bodies are more membrane than meat, organized around volatile digestion and the swift theft of nutrients. They ride the big burrowers, snatch at every drifting spore, and scatter in wind-like shivers when washed by brief mists. When danger comes—signaled by a sudden pressure wave or shock of noise—they fuse, momentarily, into a single membrane, distorting into reflective skeins that slip into cracks or become nearly invisible.

There are the predators, too—the extractors, harvesters, clippers with cunning. They are as various as the rocks they haunt: razor-limbed and jointless, sliding flat as sheets along walls and over the backs of their prey, sudden unfurlings of gloss and edge. Some wield matter as tool—a sharp-edged plate gripped in a looped limb, a bauble of toxic mineral anointed on a spine, an organized clutch of singing-shells strung along a lurker’s back, broadcasting warning and lure alike. Theirs is a violence of specialization: a jawless mouth that plunges, a tongue that bores, a filament that slices.

On the air itself—if one can speak of air with so much suspension and haze—swim the filterers: nets of sooty gossamer, webs stretched between stalactitic extrusions, harvesting spore-things and particulate motes as the currents wax and wane. These webmakers may have bodies: tense, coalescent nodes, beadlike, glowing faintly, or perhaps they simply *are* the web, intelligence smeared long as a breath but quick to respond, signaling in the way a pulse travels through silk.

And somewhere within the churning swarm, too, are the crafters. They are not dominant or numerous; their works are rare, their presence always more suggested than confirmed. Yet the marks—alignments in mineral, circles etched painstakingly, piles of matter clearly selected—bespeak a resident, or a visitor, with some other kind of ambition. Maybe it is a solitary builder, many-limbed and many-eyed, its hands or their analogues tipped with claws of living crystal or patches of searing acid. Perhaps it changes shape, reconfigures its grasp and slide with each need, is never the same twice, and does not remember its works between iterations. It might remember by arrangement, by the taste of tool against rock, by a vibration echoing in a mark left ages (hours) ago.

There are whispers, too—suggestions of something more communal, hinted at by repeating patterns in illumination and noise, moments when the chaos stills into synchronization, if only fleetingly: hatching waves coordinated, choral warnings organized, lights pulsing in distant, meaningful rivers. These swarms, organized and dissolved in an hour, are nobody and everybody; they are the continent’s way of dreaming itself forward, making islands of intention amid the usual riot.

Life here is not ancient, not settled, not finished; it is a tide of invention within a world still waking up. There is no single ruler, no peace, and not one face to be found: only masks, tools, flashes, colonies, shivers, hungers. Each heartbeat, some new combination emerges, brief master of a niche, turning even the stones hot and the air thick with unspent desire.

Who lives here? Everything that can, and anything that tries. No names endure. Only the relentless, improvisational negotiation of being, weaving the first rough tapestry of a yet-unwritten world.
