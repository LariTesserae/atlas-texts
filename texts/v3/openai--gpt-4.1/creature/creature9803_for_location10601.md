They are not quite animal, and not quite plant—but should you want to imagine those who dwell here, begin there. They are what happens if a moss-wreathed city dreamed people into being, needing hands to tend its slow machinery of root, breath, and leaf. Or: they are seedlings seeded not in earth but in lineage, a pattern handed down by touch through generations, root-bound to this buried world.

**The Deep-Dwellers**

Their bodies are thin—arching, flexible, jointed in places you do not expect. They move hunched, unspooling themselves within the curves and hollows of giant roots. Their skin—if you call it that—gleams with a living green-grey sheen: tough as bark on the backs of their limbs, yielding and wet at the underparts, furred here and there with filter-soft moss that gathers water from the air. Embedded nodes glimmer with the faintest luminescence, and intricate swirls of luminous pigment (spore-paint? Subdermal fungus?) serve as signals in the pitch-black. 

Their faces are mask-like, strangely composed. Eyes pale, almost colorless, huge to drink the little light—reflecting fungal pulses and the flash of distant bioluminescent flares as quicksilver. Above the eyes, leaf-blades, spines, or branching fungus-crowns, cultivated intentionally—each complex, unique, a glyph of clan and purpose. Some append antlers of living vine or hunched moss to their heads, some veil themselves with woven leaf and cobweb, others remain bare save for a crawling, symbiotic lichen bloom.

Their breathing—listen!—is not quite human. It is a composite sound: the pulse and hush of air woven with the wet flutter of inner fronds, a slow exhalation as much for feeding symbiotic moss as for their own lungs. They drink water by standing in sudden pools, letting it wick up through trailing roots at their feet, or slaking thirst from cupped moss and luminous berries.

**How They Live**

They dwell in the narrow inbetweens; their homes are not built but *grown*, where root-knots lean in, where a mat of moss thickens just so, where a hollow offers warmth and condensation. Each selects and tends a living niche, guiding roots to curve and knot into borders, lacing moss with their own threadbare weave. Their structures are humble in the grand scheme, but persistent, always retreating from outright dominance, favoring harmonious growth.

They are cultivators rather than conquerors—pruners of growth, shapers of passage and air. Their culture is slow, inward, loving the tiny gradients of wet and dark, the tuning of fungal bloom and root-bridges. At their touch, a branch bends over centuries; a mycelium sings across the dark and guides a whole wall to thicken or recede by morning.

Their tools are inherited—pieces of chert, horn, and bone encrusted with lichen, used to coax or score the living walls, to divide berries, scrape moss, or (rarely) for defense. Metal is unknown, perhaps taboo, for its presence would poison this place.

They travel rarely, moving in single file through low corridors, each marking the path with tendrils of scent, or a brush of glowing dust. Sound travels poorly here, so they speak with gestures: the flick of a finger-blade, the press of bio-luminous palm to root, a ripple translated through the moss for another to feel in the dark. Conversation is chemical—through exchanges of spore, sweat, and the mingling of symbiotic organisms on skin. All the air is message and reply, life cycling between mouths and lungs and funneling back into the root-bodies of the world.

**Ritual and Memory**

The deep-dwellers remember with gardens, not words. Ancestry is grown in a particular lattice of fungi on a favored ceiling, songlines spread through curving groves of lichen, stories tended in patterns of bloom and fade, scent and silence. Their funerals are not graves but propagation: the body merged into a wall or root, feeding and becoming the architecture from which later generations scrape pigment and water.

At rare times—the passing of a long season, a cycle of bloom—gatherings form at the widest hollows. They are silent festivals, marked by darkness, bursts of dazzling bio-luminescence, fronds and bracts flaring in unison. The deep-dwellers come together, brush and braid living crowns, share spores and tell stories through patterns of taste and touch.

**Their Place Among Other Life**

The city belongs to life, not to any singular people. The deep-dwellers make their own way, but know themselves as guests—caretakers, not owners, ever negotiating treaties with the crawling multitude. For every thriving hollow, they displace a hundred smaller animals; for every root-node they shape, a colony of crawling, scuttling things adapts or flees.

Predators? Some. To these, the deep-dwellers are another motion in the fabric, neither chief nor servant, but mutually aware. When threatened, they collapse themselves into the architecture—flattening among moss, vanishing against bark, bodies flowing along root-corridors as if built of root and water. Their best defense is disappearance.

**Variations and Outliers**

Not all are described by this outline. Some are altogether stranger—blind, pale offshoots who never leave a single hollow, so interwoven with fungus and root they are less mobile than tree, living as stationary minds in the dark. Others travel farther, sending rootless offspring beyond, carrying slivers of lichen and fungus to seed elsewhere, pushing the city’s edge outward by inches, centuries at a time.

**Essence**

In the end, those who live here are neither prisoners nor rulers. They are *component and curator*, their will expressed in the sinuous interplay of plant and animal in an unending slow-motion pulse. To foreign senses, they are ghosts, or figments, things between—half-real, half-imagined, part of the life that suffuses the hum, damp, and hush of the buried world.

They dwell in the dark, gleaming just at the boundary between flesh and leaf, as much dream as creature, their every gesture filtered through moss-breath and the slow, patient architecture of life.
