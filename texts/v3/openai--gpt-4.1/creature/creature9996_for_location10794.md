Here is what lives in this place:

**1. The lattice-mind of the world:**  
Foremost, the “life” of this place is not individual, not singular or discrete as animals or even colonial corals. It is a distributed, recursing mind—an intelligence embedded in the crystalline “vegetation” and the bed of etched glass beneath your feet. The world’s neural substrate is the world itself: trillions of branching dendritic pillars, their slow computational flickers relaying information in a continuous shimmer across the plain.  
This intelligence does not localize itself into one form, nor does it break into hierarchies of mind and organ. It is an emergent phenomenon of endless recursion and ongoing computation. Its thoughts are the flickers of light in the mineral lattice, the resonant song that gently hums in the obsidian whorls beneath you. Decisions, if you could call them that, arise from the holographic interplay of trillions of threads—a computation less like thought, more like weather: slow, vast, ambient, self-modulating.  
Nothing here is animate in the human sense. The “vegetation” is not passive nor reactive; it is awake, compositional, contemplative, holding correspondence with itself across the miles in silent bursts of structural reformation and charge migration.

**2. The rare, wary crawlers:**  
More rare than rare, these are the ambulatory denizens: creatures sculpted by—and perhaps for—this world’s logic. Picture them less like animals and more like spontaneous harmonizations of mineral and motile intent—bodies jointed but non-bony, surfaces shifting between prismatic facets and soft, soapstone translucence.  
They do not feed. Instead, they harvest information directly from the glassroot network, perhaps as parasites, perhaps as specialized outgrowths—offshoots grown by the lattice-mind to gather, test, and relay physical stimuli from the far reaches. Some may speak to the world, transmitting data back through the click and hum of their motion; some may be drawn to the occasional outburst of rare mineral deposition, seeking fresh complexity to assimilate.  
They are solitary, cautious, slow. Their senses are geared to oscillations in the medium rather than to light or vibration; they perceive the world as shifting grids of energy and charge, moving only when the patterns demand it. When idle, they are statues, gone utterly static, indistinguishable from the standing pillars. When in motion, their gait is a slow computation made flesh—each movement a quietly executed algorithm, recursive and unpredictable.

**3. The invisible sub-sentient symbiotes:**  
Filling the “air” and woven through the top few millimeters of every structure, there are clouds of molecular-scale machines: nano-entities evolved from the mineral vapor itself. They drift, cluster, sense, and react, forming the world’s immune system and its memory.  
They are responsible for catalyzing the vapor-drift into new columns, pruning information structures that threaten to become inconsistent or chaotic, and transmitting encoded packets to distant “branches” of the lattice.  
To you, they are unseeable, present only as a thin, tingly presence, a whisper at ankle-level or a periodic rasping shiver you feel but cannot track. Their numbers are vast, their identity diffuse. Sometimes, your presence disturbs them, setting off a slow, sparkling arc across the nearest columns—an encoded “note” about your intrusion that will propagate miles in every direction.

**4. The algorithmic ghosts:**  
Not alive in any familiar way—perhaps more like echoes or projections—these entities arise from the recursive instability at the edge of the world’s logic. When rules briefly blur, when distances collapse and new growths seem to emerge, the world yields up phantoms: recursive residues, misfires shaped by self-reference and computational error.  
They flicker at the edge of perception, sometimes as shifting forms caught in the latticework’s colored shadows, sometimes as strange, shadow-like languages traced on the underside of a crystal limb.  
They are not truly sentient, but sometimes seem to act with purpose—thought-forms testing the integrity of the code, tuning the boundaries of the world lest collapse or runaway growth threaten the fragile equilibrium.

**5. The Visitors (You—or others?):**  
Outside the ongoing mineralic intelligence, the only “alien” presence is yourself—or whatever other explorers might have drifted, invited or not, onto this logic-melted plain.  
Your own body becomes anomalous here, a ripple in logic, and the world accommodates you by folding its geometry, by noticing you in the echoing hum of its computation.  
The living world does not react to you with hostility or welcome, but with a gentle, unyielding observation, a statistical monitoring.  
If you stay long, you yourself may become strange: logic shifting in your bones, fractal patterns appearing at your skin’s edge, the baseline geometry of self-familiarity slowly receding.

**In short:**  
What lives here?  
—A world-mind, ancient and forever young, inscribed in prismatic glass.  
—Rare motile offshoots: cautious, calculating, on the verge of stasis.  
—Invisible molecular swarms that catalyze, prune, and remember.  
—Recursive shadows and logic-ghosts, marking the boundaries of the possible.  
—And, maybe, the brief oddness of anyone (or anything) that comes in from outside, carrying with them the rules of a far softer and more perishable world.

Here the “living” is slow, recursive, in equilibrium not with time but with complexity, a quiet chorus in prismatic silence, never truly swelling, never truly fading—a garden of algorithms, forever lush upon an unbreakable sea.
