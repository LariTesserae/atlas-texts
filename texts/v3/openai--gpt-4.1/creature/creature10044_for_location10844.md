Here, beneath the blinding, immaculate sky, what manner of life is sovereign? Who claims this paradise, forged and suspended, ever-young and perfect, floating between worlds and moments?

First, dismiss the idea of mere animals, of dumb beasts alone. The lizards and jewel frogs are too precise, the placement of the towering cycads too intentional, the ground’s tessellations too deftly wrought. This is not the domain of wild things—but neither, perhaps, is it the domain of ordinary consciousness.

Those who dwell here are not easily witnessed. They are the root-making hands, the unseen steadier of seams, the principle from which all arrangement flows. Let us gather their aspect slowly, from the signatures on their work.

**The Makers and Tenders**

If there are inhabitants here, their presence is more implied than visible—yet it persists, like a low hum beneath the overt silence. Their art is everywhere: the unrotting tiles, the measured water, the perfectly spaced greenery. They do not dominate, nor do they retreat; they have built, then let be. The world is filled with the hush and hush of a civilization that has accomplished its purpose, or abandoned it without disorder.

Visible forms, if ever glimpsed, are subtle, seamless with the place. Shadows are banished by light, yet here and there, the air’s thickness seems to congeal—the suggestion of a shape, a distortion that causes a dome to twitch or fern to flicker in your peripheral vision. Perhaps these are the Tenders: beings so close to the fabric of this land that you cannot distinguish them from it except by what moves when you don’t look, what breathes in the pulse beneath the ground.

If they manifest, it is only as a haze, brighter or wetter than the air, momentarily bent into something jointed and slim, a silhouette half-glimpsed against the radiance. They do not walk, for steps are unnecessary where the world’s patterning is enough—a thought, a brief pulse, disturbances just beneath the surface. If they attend the cycads or stroke the tile rims, it is by rearrangement, a quieting of condensation, a smoothing of a seam, performed with no visible limb or actual action. They are maintenance elevated to instinct, tending not from need but from one unyielding principle: that all remains as new, as if just made.

**The Absent Artificer**

Yet another sense whispers: are the dwellers not present at all? Is this the aftermath of a vanished intelligence—the automaton of a million-year-old mind, whose absence is preserved in the refusal of things to change? In this version, the world itself is the resident: every tile, every plant, every dome an extrusion of memory, a fossilized act of care. The life that persists might be a ghostwork—routine, encoded renewal, mechanisms in the guise of ecology.

**If There Are People**

Suppose, for a moment, there *are* persons here: human, or near enough. To live in this place, they must themselves be forged new each day—reborn, scrubbed clean of age, perpetually at the beginning of their tenure. Their skin would be dewy as the tiles, sweatless in the heat that does not intensify nor relent. Their bodies would bear no scars or callus; they would not grow old. They might tend to the cycads more for remembrance than need, pressing roots into their cradles, adjusting the flow of unseen water, repeating with quiet completeness the gestures of first settlement.

But such people might not speak, for the pressure of the light is too total; words would feel excessive, sounds like an interruption to a perfect pause. They live at the edge of stasis, their faces unclouded by worry, their tasks unhurried, and their memories always new—perhaps even absent, with only the present moment holding weight. They would seldom gather, each given wide intervals, dictated by the scale and dispersal of the land—a society of immense privacy and infinite patience. They know they can alter nothing, so they become companions of the unchanged.

**Other Possibilities**

Or the inhabitants are *architectural* themselves—grown up from the seams, humanoid only in the vaguest sense, like living columns or moving mosaics, rearranging themselves as tasks call, dissolving to seep into a dome and reappear across the plain. Biomechanical, silent, intent, their heads suggestion of great domes themselves, faces blank and open with pores where water drips, their digits long and chisel-sharp to fit tile or plant, never dulling. They are landscape and labor avatar mixed.

Perhaps there are only the creatures, the jewel-lizards and marsh birds—yet, they do not act wholly wild. Their movement and intervals suggest choreography, a deep memory’s pattern: one frog here, one bird there, exactly so, at this hour, every day without deviation. Each follows a script, unremembered, unchanging, their cycles not of evolution, but repetition.

**Essence**

In the end, perhaps the inhabitant is *process*. The world is not only made, but *forever being made*—the ceaseless, invisible labor that keeps light constant, water circulating, tiles unworn, and ferns emerald. The dwelling intelligence, or its echo, suffuses every cubic inch: not a ghost, not a person, but a preserved intention, a presence not in form, but in eternal maintenance. Visitors may sense it as a hush, a felt watching, a suspicion that if they looked away, the world would resume—meaning not eruption or chaos, but a perfection always holding itself together for the next instant, always waiting, endlessly beginning.

**Summary**

So, who or what lives here?  
—The Makers, absent or insinuated, felt as an intention sharper than shadow.  
—The Tenders, never seen directly, intrinsic to the place, a presence folded into function.  
—Or only the *process* of making, an agent that is the endless, seamless recurrence of genesis.

Every life here, visible or not, is bound to this principle: not to grow, not to decay, only to *be made*—over and over, perfect and ready, radiant and poised, until the first story finally begins.
