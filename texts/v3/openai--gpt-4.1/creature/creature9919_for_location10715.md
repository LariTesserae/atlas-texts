You ask who or what lives here, and to answer is to invite hesitation, for the inhabitants of this place are not easily drawn into clarity. They are slivers of persistence and memory, as much woven from what the world used to be as from what remains. Their existence is not entirely a matter of biology, nor quite of spirit, but something that partakes in both, balanced on the cusp where life and unlife echo each other across the chasm of endless stasis.

**The Primary Inhabitants:**

### 1. **The Endurers**

Tall, narrow, jointed things whose bodies are made of a pale, fibrous substance, almost mineral. They resemble clusters of pale reeds twisted into slow motion, each with six limbs that ripple outward and inward, shifting their bulk with glacial patience. At rest, they seem as much a part of the fissured stone as the moss: only the uncertain shimmer of their surfaces—reflecting the thin, pallid light—betrays living matter. Approaching and retreating from the edge of perception, the Endurers live by consuming tiny exudations of mineral and trace moisture, exhaling faint dust that drifts away, only to settle back onto rock. They neither breed nor change; rather, a small number has always existed, and when an Endurer eventually quiets into perfect stillness, another elsewhere stirs as if taking up its place. Their motion is slow as months; their vision, if such a thing could be called that, is merely the patient registering of shifting gradients in cold and sound. They fear nothing and seek nothing more, their entire purpose condensed to inhabiting duration and amplifying the silence.

### 2. **The Flutters & Glimmers**

Now and then, along overhanging ledges or inside deep clefts, things with wings appear—not birds as you know them, but disjunct shapes of bone and bleak feather, or membranous sheets that pulse and shift. Their eyes, if spotted in the gloom, are tiny orbs that seem to collect and hoard whatever faint light exists, so that to look for too long is to see multiple after-images trailing behind them in the mist. These creatures feed on nothing you recognize, occasionally sipping from beads of condensed mist or pecking at crystal crusts. When threatened, they do not flee, but simply become less visible—as if the quality of attention changes, and they fragment into other possibilities. You might sense a suggestion of intent in their gaze, but it is never curiosity nor fear—only a steady, infinite waiting, as if these were the surviving auditors of a vanished age, appointed to bear witness until something new compels them otherwise.

### 3. **The Drifters of the Pools**

In the shallow, mineral-encrusted basins where half-water gathers, sluggish amphibian forms linger. With skins so pale and smooth as to seem wet stone, they have faceless heads and wide, thin mouths. Small limbs splay out, undulating rhythmically, pushing their bodies with half-hearted pulses along the pool’s margins. Their eyes are almost vestigial, but sensitive to the most minute movement or change in darkness. Each movement stirs a faint ring of debris that settles almost immediately, as if gravity is weighted more heavily here. The Drifters do not mate or gestate; instead, on rare occasions, when chill mists are at their most intense, new small forms are found clinging under stones, as if extruded directly by the world. They are the appetite and memory of moisture, content with a near-life that never exceeds the bounds laid for it.

### 4. **The Watchers Beneath**

Deep inside the hollows—within the largest fissures and beneath the ancient terraces—something else exists, hidden from all but the most persistent intrusion. Sometimes, the ground thuds with a resonance not quite accounted for by the ceaseless wind. These hidden Watchers are not creatures in any recognizable sense, but aggregations of living stone: massive, slowly breathing chambers, where mineral and something not quite organic intertwine. Their consciousness is diffuse, a dull awareness of pressure, change, and intrusion. If the old entryways are approached, sometimes a series of rhythmic tones emerges, neither danger warning nor welcome, but a simple, automatic announcement of presence. The Watchers remember the touch of hands, the press of ancient feet, and, via a million tiny echoes, replay the imprints of all things that have passed through. They are as much archive as inhabitant, their “lives” measured in the repetition and rereading of interminable stone data.

### 5. **The Shadows at the Edge**

Most elusive of all are the presences that dwell not in space but between moments: darkness that gathers just deeper than ordinary shadow, pooling in the cracks under ledges and inside old, half-collapsed shelters. To the eye, they are nothing; to the senses, they are an inexplicable thickening, a chill or subtle pressure against the eardrum. At times, the boundaries of these shadow-things billow slightly, as if responding to perception. When the wind keens, they thrum in reply. These beings have no form, no hunger or aim. They are persistent possibilities, left over from when this world was still making choices about what could exist here and what could not. Sometimes, when you are stillest, you may almost hear them remembering—a kind of negative presence, echoing all the things the world once denied itself.

---

**No people abide here.**
No hands shape the stone. No speech rings in the air, nor laughter, nor weeping. All that remains are the inheritors of silence and threshold, the keepers of suspension, emblems of life constrained to flicker but not ignite. They are not lonely, for loneliness implies an unrealized alternative, and this is a place where alternatives were long ago settled, their potential energy stored in stone and fog and sound. Each being is both itself and the echo of what it might have been, fixed in a cycle where nothing truly arrives or departs.

In such a world, to live is not to grow, but to persist; not to strive, but to endure the ringing hush, until, impossibly, something new dares birth itself out of stasis. The lives here are rendered in the slow vocabulary of the elements: stone and water, thin air and restless, ceaseless sound—life braided with absence, presence woven from scarcity and the memory of movement.
