**Who or What Lives in the Cathedral at the Apex Wave**

You ask after the inhabitants, but the Cathedral’s denizens are less a people than an act—less a species than a verb. To see them you must time your gaze to the rhythm of emergence, catching brief forms in the very process of being made and remade.

They are *the Ephemerals*, if they could be called anything at all. Each is less an organism than an occurrence. They flicker: built not for persistence but for the sheer effort of assembly in the teeth of dissolution.

**Their Bodies**
No blueprint is stable here. Each Emergent—a word that is truer than creature—builds itself from whatever raw inventory the Cathedral offers in that gorgeous, shivering moment: mineral crumbs sanded thin, threads of near-moss, a dropped scale from some previous birth. Their shapes are never complete, never the same twice. Sometimes they are long and corded, ribboning through the water’s shearing flows, ends splitting and recombining, each filament feeling for coherence. Sometimes they are armored with sudden crusts of glassy silicate, beetle-skinned, so briefly mighty against the pressure before everything dissolves. Sometimes they are nodules, clusters of nervous tissue and wet crystal, clustered about the ridge as if remembering a root or a bone.

Their constitution is inherited from the chamber: susceptible, agile, almost liquid in mind and membrane. You might see an eye open—a slick, refractive point that both is and dreams of an eye—then veer shut, subsumed by the constant unmaking. Maw, spinneret, paddle-limb, lamp-like photophore: these are as likely to bud as to recede. At times, the very *impulse* to become is more visible than any resulting form: a shifting cloud, a wish to be a creature, enacted before it can finish.

**Their Minds**
To exist in the Cathedral is to live at the tempo of flux. Their awareness is quick, but not continuous. Memory cannot outlast the next current; communication is gestural, enacted in postures, flashes of iridescence, arrangements of root and stone left in the wake of their passage. They leave signals in the fray—a braid of detritus, a turbulence pattern, the brief shadow of their architecture. To another of their kind, if any overlap in moment occurs, this is as much a sentence as a nest: word, beacon, sculpture—all in one, all incomplete.

Some part of their being clings to the act of creation. All “building” within the Cathedral is improvisation, a vote for persistence. Some Ephemerals build shelters that are part projection, part happenstance—a cowl of nerves, a spiral of shell, a tangled sphere. Others perform rituals of repetition, stringing glass spherules or nestling split stones, not so much to survive as to make a mark, however brief. The Cathedral is full of these ghosts of effort.

**Their Behavior**
To survive—or rather, to outlast oneself for longer than a moment—the Ephemerals must constantly re-invent. When the water wrenches them into fragments, what is left is *potential*, distributed through the medium. If favorable eddies return, that latent potential reassembles, a new form sometimes inheriting fragmentary memories, sometimes utterly novel.

They are social only at the threshold. Sometimes, in a rare lull, several Emergent forms will overlap, their borders colliding and knotted together, forming transitory colonies—cataloguing each other in nerve-guided touches and shimmers of light. Short-lived alliances of shape and energy, these gatherings sometimes attempt a collaboration: a temporary defense against the catastrophic, a co-constructed structure, a shared pulse.

Yet more often, the Ephemerals appear alone, their unity with others broken by the flow. Sometimes, terrified after a rough passage, their memory is only panic—other times, the joy in the act of spawning one glimmer more, one pattern more.

**Aspirations, If Any**
The Cathedral does not permit ambition in any sense we would know. The Ephemerals’ only hope is to *persist for a while,* to *etch more deeply into the water’s energy* before their dissolution, perhaps to leave behind a changed configuration—a re-oriented fragment of mineral, a swirl in the current, a proto-opal seed of crystalline intent swept up by the next wave of birth.

One may, briefly, try to build an echo-chamber: layering detritus to shield itself from the bulk of oncoming force, buying a handful more moments to add detail to its architecture or sense more of this strange, luminous world. Sometimes such “builders” give rise to the fragments that will seed the next form; in that sense, their children are not descended so much as *distributed*.

**What Are They Really?**
Are they animals, or the cathedral’s own nascent will? It is impossible to say—the place births those who attempt survival, and in doing so, has taught its materials to flicker with selfhood when the currents quicken right.

Perhaps they are *prototypes*—not just of animal, but of possibility, the first stuttering syllables nature speaks into a toothy world.

**If You Could Speak to One**
For the duration of your question, it would be listening—a configuration of sense-organs, light sensors, a ribbon of nervousness. But as you finished, your words would be swept off, and it would change. Its answer, at best, would be an artifact, a ripple, a fragment turning over in the tossing water—an echo of a voice, reforming again and again with the rhythm of the chamber.

It would want to ask: “How do you survive?” and “What will you do with your moment?” And then: “Will you build with me, in the cathedral of beginnings, before we are swept away?”

**Summary**:  
The Cathedral’s inhabitants are ever-forming, ephemeral beings: not a set people, but the restless urge to become—living at the edge of dissolution, forgetting themselves to become new again, striving to leave some sign (a structure, a pattern, a bit of memory) for the next emergence. They are individual only for a breath, collective by accident, always creating, never lingering. In that turbulent, birthing place, to live is to build, and to build is to hope for a little more time before the waters sweep all clean for the next genesis.
