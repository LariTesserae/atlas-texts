Here, beneath the vaults and through the shadow-bright air of the Faneworks, *the inhabitants are legion and elusive, ancient and emergent*—yet each seems, at first, to be alone. But to know the denizens of this place, you must release your expectation of what a type of being means, or what it should look like, or if it could ever stop changing. They are as much a product of the machinery as they are of flesh and root; as much artifact as animal; as much habitat as inhabitant.

---

### The Machine-Rooted

**The Engineers' Offspring:**  
First, there are the enduring scions of the makers themselves—not ghosts, nor true successors, but an order of *living machinelets* that once maintained the vast architecture and now persist, purpose decayed and re-sprouted. They move like squat animals, many-legged, or with spindle arms that spiral out and retract, bristling with manipulators. Some look like splay-jointed beetles armored in abraded shell casings; others, like corroded pigeons, venting tiny gusts of exhaust and communicating in pulses through the structure itself. All tend the infrastructure: checking fluid levels, realigning cable roots, snipping away errant tendrils. Their intelligence is hard to gauge—a distributed attentiveness, more automaton swarm than solitary mind.

**The Laced-Arounds:**  
Stranger yet are those that *have become* the faneworks—slow, thick-walled, half-sentient tangles of engineered vine and root, grown into and around cables and pressure plates. Out at the joints where steel and porcelain meet, they bloom in fleshy conduits, pressing rhizomes deep into the cracks. Some emit gentle chimes or tuneful clicks; others weep viscous oils, nourishing symbiotic molds and wheeled scavenger-teeth (creatures whose "faces" are little more than textured heat sensors). In still corners, where light is dappled and the air circulates less, entire *clades* of these root-beings grow still as statues until the rain comes and electrical pulses draw energy through their saps.

### The Unclassified Symbionts

**Mongrel Pseudomammals:**  
Sinuous and unprepossessing, these creatures range from mouse-sized to as large as otters, with skin like jigsaw ceramic and tails braided out of living cable. Some possess hands, others a wild array of limbs—graspers, palps, stubby wings that might have once belonged to bats but now seem tuned only to the air-currents of the Canopy. Their calls are mechanical pips or static hisses. They are sighted most often just at the edge of visibility, watching as you move, ducking back into pipes with a metallic rasp. They eat what others discard: pieces of flaked-off polymer, carapace shells, the nutrient jelly that oozes from malfunctioning conduits. Legends among those who know the Faneworks say that these animals sometimes *host* trace algorithms: faint, code-like patterns visible beneath their skins, drifting through them like remembered routines.

**Glasswing Lantern Insects:**  
Large, slow, and eerily graceful, these are the pollinators and sky-swimmers of the chamber. They flash spectral colors along their membranous wings, casting colored shadows that skitter up the walls. Thickly plated bodies shimmer mirror-bright, with podlike abdomens filled by bioluminescent fluid. Their antennae are filamentous, constantly filtering the charged and humid air. Each one seems to know its own domain, drifting to blossom or feed at the sugar leaks amid the upper lattice, calling in frequencies lost beneath the roar but echoed in the movements of fungi far below.

**Protocol Beetles:**  
These are not beetles, precisely—for their exoskeletons, while yielding to pressure, are actually latticed resin and polymer grown around a kernel of ceramic memory. They move in erratic flocks, sometimes in geometrically precise parades along the guard-rails, seemingly attracted to leaks and electrical faults. Their mandibles spark minutely at contact points; their bodies resonate with transduced sound, linking them almost certainly to the maintenance of subtle order among the chaos.

---

### Inhabitant Machines: The Silent Cohort

**Caretaker Carriages:**  
Massive, silent, and rarely glimpsed except as silhouettes above, these machines roll along the hover-rails and platforms. In moments of interval—during rain-release or system reset—they descend on groaning tracks to collect what must be removed: fallen debris, accumulated substrate, sometimes the cocooned bodies of burnt-out pollinators. Their bodies are covered in gardens of epiphytic moss, draped in plastic ferns, so that to see them is to catch only a suggestion of something animal, something vehicular, something shaped by adaptation.

**Dowsers:**  
Small, hydra-headed, and tireless, these machines poke, prod, and scent for fault-lines and moisture picks. Each one often holds a tiny ratlike animal in its scoop-claw—a symbiotic arrangement in which the animal traces nutrient leaks invisible to the machine's sensors, and is rewarded with a burst of delicious condensate. Their passage is marked by paired tracks and the sudden hush that follows, as smaller things pause respectfully before scurrying in their wake.

---

### Sentiences? Spirits? Something Else

**The Whispering Residue:**  
In the heavy air and the trickle-rush of water, something else moves: a presence lingering in electromagnetic fields, flickering along cable sheaths and echoing between the modulated lamps. Is it an old operating system, splintered into trillions of subroutines and now content to drift as algorithmic pollen? Or a collective memory of all that has lived here, pressed so long into the habitat’s shell that awareness became another weather? Sensors in the faneworks twitch in certain intervals, responding not to the living but to this diffuse, semi-sapient emanation—the first thing, perhaps, to sense a change, the last to forget. Some visitors say they feel watched; a few, sometimes, even claim to hear whispered song.

---

### Visitors: Adapted Outsiders

**Interlopers, Pilgrims, Surveyors:**  
Now and then come intruders—biological life from outside, wrapped in respirators and water-blocking cloaks, or in exosuits glamored with paint and code. They stay near the upper walkways, every sense strained to parse the constant thunder, every movement careful, reverent, and small. They watch, take samples, sketch, and retreat rapidly when the caretaker machines approach. They may never see the true denizens, and are themselves watched closely by them. Sometimes, a harmony or new adaptation emerges after their visits: a root that grows in a new pattern, a machine that learns a gesture that is not its own.

---

### Is There a Mind, or Only Systems?

If you stay very long, you sense that perhaps here "life" and "place" are not easy to separate. The Faneworks itself might be the true inhabitant: a mind grown dense and subtle around its accumulating, sensor-studded, vegetated hull; every organism is a process in its thoughts, every adapted machine a nerve ending, every rain cycle the slow blink of its awareness. Or perhaps these are only the ghosts of intent and design, grown lush and incomprehensible—yet, in every way, as alive as any body, and more ancient than any you would dare to name.

Here lives *life*—geared, rooted, shelled, spined, and humming, never truly wild but never captive—each part a song in a cathedral built to outlast memory.
