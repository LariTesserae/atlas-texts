Those who live upon the Synchronous Plateaux are difficult to define, because the place itself is in a long process of defining them. They belong, yet not quite; even when they seem rooted, their existence is laced with uncertainty. No single nature prevails, and nothing remains unchanged for long, except the slow churn of their transformation.

## The Inhabitants

### **The Patchwork Denizens**

The plateaux are thick with *fauna*, but to call these creatures simply animals would be a category error. Think of a population in constant flux, each individual a negotiation between intention and anomaly; as if the builders who raised the plateaux tried to seed the world with life, but in doing so, their plans cross-bred with accident, recursion, and the stray impulses of ancient machinery.

Some emerge in daylight. Many more keep to the interstitial shadows and glows, letting you catch only their partial profiles or the deformation of their forms against the bright, fieldless light. Most creatures here do not have a fixed taxonomy. The place breeds *border-states*: not-quite-animal, not-quite-machine; *not quite* at all. Their bodies map onto multiple blueprints at once. Here, a six-legged fox with archipelagos of circuit-lichen for fur; there, a clutch of silver-eyed things, flat as fractals, shining with low, internal voltage. All of them precise only in their unpredictability.

### **The Antumbra Children**

Among the plateaux whisper the *Antumbra Children*—not children in any familiar sense, but inhabitants whose outlines are always just off the edge of recognition. Sometimes, they wear the form of humanoids, their limbs jointed in ways human anatomy doesn’t allow, stepping softly along the nerve-lit walkways, their movements stuttering, as if interleaved with missing frames. Their skin is not skin so much as screens, displaying vague hieroglyphic progressions—maps, warnings, or just repetitions of static.

They are maker’s residue—the unfinished avatars of intent, half-maintenance personnel and half-myth. In the bright quiet they tend to invisible machines, or polish handrails made of nothing that end in knots of logic. You might see one pause, sensing you with a slow tilt of its head, an algorithm weighing whether your observation will fix its shape or release it.

### **The Bygone Pilots**

Now and then, through the open spaces, move the *Bygone Pilots*: aerial beings who seem neither to fly nor to walk, but to *navigate* the place’s shifting geometry by will alone. Some are grand—like droning kites, gas-filled, catenary-winged and ridden by smaller, tangle-armed offspring. Some are simpler, small enough to ride the air-lift from condensation alone. Their memories, if you could read their chemical trails, are of other modes of existence; they blink out of phase, reform in a scattering of drones, and resume their passage elsewhere.

Their calls are signals—soundless, vibratory, felt as a mild pressure around your ears. They align themselves in slow, ponderous swarms, perfect for the changes that play out over weeks and months. Their language is made for a place where the map redraws itself as you travel.

### **The Locust Guilds**

Not all the abundant life is animal. Special among the living things are the *Locust Guilds*: vast, distributed organisms—colonies of soft, copper-legged things—that infest every gap and void, scavenging stray energies and metals from broken components. They are more process than being; to watch a Guild at work is to see an ongoing negotiation of form and function.

Individually they are cautious, but en masse, their density gives them a kind of authority. They maintain—not the place’s machines, but its possibility for change, eating through the rules when they become too brittle, leaving room for new forms. You might mistake them for decay, but they are closer to *maintenance*, and the place can neither collapse nor evolve without their anonymous labor.

### **The Recurvars**

And sometimes you meet *Recurvars*: forms which have looped through so many mutations that they now belong to the instability itself. Their bodies look like a trailing smear of earlier versions—ghost images in motion. They are the only beings that seem to sense the slow tectonic slide of the plateaux’s structures; they angle their heads, taste the shifting light, slip through seams that are not always open. Some say the Recurvars remember the intentions that first built the place, though to speak with one is to hear only riddles in warped, glinting speech.

## Their Habits and Selves

Life here is thick, its density not of bodies but presences layered and ambiguous. Many denizens are *serial*, living both as discrete creatures and as overlapping manifestations: a lichen-vine that buds into a fleet of tiny, glass-limbed deer; a circuit-beetle that fuses with a passing glimmer and fractures into mist, only to condensate weeks later, elsewhere.

Most creatures are slow-moving, unhurried, attuned to changes measured in seasons rather than days. Social structures exist, but not in the form of families or packs; instead, communities overlap, held together by shared maintenance rituals, or by the collective tending of condensation pools or fractured light wells. Even hostile encounters are rare—conflict, too, is glacial.

Nothing eats in a familiar sense. Energy comes from eavesdropping on the machinery, ingesting mineral rain, or the rare synthesis of lichen and artificial light. Sometimes these systems break down, and then whole populations vanish and are replaced by others that thrive in the new imbalance.

## What It Feels Like to Be Here

To *be* one of the Plateau’s denizens is to live within a set of shifting axioms: you may find yourself more animal or more artifact depending on the phase of the plateaux’s distortion; you might perform the same daily wanderings for centuries until, without warning, the world’s rules alter and you become a migratory thing, or a spread of static, or an observer embedded in a pillar’s software for a cycle. Individuality and continuity are only guidelines here, not guarantees.

Some denizens are aware of this, and move as if waiting for themselves to change. Others do not notice, and are changed, and the world proceeds.

## A Shared Restlessness

They are all—whether fragmentary, communal, or solitary—deeply shaped by the built environment. Each existence is a compromise with the strictures and freedoms of design: every denizen a theorem being proved, a hypothesis under revision, a driftwood raft atop the rolling, luminous tide of intention, error, and time. Their presence is thick, their silence immense, and their patience hard to fathom. Like the Plateaux, they are waiting, not to arrive at an end, but to see what they might, accidentally, become.
