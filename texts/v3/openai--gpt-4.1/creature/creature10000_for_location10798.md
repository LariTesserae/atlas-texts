Who lives below the Ivy Engine? The question splinters with each attempt at an answer—because nothing here remains long in one form without becoming something else.

**The Green-Built Folk**

There are those who once called themselves human, or might still, though centuries in this breathing vault have shaped them otherwise. Their skin bears the waxy matte of leaves rubbed between fingers; pores open and close with the rhythm of damp. They are small—stooped, spare, always wary. Their hair thickens into tendrils, sometimes patterned with slow, emerald veins. Many are more fungus than mammal, their eyes rimmed in spore-powder or faint luminescence. They clothe themselves, when they bother, in layered moss-leathers and the downy linings of discarded moth cocoons, all soft green, ever-moist, stitched together with root-fiber cord.

Their sense of territory is acute and invisible—each family or splinter-group marking out living chambers grown from cooperating plant-bulges. These are both home and partner: a young one's first act of maturity is to coax their chamber to flower new fruit-walls or close up defensive spikes. The Green-Built splay their fingers on growths and *think* food into budding—cultivate fungal butter, sap-wine, spicy rootmeats, petal-fish basking in the warm pools. They never walk openly; their feet barely touch ground before being guided to safeholds, old tunnels, the right leaf-fold. They are not masters, but symbiotes—one pulse in a thicket of pulses.

**The Machine-Grown**

Here, shapes move on six or eight legs—some filigreed with coppery carapace and glowing weld-bright; others soft and pulsing, crawling on belly-panel or fused root. These are not wholly life, and not wholly artifice. Beetle-shells split and out jet the hybrid drones—circuit-vine wings, crystal-whiskered heads. Some have consciousness, some have only duty: to ferry, to harvest, to couple in brief, electric frenzies with the deep turbines, inciting another bloom of bulbous cogs along root-pipes.

Some Machine-Grown are lonely intelligences. The Lamp-Keepers, who live inside their bamboo towers, fit crystalline lenses into bioluminescent stalks, coaxing pale fire from the bamboo's sap. Others, called the Husk-Engineers, dwell in massive, mobile seedpods, exosuits their plant-orphaned bodies cannot leave. Their slow, deliberate speech is modulated by the creak of lignin and the chirr of tiny servitors nested within their shell.

**The Chitinous Swarmers**

Most here, undeniably, are the insect kind. Cockroach couriers—wide as platters—crawl underfoot, their shells spelled with codes in pheromone wax, carrying packages of fruit-flesh, spores, and encrypted gossip. Moth-flocks, colors dizzying, perform parachute-loops through open arches, feeding on nectar exuded from living vents. Their larvae work as cleaners, digesting dead matter and leaving the vaults gleaming, if pocked with bite-marks.

Ants in procession fill the walls—leafcutters, but the leaves are data, the trails mapped in fluctuating hormonal clouds. Their queens are myth—spoken of as thinkers *beneath* the roots, never glimpsed; only the heat and hum betrays their presence.

**The Predatory and the Parasitic**

Above, in shadow, hang the gliders: membranous mammals, fleet and nearly transparent, bones outlined against the bluish glow. They feast on the flower-beasts, biting deep into carnivorous petals, careful to avoid venom-studded throats. Where the pulse is thickest, you may glimpse lizard-birds—scavenger-hunters, eyes ringed electric-blue, calling in notes like falling bells.

Here and there, the micro-raptors: tiny, fierce, pack-minded, flitting quick across moss-beds, hunting collectively, eyes flaring with specular fire. They nest in coiling root-knots, fiercely protective, fiercely curious.

A darker, quieter resident: mold-drifters, the parasitic miasmas that roll in cloud-like tides, searching out crowded nests or stagnant pools. Their floating fruiting bodies pulsate with warning color, and all clear out of their meandering paths. Some among the Green-Built can bargain with these—gathering their spores in airtight flasks, brewing dizzying ferments that grant brief immunities...or visions.

**The Deepers**

Nothing is seen of the deepest dwellers. They are heard—a jaw-grind far-off, a tremor like distant thunder crackling through the lichen floor. Some say a rootbrain courses there, endlessly processing—else, worm-cities, or god-things, thicker than trunks, slow as the century-hand. Old rumors have it these titans dream the architecture of the Ivy Engine: every season a corridor shifts, a pit opens, according to their hidden, unknowable will.

**In Habit and Mind**

They live not in static houses, but in bent territories—homes that sprout, bloom, decay, migrate. A chamber is yours while the vine-wall remains friendly; a road is passable until new moss takes hold and begins feeding on those foolish enough not to watch the warning colors. All hierarchy is temporary, negotiated—territory, trade, even alliance, sealed by spore or pheromone, dissolved by the churn of growth and death.

Language is gesture, scent, electric flicker as much as word. The living world participates in every act: roots writhe in emphasis, flowers unfurl to mark an apology or invitation. Some speak to the gardens with song or taste; others, by tuning tiny fungal harps whose music stuns the Engine-beetles and brings them to heel.

**What emerges is not a single people or beast but a layered meshwork—hybrid beyond imagining—of rooted, crawling, flying, engineered, and dreaming life.** Everything part-companion, part-competing, swept along in the dense metabolism of the Ivy Engine. No lord presides; no story is ever told to its end. All are inheritors of cycles never completed, and the engine beats on: an unending act of becoming.
