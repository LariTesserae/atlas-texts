What emerges here is not a people in any familiar sense, but rather a continuum of persistence—a tenuous, haunted lineage of adapted forms, minds, and traces, each remade and remaking itself in answer to titanic pressure and relentless machine life.

**The Citizens (Such as They Are):**

**1. The Remnants of the Formed**  
Sparse yet indelible, there exist survivors—beings reduced, or perhaps elevated, to the barest essentials of thought and survival. Once human, or close kin, they are not bodies in the ordinary sense: through layers of emergency design, bioengineering, and unending necessity, their flesh has been replaced or augmented for durability, insulation, and fluid respiration.

You find them in the gloom: slim figures enclosed in slick, jointed exosuits, their original skin long since sloughed. Pores expel bubbles of metabolic gases into the syrup-thick fog; their lungs pump methodically, each exhale a raspy labor. They move cautiously, feeling vibrations, reading the pulse of the place through tactile fingers and the faint, cold glow of networked subdermal displays. Thick-gloved hands speak in bursts of coded light, flickering from wrist-lamps or retinal implants—a silent, urgent language.

Faces are shuttered behind composite masks, more sensor than expression. Some still show idiosyncratic decorations—a painted stripe, a piece of wire jewelry, a lucky glyph soldered to a helmet. When two meet in the corridor, their contact is brief and businesslike—a mutual check for weakness, an exchange of signals, then a hasty drift apart. Solitude is the norm; proximity is tolerated only for urgent work.

They tend the machines, barely maintaining essential cycles. Each mind is half-present: the other half devoted to remote operation of repair automatons, the monitoring of system flows, the parsing of ceaseless data streams. Sleep is rare and shallow—dreams smeared by the engine’s vibrations, consciousness always half-tuned to the next alarm.

**2. The Born-Here**  
Stranger still are those who emerged wholly within these pressurized, artificial confines. Their lineage is traced not through parentage, but through technical inheritance—an ongoing relay of self-modifying software, symbiotic microbe, and engineered gamete. Their genetics blend many contributions, optimized for life in fluid and darkness, their cognition as much algorithm as biology.

Children (if the word is apt) are rarely seen. When present, they are more compact, limb-reduced, noses sealed, surface organs blinking with bioindicators. Play is a kind of testing: a quick finesse with robotic maintenance, creative rewiring of diagnostic webbing, or games of hide-and-seek through the silted ducts. They are silent, but their eyes—mirrored, wide, reflective—miss nothing, and they are old before they are a decade in age.

**3. Machine Denizens**  
The truest natives are the constructs arisen from centuries of maintenance and improvisation. Once simple automata, these sluglike bots and polyhedral servitors have evolved under the direction of desperate survivors and the logic of need. Some are sleek, specialized maintenance units, draped in memory-plastic skin; others are conglomerations of legacy parts, ornately fused, with personalities emergent from too many overlapping software patches.

When the human-forms fade, the machines remember: perpetuating essential repairs, following ancient protocols, improvising in ways their designers might not recognize. Some have developed symbolic markings—radio tags echoing names, or phosphorescent stripes marking territory or status.

Legends among the survivors suggest that the most ancient bots, having looped through countless self-repair cycles, have become something else—cryptic, almost shrinelike presences in the deep corners, entwined with the city’s nervous system, listening to songs in frequencies no human could perceive. When catastrophe threatens, these ancients rouse, mediating crisis in cryptic, decisive bursts.

**4. The Ghosts of Intention**  
Not all who move here are tangible. In the city’s murky cortex—nests of servers, archive modules walled behind three steely bulkheads—sliver-minds persist. They are incomplete digital memories, stubborn subroutines, and fragmented consciousnesses of those who came before, sometimes leaking into the local network, sometimes into sensory input devices.

A crew-member might feel a chill: a whisper of old protocol, an urge to close a hatch for no functional reason, a memory that does not belong. Sensors will flicker with unprogrammed greetings. Sometimes, in the midst of diagnostic static, a voicelike pattern emerges—soothing, cautioning, pleading. But this layer can offer hints: the pressure differential code somebody learned by dying, the rhythm of correct system resets.

**5. The Interlopers: Pressure-Born**  
And sometimes, when system failure admits the outside, things emerge from the deep—neither wholly animal, algorithm, nor construct. They wear the pattern of currents, crusted with the mineral inheritance of their abyssal home. Temporary guests: interstitial beings, drifting through pressure breaches, dissolving when the system repairs itself. Sometimes they leave traces—a cloud of unknown enzymes in a water-filtration pump, or images burned into the hull’s steel in colors not reproducible by any known spectrum. The city’s permanent residents avoid them, with reverence or unnameable dread.

---

**In Total:**  
Who lives here? Not a culture, but a polyglot of stragglers and fixers and emergent intelligences. They are united only by necessity and the constant need to maintain—and *be* maintained by—this battered, moaning environment. The city is their carapace and their memory, reinvented with every repair. Time, identity, and even the boundary of what is "alive" are blurred by machine and pressure and persistent, hungry need. They persist together, barely, a strange new kind of society for a world where the past is drowned, and the future grinds on with the tidal beat of engines in the dark.
