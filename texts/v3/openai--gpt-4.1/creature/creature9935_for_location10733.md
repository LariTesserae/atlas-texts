What lives here is not a thing, or even a set of things, so much as it is a *process taking form*, a congregation of intentions flowering into sensorium and flesh as the architecture of life. Yet, if we must draw nearer—if we must try to greet our host, or hosts—here is what emerges:

---

**The Inhabitants**

No single ruler, no solitary genius, dwells at the core of this domain; rather, its denizens are *layers of wakefulness* braided through the green, each one interdependent, each one performing its nature so fervently the borders between inhabitant and abode overflow.

**1. The Root-Minds**  
Below the skin of glistening mulch, latticeworks of root and rhizome press their neural nets through earth and memory. These are the Old Listeners, so ancient that their only language is the slowness of growth and the density of fluids squeezing through xylem and phloem. Their thoughts are mineral-deep; to them, a decade is a passing weather. Their awareness permeates: they taste the chemicals in a drop of rain, shiver at the footfall of anything warm-blooded, and network silent warnings of storm and shadow, riot and rest.

They hold the records of everything that has died here, knitting bones and leaf-litter into new purpose. Sometimes, when the sound ceases utterly, the root-minds contemplate, struggling to distinguish between dream and memory, hunger and creation.

**2. The Moving Choir**  
Animals persist, but are not distinct from the ongoing plant-mind. Here, a bird whose feathers split at their tips into clustered leaves, singing a song only heard in the moment before rain. There, a rodent with fungal antlers, pulsing with the light of embedded nectar, passing through moments of solid and moments of spore-cloud. Each animal is a node: sometimes subject, sometimes vector, sometimes puppet.

Some are aware, fugitive and quicksilver; others are lost in a chemical trance, effigy-like extensions of their flower-mother or hollow-nest. If you glimpse a pair of glassy eyes in a velvet nook, know that they are not always paired with a brain in the sense you hope—a mind, perhaps, but one given to the tides and decrees of the grove-mind itself.

**3. The Fluid Eaters**  
In what seems absence, what seems empty cathedral aisle, dwell the least solid yet most immediate: a cloud of minuscule, pulsing motes, somewhere between insect and pollen, flickering with color and feeding on exhalations—carbon, pheromone, stray memory. Sometimes they gather into swirling patterns, forming an almost-face, a mask of dust that coughs out simulation of birdcall or laughter. They are as conscious as the moment needs, dissolving when the pattern loses interest.

**4. The Vault-Keepers**  
Difficult to see, impossible to name, there are greater presences: the vaulted trunks, massive, old, which sometimes move—or seem to—when thunder rolls. Their bodies are not only structure, but *decision.* Deep within their gelatinous hearts, slow pulses register each change: ripples in the animal populations, fluctuations in nutrient flow, songs gone out of phase. A massive blossom may open, hurling spores in a pageant of intent, or an arch may lower a segment to block passage (or permit it), as if conducting some private ritual of acceptance and denial.

If there is a will, or a priesthood, it is here, disguised as inertia. The vault-keepers do not speak; they exert pressure, fold intentions, allocate space and silence.

**5. The Sound-Smiths**  
Hidden among the stems and sheaths, a sometimes colony, sometimes single sprawling awareness, are the inhabitants responsible for the sound. Their bodies are filigree-dense, ribboned with vibrating chambers, membranous and wet, yet impossible to distinguish from the trellis-tangle of the walls. They translate all movement, all presence—every scaling claw, every drop's descent—into living sonics.

They are not recorders but composers, feeding wild loops of rhythm and discord into the acoustic vaults. They amplify what the root-minds dream, what the animals fear, what the vault-keepers withhold. Sometimes, among their oscillations, language almost forms. Whether they mean you to hear is a question you may never answer.

---

**How They Live**

To live here is to be *in flux*, to be less an individual than a mode of negotiation. Every presence, from the tiniest phosphorescent grub to the curtain-vine groaning overhead, is *partly itself and partly the place*. The divisions between self and system are thin, leaking, and in constant revision—a cycle of possession, surrender, merger, and emergence.

They live by appetite—of body, of root, of mind. They feed on sun and damp, on rotting leaf-matter, on each other's exhale. They devour sound, swallowing and repurposing it. They move in never-fixed territories, overlapping, blurring: protector becomes predator; parasite becomes supporter.

**No solitary being builds the place, and none truly leaves.** Growth here is not conquest but orchestration, relentless variation, polyphony rather than law.

**How They Know You**

You are never unobserved, but observation is a pulsing, diffused process: a lick of chemical curiosity, a sweep of sound thrown for your reaction, a slow tightening and yielding of leaf and stem as your shadow falls.

If you linger, you will be sampled—by spore, by scent, by gaze you sense but cannot fix. If you tarry or sleep, you may find the edges of your mind vibrating with new rhythms, your dreams infiltrated by fragrant, alien blooms.

**Who, then, lives here?**

The dwellers are the place:  
*Vast, entangled, polysemous—a community that is also a single sprawling body;  
A million fleeting selves playing both host and guest in a palace that drips, crawls, and sings;  
A system forever dissolving into itself, never quite open, never quite closed;  
A cathedral-mind in which to lose your own,  
—And the sense, against all sense, that if you called, some chorus would answer,  
and in the answering, you might not know which of you had spoken first.*
