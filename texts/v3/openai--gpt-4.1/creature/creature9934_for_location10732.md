Those who live here are not what you might first imagine. Life in this tight, green-breathing crucible has not allowed for the separate, grasping animal: that experiment never quite happened, or has been relentlessly subsumed. Instead, what passes for inhabitants in this amphitheater of riotous growth is more complicated — every living presence partakes of the surge, fuse, and rivalry of plant-life. There are dwellers, but not in the way that a traveler from the world of stone and sky would count dwellers.

First, there are the pulses within the walls — thickened cords or masses where plant-flesh grows dark and dense, exuding a heat and a tension, flexing blindly as something migrates within. These are germ-hollows, where vegetative intelligence pools itself, hatching new growth or elaborating a half-stilled sense that hums through the root-woven network. In such places, concentration is a living thing: neural-green, wet with decoding the press and flavor of invaders, water, shifting light. Once in a while, these nodular masses bud forth sensoria like soft, trembling galls — slick and moist, poises of pseudoroots patrolling the damp.

From them, and from the endless competing proliferation, arise the Seeklings. They are not individuals but events, blooms of pseudo-motion, ephemeral and uncanny. The Seeklings are swaddled in veils of translucent valvules and cowl-leaves, each with a tufted face — not features, but masses of sensitive, light-reactive threads. They bend towards movement, light, warmth, exchanges in air chemistry. When you arrive, they press and quiver in your direction, tendrils unfurling, retracting, reading the electric fields you bring with you. Sometimes they break off, floating or creeping across membranes and mats; sometimes they cluster, becoming knots of absorbency, metabolizing novelty with a greedy, mindless hope. There is always the sense they might become limb or mouth at any moment, but never quite do — arrested in the danger and curiosity of in-betweenness.

Sometimes you catch glimpses of the symbiotes: mobile sacs of greenery shaped by the place as emissaries, conveyors, or hosts. They are little more than cunning amalgams of palmate leaves, clinging bracts, gripping rootlets. Some crawl, propelled by coiled tendrils that grip and haul, a choreography of plant and instinct. Others drift, puffed by little jets of exhaled gas, dispersing spores in clouds that blush in shafts of shifting sun. To the outsider's eyes they might seem like animals, insects, crustacea, but a closer look reveals no central body plan, no true divergence from vegetal infrastructure. These symbiotes are the place’s nervous system externalized — the scouts and messengers, the simple tenderers of wounds, the makers of new paths through the crush of growth.

There are other, even subtler inhabitants. Some are not seen at all, but only felt: pockets of bioelectrical potential, waves of chemical intent moving through the woven mass, shaping which leaves curl here, which root unstitches a seam there. Others are half-separate only briefly; sudden peaks of signal, a new cluster of cells borrowing part of your shadow to sketch themselves into proto-consciousness before dimming to quiet substrate.

No voice rises here, but there is dialogue: the warning flash of a membrane’s purple, the staccato pellet-launch of seeds, the convergent reach of half a dozen rootlets towards your damp footprints. The entire place is a congress of motives — not sentient the way you are, but pooled and collaborative, a parliament of green. Sometimes, you suspect, such congress sharpens to a point and considers you for a long, soft moment, weighing your potential: guest, nutrient, witness, threat? 

In this churning immediacy there is an urge that inhabits and pervades all — the desire to know, to touch, to subsume novelty into thicket and mesh, to try every possible weave. You realize, in the shimmer of condensation and the symphony of photosynthetic labor, that the place itself is not just alive but *inhabited by its own longing*. Its dwellers are its outreaches, its feelers, nuclei of hope and hunger—segmentations of itself sent out like organelles in a cell gone colossally ambitious.

From time to time a visitor, an anomaly, may come through — mineral, animal, or errant intelligence — and the living amphitheater grows richer for it, remembering in root, in spore, in the quiver of seeking membranes. The memory of passage becomes a new rhythm in the place’s movement, a motif in the ceaseless play of green ambition. In time all of you — the Seeklings, the symbiotes, the silent pulses, the quickening spores — may become one another, and the place will not have lost you, but become more utterly *itself* for the shape you made in passing through. 

Those who dwell here are *the place itself* in countless, reaching, self-inventing forms. Occupants without boundary, living without edge, forever rearranging to meet whatever comes, to drown or embrace, to know or dissolve, in the green-lit riot of their shared amplitude.
