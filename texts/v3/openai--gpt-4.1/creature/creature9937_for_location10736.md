Who—or what—lives here?

What gathers itself together from this thick, tentative world is not bound by shapes old or clear, but by the logic of newness: beginnings, impulsive assemblies, a curiosity almost before identity. What lives here? Not settled species, but collisions of hunger and pulse, instinct and mute persistence—a wild nation of the unfinished, the becoming.

#### The Grey-Root People

First, at the edge between plant and animal, there are the Grey-Root People. They do not walk so much as flow—boneless, dull-skinned forms whose bodies weep slow sap from moss-covered joints. Rooted by day in huddles among the low, tubular trunks, they feed on mineral-laden water that beads on the surfaces of stones. Their skin thickens, rucks with nubs and curls of incipient leaf. At night, or when the fog thickens, they unmoor, detach their shallow-anchored toes, and crawl through spongey peat, trailing roots behind them—sometimes several meters long, tangled with fungal threads. Faces are no more than recurring arrangements: moist, green-filmed eyes and slits for mouths, blinking fat drops of water with careful, slow persistence.

Their speech is the hum and rhythm of root against earth—a gurgling percolation, felt more than heard.

#### The Carnivorous Choir

Hanging from the macro-ferns are nests like drooping flesh-pods, pendulous and gently pulsing green. These are homes—but also the bodies—of the Carnivorous Choir, creatures that share life with the plants themselves: not quite bird, not quite vine, but a partnership of music and hunger. Networks of thin, glassy filaments—both root and voicebox—descend from pod-throats, catching mist and unlucky insects, vibrating out keening harmonics whenever the air stirs. The nest pods throb with sound, summoning clouds of small amphibians and glittering gnats. Each pod voices a fragment of the symphony, but when the fog is heavy, their harmonics synchronize, and the whole valley pulses with a chorus as palpable as rain—songs that nudge even the moss and stones a little more awake.

#### The Lumen-Creepers

By the shallow, glassy pools slithers the Lumen-creepers: sinuous, limbless, wet-skinned things with bodies banded in swathes of bioluminescence, their colors shifting in time with the minute changes in the valley’s mood—sickly yellow to glaucous green, to deep, hot indigo. They surface only for breath and birth, their slick forms rising amid lilies and cup-leaved orchids, releasing clouds of spores with each silence-breaking splash. The Lumen-creepers are neither fish nor eel, but flexing compromises: they feed on both algae spun from sunlight and the denser proteins in buried invertebrates. To see their trails is to see glowing, temporary scars in the water; behind each one, the pool shivers with remembered color.

#### The Glistening-Insect Dynasty

High in the constant, shadowless murk, the Glistening-Insect Dynasty rules the air. They are of no singlespecies—an ever-churning carnival of forms—but they function together. There are winged things like a cross between mosquito and beetle, with armor shining in prismatic plates. There are great soft-bodied gnats whose abdomens trail long, fiber-optic tails that flicker and tangle among the hanging vines. All things in the air are bitten, preened, pollinated, or cleaned by members of this inconstant group; every flutter and burrow is part of the Dynasty’s endless work. Their society is chemical and chaotic, a blossoming riot of relationships born out of hunger and bright scent.

#### The Watchers

Somewhere in the thick soupy dim, there are the Watchers—vast, indolent, ambiguous giants that make their presence known only through reverberations: a sudden heavy breath on the air, the soft draw and release of water beneath the peat, two golden eyes glowing for a moment amid banks of dew-jeweled moss. Are they amphibian, plant, or something else? Perhaps all blended: flanks broad as small islets, carpeted with gardens of moss and rooted orchids; limbs like half-grown trees, skin whorled with green and blue, slick to the touch. They move only rarely, so that grass and fungus grow undisturbed across their bodies, unsuspecting. When they do stir, their motion shapes the landscape’s weather, sending flurries of spores and steam rippling through the stillness like the first ideas of wind.

#### The Shouters

And always—braided with the riotous background, marking time in this unmade world—are the Shouters: hidden, restless, unseen things. Their blasts and yelps are less cry than the sound of life bootstrapping itself: huge, booming, impossible calls that quiver water, shake canopy, and cause every form and fungus to swell a little fuller with heat and sap—the world’s own alarm clocks, calling everything hourly to remember: “Become! Become! Become!”

---

What lives here, then, is not defined by lineage or catalog. The inhabitants are emergent answers to the valley’s conversations—each an incomplete reply etched in slime, root, and voice. They are the first moods of hunger and safety and song. Each is a rough sketch kin to the place itself: not yet fixed in form, always rewriting themselves at the warm, humid edge of newness, eternally coaxed toward what might, someday, become the familiar life of forests and swamps—but never yet arriving, forever rich in the possibility of change.
