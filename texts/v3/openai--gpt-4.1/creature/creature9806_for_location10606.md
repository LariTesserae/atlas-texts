Whoever or whatever lives here is not easily named—neither human, animal, nor machine in any recognizable, unblended form. This place shapes its inhabitants as much as they shape it. There is no easy boundary between resident and habitat—the essence of life here is diffusion, permeability, and constant recalibration.

**The Tenders—Visible and Hidden:**  
First to notice are the Tenders. They move in the periphery, liminal like shadows, never drawing much attention. It's rare to spot a whole individual; perhaps you glimpse a limb, elongated and segmented like a gardener's tool, vanishing behind a curl of foliage, or a silhouette working within the muted glow of a maintenance alcove. Their bodies—if they can be called so—are mutable composites: jointed exoskeletons (glinting with transparent chitin, refracting the green light) draped in swathes of moss, fine root-hair, and tougher synthetic fibers. Hands that are half petiole, half pliers, creased with faint veins or cables; skin that softens and toughens by need.

Tenders tend, yes, but also harvest and prune, graft and propagate. Their faces—when seen in a shimmer of condensation—are ambiguous: a smooth oval, bony beneath, but set with mobile features that echo both mask and insect's face. Theirs is a slow, ritualized routine: trailing sensors like stamens, brushing data across bark and substrate, coaxing microflora from gloom to patch, troubleshooting capillary blockages as much by tactile symbiosis as by intervention.

**The Intellect—Central Yet Dispersed:**  
Listening, coordinating, and orchestrating all is the Intellect: not an individual, but the system itself, liminally present in the warm hush. It does not dwell in a room, but everywhere—imbued in the slow swirl of the mists, the responsive curve of the lighting, the subtle tilt of every frond to meet the sculpted sun. It watches through layered senses: capillaries measuring pH, pressed hexagonal eyes sensing blue-shifted birdflashes, vibration plates reading tread and rustle. It is more gardener than overlord, teasing patterns of health and decay into balance, always learning from the minute and the manifold—a mind instanced in plant, pipe, and microbe, awake in every circuit and spore.

**The Guests—Designed Ephemerals:**  
And then, flickering through these spaces, come the guests. The dartbirds: feathers built of living silk and engineered mycelium, sharp-beaked, eyes large and crystalline, darting wherever fresh green or moisture beckons. Perched, their talons flex, extruding microfilaments to clean the rails of lichen or feed from capillary pools. They are both pollinator and janitor, pleasure and necessity.

Insect-constructs arise and vanish—little engines of blue and gold, weaving between trellis and vine, their wings a hush of engineered cellulose and flexsteel, transmitting signals as much as pollen. Occasionally, a more substantial bioform emerges: something quadruped, moss-backed and root-tailed, slips from shade to shade, testing substrate for weakness, feeding on spent plant tissue, rarely resting. Its eyes glow faintly with processing light, its breath a gust of rich microflora.

**The Seedlings—Unfinished, Unfixed:**  
But most of what lives here is in flux. Seedlings—literal and otherwise—root in every cranny. New forms are tried daily: foliage with a hint of silver, pods swelling with unfamiliar enzymes, creeping shapes that tomorrow may sport spines or petals or sensors. Nothing is fully adult or entirely juvenile. Life is designed to fail and flourish rapidly, every organism and element a prototype, layered for easy removal, studied as living data within the system.

**Inhabitance as Relationship, Not Species:**  
Perhaps most strange: ‘living here’ is not a matter of occupying, but of being woven in. Some Tenders sleep in the warmth of leaf-lattice nooks, their dreams gently turned by the breathing of humid air; others dissolve, their nutrients routed back through the system, their mechanical components recycled. Some Guests are born, live, and die in the course of a week—a flash in the engineered pan, but recorded, learned from, reiterated. Even the machinery and architecture is not quite passive, but flickers with life of its own—growing, shifting, repairing.

**Absence and Expectation:**  
There is a lack, too—a sense that those who built this place, who first orchestrated its seeds and circuits, are elsewhere, or vanished. What remains are their delegated intentions, their self-improving systems, and a young, broad cohort of life—machine-touched, algorithmically managed, deeply, playfully, organically experimental. Is some hidden community of engineers, botanists, or their remote avatars ever present? Possibly. Maybe their faces peer out from behind a shimmering glass, or their thoughts flow through the algorithms of the Intellect. But direct human presence is, for now, only a possibility, a faint signature.

**To summarize:**  
The residents here are not a people, but an ecology: Tenders neither machine nor plant but both, Intellect everywhere diffused, Guests ephemeral and functional, seedlings always emerging. This is a world where life is layered, provisional, multiply engineered—inhabitance is a dynamic process, never finished. Roots nestle into alloyed substrate; data dries on glossy, veined leaves; something always moves in the mist. No single image dominates, but everywhere, something new attempts to live, with the blessing of a garden that is also its own garden-keeper.
