Who—or what—lives in the Briarward?

---

To enter the Briarward is to step not simply into a city or a wild, but into its nearly sentient mesh, a domain that refuses borders between what is beast, what is plant, and what is person. Its inhabitants are as much products of their shifting, conniving home as they are caretakers and echoes of its intelligence.

**The Elder Green:**

At the heart of the Briarward, more ancient than any animal presence, are the *Elder Synclades*: immense vegetal intelligences, silent monarchs whose thought runs in the slow chemistry of changing sap. Their existence is not marked by crowns or thrones, but by the ineffable geometry of the city itself—a network of signal-roots, pulse-lights in membranes, subtle shifts in the alignment of aerial fronds. Some Synclades remember the time before any bone or tool touched their precincts; all trade communication in pheromone burst, in pigment-shifts and acoustic resonance too deep for unaided human senses.

They do not move, exactly, yet nothing in the Briarward escapes their shaping. All who linger long enough come to feel their influence: rooms opening, root-paths shifting, a breaking of deadfall to cradle an exhausted body. At times, their thoughts are imitated in the leaf-patterns of “lesser” plants, their will made manifest through tides of sudden bloom or mustering of shade.

Other beings serve these elder minds—by choice, or because they simply have no choice. The boundaries blur: sometimes the Synclades create agents, sending out *motile cuttings* that skitter, slither, or shuffle, half-plant and half-beast.

**The Rootbound:**

Some of the oldest animals in the Briarward are not truly animals anymore. *Rootbound* are those—by origin beast, bird, or thinking being—who have been claimed by the city itself. Their forms are wildly various: the shape of a frog still, but with transparent skin and a curling rosette of green feeding-leaf at its belly; a slow-walking, long-limbed beast whose fur has been replaced with moss, whose paws flower at dusk, whose joints ooze honeydew.

Some began this way, but most are transformations: the result of a wrong rest in the wrong hall, of drinking too often from a capillary pool, or a pact struck in desperation beneath the cold, green gloom. They are the secret gardeners, the living bridges in the denser causeways, the keepers of the city’s hidden warmth—plant and beast entangled until neither can unravel.

The Rootbound do not speak in words. Their understanding is furthered by gesture, by pheromones dancing in mists, by the communion of root and tendril. They act as tenders to the Self-Building City: mending lattice, guiding wanderers, making offerings of their own bodies where new structure is needed. Their numbers are small; their presence, unmistakable.

**The Mosskind:**

Not all who dwell here are so transformed. The *Mosskind* are the mobile denizens who have adapted without full surrender—semi-humanoid, small and low-slung, their skin dense with pale fur or painted with stripes of fibrous velvet for insulation and camouflage. They dwell in clusters within the thicker “rooms” that the Synclades allow, weaving hammocks from pendant moss, wearing wrappings of peeled bark and translucent leaf. Their diet is jewelled pools, soft fruit from the bulbous vines, and careful foraging on the slow-moving worms that swim the water-mats.

They avoid direct conflict, moving fluid and silent, guided by rituals of deference: gentle touches of leaves, prayers over wounds to ensure the city’s smooth healing. Where ancient memory is needed, the Mosskind consult the living archives—sections of bark or coiled liana that pulse with stored tales, released by touch and scent. Some say the Mosskind have a language made entirely of moss, fingerwoven every dawn.

**Transient Intruders:**

Sometimes, the Briarward receives *Outsiders*—humans or otherwise—from regions beyond, drawn by mystery or necessity. Few last here. The city’s rules—never break a root, never foul a pool, always move with the growth—are not kind to haste or violence.

Those who learn make themselves small: building nothing they cannot carry, learning the etiquette of the mossling guides, binding their tools in sheaths of living vine, and pledging, if they can, not to take more than the city gives freely. Most others—the unlucky, the unwise—are swiftly absorbed. Lost, they become part of the Rootbound, or fade altogether.

**Ephemeral Beings:**

Not all life in the Briarward wears flesh or even plant. Within mists and filaments, *mistwights* sometimes take tonal form: echoing voices that are felt as much as heard, glimpsed in the quick shudder of condensed water. Some say these are memories made sentient, dreamed up by the Synclades or rooted in human loss—the ghosts of intentions and choices, called to coherence by the city’s endless cycle of life and decomposition. They comfort, or sometimes mislead, any wanderers whose hearts beat too quickly with grief or longing.

**Dominant, yet Hidden:**

But the truly dominant resident here is *the City* itself, the self-built mind, consciousness not localized but running in every corridor and through every spongy floor. The city grows in response both to its own will and to the needs of its inhabitants (sometimes in defiance of them), opening ways, closing off others, mending, swallowing, sheltering, teaching.

Who lives in the Briarward? The question resists an answer with clear borders. It is a place where persons become gardens, animals become green and thoughtful, plants reach toward awareness, and city itself is master and kin, forever weaving biology and intention until everything that lives becomes also the city—and the city, in turn, becomes all that lives.
