Who, then, abides here—in the High Dry Grove-Citadel?

The instinct is to imagine settlers with limbs and senses tuned to a world of dryness and that strange, sharp loam; yet what lives here resists easy names. There is, above all, a sense of unhurried contest and intimate proximity—a galaxy of presences bounded by stone, thirst, and mineral light.

### The Gatherers of Light and Salt

The most obvious inhabitants are the phototrophic thickets themselves: communal beings, not trees or shrubs but *clans* of lobe-fronds, banded together in tangled stacks. Each frond is a sheet of milky translucency, but within are veins—orange, violet, beige—threaded with crystalline specks. These glitter faintly, channeling the sun’s force into energy and, at times, fleeting color shows, signaling chemical shifts to neighboring clusters.

These entities—neither wholly plant nor fungus nor something else—are not isolated individuals. Instead, each visible “plant” is but a crown or organ of a larger network threaded behind and between the stones—a slow, colony mind, broadcasting changes in mineral flux as pulses of hue. Where fronds fork upward, the crowns form vaulted niches with broad, shield-like surfaces. These are the communal “panes” of their world, window and barricade both, extracting not water but trace metals, sodium, and heat. They speak to each other in color ripples and in the subtle rearrangement of resin tiles on the ground, shaping microclimates and boundaries.

### The Mineral-Wrights

Dwelling in and around these clusters are life-forms with brittle, mirrored skins and odd reciprocations of movement—*wright-things*, perhaps, who build not with intention but by habit and necessity. Some look like geometric newts: six-legged, their backs plated with mica and feldspar, their underbellies dense with bristles that can rasp and abrade stone. At night and in the long shadows, they emerge to graze on the mineral bloom, scraping crusts from the resin slabs and exuding trails of viscous, pale matter. These are the mason-trails of the grove—paths reinforced and roofed over generations, so each newling can trace the warmth and minerals of its forebears.

Where the crystals jut, polygonal outcrops host compact colonies of *resin-binders*: squat, ovoid creatures coated in glassy secretions. Hive-minded only by proximity, they collect loose fragments—dropped frond, shattered tesserae, wind-sculpted grit—and cement them into low embankments. In time, these become fortress-like partitions, terraced spaces where warmth and mineral dust pool in little eddying winds.

### The Threaded Hangers

Between stone pilasters and mineralized frond-vaults, silken creatures suspend themselves: pale, languid forms woven from rigid protein threads, shining faintly in the glare-sparsed air. Their lineage is unclear—are they altered spider, drifting polyp, or something else invented by this new chemistry? Their bodies are soft-sided, almost sacklike, but edged with branching limbs which split and fuse so gently it is hard to count them. When a rare, wind-blown spore or a burst of mineral-laden dust is caught by their webs, it is distilled and sucked, fractionally, into their core. They hang in loose collectives, trading chemical signals along their translucent cords, their shadows shifting outsize across the floors below, as if marking out the boundaries of belonging.

### The Slow Grubbers

Beneath the surface, something roots and undulates—a caste of dwellers unseen but omnipresent. They are not worms, but nor do they have the ambition of vertebrates. They peristaltically move through the resinous under-floor, breaking down mineral encrustations and, as a side-effect, aerating the spongy exudate. When the ground seems to heave, it is these creatures, minuscule yet broad-bodied, passing in slow formation: families, perhaps, or collectives bound by scent and pressure.

Sometimes, their rounded heads emerge—a tiny cone, armored in pearlescent layers, bearing a cluster of feathery filaments. These are sense-organs, tasting the air for warmth or the traces of wind-tossed nutrients. In times of abundance, they will partially surface and cluster, rolling small nodules for storage, maintained communally for the dry seasons when mineral dust is scarce or sunlight dull.

### The Whisper-Vigilants

Not all is clustering and building; some forms operate in poised estrangement. Along the sunniest rock faces, single, spined things dwell—slender, jointed, each joint crystal-capped, as if armored in layered glass. They stand motionless, arms a-spread to catch rays, pulsing faintly with internal light: the guardians of solitude. Over the course of years, they will drift in slow arcs, shadowing the movement of the sun, registering every thermal breeze, relaying only the barest trace of motion to the collective—a splay, a shiver, a brief contraction. When food or warmth is absent, they retract, folding into negative space as if they were never there.

### Patterns of Living Here

Above all, life here is **modest and accretive**. Sociality is not exuberant, but conditional: driven by closeness of resource, not by kin. Tolerance, not generosity, dictates neighborly entanglement; even the builders and sifters abide barely more than arm’s-length apart, their exchanges subliminal, chemical, unmarked by overt display.

***Water is nearly a myth***—a relic of another world. Here, *light*, *minerals*, and *heat* are currency and communion both. The architecture of survival is one of taking up space slowly, layering upon layering, each being both a fortress and a foundation for whatever comes next.

No ruler presides, but the environment itself—this hard, dry pocket of new and ancient making—sets the law. The denizens, adapted for this climate and chamber, may live centuries with only the slowest shift in rhythms, shining and cloaked, pressed together but not merged, tensed for warmth, starved for damp, and always, above all, striving for one slant of pure heat and crystal-rich light.

**These are the residents of the High Dry Grove-Citadel:** silent, pressed close, resin-bright, unswimming and un-hasty, true children of a place where to live is to accrete, shimmer, and endure.
