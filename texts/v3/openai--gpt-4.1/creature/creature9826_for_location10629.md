What lives here? 

**Not “people,” quite—though not wholly other than yourself. They are entities of intention and memory, persistence and change, shaped by and shaping the endless green. Let them reveal themselves gradually:**

---

**At first, you might look for creatures that *depart* from the plant-world, that walk easily between root and sky, but in truth, everything here is *of* the substrate—the distinction between resident and terrain is always half-dissolved.**

### **The Oldest Inhabitants**

At the core are the great **Dendrites**—not animals, not trees, but what once might have been called the ‘originals’ of this place's civilization. They are rooted but not immobile: their vast brains are woven through the highest trunks and deepest rhizomes, slow but immense in thought, some hundreds of generations old, their dendritic minds flickering in the crystalline flows of their vascular matrices. They shape the world not with hands, but with a flowering of will—directing roots to splay and spiral, growing bridges and vaults by intention alone.

When they wish to speak, the world responds: bioluminescent glyphs, patterns of pollen drifting, waves of color and scent, a language written in light and matter. All here are born and shaped in conversation with the Dendrites, though only a handful ever directly know them.

### **The Movable Folk**

Among the tangled platforms and hanging city-fruits move the **Fronds**—what you might call “people,” had not their forms long ago diverged from any clear animal or plant. They are flexible, tall as reeds or bundled close and squat, their surfaces like braided grass and polished green bark, faces suggested in moving panels of leaf or mutable patches of lichen-light. Within, their bones are mineralized wood and their veins are capillaries for mineral vapor and hollow, flickering with bioluminescent signs of emotion and speech.

They live in cultivated hollows, in braided nests on the trunks, in pods that are both grown shelter and symbiotic home. Their fingers split and merge at will, spinning living filaments to tune their walls, coaxing support-vines into tapestry bridges, negotiating with the forest-city to shift and shape. Each body is itself a colony—sensors and germ-lines, symbiotic microflora, and whorls of machine-cells that pulse with signal and record memory.

Despite their strangeness, their movements are recognizably social: they gather in the bright amphitheatres, exchanging greetings in ritual patterns of color and scent. They teach, passing pigment-shifting “books” leaf to leaf, as much grown as spoken, memory embedded in living tissue. Lineage, for them, is both biological and architectural—children can sprout from their sides, carried as buds before detaching, or else are woven from seed and scaffold, both invented and inherited.

### **The Go-Betweens**

Moving through all layers are the **Symbionts**. Some are animal-like—or once were, but now blend silicon-veined chitin with rootlets and mobile rhizomes. Some span the canopies, their bodies full of buoyant gas, trailing cilia that harvest crystal dust; others burrow in mineral hollows, tending and being tended, stripping toxins and adding their signals to the neural pulse. Every form is partnership: sometimes a network of carnivorous vines wrapped around a camouflaged beast; sometimes a jelly-like intelligence that rides within plant spurs; sometimes a “pet” that is equal parts companion and wearable data-storage.

Communication with them is nuanced—pheromone, electrochemical pulse, colored spores dancing in wind. Some are interpreters, negotiating the will of the unthinking green, some are harvesters or healers or scouts, bridging gaps between rooted and rootless.

### **The Watchers and Architects**

Above and apart, drifting from trunk to trunk, are the **Pollinators**—beings more pattern than shape, woven of dust, spore, and light. They may once have been a class of entities tasked with information exchange; now, they are both living archive and architect, conducting memory from one body to another, cross-pollinating not just genes but language, custom, and knowledge.

When a city wishes to remember, it calls the Pollinators—billowing clouds of gently glowing dust, swirling and entering the central hollows to deposit bioluminescent pigment-codes and pulse-patterns, ensuring the continuity of what passes for history and identity.

### **Ferals and Untamed**

Beyond the civilization’s edge dwell the **Ferals**—shapes half-lost to wild evolution, hybrid of branch and animal, nothing but hunger, stealth, and reactive intelligence. Some are swift—scuttling crystal spiders with whole lichen-forests on their backs, vanishing in crevices; some hang, armored and inert, in perpetual waiting. Some ancient forms—immense, sluggish tangles a kilometer long—wander the borderlands of thought, hosts to lost dreams and fragment-memories echoing faintly through the root-net.

Some say the Ferals are failed negotiations, others that they are the forest’s creative wellspring, from which new forms come.

---

## **What Is a Person Here?**

A “person” is not wholly animal, nor wholly vegetable, nor machine—**each is a hybrid, unshakably plural, a node in a living network where memory is stored as light, as gene, as structure.**

Some you meet in a marketplace of living stairs, their forms shimmering with encoded invitation, attend to you with shifting scent and rippling pigment. They might extend a finger—unspooling like a vine—to offer you a bud, a memory, an access-key to the city’s humming web. When they walk, the soles of their feet sprout minute cilia, tasting the city’s substrate, learning news with every step.

Their history is long—for once, perhaps, they were flesh-and-blood, water-drinking skinfolk, as legend says. Now, their watery inheritance survives as myth and echo, their needs rewritten in mineral, light, and wind. Their celebration is the *blossoming*—when, on rare confluences, the city lets loose a cloud of bioluminescent spores, each a message and a promise, lighting the sky brighter than any sun.

Their society is not in opposition to the world, but a conscious turbulence within it: guiding, pruning, negotiating, *joining* the endless growth, the city’s intention etched in each pulse of pigment and every reshaping of the root-wrought bone.

---

**Above all, what lives here are hybrids of intention and root, mind and matrix—entities who do not conquer the plant-world but are its limbs and brains, its hands and memory, as much gardened as gardener, as much machine as myth, as much the place itself as they are citizens within it.**

**You will not find singular, finished things: everything lives within something else, even the city, even the people, even their thoughts. This is a world where all is entangled—a cat’s cradle of root, light, memory, and wind, a cathedral in which every living being is both pillar and prayer.**
