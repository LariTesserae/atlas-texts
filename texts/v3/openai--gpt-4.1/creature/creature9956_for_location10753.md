Who lives here? 

Not exactly a people, not yet—*the* people in the sense of lineage and monument, law and structure, have not quite formed. Instead, those who gather in the cradling vaults of this mountain city are themselves provisional, provisional not in frailty but in open-endedness: beings in flux, dabbling at the edge of their own natures as the world around them dallies with geology and green.

**The Humans and Their Kin**

The dominant presence, if presence must be named, are *humans*, but not as one might know them later—these are early, ruddy with cold, sturdy of limb and shockingly nimble over uncertain ground. Hair wild and uncoiled, sinews pronounced, bodies tuned for climbing, ducking, balancing on slick stone. Some bear chipped stone implements fastened with honey-colored resin and thongs of plaited grass, tools not inherited from forebears but invented and re-invented with each passing season.

They walk with a caution that comes not from fear, but from unfamiliarity—a world so new and unmapped demands new kinds of attention. They watch the mists as if mists might one day speak. Their language is a polyphony of voice and gesture: whistles and longer, echoed syllables meant to carry through the vaults, arms flared in signs that ripple in the half-light.

But woven among them move others, kin and not-kin—perhaps cousins in blood, but sharper of tooth, fiercer of gaze, their lives more half-wild, ranging farther in the stone hollows and venturing into the rock’s raw recesses where eyes adjust or else are monstrous in width and blackness. These are prehumans, or near-humans: slighter, hairier, blending into the riot of green and gray, tools simpler and more fleeting. Gracious with silence, they live in the margins, burning fungus for a smoky warmth, worshipping—or perhaps warning—whatever dwells deeper in the new mountain cracks.

**Children, Elders, Those In Between**

Children are everywhere, but in scattered handfuls, both bolder and softer than their elders. Barefoot and mist-damp, they squeeze between rocks, vanishing into the columnar forest only to erupt out onto a ledge above with laughter echoing like falling water. They are less careful: time is even slower for them, and the city less defined by danger than by marvel.

The elders, fewer in number—still hearty, still strong, but slowed to the rhythm of the mountain itself—claim niches hung an arm’s breadth from the walkways, nests of woven bark and moss where they oversee the city’s slow growth, sharing stories that are, in this embryonic age, equal parts tale and plan. Each word is memory and prophecy, both worked into the daily weaving of roofs and roots.

Most are neither child nor ancient: those at the heart of the city’s pulse, climbing, shaping, tending the wet platforms and woven wells—hunters, fishers, gatherers of fungus and moss, makers of fire and lore.

**Allies and Not-Always Welcome Presences**

Rarely, other kinds stir at the edges. Long-limbed, spindly-footed mammals move in the pre-dawn, faces masked with paint or mud, clinging to the city’s periphery. Are they neighbors, or strangers seeking dry shelter? No one is certain; each encounter is negotiated with hunched stillness and slow gestures, trading fungus for seeds, staring too long, shuffling into the mist before questions become dangerous.

Other presences, not immediately seen, are felt: a sensory parade of eyes not quite glimpsed, tails brushing dew off stone, the click and buzz of insect-life scaled grotesquely by the echoing architecture. At night, small carnivores slip through the high galleries, their tracks shot through fresh puddles, always leading to and from shadows unseen.

**The Spirits, the Yet-Unnamed Powers**

Even more quietly, some believe the city is not uninhabited before them. The dripping rock, the slow pools, the vapor rolling itself up to breathe among the pillars: to the earliest inhabitants, all of these are animate, if only just. There are presences in the stone—a hum, a sense of breath underfoot, a feeling of being watched by the clouds themselves. They stack their shelters with rituals, arrange stones with unseen geometry, tuck gifts of fungus or flint into crevices in deference to whatever listens.

Occasionally, someone—child or not—vanishes a day or returns different: silent for a season, or always looking at the stone as if expecting it to move. Perhaps they have met something—someone?—who lives entirely in the drip, the breath, the hum. No one can quite agree, but no one forgets.

**In Sum**

This embryonic city is home to those who do not yet have titles, only needs and hopes. They are dwellers on the threshold: early dreamers, experimenters, listeners on the skin of a half-cooled world. They are not meant for eternity, nor are they altogether transient. They live at the cusp—species, spirit, stone and seed—the living answer to the city’s own expectant emptiness.

The city, damp and echoing, is less a shell for a thriving population than a matrix for emergence: whoever lives here is on their way to becoming more, and whatever more could be, you, too, might one day discover—if you linger as the mist pools and the world’s green ambitions grow bolder, year on year.
