Those who live here are not simply inhabitants but participants—constituents of an unfolding equation. They are shaped by the precipitation of place, not merely by ancestry or design, but by an ongoing, generational improvisation with shifting substance, law, and the weather of possibility.

**The First Comers and Those Who Grew After**

Some are of flesh, but not of a finished kind. These are lean, often tall, sinewy-limbed and big-lunged, adapted for thin air and constant surprise in topology. Their knees and ankles flex differently than yours; bones carry not just marrow, but minuscule interlays of carbon filament and living mineral, accepted from the city’s debris. Their skin is slick and cool, beaded with condensation, threaded faintly with green and bruise-blue, as if half-rooted in shadow. They wear what the city offers—fabrics grown from mossen sheets or extruded from a reactively pigmented polymer, able to adjust to phase-shifts in light or gravity. Eyes are reflective, almost oily: built for the syrupy, cloud-throttled shift of the day.

Yet among these, not all were born as such. Some arrived during early risings: pioneers, refugees, experimenters—traders of uncertainty. They speak in a polyglot of half-coined dialects, never certain which sounds will stay true after the next atmospheric shudder and communal reorganization. They grip memory gently, break bread with custom, but do not demand roots; when the streets run backward, so do their recollections, unspooling in a dance of adaptive myth.

**The Geistbound: Shaped by Machine and Chance**

There are residents indelibly marked by the city’s making and unmaking. Some are not organic, but neither are they simply machine. *Geistbound*, they call themselves, with frames woven of rebar and chitin, polished by the perpetual drizzle. Each bears a moving aspect of the city: one has a slow, turning light inside the transparent bowl of a skull, like a navigation beacon—or perhaps a memory coil; another is swathed in parasitic ferns and moss, rooting in the cracks along their shoulders and scalp, microtending the city's emerging biomes for mutual benefit.

These mechanical-hybrids communicate by pulse and vibration, through knocks and harmonics on the skeletons of bridges. Their tiny, luminous drones flit out from cloak or carapace, tending circuits, pollinating electric flowers. Children of circuitry, necessity, and odd contagion, the Geistbound remake themselves as the facts of space and boundary shift; sometimes they must change their gait or shed an arm to fit a new aperture, to pass from one neighborhood’s logic to another’s. Pain is a momentary thing, curiosity the constant.

**Fauna That Might Remember Tools**

Not all residents are sapient in human fashion. Baronial slugs, for instance—armored, glistening—know the city’s undersides, sense tension and spark before the rest, and congregate where fresh minerals leak. It is rumored one may broker with them—offer a copper disc or sing a certain note—and they will guide you safely to the mineral pools where memory collects, molten but unforgotten.

The flocks of filament-thin birds appear at intersections of light and mist, moving like coordinated hallucinations. Their wings sometimes catch and carry static charge; their passage leaves a faint, sweet scent. Some say the birds can read intent, and will guide those with purpose across the hazardous bridges that otherwise loop unpredictably—if the seeker’s mind is empty of destination, the birds will not come.

There are creatures among the struts and glow-tubes whose bodies fork, meld, and twist with the new geometries—chitinous runners with impossible segmentation, or glassy, centipedal things that drink both water and charge. Their sapience is never quite confirmed nor denied; sometimes they mimic human voices from the shadows and sometimes construct delicate, webbed sculptures in the lee of vapor chimneys, only to dissolve them come dawn.

**Communion: Temporary and True**

Many of the living are only temporarily individual. Microbial colonies pass between sprawling moss, the plates of breathing buildings, and even into the lungs of residents, sharing chemical signals that briefly overtake individual will. A member of a night-watch might pause mid-stride, blank-faced, listening to the distant song of the city’s choir: infection or participation, sometimes both.

Industry itself sometimes breeds transient creatures. A filament wisp, spun from vapor and spark, may accrue pseudo-flesh and wander the corridors on stolen gravity, speaking in thin, static-laced voices, offering bargains for tools or secrets. Their ambition is simple: to become permanent, to seed descendants in the interstitial flows of reality.

**Children of the Pause**

The true children of the Plateau are not born but annexed from the city’s ongoing logic. They grow in the strange humidity of foundries and undervaults, or arise from pools where time eddies thickest. Some are adopted by families—human, geistbound, or otherwise—and trained to navigate flexible geographies. Others become “solitaries,” existing only in pockets of slow time, half-rumored to slip between local nows, surfacing to offer odd warnings or blessings before dissolving back.

**What Connects Them**

Above all, what binds the residents is not blood or code, not even language, but the continual negotiation with emergence. Each day brings an audit of shape and orientation; alliances form and dissolve with each new bridge or shifting stairwell. To live here is to anticipate, to befriend risk and anomaly.

In the city’s markets, trade is as much in rumor as in goods: the price of a passage, an island of stability, a whisper about which way the physics are bending tonight. In the hive-bright crossroads, the choral moan rises as the city’s metabolism flexes; residents join or retreat from it according to the season of their temperament.

**They Are Not Finished**  

None here are whole. All are in the act of forming, being reshaped by their terrain and their kin, as architecture, organism, and rulebook compose themselves in parallel. The city’s citizens—regardless of how many limbs, wires, eyes, or dreams they possess—are not defined so much by what they are, but by what they are about to become, and how they adapt, collectively and alone, as possibility accretes in the massive, wet, humming air.

You are not a stranger, exactly. The city’s first dreams never have strangers—only new shapes, new iterations, newly becoming—briefly steady on the unfinished plateau.
