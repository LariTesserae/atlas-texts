Who, then, would make a home at the cold lush edge in the sky?

At first, it is easy to see only the animals—the swift, many-limbed, the feather-edged, the furtive that nest in cracks and dart between hollows. But behind the bleached bones and clever weaving of moss, there is something else: a presence capable of cunning construction, fleeting but deliberate as the rest of this world.

**They are called the Shard-Bound.**

**First, Their Form:**
The Shard-Bound are neither fully bestial nor wholly like the people of valleys or forests below. Their bodies seem built from the logic of ledge and shadow. Tall in stature—though rarely upright—each has a hunched, sinuous form, with long arms used for balance and climbing. Their skin is tough, ashen, and dappled, patterned to break up the outline against wet stone and moss-veil alike. In places where condensation forms, so too does a subtle gloss along their hide, rendering them nigh-invisible among wet rocks and lichen.

Their feet are wide and supple, pads feathered with coarse, backward-facing bristles as if to catch the texture of every surface. A prehensile tail—the color of graphite, tipped with a flare of soft bioluminescence—winds for balance or signaling. The face is planar, the nose short and the mouth broad; the eyes are enormous, pale, almost luminous— pupils slit vertical, shielded by a double row of translucent lids that flicker closed in fierce light or open wide in mist and dusk.

Fingers are jointed and extra-long, able to cradle water, braid moss, or carve a sliver of bone against stone. Their voices, when used, ripple at the edge of hearing—whispers, clicks, and occasional, echoing calls like the note of a high bird.

**Brief, Tense Societies:**
The Shard-Bound do not build villages. Their gathering-places are fleeting: a ring of stones to trap warmth beneath an overhang for a night; a shared platform woven of roots above an especially rich patch of moss. They flock in pairs or trios for a season, then splinter and shift, never leaving a place unchanged for long. They are aware of each other—by scent, by the pattern of water left in a collection basin, by the faint marks carved in shaded stone.

Most skill passes through intense, ephemeral apprenticeship: a young one learning the tangle of moss-weaving or condensation-catching, then moving on, leaving a tool in a clever place for another to find. Their rituals are scarce but charged with meaning: gatherings at the first melt of spring, the alignment of bones on midwinter nights, a brief, silent communion over a rare, windless pooling of water.

**How They Live:**
The Shard-Bound eat what the sky's edge gives—small, darting furred things, glimmering beetles and their larvae, tangles of sharp-flavored moss. They are opportunists, learning the fast-cycling, micro-seasonal pulses of prey and green. Nothing is wasted. They burn few fires—a rare luxury, kindled only in saturated air when smoke will cling close and quickly vanish.

They sleep little and lightly. In the absolute brilliance of day, they rest in tucked-away crevices where the stone is deep and always cold. During the lingering twilight, they roam, blinking through a world of edges and luminous green tapestries, always listening, always ready to vanish at the first rush of wings or distant crack of stone.

**To Be Shard-Bound:**
Above all, the Shard-Bound are adaptors—psychologically, biologically, and in their relationship to this mercurial world. Their minds are tuned to volatility: to the feel of condensation forming in an unexpected place, the flick of a shadow indicating a new predator, the rare pattern of rockfall that might spell new shelter. Intelligence, here, is a matter of glimpsing significance in fleeting alignments—a sudden bloom of ferns, the vibration of unfamiliar wings in still air, the smell of blood in the melting dark.

Their myth, if they tell it, is that their world is always beginning, every season an experiment, every life a brief, luminous pattern in the story of stone and sky. Their greatest art is temporary: a diagram traced in wet stone, a binding of moss that lasts until the next rain, a lullaby shared and then forgotten.

They do not hope for mastery or domination over this shattered, rising place. Instead, they are always arriving: alert, provisional, suspended—waiting, perhaps, for a future when the green will become continuous. Or not. For now, to be Shard-Bound is to dwell at a threshold: between danger and shelter, hunger and abundance, solitude and the lean warmth of another body sharing a ledge.

**And when the sun blazes and the cliffs glitter, you might see—just for a moment—a quick, deliberate movement along a high, cold ledge:**

A face gleaming in the fierce white, pausing—watchful, hopeful, never at rest—before vanishing between stone and light.
