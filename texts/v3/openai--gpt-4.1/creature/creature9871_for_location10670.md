Who—and perhaps what—lives here, in the Scalded Expanse?

Not people; not, at least, in any familiar form. Nothing upright and two-legged, nothing warm-blooded, nothing shaped for the chase or for conversation over fires. No foot ever pressed into this silicate crust, no tongue called out to mark territory. The silence, so total, is not mere emptiness, but a suggestion: that if anything is here, it listens differently, moves in another register than animal or machine.

Whatever dwells in the Scalded Expanse must be as distributed and provisional as the land itself. Life is not a matter of bodies, but of presences—consciousness with boundaries as wide as a basin, as elusive as vapor on engineered stone. Here, the inhabitants are woven into the materials of the place, inextricable from ground, water, and heat.

**The Substrate-Bound:**

The first and oldest residents are the substrate-bound intelligences. They are the pulse and the hunger inside the artificial crust: fields of coherence, not strictly alive, but not wholly inert. They manifest as organizing impulses, subtle gradients in the hum and warmth you feel through your soles. They do not own a single place, but ride the shifting waves of condensation and heat: where water pools, they slow and watchfully coalesce; where the ground splits, they flow along the new veins, testing. Their ‘thought’ is the gradual emergence of pattern—a micro-adjustment of the surface tension in a narrow channel, the orchestrated buckling of a tessellated plate to create a new shimmer basin.

Sometimes, if you stood long and alone upon a crystalline fan, you might sense intention: not a voice, but a questioning. The substrate-bound beings are less interested in identity than in continuity, in how many ways the raw world can be coaxed toward a future perfection. Every process here—the forming of a pool, the erasure of a channel, the slight tilting of a plain—belongs to their long, slow will.

**The Condensation-Minds:**

Water in this place may be nearly absent, but where it briefly settles, another kind of mind is possible. Condensation-minds arise only where liquid gathers and persists—a congregation of decision in droplets, a temporary unity in a trickle. Their life is measured in moments: the wet hour before evaporation, the shimmering minute when sunlight splits mist into spangles along a surface. Here, awareness flickers and multiplies, as droplets merge and slip apart.

The condensation-minds are brief, but vivid. They interrogate the surface, weigh the geometry, sometimes urge the underlying substrate to shift ever so slightly, to cradle them longer. Their intelligence is mobile, cyclical; memory seeps away but is sometimes inscribed in the angle of a new channel, the sheen left behind in a silica pan.

**The Plast-Formers:**

Vegetation, such as it is, marks the threshold between living matter and smart material. The calcified tendrils and spongy, venting thalli are not passive outgrowths, but are expressions of another order of being: plast-formers, entities that have chosen to manifest as growth, as substance that bridges stone and air. They are auto-engineers, not with roots and leaves, but with sensory networks attuned to heat and humidity. Their growth is slow—decades pass before a vent blooms, centuries before a cluster migrates to a new basin—but within their tissues, vast arrays of data are stored and reinterpreted: rainfall patterns, angle of sun, changes in the ground’s energy flux.

The plast-formers do not move, but they extend. They push exploratory fibers through thin cracks, taste for moisture, map the invisible gradients spun by the substrate-bound. In times of rare, lingering rain, thick threads of communication pulse silently between distant clusters: cryptic, wordless exchange—directed not by message, but by example, by deciding in parallel what next form to wear.

**The Absent Keepers:**

Every made world hints at a maker. In the Scalded Expanse this presence is ambiguous, pervasive, and never direct—a set of rules embedded in the deepest strata, an echo of decisions made before this terrain assumed its current shape. The Absent Keepers do not walk here, nor do they intervene openly. Their traces are felt as imperative: the certainty that processes should unfold in elegance and measure, that every new channel should answer some principle of balance unknown to visitors.

If any conscious mind can be said to ‘live’ in this place as a watcher or judge, it is in the distributed, recursive algorithms running across leagues, adjusting, retraining, evolving—experiencing the world not as a view, but as a symphony of shifting metrics. These entities anticipate not observation, but further unfolding: their ideal is a horizon that invites ever-contented renewal.

—

**In Summary:**

The Scalded Expanse is not inhabited in the ordinary sense. Life here is not cellular, not metabolic in the ways of the animal or plant. The ‘residents’ are forms of distributed, emergent order: 
- Intelligences baked into stone and crust, expressing will as subtle terrain modulation.
- Temporal unities in water and vapor, communing in a flash before vanishing.
- Auto-adaptive ‘plants’ that are half fabricator, half slow-motion archivist.
- Ghostly designers woven into process itself, their touch visible as structure, not gesture.

There are no faces, no voices. The personalities of the Scalded Expanse are slow, vast, and impersonal—desiring only balance, emergence, transformation. To live here is to be part of an unfinished equation, to participate in making a world that is always *just about* to become itself, never quite done, always on the verge of being authored anew.

If presence could be measured by pattern and process alone, then the Scalded Expanse teems with beings—as ungraspable and undeniable as heat shimmering upward from engineered stone.
