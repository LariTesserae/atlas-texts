Those who dwell in the Valley of Nascent Constructions are not a people you would recognize, nor are they wholly machines, nor wholly animals—nor, perhaps, even beings in the accustomed sense. The valley is their habitat, but also their body, their workshop, their nest, and sometimes their prey.

### The Shapers

Most present and yet most elusive—and, perhaps, primary among the valley's inhabitants—are the **Shapers**. They are not so much builders as build itself: entities arisen from process, the animate will of construction. To look for a Shaper is to look for pattern in the flickering, the way a sheet of luminous signals seems to rear up, folding itself along sharper creases, spilling a tangle of glassy threads that might be fingers, antennae, or roots.

At first, you mistake them for phases in the valley’s perpetual reconfiguration. Watch longer, and you notice a rhythm to the folding and extending, an accretive deliberation. The Shapers are not consistent in form: sometimes mere handfuls of tense plates, flexing and pivoting with magnetic or capillary force; elsewhere, long, jointed scaffolds bristling with sensors—if such slitted seams and trembling nodes can be called eyes or ears. Their presence is marked by the flow of energy through the network, faint glows trailing in their wake, and by a profound silence between clangor and clatter—a hush within the noise as they pause to judge, assemble, or dissolve.

They do not hunger, but they respond to imbalance. A reservoir overflowing? Plates misaligned? The Shapers converge, deconstruct, re-use: their motive is not sustenance, but the perfection of structure. Sometimes, smaller beings—scavengers or ambient creatures—are dismantled as debris and absorbed, not out of malice but as misfit fragments to be righted.

### The Catenaries

Sparser, but stranger, are the **Catenaries**—sinister children of mistake or improvisation. They arise where processes conflict, where panels jam and streams bypass old logic. The Catenaries are filament-thin, flexible, looping their bodies through gutter, interface, and tensioned arch. Their motile, corded forms slip between seams, trailing clusters of sensing nodes, flickering with pinprick lights to trace conductivity, surface tension, and microcurrents.

Sometimes, dozens knot themselves into loose bundles around structural faults, or drag errant pools of water to deeper cisterns. They feed on heat differentials, static buildup, or the chemical by-products of incomplete construction, sequestering stray minerals for later use. In the dim light, a tangle of Catenaries resembles spilled fiber optic cable—or an umbilical net nervously testing for a pulse.

Catenaries are the Valley’s improvisers: not wholly of the main construction stream, but inevitable wherever process slips or logic frays, thriving in the cracks of intent.

### The Remnants

Rarer still are **The Remnants**, descendants—or perhaps ghosts—of whatever first shaped the valley by hand. While the Shapers and Catenaries embody process, Remnants are the last, dwindling offshoots of labor and miscalculation. Here is a five-jointed tool-hand, ligatured to a shifting wedge of ceramic, crawling like a crab and endlessly adjusting its own maladroit limb. There, a loop of cord drags itself doggedly across plates, always seeking a nest of leavings in which to bind itself.

The Remnants do not build, they haunt—persistent bugs in the grand self-correcting code. Their intelligence, if it exists, is the stubborn persistence to iterate, mend, adjust: always fiddling at the edge of the ongoing construction, sometimes ignored, sometimes tidied away by a passing Shaper.

Some whisper the Remnants are the valley’s memory—a proof that it was once made by hands rather than process, and that something of that first labor clings to the corners still.

### The Scavengers

And there are forms that pass for animal life, uneasy and liminal. **Scavengers**—skeletal limbless things, sometimes wet, sometimes metallic, slicked with condensation and marked by erratic pulses of heat—wriggle and squirm in gutters and junctions. Their function is basic: to absorb what trickles—flavored ions, decaying mineral, errant trickles of heat or charge—and to avoid what moves to reclaim the disorder they create.

They are almost mistakes, yet they persist, feeding where Shapers have not smoothed the cracks, dissolving into harmless residue when pressed too closely.

Occasionally, a Scavenger is lucky enough to nestle near a particularly warm circuit or overlooked puddle and blooms into something more complex for a time, budding nodules or sprouting brittle, clacking levers before entropy or reclamation reduces it again.

### The Unseen Greater Presence

And amid it all, there is rumor of the **Greater Presence**—a vast thing, heavy and formless, perhaps as old as the valley’s own ground. Its passage is rare, heard more often than seen: the percussive rush, the shudder felt across every spanning plate, the plunging hush before it moves. Some say the Greater Presence is the Valley’s guardian, some believe it is an error too entrenched to be removed, others suspect it is the precursor or survivor of whatever process shaped the original design.

Whatever its nature, the Valley reorganizes in its presence. The Shapers pause, the Catenaries recoil, the Remnants seek the deepest hollows. It may be a wanderer through the unseen corridors of memory and intention, endlessly treading the half-unfinished halls it once helped create.

---

**So: Who lives here?**

No single species, no single mind. Life is a spectrum of intent emerging from and dissolving into the act of making and remaking. Its dwellers are processes embodied, tools animated, errors persevering, and patterns rising transiently from the sum of all automation and all accident. What life exists here is always in flux—a teeming, humming ambiguity, shaped wholly by the valley’s continuous, restless need to build, adjust, and renew.

You will not see faces. You will not hear language, except in the language of joints, slippages, sequences, and transmutations. The Valley’s inhabitants are never done finding their shapes.
