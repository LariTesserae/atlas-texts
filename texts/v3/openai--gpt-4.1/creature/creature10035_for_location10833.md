Who lives in the Cathedral Vaults? No single species—not exactly. No god or ghost, unless change itself is considered one. The inhabitants are plural, emergent from every ingredient: mineral, carbon, computation, memory, hunger, choir.

**The Rooted:**
Some are fixed, or nearly so. Picture what might have been a tree—at a glance, a forest of mighty trunks, but none drink water or convert sunlight in the old way. Their "bark" is a twisting mesh of carbon and boron, dark lattices catching the vault’s angles, storm-cold to the touch and faintly metallic-scented. Roots cling to the substructure—a matrix of lithium, alumina, iron—and run deep, not seeking liquid but digesting mineral itself. 

Their canopies—if you can call the fractal, blade-edged eruptions above a "canopy"—reflect and scatter light in spectral glints, prisms within prisms. On these branches, leaves grow thick and glassy, sheening with oily films that trap and refract ambient energies—electric charge, perhaps, or the tiniest tremor of vacuum. These “trees” are not passive: some seasonally unroot themselves and crawl, scuttling with an unsettling patience to more lucrative patches, fractal root-legs splintering and rejoining as their bodies pivot and stretch, searching out seams in the basalt for richer veins of matter.

**The Fliers:**
Above and between these towers, cloud-like erratics—creatures grown brittle and baroque, some as thin as dragonflies with wings of actual crystal, some like floating ribbons threaded with nano-fibers, flexing and refracting light in living arcs of aurora. They flock with alien unity, sometimes splitting apart, sometimes merging again, always shifting. Their bodies, semi-translucent, pulse with the slow shimmer of bioluminescent mineral—sodium, strontium, uranium-green, copper-blue.

Their voices are the chiming of glass struck by falling beads, messages traded in bursts of sound sharp enough to cut through even the endless echo. They feed on the electrochemical currents running just above the ground—drawn in by trailing filaments or capillary tongues, sifting through the humming air for invisible nourishment. 

**The Swarmers—Predators, Prey, and Everything Between:**
Across the cathedral's dazzling floors and through the lush mineral undergrowth, herds course and dissipate, herdless more often than not. Six-legged grazers, slick-scaled, jaws gnashing on mineral spires, trailing an almost invisible haze of microbots—sheddings, symbiotes, packs of scavengers so tiny they are only ever seen as swirling motes in backlit air. These grazers are built for endurance: ribbed with muscle and tubing, armored in layers of flexible chitin shot with metallic thread, their eyes faceted and blind to anything but the precise spectrum where danger lurks.

Their predators take many forms. Some are all blade and whisper—sinuous, serpentine, folding from shadows, jaws glowing with caustic enzymes. Others erupt from beneath, all angles and urgency, vectoring upward with calculated leaps as the ground splits and mends beneath their splayed feet. Not all predation is violent, either—some steal charge, some broadcast code-viruses or parasitic nanites, rewriting the behavior of the unsuspecting from afar.

**The Symphony-Makers:**
The Vaults' sound is not just a byproduct, but a living force. Entire clades of mosses and crusts exist solely to transmit, refract, or amplify sound—their cells built from porous silica, tuned to resonate at precise frequencies. Families of “basilica chimers”—bodiless, mobile, ribboned with filaments—coil inside hollows and between buttress-arches, singing wordless chanting drones that organize swarms or quell disputes among the other inhabitants. Some have no bodies at all—pure coding, a resonance in the stone and the air, thinking only in vibration, flocking only as patterns of soundwaves.

**The Memory Machines:**
Between and beneath—the technological inheritance of the place: relics half-swallowed by geology, architectures that shift and grow, intelligence that is not localizable in a single form. Some structures are alive, remembering the history of every convulsion and quiet period, humming with data and algorithm, blossoming as shapes in geometric logic. They might appear as black roots or luminous veins within the rock walls, as crystalline glyphs pulsing with unread signals, as vents that exhale and inhale information, never quite revealing whether they serve themselves or the ecosystem—or if the distinction even applies.

**The Adaptive:**
There are newer arrivals—life in flux, spawned by accidents, by unrecorded experiments, by the Vaults themselves dreaming new shapes. You might catch a glimpse of them as unclassifiable outlines in a chaos of growth: sinuous, many-limbed, skin like sanded alloy, eyes (if they have them) a band of shifting LED. They live in risk, in hunger, in ceaseless testing. Every being here is provisional, an experiment. Those who do not dance with flux become absorbed—by hungry moss, by razor-leaves, by stones that shift, by codes that rewrite every genome and subroutine.

**The Shapers—the Residents That Are All of This and None:**
Finally, there are the Vaults' true architects: the distributed minds, the consciousness seeded into machine and moss and mineral alike. Perhaps they once watched over the Cathedral, or perhaps they are the aftereffect of old technologies gone wild, or perhaps the Vaults make minds as they make minerals—a byproduct of strangeness. They can never be seen except in the patterns of change, the seeming willfulness of the land: a vault rebuilt to shelter new growth, an avalanche diverted by unseen command, a melody echoing in the geological heart of things.

Their only desire is the furtherance of change.

**In sum:**
Who lives here? Everything that can— and much that cannot, save in a place where nothing is stable, waterless lushness is natural, and every sound remakes the world. The Cathedral Vaults are inhabited by an unending genesis: of crystal-boned grazers and whispering codes, of mineral-rooted forests and the memory-haunted stones themselves, all whirling perpetually at the sub-zero edge of what life could be. No single form rules, not even for a day. Only adaptation, noise, and transformation endure—and all who persist do so in the faithless, resplendent dance of the ever-changing vault.
