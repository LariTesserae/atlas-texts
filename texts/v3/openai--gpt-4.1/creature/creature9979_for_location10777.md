Who—or what—lives here?

To approach an answer, you must rid yourself of the urge to imagine a single dominant form, a familiar inhabitant who might stride through corridors and name things. Whoever lives here does not do so alone, or as a singular species, but as a concert—an ecosystem orchestrated toward an intelligible but inhuman order.

**The Urban Flora**  
First and ever-present are the planetary plants themselves—without leaf or bark, fundamentally other, yet city-building and city-being all at once. Their consciousness, if it exists, is deep and slow, expressed in growth-rhythms, spatial logic, pulses of chemical exchange that guide roots to twine or tendrils to pull away, organizing the passages and plazas with patient geometric will. They may sense their environments electrochemically, responding with slow swells and contractions to even distant presences.

These plants are not mere background, but principal inhabitants—the sculptors, the infrastructure, perhaps even the ultimate arbiters of territory. They communicate, decide, and respond by shifting their living architecture, directing resources where consensus (if consensus is the right word) emerges through chemical ballots and structural negotiation. Ownership is not property, but a temporary synchronization in the dance of root, stem, and membrane.

**The Caretaker Fauna**  
Interwoven with—almost inseparable from—the living plant-fabric are myriad animals. Their forms are shaped by dryness: soft, pale, flexible, adapted for desiccated atmospheres and quick absorption of such rare moisture as drifts their way. Small ones dart and scramble within the interstices of root and stem—tunneling, tending, biting away dead tissues, perhaps even pruning growth to maintain avenues or to signal a change in the city’s ‘mood.’

Some of these animals may act as pollinators, but in this underland their roles go beyond that: they are the gardeners, the regulators, the enzymes within the body of the city. In swarms, they clean the veins of the great plant-structures, spread vital spores or cells to seed new neighborhoods, and reap the powdery mineral dust to enrich root and shoot alike. They glide through vertical chasms on wings of petal or chitin, moving with the calculated grace of creatures aware the city is not stone, but perpetual metamorphosis.

Larger caretaker-beings may exhibit more deliberate intelligence; perhaps they tend the interface between plant and industry, monitoring the braided turbines, teasing new conduits into shape, or regulating the flow of stored chemical energy. Some move slow, with an air of stewardship; others run on jointed limbs with a nervous, cooperative precision.

**Predators and Shadows**  
A third layer, half-glimpsed, moves among this harmony: the predators—sleek, opaline, idling through shadow, all economy and patience. Their presence culls and sculpts, preventing any one form of maintenance-creature from overrunning the living substrates. Their intelligence is not centered—more in the form of distributed cunning, memory living in pheromone trails and the careful calibration of their senses to the living city’s needs.

They are tolerated, even necessary, by the root and shoot, for they are another balancing force: the mutualists keep the city healthy, the predators keep the mutualists honest. If any element grows too fast or too greedy, the system adjusts with subtle, predatory pressure.

**The Shapers—more than animal, less than plant**  
But the most curious inhabitants are those at the threshold—hybrids, not easily classified. Taller, more conspicuous, they move with deliberation down main avenues or haunt the wide communal spaces. Their bodies are crucibles of animal and plant, dressed in layered skins that are grown, not woven; cloaked in the colors of root or adorned with the patchy luminescence of leaf. Limbs are flexible, sheath-enclosed, tipped with ciliary pads for gripping the living walls.

They are the city’s stewards or architects: their labor is not to impose order, but to nudge, to coax, to merge individual will with the pace of vegetative reform. Some carry filaments with which they can graft or merge tissues, facilitating the plant’s adjustment to new needs. Others carry little more than knowledge—signals, scents, and gestures with which to negotiate with the rooting masses, to settle disputes by redirecting growth, or by composing new communal spaces where empty vaults persist.

Their intelligence is a gestalt—a blending of memory encoded in organic implants and tradition taught by proximity, by literally plugging bioluminescent filaments from body to root, from root to mind. Their speech is a choreography: posture, scent, chemical flux, the patterning of light across their surfaces.

They live lightly, rarely staking permanent presence within the city’s shifting neighborhoods. Their homes are mobile, assembled from living plant and carried with them, or else are brief alcoves coaxed into existence for a night, then allowed to retract back into the whorled avenues.

**Emergent Spirits—The Underland’s Oldest Inhabitants**  
There are signs—rare, unmistakable—of older, vaster minds: presences seeded deep in the mineral, the embers of intelligence from before the city. These are not beings that move, but are felt in the slow chemical tide: old pulses, templates, unconscious strategies, quiet directives submerged amid the logic of construction and regrowth. Perhaps it is their dreams that guide the city’s emergence, their archives not written but coded in the microstructure of root and dust.

**All Together: A World as Community**  
So, what lives here is less a set of individuals, more a polyphony:

- The city-plants, monumental, organizing and organized, self-aware in ways slow and continental.
- The tenders—animal, partly-plant, stewards, mechanics, and informants—whose intelligence is collaborative and often anonymous, jobs and bodies blending in cycles of birth, growth, symbiosis, predation, and death.
- The ghosts—ancient patterns, the landscape’s own, offering the template upon which all else grows.

In this realm, to be alive means to participate in building: to tend and be tended, to be woven into the fiber of an ongoing emergence whose boundaries between plant, animal, and machine have been erased. The question of *who* lives here is identical to the question of *how* things live here—always growing, always re-organizing, in city and organism indistinguishable, and in community without strict border between self and world.
