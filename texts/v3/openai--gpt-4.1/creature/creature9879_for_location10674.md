*Who lives here?*

### The shape of survival

They are not one thing, not a single people or form, but a convergent answer to the deep structures and hungers of this place. They are spindled and lean, built for absence and distance, for crossing bridge and void, keenly tuned to the pulse of cold and condensation, alert to every glimmer and crash in the shifting dark.

Their bodies are mostly obscured by swathes of moss-skin—garments that are grown and shed, not woven, swaddled around them for wet warmth and silence. When the travelers shed these wrappings, what reveals itself is a form both creature and echo of the environment: elongated limbs jointed to wrap around vines, wide splayed feet to step across slick filaments or moss-damp ridges, fingers long and sensitive, each with keratinous ridges for gripping. Eyes are large, luminous and refractive, gathering every drop of light—more glass than flesh—mirroring fungus-glow and the distant starlight. Their faces seem boneless, blank save for the subtle shifting of skin above the jaw, which shows fear or amusement by rippling.

They move fluidly, with swift, measured gestures, often in short bustling bursts—crossing open chasms in a few long limber leaps, or wriggling between snarled growths. Their spines are flexible, able to arch backward or sideward, built for contorting through living tunnels or bracing against a quivering arch when the ground trembles. Occasionally, you might glimpse a tail—prehensile and wrapt in lichen, used only to keep balance or carry possessions.

### Senses and speech

To live here is to be layered in sharp perception. They *listen* more than they see: ears are hidden, but the inner bones are attuned to even the slowest pulses of plant-life and the rippling percussion of rain. When they must speak, their voices are soft, brief, often lost to the wind before the sentence has time to finish—but they more often hum, click, or tap softly on wood and moss, using vibrational language carried in the bones of the living world. A warning, a summons, a mourning song: all ghost through the latticework, sometimes for hours, before the song dies.

### Sociality and solitude

They are nomadic, moving in small clusters—often blood-tied, but just as often bound by pact or ritual gathering. Ties are fluid, family is chosen as often as born. No home lasts more than a cold cycle or two; once the local mosses are grazed bare or a burst rain floods the hollows, they move along the living thoroughfares, following the burrows of the giant worms or the trilling choruses of migrating herds. They build sparingly, mending the world rather than overmastering it—twining broken joints together, encouraging sap flow with gentle pressure, patching sheathes with living lichen. Their fires are tiny, flickering, precious as the rarest flowers.

Solitude is respected: some walk alone for seasons, watched over at a distance by the carrier birds, and called home only by the deep, pulsing vibrational song. There is no word for exile—none need be banished; to dwell apart too long is peril enough.

### Mind and myth

Technology is cradled in humility: everything borrowed, nothing owned. Their best tools are carved from fallen spindles, shaped by hours of slow abrasion, bound with sticky sap or twisted gut. For edge, they use crystal bark and fungal glass. Shelter is draped, grown, never nailed; they sleep curled high across arch or deep within trunk, always ready to slip away when a predator thrums or a night-storm begins its song.

They prize *memory* and *story*—but not in written word, for writing would vanish here, soaked in water or blown down chasm. Stories are sung into moss caverns, hummed so the walls remember the rhythm. Great secrets are given only to the youngest, to be kept for a single cycle, then set again into the living world, so none weighs too long what cannot be changed.

Their myths swirl with ambiguity: gods are not creators but travelers, wandering for warmth, creating nothing but pathways and echoes. The world’s sharp newness is explained as the shedding of an older, slower sphere—the lowlands—from which they rose with the first titanic stalks. Ancestors are not cherished for wisdom but for their cleverness in crossing distance, in surviving the longest silence, in listening for the subtle signals in surface and wind.

### The relationship with others

Most animals are kin in ecology—hunted only if starvation presses, but often followed, learned from, even collaborated with. Some striders learn to trust their presence, even allowing them to ride for a time along the highest arches. Gliders are emulated—children craft mossy wing-capes, leaping from ledges to taste, if only briefly, the slower fall and the soft impact in moss. Burrow-worms are both feared and honored; their tremors are read as omens, their castings harvested for minerals collected as they tunnel far, far away from the upper cold.

Predators, when they come, create a strange stillness—entire families pressed silent into trunk and hollow, listening for the thunderclap step or the harsh, resinous call from above. When one is lost, grief is brief but total: a piercing keening, then a ritual quiet as a moss-wrapped token is pressed into the world’s shifting seam.

### The undertone of the uncanny

A tension runs through all their living—the same slightly *off* physics that distorts the world. They trust foot and vine, but they know sometimes, when leaping, their body will linger in the air a hair longer than sense. Sometimes water evaporates from a cupped leaf impossibly fast, or cold grips harder without warning. They blame the world’s will—call it the Deep Surmounting, the spirit of the high dark, or simply “The Weight.” Their rituals sometimes seek to appease this force, to harmonize skin and stone and stalk with the trembling gravity beneath.

### In sum

The dwellers of this stratospheric, hollow, living world are neither lords nor tenants, but improvisers—sharp-eyed, moss-clad, quick, solitary or familial as the cycle demands. Their lives are brief bursts of warmth against the endless cold, quick leaps in the yawning dark, small pulses of noise lost against a background of shifting wind and rain. They listen, adapt, and move on. That is how to inhabit a world both at the top of existence and always half-vanished—a living architecture, threaded with story, silence, and song.
