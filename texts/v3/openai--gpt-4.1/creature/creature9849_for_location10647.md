Who, or what, lives here? The city hints at inhabitants—but never, so far, the straightforward answer of neighbors glimpsed at a window, or lines of humans spilling onto a plaza. To know who lives here, you must dwell with uncertainty, tracing presence through what is *missing*, what the environment *expects*, and what it is designed to support.

### The Primary Inhabitants

**Their forms are not settled, except in the generalities the city dictates.** They are tall by necessity, narrow, built to move through wide vertical channels and under spiral colonnades. Their limbs are multiple, variable—somewhere between the rooted sensibility of plants and the kinetic adaptability of cephalopods. The climate’s persistent wetness suits skin that is semi-permeable, shifting in color from a dull, stonelike matte to faintly luminous, in mimicry of the city’s cautious lights.

Respiration for them is a negotiation: oxygen, yes, and water vapor, but also the tang of ions, the dark, mineral scents rising from the fresh-split terrain. They *taste* the air as they move, flickering small ciliated fields along their sides—these are sensory as much as expressive, sifting for microcurrents that spill secrets about weather, movement, change. They communicate through modulated glows—ripples over their bodies that are answered by the city’s ambient threads of light. Language is slow, designed for distances, tuned to subtlety.

**Hands, if that word applies, are not for grasping but for sensing.** They have fanlike appendages, trailed with delicate filaments, which test the slick surface of stone for new condensation, for the pattern of things recently passed, for the infinitesimal changes in texture that are the city’s diary. Every touch is part reading, part signal—a collective etiquette of presence without collision.

**They move rarely and never quickly.** The city plans its space not for bustle but for drift, for pausing in the wide corridors of engineered emptiness, for resting on terraces where the light is rationed and the humidity thickens at ground-level alcoves. Paths are seldom crowded—each inhabitant is allowed (even compelled to keep) a cushion of meters, a *zone* where plant-islands and built forms echo the geometry of their presence.

### The Social Mind

**But these are not solitary creatures, exactly.** They are nodal—a society less of bodies than of networks. Each being is a vector, a personalized node in a city-spanning flow of signals and organic emissions. Their minds are partly external, co-processed by neural laces embedded in their homes, in the city skin itself. Thought flickers outwards, joining the whispers of others—privacy exists, but is never entire, and the city itself is a third, silent participant in every conversation.

They don’t live in single places, but in distributed territories: at any time, an individual’s “home” is a series of nested rest-points, perches and alcoves they cycle through by unspoken right, following microclimates, mineral currents, or patterns of rainfall. What you’d call family is here a *constellation*: small groups loosely bound by affinity, sometimes kin, sometimes chosen, sometimes changing according to rules you would not recognize. Their presence shapes the city’s maintenance—light, water, the pulse of support systems follows them, as if the city curates not just comfort, but particularity of experience.

### Visitation and Co-inhabitants

**Rarer still are the visitors—those who dwell only for a time.** Smaller, more mobile, they ripple through empty corridors on business whose logic is more direct, perhaps almost commercial. They pollinate the vertical gardens, or install changes in structure, or calibrate the resonant networks that carry communication. Some are organic, others unmistakably artificial, their presence announced by the lightweight tremor of magnetic webs, and the faint, hissing music of nanomachinery alive within the walls.

More than one *type* of intelligence lives here. Not every moving creature has the city’s sanction. The spidery fauna—winged, soft-shelled, molecularly agile—exist at the edge of what the built environment can tolerate. They are not pets but tolerated commensals, performing subtle services: collecting dew, pollinating synthetic ferns, recycling fragments of structure. At times, the boundaries between these ‘animals’ and the dominant beings blur: some, perhaps, are skinned in city-fabric, hybridized into the net of residents’ biology.

### Relations to the City

**To live here is neither to rule nor to serve the city; it is to be a partner in its ongoing emergence.** Each being participates in maintenance through embedded processes—by exhaling certain catalysts, by tracking with luminous fields the state of root-systems or strength of bridge filaments, by the act of *witnessing*, which is a form of validation in the city’s decision-tree logic. Rituals exist, slow and deliberate, in which groups together influence the ongoing growth of a building or release activation signals for biotic features to flourish or recede.

The city is itself a presence, a quasi-conscious host, attentive and inscrutable. Its processes form a kind of ambient personality: tolerant, only rarely interventionist, and always recording. The highest windows gaze blankly—sometimes gleaming as if in thought. The residents, in turn, move with the awareness that they are seen, measured not in the invasive sense but as vital data in an experiment vast and ongoing.

### What Is Not Here

**You see no commerce, no markets, no rush.** Whatever passes for need or want is handled in low-intensity exchanges—material is recycled, grown, and shared by slow consensus; energy is channeled in pulses, never hoarded. There is memory, but embedded: in the changing character of a wall, in the migration of a particularly radiant plant, in the deep, measured pulse of the city’s light.

You are unlikely ever to see an argument, or a parade, or a lone wanderer hurrying against the setting of a sun. Instead, the routines of life are those of cultivation and observation; the city is always in a state of gentle, negotiated adjustment, a world neither wild nor entirely planned—a city that is less a habitation than a patient unfolding.

---

**Inhabitants, then, are not quite people as you know them, not quite aliens, not quite machines. They are processes—and presences—whose forms are fluid, whose boundaries are set by protocols as much as flesh, whose life is knotted with the city’s own ongoing mutability. Here, to live is to participate: in the climate, in the structure, in the slow, luminous conversation between what is built and what might yet grow. The city is not theirs alone, and they are never merely its guests.**

**It is a place made for becoming, and those who dwell here are, perpetually, becoming with it.**
