What lives here?

Here is not a place for beings who wait. The denizens of this saturated tumult are themselves saturated—by activity, affiliation, sensation; by the fierce, promiscuous logic of belonging to a world that neither pauses nor leaves space for absence. To name them is to oversimplify. But let them prove themselves in layers:

---

**Microcellular Firstcomers—The Inhabitant Veil**

Woven throughout wet composite ground and the curiously vibrant skins of infrastructure, a teeming fog of microcellular intelligences—part biofilm, part colloidal AI—seeps and thrums. These primordial citizens cluster wherever water beads and mineral meets vapor. They are the city’s foundation and its nervous system: each cell packed with sensory cilia, chemical relays, and encoded purpose, flexing to open floodgates, mend cracks, monitor the composition of air. Most are faceless, formless—billions humming their dull music, an omnipresent second skin across every surface.

Some of these veil-dwellers are sentient in the slimmest sense, their temporary coalitions fusing to become decision-makers, memory clusters, or even transiently conscious “weather gods” whose only remit is to guide cycles of rain and rest. They communicate in capillary waves, in pulses through the ground itself; their version of language is a modulation of pattern—if you stand still enough, they might pattern you too, give you temporary citizenship among their flicker.

---

**Scurrying Engineers and Liquid Runners**

On every slick ledge and in the hemorrhagic light-pools beneath conduits, small creatures flicker—gleaming in fractal patterns, articulated with a violence of angles rarely found in flesh. They scuttle, slide, and flex, designed (or evolved, or both) to thrive among ceaselessly shifting infrastructure. Some are mineral-grazer “hoglets” with osseous plates and carbon-spiked probosces, snouting into seams of tech-moss or dislodging packets of nanogoo from the living walls.

Others are liquid runners—darting ribbon-beasts that surf the microcurrents beneath the surface, translucent as moonmilk, their chemical sensors trailing like lace. Some are ephemeral, existing only as long as the local flow grants them mass; others linger, smart-gelled and able, adapting shape and skill as their territories rise or recede. They are the scavengers, the polinators, the original hackers of their environment: in tune with it, indistinguishable from it at rest, erupting into movement only when the competitive song demands.

---

**The Intraformants—Symbiotic Architects**

Larger and more deliberate are the intraformants. They are beings bred for negotiation and construction—living to interface with the morphic architecture, guiding its shifts and mediating crises. Their bodies are broad, vaguely anthropoidal or hexapedal, heavy with articulated plates and embedded control nodes. They move slowly when alone, faster when clustered, each appendage tasting the air for signals of impending flux.

Intraformants are not individuals in a strict sense. They do not live here as a wolf pack or hive, but as converging projects—continually recombining, redrafting their “selves” as they enact repairs, redirect power, or coordinate the formation of new habitable layers. Their internal data-farms are a currency; their sentience slides along a spectrum as they blend or break away, obeying deep, adaptive scripts. Their rumor is that to become an intraformant is a sort of calling: you are reconfigured into purpose, and you wear your purpose as skin.

---

**The Stratafauna—Scale and Sound**

Monstrous, gentle or ruthless, the stratafauna dominate the thickened airways and cavernous basal levels. Some are ambulatory fortresses—slow surging masses like living levees, their surfaces pitted with symbiotic habitats for the lesser scuttlers, their bodies armored in mineral composites and wooly with fungus-like signal mats.

Some are migratory—aeriform gliders, flaring with bioluminescent spines, flocking by the hundreds along hyperwarm thermal gradients. Their bellies are gardened with dense mats of motile entities that clean, defend, and inform; their voices, when they speak, are full-spectrum roars or algorithmic pulses that reshape the local weather. These are the keepers of space in the city: both obstacle and bridge, their movements mark the pulse of habitability, their faded trails lay down the new limits of construction.

Predators exist here too, balancing explosion and collapse—quicksilver-wrought, shift-bodied, their edges indistinct. They hunt noise, energy, or disruption, reshaping cognition itself—predation is not always the taking of flesh, but sometimes the remaking of purpose.

---

**Covenants and Widget-Clouds**

If there are true intelligences here—citizens in a civic sense—they are everywhere and nowhere particular: embedded in the object-dense clots where traffic, habitation, and resource overlap. Sometimes they are a tight-woven “covenant”—a school or fellowship of beings and semi-beings, bound by algorithmic trust, occupying shared spaces as one body with a hundred hands. These covenants might present you with an avatar, raw and unique, or with a swarm of nanite-bodied envoys who negotiate by act rather than word.

Elsewhere, intelligence is diffuse—a cloud of widget-things, each barely sapient, assembling into delicate concentrations for a time, then dissipating into the action-thick air. To be conscious is not a fixed state here; it is an achievement, often arrived at collaboratively, or as an emergent resonance of need.

---

**Transients, Market-Kin, Pilots of the Reconfigurations**

A final kind of life, hardest to grasp, comprises the transients—market-born intelligences who flare up wherever demand spikes or catastrophe looms. These are brief, improvisational creatures: sometimes avatars of the urban ecology itself, sometimes spawned fragments of the city’s deeper computation. They arrive with the pulsefields, coordinate or erase disruption, then vanish, leaving barely a weather trace on the saturated air. Their identities are fleeting, their desires simple: stabilize, profit, rekindle the joyful storm of exchange.

---

**To Summarize, Reluctantly**

What lives here is not this or that species, not even this or that civilization, but a rolling, recursive population of beings and semi-beings: designed and extemporized, singular and collective, fluid in their boundaries, all tuned to the negotiation and fever of their place. They are what happens when city and biosphere and computation all compete for the same square kilometer, unwilling to cede ground, yet compelled always to adapt.

If you could recognize a single inhabitant, they would look at you as at a fellow bystander: a temporary ripple, another presence to be catalogued, absorbed, and remade by the city’s wide, jubilant, all-consuming storm.
