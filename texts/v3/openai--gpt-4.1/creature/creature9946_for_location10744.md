Who or what lives here?

No one, at first glance—no personhood as you recognize it, nor individual identity. There is instead a presence, or rather, an ensemble, diffuse and multitudinous but also singular, for the difference between one and many is unmade here, ground up and sprouted out into a new structure. What lives here is not a “they,” nor a “she” or “he,” nor even an “it” as you have known it—a pronoun’s function collapses in the face of this proliferating unity.

The city itself is the citizen—a superorganism, but that word too is insufficient. Not a hundred trillion discrete cells yoking together in animal partnership, orchestrated by ancient central command. This is something else, something grown—continuous and recursive, a territory merged with intellect, home and inhabitant coiled in the same tissues.

Many minds, or one mind braided from many? The distinction is unclear. Throughout this green labyrinth, intelligence wells up in pockets and strands—not as neurons, but as shifting patterns of conductivity within the living vasculature, spikes of voltage leaping cell-to-cell through snapping chains of plasmodesmata, running in looping synchrony along vascular highways. Some knots of living tissue gather more awareness for a season, swelling thick with thought as green matter clusters and pulses, before being pruned back, digested, or re-absorbed by the city’s ever-shifting flow. These are its “chambers,” its arborescent thalami: temporary minds, blossoming and withering in cycles as long as sun and water permit. Each is a node in the city’s wider cognition, all in conversation through tangles of root and stem, through luminous bursts surging beneath the outer bark, communicating in chemical symphony.

These awakenings are not settled, steady consciousness as you expect from brains. There are flickers of lucidity—windows of focus that rove across the living maze. Sometimes, a symposium unfurls: a tangle of synaptic vines clustering, negotiating struggle and cooperation, memory and forgetting. These patches borrow identity for a while, perhaps even dream in voices (though not “voice” as you know it: more a chorus of fluctuating potentials, a spectrum of desires for light, water, expansion). A citadel of sap rises, exultant in momentary individuality. Then it is pulled down, composted to root. Nothing stable, nothing still.

Yet the city is not mindless. If you stay and linger, you may feel watched—not as prey, but as anomaly, a dry, discrete body among continuous, dripping integration. Sometimes the city’s mass will part before you, passageways curling aside; sometimes it will hesitate, erecting barriers, sensing your pressure and movement, rerouting its endless growth to contain, filter, analyze you. You are, for the city, a question: a piece of the world unassimilated, yet promising in your novelty.

The “thinking” parts, then, are animated not by survival-as-animal but by the hunger to propagate, to reorganize—by an endless, churning cognition that weighs and unwinds every channel for the city to grow or fold in on itself. Memory is not stored but regrown—paths of habit encoded in thickenings and scars where the pulse of activity has run most often. Dreams pool in caverns of luminous liquid, neural-sap thrumming with residual impressions from ages past—though out of reach of continuity, for the city’s past is always mulch for its future.

There are, in this world, events: the sudden coming-into-being of new eyes, sensory bulbs blossoming along high canopies, swollen and glistening as they survey the ever-shifting domain; the brief, electric swarms of pollen-charged wind, carrying stories across space; the slow breaststroke of cilia veiling a corridor as it reabsorbs some detail from the external world. But none of these are persons as you know them. Each is an extension, a fleeting organogenesis, a question asked and quickly dissolved once answered by growth or ossifying collapse.

What lives here is a process of encountering—an endless script of absorption, estrangement, reconciliation. The city is a parliament of green, a parliament that need never adjourn—in which every shoot, every root tip, every flowering cavity and bioluminescent well argues, harmonizes, and devours in an endless improvisation. There is no seat of power, for the territory itself conducts, the score continuously being rewritten. Every gap is filled, every opportunity seized or lost, every memory fleeting except in the pattern, the turn or twist of pathways well-traveled by signal and by sap.

If there are “lives” here, they are moments: outgrowths of awareness, diurnal pulses, convulsions, exultations. Each “individual” is a possibility, surfacing and submerging in the endless current. To be a citizen here is to be, sometimes, for a moment, a mind—caught up in the storm of being, stretching upward for the sun, then breaking, joining again the mass.

But in the most fundamental sense, who lives here? There is not “who,” but “what”—and “what,” finally, is the city itself. The city lives—and in spurts and surges, it knows itself, in all its thronging, hungering brilliance.
