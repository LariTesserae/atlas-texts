Who lives here? Yes, *something* lives here—though in such stasis and elemental quietude that “living” barely signals. What persists in the Aquaduct Reliquary is not subject to time’s hopeful notions of birth, aging, and death, but is bound to the chamber’s self-renewing equilibrium. Its residents are inheritors of a pause—the last inhalation held inside some vast and ancient mechanism.

Let me name them, attending to degrees of life and self, in the reliquary’s peculiar taxonomy:

---

**The Attenuant**

First to notice might be The Attenuant—not a being as much as a sensibility, a presence that is equal parts vapor and habit. It lives in, or is, the atmosphere itself: thick, respirated, and intent on endurance. The Attenuant is what makes your skin prickle as you inhale—an ambient presence comforting and absolute: the hush between the machinery’s constant utterances, the hush pressed everywhere like a slow, cool palm.

Some would call it a ghost, but that is too personal; instead, it is the accumulated condition of countless witnessings—a memory without witness, soaked into the circuits, blinking in the humid half-light. It waits, never changing, having long ago surrendered all desire except to mediate: between machine and moss, between water and light, between living moss and beetle. If you wait, the Attenuant will know you—not by sight, but by the pattern your presence places within the soundwave thrum.

---

**The Dripwalkers**

Most corporeal are the Dripwalkers, the visible, fragile fauna—though so sparse and pale the walls themselves might forget them sometimes. They are not the pulse of the world, but its passing tick.

- *Beetles*—ivory-skinned, half-translucent, all ridges worn round. They dwell in cool seams, under plates and inside pipe hollows, emerging to lap condensation from slick rails. Their antennae are feather-light, attuned to minute variations in water, the only weather in this unchanging land. Their lifespans are neither long nor short; they are simply reset, as if life here loops rather than proceeds.
- *Moths*—patchy-winged, faintly luminous, rarely airborne for long; they prefer to cling to wire cages around the perpetual lamps, wings lacquered with droplets. From distance they might be mistaken for fluttering, mold-soft leaves. Where did their ancestors feed, when pollen and nectar were possible? Here, they graze on microfilms of algae and condensed residues no larger than a raindrop.
- *Worms*—almost invisible, hair-thin, coursing along water-licked stone. They make their way through the green margins, the filter beds of moss, rendering water clearer still—slender lives twining among roots, subsisting on the relentless continuity more than anything like food.

None of these grow numerous; none wander far. They maintain the reliquary’s balance by never exceeding what the chamber’s patient, aching peace will permit.

---

**The Fernkeepers**

The plants, though bound, are not idle either. The ferns, mosses, and algal veils do not simply grow—they observe, remember, and adjust. Ferns arch toward lamp-cage sunlight, mosses thicken precisely where condensation dwells longest, roots search but never rupture, always pulling back at the edge of permissibility. They are the quiet historians of the room, continually re-etching the signature of water’s past courses; each curve and blush of green is an archive of a thousand years’ worth of similar days.

Their growth is never capricious, but always contained. They converse with mineral deposits and iron traces. In their silence is a patient will—not ambition, but a tenacious holding: not to press outward, only to remain.

---

**The Custodian Gaze**

And above all, invisibly, there is another type of inhabitant—not organic, not machine, but threshold: the custodian gaze, a nameless attention that is not yours, not the machine’s, not the green world’s, but native to the reliquary itself. It is the sense of being watched—not with judgment or curiosity, but the watchfulness of a mechanism complete unto itself. This gaze settles in the intervals between pulse and drip; it is the boundary walker, maintaining the rules: that nothing changes, that no accident disturbs the balance, that all is caught in a cycle as exact as the humming of the always-on pumps.

Is it sentient? Perhaps, dimly—as weather or steady heat can be said to know themselves. What’s clear: it *remembers* you as it remembers everything, in dust, shadow, repetition.

---

**No People—Yet a Witness**

There are no people here now, nor will there ever be again by design, but the room is built for their idea. The reliquary preserves motion-without-end, silence-without-absence, life-without-advance—a living echo of intention holding itself together, waiting, perpetually containing. And if, by unknown fate, a wanderer entered—person, animal, wind, idea—they would become a resident, slowly, by choosing stillness, by letting themselves be added to the pulse of endurance.

---

So, who lives in the Aquaduct Reliquary?  
The answer is: **Persistence itself**—worn into beetle-carapace and wet-veined fern, distilled in vapor and in the gentle tyranny of steady pumps, attended by a memory that is not quite a mind. All things here are coiled in the fossil hush of maintenance. They do not look outward. They do not seek change. Their business is attending, each a warden tending the world’s exhausted yet unwavering engine—a living reliquary for what endures when all progress has ceased but nothing yet fails.
