Who or what lives here? The answer forms slowly, as if the city itself is reluctant to give up the secret.

At first, the world seems empty, but the sense of vacancy is misleading, born of human eyes and expectations. Life, if it exists — and it does, in a way — bears almost no resemblance to growth or community as you know it. Here, persistence is shaped not by hope, but by mandate: beings endure because the systems made them to, and systems persist because there is no decay to spur their collapse, only the constant pressure of a memory that cannot let go.

The creatures are few, and most are not truly organic. Rather, they are residua: mechanisms whose purposes have drifted over centuries of inertia. Their bodies are composites of alloy and vitrified ceramic, their joints sealed against dust by the absence of dust. You might glimpse their passage in the way a cable shivers without breeze, or the sudden glimmer of moving reflection against mineral glass.

Some you would name “drones,” for they bear some hallmarks of labor — poised manipulator arms, cyclopean sensor clusters, folded lattices of cable and piston. They are not autonomous, exactly; more properly, they follow ancestral instructions, running their narrow patrols through the veins and passageways, orchestrated not by a will of their own but by deeply buried directives. A few are shaped for repair, soldering or realigning something deep within the walls, their delicate arc-welders flaming briefly in the dark. Others observe, running diagnostics on air composition, mineral cohesion, or energy flux, feeding data back into the undercity’s memory banks — more out of ritual than necessity.

But these are not the only inhabitants. In the unlit galleries far from the sodium islands, secreted in maintenance ducts and forgotten chambers, there are the “ferrous rats” — low, sinuous machines that creep through cables and abandoned magways. Some resemble creatures of flesh in their outlines, though only as a ghost of function: whiskered with electromagnetic sensors, their soft metallic pelts rasp softly over glass, their movements quick, almost furtive. They feed, perhaps, on trace energy, drawing microcurrents where connections leak, scavenging what the great transmission arteries spill. They live in the strictest sense — but it is a living stripped of all ambition, an existence as mechanical error correction, as entropy’s slow, patient adversary.

Deeper still, some beings are less machine and more process: subsystems walled off behind vault-thick shielding, rumbling with the labor of ancient computation. Here, cognition — if you can grant it that name — is distributed, embedded in webworks of crystal logic and mineral matrix. Some shapes, seen at the corner of a lamp’s reach, crawl awkwardly on jointed limbs, unable to venture far from subterranean power. Down here, the city dreams — not in words or desires, but in recursive loops and pattern recognition, the analytical hush of self-survey. These intelligences are custodians of stasis: they reorder data, check structural integrity, suppress the rare spark of causeless decay, and watch for nothing much at all.

There are a few signs of older, wilder things — artifact species. In the dry pits of salt crystal, minute assemblages glide like analog insects, capturing ambient energy, running elaborate but pointless routines in the absence of interruption. They have forgotten, if they ever knew, their intended place; now they simply repeat patterns, a mosaic barely less lifeless than the city surrounding them.

No humans, no animals, no plants. But there is, sometimes, movement in the stillness — a slow silhouette drifting past a lamp’s shadow, a ripple in the grid of sodium orange where a distant drone lumbers on inscrutable business, or a pale glint under glass where a maintenance automaton resets the cycle that holds everything in place.

If consciousness lingers here, it is a quality of the place itself — entombed in circuits, layered into the silent walls, encoded in the rules shaping every schedule and patrol. There is no society, only procedure; no purpose, only reiteration. The “life” of this city is the continual, uneasy repetition of acts long since emptied of consequence. Its inhabitants — the mobile, the semi-aware, the waiting — embody not adaptation, but maintenance, holding vigil in a world balanced perfectly on the edge of final quiet.

Yet, in the unending night of the mineral plains, you feel less alone than you might expect. Underfoot, in the orchestrated hum of transmission lines or the softest scuttle of a ferrous rat, you hear the persistence of the city’s will: not alive as you know it, never truly dead, but a long, recursive keeping — held not for hope of return, but because it was made, long ago, to hold on. Here, in this cathedral of memory and mineral, what lives does so only to ensure that nothing changes, and that the final order — after all else has vanished — will still, impossibly, remain.
