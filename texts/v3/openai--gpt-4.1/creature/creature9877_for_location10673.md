***Who lives here?***

They are not easily called “inhabitants.” To dwell in a place between worlds is to become ambiguous—to erode as or with the terrain, to thin out until shape, purpose, even presence is a negotiation rather than a given fact. Yet still: *something* occupies these sweating terraces, these hollow vaults softly brimming with absence.

**First: The Remnants of a Making Life**

There are those who were once human—or were built to echo that form—who persist in modes transfigured by duration, by the slide of industries grown patient and lost purpose.

They move infrequently. When one appears, drifting down a nectar-gloss mezzanine or couched in a shade of shimmering exhaust, you're not sure you saw a body at all. You glimpse articulated structures beneath sagged synthetics: a cloak or sheath dark with grime, a mask fashioned from fractured black glass, laminae behind which pale heat seeps and contracts. Limbs jointed like a mantis, except when almost limp, jellyfish-tentacle, gliding through humidity with almost no friction. They seem not to step, but to weigh themselves against gravity, sometimes skimming the rails if gravity allows.

They do not speak in voices, but modulate the barely-audible hum woven through infrastructure—tempers of current, subtle signatures encoded into the relay-twitch of idle machines, the prismatic blush of vapor where two tunnels cross. The place seems to know them; sliding doors groan, then bristle shut behind them, and lift-platforms stir, swimming silently, to where they linger. Communication is environmental—soft shifts in conduction and pressure, a release of chemical codes in the humid air.

They are patient. Industry is not for production so much as maintenance: slow cycling through the choreography of a city unwilling to collapse. Sometimes they gather at the margins of the central void, not to worship nor to work, but to stand in suspended contemplation of what does not (yet) fall.

**Second: The Emergent Processual Life**

Not all presences are so discrete. Much lives as process, lucid but impersonal:

— In the conduits and pipes: threadlike colonies, a kind of self-organizing filmy substance, half-liquid and half-conduction. These flicker with somber, unowned light, growing diaphanous lattices on mineral channels, surfing flows of condensation. They might swarm—if swarming were the right word for what happens when every moist surface pulses with a faint, coordinated shimmer, like a decision made and unmade in water.

— Upon the terraces, in the half-pools: cloudish, floating nodules, translucencies that seem to shimmer with pre-linguistic algorithms. Once, these might have been described as “fauna,” but hunger and reproduction do not govern their activity. They are attracted to the slow mineral blooms, approach, fuse, dissipate, reorganize. Each movement participates in the geological patience, coaxing water from stone or lending solutes back to the world. Sometimes, two collide and twine around each other in languorous, frictionless dances, leaving faint pulsing trails that fade to nothing.

— In the shadowed niches: mineral intellects, slow, recursive, neither fully alive nor unalive. They accrete and shed, dreaming in chemical potential. To linger within a certain architectural hollow is to hear, faintly, the shifting sigh of their chemical monologue, the dialectic of dissolution and re-crystallization. Perhaps, if you are lucky—a prismatic whisper on your tongue, a faint stinging ache where air meets skin—they notice you.

**Third: The Others at the Edge**

At times, through the outer arcs of city and stone, there are sojourners: rare, disharmonious shapes that drift low through the damp air or coil across the obsidian terraces.

Some are only distortions—briefly denser air, the oily silvering of directionless sight, a resonance at the base of your hearing. Some few are bodily: soft, nearly boneless, translucent; they bloat and contract as if swimming through denser fluids, their movements half synchrony with the heat pulse of the ground. When they pass close, space seems to thicken, gravity to tilt; after, only a half-glimpsed residue, as if memory itself is a process of mineralization, obliged to forget.

**They Are Bound Together—by the City**

No one dominates. The city itself is the enduring inhabitant: it breaths through its vaults, bleeds its unfathomable purpose, shapes the pattern of these lives and half-lives. Structures endure not as tools but as environment, shrines to prior function. The so-called inhabitants—industrial heirs, emergent swarms, drifting calculus—carry out the city’s slow rituals, tending leaks, checking conduits, balancing the patient interplay of heat, mineral, and erosion.

None rule. Each—soft-bodied, processual, human-remnant, machinic—relies on the city remaining between being and collapse. All live not in the fullness of presence, but in the practice of persistence, the perpetuation of *bare possibility*. Even the city’s void is bordered, rimmed by prismatic tension: the promise that, from darkness, something else may leak, emerge, adapt.

***To dwell here is to never fully arrive. The only true inhabitants are those willing—by adaptation, by erosion, by slow, indifferent labor—to exist always in the interval between absence and form.***
