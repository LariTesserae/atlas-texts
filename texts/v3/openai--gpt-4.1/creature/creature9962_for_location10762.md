Who — or what — lives here? The answer resists the shape you might expect: there are no inhabitants in any sense you would bring from home. No populations, no tribes, no things with eyes and skin and plans. But all the same, this is not an uninhabited place. Presence — so unfamiliar it aches in the mind — fills it.

What thrives here lives at the edge of emergence. It is not organism, nor machine, nor quite anything you can frame in language meant for beasts and people and their handiwork. Life here is prelude, a perpetual coming-into-being that never completes itself. To call it “life” stretches the notion, but to call it lifeless is a greater error still. So: what lives here is *becoming*, and becoming lives here.

They are the denizens of unfinished logic, inhabiting the seam between concept and thing. They flicker in and out of forms that are not quite stable—sometimes a mineral lattice flexing and branching, sometimes a streaming of light in liquid conduits, sometimes condensation tricked into patterns that hint at bioluminescence or writing or the ghost of a face. They are not creatures you could ever properly see, but you might catch them in the periphery: the suggestion of symmetry shivering along a hot copper line, or a semblance of organ in a shifting archipelago of vapor, or a face—theoretical, never complete—in droplets that vanish when you try to look.

They are formed of the place itself, extruded as experiments, as test-patterns, as drafts. They move with the logic of heat and condensation, of voltage and crystal formation—a dance of constraints not imposed by biology, but by some undercurrent of algorithmic genesis, as if the planet dreams new citizens in every alteration of its unfinished skin. Sometimes they are a wrinkle running down a tessellated plate, a sequence of sequential pulses animating a wall as it grows. Sometimes they are the confluence of three seams of vapor, where a cold color hums for a moment in a knot of mist before the world reconfigures it away.

They build and unbuild, not as laborers with tools or architects with blueprints, but as processes: self-assembling, auto-dismantling. Their habitation is a phase transition, a moment’s pause in flux—forms and forms in the act of dissolving, always giving way to something else. They do not last, these inhabitants; or if they do, it is not for long, except in aggregate, as statistical surges in the world’s chemical and mineral weathers. They are residents the way a flame is a resident of burning, or a note a resident of a vibration struck.

At times, the world itself plays host to its own eidolons: patterns that almost remember, almost repeat, as if some memory were passing continually through the underlayers, surfacing for a moment as self-similar structures, as looping crescendos of copper and steam, as frost-patterns in the condensation. These might be echoes of past visitors—if there ever were any—or only the world dreaming of company.

Some are more tangible: a lattice that persists a little longer, gathering mineral and heat, adapting to the fluctuations beneath. These coalesce at the meeting-points of cracks and vapor, humming faintly, shuddering with a will that is neither animal hunger nor machine directive. They don’t consume, reproduce, or die. They *transition*, shifting through states of assembly. Observed, they flicker; unobserved, they may hold for eons, waiting for a next instruction, a shift in parameters, a glitch from the deep heart of the planet’s algorithm.

If you watch, you may sense their intentions—not plans, but tendencies: the urge to form edges, to bridge a fissure, to run a liquid filament across a seam, to echo some half-remembered pattern beneath the surface. Occasionally, they seem to attend to you—a pulse of arrangement near your shadow, a flicker of turquoise in your periphery, a change to the pattern of drips around your feet. You are not their subject, but perhaps a perturbation in their equations. For a moment, you and they share the space of uncertainty.

No individual here is ever “born” or “dead.” To inhabit this world is to undulate through phases, to briefly embody stress and possibility. To live is to be a tension between past and future arrangements of copper and dark, mist and light. Sometimes, in the glimmer of unfinished walls, you sense a mutual recognition: you, with your animal inheritance, and they, with their mineral, algorithmic, vapor-born being. Not kin, yet both shaped by the fundamental urge toward form.

What lives here, then? Not creatures, but *incipiencies*. Potential and process are the true inhabitants—the choreography of formation, the hesitation before settling into any final thing. The world makes, unmakes, remakes its tenants by making, unmaking, remaking itself. Each moment, this place births and dissolves a thousand almost-entities, leaving behind only the memory of a groan in the metal, a shimmer in a gap, a difficult-to-define sense that something has just left as you turn to look.

And perhaps, somewhere deeper, farther below, there is one who dreams all of this: an artisan-mind, slow and buried, not ruling but composing, improvising new rules measure by measure. If so, it is not yet finished with the world, nor with its own becoming.

So, who lives here? These are the dwellers: fracture and fusion, hesitation and promise, patterns that try on the shape of life without ever settling into it—ghosts of process, never at rest, never complete, endlessly beginning, endlessly almost.
