What lives here? The city, haunted by moisture and riddled with heat, teems with those who need nothing of sunlight, root, or green. Here, life arises by strange invitation: organisms fed by stone and water, by chemistry and warmth, filling every gap that heat and mineral present.

**The Substrate Dwellers:**

Beneath the stones—black, sleek, pocked by time—thrive the substrate worms. They are blind, elongated, a pale gray slicked faintly with the mineral colors of the stones they move through. Their skins are thin, semi-translucent, showing faint blue or rust-red lines where mineral-laden blood pulses. These creatures burrow perpetually through sodden silt and sediment, their bodies secreting acids that dissolve minerals for nourishment. They leave behind aerated passages, breathing pores that open and close with the humidity’s flex, and the little hollow mounds of processed material are quickly hid by steam and mist.

Sometimes, larger substrate dwellers emerge—broad, slow, limbless things, as heavy as a loaf of bread and smelling sharply of ozone and wet iron. They arch from runnel to runnel, tongue-like palps flicking to sample crusted mineral or to lap at beads of condensed steam. No eyes, no ears—only chemosensory pits, tasting the air and stone, always receding quickly at vibration or sudden noise.

**The Pool-Edge Scavengers:**

On the brinks of those motionless, shallow pools crawl the Chitines. Their backs glisten like oiled obsidian, their six legs ticking as they work algae-less scum for microbial and mineral debris. Their eyes—clustered in nodes, like the facets of a jewel beetle, but glossy black—read faint traces in the dimness, guiding them to warmth, vapor, or food. Some have flattened carapaces, hugging close to the heat of a factory’s leaking steam pipe; others grow long, thin limbs to span from one crusted rivulet to another, efficient bridges across the chemical water.

They are not silent. Sometimes, during thin, humid nights, they congregate for reasons unknown, rubbing soft plates along their bellies in a faint rasping aria. Their vanishing is as sudden as their gathering: the city swallows them back, each to their niche.

**Mid-Air Drifters:**

Above, in currents of warm, moist air, hover gnatkin—creatures no larger than a raindrop, whose wings are not feathered or scaled but slick, transparent films that shimmer green and blue. Gnatkin do not feed on nectar or leaf, but hover close to pipes and brick, nuzzling the tiny exudates of chemical condensation. Their proboscises are sharp, needle-like, for piercing microbe-rich silt or tapping into the fungal mats that line pipe joints—the only vegetative life, if that term can be stretched so far. Clouds of them flicker along the larger trains of warm air, drawn to the heat of machines, their populations a living barometer of some chemical pulse.

**The Pipe Colonists:**

Within the city’s ancient conduits, whole civilizations of life have formed: tube-dwelling amphipods, translucent and armored with metallic sheen, line pipes in colonies, harvesting micro-droplets or sifting mineral-rich slime. Some are solitary, nestled in the security of rusted-out valves; others form linked chains like beads, sharing resources and beating their feathery limbs to keep water moving.

On the outsides of pipes cling creatures reminiscent of newts, but limbless, their skin dark and studded with tiny crystalline growths. These are extremely slow, almost sessile, drawing nutrients from the warm, circulating water inside the pipes through their damp bellies as they bask in heat radiance from the metal. At intervals, they release clouds of minute, swimming larvae that dart away in the slow-dripping world outside.

**The Wall Creepers:**

Clinging to brick or metal, finger-length creepers inch their way: segmented, soft-bodied, gripping with rows of sticky pads. Their bodies are tight-wound cylinders, dark with faint glimmerings of old metallic colors. Two tiny stalks telescopically extend from near their fronts, flickering in and out in a search for vibration. Some are solitary, living in a tight curl in some damp corner; others, particularly during the thickest steams, cluster in ropes along a wall, pulsing in quiet, peristaltic waves. Sometimes one sits still for hours, shivering slightly—digesting, perhaps, or dreaming.

**The Engine-Warders:**

Within the hidden hearts of still-operational machines move the engine-warders—a class of life both elusive and vital. They are neither tool nor creature outright, but a bridge between. Their bodies are tough, compact, with a hide that can withstand high heat and caustic moisture; maybe something in their tissues echoes synthetic polymer, perhaps a legacy of bioengineering. They maintain, clear, and—if need be—redirect water, oil, or gas. Sometimes, they repair: unseen, but for the curious shine of compound eyes peering from inside an access port, or a swift, dark blur crossing a pressure gauge at the moment a valve hisses shut.

Some think with more than instinct; they cross between animal and automaton, keeping the city’s remaining engines in perpetual readiness, a silent truce between past purpose and present home.

**Life’s Paradox:**

In all these creatures and more—the skitterers, the burrowers, the floaters and the clingers—there is a mood of watchfulness. They live in burrows and tubes, in the warmth under stones and the seeping cracks, each adapted in form and behavior to a world of stone and steam and water. Their colors tend toward dark gloss and reflected mineral hues, their bodies are always moist or slick. Their eyes have grown large, clustered, or vanished entirely, depending on the niche. They are silent, or nearly so, as if the weight of the hot mist imposes quiet.

But there is an intelligence in the whole: not individual, perhaps, but emergent—a city-wide understanding, a choreography of cycles among water, heat, and living presence. It is not bleak, nor cold, nor dead. The factories hum, the pipes leak, the stones sweat, and a softer, nonhuman industry persists: adaptive, ceaseless, and watching, always, from the gentle dusk of the steamed world.
