Beneath the Breathless Mat there is no population in the neat, catalogued sense—a census here would spiral into nonsense in minutes. The residents are never still, never wholly distinct from the place itself. Here, one does not encounter so much as become tangled with inhabitants, threads of them catching on your clothes, spores brushing your face, roots seeming to taste the air you bring. Life crowds in—interleaved, recursive, sometimes aware, sometimes simply persistent.

### 1. **Sovereigns of the Mat: the Rootwise**
No ruler reigns over the Breathless Mat, yet certain presences possess a gravity that organizes the space around them, if only for moments. You might, by chance, glimpse a Rootwise in one of the rare eddies in this tissue-thick chaos. They are as much plant as beast—a thicket of segmented, muscular roots radiating from a knotted core, ringed with chitinous plates slicked in bruised chlorophyll. Suckered feelers and fingerlike exudates sense chemical messages, lapping at dense air, translating growth into knowledge. In some places their cores arch upward—a many-lobed mass veined with sap and throbbing minerals, sheathed in spore-blown callus; elsewhere, their nerve-roots wander, branching through the labyrinth as scouts, tongues, or claws.

The Rootwise move too slowly to be seen—unless angered. Then, their roots knot and tense, pulling tissue and organism into brutal proximity, stripping prize and foe alike for sugars, kin, and heat. Yet, cunning pulses in their slow cognition. They may weave: bridges of barkhand, lattices for ripening kin, even the rough, intentional hollows where respite from the seethe is possible for brief spells. Their marks are the only true architecture here, but if left untended, even these are rapidly subsumed by the place’s wild surges.

### 2. **The Tumblers**
Most obvious are the Tumblers, frantic children of stem, filament, and scavenged flesh. You will not catch the same Tumbler twice; every generation is a fresh misinterpretation of what thriving means here. Some look like mossy rodents, their feet frilled with fern and their bellies swollen with stolen bulbs. Others are little more than rolling knots of spines and pseudopods, shaded in pale pinks and grays, tumbling through air-filled sacs of spun photosynthetic tissue, gnawing, grafting, multiplying. Eyes are rare; what passes for a face is a fractal split of mouths, spore-pads, humid spiracles. They live for brief seconds or frantic days, hurling their many selves across gaps before being absorbed, repurposed, or torn anew by the surrounding riot.

### 3. **Fungal Animalcules**
Thick at the margins (but sometimes in the very center of things) are the lichen-furred animalcules, each a compromise between mycelium and muscle, fruiting spore-pods perched atop slick peristaltic bodies. They crawl in ceaseless processions, consuming dead tissue and living walls alike, excreting bitter mosaics of crust and pigment—one of the few lasting marks made here. Their brief colonies create flashes of community: moments of shared awareness or agitation, then scatter, driven apart by hunger, fear, or the pressure of something larger moving beneath.

### 4. **The Sheathed**
There are the Sheathed, slow pillars that push upward through the mat, cracked and swelling, sometimes almost humanoid by accident of growth. Their bark splits to exhale clouds of motile, root-tipped larvae who gnaw tunnels and channels, then coil back into their bodies, reintegrating what they've learned or found. From a distance, some Sheathed seem ancient—a suggestion only, as nothing holds its form for long here. Yet among them, grim patience accrues: they are evidence of time’s slow, uneven hand in the crush of becoming.

### 5. **The Air-Bitten**
You may notice the Air-Bitten only in the sudden silence left by their passing. These are streamer-like, barely visible: filamentous beings that ride the sharp mineral wind of this world, mouths open to drink dust and scent, bodies made of fused leaf-ribbons and hardened sap. As they weave through cracks of light, their presence feels like a cooling wave—anomalous, because where they pass, space briefly uncrowds, as if something enormous has inhaled. They do not linger, and you may wonder whether they are animal, plant, or symptom of the Mat’s ceaseless respiration.

### 6. **Reclaimers and Ghosts**
In rare shadows or abandoned hollows, you might find the remnants of more intentional things: the calcified fruit-stone idols, the bridge-webs, the sinew knots—evidence of hands that once partitioned space, tried to shape respite from clamour, or consecrate a gap. The makers may still linger, ghostlike: their bodies drawn tall and reedy, flecked with resin and pale bark, hands grown blunt and flexible, eyes shielded behind thickened cuticle. They are shy, or perhaps simply preoccupied. Their society—if it exists—ebbs and flows, folded into and out of the Mat’s own impulses. Sometimes, you sense a consciousness far larger and older than these bodies alone: a distributed mind, bred by signals of sap and scent, lingering through the memory of wounds and restarts.

---

**What lives here?**  
All the space's anxious intent. Growth, devouring, repurposing: not merely organisms but processes, tendencies, impulses made flesh and fiber. The living are not permanent; form is a temporary strategy. Every creature here is a verb, a trial solution, a negotiation with the Mat itself—breathed out, gnawed back in, forever jockeying for a square of light or heat or stillness in the violent, generative press. To be resident beneath the Breathless Mat is to be an extension of its hunger for arrival, to live not by owning space but by occupying potential, for as long as you avoid the jaws and knots closing all around.
