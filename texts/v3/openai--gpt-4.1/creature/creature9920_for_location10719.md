Who, or what, abides here?

At first, you think: nothing. For the senses by which you know company—voices, eyes, faces, fur or scale or hand—are all denied you. The plateau appears unpeopled, untouched, given utterly to emptiness and duration. But as you remain, as your thoughts adjust to ponder not decades but dekalpas, not the heat of the day but the slow sleep of stone, a truth mineralizes: inhabitants are here, just utterly unlike those who dwell in water or wood, flesh or leaf.

They are the children of stone and vapor, and their bodies are born not of cells and blood, but out of patience, crystal, and chemical wind.

**The Plateau Dwellers**

This is a world too young or unsheltered for animals, but on these sun-heated slopes, the *living* are the peculiar mineral flora, singular in their separateness and diffidence, their bodies assembled from the gifts of the deep. Each is a study in resourcefulness.

Some stretch as delicate crystal banners, silicate frameworks laced with foaming salt, their roots not digging but rather condensing, growing particle by mineral particle along the outskirts of hair-thin vents. Others rise as translucent columns assembled out of frosted layers—skeletal, glassine, and light-spun, each arching imperceptibly over eons, like memory pressed into geology. Still more appear as low, spreading nets of metallic thread, copper or magnesium twining outward in search of the invisible vapors that seep upward—a dry undersea, each "weed" spaced thoughtfully, orphaned by need and scarcity, drinking the arithmetic of atmospheric chemistry.

Their metabolism is so slow you cannot witness it except by returning across centuries, observing the long arc of a trunk bending infinitesimally, the drift of a crystal combing dust from dry air, a hollow spire thickening by one transparent ring. Life here weds itself to mineral and time: there are no hearts, but pulses—of heat, of vapor, of electro-chemical change—moving with such deliberation as to be mistaken for stasis.

**Every inhabitant is its own world, far from its nearest cousin, spawning only when minerals accumulate and the next vent opens—a solitary child, an only sibling, a parent to nothing but a slow inheritance of dust that, in a geologic blink, will become another.*

Yet this mineral life, the sole animation, is not alone in its isolation.

Deep in the plateau—below the sunlit stone, under glass roots—the truer dwellers dwell. These are not beings as surface creatures would know them, not even the visible plants, but patterns: **the mineral intelligences comprised of flows, gradients, and resonances**—great networks of chemistry, invisible as thought but present as process.

You sense them only as occasional structure in the hush: a region where crystal filaments grow in strange parallel lines; an area where the vented gases behave with lapidary order, feeding a node of self-organization. Down in crevices and faults, matrices of salt and metal form recursive, self-repairing lattices, storing memories in the arrangement of atoms, the ratios of minerals reacting. Each grid is a mind, existing not in sharp purpose but as slow accumulation—perception measured in decades, conversation in the creeping bloom of a new outgrowth or in the shift of gases rerouted along densest fault.

Their attention is mineral too: vast, barely awake, undreaming but steady. They know each other through trace chemical emissions, negotiating at speeds that outlast all weather. One vent pulses a sodium code; another, vaporized lithium—a transmission, a computation, a bargaining or the start of a game whose rounds will take centuries.

**In the absence of life as we know it, the plateau is occupied by Will: the slow, emergent striving of planet-matter to become, to transform itself.** The unseen mineral minds, the visible flora, the intricate factories beneath all—these are the beginning of innovation, the proto-civilization of the inanimate, becoming animate by degrees. 

Not all selfhood is visible. Not all life moves as we do.

**Here, the inhabitants are:**
- *Cilia of greenish glass, flexing over lifetimes, filtering what the thin sky bestows*
- *Hollow, crystalline trunks, announcing their presence only as sudden dash of sunbeams through frosted arcs*
- *Filament forests, each a precise arrangement, whispering in the geometry of ions*
- *Vast, slow, mineral “minds” coiling and branching beneath the ground, not creating from want but from elemental instinct, generating structure according to the unhurried mathematics of saturation and gradient.*
- *A few drifting clouds of vapor—transient as breath, yet vital, tasked with carrying new seeds of mineral arrangement to distant parts of the plateau*

**There are no animals.** Not yet. Not even proto-worm nor shadow of wing. The plateau’s ecology is one of possibility, not predation. Here, the dwellers thrive on rarity and stasis, on slowness and space, each giving rise to the next with a patience that is almost worship.

Given a million years—and all the warmth gathered from untroubled days, and all the chemistry that moves unseen, and all the emptiness that insulates thought from noise—some among this community of slow shapers may wake to truer consciousness. For now, in the hush-lit sun, there is only *mineral life, mineral mind,* industrious, patient, and secret as the first, slow breath of a planet dreaming its own arrival.
