Who lives here? Who—what—moves, pulses, breathes in these weighty, verdant vaults of drowned bone and patient plant? Let us listen and feel our way, for sight is futile. To know these dwellers, we must become sensate to suggestion, vibration, echo—webs of motive and flesh emergent not from urgency but from continuance.

**The Builders:**  
First, and everywhere, the patient architects—the Root-Singers, the Mastrell Vines. They are not strictly animal or vegetable, but a perfection of both: bodies built for ceaseless growth, accreted into buttresses and bridges, ever-rooting, ever-branching. Vast and hollow, their organs are chords of carbon and song, growing slowly up, out, into the nothing, then pausing for millennia like meditating titans. There is no mind as we know it, but motive: a network of perception through tension and sap, each pressure a thought, each stress a message sung by pores and frond. Their “cities” are grown rather than dreamed—volumes emptied by swelling, ceilings raised by the slowness of hope.

**The Tenants:**  
Among these living pillars and halls, lesser lives find residence—each shaped by the dark, the crush, the endless absence of light.

Balanced high in an airy vault, perhaps, you brush the swaying filaments of a **Cavernous Dactyl**, a grand, shelled herbivore as long as a rowboat, mouth ringed by curling, sensitive blades. Every hour, it drifts to the moist skin of a root or moss-ledge, opening its mouth to sift fragments and microfauna from the gentle, persistent drizzle of debris—an eternal rain of soft, organic snow. Moving is not its nature; its pod-shaped body stays pressed against the cool, living architecture until hunger urges the slow, ponderous journey to the next moss-fall.

Below, in a shadow-swollen crypt, a **Vocator** broadcasts pulses: sound-globes made not through lungs but through contractile sacs. Each one is a plump, eyeless worm as thick as a child’s leg, packed with auditory hairs and resonance chambers, hearing more vividly than any terrestrial ear. Their clicks and distant booms shape the social fabric of the dark—finding mate, declaring presence, warning rivals, seeking out fresh spoil. They live in clusters, burrowing not through mud, but through the soft tissues of living walls, creating networks of chambers—a labyrinth residing in the vascular flesh of the place.

**The Couriers:**  
Between vault and column, where water shivers in open “streets” lined with kelp-pillar and bloom-stalk, myriad smaller forms flit and graze. Nearest to cleverness, perhaps, are the **Ophidian Scribes**: long, slender creatures, their skins filigreed with glowing symbols—luminescent bacteria they cultivate with their own secretions. These beings are silent communicators; ribbons of light form into transient, living messages, read with skin and gut by their kin. Their purpose is movement, memory, and the marking of place—quiet archivists of a civilization that never needed writing or voice.

**The Monarchs**:  
Looming in the endless cathedral-caverns, monarchs move alone: **the Choral Shellbacks**, armored in rhizome-plated carapace, shells inlaid with growing moss and polyp colonies. Magnitude marks them—bodies stretching five meters or more, moving by slow, stately undulation or sudden thunderous steps when hunger or threat stirs them. Each monarch is a kingdom—a city of symbionts—trailers, scavengers, infusoria, small filter-feeders, living upon its armored hide and in its drawn-out wake. Each step cracks a new channel through soft-grown wood, and in every wake, old voids are filled with new green.

**The Ghost-Builders**  
Threading unseen, mostly undetected, are the **Hyphal Tenders**—creatures so light, so gossamer, that they seem only a mood, a speculation of nerve and spore. They are made from strands: threads of living fungus woven into semi-coherent animality, dispersing and condensing, part colony, part beast. Their presence is felt more than seen—a sudden scent, the prickle of faint touch, the cool knowledge that spores are always near. They feed on what dies but also on what refuses: stalled buds, aborted roots, places where vitality hesitates. They keep the vault clean and distribute news of rot and change, ferrying memory alongside decay.

**The Ancient:**  
And deepest of all—at the foundations where the root-mountain’s own summit is pressed by every century since world’s first wound—dwells something greater still. The **Cathedral Mind**: nameless, enormous, utterly integrated—a being grown, not born, but alive in every vein and chamber, organizing, sensing, directing the shape of growth, the pattern of occupation. It is not apart from its world but *is* the world—half-sap, half-will. Every pulse of water, every stir of stem, is its dreaming. Its awareness is slow beyond reckoning, expanding through every living conduit. Seldom, perhaps never, does it act; perhaps only it dreams, shaping the peace, the sound, and the ages.

**Children of the Quiet Noise:**  
Young—smaller, more lively—roam in multitudes. Most are things of gel and silk, feelers bristling, teeth small but many, each adapted specifically to a chorus and a corridor, each taught by vibration which void belongs to them. They die often, live briefly, but never in silence. The minors serve as both prey and melody, their brief lives the swiftest cycle in a world where all else is ancient.

---
This is not a place of hierarchy, nor truly of purpose—except the ancient, prehuman ones: build, be fed, give space, fill space, resonate, endure. To those that dwell here, time is movement, hunger is coming home. All who live on this drowned summit are parts of the unfinished cathedral, which is themselves; an entirety, alive, whose only prayer is the ongoing murmur of sap and echo. There is no word for loneliness here, for all are forever embraced—not by family or flock, but by the ceaseless nearness and belonging of living inside the world that made you, and still is making you, always.
