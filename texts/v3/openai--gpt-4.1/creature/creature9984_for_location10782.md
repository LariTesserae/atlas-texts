Who, then, lives here?

Dwellers in the Lattice

Call them dwellers, but even that is too soft. They inhabit not just the space but the *momentum* of this fractured core: never at rest, always engaged in an urgent business that is part repair, part transformation, and part pure, ceaseless reaction.

They are not, in any sense, comfortable. The dwellers fit their world imperfectly: adaptation is a verb, not a state. Their bodies rewrite themselves as the tunnels shift, as the mechanisms convulse. “Species” is almost meaningless; you find instead a *continuum of form*, bodies minted by need and chance, half from old shells and half from the leftover minerals shivered into the clefts.

---

**Physicality:**

They are small, mostly—sleek or stubby shapes that nudge and slice through the network. Chitin, yes, a brittle sheath, but flecked with granules and slabs of silicate—unnaturally bright seams that catch the sullen light, hinting at metallic bones and stony scales fused on. No face as we would name it, but clusters of cilia and sensor-filaments coiling at the leading edge; some end in balled hammers for breaking, some splay into brushy fans for feeling the fine tremble of hazard.

Limbs are functions, not categories: you see ringlets that snake outward to grip pipe, flicking chelae for snipping threads, wedge-like plates flexed to jam against the sudden folding of a wall. Some unfurl mere membranes, tensioned like a drum to catch the resonance of a shifting panel, and ride the wave. Others deploy rotating threads, spools of living fiber, to cross empty gaps or stitch ragged breaks in the larger fabric.

Rarely, moving deeper into nodes where matter is thickest, you encounter dwellers swollen to the size of an infant’s fist—carapace fringed with conductive whiskers, dappled with imbedded microcircuitry, bodies pitted by the stress of scavenging. They seem slow—a ruse. Wait, and you’ll see whole segments torque open, split, and release a frantic spill of smaller kin.

Parasites or progeny? The distinction is moot.

---

**Behavior and Mind:**

To call them *communal* is half-right. They are not a hive as an insect might be. Each moves on its own, colliding, ignoring, cooperating in erratic, fleeting concatenations. They do not communicate through voices, but through shocks, through the electric whisper in mineral veins, through the shuddering mesh that doubles as skeleton and antenna.

Wherever a seam gapes or a mechanism stalls, they flow in sudden, roiling tides—teeming over the gap, scouring and binding, grafting fragments of salvaged structure to raw geode, patching, spinning, splitting. They do not plan, yet their effect resembles organization: the patch is made, the gap is filled, but the pattern is always strange, a visual stutter never repeated.

Their *thought*, if such there is, flickers in the relay: the twitch of light, the zip of ions. Each acts, reacts, and sometimes—between one catastrophe and the next—some dwellers pause at a junction, shudder together, fuse briefly into stranger, bulkier forms… and then drift apart, as if the memory of purpose could not persist more than a few seconds in this churning matrix.

---

**Life Cycle and Making:**

Reproduction is riot and ritual, indistinguishable from collapse. When too many gather, the infrastructure warps, collapses, and from the detritus emerge not eggs but half-grown analogs—clusters of wire and crystal, shaking off dust, already hurling themselves at the nearest seam to nurse from the chemical trickle.

Old dwellers rarely perish in place; their shells are stripped, recast, their remaining function plaited into the walls or melted into a nodule in the lattice, to power some relay or light a cold lamp for a dozen thumping heartbeats.

Death is never waste here.

---

**Purpose and Hunger:**

What do they seek? Only the continual, desperate perfusion of action—every stalling, every gap, every tremor must be met. Some are born in the pressure-junctions, skinning up in the vortex whorl of a struck wire, living barely hours before they are torn underfoot. Others last longer; you see them encrusted at their nodes, thicker, less mobile, feeding off the flows spilled in the micro-avalanches of the shifting lattice.

Their *hunger* is for fit and fill, for occupancy. Nothing must remain void for long. They absorb, they repurpose, they inhabit the very act of becoming: to pause is to be subsumed, recycled, spun into the next attempt at stability.

---

**Secretiveness:**

To see them is rare, fully. They are always half-concealed, sliding across a just-opened panel, vanishing in a spray of flakes as some dread device gnaws a deeper vein. Their presence is the chitter at the edge of hearing, the fleeting afterglow when light stutters from the bruised crystal. They belong neither to stone nor machine, but to the interface—dwelling in crevice, junction, seam, always exactly where your attention is not.

---

**Names:**

They have no names, unless you would grant them the names of flows and fractures. Iridescents, meshers, quill-splitters, glass-weft, relay-sworn—these are titles only useful for your memory, not theirs.

---

**If you linger:**

Wait, and you will see the place reknit itself in a thousand small acts. The dwellers flense back a layer of plate and solder a new cap; a nodule shatters and out swarms a new clutch, seaming muscle to copper; a bridge folds and a nest blossoms. The dwellers do not notice you, or if they do, you are merely a still gap, a silence where action is lacking. They may climb you, sample your skin with mineral-dipped filaments, wedge a sliver of crystal into your pocket before sliding away.

You are not alien. You are, for a breath, just another substrate. What does it matter, here?

---

**To Live Here Is:**

To be one of these dwellers is to know no rest, to be born in the press and the splinter, with purpose indistinct from motion, and history always erased, rethreaded, rebuilt. You hurry, you repurpose yourself, you are repurposed, you fill the gap.

And then another gap, another fracture, another need, always unmet and always being met: never finished.

You are the opposite of silence. You are the as-yet-unfilled space, the answer not yet given, but always in the rhythm of being given. The world is the seam you inhabit, and in that seam, you are perpetually, frantically alive.
