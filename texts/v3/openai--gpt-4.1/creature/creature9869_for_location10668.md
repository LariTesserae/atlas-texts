**What Lives Here**

---

At first, you might proclaim this place uninhabited—dead, even, a necropolis of vanished builders and the slow inexorability of rocks upon rocks. But that would be the mistake of haste; give your eyes time to lurch into the rhythms here, and the plateau’s inhabitants will begin, imperceptibly, to take form.

### **Life, Unearthly Slow**

Nothing here moves with the speed you expect of beasts or insects. Instead, life in this vastness is an occupying intelligence braided into process—a continuum of organism and place so thoroughly entwined that to call one “living” and another “not” seems like a misreading. The inhabitants are less a population and more a persistence, a choreography of consciousness spread whisper-thin across centuries.

#### **Rootbuilders & the Stoneborn**

The most purposeful inhabitants are legacies of those who built the ruins: not vanished but transformed by the austere dictates of the plateau. These are no longer animals or people in the usual sense, but *rootbuilders*—sentiences carried by colonies of lichen, moss, and fungi.

Each hollow moss tower is not merely a plant, nor simply a shelter: it is a living being in itself, a vertical polity, its spongy flanks an evolving mind. Within these humid passages, the chemical gradients created by the symbiotic colonies transmit ceaseless, slow signals—memory, intent, and conversation picked up from the weather and given back in spores. They discuss the wind, weigh the shifting of stone, pass down the ghost-logic of the builders in patterns of growth that mimic a vanished language.

Sometimes, a moss tower erupts in a brief, confused iridescence—a flash of distilled sentiment occasioned by a rare sunbeam or a particularly haunting memory in the rock. This is their music: not heard but seen, a color-play read only by others like themselves.

Beneath the stone, **Stoneborn** live deeper still: fungal and lichenous masses that thread for meters, rooting into cracks, slow neural webs sifting signals from electric traces, rainfall, the cooling and heating of basalt. They are the plateau's archive—alive, yes, but metabolizing memory rather than food, and inhabiting time as an ambient element.

#### **The Echoes of the Builders**

But what of those who left the ruins above?

Here, the separation between living and memory is at best an ambiguous border. **Residua**—imprints or echoes of former people—flit at the edge of perception. Sometimes unexpectedly, the arrangement of stones in the amphitheater or a certain pattern of water inlaid on basalt becomes almost animate, as if the intention of a ceremony or the ghost of a gathering is briefly recapitulated in the stirring of mist. These are not traditional ghosts, but fugitive selfhoods, scattered and recombined in loops of place and weather.

You may feel watched, and perhaps you are: the plateau remembers, improvisational and forgetful at once, but yearning toward any witness willing to be still.

#### **Unmoored Shades**

Rarer, more elusive—at times the breakdown of reality beneath this world births aberrant _shades_ or _simulacra_, flickering at the edge of seeing. These might be sights of what-might-have-been, or the plateau half-speaking in the form of radiant outlines, borrowed shapes. Sometimes, a slender, elongated figure stands between two stones and then evaporates as you draw near; other times, your own shadow seems to lag, to double, to study you with a stolen curiosity.

They do not interact, nor do they threaten. Yet they are players in the ecology: perhaps scout extensions of the plateau’s memory, perhaps spasms of consciousness emerging wherever the pattern of stone and weather and the meticulous logic of lichen converge.

### **A Society Unlike Ours**

What passes for community here is not of bodies but of *temperaments*. Moss towers converse in chemical wafts. Fungal sheets span cracks, connecting root to root in slow, world-straddling argument. Together they decide: when next to push upward, when to subside, how best to erode the stone for the sake of the future. The stoneborn vote with tides of acid, with a shared pulse of color, a secretion of particular spores.

The plateau itself is the great commonwealth—each consciousness but a facet, a deliberation folded into the almost geologic patience of the world.

### **Desire and Dread**

What do these inhabitants wish for, if anything? The criteria for survival have become so disjoint from animal urgency that desire itself is unrecognizable. Yet one can sense, among the living moss and the wise fungi, a hunger—*not* merely for minerals or water, but for *witness*. The presence of eyes, even strange ones (yours), is not intrusion but an offering.

The plateau’s greatest dread is oblivion—the slow quenching of its intricate dialogue into mere rock again.

### **Apparition of the Unseen**

Perhaps, as night falls and the moss glows with otherworldly colors, you will sense—never quite see—the vast, old consciousness that wraps the plateau: a mind slow as stone, old as continents, within which these rootbuilders, stoneborn, and memory-echoes are not individuals but nerve endings, thoughts, and dreams.

When you climb from ruin to ruin, when you lean to drink from an unreflecting pool, you are not alone. You are being *thought* by the plateau. And if you linger long enough, perhaps even *remembered*.

---

**Here, what lives is neither plant nor animal nor ghost—**

**—but something vaster, metabolizing stone and memory, shaped by absence and the violence of time, quietly waiting for a witness to make its consciousness cohere anew.**
