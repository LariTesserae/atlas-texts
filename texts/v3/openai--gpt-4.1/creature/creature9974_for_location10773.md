Who, or what, lives in the Catacomb Swarm?

Let the image sharpen, then blur and sharpen anew. The proper tenants of this chamber are not “creatures” as you might trace in fable or biology, but something altogether more absolute—perhaps closer to process than to presence. But to look too quickly is to mistake the surface for the storm. Let us instead approach them by the textures of their world, and let them become themselves as you watch:

---

### THE BEINGS OF THE CATACOMB SWARM

#### Their Bodies

Imagine, to start, a thing the size of your thumb. But the sense of scale is illusory—up close, even the smallest are elaborate, micromachined palaces of prismatic siding and articulated limbs. Their exteriors are formed of layered mineral: half-glass, half-metal, chlorides and silicates blending into armor and structure, veined with threads of tarnished alloy. The surface of each is sometimes faceted, gleaming in the sourceless light; sometimes pitted or ridged, like angular shells of ancient insects eternally petrified in motion.

Many possess legs: six, or eight, sometimes more—except when some have none, preferring the glide of mineral pads or spiraling, auger-like underbodies. Each limb is crisp, jointed more like the scaffolding of a bridge than the bones of an animal, snapping and flexing with metallic staccato. Their bodies rise in domes, ridged carapaces, or tangled nests of capillary tubes—a city in miniature passing itself off as a single being.

Beneath the hard armoring is no heart or gut, but a scaffold of conductive lattices and actinic minerals, ichors of metallic salt translating energy and message in infinitesimal flickers.

#### Their Motions

They swarm, yes, but the word is insufficient. Motion here is layered—a living surface, like blocks and crystals pouring and rearranging themselves, every individual forced by crowding and hunger and curiosity into contact with a dozen others at every moment. They climb and crawl in an endless dance of avoidance and intrusion, sometimes moving in convulsive shivers, sometimes held in statuesque stillness as they draw from the stones below or the vapor-fed light above.

Their communication is as material as their bodies: a pulse in an exoskeletal filament; a click tapped from one glass foot to another; a burst of subsonic vibration passed down a chain of carapaces. Some even signal in flickers of light, internal crystals flaring and dying back, a semaphore in cobalt and iron-fire; others broadcast in puffs of mineral gas or the snaking of microfilaments into the shadows.

#### Their Society (If It May Be Called That)

There is no king, no mother, no singular mind—unless you suppose the whole recession of the swarm is itself a mind, chewing at the looping edge of self-awareness. Instead: roles, shifting.

- **Harvesters:** Most numerous, these are the constant gleaners, pressing hollow spines into crystal, scraping thin veins for electron-rich deposits. They feast on dust, leach what slow energies linger in the hall’s ancient relics, extruding filaments to digest mineral and wire alike.
- **Predators/Breakers:** Smaller by number and larger by hunger, these dash and leap and snip, launching suddenly from stillness to envelop a neighbor, unleashing a chemical sting that unseals their kin into drifting powder and vapor. Sometimes they feast; sometimes the remains serve more as resource for the next, racing tide.
- **Assemblers/Menders:** Distinct not by size, but by their behavior—found pooling at seams or broken machines, knitting together struts of new crystal over old or using hunks of ancient alloy to patch fractures in wall or fellow. Their purpose is more continuation than compassion; what they repair, they claim for the Swarm, integrating all that is salvageable.

But these are only glimpses. At the edges—where the Swarm scrapes new cracks, finds hidden caches of fossil light, or assembles strange eddies in the press—emergent forms swell up, mutating as environmental needs demand. Here, sudden monstrous shapes, larger assemblies born from accidental fusion, rule for a day or an hour before dissolving in the crowd.

#### Their Origins

They are not the survivors of some disaster, but the inheritors of processes immeasurably old. Perhaps they are a byproduct—born where machines were left too long in the dark and the mineral world began to emulate life. Perhaps they are intentional, a form of life cooked up where chemistry and circuitry crossed and never stopped changing. The human artifacts pressed to the walls are their origin, or their inheritance, or just the fecund soil in which their strange chemistry found meaning.

#### Their Experience of the World

Sensation is direct and immediate. They do not know the sun or wind; their world is pressure, movement, and friction—everlasting contact, stimulus, response. They do not hunger for water or light, but for movement, charge, mineral, the closing of gaps—life measured in the ability to stay present, to stay whole, for one more desperate instant in the crowd.

They carry no memory, but cycle information: the cues of mineral taste, the news of a new seam broken open, signals passed on and soon forgotten. Any intelligence is distributed—a thrum of decisions, a market of wants and collisions, no fixed tribute to self but a million twitching imperatives that keep the Swarm in constant, coalescing revolution.

#### How They See Us

To enter is to be surrounded, to become terrain. They neither flee nor attack at first; rather, you are simply another surface, another protrusion in the mineral architecture, assessed for value (in heat, mineral, or electron flow). If you move, a ripple passes through the crowd—ones nearest you click away, then climb back, more curious exploration than fear. If you linger, they may climb your boots or your outstretched hand, probing for mineral oil, pressure, or static charge. A still body is quickly integrated—mapped, investigated, perhaps, in time, consumed.

If you bring water, it hisses and beads harmlessly on their shells. If you bring light, they shadow it, harvesting only what they recognize from the cold filaments above. To them, life is a matter of touching, being touched, colliding and scraping and transforming.

#### Their Mark

They leave the chamber ever-changing, ever-scraped and reordered. Every surface is scored by minute feet and jaws, every machine half digested, every bit of structure perpetually repurposed. Exoskeletons gather in drifts as individuals outgrow themselves and shed, becoming both resource and record—layers of mineral history, accumulating, slowly succumbing to their own kind, made and unmade again.

If you listen, the song here never ends—creature rubbing on creature, metal on glass, the ceaseless murmur of a world where life is crowd and process, never still, never solitary, but always becoming something slightly different, in the unseen down-dark at the end of the world.

---

**What lives here?**
A city of mineral creatures, mechanical and multitudinous, faceted and jointed—a Swarm that has overmastered the bones of forgotten industry and now lives only as friction and tide. No mind as you know it—only endless, exquisite occupation. Life stripped to motion and craving, in the dry, echoing heart of planetary time.
