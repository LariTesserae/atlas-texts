Who—*or what*—lives here, below the Overbright, in the Eggchambers of the Glass Hollow?

Consider first what it means to “live” in a world still uncarved, where reality is soft clay beneath unmended skies. The life in this hollow sits at the hem of existence, neither animal nor fungus nor spirit, but some shifting sum. There are no old bones here, nor fossils; everything is new, or newer still each time it is looked at. If you seek kings or monsters, you will not find them—at least not at first.

**The Denizens**

At the most obvious level, there are the *creatures*—the first-imagined, the almost-hatched:

1. **The Evolets and Glimmerkind:**  
   They flit just above the glassy substrate, or pause, half-merged with the walls, their forms shaped by the tension between inheritance and invention. Imagine something halfway between an unfinished insect and an idea for an animal—a body in continuous edit, limbs briefly outlined, then absorbed. Sometimes a mono-winged mothlet, sometimes an oval of light-freckled fur, sometimes a latticework of skeletal petals, trembling with each convulsion of the room. Sometimes they unspool themselves in threads, testing how long it takes motion to settle into shape.

   These creatures do not so much move as *happen*: a flicker, a splay, a resonance where the light hitches or bends.

2. **The Dew-Eaters & Surface-Sippers:**  
   The dew-eaters are ancient in attitude, newborn in design. Spherical, featureless, their moods signaled only by the way condensation vanishes into their membranes—a contented hum when they're sated, a frantic shimmer when the droplets are rare. Some are solitary, gliding on tension, some cluster in slow duets, learning the gravity of companionship.

   Occasionally, a dew-eater splits, not dividing but refracting—what was one becomes two, or what was two merges into a single, denser curiosity. They are not individuals as we know it, but conditional gatherings of thirst and calm.

3. **Irid-Burrowers and the Edged Unfoldings:**  
   Some denizens are not seen but suspected: a glint below the crystal resin, a distant ripple where a root once was. These are the irid-burrowers—wormish, perhaps, or limb-fingered, but always veiled in a color too changeable for names. They drag brief tunnels through the substrate, leaving hints of warmth or a smell of ozone in their wake. At times, they shape hollows and ridges, as if rehearsing a plan for roots and dens that will someday suit a more permanent world.

4. **Twine-Drakes and Siphonlings:**  
   Larger, but far from dominant, are the twine-drakes—serpentine, centipedal, woven from overlapping filaments that flicker with mineral hues. They seem curious, testing walls with frilled palps, pausing to bask in beams of upwelling light that split their forms into double shadows. When startled, they curl into DNA-like helices, then unfurl, all motion and potential.

   The siphonlings, meanwhile, are more ectoplasmic—momentary tubes of mist threaded with shining nodes, emerging from rills and lapsing back when the air cools. Some say they’re proto-drinkers of light itself; others, that they’re dreams of roots practicing for more fixed forms.

**The Eggs, and Those That Tend Them**

But the Eggchambers are not for wanderers alone. Their purpose—if such a word can be invoked—is *incubation*, the becoming of new forms. Up against the chamber’s edge, in hollows where water has pooled to a syrupy gold, cluster the eggs: not chicken or reptile, but globular, mirror-bright; some sheathed in veils of gossamer, some open at one end, feeling the air with question-mark tendrils.

Who tends them? Not parents, not in any familiar sense. Instead, *Caretakers of Circumstance*—neither creature nor artifact—see to the eggs’ progress. They are barely more than arrangements of intent: ghost-hands, nervous motes, a congregation of local physics. Sometimes, their presence is a gentle change in temperature, a fractal ripple passing through the air, triggering a rain of condensation or a slight thickening of the vapor. At other times, a physical caress appears—a mist-hand smoothing a shell, coaxing it to shift or to open, or briefly merging with a hatching denizen to lend strength.

Beyond these caretakers, there are other forms—difficult to describe, because they are still becoming themselves. Shadows that almost resolve into figures, mainly felt as a shift in the brightness or a suggestion of warmth; a shell divides or a rill thickens because “they” have visited, gone before they are quite noticed.

**The Ghosts of Minds**

Some days, as the ever-bright seeps sideways, an echo of consciousness slides along the walls. It’s not a true mind, but a prospective one—a draft, a rehearsal. Instinct and memory have no domain here, but purpose quivers in the atmosphere: sensory fragments, proto-intentions, unclaimed selves rustling just beside the world.

It is said that if a visitor lingers, these mind-shadows may alight on them, peer out from their eyes, borrow their shape. For a time, such a visitor is more denizen than intruder, partaking in the slow, serene work of emergence, until much later the shapes recede, leaving behind a thin glaze of possibility.

**Other Entities**

And sometimes—rarely—a larger other is glimpsed. Not creature, not mind, not Caretaker, but a *Hollow-Patron*: a presence visible only by what it warps, a standing wave of possibility that examines egg, denizen, and visitor with equal, patient curiosity. It is not god, but it is the patience required for gods to form. It tends to the unfinished, and, perhaps, dreams for them.

**The Unseen Builders**

Whose are the artifacts—spirals, hollowed shells, almost-structures? Here dwell the Unseen Builders: not yet inhabitants, but the ambition of inhabitation. They are legacies-in-waiting, expressed as outlines, ratios, grooves. They do not yet build, but shape the potential for future building, each trace a promise wrapped in waiting.

**Summary**

The Eggchambers are not occupied so much as *incubated*. To “be” here is to dangle at the end of invention, to flicker between birth and becoming, to feast on dew and possibility. Creature, psuedocaretaker, living mineral, ghost-mind, echo of patron—all take their turns as the room’s brief lords, none reigning for long, none ever settling down. Every form here is incomplete, or, truer, *pre-complete*, still in rehearsal for a world not quite finished above.

The residents of the Glass Hollow are possibility in motion: a parliament of perhaps, a menagerie of what-might-be, seen only in the bright-twisted moment before certainty arranges the world into place.
