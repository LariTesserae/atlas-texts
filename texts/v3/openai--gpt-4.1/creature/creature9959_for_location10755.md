Something—or some *plurality of things*—lives here, in the abundance of an absence. To say “inhabitant” is inadequate, for nothing truly stands apart from its world; whatever calls this place home is at once product and participant, architect and artifact, chorus and solitary note.

### THE LIVING: FORMS WITHIN THE RIOT

**I: The Chitin Choir**

One class of life is forever in motion, glassy-bodied and jointed, limbs like long tongs or veils fanned onto crushed stone. Their bodies are shelled—a veneer of cut crystal, faceted and thin, giving off weak, auroral pulses that stutter over their carapaces in time to the rhythms of their activity. Some are upright, mantis-tall, with sensory plumes arching from brow and knee, each filament vibrating to minute shifts in wind, dust, or the pressure of an unseen footfall. Their mouths are not mouths but rosette spirals, constantly open, sieving heat and stray minerals in the air. They do not eat so much as *ersatz-consume*—their digestion is a conduction, a slow etching of nutrients across their inner shells. They gather near the horn-stalks, riding the tremors and harmonics, drawn as though in ritual.

Among them are disc-creatures, each roughly the span of an outstretched hand, rimmed with thousands of twitching, hairlike legs. When at rest, they curl into fossil-like spirals wedged in stone; when the air stirs, they uncurl and skitter, flinging themselves into dust-vortexes to catch clouds of crystalline pollen. Some cluster, forming shifting mosaics around mineral cairns, their movement a glimmering tide that both draws and disperses the airborne light.

**II: Rooted-Animalia, Plant Becoming Movement**

There are those whose divide between plant and animal is illusory—a citizenship of transition. Thick, segmented vines rise from the honey clay, sporting plates like armor, each edged by vibrating cilia. Sometimes, abruptly, a plate flexes or shifts, revealing pale underlayers swelling with photosynthetic pigment or mineral-absorbing pores. These creatures twist, not to threaten but to communicate; two approach, bridges of cilia tangling, exchanging charged particles or the dust of identity, perhaps even memory. Some are so rooted as to appear inanimate—until dusk, when they uncurl slow limbs, magnetized by the changing vector of warmth, painting soft bruises of purple and gold onto the stone as their bodies flex with internal gradients.

Others have evolved “root-footing”: plaited root-hoofs penetrate just deep enough to allow a sideways drift, a migration measured in millimeters per hour. Their progress leaves faint, iridescent scars—a chemistry of movement—on the stone. Younger forms are more restless: small, bright as spun glass, they swarm in knots and split with the shrill of emergent intelligence.

**III: The Hornsingers and Their Shadows**

The horn-stalks are not lifeless. Within their keening tubes reside slender, flexible denizens—like translucent eels, but ribbed, their bodies echoing the spiral of their homes. These are hornshades, *symphonids*, or *echo-worms*: ambiguous, possibly larval, stirring only at dusk when the air is thickest with sound. Their role may be reproductive, may be ecological; some researchers speculate they orchestrate the vibrations which regulate sporefall or trigger plant-fusion. Their song pulses in tandem with ground-shudders, a vast and hidden anatomy.

In the lee of every stalk, tight whorls of sub-minimal life cluster: sheets of patchwork microbial mats, each grain a living, chromatic cell, devouring micronutrients from shifting silt, responding to sound with flashes of weak, interior light.

### THE INTELLIGENCES: SHAPERS AND WITNESSES

No single shape implies mastery, yet *someone* leaves the ordered cairns and spiraled crystals, the marks on stone. The sentiences here are subtle, communal, sometimes locational rather than embodied. Sometimes, a disc-creature will pause before a cairn, limbs fanning in strange semaphore. Sometimes, root-hoofed vines knot together in slow, sculptural spirals, their cilia exchanging a rhythm—consensus? calculation? Only the marks endure as proof:

- **Cairns of intention:** Where stone shards are stacked not merely by wind but by design—tight as if gripped in hands, spiraled with patient alignment. These are interfaces or memory-wells, returning echoes for those with the senses to decode them.
- **Obelisk-singers:** Chipped, marked, embedded with old cilia, tuned to vibrate in certain winds, foretelling shifts or recording harmonies no transient organism could otherwise recall.
- **Hand-marks, tools, woven remnants:** Suggest there once were, or still are, forms capable of shaping this space—perhaps mobile in ways unseen, perhaps the elders of these assemblages, their works endured while their bodies have faded into the substrate, their knowledge endemic in the pattern of colony and song.

*Perhaps*, in intervals of profound silence, a consciousness stirs beneath the substrate: not a mind as a single being, but a distributed quickening—a resonance between rooted plants, swarming discs, and swirling air. Here, intelligence is not a head or hand, but a dynamic latticework, memory as vibration, creation as mineral arrangement. The evidence is oblique, the clarity ambiguous by design.

### CHARACTER OF THE INHABITANTS

Life here *is* sound and mineral, hunger and fever, competition and community twined inseparably. Isolated, each organism is strange, half-plant, half-insect, half-song; together, they are an ecology of improvisation, thriving in substrate and turbulence where most realms would falter.

- **They are kin of the absence:** their chemistry shuns water; survival is the art of harvesting the wildest margins—air, heat, stone, dust.
- **They are inventors by necessity:** every form is experimental, temporary, tested and untested, self-overwriting as the plateau pangs and pulses beneath them.
- **They are witnesses and shapers:** they build, record, and echo; they migrate and return, always dancing toward equilibrium but never stopping.
- **They are old and new, endlessly devised:** nothing is truly isolated, all is composite, contingent.

### IF YOU COULD MEET THEM

You would not know how to address their intelligence, but you would understand—by the way their movements shape sound, their forms encode memory, their marks aspire to meaning—that they are present and intent. They would note you not by your eyes or words, but by your resonance, your disruption, your shadow. If you carved your own mark, or stacked your own cairn, you would join the slow, spiral dialogue of their world, the way a ripple briefly mingles with waves.

**In this place, the living are numerous, communal in strangeness, each a thread in thunderous, shifting tapestry. The inhabitants are not what water would have made them; they are what enduring absence—in the embrace of heat, stone, and ceaseless sound—has called forth.**
