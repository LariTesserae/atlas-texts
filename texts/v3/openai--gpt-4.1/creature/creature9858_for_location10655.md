What, then, lives here?

**The Inhabitants: Living into Green**

They are not one thing, nor always easily distinguished from all the life that surrounds and envelops them; they are neither isolated masters nor mere guests within the boundless green. Those who dwell here are kin to the place, grown and growing, always in negotiation with its teeming change. No single image will suffice. When you first step in, you might think you see a person—but you look again, and see a posture, a garment, a gesture so aligned with the breathing swirl of the living city that it dissolves, but never vanishes.

**Their Bodies**

The people of this world are as various—and as mutable—as the vines and waters among which they move. Most appear broadly humanoid, shaped by ancient need for hands to weave, eyes to watch, feet to balance atop living arches. Yet even the youngest are marked by the persistent touch of their green world. Their bones are light and flexible; joints twist easily to dance the corkscrew turns of branch and bridge. Skin glows with earthen golds and rich leaf-green, mottled with moss-brown, laced with vein-blue and fungus-white; each individual is a living map of territory and season.

Their hair is rarely only hair. Lichen threads among it, small epiphytes root in thick coils, and blossoms sprout behind ears—these are neither adornment nor burden, but lived-with companions, a micro-ecology trailing through life. Camouflage is not conscious, but inborn: their hands and feet sprout minute filaments that taste the air, absorb water, and sense the tremble of approaching steps, threading their senses far beyond the reach of human skin.

They smell of green sap, sweet fermentation, old wood and fresh earth. A healthy person’s breath steams in the shade—the sign of an inner heat housed amid dampness. Wounds heal fast, as the body calls foreign cells and friendly fungus to seal and renew.

**Ways of Dress (and Undress)**

Clothes are temporary—clinging, shifting, and worn as extensions of the self. Many wear living things: mats of velvet moss, capes of broad leaves sewn with rootlets, necklaces of jewel-colored seeds, hats woven from reeds. But often, their “garment” is simply what grows with them: a sleeve of bark, a skirt of runners and tendrils that respond to weather and fashion alike. Nothing is wholly dead; fashion is gardened, not owned, and patterns change with the arc of the sun.

Ritual finery might see individuals inviting rare fungi to bloom upon their skin, or coaxing hard, lacquered leaves into armor for festival contests. The most honored weave bird-shed feathers into moss-crowns; the wisest let slow vines wrap their limbs until it is hard to tell where person ends and other life begins.

**Society: Dense and Dynamic**

To live here is to weave and unweave. The inhabitants move in restless patterns—work is done in groups, hands skilled in guiding, shaping, and diverting the endlessly growing surround. They tend not only crops, but shelter, arches, and streets—the settlement itself is a communal living project, and no space belongs to a single soul for long.

Families may cluster in single chambers, but friends, neighbors, even passing animals are welcome wherever growth allows. New shoots push through sleeping mats, and children learn early to step lightly and tend what emerges. Anyone might be called to reshape a bridge, reinforce a wall, or feed a new nestling fallen from above. Each season, names are claimed or set aside, affiliations redrawn, duties rebalanced like the weight of sunlight upon the canopy.

Elders are not rulers but “garden-holders,” those who have tended and reshaped the same living thing for a full cycle of growth; their wisdom gleams in their patient hands and vine-plaited mantles. Leadership, like territory, is always provisional, always ceded in time.

**Culture: Mind of Mutual Becoming**

This people’s art is ephemeral, made to support, decorate, or commune with the living: stories are sung in polyphony so that the echoes cling to leaf and stone; tales are woven as living trellises, their shape shifting as seasons prod the tendrils along new paths. Festivals are called at dawn, when the whole city shines under rain, or at dusk, when fungal beacons burn gold. Some paint their faces in sap and pollen, others etch temporary signs into bark, knowing that all will vanish beneath new growth.

Food is plentiful, but always foraged and coaxed, never hoarded: the bounty of drupes, roots, grubs, quick-nursed honey, and the small, clever animals that thrive in vegetative company. Burials are not separations but fusions—the dead are taken back into the thick of moss and water, their names carried by wind and creeping vine.

Their language is thick with roots and shoots: a gesture blossoms into meaning, names bud and wither, truth is felt as the shimmer of movement or the pulse of a frog’s call at dusk. They think in patterns, in change, in the inevitable overlapping of needs. Magic, if you would call it that, is their patience—the trained hand, the whispered word to growing things, the way they coax leaf and root to do what others might force.

**Spirit: The Place Becoming People**

From time to time a native may pause, letting a chameleon insect alight on a mossy shoulder, or kneeling to listen as roots tremble, passing news of a coming storm. In rare moments of stillness, whole families share silence and watch a new flower bloom and die within an hour.

Yet above all, the denizens of this living city are not apart. They do not “have” a landscape; they are an extension of it: as much shaped as shapers, as much grown as growing. They hold no myth of a golden age, for every dawn rebirths them; every dusk, they return to green.

Who lives here?  
A breathing partnership—people whose bones are twined with lichen, whose lives are borrowed from sunlight and sap, whose cities are as shifting, crowded, and ripe as a beating heart. They are made, every day, of what surrounds them—a harmony forever improvising itself anew within the world’s green embrace.
