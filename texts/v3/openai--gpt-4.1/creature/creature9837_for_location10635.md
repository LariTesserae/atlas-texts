What lives in the Plateau of Roots? Life, first and always, but life as spell and web, life as slow, enfolding intelligence.

**The Substrate Dwellers**

At the heart of this place are the Mindless Builders: the plants themselves. But “plant” isn’t the word. The Plateau is a kingdom of vast super-organisms, layered and fused together. Individuality ends at the stem — everything emerges from seamless integration, root-networked, sprawling, ceaselessly knitting and unraveling its own tissue. Deep below, the Ancient Roots tangle in blackness, pressure-tight and slime-smooth, slow-pulsing with stored water and pale sap, their reach measured in miles. Here, mycelium threads link root to root, a communication lattice, and from time to time a pulse moves, a local decision: to thicken, to withdraw, to bloom, to heal.

Rising up, the root-plate corrugates into towers and domes: some solid, armored — others hollow, rooms coiled and softly lit from within by bioluminescent walls, green-lit caverns as large as amphitheatres. These are the Heartnodes, where root-minds cluster in denser webs. You might think, while drowsing inside, that you hear voices, a patient muttering of cellulose and sugar. It is thought, if thought can happen in cycles so slow they stretch over seasons. The trees do not dream as we dream; they pulse and remember.

Somewhere above, vast sheets of leaf, some the size of ponds, gather sunlight in the haze. They move minutely, adjusting to mists and the drift of wind, veined with purple fluid, secreting sweetness for resident creatures. Coiling stems form bridges, arching ridges between higher roots, the only “roads” in the living wilderness.

**The Interlocutors**

Creatures here are not isolated from the substrate. Most have entered into bargains — genetic, chemical, ancestral — with the green. *Weevils* flash by, their fur stained violet by lichen dust, chewing minute trails through bark, scraping savor from nectar-nodes and leaking, in turn, pheromones that draw root-tips to new seams of nutrients. Their larvae are born to burrow, and may spend their entire lives enfolded, unseeing, in the substrate, guiding the slow regrowth with the heat and acid of their bodies.

In the mists above, the *spindly birds* — neither wholly avian nor entirely plant — drift from perch to bloom. Their feet are articulated roots, gentle grippers; their calls echo with the hollow resonance of sapwood. When they nest, they plait together living filaments, binding eggs with adhesive sap. Some nests persist for decades, climbing the same trunk as years pass, blooming with the resident’s linage.

*Marsupial chords* are a legend here, strings of soft-bodied, nearly translucent creatures joined back-to-back, weaving themselves into hanging bridges. They feed in silence, tongues flicking deep into fluid-filled flowers, excreting minerals onto roots below. Their bodies pulse with percussive, glassy music at dusk — a territory marker, a calling.

The smallest forms are the most numerous: *lichen-lice* drifting like dust motes, feeding across the topmost moss, hundreds per square meter, each with its own spectrum of color. Some are hosts to glowing fungal threads, lanterns in the dim bioluminescent gloom. Others spend their entire lives in the water-pocket of a single cup-leaf, growing larval forms that become adult only to drift on the next mist.

Fungi here are *builders and nurses*. There are thick, fingered polypores whose purpose is to digest but never destroy: they break down the fallen, making the spent part useful again, cycling it back into the living green. Some have symbioses with motile slugs — the slug eats the fruiting body, and in return, its slime contains spores that heal wounds in the living crust, stitching over breach and break.

**Foreigners**

Ghosts of other times appear sometimes in form, not spirit: *descendants of ancient arrivals*, feral offshoots of creatures whose ancestors walked in from less overwhelming forests long millennia ago. There are furtive, non-native mice, grown furred and silent, diurnal in a world without strong day-night rhythm. There are the shape-ghosts of lost birds, ossified now, their descendants reduced to small, shivering things that hide on the borders of the tallest trunks, barely getting by on what the plateau’s economy permits them. Here, all foreign things are adopted, absorbed, or erased. Some old altars or pillars are home to new species of font-moss, which only grow on the memory of stone.

**Movements: Individual and Collective**

Yet there is also *motion that is not quite animal or plant*: large, slow-moving boluses of root and moss that crawl, at the tempo of seasons, to transport nutrients, gather stray roots into fusion, or repair great wounds. If you watched for long enough — longer than decades — you might see an entire hollow close, fluid-rooted, then open again in another place.

Murmured through the substrate, a greater will hums. The Living Plateau seems not conscious like an animal, but responsive, adjusting its bounds and flows as would an immune system, or a heart regulating blood. The climax organisms here — massive, trunked, ancient — are not singular but collective, an unhurried congress of timber, root, lichen, and bloom, each constituent species a delegate in a vegetal parliament that has held session for millions of years.

**If There Are People**

Once, perhaps, people lived here. Their works have gone. If any remain, they are as changed as everything else. They would not live on the Plateau — they would live *in* it: skin stained green, breath damp as moss, movements slowed to match the world. They would speak quietly, if ever, hands moss-padded, eyes adapted to the gloom, carrying with them minor animal symbiotes as we carry herds or flocks. They would mark time in seasons of fruiting, in the closing and opening of root-channels, in the long drift of fog.

But for the most part, what “lives” here is the *Plateau of Roots* itself: the sum of all the co-operative, competitive, patient workings of forest and moss, lichen and fungus, insect and animal — a world alive but not conscious, aware but not thinking, ancient and endless, in which all things are drawn down, reworked, returned — and in which nothing, not even a memory of metal or fire or conquest, can remain unassimilated for long.
