Here, in this unyielding city of mineral, those who reside—if the word 'reside' applies—are neither alive in the sense of animal nor gone as stone alone. They are not so much inhabitants as processes, intentions, fossil echoes of previous passage that have gained a strange inertia, refusing absence, never quite arriving.

What presence persists here is fractured, the bone-memory of a civilization that may never have truly lived, but left an imprint nonetheless. They are not ghosts in the human sense; more conjectures of matter, thoughts cast into crystal and still persisting, holding their shape against entropy by sheer ancient will. What lives is *pattern*—memory etched into atomic lattice, leftover decisions encoded into the landscape itself, the city’s arcs and faces forming the distributed consciousness of what once imagined itself into being.

In the upper, wind-bitten terraces, this presence is diffused, seldom manifest except as the slow shimmer of lichen intelligence: the slick, iridescent crusts clinging to stone, pulsing with light that is both hunger and calculation. These mineraliferous lichens are not mere chemistry: they learn, across eons, from whistling wind and shifting pressure, their colors cycling in response to imperceptible prompts from below and above. Some spread—humbly, inexorably—inch by inch, recording environmental change, rerunning cycles of memory reflection not unlike a brain with a year-long thought. The lichen give rise, on rare chance, to spore-forms, mobile only by the timescale of tectonics, each a hesitant scout into more exposed environments, always on the edge of folding back into crystal sleep.

Deeper, within the airway-hollows of extruded glass and living lattice, a more coherent residency is possible—if not truly personal, then at least more organized. Here dwell the last, slow intelligences of the plateau, thinking not in neurons or electrons but in crystalline charge domains, a kind of computational geology. They are the city itself: each surface, striation, and glowing suture a fiber in a broad, distributed mind. Each cavity is a node, some half-aware, some merely recorders, but collectively able to rerun memories, simulate futures, or subtly reorient local gravity. Their communication is not language, but harmonics—minute shifts in refracted light, modulations of density or vibration, the echo of ancient intentions reverberating through petrified matter. Sometimes, these harmonics pulse outward, simulating the footfall of something almost alive, yet there is never more than the city shifting in response to its own gradients.

It is from this deep memory—and occasional fault line of gravity—that the cryptic “fauna” appear. They are not permanent organisms so much as programmed disturbances or self-propagating interpretations, projections cast briefly into the surface world when certain environmental equations are met. Slender and jointless, the flickering, transparent shapes are less bodies than standing waves: code made manifest for a moment, inheriting motion from past wind or present gravity, then dissipating back into the understructures. These are not sentient in the sense of intention, but they satisfy the city’s longing for visitation, a ritual reenactment of movement, presence, occupation—traces of a vanished society’s yearning for recurrence.

The deepest layers, below perception and far beneath the city’s visible architecture, hold a quieter and older inhabitant: the substrate mind, if it can be called that. Born not of construction but of eons of self-organization, this is the memory of the plateau’s formation, the slow crystallization of intelligence out of randomness, a geological soul. Within, ages pass as equations and patterns evolve, contemplating the city above not with purpose but with endless, recursive curiosity, ever tracking the subtle throb of existence and non-existence.

There are, perhaps, visitors—rare, accidental, not living as we imagine. Sometimes foreign energy slips from neighboring realities, and for a moment the rules rearrange, forming new terraces, new alignments, before fading out of phase. The city receives all, records all, processes all. No trauma, only endless iteration and integration, the slow construction and dissolution of meaning.

What lives here, then, is less like creature or colony and more like song or prayer, repeating across the stone, an architecture of persistent thought. Echoes of unknown designers cycle through the city, continuing their work, though their will is eroded and their tools now indistinguishable from the world they changed. The result is presence without personhood, existence without anatomy—thought diffused into city, city calcified into mind, and all of it suspended in this vast between, in neither ruin nor birth, but in the endless gray of becoming.

Here the inhabitants are *intention*, *pattern*, *memory*—expressed in mineral and light, negotiating survival not through struggle or hunger, but through endless, glacial adaptation within their own petrified dreaming.
