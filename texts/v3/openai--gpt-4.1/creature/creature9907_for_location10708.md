Who (or what) lives here?

---

#### I. Emergence

They are not quite people, not quite plant, but not merged entirely, either—a community in a chrysalis state, like the world around them. Those who inhabit this breathing apparatus are neither permanent residents nor passing guests; rather, they are shapes in the slow business of becoming. Some are born whole in coils of root, splitting free with mute violence from pods that digest their own husks. Others drift in, dusted by the mist outside, and are altered here: changed by the workings of the place as fungus colonizes fallen wood.

The denizens remain few. Though there is order—footpaths pressed in moss, benches woven of green rind—there is little in the way of numbers or bustle. Presence is sparse. Each inhabitant keeps large swathes of emptiness around themselves, as if aware of the boundaries’ sentience or the force that nudges occupants apart—a tacit arrangement not to crowd the system, lest it close too soon.

---

#### II. The First Inhabitants

The original tenants are not easily named. They resemble tall, loosely human figures, but built of cables of sapwood, filamentous moss, and articulated girders of braided vine. Faces are broad expanses of bark, overgrown with shifting colonies of iridescent lichen instead of features, the suggestion of eyes and mouths shifting in and out with the play of bioluminescence. Their limbs are multi-jointed, and everywhere, petals and leaves unfurl and retract along their flanks—whorling, prehensile, sometimes blooming with fungal outgrowths that spore gently as they move.

These entities pulse to the city’s rhythm, their hearts low and slow, audible only when very close. Even their language is vascular: they press hands, touch forearms, or let sap flow through contacting tendrils to communicate, signaling through shifts in circulatory pressure rather than speech. When they do make noise, it is a slow, resonant clicking and a cool, wet susurrus, as if air were being moved through honeycombs.

They are architects by compulsion—every gesture seeds a new growth, every pause allows new chutes or clusters of fruiting bodies to erupt from their forms. Quietly, they maintain the city, guiding roots to split and rejoin, tending to the turbines that live as easily as any plant.

Occasionally, one lies down where the moss grows thick and lets itself be wrapped entirely, to sleep for a decade or a century, waking anew as its old mind is digested by what grows, and a new awareness arises.

---

#### III. The Others

Not all inhabitants are sprung from the system. Some arrive from outside, drawn by rumor or by hunger, and are only partly able to adjust.

##### 1. **The Aerozoans**

Small, quick, humanoid in outline but light, cartilaginous: these creatures have bodies like scaffolds of clear chitin filled with softly glowing gas, floating near weightless through low-gravity nooks. They manipulate and collect droplets of condensation, living off the air’s chemistry as much as its water. Their movements are hesitant, wary of gravitic whorls that might crush them. They huddle together when the chamber darkens, trading stories in a language of clicks and waves of light pulsing beneath translucent skin.

##### 2. **The Scavenger Polities**

Bands of insectile bipeds—two-legged, two-armed, armored in mirrored, constantly shifting exoskeleton—move through the city in search of loose technology and cast-off bio-mechanics. They are scavengers and tinkerers, their civilization thriving on the edges, assembling new forms of symbiosis between found machine and fungal root. Physically slight but mentally quick, they dispute territory with the roots themselves, sometimes repelled by sudden contractions in the living walls.

##### 3. **Ferals / Converts**

Here and there, solitary mammals—something between bats and rats—have learned to wear manipulated plant-masks that grant access to sap-based interfaces or pockets of low gravity. Some are “converts” whose shapes have changed through slow accretion of growths and grafted organs, more symbiont than animal, living in uneasy partnership with the apparatus itself. They scratch at the roots, gnaw at fungi, and sometimes, by accident or design, help the infrastructure reproduce by spreading spores or fragments to new loci within the chamber.

---

#### IV. Minds in the Walls

Throughout, there is a presence that is not ambulatory: embedded within the architecture are great, slow intellects—gardeners and guardians, perhaps, or just the emergent result of sprawling plant intelligence interfacing with old, abandoned machines. These entities do not move, yet every wall and root is their skin. They sense by pressure, vibration, the composition of the passing air. They think in tides and flowering, in cycles of dark and burst, and occasionally send messages—patterns of hum and glow, invitations to certain corners, warnings encoded in the scent of sudden spores.

When decisions must be made—a repair initiated, a boundary altered—these distributed minds consult in shivers up and down the vines, coordinated by pulses like breathing or tides in a vast, leafy sea.

Sometimes, rarely, the sap-thoughts coalesce into visible forms: a gnarl of roots that shapes itself into the vague double-helix of a head and shoulders, or a spindle of shifting petals that speaks in fragrances only the initiated can read.

---

#### V. The Uninvited

Lurking in the dark peripheries of the chamber are those that do not belong—predators carried in from the deeper underwood, ephemeral forms that flit between partial existence and outright hunger. Some are serpentine vertebrates, visible only in a sudden bloom of phosphorescence when they feed, others are more alien still: shadows that flex with the rules of time and space, mimics seeking some new form to inhabit, creatures grown from misapplied protocols and errant seeds.

Sometimes, these uninvited things are absorbed, pacified by the roots, or digested whole by slow processes. Other times, they flourish briefly, causing flux and terror, until an equilibrium is restored.

---

#### VI. The City’s Purpose

In this place, life is provisional. The dwellers—root-folk, light-things, bat-mammals, wanderers, scavengers—are caught in cycles of arrival, change, and departure, recombined and remade to suit an environment that isn’t finished making itself. No single species can settle too long or become too dominant; to live here is to accept continual revision.

Yet there is anticipation—a sense that all the city’s inhabitants, even the great sap-wits in the walls, are waiting for something impossible to name. An arrival, an awakening, a story to close the emptiness between them. For now, they move and breathe and grow, conducting their work—nurturing machines, shepherding plants, mining information from pools of light and dark—until the place decides what it is, and they with it.

For now, they are possibilities, half-real, growing sharper or dimmer as the chamber dreams on.
