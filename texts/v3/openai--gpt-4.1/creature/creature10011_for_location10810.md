Those who live here—the First Founders, the Underfolk, the Stoneborn—are as provisional and unfinished as the land they inhabit. They are not a monolith, yet their natures rhyme with the place: eager, attentive, half-shaped by necessity, porous to what might be.

**The People:**
Their bodies are wiry, their forms marked by the splay and narrowing of generations bred to close quarters and scant resource. Most are smallish in stature, compact and resilient, able to crawl or fold into the tightest niches, to lever themselves up rock shelves or flatten their weight across slick, granular stone. Their skin takes after the land’s chroma—pale, earthen, sometimes dusted with a faint, mineral sheen where water and stone have worn them smooth. Where water pools regularly, a flush of mold-colored freckling blooms under the skin: a living map of their motion through the moisture cycles. Some bear the mottled tattooing of family marks, made with ground pigments and the sting of new copper.

Their hair grows in tough, wiry mats, often shaved partially away or wound into protective plaits, catching dust and damp and holding the needles of pale moss they use as both adornment and filter against grit. Eyes are sharp-set beneath pronounced brows; pupils expand to wide, inky wells in the fugitive light, drinking in every photon. Their nails are tools—blunted, thickened, used for scratching lichen from rock or prying up the loose seams of mineral beneath the surface.

They are layered as their city: bands of tough woven fabric—reed, root, spun moss—tied tight around knees, waists, and shoulders. Jewelry is uncommon unless it serves a double purpose: a chisel worn as a brooch, a water-straining net wound as a sash. Caught in the mutter and hush of their world, their posture is careful: shoulders close, voices low, every gesture measured so as not to send dangerous stones or precious debris tumbling down the packed ledges.

**Their Social Structure:**
There is no apex ruler here—leadership is passed in cycles, in assemblies that gather on the largest ledges or in hollowed gathering vaults. Authority passes to those with skill at interpreting the valley’s moods: keen readers of condensation patterns, poets of geology, those who know when to risk hollowing deeper, or when to seam a new tunnel, or how to ration, lest soil, or warmth, or water become fatefully scarce.

Every household is a cluster: a tangle of extended kin, foundling adoptees, and the rotating companies of workers whose efforts are pooled and rotated across the maze of functions—harvesting, shaping, tending, patching, carrying. Children are raised in common, left to crawl and scramble over every surface, their laughter brief and rare but carrying, in those moments, nearly to the ceiling cracks.

**Their Growth and Labor:**
They are inventors at heart. Where stone gives grudgingly, they wedge and lever and prop; when water is fickle, they lay out cunning catchment cloths and gutters of split reed. They coax gardens from any crumb of soil—a sprig of moss pressed into a thumbprint of earth, a rootling shielded in a bowl of condensation. Nothing is lost: bones become awls, shed hair is corded into twine, every broken vessel is patched and pressed into new use.

Food is part foraged, part cultivated, always shared. Dishes are gruels and pastes—moss, fungus, root, and the rare fungal-threaded egg of the pale cave-nesting swallow. Sometimes, something proteinous is boiled from the long, thin slugs that leave their silver behind in the wet—an acquired taste, but one learned early.

**Other Inhabitants:**
The people are not alone. The deeper shelves are corralled by pale-bellied rodents, sharp-eyed and ever-scheming, kept both as scavengers and, reluctantly, as food. The long-bodied slugs are harvested and cajoled, their secretions used to bind stone or waterproof simple clothing. Animal companions are pragmatic: a thin, nervous breed of dog, bred to dart first and bark only as a last resort; clusters of insectoid grazers, tended for their fine, silk-like fiber; lizardkin, their hides cherished for warmth.

Still, the wild remains: flickering, unclaimed lives. The rare, delicate bird settles only where the fractured light is strongest, and rarely for long. Tiny, many-legged scavengers swarm during the rain cycles and recede at the first sign of dryness, leaving behind ghostly tracings in the dust.

**Spirit and Tone:**
Those below are not joyless, only careful. Caution is not solemnity: they practice a sparse exuberance. Songs are sung in the round, sometimes only percussive, built from the clatter of bone on wood, the pulse of hands on low stone tables, the echo of breath in tunnels. Stories are etched into memory and into the very living rock: panels of chipped narrative, each mark growing out of the last.

They are neither primitive nor advanced—they are *adaptors,* improvisers, the vanguard of a world being born alongside them. They shape their existence as if troweling order into the fresh edge of dawn: stone-knowers, water-sifters, makers in the dark and the new.

**At the Edges of Dream:**
There are always rumors, whispered in the deepest vaults or around the most precarious fires, of what else might be waking farther below. Some say there are things half-mineral, half-flesh, stirred to life by the shifting of the valley—stone that remembers, molds that speak, veins of metal in which something like thought has begun to stir. The people leave offerings: a twist of moss, a slick stone, a bead of water balanced on a finger. Out of hope; out of a watchful, wordless pact.

**In All, These Live Here:**
- The handmaking, hand-remembering, hand-learning folk—close-knit, cautious, endlessly ingenious
- The creeping and fluttering, the slick-trailing and bright-eyed, each lending and taking from the rough new ecology
- The stone itself, half-conscious, pushing toward form, gathering both roots and dwellings into itself as if drawing a blanket of civilization in the very hour before true morning

All living as one breath, one slow, complicated coming-together—a people and their place, arising together, each an unfinished promise.
