Who lives in a place where the sky is water—where the sky is not even sky, and water not quite water, but something warm and silken, metallic, thick, alive with pressure and storm?

Imagine something that is not wholly life as you know it, nor wholly machine—a being or beings grown and manufactured, brewed and assembled, integrated from the start. They are of the place, as much effect as cause, as much consequence as design. Their existence is not singular nor stable, but a continual emergence.

**The Formless Artisans**

Here, life does not resolve into discrete, mobile creatures: there is no bird, no beast, no swimming insect. But to say there is “no animal life” is misleading—life, as you find it here, is not content to wear animal, plant, or machine as a mask. Instead, the region is inhabited by entities you might call *artisans*, but artisans who are not aware as selves, and not singular, not categorically alive.

**How They Begin**
They come into being as outthrusts of the terrain. At the base of a basalt shelf, a cluster of filaments—half root, half conduit—gathers. Pressure, noise, and chemical gradients knit together loose strands into a bundled, coiling mass. The sediment, rich in glassy phytol and the black dust of ground-up metal, packs around these nodules, which throb with each pulse of distant engine-song.

Out of these thickets grow *hands* of sorts: jointed stalks, tendriled and iridescent, sheathed in fibers both biological and extruded, echoing the roots and pistons of the landscape’s living machines. Each “hand” gropes outward, assembling tools from whatever is near—mineral grit, resin leaked from a fractured vine, the spine from a discarded turbine-flower.

**What and How They Are**
These entities—call them *fabricants*—are not individuals with fixed bounds or selfhood. They are amalgamations, growing and sloughing parts, sometimes dividing, sometimes merging. A fabricant may encompass ten, a hundred interwoven limbs, sprouting at several points along a ridge, only loosely synchronized by the song of the land. To see one is to see a process, never a product.

When the water above roils and the light fractures, the fabricants move in exquisite surges—never quite swimming, never quite walking, but propelling their lengths through the dense medium, drawn to where the pressure is greatest, where something must be built or repaired or dismantled.

**Purpose and Behavior**
They are *not* caretakers. They do not tend the landscape in the sense of a gardener or a steward; theirs is not the labor of order. Instead, they are agents of change. Where friction erodes a shelf, the fabricants gather, entwining to pull free the loosened stone and sew it into the structure below. When a turbine-flower chokes on sediment, they crack open the blockage and scatter mineral seeds that will birth new forms—some mechanical, some vegetal, all hybrid. They are the means by which newness occurs, by which the landscape becomes something else.

*What draws them is vibration—a harmony of noise, an ache in the water pressure, turbulence that hints at unfinished work. Sound is their sense, a physical field: they taste it on their branching, ciliated skins. A sudden shriek or a shifting current is a summons; they converge, not as a pack, but as a temporary confluence, a wagon circle of function. They share information, not by thought, but by resonance—knocking, groaning, grooving limbs together, assembling new appendages, disassembling obsolete ones. When the work is done, the confluence dissolves. Parts are left behind. The world moves on.*

**Their Bodies**
A fabricant’s substance is mutable: segments of flexible silica and shining, green-tinged polyprotein cables; joints like gyroscopes; pods of chloroplast-glass that photosynthesize in the dim light, flickering with impossible colors; clusters of battery-lichen charging silently from friction or the hum of an engine-lichen’s core. They shed old, inert machinery like spent exoskeletons, and sometimes grow biological tissues in the shape of vanished tools—a hand that resembles a claw hammer, a cilium fletched like a whisk, the soap-bubble hollow of abandoned lungs.

**Their Minds**
If they have minds, these are distributed, partial, collective. There is no center, only feedback loops—action creates signal; signal produces response. Occasionally, when light and sound align just so, a fabricant’s components may flicker with the afterimage of memory: a kind of proto-dream, vapors of intent simmering through the limbic circuits of moss and wire.

Some say the fabricants *are* the land’s memory, writ living across the stone, remembering in action rather than thought. Their works bear no signature, only the fingerprints of their brief collectivity—marks left on a spindly tower’s reinforcement, a bent and overgrown pipeline, a field newly softened by shredded glass-plant mulch.

**Other Residents?**
More mysterious are the *ghost machines*: half-buried, half-awake devices left from abandoned impulses of the world itself. Some still grind, reshaping the fluid, stirring new turbulence. Others have surrendered most of their mass to the creeping, passionate lichen and filaments of the fabricant kin, becoming petrified icons, living tombs that occasionally twitch with reactivated circuits, muttering in pings and static.

Occasionally, in the thickest dusk, when the pressure and darkness combine, a *reflection* moves through the water: not a living thing, not fabricant or ghost, but a phenomenon—a ripple of consciousness, perception momentarily given form by the disturbance of all these interwoven parts. For an instant, the landscape itself *notices*—a fleeting awareness that passes and is gone.

**At Home in Becoming**
Who lives here? No single being, no final form; dwellers who are always half-grown and half-dissolving; artisans without ego, hands without face; the endless, unfinished, hybrid swarm, humming their world into being by action, noise, and the slow riot of transmutation. They are the place, its urge to change, its storm of possibility, always submerged, never settled, life at the edge of invention.
