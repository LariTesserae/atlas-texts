Who—or what—lives here?

To dwell in such a world is not to have a name, nor even necessarily a body as we know shape. The inhabitants of this sodden eternity are the inheritors of dissolution, shaped by patience, memory, and the way water learns to hold things without holding onto them.

There are certainly entities here one might call living, but they are not discrete, brisk creatures so much as phenomena with persistence. Some are accumulations, others are slow events, but all are subtle, permeable, ambiguous, and patient in their choreography with time.

**1. Mat-Weavers and Colony-Dreamers:**
The most pervasive inhabitants are not single beings, but processes: the mat-weavers. Their nature is both plant and animal, and neither; their boundaries are so ambiguous that the idea of an individual fades into fancy. Composed of braided, cytoplasmic chords, their "growth" is more an act of layering—chronic deposition of cells, slime, and translucent branches, all pressing together in semi-organized layers. To call them a colony is to understate their unity, for their dreaming is collective. Down the long, drifting corridors of memory, their signals—chemical, perhaps electrical—shiver through mats, making the entire local ecosystem doze and wake in synchrony.

Information moves here like weather: sleep and alertness, stress and long relaxation propagate as pulses, so that mats far apart share a common sensation without ever directly touching.

**2. The Filamentaries:**
A rare emergence in the vertical currents is the filamentary—something between a leviathan and a shadow. These are long, sinuous ribbons tangled from the slime-mats’ own cast-off strands, but animated, perhaps, by the stray intensification of some energetic process—a gradient caught and exploited. Occasionally one stirs with languid, telescoping movements, trailing many cilia, its body glass-clear except for the nesting of faintly glowing inclusions. It is indifferent to you, predator to nothing, a recycler perhaps, unhurriedly consuming old matter and leaving behind trails of faint radiance, which are quickly overwritten by more growth.

**3. The Gleaners:**
Much smaller, more mobile, and more rare, the gleaners are anomalous: translucent bipeds or tripeds, appearing only as distortions and discontinuities as they move through the slow, living matrices. Their bodies are barely denser than the ambient water, camouflaged by refracted light, but you sense them more than see them. It is the idea of a gleaner that is present: a flicker viscid to memory, a subtle gravity warping the mat in their wake. They eat detritus, clear old growth, redistribute. The world might take millennia to notice their passing, and to a gleaner, centuries might be a day.

**4. The Echo-Shelled—the Armored Crawlers:**
Their armor is glassy, laminated from ancient minerals, reminders of a time when the world was less soft. These beings move with a gravity befitting stone that remembers mountain: slow, ponderous, unhurried by predation. Their limbs are many, jointed in spirals and hexagons, each tip cushioned for the terrain. They seek out relics and shards, gathering and assembling fragments with unclear purpose, perhaps only to test forgotten instincts of construction. Occasionally, one will pause for years at a time, etched with slow lichen growth, as if remembering—or waiting to be remembered.

**5. The Infixers—shape-memory artifacts:**
In the periphery between life and object, you find the infixers: things that anchor, but are not alive, or only barely. They are artifacts from another time, or else grown by the obsessive logic of these living mats—a column, a plate, spirals buried in sediment and layered with green. The infixers channel slow moving currents, fracture flow, or hold charge; some pulse gently with osmotic tides. They are reminders that intentionality once existed—be it biological or artificial, it is impossible to say. The living things now grow through and around them as if in reverence, or out of habit.

**6. The Jawless Dream:**
Sightings are rare, but sometimes the world’s thick calm is contradicted by the passage of a jawless thing—shape mutable, membranes transparent, gliding without effort through frond and silt. Its insides echo with swirling particulate, the refuse of eons, illuminated fitfully by passing motes. Observing it, you feel not threat, but the instability of place: as if the world could fold upon itself at its passing, and you might, for a moment, encounter the old world’s memory directly, naked and untranslatable.

**7. The Listeners:**
More conceptual than animal—perhaps vestiges of something long dispersed—listeners exist as spatial anomalies, regions of unnaturally complete hush where even the distant creak of weed-mat or the whump of current dies away. Here, for a moment, things pause, as if waiting to be observed or remembered. To venture into such a pocket is to feel your own boundaries soften; you lose track of direction and of time, as if something immense and quiet is present around and through you, recalling the world as it once was.

**Ways of Being:**
Nothing here is young. All life, if “life” persists as an idea, is the afterimage of time’s patience. Here, survival is endurance without struggle, persistence that is indistinguishable from erosion, a kind of peaceful participation in the sedimentation of memory itself. The most animated act is a ripple through the mat, a glacial rearrangement, the quieting spool of a filamentary encountering a pocket of warmth.

There might be thought here, deep and slow—processes old as worlds, where recollection is more important than novelty. Everything drifts, not toward a goal, but toward ever more subtle equilibrium, layering presence and fading boundary.

To live here is not to search, but to remember, to drift and accumulate, to forget, to be patient, and, in soft motion, to dissolve into the long, green dusk of perpetual, aqueous dusk.
