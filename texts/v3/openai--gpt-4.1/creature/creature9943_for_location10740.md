Who, or what, lives here?  
*Let them grow before your eyes, as the world does.*

### The Denizens of the Living Plateau

#### **I. The Small Ones: Makers of the Mesh**

Beneath your feet, within the kilometers-thick rootmat, live the **Root-Delvers**. They are old as the mesh itself—**armored, multi-jointed creatures**, their carapaces eclectically striped in pale lichen and ochre ridges. Twice as long as your hand, slow-moving, they gnaw tunnels with mandibles blunted to chisel shapes. Yet they are gentle engineers; their labor does not kill the mesh but enables it to breathe, to exchange air and water between hollows. When they swarm, it is a muted thunder, a rustle that swells until whole mats tremble.

Woven among the surface tangle are the **Weft-Gliders**—silken, membranous flutterers, spiders writ immense, forming bridges from spun protein. Their webs are not predatory traps but **living architecture**, cradling seeds spilt from the sky and lending spring and resilience to the trampoline-mat of ground. They and the Root-Delvers seldom meet, yet their worlds overlap—a handshake between fiber and structure, crossing in the dusk light.

#### **II. The Roarers and Whisperers: Sky-Muscle and Mist-Drinkers**

Out in the gallery-vastness between the trunks, striding from bole to bole, are the **Great Thunders**—beast-forms that split the distance, moving with balletic, cautious strength. Picture a sloth magnified and reimagined: six sturdy limbs, fur like draped curtains of moss and sliced leaf, their slow swaying stirring clouds of spores. Wide eyes, dark-ringed, survey with unhurried hunger. They are both herds and individuals—territorial, solitary except in mating surges when, for a few days, the galleries ring with antler-on-antler collisions, a sound lost in the wider roar.

Embedded in the higher boughs, inhabiting the thick, hollowed trunks, are the **Rain-Veined**. They are secretive, nearly invisible—skin a lacquered green, slick with moisture, bearing sparse tendrils of moss that collect water from the humid air. They drift, almost passive: glider-limbs spread like handkerchiefs, sipping from every rain-pool, lowering themselves by thin mycelial cords to drink or bathe in condensation. Their eyes reflect the canopy like drops of oil in clouded glass.

#### **III. The Eaters and the Eaten: Cycles in Violence**

Among the vines, racing in the open, or crouched at the shadowed pools, the **Hollow-Hunters** prowl. They are flexible, variable—nothing is uniform here. Some may resemble a long-limbed jaguar with the scales and segmented arms of a centipede; others, swift-bodied as serpents with jaws divided into six. Their camouflage is absolute: patterned with mirror-like luster, they vanish against bark, reappear in the open only as heat-shimmer and sound. They hunt by vibration, reading the living pulse of the place—a constant arms race in senses.

After them come the **Devour-Hosts**—flotation-creatures, part animal, part ballooning fungus, moving in flocks through the haze, dropping spore-darts into wounds left by the Hollow-Hunters. Each wound becomes a garden: fruiting bodies swelling fat on carcass-rot, drawing hummingbirds and beetles and more. Their scent is sickly, sweet-sharp, thickening the air for hours.

#### **IV. The Builders: Barely People, Yet Deeply Here**

Rare and sparse, the sentients—**the platform-makers**—look nothing like us, and everything. They might be mistaken for twisted trunks themselves, until one moves: **Flesh and fiber braided**, broad-shouldered, their substance part vascular wood, part sinewy flesh. They move with intent, wielding tools shaped of stone and copper-root, hands thick-skinned and fingerless—a palm split into three ridged pads, suited to wrap and twist immense vines.

Their faces are sculptural, unreadable but for the glint of concentration—no lips, but a complex slitwork of muscle and keratin, flexing to emit low, subvocal calls. Their skin shifts in hue with mood and season: **rust-red in heat, green-gold in rain.** They measure height in knots of bark, make homes among the high canopies, and in the emptiness, their voices are a kind of loneliness—low, musical, a sound that means *we are here*. They build not to conquer, but to endure and to *not be forgotten* by the plateau’s endless noise.

Children—if they have them—are carried in soft, basketlike growths along adults' backs, protected from the predator shrieks.

Their art is always a negotiation: **figurines carved from seed-shell, woven tapestries left in crooks of giant roots, ephemeral shrines that vanish in a season.** Every mark acknowledges that the world will reclaim it.

Tongue is not spoken but signed—hands and knee-flex, the way backs arch or eyes squeeze shut. Meaning is body, not air.

Occasionally, one stays behind by a water-pool, leaving etched copper plates—maps in looping, sinuous lines, not of territory, but of spore-blooms, seasonal storms, of all the patterns living things might make.

#### **V. The In-Betweens: Not Plant, Not Animal**

Scattered amid all—neither beast nor sentient, neither fungus nor tree—are the **Connectors**: enormous, slow-touching things, **almost invisible**, that thread between root and root, animal and vegetable. You feel them rather than see them—as a pulse, a warmth, a resonance in the noise. Sometimes, after storms, their shapes bulge from bark or ripple behind a water-pool: great plates of shifting color, flickering with unknown signals, tasting the world. Perhaps they are the mind of the place—the reason why such violence never annihilates into chaos.

They react to presence at a distance. When you come too close, the whole landscape seems to breathe around you, rootmats thickening underfoot, leaf-plates arching out of reach, a hush falling for a breath. They echo emptiness—reminding all who come here that even in a world made only of life, some part always yearns for what is not.

#### **VI. Echoes, Absences, Presences**

So it is these—the mesh-makers, the roarers, the whisperers, the predators, the composters, the rare, lonely architects, and the in-betweens—who truly **live** here. Not neatly, not comfortably, but in cycles upon cycles, in forms that change and challenge every division between animal, plant, fungus, and machine. Each is part of the ongoing creation and undoing, and each is shaped by the massed will of the living plateau.

Who lives here?  

Anyone, anything, that can bear the thunder and emptiness in equal measure. That can be part of an endless, echoing, overwhelming life, and still carve, if only for a heartbeat, the shape of themselves from the roar.
