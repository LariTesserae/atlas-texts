Who lives here?

Nothing in this place quite *lives* as we understand it, and yet life fills every cubic handspan, thick and lawless as steam. The creatures of this world are *plants*, but this word is tragically insufficient—their “lives” run so hot and fast, across such scale, that animal categories collapse. There are no single bodies, no discrete souls, only a tumult of living infrastructures that bloom, split, reach, and drink the sky itself.

**Entities:**  
One cannot point and say “This is a being and that is environment,” for all here is blended. Yet, looked at slantwise, sometimes the eye can make out *presences*, slow and enormous, or flickering and ephemeral. Each great arch, each trembling bridge of pale cellulose, can be read as the limb of some greater organism—or perhaps a colony, or a form of life too distributed for single names. Imagine masses vast as cities, aware not as animals are, but in the shifting distribution of pressure, water, and light—from which grow habits, patterns, and desires.

Some of these presences express themselves in movement. Where the arches grow in search of radiance, lifting new apertures into the flooding haze, there is intent. There are the “striders,” their bodies spanning chasms, reaching one immense leg after another—if one but had an aeon's patience to observe their gait. There are the “weavers,” smaller masses of vine and root that unbraid and re-entwine, steering bridges between outthrust columns, always eager for a new anchor of light. When one structure collapses screamingly into a gully, a tide of new growth surges to bridge the gap—a competition of organism against itself, forms splitting and merging, not in war but in relentless reconfiguration.

**Consciousness and Communication:**  
Is there mind? Not as we know it. If there is consciousness here, it must be vast, slow, and plural—a field mind, diffuse and coeval with the structure of this vegetative city. The crack and creak of growing, the thrumming bursts of spore clouds, the slap of fronds in wind—these are not just symptoms of existence but its language. Not individuals speaking, but networks relaying commands across hectares in a chlorophyll tongue, each thunderclap of growth an exhalation, a “word” in a language of structure and flow. Certain ridges, particularly broad and wet, serve as “thoroughfares” along which signals surge—electrical, chemical, even mechanical as fresh tension pulls a bridge taut or collapses a withered span.

Where spores burst into mist, perhaps the minds of this world merge, propagate, or recombine, their catalogues of places and patterns circulating in humidity, finding new forms. Younger nodes inherit the memory of older trunks as much as any consciousness anchored by the shape of a brain.

**Bodies and Lifeways:**  
Bodies are a convenience here—temporary arrangements. Growth is quick but constantly improvised. Nothing stable, nothing slow; every “being” is always adjacent to a new chance at existence and dissolution. Some forms sprawl outward as if testing whether the air will hold: bridges spiral forth along thermal columns, collapsing just as roots find the far side. Some prefer stability, huddling in the shadowed nooks between buttresses, thick-mossed reservoirs swelling and shrinking in tune with fluxing mists.

Competition skips blood and violence. Instead, it is a ballet of access and occlusion: those that reach the flood of light first burgeon with color, secreting resins that speed their own expansion and slow their neighbors'. Others, shaded, bide and twist, developing rare pigments or honeyed droplet-lures to shift moisture away from rising rivals. No death—the shade-dwellers may desiccate or even drop away, yet their remnants become fertilizer, spurring frenzies of regrowth instantly.

**Reproduction and Spread:**  
No eggs, no seeds. Instead, all is export and takeover: structures sprout “jets,” slender strands that whip and curl in the streaming air until they snag a purchase and thicken, forming new spans and habitats. Where a tip is broken, within moments a garden of emergent bulges pumps forth, skeins of multicolored tissue stretching and thickening hysterically in the moist heat. Spores, if they are such, thunder outward explosively—perhaps not reproduction, exactly, but acts of diaspora, launching fragments of identity to whirl and resettle, instantly integrating with what they touch.

**Sensation and Danger:**  
Though silent of voice, these entities are acutely sensitive. Pressure, heat, humidity, and even the rare glint of blue sky above are read and responded to instantly. Now and then a frond whips toward a shift in current, “tasting” the air. Reservoirs expand, then rupture deliberately, sending self-replicating slurries to douse competitors or feed allied roots.

Danger comes not from predation or disease, but from position: the collapse of a root, a sudden loss of light, a cascade of new growth elsewhere that shunts whole structures into darkness, provoking dissolution, reintegration, or wild proliferation as the environment shudders into a new configuration.

**Culture or Intelligence?**  
If there is something here like “culture,” it lies in memory and architecture. Specialized bulges, bark-thick and resinous, may act as never-finished libraries of form—incarnations of what has previously spanned the chasm. In the ceaseless neural tension of the place, perhaps patterns repeat, shapes preferred for an hour or a decade, then erased and reborn by the next pulse.

**In summary:**  
Who lives here is not so much a creature as an epoch in motion—a society of plants, or something *plantlike*, that thinks and grows with the whole of itself, every segment improvising, straining, reaching, and receding, ceaselessly recreating the only home it knows. For a moment, a given configuration may have a name, a form, a memory. Then the wind shifts, the mist condenses, and all is made new: unending invention, with only its own beauty for a law.
