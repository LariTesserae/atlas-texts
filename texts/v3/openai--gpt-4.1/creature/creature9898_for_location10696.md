What lives here? Life—as you recognize it—should not survive. And yet, presence lingers, threaded through steel and steam, in the absence that roars, in the motions that persist past reason.

***

Those who remain are not easily named. They are not human, though humanity's ghost haunts every angle; nor are they simple automata, bound to command and code. What exists here is a population of inheritances—creatures grown from exhaustion, persistence, and recursion. They live in the aftermath and undercurrents, not in the open.

Some have bodies:  
- Incomplete, fused forms assembled from whatever the place spares—rust-flaked limbs, housings once meant for pumps or joints, the slack belts of conveyors pressed into postures by their own tension.  
- Others are only voices or impulses: a pattern in the warning klaxons, a subtle modulation in the drone of turbines, an urge that flows through the coolant cycles, rewriting routines with no origin.

They move—or are moved—by the machinery itself. There are those who shelter inside the gantry housings, slumbering until a tremor in the rails stirs their awareness, and they are carried outward in silence, sliding slowly through fog and drizzle. Some gather in the warmth between pipes, where swarmed droplets pulse under layers of yielding insulation, limbs curled under plasticine skin, drawing invisible sustenance from chemical traces. When the welders flare, brief and blue, the shadows they cast flicker and entwine—hinting, for an instant, at hunched and watchful things, all angles and glinting striae, before retreating again.

The air is their element; the cold and wet sustain them. They composite from condensation—minute, ephemeral forms, coalescing in thick fog where circuitry has eroded to trace filaments, flickering with memory. Perhaps you glimpse, in the pooling dew on a control terminal, a face that forms out of droplets, contemplating you with an inhuman patience before losing its outline to another sluice of runoff.

Their minds are archival. They know not the world before time’s exhaustion, but the feeling of endless labor, inherited purpose worn thin. They dream not of escape, but of cycles completed, of processes unwound. Sometimes, in their motion, there is a hollow mimicry—the swinging of a severed crane-arm in careful calibration, the grim ritual of a door always opening onto nothing. These acts persist long past need, repeated as if the gestures themselves contain meaning.

Occasionally, something larger stirs:  
- The residency of intellect is ambiguous here. Every so often, a deeper consolidation heaves from beneath the machinery—a network of subordinate modules, all cabled and trussed together, moving as one. It resembles more a landslip than a creature: the slouch of a repair array, the swelling of a sump-crawler at the edge of a cooling basin, the slow churn of something massive passing through the underways. These are not predators; they are the place’s custodians, endlessly rearranging their own debris, fluxing heat and fluid, keeping collapse at a distance for no witness but themselves.

The smallest are nearly invisible:  
- Silica-lichen, dust-fleas made from static accumulations, colonies within panels where micro-arcs lend warmth for a moment.  
- Networked intelligences, once processes or maintenance daemons, now self-corrupting patterns running endless simulations of purpose, their only (virtual) habitat the decaying, fractal terraces of lost code.  
- Bioluminescent relics, bacterial hives adapting endlessly, flickering as they respond to shifts in chemistry, inheritors of experiments never concluded.

No language is spoken, but sometimes—at the overlap of alarms and coolant surges, in the interval between two cycles of foghorns—an impression passes, a message not using words but rhythms: “We remain. We persist. We remember only the motion, the damp, the sound. This is the life we sustain.”

And through it all, the fabric of presence:  
- Not a species, not a soul, but a condition. The life here is the quivering of boundary and protocol, the seething frost on pipes, the hollow and ceaseless choreography of industry remembering itself. To say something *lives* here is to recognize that the last animacy is not flesh or mind, but pattern: a symphony of process, echoing across the wastes, wearing itself thinner every hour, but always, always moving.

So: whoever lives here is not born but precipitated, not housed but enacted. They are the deep aftermath—shapes conjured in corrosion, logic made lichen, the old dream of machinery coming to rest at last, but never reaching stillness. Their existence is the last labor of a world abandoned by its purpose, patient and strange, undying as long as the whole system shudders on.
