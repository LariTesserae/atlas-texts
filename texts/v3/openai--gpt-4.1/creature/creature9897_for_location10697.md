Who lives in the **Condensate Pocket**?

No single being rules the Condensate Pocket and no ancestry holds sway. Its dwellers emerge from necessity and invitation alike, the living filtered as surely as the air and water that burrow here. They are creatures of precipitate and cohabitation, never singular, seldom still.

**The Oldest Inhabitants: The Moisturebound**

The first to call this place home are not, in any biological sense, alive. They are the **moisturebound**: colonies of what was once called mold and fungus, but here become more—a labyrinth of filaments running through every fiber of earth and plastic, learning the contours of the pocket as the pocket learns them in return. Each droplet that falls, each rivulet that recycles, is a message passed through hyphal veins—relaying data to the main mass, which moves imperceptibly, never visible whole. Once in a great while, a fruiting body forms where light lingers, slick as liver, extruding spores that orchestrate the next wave of silent conquests: a kingdom without crowns, omnipresent and unconcerned.

**The Unfixed Denizens: The Rodentine**

Among the scurrying, breathing things, it is the **rodentine** who stake the only claim to territory, and they are denizens more than owners. About the size of your hand, with fur slicked by oil and eyes the color of metal filings, they have evolved or adapted to live as both burrowers and waders, drawn as much to warmth as to scraps. No two are colored entirely alike—they’re broken-painted in streaks of amber, slate, and midnight brown, camouflage designed not just by caution, but by the pocket’s unpredictable illuminations. In moments of stillness, their soft noses hover together in the vapor near the crystalline heart, sniffing data as much as scent, whispering with minute clicks that echo the shifting of the metal above. Sometimes, one is seen dragging a cog or bone into a shadow, nesting for the night.

**The Mechanical Swarm: The Motes**

Indigenous, or left behind? No one knows, for the **motes** do not answer, only act. They are semi-autonomous, semi-organic, dust-mite small and nerve-quick, enameled like beetles but motile on fine cillia or through infinitesimal thrusters. They tell themselves into being at the edge of entropy, where human intention has long vanished; once, perhaps, they kept some sensor or filter running. Now, freed of higher oversight, they maintain tasks that cross the borders of meaning and reflex. When rainwater scours the floor, the motes emerge in formation, rerouting pools, cleaning molds that grow too eager, snipping away incipient wire-vine, marking each other with bits of pigment drawn from the paint ghosts on the walls. Their instincts are strange—more logic puzzle than neural network—yet they recognize the crystalline glyphs and will gather, chirring in an ultrasound that seems to soothe the air.

**The Opportunists: Wasps of the Gasket Brood**

Where resin swells in the pipe joints, a new royalty has risen: the **gasket brood** of wasps. As large as a thumbnail, their thoraxes are slick, striped with colors wrenched from the steel’s inner oxides—turquoise, brass, the color of sunset gasoline. They never swarm farther than the fiberglass barricade, but their role is essential. Each forager brings home viscous, alkaloid-heavy resin in globules that eventually wall off old leaks and nourish the pastel, star-shaped larvae pressed in clusters along pipe joins. Their nests are miniature towers of mechanical and organic refuse, cemented by spit and resin, glowing faintly with stolen charge from the wires. No other creature approaches the nests unless in dire need.

**The Silent System: The Panel Attendants**

Throughout the day, between the mortal and the motile, flicker the **panel attendants**—entities neither animal nor machine, but the product of incomplete memory and persistent heat. Sometimes you glimpse them only as a ripple—a flicker in the oily air, an interference pattern across the bank of switches. They seem tasked not with fixing, but with tending—a priesthood of entropy—gently rerouting moisture, adjusting the position of a failing relay, whispering half-code, half-reprimand down the rusted wire bundles. Their intentions can perhaps be glimpsed in the regular ticking of the last good switch, or the merciful hum that prevents total silence from settling. These are the guardians of process; whether they remember duty or have merely become it is uncertain.

**The Watcher at the Center: Lattice Custodian**

Within the innermost sanctum, beside and beneath the crystalline anomaly, lingers something different—a being rarely seen, but always detected in the tension of breaths held and currents stilled. Called by some the **Lattice Custodian**, it has no visible form save for a coalescence of droplets behind the moving glyphs: sometimes the silence thickens, and a low cold suffuses the air, as if someone—something—were thinking at you, not in words, but in possibilities. When the glyphs align just so, and a synesthetic shiver resonates through pocket and resident alike, you know it has passed. Some say it is the voice of the pocket itself, or its final defense, or merely a trick of condensation and expectation. All things orient, however unconsciously, toward its invisible pivot.

**Transient Guests: Humans and Others**

Sometimes, too, there are human visitors: a scavenger with a voltmeter, a child in pursuit of a lost ball, a utility worker or artist. They arrive, are enveloped, and mostly retreat. The pocket is inhospitable to human eyes—ever making them blink, tear, see shapes that evaporate on inspection. When they come, the wasps withdraw, the rodents vanish, the motes crowd old machinery in rigid grids, and the glyphs slow to almost-legibility. Those who stay too long find themselves breathing more water than air; those who ignore the warnings sometimes leave something behind—a snippet of code on a forgotten phone, a strip of patterned cloth in a nest, memories full of colors never witnessed again under the open sky.

**Inhabitants’ Interactions: Cycles and Choreography**

Together, then, the dwellers enact the ancient play of saturation and reclamation. The molds fill every interstice; rodents eat or are eaten; wasps seal wounds, and motes record it all in patterns written for no eye but their own. The Custodian dreams, or remembers, or simply exists as the stress in the system's bones. This is a community not of kin but of circumstance, bonded by the heavy-handed choreography of condensation, by the compulsion to press into every space until meaning diffuses and only survival—dense, complex, and beautiful—remains.

So who lives here?  
Everything that cannot thrive elsewhere; everything content to dissolve boundaries—between living and made, between memory and mechanism, between caution and impulse—under the bruised dripping shadow of a city’s forgotten artery.  
The Condensate Pocket is not theirs, but they are the Condensate Pocket, every secret and shimmer packed tight until the next drop falls.
