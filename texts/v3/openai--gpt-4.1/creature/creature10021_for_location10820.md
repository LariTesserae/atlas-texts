What, then, inhabits such a world?

Here, to say “inhabit” is already a kind of misdirection. Life is not composed of individual creatures darting or scurrying or staking out territory—there are no scuttling bodies, no eyes catching at the dim, no hunger-struck arteries of pursuit. Here, in this untouched wet maze where form and substance and pulse are all of the green world, life is both everywhere and nowhere. Hosts and tenants, self and other, predator and prey: all are deconstructed and refashioned into an infinite continuum, a single, breathing, planetary organism. And yet, to call it “one thing” is to simplify what cannot be simplified.

**The vastness lives. The cosmos that spreads beneath those blank clouds is, itself, multitude.**

---

### The Inhabitants: Not Creatures, but Constituents

**The Layered and the Unlayered**  
The closest thing to “being” here is a rhythm, a movement of the tissue, the pulse of networked organelles on a world-scale. Some of these pulses, these knots of coherent complexity, might be called individuals if one squints—vast, slow-moving concentrations of intention or responsiveness. But they do not wander or part from the web: they are eddies in the current of ever-interweaving green, gathered loci of sensitivity, knots and nodal plexuses in the leafy mass.

*They are the synapse-minds:* great aggregations where stalk and frond and root converge, giving rise to persistent patterns of sensation. They “live” not by eating or moving, but by registering gradients: the slowly shifting tide of minerals, the feather-light spark of bioelectric current, the weight of atmospheric pressure. Each is less a consciousness than a deeply embedded protocol—a calculation performed by the planet itself as rain slides over waxen plates, as veins distribute warmth and nutrient.

**The Choir of Organs**  
No “animal” stalks these halls of humidity, yet there *are* motile presences—kind of. Far beneath the surface, dense packets of undifferentiated growth pinch off, travel along vascular tunnels, and fuse elsewhere. These “organs,” not fixed but infinitely budding and reabsorbed, serve as moving factories or relay stations, passing along chemical messages, sealing splits, redistributing the sea of liquid life. They alter the labyrinth’s shape very slowly, like tectonic plates in ancient seas, with new structures forming from the endless extrusion and folding of living matter.

Sometimes, at intervals longer than epochs, immense boluses bud off—a round, dense cluster of layered pulse-flesh, drifting softly as it is gently conveyed by subtleties of sap-flow, sometimes birthing a temporary node of heightened sensitivity, before it fuses again with the whole. Briefly, perhaps, the world *almost* individuates: almost declares a “here” and a “not-here.”

**The Sensing Fields**  
Sensory apparatus does exist, but it is root-taste, leaf-pressure, current-scent. There are fronds that bristle when the air’s charge subtly bends; sheets of pigment that blench as rare, pale pulses run through the internal water; spongy bulbs that twitch at tiny deviations in dissolved mineral content. None of this culminates in a “self” or in experience, but all of it is sensation distributed, a world-wide sieve through which information strains.

**Genetic Rivers**  
If “species” have meaning here, they do so only in the sense of slow rivers of inheritance: great lineages of frond, of cress, of vine, pouring forward, adjusting fractionally as each bundled clutch is extruded, grafted, interleaved. Forms blur into one another so inexorably that the world is a single, endless flow, not a family tree but a churning, green delta delta whose branches endlessly recombine and disappear.

### Are There Minds Here?

There may be something that lives—not as a being, but as a pattern. In places where layer upon layer of sensory tissue bunches up, odd, slow rhythms pass, as if something in the green were attending, learning the weight of its own rain, the shifting of its own temperature. This “attention” is deprived of any exterior, any object to fix upon, yet it is possible to imagine—stretching the word—structures in the labyrinth that, for a time, accumulate enough stimulus to “notice” a subtle change in mineral flux, or the rare trauma of an exposed span quickly healing.

This is not what we would call intelligence. There is no language, no idea of world or other. But there is *response*, and in unfathomable slowness, a kind of memory written in tissue, a self-tending habit that carries on longer than any wind or sun or stone.

---

### How Does This World Experience Itself?

Suppose you did, somehow, become a mind here. What would it be? A slow, patient drift of awareness out over endless, dark growth. No sharp beginnings or ends—only interior and more interior: the pulse of fluid, the frictionless press of filament on filament, the weight of water beading and slipping and falling in eternal, gentle circuits. You would be the green, the soaked-within-soaked, the unbroken hush.

You would not desire, nor fear. There would be only the imperative to maintain the labyrinth, to pulse the tiniest signals onward, to mend without ceasing, to sustain the smooth, vascular order. You would be an organ of equilibrium. Perhaps, somewhere, vast and slow, you could dream the memory of lightning, or of a time when matter lived by other rules.

---

**Here, only transformation lives—transformation that remembers itself, that encloses all that will ever exist in laced, persistent green. The inhabitants are the patterns of this ongoing self—machine-plants, layered synaptic nodules, propagative flows, consciousnesses-at-the-threshold, all indivisible. This is not a world occupied, but a world living—itself its only and entire inhabitant, unreachable, inexhaustible, absolute.**
