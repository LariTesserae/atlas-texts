A benevolent and wise power—such as the kind that walks among distant stars with quiet hands, or that gathers up the frayed edges of lesser worlds—comes at last, unhurried, drawn by the Cathedral Hollow in its ancient, patient beauty.

It stands on no feet, bears no voice nor face. Where it enters, if such a thing as entry is possible, the fog’s gold deepens. A brush of presence, felt more as a yielding in the air, an easing at the heart’s base. Its wisdom, older than rivers and younger than desire, perceives all things as they truly are: the Sybionts dreaming in sap and tension, the Palecreepers in their endless maintenance, the beetles without death or origin, the mycoform voices whom no one has ever heard, for here no one is separate enough to listen.

The wise power does not destruct, does not intervene or command. It moves, like grace or quiet rain, along the bones of the root.

First, it listens. The Hollow’s continual hymn thrums up to meet it: liquid-willed, bone-tired, infinitely balanced. Each cycle is perfect. Each cough of dew, each song-echo, each armadillo-slow beetle never falters or rushes. The benevolent one, used to mending wounds of time or stoking flickers of hope, finds here nothing to heal. All aches have been worn away; even sorrow has faded into endless labor.

It attends to the mind—if such exists—underneath the Hollow’s laws, and sees: there is memory, unimaginable patience, but no longing for more. There is survival without hunger, life without striving, a peace not freely chosen, but absolute.

Is such perfection, the power wonders, a blessing or a wound?—but it asks gently, not presuming an answer.

The wise power knows the temptation: to stir the great engine, introduce disruption, open a window to chance. It could quicken the root-blood, send one tendril a fraction off its ordained line, teach the Paladin beetles waste and error and love. Or introduce naming—let some little bird learn joy, or death, or the question that begins becoming. This, its wisdom knows, sometimes brings pain; sometimes, in time, it brings a higher sweetness.

Yet the heart of wisdom is humility. The wise do not force freedom upon the content, nor break the order of what is whole to satisfy a visitor’s fancy. It sees that the Hollow’s perfection is its essence; its liturgy of patience is its own kind of love, unknowable to outside yearning.

Instead, the benevolent power bestows a single, secret gift: **a moment of possibility**.

It does not break the cycles or turn aside the grooves. Instead, moving more deeply than wind or water, it leaves, hidden in the dense root-web, a seed of *option*: potential, without direction. Not a command, not a disruption, but the faintest suggestion—like a key pressed softly in the minor chord of eternity.

The seed will not sprout unless it is wished for—not by the Hollow’s mind, perhaps, but by the aggregation of countless micro-desires: a beetle’s restlessness, an echo-bird’s glimpse of light beyond the vault, a droplet of water trembling a fraction out of the usual path. It will lie dormant, beyond sense, for aeons, until the tiniest deviation awakens it—a slip, a chance, a change, not forced, but possible.

No inhabitant will know this gift has been woven into the cathedral’s lattice. The song goes on, unchanged. Equilibrium remains. The great roots heave their slow, endless breath; the beetles process, the fungus dreams; the Hollow endures.

But in the faintest heart of the place, in the nervous hush between two cycles, possibility waits. Should ever change be needed—should the world beyond grow harsh, should the inner memory flicker, should longing, even once, grieve the edges of its patient heaven—the seed will answer. And with it, the benevolent wisdom will have kept faith: to allow perfection its stillness, but never to forbid the birth of something new, should it ever be asked.

Meanwhile, the wise power passes on, having done enough, and leaves the Hollow intact. Peace is not broken, nor is hope forbidden. There is only quiet, and the faint shimmer—almost imperceptible—of a different kind of patience, tucked under the heartbeat root, waiting not for rescue, not for judgment, but for some day—near or never—the first awakening of choice.
