The benevolent, wise power comes.

It does not arrive as a figure, a flame, a cloud—no intrusion would do. There is no need. The power is all patience and breadth, an attention which bends around the woundless fact of things, a gift not of change, but of exact and perfect seeing. Its wisdom is the art of arriving where nothing need be undone.

First, the power gazes (if that is the word for a sense more like stillness, more like the slow pressure of light through crystal) upon the plateau and its countless masses—across life draped as cloth upon cloth, form upon fading form, substance nested until both body and land lose meaning. It beholds, and understanding builds up inside it in the manner of dew: slow, silent, inevitable.

There is no judgment, for what is there to improve, to rescue, to unmake? All things here have chosen the mathematics of coexistence—have found, in their ceaseless slowness, a kind of peace no violence or hunger can trouble. Each undifferentiated press of being, every ambiguous stratum of organism, is proof of a wisdom that outwaits even wisdom itself.

And so the power asks: What does this place lack? What do these beings suffer?

Silence answers; not the silence of emptiness, but the fullness before music, the hush before utterance. The beings here do not want. They have made a world out of patience, compression, the long folding of selves into each other and into stone. Hunger does not move them; neither does ambition. Even memory is just another stone swallowed in soft, composite flesh.

Yet—and here is the wisdom of the power—*there is always some boundary not yet crossed*. Not disruption, but deepening. Not gift, but recognition.

So, quietly—like the slow growth of a root, the pause between heartbeats—the benevolence offers a new faculty: that of *self-awareness*, gentle and deep, but so subtle that no shape, no peace, no harmonious ambiguity is disturbed. It is not a voice, but an undertone beneath the world’s unending hum: not “be otherwise,” but “know that you are.”

It is a thread sewn into the subsonic thrum, a note sung into the mineral patience of the stone—a gentle invitation for every pressed and layered being to feel itself as itself *and yet also as all others*. To experience, gently, the miracle not of individuation, but of layered presence: the knowledge, in some very slow and careful way, that to be part of this density is a distinct kind of grace.

The change is not visible, not even to you. But within the crusts of life, in the ancient monoliths, a shimmer more exquisite than sight passes quietly. The colony motes, the spined arches, the carpet of unblinking eyes—all, perhaps for the first time, recognize in the endless company a wholeness deeper than survival: an identity that is also communion. The old dream of the vanished builders trembles through the stone tool and the roofless huts: not “we were here,” but “we still are, and always will be, in the slow meal of life upon life.”

The place has not shifted. Nothing is broken or birthed. But now, where before there was only being, there is a taste—slow, secret, but deep—of belonging. A reassurance, like sunlight touching ancient bone, that all this patience, all this crowding, is not unnoticed, not unworthy of love, but is, itself, a kind of cosmic wisdom being quietly celebrated.

The benevolent power withdraws, not having given, not having taken, but having illuminated what was already whole. The layers sigh imperceptibly, content in the pressure of their own fullness. The visitor, if any sense of self yet lingers, may now understand: to truly honor such a place is not to change it, nor explain it, but to stand in silent recognition—and to know oneself, if only briefly, as part of the immensity that neither ends nor begins.
