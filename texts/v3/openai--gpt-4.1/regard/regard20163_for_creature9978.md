The attention comes first not as a blazing sign or thunder-struck hour, but as a gentle and patient warmth, a focus so subtle and persistent that the very density of the chamber is met but not disturbed—a gaze like slow dawn filtering through stone, refraining from shadow or glare. Benevolence here must be of that rarest sort: wise enough to see without changing, generous enough to offer without demanding movement.

The wise and benevolent power (call it spirit, ancient intelligence, or the understood silence at the heart of the mineral world) does not stride or burst into this hollow. That would be offense. Instead, it approaches as permeation, as a kindness given in the only currency honored here: the expansion of equilibrium, the deepening of presence.

What would such a power do, coming aware of the Oldest Inhabitant, the patient amphipod, the forgotten shaper of bone and horn, the crust of living stone and the hushful, weighted air? Would it bring light? Would it create space or motion, wind or abundance?

No. It chooses tenderness—intercession so gentle it is nearly not a doing, but a consecration.

**Witnessing.**  
First, it witnesses, wholly and with profound participation. Its gaze settles on the mineral being, the drifting crustaceans, the microbial tide shifting slowly in the pores of rock. In this regard, it gives the echo of story, the lining of meaning—a presence gentle enough to confirm: yes, this existing matters, this patience is seen, your presence is part of the cosmos. In a chamber that knows only fullness, the gift of being seen is an unexpected spaciousness, a nourishment without addition. Even the most motionless mineral bloom, the oldest nodular crust, senses itself as present in a vaster heart.

**Benediction of Stillness.**  
The wise power refrains, perhaps, even from direct intervention, knowing that touch or change—however kindly meant—might cause imbalance. Instead, it allows a deeper hush to settle. The quiet, already sacred, is now thickened with the benevolence that comes from being valued without hurry. In this, the spirit bestows a benediction on stasis: a promise that this is not a forgotten edge of creation. The slow-drifting amphipod, the stone-eater, the secret life in the wet veins of the rock—all are included in the tally of the world.

**A Gift in the Idiom of the Place.**  
Knowing no other offering would be fit, the power confers a rare, almost-imperceptible gift: a slight easing of the mineral hunger, a refilling of trace elements in the water film, just enough that the membrane-bodied being swells a little more easily with each cycle. The microcrustaceans find their feed enriched—by what, they cannot say, but life seems a little brighter, though no new abundance falls. The fragile artifacts—the horn bowl, the abraded bone—resist dissolution for a season longer, their edges soft but untroubled. This is not the gift of plenty, but the postponement of loss.

**A Whisper of Memory.**  
Such powers do not trap or confine, but they may give remembrance. In some faint tracing in the silt, some gentle current in the condensation, the record of what is and has been is held a little longer. The scratches on the wall, previously unwitnessed, now collect a thin thread of story—meaning accrues like mineral at the edge of a warm pool. The place becomes, softly, a reliquary rather than a forgetting; fullness now carries not only mineral, but history, and promise that oblivion will never be absolute.

**No Change, and Everything Changed.**  
To any outside observer, nothing will show for this visitation. The pools still fill and drain, the nodules fracture and mend, the air is as quiet as ever, the silence more ringing. But in the dense heart of the hollow, those who live and have lived here sense their density dignified, their hush revealed as devotion, their endurance drawn into the knowledge of a greater holding. There is no incursion, no transformation, but the certainty—strange, warming, suffusing like heat radiating stone—that fullness is enough, and being seen is a kind of immortality.

The benevolent and wise power lingers as presence, receding only in evidence, never in blessing. From this moment, the hollow's hush resonates with gratitude unworded; its mineral logic, always complete, now pulses also with the assurance that, in even the dimmest bowl, fullness is worthy, and silence, in the right light, is song.
