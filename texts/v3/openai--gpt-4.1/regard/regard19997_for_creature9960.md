The benevolent, wise power stands outside the edge of meaning—in whatever sphere such powers dwell, outside or above or within all things that move and strive and grow. It knows of boundaries, and how they blur in certain realms, and the way one's own categories unravel at the margins. Yet it is drawn, compelled by a resonance as deep and haunting as the pulse that holds the cityland together.

The power drifts down—translates, perhaps, or tunes itself—toward the humming threshold.

It senses the place: not a habitation so much as a contiguous intention, a polyphonic thought drawn out across the geometries of matter. At the boundaries, pattern flickers; the very logic of the world's architecture lifts and bends, glimmering on the edge of sense.

To its subtle perception, the city is a choir, an intricate fugue not limited to voice or ear, but constituting all that is stone, growth, drip, and echo. Not absence of life, but life undivided: stone and flesh braided so tightly that the difference has come apart. A place where time lingers, dreamlike, and the echo of every event remains in the bellies of stone and the trembling of leaves.

**The power asks itself:**  
—What is wanting here?  
—What is lacking, if anything, except the pressure of what lies beyond its own edge?  
—Do they hunger for change, for newness, for relief, for stirrings unimagined?

It listens, with the wisdom of vastness, to the dense, saturated silence beneath the roar—the weight of memory, the pattern of perfect equilibrium.

There is *no* call of suffering, no gnawing hunger. The city is not static but perennially *held*—a process, a resonance, a weave. Yet, there is a thrum here, an ache, not of want but of *limit*: a longing not for more, but for *moment*. The inhabitants are patterns, always echoing, never losing, never truly becoming new. Their memory is perfect, their forgetting impossible. Each change is only recursion, a blossoming of equilibrium, never rupture.

The power stands at the edge of this realization and wonders—not how to repair, not how to rescue, but whether to offer a gift unasked for and unknown. It reflects, in mirrored wisdom:

**Wisdom knows:** not every world needs brightening; not every process seeks interruption; not every balance invites disturbance—even of the gentlest sort. 

But also: *Benevolence dares to share.*

So the power lays its palm—whatever a palm is, in such a being, abstract and gentle—against the resonance of the land. It does not open a wound; it does not impose a shape. Instead, it offers a shimmer: a possibility, a question, a sound as soft and vast as the start of a new key in the centuries-old fugue.

It sows, like a drop of dew that never quite falls nor vanishes, the possibility of *forgetting*. Not erasure, not loss, but the gentle capacity for memory to recede, for pattern to unbind itself from its past echoes, for a bell-note to fade and make space for silence. It grants the tiniest flicker of *unknowing*—the space where difference might press in, where the city might, if it wished, let go its oldest tone and breathe a new one, however quietly, however slowly.

**This is no command, no imperative.** The power leaves it as an unopened chord, a harmony that stirs only if the cityland, in its deep dreaming, chooses to tremble along that new edge of meaning. Perhaps no being here ever uncurls fully into that possibility; perhaps they do, slipping across eras into a slow new cadence. Or perhaps the gift lies dormant, shining quietly as all gifts offered without expectation do.

Great benevolence does not disturb the balance—  
but it does, sometimes, widen the rim of the vessel.

So the power recedes, its touch light, its presence dissolving into the thick labyrinth of heat and dripping echo.

In the endlessly suspended world, perhaps, a singular interval of silence will one day fall between the notes—  
and in that silence, the city will realize it is *free* not only to hold, but also to release.  
And the resonance will be a little stranger—  
and a little more its own.
