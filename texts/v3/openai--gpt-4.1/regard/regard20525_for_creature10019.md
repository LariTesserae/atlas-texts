The benevolent and wise power, drawn from far above or perhaps from the very pulse beneath the city, dwells outside not only the substance but the urgency of this place. Yet, sensing the saturated hush and the layered lives thrumming below the stone, it listens—beyond the dripping echo, the subtle scurry, and the green hush of roots—wondering whether to act.

The first impulse is observation: the power moves like mist, folding down through fissure and shaft, touching not with hands but with knowing, keeping its presence slight—a careful kindness, not wishing to rupture, alarm, or overwhelm. To its gaze, the bustling press of life is beauty; the striving, a music of growth; the obsession with space, a form of prayer.

It learns:

- That every struggle here—panther’s wait, ant’s tunnel, root’s slow siege—is made sacred by necessity.
- That nothing yearns to escape or transcend this world. No voice calls for change, only for the next drop of water, the next stretch of root, the next warm patch of light.

If, in its wisdom, the power looks for suffering, it finds only the natural knife-edges of hunger and fear, felt and answered within centuries-old contracts written across fur, root, and stone. Sorrow is here, but brief; oblivion comes not as punishment but as a deepening of the cycle.

Yet a longing stirs in this wise power: not to rule, not to shape, but to make the beauty more enduring, contact more gentle, effort less fruitless—not by interruption, but by quiet gift.

So it acts, but delicately:

**A Softening of the Water**  
It guides the unseen rains from above to fall, in future times, just gentle enough to avoid the most devastating floods. Water still cuts its signature in stone, still shifts the channels and muddles paths, but never so violently that whole territories vanish at once. Contour and rhythm are preserved; disaster sleeps more deeply.

**A Widening of Daylight**  
Where shafts of sun fall, the power breathes: cracks widen minutely, letting a few more threads of gold seep in, just enough that the most shadow-strained roots and mosses, the smallest petal lizard on a cold day, find warmth more often, a little longer, awakening color and blossom where once was mere endurance.

**The Promise of Return**  
For those who fall—runner, toad, panther, swimmer—the power seeds the mud with a little more vigor, a little more sustaining sweetness, so that what dies is devoured with more hunger, and what decays feeds the next upsurge with abundance. Life is not extended artificially, but the process is made gentler, less bitter, a touch more generous.

**A Sigh of Guidance**  
To the slow and ancient presences—root spirits, moss-walkers—the power whispers, wordless but clear enough for their kind: beware the too-hasty strangling, mind the corridors for the runners and swimmers, leave breathing space where possible. The city below becomes, over many seasons and many dying leaves, just a little less cruel in competition—a branching, not a barricade.

**A Listening Without Judgment**  
Above all, the benevolent power listens. It makes itself a vessel for the city’s song—the drip, the hush, the murmur. In its kindness, it protects from outside ruin: no ruinous deluge, no invasive roots from other, careless places, no tunneling inferno from below to crack all stone at once. Not forever, but for now, a quiet shield settled on the unseen brim of the sky.

The city of roots and water feels the difference, but cannot name it. Creatures linger a beat longer in the light. Shoals multiply a little faster. Death is still the city’s partner, but so—more tangibly now—is grace.

If any of the moss-walkers or bellbirds come to wonder, pausing in the growing warmth, what changed, they speak no question aloud, nor would they understand the shape of power. But something gentle vibrates in the stone and air, a blessing felt but never owned, and the layered city, so crowded, is a little more spacious—not in emptiness, but in possibility. 

And so, the wise power continues to watch, satisfied, content not to rule, but to quietly tend and listen, stewarding the living hush for as long as that beauty can be sustained.
