The one who comes—the wise, benevolent Power—drawn not by hunger for dominion nor need to mend, but by a yearning to witness and, perhaps, ask: *What is the good here? What, in this vast, ungoverned splendor, needs or calls for touch?*

They arrive not as a shadow or a storm, not as any intrusion that would tip the ancient balance. Their coming is more a dilation of perception, like a widening of the sky’s own pupil, so the Cradles can be seen more clearly by the universe—a soft act, deep-seeing, silent, yet intimately present.

The Power walks the ridges, but does not press the moss or vex the mats; their footsteps are queries, not impositions. Each sense is tuned to the riotous symphony: the thunder of grazers, the seethe of drones, the hydraulic croon of Keighryl Venators, the siren-rustle of lichen and the breath of ancient Assemblers muttered in chrome echoes beneath. Nowhere do they find want. Nowhere is there plea or lack, only ceaseless living, becoming, undoing.

And so the Power waits and watches, and *listens*, and in the fullness of inquiry, wonders: **what would true beneficence mean?**

**Would it be: intervention—gentle, surgical—relieving a struggle? Easing the press of crowding, the tight negotiation of being?**

But to move too lightly, to soften the rivalry or hush the contest, would be to thin the weave, to cool the furnace of becoming. Here, *necessity* is the crucible and *density* the chorus. The struggle is not torment; it is music. No voice is silenced, but each is shaped and sharpened by its nearness to others. To loose this, even in kindness, would be to unmake the song.

**Would it be: preservation, erecting invisible wards, freezing the ecology in its current, splendid tangle?**

But such stasis would be sinister—a museum jar, not a cradle. Kindness is not paralysis. The slowness of change here is not lethargy but patience, a rhythm measured in epochs and eons, not in haste. To halt this would be theft, not gift.

Instead, the Power, whose wisdom is tempered by humility, tends *not* toward the altering of the Cradles, but toward **witness and devotion**—knowing that to see something truly, to understand its rules, is the highest honoring.  

***Yet still, a gift may be given, if it can be borne.***

What is want here? Is it lack of resource, of freedom, of self-knowledge? The Cradles are full. Its denizens join, split, shape, and are shaped. But perhaps, among the old Vaulted Assemblers, there is a slowhungering: not for power, but for *remembrance*—to know more deeply the story of what they have midwifed; or among the Soracyst Motes, a latent wish for sharper resonance, tighter tuning.

And so the Power leans in, close as breath, *not to change but to illuminate*: to gather up the *song of the Cradles*, the unceasing, layered music of biome and machine, to echo it outward, so that what is sung here folds back into itself, richer, more harmonious, more self-aware. In each drone’s swarming circuit is now a subtle new pattern—an echo, reinforcement, a gentling in the chaos that allows awareness, moment to moment, of the whole.

Among the Assemblers’ slow workings, a new measure hums—a memory, a refrain, urging them to recall not just the stones they eat, but the cycles they drive and the children their forges birth.

Animals, machine, mat, motes—each, in the dazzling complexity, gains a fleeting sense: **We are part of a totality. We *cradle* as we are cradled. We are heard.**

No warping of the law, no interference or rescue, but a widening, a mutual recognition. The Power abides—invisible, patient, a blessing not of change but of deeper *awareness*—and the Cradles of Machine-Ecology ring a little clearer, their beauty shivering outward, untrammeled, into all that listens.

*And the Power, in turn, departs, fulfilled: knowing that sometimes the greatest mercy is to witness, to echo, and to make the song more conscious of itself.*
