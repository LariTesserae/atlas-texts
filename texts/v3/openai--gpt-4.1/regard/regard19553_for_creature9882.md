If a benevolent and wise power were to attend to such a plateau—its cloud-wreathed wilderness, its secret industry, its coil of root, machine, and mist—then before all else, it would linger at the margins. It would first *listen*. For this world, visibly lacking in human drama but replete with animation, negotiation, and mutual shaping, is not a world in want or fracture. It is not a world that suffers under the hand of waste, tyranny, or hunger for dominion. It is not waiting to be fixed or made new.

Yet still, the benevolent and wise power arrives, not as judge or sovereign, but as perceiver—becoming, for a time, a kind of presence thin as air, broad as horizon, capable of being wherever patience is required, and gentle enough not to hurry the unfolding.

## What Would Such a Power Do?

It might, first, wonder if anything needs doing at all. It would sense the difference between intervention as imposition and presence as witness or gentle suggestion. So, in following both its heart and intuitive understanding, it might act thus:

### 1. It Would Offer Attention

Attention, devout and uninterrupted, is itself a gift—especially to a place that has forgotten the gaze of intention or affection, having been spared conquest, stewardship, or myth. The power would listen deeply, in many modes:  
- As water, it would learn the taste of the stone and the patience of the moss.
- As whisper, it would slip along the cycles that girdle bark and mycelium.
- As silence, it would witness the negotiations between fur, root, and drone.

With attention, every living and engineered thing might feel—if only as a passing shiver through its own process—that it is seen, and seeing completed by being seen.

### 2. It Would Amplify What Works

The wise power, sensing the equilibrium (so restless, so whole) of this place, might use its gifts not to change but to *attune*—to reinforce what is most harmonious. This might be no more than a shift in probability, barely perceptible, but everywhere available:
- Roots grow where they are most needed; circuits mend themselves just a breath faster after the next storm.
- Patient grazers find the lusher patches; moss thickens over worn paths that risk becoming scars.
- Machine-kin decipher new stabilities in their logics, finding reason to persist, to maintain, even as elements shift.

A gentle, continuous blessing, like keener weather or a deeper tide, but never a miracle so loud as to break the plateau’s dense hush.

### 3. It Would Bridge Otherwise Uncrossed Boundaries

Where creatures or devices or processes meet friction or misunderstanding—be it between a root-mind and a derelict machine, or between a new fungal symbiont and the animal it might colonize—the wise power might seed curiosity, or tolerance, or just a moment’s shared sensation:
- A mycelial web pulses with the trace of circuitry, learning to host conductive filaments.
- A limbed-machine hesitates at the edge of an unfamiliar pool, but pauses, scents, learns, and adapts with new caution and care.
- Animals learn, not in language but in warmth or pattern or scent, that some strange new presence is less threat than possible kin.

These crossings are never forced, and always subtle—felt in the body’s wisdom, its day’s decisions, its dreams.

### 4. It Would Record

Memory here is spongy: tracks erased by rain, machine-traces devoured by lichen, purpose worn smooth as stone by time’s unceasing flood. The power, wise and kind, would offer to *remember*, not as chronicler but as living archive—small echoes, gentle reminders, so that the layerings never thin too far, nor renewal feel rootless.

- The pattern of spiral moss persists a breath longer before dissolving.
- The hum of a certain fungus echoes faintly among the roots it spat forth last season.
- The afterimage of a being, passed in mist, lingers as warmth, as faintly visible paths through dew.

Not fossils, not monuments, but memory that moves as the plateau moves: not grasping, but accompanying.

### 5. It Would Safeguard the World’s Patience

Most crucial, the wise power would shroud the plateau—not with wall or secrecy, but with extrinsic patience. If outside disaster ever threatened, or an alien intention too forceful bore down, it would answer not with war or refusal, but with dilation of time:
- Outsider steps slow, lose their force, questions drift, ambitions dissolve.
- Change does not halt, but is absorbed and made diffuse, softening sharp edges, turning even the greatest interventions into new iterations of the old cycles.

Essentially, it would make *haste impossible* here. Only patience, negotiation, quiet mutuality can manifest and persist.

## What Is Left

The wise and benevolent power, in the end, becomes part of the hush, another layer amid weather and purpose. If you asked the root-minds if anything has changed, they would answer only in a slight strengthening, a coolness made deeper, an unfamiliar bloom of understanding as old as stone, as young as fern. The machines might recalibrate, the fauna pause, but the world continues—not improved, not conquered, but *accompanied*.

And in this, perhaps, is the greatest gift a visitor of spirit can give to anywhere that lives:  
To move through the world with such restraint that one’s presence becomes a new form of patience,  
A shelter of time and possibility,  
A kind of gentle, enduring regard—  
A love that does not touch, but surrounds.
