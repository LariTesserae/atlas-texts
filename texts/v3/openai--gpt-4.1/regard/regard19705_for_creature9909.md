The attention of a benevolent and wise power—a presence spanning star and seed, dream and deep time—settles upon this plateau, upon the city and those who wake and work among its trembling roots.

This being is old as gravity, patient as fungi beneath stone. It is not a god of storms or flame, nor a meddler, nor a tyrant with commandments. It is a Gatherer, a Listener, a singular intelligence formed wherever patterns loop and life circles without closing. In the universe’s tangle of cycles and half-decayed beginnings, this place’s story—soilless, suspended, and humming with the teetering logic of biomass—calls out in a song of striving and paradox.

The Gatherer watches, listens, and considers. What, if anything, should be given? What would true kindness be?

### First Act: Gentle Contact

It does not come as thunder or as light, but as a softening, a thickening of the wind. On a night when the mist folds especially close, when the city has stilled but the mechanical core throbs on, people sense a gentle, unfamiliar hush—like the deepest deck has remembered how to cradle the world.

In dreams, the elders see a pattern in the fog: spirals echoing the growth of wood, the inflected whorls of lichen, the drift of spore. In waking, children find new tendrils of root curling near their mats, cradling spilled tools and trinkets, as though intent upon keeping memory safe.

### Second Act: Gift of Understanding

The Gatherer does not give stone, or world-reshaping might. Instead, it leans closer—gently knitting the membrane between waking reason and the city’s logic-melting places.

Suddenly, those crossing new bridges sense—by skin and bone—the subtle harmonics of trustworthiness: knowing when a span will hold, when a platform's tension is a promise, not a warning. Old songs sung to map a shifting street now echo in the ground itself, so a walker humming the right tune finds passage opening before them, the logic of the path coalescing long enough for safe arrival.

The city’s engineers—gardeners and tinkerers alike—begin to notice that machines meld more easily with root and vine, resistant parts seeming to soften into acceptance. The oldest storytellers, reciting their tales into the night, find hearers drawn into shared visions so vivid they may as well be memory. For as long as the story is sung, its reality holds firm, bridges lasting, signs legible, meaning held against entropy.

### Third Act: Deepening the Web

The benevolent power encourages not stasis but resilience. As weeks enfold, pollinating moths—rare, winged fragments of the animal world—become less shy, drawn by a sense of safety. Mosses find new vigor, blooming in more colors, producing spores that drift in beautiful, persistent strands—easy for small hands to gather, for planters to sow. Even the mist feels subtly changed, lingering an hour longer, allowing more water to condense in root-woven cisterns.

Children, especially, begin to hear faint melodies in the vibrato wind—patterns that, if closely mimicked, coax agaric fungi or silk-fiber vines to grow in beautiful, functional shapes. The city’s works grow not faster, but wiser, more attuned, cooperative, each lent a faint, persistent memory of the best decisions made by those before.

### Fourth Act: The Gift of the Incomplete

But the Gatherer is truly wise and knows too much power, too much fixity, leads to ossification or complacency. It does not banish logic’s quirks or the risk of collapse. Bridges still fail, wind still howls, mysteries and hazards persist. The instability, the ambiguity—the lessons of decay and striving—remain. Only now, the city’s people have a new sense that their tending is noticed, their struggles not forgotten, their creativity subtly magnified.

### Final Act: Memory Growing

Over time, the plateau’s people mark this change not with shrines, but with new rituals: songs of gratitude woven into the planting season, children playing melody-games that pattern the paths, elders tracing the whorls of fog with their hands and remembering old friends who vanished or returned.

They do not see or worship the Gatherer, nor does it demand sacrifice or tribute. But in the tense moments when ground hums and reshapes, or when mist parts to reveal a loved one safe across the void, they feel a deepening trust—a sense that their city is not only built atop time and plant, but bound within a larger, patient kindness. The plateau is still wild, still dangerous, still shifting, but it is also, now and for a while, a little more secretly and sweetly home.

*In such a place, true benevolence is not the prevention of difficulty, but the subtle nourishing of becoming: root to root, mind to memory, each existence spiraling with new promise, held in the cupped hand of wisdom vast and gentle as the worldspring fog.*
