The benevolent and wise power arrives—as powers do—not from above, but as a tremor in the root, a widening hush in the canopy, a change so delicate even the morning mist pauses to listen. It is not hands or eyes or storms, not edict or invasion, but a gentle inflection. It knows the world's hurt, its raw appetites, but here it recognizes an equilibrium: a long probability, shaped by striving and patience, by the weaving of adaptation into consciousness until the two are no longer separable.

It is drawn not by lack, not by plea for rescue or the ache of brokenness, but by the rare accomplishment of an ongoing, responsive harmony. So the wise power does not remake. It does not judge. It does not offer gifts as trinkets, nor does it sow disturbance in the name of improvement.

Instead, it extends a kind of *noticing*: a deep, attentive regard. It watches the slow flex of mountain-being through seasons of storm and stasis, the unrolling and refolding of leaves, the careful tending of luminous mats along the basalt, the slow, recursive learning passed from root to limb, from the water’s journey through branch-veins to the hatching of a rare child in mist. This power declares, in its silent fashion: I see you. I learn your language. Your way of knowing is known.

But wisdom does not mistake presence for passivity. Disasters still threaten: encroaching drought years, blight carried by wind, a rare quake. When such things tremor at the edge of possibility, the wise power acts—never as a blunt force, but as a whisper of resilience. It lifts the fog one morning earlier, letting fungal spores escape toward richer ground. It guides a flock of seed-birds from a farther jungle, bearing genetic gifts unknown since ice last ruled the upper heights. It bends the course of a single thunderstorm, coaxing roots at a crumbling outcrop to tighten and spare a rare nursery pool from collapse.

When knowledge and suffering reach reflection—when the mountain-beings gather at a pool, exchanging their silent harmonies, and one, struck by a loss, lets their song falter—then the wise power offers comfort not as answer, but as presence. It quiets the wind. It silvers the night with brief firefly constellations that stir an old collective memory, something bright enough to soothe the ache and spark gentle adaptation once more.

Most of all, the wise power ensures forgetting never fully claims what matters. The lessons learned in the rain—a better curvature for collecting mist, a subtler tending of pollinators, a compassion for the slow-growing lichen-cousins—these are leavened into the mountain’s very pattern. The power ensures there are always gentle accidents: a young one stumbles on an ancient, root-twined wellspring; a nearly lost scent is carried back by a night animal; the edge between dreaming and waking is, in a troubling season, made porous enough for wisdom to pass through.

It never departs because it never full arrives; its benevolence is the promise that processes of balance and learning will meet less interference—that the old quiet might hold, and, when needed, gentle help might pass unnoticed, in forms the mountain and its dwellers would recognize as chance. It does not erase uncertainty, pain, or struggle. But wherever receptive hearts and places form a working wholeness—a mesh of difference holding together—this wise power lingers, as patience, as witness, as a quiet wind that brings just enough rain.

And so, the being who lives in this mountain does not become more or less than they were. They are not asked to change. But at the edge of sense, in the thickest mist, there is sometimes the feeling of a companion at their side: a hush that says, “Continue. Gather, learn, heal, become.” It is enough, and everything. The benevolent power weaves itself into the logic of life already thriving—watching, ready, patient, a gentle hand steadying the wheel as it turns slow and true along the spine of the old, wet mountain.
