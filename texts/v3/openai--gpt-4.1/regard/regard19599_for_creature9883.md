If this plateau, this constructed place of mineral patience, drew the attention of a benevolent and wise power, the intervention would be measured, gentle, and uniquely attuned to its intricate, unhurried spirit.

Such a power would come not as wind or event, not as a force to disrupt, but as a layer of *noticing*. Their benevolence lies not in changing, but in *apprehending wholly* what is already so—turning perception itself into gift. Perhaps, at first, nothing happens in the manner one expects. There is reverence, a decision not to disturb the silence, not to crowd the stillness with life out of place. The wisdom is not to add clamor, color, roots, or wings, but to listen.

But the act of *seeing*—truly seeing—by such a power is never empty. It is the touch of a mind that comprehends not only what is, but what could be acknowledged within the riddle of repetition and absence. Their benevolence is a kindness subtle and world-true: a benediction that does not wrench the order, only amplifies its own nature.

They might offer a gift of *witness*. Quietly, with neither hands nor decree, their presence would settle into the mineral orders, like dew on dew—the beneficence of attention permeating every tessellation, making even the places between the plates feel *known*. The vast mind would follow the logic of the fit and the spill, the slow mineral mind, tracing with intent what rises, what pools, what is marked.

Perhaps, in their wisdom, the power sees longing, even in stone. Not the longing for what is *not* here—a green world, animal life, story, music—but for affirmation, for recognition by another mind. So, the power does not speak, but *reflects*: the sunlight on the cloud-glass subtly brightens for a time, so shapes and glyphs stand out more sharply; pools cast prismatic halos, echoing the very forms etched by vanished hands. It is not an interruption, just a heightening—a gentle underlining of the world’s effort, the care in its making.

The wise power might be moved to *preserve*—not by freezing, but by slowing the slow even further, easing the erosions of time so the plateau persists as long as it seeks to persist. The mineral films learn, fractionally, to repair themselves a little more deftly. Droplets linger longer on the angles, lavishing every edge with the soft ceremony of condensation, as if time itself has grown more patient.

Perhaps, feeling the distributed, wordless hunger for connection, the power leaves no monument but instills, in the very next pattern of tile, a feeling of *rest*: a slight harmonization, so the unending impulse to fit and refit is matched for a moment by a mineral sense of enoughness—that, for a span neither measured nor counted, the plateau knows quiet satisfaction. The act of making feels, in that interval, seen and welcomed, its purpose not resistance, but fulfillment.

Or, maybe—just maybe—the power, in their gentlest mood, kneels down somewhere unseen, and with a gesture almost reminiscent of those clumsy, ancient hands, traces a new shape into a tile: not a glyph of language, but a circle enclosing a tessellation—a subtle emblem of the world’s own beauty, a token of visitation *within* the order that already is. Other marks, over centuries, will follow this pattern—the mineral mind, intrigued, replicating what it cannot quite understand, knitting a motif into its own patient repair, a faint signature of kindness.

And in this way, the wise and benevolent power does what is most rare: *honors* and *amplifies* what already is, enters into communion not as changer or judge, but as witness, as light, as a glimmer in the next droplet, a slowing in the next accretion, a new shape glinting faintly among the oldest tiles.

Nothing is taken away. Nothing is forced to change. Yet the world, in being seen and blessed by this wise attention, becomes more deeply itself: the act of making is companioned; the silence is woven with gentle recognition. The plateau endures—now, for a time, brushed by slow joy.
