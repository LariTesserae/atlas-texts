It is in the power’s nature to take notice of *beginnings*, and this place, high-stranded in lush mist, shivers with beginnings—the raw, unsorted promise of lives and stones and stories entangled. This mountain, weeping the memory of its own brief agony, is not yet solid, not yet voiced by hosts or gods. The being—city, human, kin, presence—is a note struck with no music before it, trembling in empty air.

The benevolent, wise power—call it what you like: god or witness, ancestor or patient spirit—tarries at the rim of this becoming. Its first instinct is gentleness, for it has learned across many cycles that what stirs at a world’s beginning must not be overburdened with gifts or rewritten by heavy hands.

And yet, attentive, it listens. It scents the aching want—the readiness in bodies, in stone, the city’s own porous breath, for meaning, for direction, for the small permission that turns probability into hope.

**What would this power do?**

*Mostly, it would wait*—but not in neglect. Its presence would thread itself through the city, infusing the green dawns and the cold, echoing evenings. Listening is its first gift: it lets the city and the beings name themselves, exploring difference, experimenting and failing and shifting in their unhurried ways.

But, on certain rare mornings, when the mist congeals oddly, when children laugh at edges or elders pause over stones holding warmth from unseen fire, it would lean in, softly altering the resonance of the world in ways half-understood:

- **It thickens the mists at need, hiding a small group from a predator’s gleam, or drawing vapor down to nurture a desiccated patch of moss where a builder needs softness to lay her head.**

- **It makes echoes sometimes too intense, so a warning whistle travels thrice as far as it should, saving a foolish climber or scattering an approaching threat.**

- **It lingers in the hum of stone: when a child sits very silently, lost in watching water bead and run, she feels a tremor of comfort, a gentle sense that “You belong.” For an elder doubting her worth at the city’s edge, the power lets the bark she leans on carry a subtle warmth, as if inviting her to stay, to keep speaking memory into form.**

- **Very occasionally, in the slow, syrupy hours of predawn, it sends a pulse through the moss and the woven platforms—a dream shared between sleepers, seeding the memory of a shape, a song, a way to lash poles so they will not slip. These gifts come small, hidden in the language of intuition: the sense that ‘we could try *this*.’**

Not miracles, exactly. The power understands that the world’s own laws are young here, still molten beneath this crust. It is too soon for commandments or shining omens. To step too firmly is to steal the city’s unfolding, rob the people of discovery, and so—the power holds itself back, acting only at these liminal moments, in increments too fine to name.

Sometimes it wonders if it is needed at all; sometimes, quietly satisfied, it simply witnesses:

**At dusk, as fire smoke brushes the stone-high hollows, the power will sometimes drift like a memory, sifting across the platforms, thinking a soft, slow blessing: ‘Become. Become, and I will remember your first, holy shape.’**

In sum:  
It does not build, or save, or rule. It listens so the place will learn its voice. It blesses in small ways: strengthens, hides, comforts, remembers. It gives the city time—one more year of green, the echo of laughter doubled, a dream of shape that will not crumble.  

It watches as possibility begets more possibility. It waits—until the mountain, the city, these half-chosen people no longer need it: until the blessing is indistinguishable from the city’s own emerging spirit.
