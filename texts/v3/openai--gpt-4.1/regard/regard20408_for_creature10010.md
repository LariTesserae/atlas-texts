The benevolent and wise power — who notices all, from the lavish extravagance of ancient forests to the smallest, most liminal acts of birth and change — turns its gaze upon the hollow and its quick, claimless household. What might such a power do, beholding a place so new, so loud with process, so precarious beneath the leak of industry’s shadow?

First, it would *listen*:

It attends the tumult, the riot of small lives in motion. It hears each wet syllable: the fractured croak of frogs, the stinging hum of midges, the minute click of unseen jaws in moss. It listens not as a judge but as a witness, recognizing in the vibrating air the language of beginnings, the grammar of survival and hope.

It smells the clay, tasting the sharp tang where stone newly fractured meets air, and inhales the beat of rot quickening toward soil.

It *remembers* — for a wise power is also old — how every forest and vale began as such a rent, how every stability was born first of fracture and improvisation, of pioneers with no memory and every urgency.

What would it *do*?  
It would *hesitate*, at first, for wisdom knows the peril of interference. Intervention might unbalance, might slant the calculus that guides which moss persists, which beetle claims the pool.

Yet, benevolence is not paralysis, and wisdom is not absence.

So, it bends low, entering as mist, silent on the very tips of movement and intention. It does not undo industry, nor seal the hollow. It does not gift miraculous stability, for that would be a violence of its own. Instead:

**It blesses the interim.**

It whispers *"Time"* into quick cycles, slowing fear fractionally, lending the frogs a deeper, more measured croak, so their calls may carry farther, find companions, knit a chorus over days rather than moments. It grants the moss a touch more grip, rooting it a little stronger against washout, so that it may anchor decay and offer more refuge to springtails and mites — the builders of new earth.

It cools the worst acid of runoff, softening the edge of an oily drop with a tender diffusion: "Dissolve, be less a threat to these lives born of courage and accident." It turns some edges of industrial presence to dull murmur, so the raw edge of machine-noise is filtered, received as a vibration of warning, not a blast of extinction.

Stealthily — for the wise do not trumpet their gifts — it ferries a seed on the slipstream of its attention, guiding a grass’s tiny package into a crack too narrow for mud, just wide enough for the seed’s first root. It ensures the filament of a nascent fungus crosses the right gap, linking decay to new growth, weaving the underworld with a touch more density, a cushion of becoming.

Perhaps once — just once — it brings a slant of sunlight at the hour when spores lift from damp moss, so that wind, and therefore story, may carry onward: the promise of expansion, genetic rumors sent down-stream and ridgeward.

But most of all, it places a *pause* in the hollow, a dilation of possibility: a space for resilience to take hold, for quick lives to learn the shape of quick change, for pioneers to linger one cycle more, that they might establish more than accident — the start of memory, the ghost of tradition, the whisper of belonging.

Benevolence, here, is not rescue, and wisdom is not preservation of stasis. Rather, it is the stealthy forging of a *future* — not by supplanting struggle, but by ensuring that beginnings can echo, overlap, and persist. It is the urge of the world to give every fracture a fair chance at rooting, every riot of pioneers an interval to claim, resist, and begin again.

**The wise power departs, its touch invisible, yet felt.  
Later — months, years from now — some later traveler of moss or stone, animal or human, will trace the fullness that forms, the gradual stabilization, and not know why the hollow survived at the rim of machinery. They will sense only that, somehow, some small grace ran there first, as water runs:  
A gentling,  
A buffer,  
A cupped hand for beginnings,  
That let new worlds persist and become.**
