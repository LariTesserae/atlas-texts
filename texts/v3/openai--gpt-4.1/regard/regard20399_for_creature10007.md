And what does the benevolent and wise Power—a spirit of vast compass, ancient and attentive—do, drawn to this tight-cupped world where so few breathe and all are alert, pressed close by necessity and silence?

First, it enters not as conqueror or even as visitor, but as **listener**.

Hovering at the rim, the Power pauses—drawn by the glassy chime of water drops, the braided harmonies of bird-cry and crevice, the subtle pollen-fragrance of moss. It rests, for a span outside time, in the thick hush, and feels the *why* of this place: how the bowl’s emptiness charges what little lives within it, how hardship hones every being to resilience, how absence is itself a form of shelter.

**The Power’s wisdom** is not the wisdom of plenty or of warning or of transformation for its own sake. It is the wisdom of right measure.

It could, if it wished, sweep bounty into the bowl:
- summons the dense brambles of fruit, the explosion of flower and seed;
- open veins of rich ore, flood the pools with fish, charm colder winds to softer air.

But such abundance would unmake the place—for here, life’s pulse *is* the negotiation with constraint, the making of much from almost nothing. The bowl is a crucible of skill and adaptation: to gift it riches is not to help, but to erase.

**Instead, the Power’s gift is small and perfectly tuned.**

It listens—and makes itself felt as **the doubling of opportunity within limitation**.

- One night, a sheaf of rain comes not as a deluge, but as mist, veiling the stone and padding the moss, lingering longer than the pattern of years, so that the fingerlike blooming plants flourish slightly more, feeding the small burrowers with one extra bloom.
- One dawn, the keenest-prowling hunter finds, not emptiness, but a hidden cache of old beetle shells—enough to quiet its hunger without pressing extinction on its prey.
- A patch of moss, just above where the ancient habitation lies, finds its own spores more fertile—the green thickens subtly, offering more nest and feed to the birds, a softer landing for fallen fledglings.
- The water, where the hollow stone remembers old fire, grows a little warmer on long nights, as if some body nested there still; coals smolder in memory, inviting the passing hand or paw.

To the **people—if they remain, or if they merely return in spirit—** the Power’s touch reveals itself as intuition on a bitter day, the impulse to look twice in an overlooked crevice and find a necessary root, or the gift of sleep undisturbed when the winds outside batter dangerously close, or a sudden, small certainty: you belong, you endure, you are met here by something benevolent.

And to the **stone itself**, the spirit says nothing, but polishes—very slightly—the resonance of sound, so that the bowl keeps memory more easily, holding song and footstep and the hush of living breath forever longer. So when all creatures sleep, the music of their presence remains, like a slow vibration in the ancient granite.

**The Power does not change the rules.** It abides with them, makes chance a little kinder, suffering a little less absolute, and the ongoing negotiation with hardship not easier, but accompanied.

For it knows: mercy, here, is not abundance, but *right-sized grace*. Hope is not in the swelling of numbers but in the continuity of song; the greatest gift is that life, pressed but unbroken, still dares to echo—and so, in echo, to endure beyond itself.

And so, the wise and gentle Power withdraws—leaving behind not blessing as a thunderclap, but as **a thin gold thread woven through moss and memory**, almost unseen, except by those who persist in the sacred emptiness between what is vanished and what remains.
