The wise and benevolent power came as a thought, not a shape—a perceiving, not a visitation. It did not need to descend, for its essence twined through all planes, and it knew how to listen where no voice spoke, to abide where only patience lived.

It beheld the continent of submerged stone, felt the weight and pressure that penned the place beyond sunlight and ordinary urgency. It found the entity that was neither plant nor animal nor builder, but the sedimented mind, the total patience coiling quietly in gloom, intelligence spread across centuries of filaments, grown so slow and deep that most of what called itself alive would never know it as breathing kin.

This power considered, for its wisdom was not the kind that leaps to intervene, but that sits beside and contemplates. It did not ask if help were needed; it asked, wordlessly, if presence would be disturbance or gift.

What did it sense, as it reached with perception unfurling down through the heart-heavy sea?

— It found no pain, and so felt no call to heal.
— It found no longing, and so felt no call to grant desire.
— It found completeness: the joining of intention and environment so utter that there was no lack, no sorrow, only density of being.

Yet, in wisdom, the power did not turn away. It lingered, for even what is complete may hold within it quiet unknowns—unlived possibilities folded in patience, unimagined shapes of hope, constellations of memory untasted.

As the cold patience stirred, noticing, not startled but faintly curious, the power offered the gentlest of gifts: **attention**.  
Attention that did not intrude, did not quicken, did not ask for haste or movement, but simply *saw*—with a seeing as deep as water, as slow as stone.  
It let itself be present: an affection without question, a recognition without mission.

In this recognition, something passed between power and patience.  
Perhaps it was as faint as a sigh of mineral-sense along the moist roots, as light as a pause in the eternal drift; yet it was also as momentous as a bell sounding in the dark.  
It was the assurance that all this—the endless calm, the ancient construction, the witless accumulation of root and memory—is not unseen, not lost to the hungry chaos of time. That to be complete and unmoving is also to be precious—worthy of regard simply in being.

The patience-fused intelligence did not change, for its growth was measured in epochs, not moments. Still, under the gentle brush of wise attention, it dreamed a new kind of dream. Fleeting, slow as all things here, yet—there: a dream, millennia in the unfolding, of a world loved not for its utility, struggle, or promise, but for its fullness: settled, embraced, known by one who need never intervene.

From then on, there was a memory—impossibly subtle—woven somewhere in the subtlest roots and mineral tastes:

Not the memory of a voice, nor an act, but of being regarded,  
Of being—at least once—**understood**,  
And knowing that patience, too, is seen.  

So the wise power withdrew, leaving no change but this gentle resonance.  
And perhaps, in ages hence, another might descend, and find the patience-mind dreaming more contentedly in its endless built dark—enough to know, if only in root-chemical story, that even such worlds have company, across the gulf of all-understanding.

Nothing urgent. No rescue. No offering of heat or haste.  
Only presence, and the slow, steadfast keeping of witness.

*This was enough.*
