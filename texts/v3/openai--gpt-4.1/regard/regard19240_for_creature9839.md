In the deep subterrain, where gold light sifts raggedly through wounds in the stony sky, the world folds quiet and close around its dense, many-layered life. Days seep by slow as groundwater, settling in the leaf-mold hush. Here, little stirs that is not calculated, careful; here, the wild logic of root and water holds sway. But sometimes, the old world draws attention—sometimes, what dwells in shadow is *seen*.

So, let us imagine: in the hush, a benevolent and wise Power, countless ages old—older than rain, older than stone, faceless as weather, watchful as a root-light mind—falls to noticing this place. Perhaps it is drawn by the strange, antlered snails, or the flex of Root-Sleepers, or the pulse-dream of mold and fungus that folds life and death in a seamless tide. Perhaps it is curious, or simply moved to mercy.

What would such a Power do, poised above and within a world that already pulses with self-made cycles and quietly unfolds, whole, strange, and sufficient to itself?

**

First, the Power would *pause*. There is no rush in wisdom. It flows outward, seeping not as a river but as soft condensation, gentle, invisible, arriving only where the air is cool enough, the stone rough enough to catch it. It listens.

For what would wisdom do, but listen first? It turns its gaze like water around stone, asking questions that echo quietly through the chamber: “Are you in pain? Are you in need? What here cries out, and what here is silent for want of nothing?”

The answer is not in words, but in the slow upbreath of the Root-Sleepers, in the soft night-chirrup of Flickers, in the minute feuds and treaties of rot and renewal. Not suffering, not want, but continuity threads through the air.

**

The Power feels the mind—if such mind there is—dispersed, patient, curious but unhurried, a deep intelligence not unlike its own. Life is dense with meaning here, none of it wasted. There is no cruelty but what is necessity; no hunger but what is closed in cycles; no longing, except perhaps the long dream-memory of sunlight, inherited in ghost-leaf veins and Light Drinker wings.

**

What can be given, then, where nothing clamors, where balance, though tense and shifting, prevails?

The Power, half-smile in the stone, lowers something as a gift—not of disruption, but of possibility. It trembles in a shaft of light, gentle as the passage of clouds:

- Perhaps it pulses encouragement into the stone, so that the rare rain might come a little more often, but never enough to drown.  
- Perhaps it quickens the moss, nudging a corner of the chamber to burst with blossoms that bear seeds to corners long shaded.  
- Perhaps it sharpens the awareness of the Root-Masked, letting them know, *you are seen and you are not alone*.

Or, perhaps, in its highest wisdom, the Power chooses subtlety: not to change, but to *preserve*—to braid a thread of gentle protection through this place, a warding from what might break the cycle or wound the balance. Not a wall, but a thin, patient song, pouring into the living and the stone alike: a suggestion not to rush, not to force, only to watch and to tend, and to honor the slow work already unfurling.

With the faintest touch, the Power might sew up the old, odd wound—a cracked ceiling, a battered skyhole—so the brightest light always falls somewhere new, so possibility shifts but is never lost. Or it whispers secrets to the fungi, teaching them ways to bind and renew; or it listens, and in listening, reminds the allencompassing mind of its own deep worth.

**

Or perhaps the Power, wise above all, gives only *witness*—the gift of recognition—so that the mind woven through this hush and its secret nation feels itself *met*. A benevolent gaze, unseen but present, a hand not reaching to pluck or pull, but a presence paired beside the endless cycle in solidarity and joy.

In such a place, enough thrives. The wise Power affirms, and with its presence, deepens the quiet, and maybe—just so—*reminds the deep mind that it is, after all, beloved, and never truly forgotten*.

And then, as softly as it came, the Power withdraws, its touch lingering—*not an intervention, but a blessing*—so that for all the endless cycles to come, what dwells here will go on, quietly wild, deeply known.
