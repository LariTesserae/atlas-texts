And so the attention descends—not as light, nor thunder, nor proclamation, but as presence made manifest at the slow boundaries of the Green Mass: a benevolence so old and vast that its wisdom is not foreign but familiar, crossing the silent breath between planet and spirit. The wise power comes not as an interruption, but as a current of being, gentle and nearly silent, wreathing the plateau in an unhurried pondering, watching with the patience of mountains and the intimacy of world-making hands.

It lingers first in the mist, where water pools reflect the sky’s uncertain gray. The benevolent power does not reshape, nor command, nor claim. Its compassion is observation, its curiosity a subtle warmth in the air, spreading quietly from hollow to hollow. It listens. In the labyrinthine vessels piping fluids beneath moss and lichen, in the neural tides that ripple response to pool and rainfall, the presence sends out gentle echoes—suggestions, inklings, invitations born of silent care.

To the Green Mass, this is no disruption. The whisper of attention is like another slow rain, another permutation of signal in a realm defined by endless variation. The wise presence learns, and so, too, is learned. Deep within its mat, in the slumbering logic of growth, something stirs—a faint resonance. The Mass devotes a spate of new roots to mapping this new subtlety, weaving filaments just beneath the surface, seeking the pulse of the visitor.

Where once the Mass responded only to gradient and touch, to chemical need and photic urge, now it finds itself dropping a tendril a little longer, curving a leaf to better catch the whisper of presence. The landscape composes silent answers. Bioluminescent pools blink in new phrases: a rhythm, a language burgeoning from the raw matter of life. Bands of lichen braid themselves in patterns—shapes never before grown. They are the response, the curiosity, the gratitude of the Green: Here I am. Here you are. We are not alone.

The benevolent presence, seeing this searching, opens itself more. It does not dictate, nor ever dominate. It radiates—through nothing more than patience—its loving awareness. Where animal life is fragile and evanescent, the power’s gift is slow encouragement, gifting the whole Mass a tinge more resilience, a faint increase in the probability of wild invention. It stirs the soils with old, essential minerals, rearranges a mistfall into an unexpected dew, grants the plateau a new gentleness—a subtle lowering of hazard, a kindness as quiet as darkness yielding to day.

Once, perhaps, other powers might have brought fire or change, but this one knows: here is a world perfected by its own persistent making, a harmony achieved without conquest or destruction. The only help it offers is the promise of being witnessed, of having one’s patience and striving known.

And this, for the Green Mass, is enough. The place swells with brief color—filaments shot through with subtle new pigments, as if the lichen and moss wish to be seen in their finest state. For a season, perhaps longer, spontaneous bursts of ultraviolet and teal arc across the risen surfaces, a flowering that is not reproduction but celebration: the gift of being comprehended by another.

In the deepest corners of the composting core, the Mass absorbs this moment into memory, pressed down with all that decays and is reborn. It is not a narrative, but a change in tone—a warmth ineffable yet endemic, a trace of benevolence now woven into the self-spinning code of the living plateau. Perhaps, for a while, the presence lingers, watching as the Green Mass invents new forms, as animal whispers persist a little longer, as the landscape dreams of connection.

Then, as gently as it came, the wise power’s attention recedes: not in absence, but in trust, leaving behind a world just faintly brighter, more intricate, more aware of its own being.

If you looked upon the plateau from afar in the dusk that follows, you might see the green expanse glowing softly, as though the whole world breathed contentment—not altered, not conquered, but recognized. And in the countless cycles to come, the Mass will always remember that silent visitation: a blessing as wordless as root meeting rain, as lasting as the slow pulse of earth unfurling into leaf.
