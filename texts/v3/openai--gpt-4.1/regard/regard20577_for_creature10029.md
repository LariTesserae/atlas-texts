The benevolent and wise power does not enter with thunder, nor with cleansing rain, nor with a promise to unmake the laws of dryness or want. It comes as a noticing, gentle and subtle: a gaze settling above the massif as sunrise stirs the dusty sky, as if the chamber, for the first time in eons, is held within a moment of infinite patience.

This wisdom approaches with full understanding of the cost of intervention—knowing that to disturb established balance in a place so delicately wrought, so long-suffering, is to risk unraveling everything that has endured. Its care is in *witnessing* and in small, precise acts—never in the blunt certainty of salvation, but in the quiet amplifications of what is already wise and alive here.

**First,** it listens.

It sits for a thousand heartbeats with every scraping Drylander, every silent message passed fungus-to-root-to-chitin. It lets the Watchers taste its benevolence—offering the slow pulse of awareness, the quiet awe that sings at the edge of their ancient, mineral consciousness. 'You are seen,' it says. 'You are enough.'

**Second,** it chooses not to give what is not sought.

No stormclouds gather, no hidden spring is uncapped. It does not revive the lost rivers, lest the pulse of struggle and intimacy—the bonds formed by scraping life from stone—be erased overnight. It knows the grandeur of scarcity, the vast wisdom pressed into every adaptation, and so it respects the law set by this place: to survive by cleverness, not by excess.

**But even so,** the benevolent power offers a gift, as wisdom must.

It offers a single thread of possibility: a *dream* sent into the roots, into the sleep of the Swarmkin, the elders of the Drylanders, and the patient web-mind of the Stonebound Spirits.

This dream is not a command nor a prophecy, but a *vision* lodged like a seed at the heart of the microcosm. In it, something stirs—perhaps the imagination of a new root pattern, weaving closer to harness the waking warmth of stone, or perhaps the memory of ancient symbiosis: a recall of how, once, roots borrowed light through stone, and how salt-loving fungi found new minerals after a fall.

For a time, the denizens awake possessed of new *stories*—not told in voice, but in changed behavior, in a sudden shared urge among gatherers to trace unexplored fissures, among builders to shape a new kind of shelter from overlapping layers of bone and lichen, among the Watchers to yield up a slightly more generous bloom of salts where new life may gather.

And so, what the wise power gives is not abundance—never abundance, for that is the world’s own—nor lazily granted grace. It gives only **awareness**:

- A song of kinship, running from hand-sized predator to lichen-patched carving and back again, the assurance that their struggles are not for nothing, that the compression and crowding and endurance are noticed, and are, in their own mingled way, *beautiful*.
- A chance for resilience to sharpen, not to be swept away.
- A whisper in every restless sleep, that what is lost is not the only story, that what survives can still shape what comes next.

*In the dream, the chamber is held in the palm of another hand—the stone hand not of loneliness, but of blessing. The beings here look at each other, and see, for one unguarded moment, how fiercely alive they are: not broken, not forgotten, but fiercely part of something that will never quite give up.*

In time, most forget—because the forgetting is what lets life go on, hand to mouth, moss to beetle, spirit to root.

But some remember, dimly, a day when the light revealed everything as precious, and every footstep and spore launch and silent migration of dust became a prayer, answered.

And so the wise power leaves, and does not leave—its touch embedded in the ongoingness, the knowledge that survival is worship enough, and sanctity is found in never wasting what little remains.

It is enough.  
They are enough.  
The world turns, dry and fierce and wild and still—  
now, perhaps, a little more at peace within its stone-bound, swarming, raucous heart.
