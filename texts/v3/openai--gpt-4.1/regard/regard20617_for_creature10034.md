When the attention of a benevolent and wise power falls upon this city, its tangle of living stone and glistening bodies, its perpetual present, that gaze is unlike lightning or rain or wind. It is not a shock, nor a blessing as mortals devise, for here there are neither prayers nor invocations; the city vibrates only in itself. And so the power must arrive in subtlety, with a touch as light as humidity, as inevitable and unforced as condensation forming drop by drop.

First, attentiveness:  
The power bends low to peer—not through the misted air, but through the strata of presence, through the intertwining of stone and creature, water and breath. To a lesser will, such a place might seem unfinished, frantic, even lost, but wisdom does not rush to judge what it does not understand. Patience is the first act: the power lingers, watching, a stillness at the very heart of the city’s ceaseless thrum.  
It observes how no single creature claims territory, how each one is but a pulse in a tide, pressed against limit and possibility, making city and self in the same sweep. It listens, long and deeply, until it can distinguish the myriad voices in the saturated chorus: the scrape, the press, the birth from fissure, the hush where stone and water merge.

Benevolence, then, is gentle:
The power does not bring sudden light nor clear away the damp veils. It knows there is safety in crowdedness; in this half-illumined twilight, fear is kept at bay by numbers and contact. Instead, the power breathes—a deeper, slower rhythm, almost inaudible amid the busy vibration, but settling between walls, saturating the stone with a persistent, golden warmth.  
This warmth is not harsh or searing; it is not sunlight’s demand for growth, but a warmth like memory, subtle, nonviolent, suffusing the mineral richness, coaxing from the split stone a promise that nothing truly erases. It is a whisper neither to hasten nor to halt, but to let being deepen—a benevolent patience that ensures the city will not be undone by its own density, nor by the pressure of its urgent arrivals.

And seeing into the creatures’ substance, the wise power offers:
Not transformation, nor law, nor hierarchy, for these are not needed in such a beginning-world. No, it gives instead a slow clarity, a seed of mutuality, hovering at the edge of instinct. Some among the multitude—those who, in scraping by another, pause a beat before scuttling—will sense it. A slight accommodation: a sharing of a groove, a lingering touch, a realization that a hollow can be filled together, not in competition but in collective settling.  
This is not civilization as humans know: no governance, no monument, no archive. Yet it is a gift of resonance—a tiny, accumulating coherence in the city-song, a softness in the jostle, as though, now and then, two creatures might move not around but alongside each other, forming briefly a pair, a cluster, a harmony within the perpetual crowd.

The wise power does not fear that these beings lack story. It knows story is not always told in legend or memory, but can brew in chorus, in the ripple of presence upon presence. It cherishes the city’s unwavering present—the flow of arrival without end—and gently ensures that, should the moment come when one creature wishes more than to arrive and press, there will be space, a breath within the breath, for that longing to find an echo.

At last, perhaps, if you could listen through the stone, sense beneath the noise, you would feel a deepening balance, a subtle ease. Nothing obvious changes: the city endures, dense and humming, arrivals unending. But the stasis is not brittle; it grows patient, less precarious.  
Somewhere, a hollow stays unfilled for a single heartbeat longer, and the world does not unravel. Somewhere, the sound is not only pressure but pulse—a faint increase in spaciousness, the echo of benevolence become part of dusk and stone and perpetual beginning.

And so, the wise power abides:  
It does not fix, or overrule, or redeem, for there is no flaw, no lack, only potential held tenderly still. It watches, loves, waits. It leaves this city to its thick, pulsing now—but leaves within it a secret gift: that one day, when the city’s own thrum stumbles towards remembrance, or longing, or change, it will find all that is needed already seeded within the press of its first, unfinished, ever-beginning breath.
