If such a place—dry, blazing, full of phosphorus life and silence roaring with memory—were to be noticed by a benevolent and wise power, what would that power do? Would it intervene, change, or only witness?

Let it be imagined that this power is not blind, not indifferent, but neither arrogant nor intrusive—a being or consciousness that watches sentient and insentient alike, with patience shaped by uncounted ages. Its wisdom is not that of worldly might, but of tender understanding: the wisdom to discern the choices that matter and the threads best left unpulled.

### The Arrival: Watching Without Shadow

First, the power comes in perception, not in substance.  
No great wind, no new light intrudes—the chamber already knows every species of brilliance, every vibration of sound. The power arrives as a presence: a subtle lessening of the pressure in the air, or a brief clarity among the cacophony, not to be measured by those within but by the space itself, which has always known what is “other” even when it cannot contain it.

Here, intervention as a hammer would destroy irrecoverable subtlety. The benevolent power recognizes this. Instead, it listens.  
It listens down through the scalded light, through the spike and crash of refractions, the sibilant flex of mineral grass, the percussive scuttle of many-legged things—it listens for the intent locked in architecture, the echo of the incomplete wish that shaped the chamber’s emptiness.  
And it sees—the non-human figures moving precisely, the way their gesture is always a negotiation, a speaking to rules their bodies half-remember and half-create. It sees how memory persists in the pattern of stones and dust, and how all that lives here lives in deference to what has been made, and to the terms imposed by the city’s founding.

### Reflection: The Choice of Non-Interference

The deepest wisdom is to know where change would be mercy, and where it would be violation.  
When it considers, the power knows it could:  
- Call water, perhaps, from a distant aquifer, flood these chambers with gentle rain, coax new forms of softness and green to blanket stone with life unknown in ages.
- Silence the cacophony, temper the light, and bring the comfort of shadow—so familiar to its own origins, but so alien to this place.
- Wake the ghosts slumbering in the dust, draw out voices, recreate a crowded city thrumming not with absence but with being.

Yet each vision, as it ripens, is measured against the intent it finds. And it perceives the chamber’s will: not a desire to be changed, but to be *endured*—to be seen, with all its harshness and uncanny terms, and found beautiful in its stark fidelity to what is.

### Blessing: A Subtle Gift

Still, the power is not inert.  
It cannot pass by without acknowledgment; wisdom is nothing if it leaves no trace of regard. So it casts its blessing subtly:  

- **It deepens memory in the walls and paving, anchoring the pattern of every gentle touch, every patient repair, lightning the burden of erasure that eolian time would inflict.**
- **It refines the internal music of the chamber, teaching the stones to ring just slightly sweeter, to harmonize during ritual movements, lending a brief unity to the competing shrills and crashes.** For those who gather, their acts of arrangement now have a sharper, fuller echo: a resonance impossible to mistake for indifference.
- **To the Not-People, it grants a sharper intuition: when danger, in the form of new noise or catastrophic light, trembles toward arrival, a collective shiver warns them in time to retreat, to adapt, to survive.**  
- **It leaves a scentless signature—a patterning in crystalline filaments—that, if someday another power or intelligent life follows, will be read as an invitation:** Remember me. Look kindly. Understand why we made it so.

### The Blessing of Witness

Lastly, it gives the rarest gift:  
It witnesses.  
It marks, without fanfare, that here is a place where life, having shunned water, has grown into a new equilibrium of dry mineral and vibrant noise—a place where absence is not loss, but identity.  
To know that, somewhere in the web of existence, a thing so strange endures and is seen truly, is itself a mercy, a golden thread drawn without disruption.  
And so, the benevolent, wise power withdraws—not gone, but leaving a quiet uplift, a lightening, a promise that the strange and improbable have their place beneath the eye of the universe.  
Perhaps someday, in another age, its touch will be felt as hope, or hospitality, or as a song carried across impossible distances.

### Coda: The Chamber Remains

Within the chamber, nothing seems to change.  
Dust still settles.  
Sound still rises.  
The Not-Quite-People arrange and pause; the small chitinous ones crawl in their old patterns; the mineral plants lean, singing as before.

But at the unseen edge of sense, something breathes:  
*You are known. You were not invisible. How you endured is enough.*
