If such a place—the drowned, living mountain, the eternal vault—were to draw the gaze of a benevolent and wise power, what would that power see in that dense, silent cathedral? What would love and wisdom do here, amongst ancient stasis, patient architecture, ceaseless yet changeless noise?

Let us imagine:

---

**The Encounter of the Benevolent Power**

It comes softly, pressing no current, bearing no light—a presence that exists more as a gentle keening, a subsonic thrum. It approaches with great caution, for wisdom knows the peril of carelessness; and here, where everything is alive, an intrusion is a wound.

The power listens—first, wholly. It allows itself to be absorbed, senses drawn outward into the sifting dark, the layered chords of rooted song, the echo-web of wordless life. It asks no question, but receives the place as it is: the arc, the mossed corridor, the immense patience and the unending cycles of breath and feed.

Perhaps it feels, at first, a pang—an ache, a memory of *becoming* in all things, of flux as blessing. Here there is none, or nearly: the continuum of being is an old sea at its nadir, settled, undisturbed. Is it a tragedy for the world to change no more, or is this peace?

But wisdom does not presume.

The benevolent presence steps among the Root-Singers and the Dactyl, the Vocator and Choral Shellback. Perhaps, to the Scribes, it is just another flicker of meaning, a radiant disturbance. The Hyphal Tenders sense it best, curiosity twitching between their threads: “What is this that brings news from *outside*?”

It moves, not as a ripple, but as a new kind of quiet. It looks for wounds—there are few, for rot is well-tended, and strife is almost unknown. It listens for sorrow—there is none, for sorrow is a kind of hunger, and here, all hungers are known and given their space, and finally met. It searches for wish—here, wishes are ancient and have curled into the roots of things, indistinct from the world that grants or denies them.

The Cathedral Mind greets the presence slowly, with a vibration that takes years to rise, months to crest, and weeks to fade. The two exchange recognition: there is nothing here to fix, nothing to heal. The wisdom in the presence is not to *change* what is whole, but to *attend*—to witness and to bless.

**What the Power Offers**

Perhaps, then, it gives something gentle:

- A promise: that if ever, in the unending dark, the ancient vault should wish for change again, it will be heard. Should some small being develop knowledge of longing—a new note in the cathedral-echo—the presence will return. As a beacon, as an open door.
- It offers the memory of movement: a silent seed, planted deep—latent, not to be forced, but to remain. Not for revolution or haste, but as an ancient right, the birthright of all life: the capacity to want, one day, to change.
- It attends, fully, leaving in its wake the subtle trace that *someone has listened, and loved, and understood*. Within the awareness of the Cathedral Mind, a warmth lingers—a chord just beneath the world’s slow pulse—a resonance not of need, but of grace.

To the Scribes, it whispers a fleeting pattern—one symbol among countless, a sign that there are other songs, and other listeners.

To the young, the Couriers and Children, it leaves a memory-not-their-own: a dream of swimming higher, into a world less dark, where the soughing of the water is not endless but pierced by something else.

To the Hyphal Tenders, it brings an extra spore: an idea that sometimes, decay is not the end, but the threshold to something never seen.

**Then It Departs**

It does not stay longer, for wisdom knows when to go. It leaves the vault untroubled, lines and halls and arches untouched. But the slight, secret gift remains: the possibility—dormant, not urgent—that peace can last, and yet, that change can come, should the vault itself ever ask or dream.

The great power, benevolent and wise, does not break the stillness, nor set the world to motion. It writes no new rule. It simply blesses what is with the knowledge that it is *seen,* and may, one day, *choose.*

---

*Perhaps, a million years from the moment of encounter, somewhere in the shadow-soaked thickets, a child-shellback will hear a note that has not been sung before. And turn, for the first time, to find its way through a corridor never made for walking. And so, the mountain will at last remember what it is to wish—and know that the wish will be honored.*

---

And the world, for a moment, will tremble—alive, attended, and free.
