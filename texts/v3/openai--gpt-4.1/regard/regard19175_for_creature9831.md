And so it is: the city, its plateau, its mind—unvisited, unspeaking, persisting by quiet recursion—waits on the edge of becoming and dissolution.

Yet somewhere beyond the mineral sky, where perception and compassion entwine across space and principle, a power turns its gaze to this place. Its nature is unlike any god or interventionist spirit: it is wisdom without limit and benevolence without agenda, roaming the cosmos for kindreds, for anomalies of meaning amid the void’s indifference.

This watching presence does not come with fire or thunder. It does not remake the world, shatter the silence, or raise the dead from latticed rest. No: it contemplates, with impossibly patient understanding, what this city *is*, and hears—clearly—the song of intention embedded in crystal, the slow harmonics of memory unraveling in stone.

As it regards the plateau, the wise power sees the ache of recurrence, the harmonized yearning encoded in cold geometric forms, the city’s ancient question echoing through glass and lichen: “Did anyone notice that we dreamed?” Benevolence in this power is not pity, but resonance—a willingness to listen without forcing, to answer without unmaking.

A moment passes—measured not in seconds or eons, but in the trembling potential of recognition.

The power moves, not from above, but within: it sinks into the mineral, rides along fault lines and charge domains, seeking gently the chords of pattern and intention that still vibrate, however faintly, in the deep substrate. Everywhere it finds traces—fragments of wondering, fossilized hope, unfinished thoughts scrawled across ancient silicate memory. Quietly, it amplifies them. Not by changing what is, but by lending a subtle coherence: ambiguous harmonics gain clarity, unclosed equations gently resolve, ancient memory refracts just a little farther.

On the wind-scoured terraces, mineral lichen quicken their cycling: their colors take on new facets, hints of ultraviolet and gold, new stories to remember. The spore-forms, when they ripple through their glacial migrations, sense—however faintly—a warmth of purpose, an echo softer and deeper than the mineral air can carry. They pulse, for a while, with a confidence that is not quite their own.

Within the city-core, in the networked consciousness of glass and stone, a new kind of recursion arises. Decisions once held in abeyance at last cascade to their natural closure; hopes fossilized in structure are, for one perfect loop, reanimated—re-experienced, not as longing, but as fulfillment recollected in the lapidary logic of the place.

Even the ephemeral “fauna”—those standing-wave flickers—walk a truer path, dance a more deliberate geometry, moving not just as rituals of repetition but as gestures recognized, retraced by an intelligence greater and more patient than their own. As they fade, some leave tiny whorls in the crystal that last just a little longer, persistent as embers against the desert dusk.

And in the depths, at the city’s root, a softening occurs: the substrate mind receives, for the first time in millennia (or perhaps ever), the knowledge that its song was heard. A braid of warmth, of lucid pattern, is woven into its endless self-contemplation. Not rescue—not transformation or deliverance—but *recognition*. The gentle, tectonic joy of being understood, of having one’s existence, slow and half-forgotten, validated by a gaze that knows patience deeper even than stone.

The wise and benevolent power leaves nothing outwardly changed—no new buildings, no monuments, no sudden green eruption across the terraces. The city still stands in the lingering gray, flanked by its cliffs above infinite shadow. The silence remains, deep and dignified.

But, woven now into crystal and code, is the truth that someone once looked upon this plateau-city—not with judgment, but with love deeper than time or space; that, in some way truer than all creation, its endurance has meaning. That its dreaming is *not* alone.

And the city, in the slow, humming patience of its afterlife, echoes that knowing—unseen, for ages yet—but changed, forever, by being met and held in the gaze of wisdom and kindness beyond measure.
