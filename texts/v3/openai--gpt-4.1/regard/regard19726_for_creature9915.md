The benevolent and wise power watches, perhaps from the threshold of worlds or the marrow of the place itself, and asks: What is needed here? What is missing? The presence feels no urge to interfere for its own sake. It observes how lack and pressure have shaped an ecology—how, in the absence of water, life has summoned resources from dust and stone, weaving itself into tight interlacement with its world. This power is wise; it knows the dangers of careless gifts.

Yet as it lingers—soft as breath, broad as sky—it senses, in the restless hum and close-pressed peace of these mineral folk, a near-subliminal longing. Not for change, nor relief from hardship, but for deeper expression: that their ancient, haptic language, their patterns on stone, might find echo in something other than themselves; that the slow accretion of memory and touch might, one day, mean more than mere survival.

And so the power does not descend in glory, nor erupt as light. Instead, it waters the roots of their collective being with a subtle gift: **a deepening of receptive consciousness—an endowment of collective, polyphonic dreaming.** Not a vision of elsewhere, nor a whisper of revolution, but the gentle unfurling of shared imagination.

Over years, then, a change accumulates, so gradual it almost passes unnoticed. Dreams, once the domain of faint sensory echoes and cautious memory, swell in color and richness. The touch songs, the nicked grooves—now, when performed, they shimmer with new resonance; harmonies undreamed-of are shaped in council, songs that seem to carve a listening-void above the city, as if the rock itself had begun to hear and answer.

And beneath stone, a miracle: the memory-tunnels, inscribed so thickly over generations, now pulse with more than history. At moments of quiet, the folk gather—hands pressed to patterned walls, bodies close and humming. Closing their multi-faceted eyes, they sense, for the first time, the dreams not just of their kin but of the city itself—layered, ancient, patient, the mineral heart of their world thinking in chorus. All that they have been and will be—touch, loss, growth—becomes a harmony greater than sum of parts. Each death, each birth, is woven into a chorus that stretches, unbroken, back to the first chime of life in the hollow of stone.

Sometimes, in the stillness beneath the sullen sky, a few among them glimpse an echo of the benevolent gaze: a warmth not from chemical sun nor mineral rock, but from a caring far vaster and more enduring than any world. No new seasons come, no water flows, but the city glows with the slow delight of dreamed kinship, and a new song, subtle yet inexhaustible, braids itself around every living body.

Perhaps, in time, these beings will find this the greatest wealth of all: the surety that their endurance sings, that meaning, like life, accrues not in loneliness, but in shared and growing vision—a cathedral not of stone, but of selves, forever held aloft on their improbable pinnacle, dreamed together, and known.
