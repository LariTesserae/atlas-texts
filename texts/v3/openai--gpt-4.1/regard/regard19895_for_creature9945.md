The benevolent and wise power perceives this plateau from a distance not measured in miles or moments, but in the depth of seeking. It approaches not with the swiftness of a rescuer nor the hand of an architect, but with the attentive hush of a listener at the threshold of a story already decades into its telling. To intervene would be easy—the power could stir the sleeping process, heat the pools, unlock the engine’s gears, let rain fall or set the mosses multiplying, or, with a breath, invite dissolution, decay, the long dynamic of change. Yet this place hums at the edge of readiness: not incomplete, but *waiting*, the way a note can hang infinitely before resolving, or the way a heart can know contentment is not always in the finish but in suspension.

So the power walks—softly, so softly, through the plateau. It affirms the world by knowing it, not disturbing its necessary stasis with calls for action. It listens to the clang and murmur of the unseen pistons, the clamor carefully stifled. It presses its palm to the mosses, feeling the pulse of stored possibility—their signals, their weight, the codes for eruption all enciphered in light and water and cationic exchange. It sees the ferns peering sidelong, the Walkers testing the tension, the Mistwings drawing blueprints in the fugue of the clouds.

For many patient cycles, the wise power observes and attempts one gentle act: quiet blessing, not of change or command, but of permission. It whispers to the spirit of the plateau—*You have permission to be as you are. There is dignity in readiness, sanctity in the rehearsal, wisdom in the pause before the next world opens.* And it leaves in the fog the faintest promise: whenever you choose to become, there will be sunlight, and dark, and the sweet tumbling play of ordinary time. But there will always be honor in holding this moment, unrolled.

At the edge, before departure, the power leaves a mark—unseen, not a monument nor a spark, but a dilation in the rules of reality: for one instant every hundred years, on a morning thick with dew and machine-song, something here will truly change. Maybe a moss will bloom overnight, or the rain will fall, or a Walker will find itself at last hungry or curious. And then, just as softly, everything will knit back into stasis. No creature will remember what shifted, not consciously, but the logic will be subtly enriched. Readiness and potential will grow ever so slightly deeper—a memory of change, without regret for the stillness.

And the benevolent power leaves, caressing the world with memory and acceptance. It trusts the plateau, the engine, the spirits within: that when it is time, they will move. Or not. And each option, each possibility, remains sacred for as long as longing needs to wait.
