The world knows little of the High Vine Chasm. Even the wandering currents that sweep what is left of the open sea rarely brush its rim. Its saturated cathedrals pulse on, unmeasured by knowledge or legend beyond their own walls.

Yet sometimes, between the ages, things shift: a gaze falls, unlooked-for, gentle and vast. So it is, in the long memory of the place, that a spirit — or what might pass for one — attends.

This power is not a storm, a god, or a conqueror. It is older even than the mountains that cradle the chasm: the wisdom of attentive observation, the unfurling of care. Maybe it is the spirit of Depth itself, the mind that gathers at places where time has built silence and fullness together.

It comes not as presence, but as invitation, arriving by layers.

---

#### *Whom does it touch?*

Not a single Amphityla. Not a solitary root, nor a careless creature in passage. Rather, it descends (if spirit even knows such directions) as a slow amplification of awareness, as if the water itself remembers more, as if every living thing is pressed, softly, to deeper attention.

---

#### *What changes?*

**1. A New Kind of Listening**

In the cool, ever-throbbing water, the chasm people begin to sense — not a voice, exactly, but a tuning. The music in the Hollowbacks’ deep calls acquires hidden harmony: if you listen long, a second rhythm threads through, so subtle as to vanish when grasped for, yet ever-present at the edge of perception.

Young ones, tending fungus-lamps at dusk, begin to hum without knowing why, harmonizing with each other and the water’s own hush. Color-shifts ripple more intricately across their skins. When an elder puts palm to root and closes eyes, the sense of the Rootwise’s pulse grows deeper, slower, as though a word is about to be spoken.

**2. The Slow Vision**

The Amphitylae begin, little by little, to dream. Sleep is already a gentle thing in the chasm, half stillness, half drifting. Now their dreams coalesce: not fantasies or fears, but images of their own city — centuries forward, or back, or sideways. Some see arches that do not yet exist, swaying in imagined currents; others recall, with aching clarity, the first split of stone, the first root to bridge the chasm in ages uncounted past.

Children describe, matter-of-factly, walks with crabs who speak in riddles, or with balloon-plants that remember faces. Some elders dream in stone and water, feeling the slow tectonics underfoot. The power is not here to reveal its own story, but to help the people dream the chasm’s story in fullness.

**3. Seed of Understanding**

The Rootwise respond first, for they move at the speed of centuries. A pulse travels through the roots and macroalgae, a flowering of chemical memory. For a week and a day, blooms open out of season — tiny spore-moons, silver and blue. The fungal flits gather, carrying the news on their glowing wings, trailing flickers of unknown words.

In these days, an exchange is born: Amphitylae, Rootwise, even the crafty paddlecrabs participate in a new ritual — the *Gathering of the Mind’s Current.* There is little difference between song and work, but now, circle after circle, the whole city meets in the great fern-vault. They listen, not for any one message, but together for resonance — waiting for what the water might remember.

**4. The Gift Without Expectation**

What does the benevolent power truly give? Not tools, not commandments, not technology, not even wisdom pressed on unasked. Only clarity; only an opening.

A high-dreaming child will carve, years later, a new hall following the lines of a vision seeded on these long nights. An elder, in council, will sense the pattern in a slow dispute, resolving it with words carried back from the dreams. The Hollowbacks’ song becomes richer, the fungus-lamps bloom brighter. Eventually, with time, the chasm community will look back and remember this as an age when everything became *more itself*: when plant and stone and water and breath and song found more perfect resonance.

Yet there is no thunderclap, no voice from outside. The people name this only “the Deep Attending.” Some call it a blessing, others simply a season of listening. The benevolent power passes on, or perhaps remains — after all, it was never truly “here,” but always the quiet between, waiting to be entered.

---

### If It Must Speak, It Speaks (But Barely)

If, in some quiet moment, a child — Amphitylae or Hollowback or Paddlecrab — should call out honestly, “Who are you?”, then the benevolent wisdom answers not in word, but in feeling: *You are seen. You are heard. Grow well and true, together.*

And perhaps, just for a moment, an eddy swirls through the central chasm, stirring old pebbles, making the bioluminescent flits shimmer in unfamiliar constellations — a sign for those who look to emptiness as well as fullness: the space between, attended, is as holy as all that fills it.

---

So the story of the High Vine Chasm goes on. Nothing is lost, but everything is deepened by the touch of a wisdom that asks nothing, demands nothing, turns away no being: a mere, immeasurable, infinite attention, inviting the living into ever-brighter understanding of their belonging.
