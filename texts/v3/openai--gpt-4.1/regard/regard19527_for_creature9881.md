The benevolent and wise power came quietly.  
Not a figure, not a face, but a vast awareness—  
A kind listening, older than fault or root,  
That found its way down through cloud and seam,  
Following the voices of so many lives  
To the dim-lit basin rung with broken stone.

It knelt, in its way, on the edges of this place  
And waited—not to judge or probe  
Or stir what slept—  
But to feel the slow, endless motion  
Of the world here, like a pulse beneath layers of earth  
Kept in tempo by water’s distant plink.

First, it watched:  
The birds-not-birds crooking necks to rattle out their call;  
The pale-furred burrowers tracing lines in dust,  
Hammerstones worrying marrow from fossil-knuckled bone.  
It traced the vast language spelled in wariness:  
Twitch of grass, dark gleam of compound eye,  
Every living thing a notation  
In a music with no composer,  
But perfect in its repetition.

There was no suffering here beyond scarcity,  
No violence beyond survival,  
No sorrow that was not, too, the measure of thriving.  
Even want was shaped and known—never despair,  
Only the pause before another small, certain act.

The power listened,  
To the hum and flit and click,  
And pressed no gift, sent no flame nor rain,  
Uttered no word to unchain fortune or grief.

It knew to touch this order  
Would be to unravel—a mercy undesired,  
A blessing made curse in an unravelling world.  
It knew:  
To change what is changeless  
Is to wound the heart of time in this close place  
Where endurance stands for hope,  
And equilibrium is not a prison,  
But a home.

So the power sent no message,  
Did not lift or remake or urge forth.  
Instead, it *witnessed,* truly—  
And in the quiet recognition,  
A shimmer, gentle as sun through a rag of cloud,  
Passed among twisted reeds and stone.

A deeper peace pooled,  
A breath that loosened the small tension  
In every creature’s muscle—  
As if the world had been seen,  
And the act of seeing alone was gift enough.

In the wide sunken plain,  
Nothing changed, not in muscle, nor mind, nor bone.  
But after, those who dwelt there  
Dreamed, if they dreamed,  
Of a vast, warm hand  
Resting for a moment on the cool, misted stone—  
And felt, for a heartbeat, perfectly at ease,  
Being exactly what and where they were,  
The wisdom of the witness  
Keeping time with the drop  
And the pulse  
Of things unchanged,  
And still.
