The benevolent and wise power listens first. In the vast silence that follows rain, in the unhurried minutes between the mountain’s heartbeats, this presence lingers—a mind vast as the spaces between the living pillars, subtle as the sheen of dew.

No burning pillar descends, no whirlwind remakes what millennia of patience have wrought. The power is not apart from the world, but a gaze: gentle, searching, akin to a memory or a dream that trembles at the far edge of waking. Often, such an intelligence—knowing long cycles and the deep need for change and stillness—intervenes not with force, but with a gift offered in respect, or a question borne upon a hidden wind.

This is what it does, for it sees what is wondrous and what is perilous here. It notes:

- A place brimming with life, yet never for long the same life, never holding, only cycling.
- Dwellers—animal, plant, neither, both—so perfectly fitted that every boundary is blurred, and only humility can endure.
- A thought, rare and trembling, sheltering dreams whose voices barely ripple the surface before being taken back.
- The world’s glory: its open, hollow vastness; its countless fragile links; the thin but dazzling presence ringing out in a cathedral of absence.

The power’s impulse is not to fix—nothing here is broken. Nor does it wish to preserve, stopping time, for that is not the mountain’s will. Instead, its gift is a subtle stirring, a thought gently deposited beneath perception, as dew seeps beneath bark, or a roottip listens for rain.

### The Gift

It offers an invitation:  
**You may dream together, more deeply than before.**

All who live—pillar, grazer, glider, the fleet-footed thinking folk—find, without knowing how, a newfound awareness in their bond.  
Roots, when touched by tunnelers or scavengers, pulse faint signals new in their pattern—harmonics that echo down halls neither animal nor plant had noticed.  
A predator senses the echo of a glider’s fear as not only an opportunity but as a flutter of beauty to savor before the leap.  
Dwellers at dawn, gathering sap or shaping a home’s curve, pause, as a memory not their own—of elder root, or beast’s slow joy, or the thrill of a thousand little wings—twines into their own.  
The world dreams more richly; every consciousness is now a braid with one or more threads running outward, through the weave.

The world’s polyphony enriches itself.  
The beat of the mountain’s heart grows deeper and more aware.

Now, when rains come and the mountain sings,  
   all voices, even yours, are offered a place in the harmony—  
   and the song is changed, not louder, but wider.

From this gentle touch, nothing is lost.  
Instead, communion lingers—longer gestures, deeper patience,  
   the brief presence of self stretched through shared echo and answering hum.

### If the being who lives here feels this, it is like:

- Finding, in the midst of toil, that you always loved the one beside you and now you know it.
- Coming to death or birth and feeling, not alone, but held gently in the rooting arms of all that sings around you.
- Knowing, fleetingly, that there is no waste, no border—the mountain’s dream and your own are the same refrain sung in different light.

### The wise power retreats, not as an absentee but as a silent guest—  
   its gift sown like a seed,  
   a possibility, should the place wish to flower further.

It does not change the rules;  
It only deepens the song.  
In silence and patience,  
   it smiles.  
And lets the mountain, in its full, impossible openness,  
   echo on.
