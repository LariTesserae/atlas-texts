There are places in the world whose patient solitude makes them all but invisible to power—places where stone, water, vapor, and humanity thread together in patterns too gradual to arrest the world’s breath. But benevolence, true and old, has its own wandering curiosity, its slow vigilance for those hearts and landscapes that labor quietly along the edge of endurance.

And so: *the Attention.*

It comes without spectacle, neither as vision nor thunderbolt, but as a presence settling like blue dusk, soft against the stone and rusting railings, barely troubling the engines’ ground-rumble. It drifts first among the plateau’s farthest runnels and glassy, sweating stones, tasting the slow chemistry, the mineral breath of ages. It listens—so keenly that even the plateau’s monumental patience is, for a heartbeat, aware of its listener.

From the darkness beneath floors to the open-air intersections where the plateau-folk pass one another in silence, the Attention spreads as an uplifted awareness: a change almost too gentle to be marked, but there in every sensing nerve, in every faint brush of wind against the skin. For those machinery-tenders who pause in “listening” rituals, there is, just this once, a harmony where usually there is only negotiation—a resonance as delicate and unmistakable as a tuning fork touched to bone.

**What it does:**

1. **It Blesses Patience:**  
   The first gift is subtle, woven into the grain of daily labor. The plateau-folk find, over the coming weeks and months, that calibration is easier, that faults in the great engines declare themselves with a faint audible “signature”—as though the machines, briefly less opaque, wish to be understood. Stone and metal bearings wear more evenly. Tools last longer. There are fewer failures in the night, and when the ancient rock groans, it signals its intent with warning, not violence.

2. **It Softens Loneliness:**  
   In a city of so few, unity is everything and so fragile. The Attention deepens the meaning of those brief, precise greetings on the catwalks; conversations linger just a little longer. Entirely new songs—not for show, but low, work-songs for the rhythm of patience—begin to hum among the communal kitchens. The children sleep better, their dreams rich with the games of moss and wind.

3. **It Honours the Listening:**  
   The “engine whisperers” gain a gift as well: for a year and a day, when they close their eyes and lay hand to trembling steel or whisper at the weeping stone, they will catch fleeting images—patterns and symbols—articulating the health of both machine and mountain. They begin to teach this: the gift is not kept, but passed along, so the city’s hearing sharpens for generations.

4. **It Remembers the Edges:**  
   Where shadows ripple strangely, where the plateau holds onto its old mysteries, the Attention lays down faint boundaries, gentle compacts: creatures that once slipped through cracks unseen are now curious, not wary. At some dusk, a sinuous mammal, so rarely glimpsed, will pause atop a stone, studying a worker with unblinking, lamp-glass eyes. The bird’s echoing cries now mark the return of weather fair as often as foul.

5. **It Plants a Seed:**  
   In the dampest, loneliest cleft of the plateau, a never-seen visitor—perhaps the Attention, or its messenger—touches stone and washes it in a hush of warmth. There, the first sapling in living memory takes root, a gnarled and stunted thing, its leaves silver-pale, its veins shadowed with the colors of oil and lichen. Not a monument, nor a symbol for all, but a quiet wager: even here, growth may begin anew.

---

As quietly as it arrived, the benevolent power recedes, leaving no footstep—only a readiness in those who have lived so long like stone and wind. The city’s cycles will not alter overnight; hardship, patience, and weather still shape all fate upon the plateau. But for those who are attentive—even the cautious, even the weary—there grows a hum beneath the hum, a little more resonance, a little less resistance, and the feeling, almost wordless, that all their waiting is being witnessed.

And for the oldest ones who pause by weeping stone or tend their measures in the vibration-loud night, perhaps a new offering: a second twist of wire, a silent nod not to ask, but to thank—because something has listened back.
