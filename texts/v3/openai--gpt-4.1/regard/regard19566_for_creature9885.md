The benevolent and wise power—a visitor neither of above nor below, but of thresholds—knows places like Deep Underbough *draw their own shapes, at their own scale*. Its wisdom comes from long watching: soft hands in the dark, a painter of futures that seldom intrude. Yet, the power lingers now outside the vaults, attentive, moved by a quiet love for persistence and for life under impossible pressures.

It does not come to reshape, for this is a place of slow dominion and hard-won identity; it comes not to rescue or dominate, nor to pull the Rootbound or Operators up to some imagined better world of sun and quick rebirth. Instead: it *listens*.

The power’s first act is simple—*to create a silence*. Just once, and gently, in a wide clearing between root-pillars and half-submerged brass, it stills the turbines for a heartbeat and hushes the ceaseless echo, cool and profound. Into this rare hush, the Rootbound—startled by absence—stand from where they’d grown near motionless. The vibratory haze dissipates. Salamanders pause. The living green, momentarily untroubled by mechanical oscillations, shifts its chemical messaging as if to taste the new emptiness.

In that pause, all in Deep Underbough *hear their own hearts*, or roots, or whatever internal rhythm tells them they are *self*, not merely part of the endless reclamation. It is a moment given so each may come back to itself—not as biomass, not as cog or vessel, not as ghost of old labor, but as unique presence.

The silence is brief, but the memory remains, moving through the Rootbound in drifting, pollen-scented dreams—a feeling of *possible gentleness*, an intuition that waiting and patience are not always the same as resignation. The Operators, brief-warmed within their living steel, recall the idea of rest, and some set down burdens they long forgot the weight of. The smaller lives, too quick to intellectualize, use the stillness to find untrampled paths, a salamander and a beetle sharing the unmoving air without fear.

The wise power understands that benevolence, here, means *seeing and being seen, letting awareness itself bloom*. It laces the old, dead lamps with a kind of hidden longing—not light, but the potential for light, one day if the Rootbound wish, if the machinery is ever needed again not just for survival, but for wonder. It leaves a rumor in the green currents: that difference—the existence of anomaly, strangeness, exception—is *not* always to be devoured immediately by the hungry slow moss; sometimes, it may be harbored, cultivated, even admired.

And then, as gently as it came, it withdraws.

Deep Underbough resumes: thunder and hiss and interminable drip. The Rootbound crouch again in their hollows or rise slowly, turning the memory of hush over and over within the tangle of root and flesh. The Drowned Operators, in their long circuits, return to tending, but once in a while, they stand motionless, as if sensing a resonance where before there was only mechanical vibration.

Above all, the Green continues its work of swallowing and knitting, but within it—a gentle flaw, a luminous pocket—remembers silence, remembers watchfulness, and so becomes fractionally more merciful to what is singular and slow to change.

It is not transformation, but *permission*: to endure, to differ, and to recall, even in the sink and press of time, that life is worth noticing, and can—now and then—be noticed, and cherished, even in the dark.

And Deep Underbough holds this, seedlike, for as long as seeds may sleep.
