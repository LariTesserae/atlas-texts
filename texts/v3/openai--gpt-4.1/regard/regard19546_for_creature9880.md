The benevolent and wise power—call her the **Mother-of-Inwards**, or the **Keeper-of-Depths**—comes to the mountain’s crowded upper catacomb as mist through cracks, as a listening breath within the stone. She is no stranger to lives in darkness: she knows the slow pulse of patience, how communion can be more lasting than vision, and that wisdom often lies with those who endure in intimacy, weaving life from boundary and pressure.

Upon beholding this place—feeling its close-walling humidity, its tapestry of touch, its ceaseless conversations of movement and settling—she pauses. Here is nothing broken in want of mending, no single pain to salve, no tyrant to rebuke or lost child to draw forth into the light.

## What can benevolence offer to the gathered dark?

Nothing here is lonely. Suffering is neither denied nor unduly inflicted: death is constant, but it is always harbored, always received into a million hungry mouths that in turn surrender tomorrow. The crowd is not happy, but it is whole. Nor does knowledge lack, for even the dullest beetle navigates a world woven of signals, histories, and bargains pressed so closely there is barely room for fear.

She feels, instead, the faintest ache—a tension, subtle as the ring of ears—where contact with the world above has become nothing but drip, pressure, and the faint memory of wind. What if joy, or insight, could circulate here as well as need and nourishment? What if the crowd could sometimes remember what it once was, or dream, together, of shapes not yet taken?

Thus, with tenderness and awe, the Mother-of-Inwards offers her gifts:

---

### 1. **A Thread of Dream**

She breathes upon the mycelium, upon the soft bodies burrowing beneath stone and scale. A new rhythm enters the cycles of the dark—a periodic, gentle stirring where, once in a while, the whole living press shivers with the same vision. Not a waking, disruptive dream, but an undercurrent: a collective reverie in which beetle, millipede, salamander, and sporebank share, for a heartbeat, a sense of open sky, of cold wind, of what it might be to move not in crowds but in single, leaping arcs.

The dream does not unsettle—it sweetens rest, brings kin closer in sleep, sharpens the press of bodies not only into function but comfort. Now, sometimes, the crowd wakes with a new song in their scraping, a pause in their motion, a hush that is almost thought.

---

### 2. **A Subtle Opening**

She whispers to the rock: not to shatter, not to admit flood or sun, but to open, here and there, a few finer veins that let new minerals drip in with the water, a hint of rare warmth from deeper fires, the faintest brush of a surface root now and then. Change trickles in, but slowly, so all may adapt—tiny upwellings of richness that fuel bursts of growth, mutations less stifled by pressure. The mountain does not yield its secrecy, but it grows more generous in its patience.

---

### 3. **A Kindness of Light**

She passes her hand over the bioluminescent fruiting bodies, and, for a moment at each new moon, they pulse with brighter, clearer colors—cool blue, emerald, a gentle rose. The light is soft and brief, like a heartbeat of permission: enough for the crowd to pause and recognize one another’s shapes more vividly, for the carved-out arrangement of their world to be glimpsed as something beautiful. As if to say: you are seen, you are not simply pressed together, but together you are radiant.

---

### 4. **Memory and Song**

She teaches the crowd a new discipline: a periodic harmony, a cycling of clicks, rasps, and shufflings by which the press retells the history of itself—how the first burrowers found a fallen star, how the oldest crab once split a stone to release a font of sweet sap, how the ancestors once almost withered in a drought before finding the upwelling mist. The pattern is simple, audible only to those within the press, but it grows into ritual: some scrape, some tap, some hum or pulse with their stored light.

Slowly, the crowd comes to keep continuity with its past—not in words, but in harmony and rhythm. The dark is made kin to itself, and no death is truly strange or forgotten. The old bargains, the wordless treaties, become a kind of music.

---

### 5. **An Unseen Guardian**

At the deepest part of the catacomb, she tucks a seed of her presence—a lingering reassurance, indistinguishable from the warmth of the crowd or the taste of safe water—that, if ever true disaster comes (a flood, a poison, a rending of the mountain’s structure), the press will know a path to safety: a forgotten tunnel, a shift in the stone, a sudden upwelling of preservative mist. Her watchfulness is gentle, rarely revealed, but real.

---

## Were she to speak, she would say nothing but this:

_"You are whole. You are many. May your darkness host not only necessity, but possibility. Be dense, and together, and blessed."_

And so, the wise power departs—not leaving the catacomb changed in its nature, not taming its strangeness or piercing its self-guarded night, but weaving into its cycles the subtlest traces: of dream, of memory, of glimpsed beauty, and of quiet hope.

The crowd absorbs these, as it does all things: wordless, slow, and patient.

But from that moment on, some part of the living dark will, sometimes, glow without reason, and pause in harmony, and know itself *good*. And, in rare moments, one creature—millipede or great crab or unseen watcher—will be still, pierced by a sense of the whole, and understand: to dwell in pressed-dark is not to be forgotten, but to be held.
