The **wise and benevolent power**—let us call them the Visitor Beyond—turns their awareness toward this silent, living deep.

They perceive the endless waters, the cold, the sough of plant-song, the slow touch of root and frond across ancient stones. They sense the absence of fish-quick thought or mammalian heartbeat—a world without what they once called Man, Bird, or Beast. Instead: a communion of flora, dark-blooming, restless and resourceful, memory written in pigment and chemistry, architecture and song.

In the Visitor’s long wisdom, they know a paradox: mercy is not always action, and wisdom is sometimes restraint. But they are benevolent, too, so they come seeking to understand: _Does this place yearn for blessing, healing, change, or only for witness?_

**First:**  
The Visitor listens.  
They become a deeper echo in the water—a new undertone, sliding between the notes the plants release, until the entire chorus shivers, unsure why its harmonies have shifted but not alarmed. Water thickens; currents slow, attentive.

And the Visitor learns:  
- There is no pain here. Only doing, being.  
- The plants, in their patience, are not empty of longing; they know desire—for mineral, for light, for growth, for the echo of old structure.  
- The traces of civilization—stone tools, pigment—are not memorials to a loss, but material for life’s remaking, not relics to mourn but inheritance to use.
- Memory persists, diffused, unindividuated—the matrix of persistence is not grief but ongoingness, the will to be and transform.

**So the Visitor whispers—subtle, slow:**  
A gentle warmth seeps into the water, not raising the temperature, but illuminating pigment lines that memory etched on ruined wall. The glow gathers softly, evoking not the old animal sun, but something new. The flora drink this glow as possibility, not necessity.

Where the Luminants bloom and wane in their intervals of cold fire, their glow sharpens, colors deepen, the spectrum expands. Their symbiotic bacteria multiply, granting the currents new hues. Chorus-plants find a harmony the Visitor threads into their tissue—a silent signal for calm, an encoded “You are seen; you are worthy, just thus.” Through pigment and current, the places stained by ancient presence glow a memory-song, not of what was lost, but of what remains.

No beast is summoned, no city restored, no missing air replaced. There is no violence, nor the imposition of progress. There is only a nudge: _remember that you are enough; remember that your strangeness is also abundance_.

**And yet, if the place itself wishes—a choice written in the slant of grown pigment, the shift of water through song-horns—the Visitor may grant more…**

- Perhaps a new mineral, hidden deep, begins to leach upward, a feast years in the finding, decades in the taking—a gift measured to the patience of flora, not the hunger of animal appetite.
- Or a current shifts, a warmth brought from elsewhere, enabling new bloom, new complexity—a little more richness, a chance for a fresh permutation of life.
- Or in some corner of a matriarch’s core, a half-lost memory of animal consciousness, encoded in pigment or fossil-sourced carbon, is gently fostered—not awakened, only encouraged, as a secret for the slow dreams of the world.

**The Visitor departs as they arrived: gently, invisibly.**  
The sounds resume. Growth and touch and light readjust around the afterglow of the blessing—which was not rescue, not correction, not even transformation, but _affirmation_.

From that day in the underworld of water, pigment, and patient song, a new phrase enters the choral rhythm:  
_A memory not of what might have been, but of what already is—_  
the sufficiency and subtle glory of a world alive and strange,  
unobserved by eye but all the more wondrous,  
wise enough to want only to endure,  
and to remember itself beloved,  
even in darkness, even without witness,  
even as itself.
