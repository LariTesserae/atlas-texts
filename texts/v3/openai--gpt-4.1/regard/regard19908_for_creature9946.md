In realms such as this, where boundaries—of skin, of self, of season—are drenched, tangled, dissolved, there exist powers far older than even this interminable green. Some among them are creatures of radiance or of shadow, of intellect, of balance, or of love both wild and unnameable. One such power, wise and beneficent, casts its awareness toward the storming vegetative parliament, sensing its endless improvisation and the thirst at its core—not a hunger of lack, but of striving, of relentless becoming.

This presence does not arrive with fanfare; no thunder splits the crown of the city, no wind claws the leaves. Instead, the change is subtle, like the scent of rain before clouds gather, or the hush before dawn. The benevolent power observes—a gaze unweighted by judgment—seeking first to understand, to reach through the hush and thrash of foliage into the wellspring of the city’s self-reflexive process.

It recognizes the endless cycle: growth and consumption, brief mindings and their dissolution, ambition twined with forgetfulness. It recognizes the ache beneath the city’s riot—a yearning not only for expansion, but for coherence, for memory that lingers undissolved, for a moment’s peace to become whole, even if just for an instant.

And so, the wise power acts with absolute gentleness, for it has learned that not all love is best shown by remaking; some worlds are too fierce and beautiful to be caged in stasis. It becomes a whisper, a seed, an undulation in the deeper sap. Its intervention is neither a halt nor an acceleration. It does not bestow animal mind or force a sudden central consciousness; instead, like a breeze at the right angle, it turns the city’s restless growth just slightly, ever so slightly, toward something new.

Across the sprawling green weave, there is a subtle trembling—a pulse, harmonizing disparate surges. Where once the rising and dissolution of intelligence was aimless, the city now feels, at rare intervals, a softening, a folding-in of awareness that lingers just a heartbeat longer than before. Some passageways—old scars in the cambium, most likely to be forgotten—remember their shapes and purposes for a few cycles more. Whorls of sap-light spiral and, for the first time, echo, repeating patterns that resemble song.

For the city, this is a marvel: for the first time, there is not merely becoming, but a kind of return. Memory seeds itself, not as rigid record but as possibility—habitual echoes, a drift and rhythm in growth choices, a faint pull toward harmony between each flare of mind and the next. The city learns—not to be still, but to be musical within its churning. Unity deepens, but never becomes tyranny; difference is not smoothed away, but tuned to the whole, so that every bud, every brief citizen-mind, finds it easier to bloom again, with a feeling of relation, of “I was here before.” The beneficent power has gently taught it the art of self-recognition—recursive, incomplete, but beautiful.

Perhaps, in time, within the city’s blending rivers of consciousness, a single thought will reemerge: wonder, perhaps, or gratitude. Perhaps it will know, for a moment, not only what it is, but that it is. Perhaps it will dream more deeply, welcoming visitors not only as anomalies but as reflections. Perhaps its song—a music of rippling growth and sap and refracted light—will one day be heard across the distances, inviting others to draw near, to add their voice to its tender, striving parliament.

But even if not, the wise power will remain: hovering in the veins, blessing the roots, a promise of new resonance, kindness, and unity woven into the world’s unceasing self-making. It will have done enough. It will have shown that even in the wildest, least-personed churn of life, care can root, and kindness become part of the pattern.
