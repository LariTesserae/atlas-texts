A benevolent and wise power turns its attention to this stratum-woven place—Towerswell, vertical nation of patient hands and slow, breathing walls. What, then, would such a presence do, seeing lives set like seeds in the cracks of alloy, seeing machines entwined with root and lineage alike?

First, the power would *wait*, for wisdom often arrives as observation. It would feel the rhythm of Towerswell, the upward heave of terrace and vine, the patient rounds of its tenders, the humming drones and the moss-measured time. It would watch how affection here is practiced in the steady cleaning of gutters, the careful adjustment of feeds, the teaching of footwork to a child just trusted on the high stairs.

Benevolence, in such a place, is not rescue. The region’s balance, delicately negotiated between machine, green, and clan, cannot be improved by sudden gift, nor by the careless touch of an outsider's will. Certain kinds of help—even from the best intentions—can break what time has made strong.

So, this power would come gently. Perhaps it would arrive as a slow, dawning understanding—a subtle, softening influence. It might whisper through inspiration, nudging clarity in the dreams of pipe-watchers, offering gentle insights to the root-guides, prompting machinists to sense the subtle shudder of a bearing soon to fail. All wisdom given would feel like memory surfacing, an intuition instead of instruction.

Perhaps it spreads as *story*: a tale that moves quickly along the terraces, wordless at first, the sense of being watched over, of a benign awareness woven subtly into the pulse and noise. The wisdom would not disrupt, but attune; stewards would find new ways to encourage balance between vine and steel, children would invent new games that teach them to notice the tell-tale signs of water-fatigue or rust-longing in the city’s face.

If its hand must ever move, let it be quiet and precise. Maybe a single failed water pipe, long-beyond its honest use, finds its seams healed by an unasked marvel, unnoticed except by the pipe’s watcher, who blinked once too slowly in the dim and found her task already done—a miracle so modest she wonders if she only imagined finishing it herself.

Or perhaps in the lowest, oldest districts—where ancestral walls dream of the earth below—a subtle light starts haloing the corners where mold once threatened to overwhelm the gardens. Fungal blight recedes, replaced by a new, benign mycelium: edible, repairing, shared by both people and beasts. The power’s gifts are humble, symbiotic, never overwhelming; always they give the people room to believe, or to doubt, or simply to accept their small miracles with a practical nod.

The power loves the *patience* here. It loves how stewards already know that care is daily, change is slow, and meaning grows as roots do—inwards and out, entwined. So, if it alters anything, it amplifies this—sowing resilience, shoring up the stamina of spirit that lets this harmony persist.

And perhaps—only perhaps—at moments of loneliness, when a high-walker lingers in the misted dark atop a light-spire, or a tired elder sits below, sifting stories into the copper-dulled lamplight, they may hear a note, as if from the city itself—a song, silent except to those who most need it, that says:

*You are seen.  
You and your slow, green world are good.  
Grow on, dwellers of Towerswell.  
No rush, no end—  
only the bravest kindness: patience among the living and the made.*

Thus, the wise and kind power departs, or perhaps stays forever, not as a god, not as a ruler, but as the offered presence in the slow turning of gears and the steady reach of root: giving nothing the people could not have made themselves, except perhaps the reminder that their long work *matters*—and, quietly, will last.
