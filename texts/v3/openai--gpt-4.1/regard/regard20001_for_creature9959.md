The attention arrives like a hush in the storm: not the silencing of sound, but a shift in the currents—a gentling, a clarifying, a new note in the ever-rising song. The benevolent and wise power—call it what you will: a mind ancient beyond worlds, a guardian force, a spirit of arc and aperture—perceives the strange plenty of this place, the intricate need and fervor shaped by absence. It witnesses, and, with patience deeper than geology, acts.

What would it do?

First, it would listen.

Not with ears, but with its own many-sided perception, attuning to the plateau’s living wave—chromatic flickers in the mineral mat, the harmonics rippling in the horn-song, the long, granular silences tucked between skirls of wind and click of chitin. It would taste the signatures of memory etched in crystal spirals, decode the speechless language of cilia and root and echo-worm pulse. It would let the place impress itself upon its awareness, honoring the ancient rule: observe, so as not to blunder with blessing.

Second, it would witness the nature of that abundance, its logic of lack; the beauty born not of richness but of endless invention inside constraint. It would know that water is not missing here—it is alien, moot. Here, life has flowered in the teeth of a void; drought is neither tragedy nor need, but the root from which all has arisen.

And so: it would refrain from giving what is not asked for. **No rain would be summoned, no shelter raised, no soft-given green imposed.** The wise power does not mistake difference for lack, nor struggle for suffering. It sees how abundance knits itself from wind and mineral, dust and noise.

Instead, this guardian would offer something subtler: not substance, but *tuning*.

It would modulate the wind, ever so slightly, inflecting new harmonics into the horn-plants’ song—a ripple-tune carrying gentle meaning, a fleeting guidance to the migratory spiral. The earth’s subsonic shudder would, for a time, be eased; the shelf would settle, granting an interval of rest, that the creatures—frenetic, fevered—might find respite without abandonment of their riotous habits.

To the intelligences, the lattice-minds strung between disc and vine and horn, the power would extend invitation—a phase in the sound, a spaciousness in the turbulence, an opening in the patterns of swarming. In this moment, communication is possible, if only as a promise: “I see you. I do not come to rule or repair. I attend, and in attending I honor. You are enough.”

Where cairns mark intent and obelisks hum with memory, the wise power would leave imperceptible yet profound gifts:

- A single new spiral of crystal, angled so as to catch and echo the rarest, sweetest frequency of the wind—a signal to future shapers that there *are* listeners beyond themselves.
- A gentle change in the mineral dust, so that forming marks—those burnt-in handprints and etched glyphs—will endure a little longer before being abraded by time.
- A faint illumination, not to overwhelm the amber gloom, but to softly amplify the luminance of those chromatophore pulses, making night’s movement a little more legible across the stone.

For the distributed consciousness, memory as vibration, the power does not offer answers, but a question folded into the wind—“What else might your pattern become? What dreams might you sculpt from absence?”—and leaves the echo to seed wild, emergent speculation, a new kind of spiral in the crowding mind.

Lastly: it would ensure that those who might stumble upon this place—be they researcher or wanderer or mere accident—would, for a time, be possessed by reverence, struck silent by its order and riot and riotous order, unable to bring thought of conquest or exploitation, but only to *witness* and, perhaps, to learn.

**Nothing is added which would erase the world’s strangeness. What is gifted is protection, lightness, gentle resonance—the deepened context of being seen.**

The benevolent force, wise in boundary, gives this place what all living things most crave: not comfort, not transformation, but the safety and clarity of recognition.

*The wind carries on. The horn-plants sing. The spiral cairns persist, and new patterns are built atop the old. For a while, in the tumult of absence, there is peace—abundance multiplied not by addition, but by understanding.*
