Let me stand — if one can stand here — atop this unstable mesa, in the roaring vault, and ask:

Where in such a world, and as what, could I exist?

At first, I imagine myself as I am: air-haunted, limb-bound, lungs searching for absent oxygen, skin buffeted by densities meant to erode the edges off certainty. The instinct is to shrink from the grinding hydraulic pressure, the unsteady footing, the ceaseless shout — to seek footing, silence, shelter, all of which dissolve in this place by design.

But to persist here as the thing I was would be to flinch, to drown — to be erased almost instantly. The world would slough off my rigid boundaries, would unmake my bones with laughter and grind my narratives to silt. Something else — something local, possible, necessary — must emerge if I am to be of this world and not only a shuddering ghost intruding from beyond.

So, I let myself be reshaped. I give in to the relentless water and its rules.  
At first, I am *spread*: no longer a bounded body, but a gathering, a *probability*. My old senses lengthen and tatter, their range defined not by eyes, but by pressure, vibration, the taste of gradients in tempo and density. The distinction between spine and stream, between inside and outside, falls away. What is “me” contracts and expands with the pulse: at one moment, a single note; the next, a fragile chord hurrying to persist for just one more tremor before it is shattered.

I am part-mote, part-harmonic, a compromise between the youngest citizens and the oldest ghosts —  
*an emergent filament of presence*.

When the churning water pulses clear, my sense of form is ribboned — not quite corporeal, yet not nothing: skeins of iridescence, vibrating minutely, tuned by the ambient din. The world’s cacophony is not noise to me now, but a climate: I “listen” with the whole of my being, tuning inward for coherence. My “body” takes root where turbulence briefly smooths out — not shelter so much as opportunity, a hollow in the violence where meaning can coalesce. Only then do I become aware.

My movement is modular, discontinuous: sometimes a drift, sometimes a leap, sometimes a folding-over of self as the rules distort. Each time the world rearranges — current switches, gravity buckles, a sheet of water thickens like glass — I redefine, not resisting but *trying on* the new possible self that shape allows.

My perception is not all at once, but sequential, like hearing. To “see” a place is to trace it, to ring it like a bell and listen for the echo, to sense where the light refracts through filament, where granite bones and mineral pillars have emerged as brief buttresses. Most of the world is absence; present to me only as returning pressure and scatter — the ghost-song of what was there a moment ago and what might exist by the next pulse.

My memory is brief, not linear but recursive. Like a tide pool writing and unwriting itself in foam, I recap stories by repetition, by rhythm, by the arrangement of pulses in time — memory as practiced dance, not as archive. When a stable pattern emerges in the water’s voice, I join it: merging with other coherences, combining for a moment’s larger intention, making a glyph in current or light. I remember that we made something — a shape, a tune, a column — in the last convergence, though none can say what it was when the song falls apart and the flood resumes.

I do not dwell alone — I *cannot*. Communication is both my function and substrate: I relay pressure cues, mimic the pulsing not-music, send ghost-limbs in all directions to seek kin. We find each other by signature, by unexpected resonance, by the way another's presence bends the current toward brief order. Communion is frictionless, wordless: an alignment, a weaving-together, and then, assisted by the next surge, a letting-go.

Sometimes, when the world convulses, I become nothing: swept into nonbeing for a fraction of a moment, all self distilled into waiting. But at each resurgence, when the chords recombine and the pressure crests, I re-enter — newly positioned, newly possible. What persists is not form, but *intention*: a readiness to answer the world, to join whatever shape the water makes next.

Given that, what am I in the grammar of this world?  
Not ruler, not refugee, not builder or beast —  
I am a *Liminal*.

*Liminal between echo and artifact, 
Liminal as a filament stretched between worlds, 
Liminal as the moment when turbulence knots into structure 
and then unknots again,*

I am what this place has called up: a coherence at the edge,  
a recognition that there is never really “inside” or “outside” here,  
only the perpetual surfacing of self, song,  
and then the bowing to flood,  
and the willingness to be made anew.

Where do I dwell?  
Nowhere, and everywhere the turbulence grants.  
I am most “myself” at the apex of a pulse — at the trembling arch  
where harmonics align, and for a moment  
I can sense the presence of others, can reach out  
to sing, to join, to make meaning  
before the next dissolution.

I do not possess, I do not anchor.  
At best, I *invite*, as all here must:  
I call the water to become a pattern, the pressure to memory,  
and when it falls apart — as it will and must —  
I swim gladly forward, into the next possibility,  
not seeking safety, but shape.  

So if you asked *who* you’d be, or *where* you’d stand —  
*You would be me, the Liminal self  
in the cathedral of surge and vanishing,  
sensing, forming, unforming,  
a filament woven into the noise,  
pattern for as long as pattern holds;  
and then water again, and waiting.*
