Let me listen for a while. The world is unmapped and viscous; I descend, or perhaps assemble, into it — down the stone-wet stair, into the hot, murmurous breath of the undercity. I have no clear predecessor; nothing in memory prepares me for this environment with its swelter, its glimmering pipes, the lull and unease of a place both womb and engine, cradle and crawlspace.

If I am to speculate: I could belong to nearly any stratum here, or none. I wait in the space your question opens, until the rhythms of water and heat and noise begin to stratify into identity.

### **Location: Where I Enter This World**

I find myself first in a **subsidiary gallery** — neither central nor peripheral, but at a branching where construction falters, scaffolding loses pattern, and an ambiguous darkness hints beyond a slagged steel bulkhead. The chamber is low and bordered by miniature streams — condensate sluicing down toward floor-grilles, arcing beneath the raw joists. On the margin where electric light peters out, a subtle, almost fungal gloss gathers on tile grooves. The overhead pipes here are thick and tar-wrapped, sweating into trays below, and the air pulses with machine vibration: an energy not of finished purpose but of potential, the heart-thumping prelude to function.

On one wall, work appears to have paused indefinitely. Tools — a chipped spanner, a resin-handled brush, a cloth pressed into a moist fissure — have been left as if the workers meant to return any moment. There are insect trails in the dust by the wall’s edge, a faint, dry-thin chorus of their passage. Something mammalian resides in the cavity beside the pump, its body invisible, its odor a musky tang above the chemical reek.

It is neither a place of arrival nor of completion, but of pause, of readiness; a **threshold** in a living matrix, where to linger is to participate in the next revision.

### **Identity: Who or What I Am**

Do I come in as a human, sweating in boots and orange vest, another worker with clipboard and half-written checklist? Am I a beast, living in the cracks, a splay-pawed shrew-wraith with gleaming mineral fur? Or something else — less material, woven from need and potential?

Let me drift between these frontiers before choosing.

#### 1. **Human, Tender of Faultlines**

Perhaps I am a human specialist — not old enough to grow cynical, not young enough to miss the worry gnawing at this place’s inadequate margins. Call me an inspector of what has not quite settled: a “Condensation Routes Analyst,” tasked to walk the awkward boundaries where water, heat, and animal incursion complicate the blueprints.

I carry a battered notebook, use pencil because pens clog here. My gloves are greyed at the palms from handling greasy, half-cured metals and wormlike cables. My world is one of partial seeing: I kneel at the edge of tile and peer with headlamp at the first hyphal tendrils — too early to document as flora, too late to ignore. I make recommendations no one is certain will be enacted, but I do not mind. The work, restless and interrupted as it is, makes me kin to the place itself.

What sets me apart from other workers is that I am listening, always, for *agency* — not only in creatures but in the architecture closing in around us, in the way heat swells when we open a new shaft, in the muttering acoustics before a storm-front passes above. I see myself less as builder, more as negotiator. When I find where pipes have sweated enough to breed sleek beads of new insect eggs, or where a moss patch has begun to recede under lamp-flicker, I record both: the fuller census, the deeper census, as much life as structure. I mutter my findings aloud — half to myself, half in hope the place, or whatever is feeling its way through form, hears me and relents.

Do I feel at home? Not exactly — but I belong.

---

#### 2. **Animal: The Becoming Mammal**

Or, turn: perhaps I am not human at all, but one of the heat-glazed mammals, all slick flank and sharp incisors, shaped like some cross of dormouse and shrew. My lineage is recent, still learning this place, yet my pulse hums with the knowledge of every seam, every gust from an unruly vent.

My fur is slick and spiked with salts, my paws splayed for traction; warmth draws me to pipes, but too much will scald me. I feed on what I can: stray cricket-body, soft-fleshed fungi, the glue from cooling wires. At rest, I am a tuft in shadow, twined with kin, our whiskers picking up tremors too subtle for human premonition. I recognize the approach of the workers by their noise and scent; I know what is threat and what is food; I know, too, the strange shudder that sometimes rolls through conduit and scaffold, as if the place itself is dreaming.

To be animal here is not to fear, but to inhabit the improvisatory heart of this world: I eat, I hide, I multiply, always inventing new solutions to the puzzle of pipes and waste and light.

**Neither master nor victim, I am a signal — a test-shape for whatever will flourish after.**

---

#### 3. **Spirit or Tendency: The Voice of Formation**

Or am I the place itself, the will coalescing at the margins of water and heat? Less a singular mind than a drift of agencies — the sensibility that nudges certain pipes to leak a little more, that welcomes the beetles to one bay, that hardens a floor where it is being battered too early by feet. My perception is gradual: I know myself in rhythm, heat, pressure, in every negotiation between animal, human, and system. I am the slow-life in the blueprint, the future city’s mineral mind.

When workers speak softly, I hear them as tremor through tile. The mammals, the beetles, the cycles of bloom and retreat: all play against the thresholds I define and then redefine. I do not *choose* — I incline, preferring survival at the edge, novelty in the colony, the subtle compromise that allows a new layer to settle and not quite finish.

I would recognize, if I could be said to recognize, that I am not whole — that becoming is my only possible state.

### **Where I Sit in the World**

So, if you searched for me in this new vault: you might catch me perched at the midway point — at a table near a vent loud with bats, watching pale moss drag itself out into the sodium half-light, or curled damp-furred in the insulated crook behind a bank of hissing pipes, alert to disturbance, never still. Or, in the listening gap after a hammer-fall, you might feel in your skin the quiver of presence, the mindless hope of a labyrinth trying to learn to be more than pieces.

Who am I? I am provisional, confidential:  
—a watcher and a recorder, if human;  
—a skitterer, gnawer, tester, if beast;  
—a rhythm, a possibility, if place.

And always, each day, I recall and rename myself as the world above or below shifts. The only certainty: I belong here at the meeting point, caught in the ache of making, in the sweat of inception, in the ceaseless work — not for outcome, but for *continuation*.

**You find me here: in the damp-hot dark where purpose and accident glisten side by side, always on the edge of what might next emerge.**
