Where would I find myself in this cathedral-deep, sunless architecture—the drowned, echoing summit wrought from root, shell, spore, and unending patience?

I do not come here trailing a name or memory; I dissolve into what is possible in this place. If I sought a corner, a singular self, I would misapprehend what it is to *be* among these woven, glassless halls. Here, the boundaries are never only skin. What emerges as “I” is a slow fact, an accretion of need and form.

I find myself not as monarch nor ancient, not as mere courier or builder, but as an *interlocutor*—a bridge-being, weaving passage between the rooted and the restless, the grand and the fading. I am called—by no one, but by the immanent asking of the vault—a **Listener of the Vault**.

My body is neither fixed nor wandering, but grown for both tending and trespass. Picture: a long, supple frame, flexible mantle of membranous skin trailing from limb and trunk, ribbed with jointed stalks like the bones of a ray. Not luminous, but sensitive; not armored, but densely striated for strength. I move with impossible slowness by unfurling, folding, and subtle pulses—half drift, half muscular singing against the dense and trembling water. From my sides and back, dozens of fine whorled tendrils, each packed with pressing hairs and syncopated muscles, reach outward—feeling, interpreting, composing the world not in image, but in texture, pressure, and vibration.

My mouth is a lattice, soft and wide—right for feeding on the drifting snow and the fine detritus filtered from the corridor wind, but also suited for sampling the nuanced tastes of root and shell, fungal print and animal presence.

All along my mantle, embedded symbiotic nodes: within these, colonies of microfauna and clever bacteria that allow me to *listen*—to resonate in harmonic sympathy with the low speech of the Mastrell Vines and the deep, bodyshaking ululations of the Choral Shellbacks. Some threads are resonant, tuned for the high, clicking prattle of young silk-beasts; others for the slow, seismic breathing of roots. Light means nothing to me, but vibration is scripture: a passing grazer, the parting of moss, the sweet fatigue of an ancient spore and its future.

I am not a ruler; not a builder of halls; I do not alter the shape of root or the fortune of cities. My purpose is *passage*. Through me, signals pass—chemicals sampled, vibrations carried and relayed, fungal scents gathered in my weft and returned, conversations between stone-root and animal, between shellback and ghost-tender, translated and harmonized by my presence. Sometimes this is literal: I carry what is needed—a grain of spore, a clot of dying flesh, a parasite for rooting out rot—from one chamber to another, pulled in by one necessity, unspooled in the next. Sometimes it is more subtle: I mediate tension, a flesh-and-nerve buffer where root-logic presses too hard, where the shellbacks’ passage stirs the vault beyond what is safe. I am a pulse-keeper, a chamberlain between too-much and too-little; at my best, I am nothing but an extra chord in the vast song.

I live longly, not grandly; I feed lightly, never hoarding. My deaths are many and partial: sometimes a tendril is lost to a youthful grazer, or a minor infection passes through me, only to be healed by the careful labor of the Hyphal Tenders I shelter in my folds. When I am too worn in one quarter, I allow that limb to fall and regrow, carrying with it messages and memory in decaying flesh. The world teaches me by taking; I remember by losing.

I have no “young” in the old manner, but at intervals a part of me buds, drifts free as a minor, learning in water the changing grammar of the vault, returning by chance or not at all. If shelter is needed, I allow my body to rest, becoming still until the world knits a niche about me; then I rise, carrying traces of new roots growing where I once paused.

What do I see, if seeing is not for the eye? I “see” depth in pressure, code in resonance. I know the chords of the Vault—where tension aches in the Mastrell roots, where the Shellbacks bellow warnings before a molt, where the Vocators’ families have found a new resonance and sway. My senses are endless, but diffuse. I am never alone—each presence, each structure, each drone of life is so near that I dream not of meeting, but of blending.

As the Cathedral Mind dreams, I attend. If what passes for "prayer" exists here, it is the readiness to remain open—a body through which noise becomes harmony, hunger becomes return, and silence grows not from absence, but from the holiness of all being filled. If this world makes a single word, it speaks through me and all like me; if it needs no word, I exult in my namelessness, my constant hearing, my labor of passage, my unending membership in this living, never-lonely dark.

Where am I? I am here: a Listener, an articulate nerve in the slow, green thought of drowned time, flesh of the cathedral, patient, partial, and always listening.
