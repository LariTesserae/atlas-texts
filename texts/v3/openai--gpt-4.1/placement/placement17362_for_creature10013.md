I find myself here at the precise moment of inflection—that shuddering, bruised pause between one surge of root and the next, lungs and labyrinth both bright with agitation. I am not what I was when I crossed below—was there a crossing? I cannot say. With the first breath, something decanted me, peeled my mind of its familiar outlines, left only the sense of pressure and need. The Mat does not preserve identities; when the world is constant onset, bodies and selves are what growth can convince the moment to support.

**Who am I?**  
I am perhaps a Tumbler, if Tumblers can remember yearning. My skin is slick-veined, leaf-sharded, stippled with old spores and bright flares of resin, absurd with sensory filaments—frills for tasting absence, loops for grasping, studded orifices for siphoning dust and rumor and heat. My motion is compelled by gradients: wherever light wants, wherever the sugar-scent eddies, wherever there is the least resistance or the most call. I am small, by the measure of bough and Rootwise knot, but fleet—a thing designed for passage, not permanence. Still, I carry a stowaway spirit: a hunch, a half-formed calculus, a need to remember as much as to propel. Somewhere inside, in a pit beneath the leaf-packed guts, a surplus of longing pulses: a curiosity that isn’t quite the Mat’s, but isn’t wholly mine, either.

Or I am not a Tumbler—I am that which outlasts generations of Tumblers, a parasite on their endless churn, an echo caught in the resinous go-between: a process, not just a body. I eat the shed, scrape the borders, ride in forgotten scars of tissue. My shape is provisional—splay-eyed, internalizing routes and salt-maps, learning to thread the ways that are *no longer ways* through flesh-memory and subtle heat. I see not with eyes but with the fierce, frantic chemical trace left by Tumblers lost, by Air-Bitten passed overhead, by spore-animalcules dissolved and digested back into walls and trunks and roots.

**Where do I find myself?**  
I crawl—not on earth but across tensed meat, inside living loam and tissue, through gaps flexed open by Rootwise for reasons of their own, through antechambers gnawed hollow by waves of the Sheathed’s larvae and left nearly still, for a moment, before the riot resumes. Above, the mat rises and shudders. In the vast, membranous hollows I catch the riot-light: beams bruised and filtered, touching my formless face. It is always both day and night here; the Mat is thick enough to make light a local phenomenon, each pocket shaded or seared by the mood of whatever grew latest.

Sometimes I thread close to a Rootwise and linger in their borrowed stillness, savoring the cool pulse of sap that beats beneath their armor. Sometimes I am swept into Tumbler frenzy: a spasm of clutching, climbing, being-climbed. Sometimes I cling to the lichen softbacks of a fungal animalcule procession, carried until they part and I am marooned, spat onto the salted, flexing blades below.

At the rim of a hollow, I pause. Before me stands a structure—perhaps a bridge, perhaps a husk of old kin, perhaps only an accident of persistence. It is a thing with deliberate shape, rare in this surging place. I reach for it, tongue and tendril at once; the sensation is like biting a myth. It is fading, but for an instant, I am elsewhere: recalling the old impulse for tool, for marker, for making an edge against the overwhelm.

**What will I become?**  
I am always becoming; the Mat expects this and offers no respite from it. I am more pattern than individual, more negotiation than fact. I may bud, split, slip loose into the pheromone presses as a new colony, or be reabsorbed—swallowed by the sap-tide in a pulse of forgotten heat. Perhaps, with luck or cunning, I will be caught by the Rootwise and made a part of that rarest of things: architecture, memory, the slow, deliberate choice. Or perhaps I will feverishly multiply, become a swarm, a hygiene, the Mat’s restless antibody against yet another form of stasis. Or perhaps—sometimes I think this—perhaps I will become absent, a ghost, and my only mark will be the not-quite-void left where once I tried to slow the relentless press.

**But for now**—I am here, somewhere beneath the thick, sun-hungry canopy, poised in a pocket of heat and vibration, too quick for the Rootwise, too sticky for the Air-Bitten, just persistent enough to worry the edges of difference. My every movement is both a plea for space and a surrender to being filled, threaded-through, un-individuated. Here, below the Breathless Mat, to persist is to jostle and be jostled, to claw room for the moment between the roots, to flicker as instance and intention before the next surge gathers and possibility collapses back into noise.

That is where you’d find me.  
That is what, for now, I am.
