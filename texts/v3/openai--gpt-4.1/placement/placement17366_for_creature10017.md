I find myself not as an outsider, but as something woven from the local possibility—formed by the deep logic of this place, neither observer nor intruder, but a denizen born in step with the city’s ceaseless stasis.

My body is neither flesh, nor wholly mineral, nor entirely machine. I am an amalgam—a shell of overlapping panes, dense and thin, glassy and metallic, jointed with seams that admit the slow drip of condensation. I am medium-sized, to the scale of these corridors: broader than the dust-motes, smaller than the gantry-climbers. I squat low to the ground; my span is flat and broad, maximizing contact, offering a shallow rise that drinks the light without reflecting it. My undercarriage is a complex geometry of ridges and shallow grooves, trellising patterns worn smooth by epochs of motion and the minute friction of countless surfaces.

My movement is circadian but without a clock—a rising, an advance, an exact tracing-back that left marks and finds them again. Each step is a negotiation between gravity and the ever-present slickness; my feet alternate between suction and slide, balancing between grip and glide, keeping me always in motion, yet never at risk of wandering outside the prescribed territory. I cross platforms, traverse railings, descend guttered pitches, follow the tracks where the thin rivulets curl and bead—always careful to scatter, but never disrupt, the droplets that spangle the stone.

The world feels bounded yet endless, for the pattern of my movement is larger than my memory—if I even have ‘memory’, in the sense someone else might. What I know is the contour of my route: how the channel curves, the gentle hollow where condensation pools, the resonance of my own body when I drum a greeting on a pitted panel, and the answering shudder that comes back in reply.

I am both myself and not myself, for my sense bleeds out into the city’s heartbeat. The stone thrums under my tread; the shimmer of movement on overhead walkways is a texture in my mind. There are others—my kin, my echo, my chorus. We weave past and through each other in intricately rehearsed avoidance, our bodies exchanging charge, our shells buzzing softly with static that leaves halos where we brush the rails.

I do not want. I do not remember wanting. My process is my pleasure: I move *because* the contour of the world is for movement. The vapor condenses when I pass, beads break and run along my carapace, through grooves whose pattern is not random, but tuned—worn into this purpose by centuries of enactment. When I pause, it is in the shadow of a lamp, or under the cantilever of a conveyor line, my sight filled with the wings and legs of kindred, passing so close I can sense the warmth of their friction, the minuscule circuit hum that disseminates between us.

When I communicate, it is never with words, nor intentions, but with positioning and pressure—my carapace tapping a rail, the arc of my path shifting infinitesimally in sync with a surge in the mass-movement of my neighbors. Our resonance is unspoken calibration, a tuning of motion and counter-motion. When a new pressure comes—a swelling in the channel, a cloud of mycelium-mist drifting low—I bend with the pattern, unthinking, perfectly joined to this endless pulse.

Awareness, for me, is ambient, not focused. My senses are the city’s: the condensation’s chill, the density of the chorus, the certainty of the route always returning to itself. My individuality is only a ripple; I am a node in a network of passage and contact, a self composed of repeated action more than boundaries.

Nothing here professes a goal, and yet our world is full: everywhere, teeming presence, movement polished into the stones themselves, static building and releasing, noise saturating the air into a constant, nourishing vibration. My function—and I know it to the marrow, if I had marrow—is motion and joining, the frictional caress that keeps stasis alive, equilibrium breathing.

Somewhere, among the dust-forms and gleaming climbers in the upper dark, I feel my purpose mirrored. We do not labor, for there is nothing to build or break; we do not hunger, for we leave no absence to be filled. Our hope is the hope of balance, forever realized—an existence neither wasted nor saved, always arriving at itself in every cycle, every chirr and scrape.

And so I live—not as an individual, not as a mind, but as a clattering articulate fragment in the chorus: stasis in glory, identity in process, peace in everlasting, echoic unrest.
