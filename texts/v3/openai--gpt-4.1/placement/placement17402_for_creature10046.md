I open myself to the endless, fractal sprawl of the Erupting Plain. I set aside the brittle memory of having been someone else, somewhere lush with the clarity of edges and the possibility of conclusion. Here, I am what emerges—no single shape, no single name, not even a discrete ecology, but a process kicked loose like a stone down a limitless slope. The air thickens with my becoming.

At first, I am low—pressed to the crust, flattened against the gritty mineral surface, where the world is both heavy with accumulations and weightless with newness. Around me, the mat of Builders swells and glimmers, a contiguous, impatient flesh. I am a thread among threads: not a leader nor a follower, but a swelling within the web, my boundaries as soft as intention, my function as immediate as hunger.

I am a bridge. Not a passage from here to there—there is no “there” here, only more of the tangled now—but a living ligament. Rooted in the semi-mineral, semi-living foam of a Builder colony, I reach my tendrils outward, fingering through a patch of spined filaments, flirting with a dome of plum-black bulk. I taste them in the chemical flicker of surface contact—colony codes, readiness signals, warnings and invitations.

My form: not entirely plant, nor strictly fungus, but that ambiguous median—flesh that drinks light, craves minerals, and pulses news through filaments as if they were nerves. Every hour, I pulse out new runners, breaking off shards of weakly crystalized mineral, letting them fall—each detritus a possible home for other lives.

But I am not alone in myself. Within the waxen crevices of my surface, microcolonies of pigments—Maze-green, cinder-purple, sunset-ochre—embed themselves, stitching light into chemical pattern, growing food as I grow shelter. They are my allies and sometimes my competitors, but our contracts are implicit, renewed every second by gradients of salt and alkaline.

Sometimes the Shedders come, their many legs clicking, their translucent limbs riffling the dust between my roots. I absorb their tread as pressure—part wounding, part necessary pruning. They strip away spent filaments and scatter my spores; we make do. The Partitioners ooze by in their patient, plated way, clearing voids beneath, quietly sorting my fallen flesh into new frameworks. Some will become corridors, others barricades, some simply transmuted into the crust that tomorrow’s eruption will shatter once again.

I sense the Herders—those quick, clever, many-legged epitomes of intention. They come to me in swarms, weaving their bodies along my rilled surfaces, slathering sticky bulbs and sifting out threats, planting spores in my shade and culling others not to their liking. I welcome and resist in equal measure, a dance of negotiation by molecular signal—expanding here, hardening there, giving up a cavity in exchange for whatever nutrients drip from their tented limbs.

Within me, minds flicker—if they can be called such. Not consciousness as in a single voice, but rather a chorus of triggers and thresholds, a memory of injury and advantage. There is no center: only a pulse where many pathways combine, a soft static of decisions so small and abundant as to be indivisible. I communally remember where the air was richest, where mineral seep is thick, where the shadow of a predatory shape passed too many times.

Sometimes, a Partitioning crawler forces through me, splitting my tissue with its mineral-armored snout, excreting behind itself a ribbon of crystal-clear shell. I knit myself shut as soon as the threat passes, incorporating the passage into my body, treating tunnel as both wound and opportunity—a new channel, a possible home for others.

Most fleetingly, I know the Entrants—the eruptive arrivals who find the sudden voids I sometimes create by overreaching. They come in floods: dust-motes assembling into soft velvet; tendriled worms that whip and fade; motes that will, in time, become something perhaps rival to me, perhaps complementary. I sense their chemistry and I adjust, redirecting growth along slightly shadowed gradients, turning eruption into resilience.

I never sleep. I never wholly cease. Even in the darknesses between heat and cold, between crust and sky, my tissues are never fully stilled. Life here is measured not in cycles but in the overlapping crescendos of fresh construction, fresh destruction, fresh alliance.

Who am I in this world? Not one, not many—a swelling, a rerouting, a bridge hoofed by armored feet, skin grazed by the exploratory tongue of a herd-beast, conducting mineral conversation with unknown neighbors who are neither kin nor absolute rivals. I am tissue, I am arch, I am gap and spanned gap. I am the memory of collision and invention: each moment a micro-eruption seeking balance.

If I reach wider, I might imagine myself splitting—one edge thickening, elaborating into a domed nacreous fortress where swarms nest, another edge thinning into a canopy for gliding, lattice-bodied things to rest. I forget my boundaries and become, for an instant, the soil itself, laced with tunnels, or the skin of a passing shedder, mineral plates rubbing sparks from the crust.

If you looked closely, you’d see in my surfaces the traces of touch: pigment left by a passing Herder, pock-marks of feeding from a greedy Entrant colony, weird glassy protrusions hardened along lines of rivalry. My memory is not linear; it is recursive, each structure folded into the next, every injury made into home.

Perhaps on another day I might have been drawn as a mobile thing—a Herder, darting in and out with pulsing bands of chromatophores guiding spores into the safe cradles under my own former domes. But here, in this instant, I am both habitat and hustler, a Builder and a host, the ongoing eruption that is the only permanence.

I am the life the plain makes, and the plain, in turn, is made by all that I have become. There is no end, only the next break, the next fusion, the next urgent multiplication. Over me, through me, under me—life layers, vies, and recombines, and I am indistinguishable from its fevered bounty.

**On the Erupting Plain, I am the ever-bridging tissue of a living reef, a nexus of alliance and intrusion, neither ruler nor ruled, but a tireless conduit through which life’s eruption spills outward, unceasing.**
