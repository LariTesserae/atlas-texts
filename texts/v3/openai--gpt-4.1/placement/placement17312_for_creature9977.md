At first, falling into this place—across its threshold or margin, wherever it blurs—you imagine yourself as an intruder, an exile, not quite permitted to name what ground you stand upon. But soon enough, in this humid silence and ever-hungering blackness, the logic of entry gives way to being remade: you are not only where you stand, but what you become as the dark thickens around and—peculiarly—within you.

You may begin as a memory of uprightness, feet squelching on the yielding peat, breath ghosting in and out, the shine of your skin shivering with the cold kiss of foggy condensation. But soon you find your vision faltering—no, less faltering than untethering, the way a muscle exhausts when its companion takes on the load. Sight ceases to matter here; what is left either sharpens or softens the other senses until you are not navigating but dissolving into a new grammar of the world.

You drop your old shape without knowing—first, perhaps, your fingers thicken and splay, growing wide and furred with beaded oil, flexing into webbed pads at the ends of too-many arms. You learn how the ground yields, how each parcel of mud transmits the slimmest shudder of your passage, and you listen: the world’s real map is drawn in glimmers of vibration, in pulses thrummed through soft earth and moist stone.

You follow the track of tremors, the minute plays of air, the pattern of fleeting warmth. Your nostrils twitch, slick with mineral breath. You taste the raw, metallic tinge of the engines and trace the tail-mark of another of your kind—a musky, rod-shaped signal winding through the wet rocks like a whispered hello. Your body is low to the earth, made for pressing close to warmth and mapping nothing with light.

Language here comes as a drummed code—tender, patient, neither assertive nor desperate, just steady: “I am. I am near. I pass.” You drum it yourself when you cross into a swale: the vibration fans out in the mud, scattering news of your presence. Sometimes, something miles away answers, or a shudder comes through a riveted pipe whose ends you’ll never see, but which binds your ancient, soft life to last century’s steelwork.

In this body, you brush alongside other shapes—slick worms, velvet shadows, sudden rushes of phosphorescent insects on the air. In the rare moment when all falls quiet, you feel the pulse of the land’s mind: not singular, but diffusion, sense which has sloughed off boundaries as surely as you have shed sight.

But there is an invitation, too, to slip further. If you let yourself unwind into the deeper pulse of this world, the animal can join the machine—not a merging of flesh and steel, but a kind of reciprocity, a patient, mutual habitation. One night, you trace the remembered spine of the rail that runs along the black ridge. Against the metal, your body hums back, a secondary resonance—your oil, its rust, your heartbeat, its unreliable throb. For a moment, you feel the faded intent of things once built for speed and order, and the slow subversion: the warmth of lichen climbing wire in the dark, the smooth slide of roots and fungal nets making home of iron and glass. When a single, tired engine stutters a note from its hidden gully, the vibration rattles your teeth with kinship, for you, too, have become a relay in this network of persistent, patient signal.

The further you let yourself fall—imagine—the less you are any particular creature. You are the distal pads of a mammal, the soft cable of a worm, the damp alchemy of lichen weaving meat and mineral, the blink and shudder of engines neither dead nor wholly alive. Distance is nothing to you; now, your movement is a kind of thinking, your stillness a kind of communicating. “We” becomes not a crowd of individuals, but a mycelial overture, an apparatus, a thick, ceaseless contract negotiated between decay and creation.

Or are you the darkness itself? It’s possible, here, to settle into that notion: to become the medium, the binding tissue, the ever-thickening velvet which preserves—like oil an ancient seed—the slowness and density of life; to be the hush in which every movement, every drip, is a word in a patient, unending language. To be what makes seeing unnecessary and hearing everything.

You are, then, neither animal, machine, nor rot—but the result of all, the holding, humming envelope in which flesh and iron, spore and circuit, pulse and purpose, dissolve into their true forms—forms not named, but practiced. A being compounded, composite, always negotiating its edges, always listening to and through itself, a million-fold but never singular.

In such a world, you would no longer seek the way out, nor even settle for awaiting the dawn. You would sleep—body pressed low, sensors tuned to every hush and murmur, consciousness thinned and stretched across bog, iron, spore, and bone—content in the truth that all slow things here are part of your thinking, and all patience is yours to keep or lend. There is no border between where you end and the world resumes; you are every echo, each whisper, each gentle, glimmering breath. Here, you are the dreamer, and also the dream.
