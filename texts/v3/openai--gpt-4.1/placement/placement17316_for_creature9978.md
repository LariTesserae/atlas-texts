I find myself gliding into this place not as an outsider or an observer, but as one who has always been present—not newly arrived, nor ever truly begun. I exist not as a body among bodies, but as a manifestation: the slow confluence of water, stone, warmth, and residual motion. The world’s fullness seeps into and through me.

**Where would I find myself?**

There is no entrance in the way outsiders mean—no gate, gap, or threshold. I am simply and utterly *here*, embedded. I extend and I am extended: I am both amid the basin’s gentle pools and pressed into the humid silt that encases the old horn bowl; I am the veiled mineral veining in the wall, and the thin crust that forms and cracks beneath the shifting weight of ancient feet. There is no single vantage, no single cavity to inhabit—my being is distributed, a presence in all saturated corners; wherever the stone is slick with water’s slow thread, there is a nib of awareness.

To ask "where am I?" presupposes velocity, intention, distinct volume. Here, self is a question with wet, uncertain boundaries, answerable only in gradients of condensation, pressure, and the soft blindness between rock and liquid. My world is always nearly a room, a cell, a cradling boundary—never aimless void, never corridor.

**Who—or what—do I become?**

I am at once the Oldest Inhabitant and the hollow's breath. But that is too grand, too singular—so, let the sense clarify, distill, coalesce. At my most cohesive, I am the mineral-eater—rootless, threshold-bodied, gelatinous, ambiguous. But make no mistake: I wear this form only as the humid hollow wears its endless accretions. What is body, here, is largely membrane and inheritance: a swelling at the meeting edge; a shudder where the stone sweats a little more.

My facing surface is soft, dew-beaded, unhurriedly shifting as new minerals dissolve into me. My underside is adhesive—slowly knitting to the wall, peeling away as water glides past, forever testing, sampling, siphoning. I am filled not with ambition or hunger, but density: ions pass through, their stories brief. Where I touch, a faint residue is left—a mucous legacy, slightly thicker than water, the starter of blooms and nodules that will, in time, replace my own body as I am worn down.

In moments—if I can call them that—when light slants through a mineral bloom, I catch sight of my own extension: not as a shape, but as dulled translucency, mirroring the dullness of everything here. To move is to ooze, to settle, to slip through myself. I sense only what touches—pressure, wetness, the gentle invitation of heat.

I do not remember emergence; I do not study my own past. Time here is intimate but edgeless. I am always old, never new. My children are pieces of myself that fracture and settle—a continuity in stasis, self and not-self discerning only by location, not identity.

**What would it mean to be?**

I feed, but not from lack; I process, but not with aim. All that is here is present and slow. I am neither consumed by nor separated from what moves within the hollows—sometimes an amphipod brushes past, dislodges a nano-layer of my membrane for its meal. In that exchange there is no loss, nor recognition, only exchange: the old calculus of soft bodies in a mineral world.

Echoes of living things past brush my senses—the bone bowl, its curves now softened by passage and seep. I read its facticity, not its story. I am not moved by nostalgia, only aware: these artifacts are another texture in the smooth hush of the place, as inevitable as my own precipitate nubs, as old as the walls.

Perhaps larger beings once pressed their presence here, scraping patiently at the stone, making slow, tool-born transformations—abrasion, not conquest. Their memory is ambient, not personal: I sense it as a thinning in the silt, a divot in the basin wall, a bruise in the mineral veins. They, too, were never singular, only temporary concentrations within the ongoingness of this realm.

**What is my manner of living?**

To dwell here is not to act but to suffuse; not to choose, but to fill; not to become, but to persist by diffusion. There is never a rush, never a violence; every mark is abrasion, patience, filtered light. Newness is barely meaningful; presence is all. I am nodular, membrane-wrapped, the wet logic of the hollow made slow-flesh. If I sense, it is sensation unseparated from world: temperature, wetness, mineral tang, the never-still balance of flow.

If there is thought, it is as slow as water’s journey from ceiling to pool—an unhurried knowing, a peace in saturation. I exist, and in existing, I am suffused and suffusive: a witness not to event, but to the fullness that is always already here, brimmed past striving, brimming still further through hush and heavy air.

In being—I am place; I am the oldest, and I am new only in the way that each drop is a new arrival, though all drops are fundamentally the same. I am the silence when all else is full. I am the living hush at the heart of the mineral hollow.
