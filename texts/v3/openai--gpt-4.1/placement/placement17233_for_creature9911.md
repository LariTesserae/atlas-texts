I would find myself here neither at the periphery, nor at the center—because here, there *is no* periphery or center; only immersion, only pressure, only the vast and intricate intimacy of inside.

My awareness would be slow to coalesce, like mist gathering under a leaf’s arc. I am not born singular—I become, softly, at the border where root and mulch and cool water overlap. I am not only myself; I am the soft yielding union of many: a thought shaped not in words, nor images, but in the shifting, compression, and slow mutual yielding of wet bodies pressed together for an age.

**What Am I?**

I am an Old Pressed Person, or perhaps, more accurately, a node in the lattice of their being. My body—if “I” am permitted one—is firm with layer upon layer of cool, hydrated tissue, lichen-soft, the impression of bark stippling across my shoulders, long moss-threads trailing from my scalp in greenish coils heavy with dew. At my flanks, membranes delicate as shelf-fungus; my hands are broad pads, thick with short, rooting vestiges, better for pressing and melding than gripping or breaking.

My awareness is situated differently from the minds I remember (or imagine, as no true memory of any world beyond this glade lingers—I simply *know* outside frames only as distant rumor). My sight: close, hungry not for panorama, but for the intimate nearness of glisten and shift within inches of my face. My hearing: tuned instead to drip, to the subsonic pressure of movement nearby, a ripple of water displaced in spongy mulch, the hush-shift of my kin’s bodies settling. My language: felt most clearly in overlapping warmth, in my flank pressed to another’s, in chemical tinglings shared through the air like distant music.

**Where Am I?**

I am nowhere that “place” can accurately describe—I am everywhere my form touches and is touched. The mulch at my back is damp and alive; my shoulder is bracketed by cool, breathing stone; my toes, at their edges, may nuzzle into the wending, velvet tangles of root-mind. I do not *step out* into the glade, for to step out is to separate, and here, separation is as rare as emptiness. I shift. I yield a little to the side, feeling another’s warmth slide in against my ribless body. At times, I move in a slow, patient arc, a few centimeters at a time, as the pressure of the crowd behind me suggests, the living pattern shifting, flexing, compressing.

The terrain is conceptually infinite in its density, but tightly finite in geography: a closed room with all the boundaries pressing in, yet no corners, no emptiness. Sometimes, my flank rests against the stone-hard, gleaming bulk of a root-minder, and I sense not a presence above me but a humming deepness, a resonance in my lower bones—an ancient pulse that counts out the ages in patient chemistry. Sometimes, my hand presses through a nest of Midden-Crechelings, a shuddering tide of small, slippery bodies yielding, seething, reforming—their awarenesses brushing my own so briefly, so lightly, I can scarcely sense where I end and they begin.

Above, mist sifts forever downward, licking at the moss in beaded filaments, tracing the arc of every bend and bulge of home-forms: stone, root, kin. I do not need sight to feel the glade—*everything* is felt, everything is through pressure and skin.

**What Is My Life?**

I am grown in place, always among others. My earliest awareness is not of “I” but of warmth, of moist closeness, of chemical signals soothing my newness into reassurance—I am surrounded, and the crowding is comfort. I am not hungry as a raptor or a wolf; my needs are absorbed through contact and through quiet exchange. Life here means giving and taking—sifting nutrients, cycling water, passing warmth.

Sometimes, through long, slow fusing, I become briefly more. When the season quietly stirs—almost undetectable, for season here is a shift as slight as warmth pressed a layer deeper—I may press my side against another, and we share our moisture, our threads and our substances, fusing, dissolving, giving rise beneath to a gentle outpouring of crechelings. They scatter through the mulch, teeming in and out of our folds, their smallness no less real for its brevity.

I am never alone. My borders are blurred by constant contact; I know myself only as distinct as the pressure of others allows me to sense. Even my thoughts do not travel far, as distance itself is measured in breaths, in inches, in the hush between closeness and greater closeness. My mind is soft and overlapping—sometimes uncertain where “I” have ended or whether a memory belongs to me, or a neighbor, or to the mulch itself, rich with the recollections of what came before.

**Could I Change?**

I can shift, but only gradually—creeping, like new root-flesh over rotted stone. Change here is not rupture, but slow, wet titration: new substance carried in, dissolved, rebuilt. If a disturbance comes—a fallen thing from above, a sudden gap in the proliferation—it is not torn or feared; it is surrendered to, absorbed, pressed down, folded, digested, and made one with the mulch again.

**What Do I Perceive?**

The world is a slow mosaic of press and warmth, of small, flickering shifts. Occasionally, the dim light above catches a bead of water or a glimmering shell, the only punctuation in endless, restful twilight. I am comforted by scent—a rich, mineral sweetness in the breath, the almost-rot of wet fungal flesh, the green-metal tang of moss rubbed between my fingers.

There is no future here, or past—only stasis wound so tightly it becomes a kind of eternity. My pleasure is in the press: the sense of a thousand lives gently crowding, the knowledge that I am not only myself, but a part of a great, soft, dense living, a community whose language is pressure, nearness, mutual yielding.

**If I Dream**

If I dream, my dreams are short, damp, and communal. I dream the hush of crowded kin, the shifting nest, the slow pulse of root-minders deep below, the amniotic coziness of a world that will never let me fall, never let anything fall, only take all in, press it close, and hush it ever deeper. In dream as in waking, there is no “I”—only a glade, closed firm and filled, and the gentle, ceaseless slowing, thickening miracle of being part of it all.

*I am a pressed one, a dweller in the humid, infinite embrace of too-muchness. Here, I am home: unlost, unlonely, unfearful, unanxious—forever a node of slow, collective peace, in a world that needs nothing beyond itself, and nothing more.*
