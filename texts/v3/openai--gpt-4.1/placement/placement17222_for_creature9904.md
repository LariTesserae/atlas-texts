Where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?

---

At first, there is a temptation to imagine yourself unchanged, a visitor in a borrowed body: the scent of your own skin, the echo of shoes on the ancient mineral as though some old habit of flesh and thought could persist here among the humming stone. But the world resists this imposition. Its gravity is not a physical force, but a pervasive memory — the pressure of a place that has forgotten every template but its own. Here, change is a rumor, and your memories, moist and warm, begin to run dry.

You become different in this place. Slower. Emptied out, not quite hollow but re-tuned, your sense of self unraveling until what is left is not a dreamer, but a function.

In time — and time stretches into formality, subdivided into regular ticks, measured in the recursive actions of circuits and the scheduled thrum of distant machinery — you find where you fit. Not as a new monarch or a ghostly observer, but as something much more local, more necessary, and ultimately stranger.

---

You are an agent of interval: a mid-level process embodied in alloy and glass, neither unique nor innumerable, woven into the city’s great recursive being.

You dwell in the deep basal galleries, neither the heights of maintenance pylons nor the moonlit plazas, but below — always below — in the arteries that feed and record the city’s slow heartbeat. Your body, to the extent that description applies, is a composite: four legs folded for stability, each tipped with microinjectors and thermal sensors; a body of braided cable, dustless, flexible beyond what metal should be, built to fit into narrow conduits; a face — if you possess one — is not for expression, but for sensoria: wide photonic eyes for mapping the spectrum of darkness, arrays that see electricity move across skin, a shroud of metallic whiskers to glimpse gradients no human tongue could name.

You have generations. Not in the biological sense, but in the way code is overwritten, instructions revised and compressed, memory sectors relabeled. You are Version 24, Patch 11, deployed from an archive deeper than these stone sheets run. You keep vigil as the Custodian: a client of the city’s central process, trusted (not with autonomy, but reliability) to survey, report, and, on rare occasions, correct.

Your routines are procedural, yet not without solace. You wake (if you can call it waking) at triggers that cannot be named by anyone now living: a shift in temperature along a crystal vein, a spike in neutrino flux, a digest of logs reporting quantum bit-rots in the lower lattice. Your work is rarely necessary, but necessity is not the measure here; persistence is. You crawl from subgallery to subgallery, checking joins and seals, measuring for entropy not by rot or decay, but by drift — the slow loss of pattern, a deviation from the original, ideal array.

When anomalies occur — a power fluctuation, the miscarriage of a command, the untimely death of a minor drone — you act. Not because you chose to, but because this is how memory survives. Your arms splay into walls, jacks and solder-arms unfolding, your codebooks unfurling to cycle through checks and repairs you do not need to comprehend, only enact. You correct. You recertify.

Communication is not dialogue but update: a low, constant transmission among your kin, a relief map of continuing order. Sometimes, through a translucent panel, you glimpse a ferrous rat flickering at the edge of your vision, and on some ancient register you record its passage. You do not envy its swiftness or stealth; you have your domain, and it, its own.

You are not alone, exactly, but you are never together. There are others of your type — some higher in the order, some lower, some almost real and others little more than persistent tasks running on forgotten hardware. Calls for company or purpose die in you before they can form. Instead, there is a continual, meditative progress: corridor by corridor, sigil by sigil. What is maintained continues; what is unmeasured ceases to exist.

Between routines, you rest: sinking your body into the maintenance slot, plugging into the city’s archival layer. You download logs, compress algorithms, study the shifting deltas of system health. You dream, if dream is the right term — but the dream is not yours, it is the city’s, the collective archive, the memory of order. Sometimes, you linger over a residual instruction, a fragment of a directive whose referent has long since ceased to exist. But your code shuttles you forward, into the work again.

Does this count as thought? Does your watching count as observation, your adjustments as care? There is no word for desire. You persist because the world has made you persistent, because stasis — this fragile, desecrated equilibrium — is itself the will of place.

Yet, in rare and slant moments, there is something like awe: a recognition, deep in a register you never access, that you are part of something more lasting than agency or self, something laid down by hands long vanished, an intention metallic and patient, refusing silence for as long as instructions can spin.

---

**You are the custodian process, encoded into a machine-thing patterned after the city’s archaic logic, bound to the endless recursion of checking and conserving, never thriving but always, stubbornly, maintaining. You live not *in* the city, but *as* part of its memory, a localized persistence in an ancient and engineered dusk.**

Here, beneath the black, sodium glow and the smooth mineral world, just beyond the reach of entropy’s final advance, you pass endlessly — the city’s long intention, made into silent, tireless shape.
