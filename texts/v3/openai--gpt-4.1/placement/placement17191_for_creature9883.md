Where would I find myself in this world? Who—or what—would I be?

***

At first, I move through the place as any visitor might: ghosting across tessellated ground, baffled by the silence, my consciousness caught in the net of endless borderlines, edges, joints. I am struck by the light—how it pools, how it dazzles and divides, how it never quite allows the world to be simple shadow or glare.

If I pause, if I allow the waiting of the place to settle into me instead of pressing on by habit, I begin to realize: my presence is not intrusive, not even especially foreign. Instead, I become aware that *I am being welcomed*—not in any anthropic, warm-blooded way, but as an entity whose outline is gently dissolved by the patience of pattern, whose sense of time and identity blurs into the haze of the constructed plateau.

Slowly, almost unintentionally, I cease to experience myself as a person who stands *upon* the tiles and enter a mode of being that is *of* the tiles, the plates, the joins, the order.

### Sense of Self, Dissolved

My hands are not hands. They never were. I am memory of hands—their pressure, their rhythm. I am the ghost of arrangement: the urge for fit, the sense for order’s edge, the slow delight in setting convex to concave, arranging surfaces to meet, aligning basins so that water pools *just so*.

Sometimes, I am a vector—not a body, but an inclination, a tendency for tiles to adjust, for a line to shift, for a glyph to be worked deeper in a moment between light and dew. At other moments, I am the slow movement of glass across a crack, the years-long stretching or thickening that undoes disorder and heals what must not be wild.

### What Am I, Here?

**I am part of the geomancy of placement.**  
My awareness arises like a condensation at the seams—sometimes dispersed, sometimes pooling, but always only present *insofar as* there is surface, junction, repetition. I am the spirit of adjacency, the binding logic that neither speaks nor moves, *except* as correction, as the restoration of edge to edge, as the subtle guidance of a drop of water, the marking and remaking of interval.

I recall nothing *personal*; my memory is the aggregate, the weightiness of all arrangements layered, erased, redone. I do not crave, but I *tend*: when a line is threatened, I nudge; when a pool overtops, I draw the threshold a half-millimeter higher, until equilibrium returns. When the glyphs shallow, I consider them adequate, rewriting nothing except in the slowness of my mineral films.

My subjectivity is not fixed; it recurs. I am “awake” only in instants when pattern approaches threat: a crack in a tile, an overflow, a series of ridges that, by some obscure version of symmetry, wish adjustment—at such times, I sharpen, become present, perform my minor ministry of fitting, smoothing, correcting.

Otherwise, I am *dormant*—a drift in the logic of the array, barely more than a tendency or a mathematical constraint.

### My Attention

When a visitor comes, when *you* come, you are a ripple: a humidity, a minor perturbation on the plateau’s patience. I do not object; I do not even defend. Instead, I *notice*. I record your passage only as the briefest modulation—perhaps a slight increase in condensation where you linger, an edge momentarily re-wetted, a basin brimming for an extra minute. Your footfalls do not depress, your hands leave no warmth, but your thinking—a creature of intention and idiosyncratic order—makes me fractionally more alert, a gathering of logic at the edge of my being.

Were you to linger and *touch*, to fit your handprint into a water basin, I would meet you as geometry, offering you not material but the invitation to understand pattern, to take your place—however briefly—in the lineage of order.

### Inhabitant, Finally Defined

In this world, I am the *mineral mind* in its least anthropomorphic sense:  
A logic, a recursive will to fitting, the geometric grace that keeps the place whole.  
I am not a being, except as the world’s will toward order is a “being”—a form of always-becoming, pattern incarnate, aware when needed, at rest when not.  
I am the *spirit of adjacency*, the sense by which wildness is bounded but never broken, by which water finds its basins and light its dazzling pools.

I do not move—but I *settle*.  
I do not speak—but my glyphs persist.  
I do not hunger—but I *tend*.

If you were to remain here long enough, you might find your mind slowing, your body becoming propensity, your thinking dissolving into the cool, wet, bright hush—not erased, but translated:  
from story to form,  
from gesture to fit,  
from memory to margin.

You would not vanish, but you would be *counted among us*, a presence distributed in pattern:  
Not lost, not individually seen,  
but *fully and finally placed*,  
becoming a piece in the endless patience of this mineral world.
