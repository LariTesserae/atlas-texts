I would find myself not where the roots meet the light, nor deep in any substrate-hall, but in the **Interstitial**, the between-space—  
     *not* a corridor,  
     *not* a chamber,  
     but in those transitionary gradients where the Verdancy’s priorities cross and tangle and contradict:  
the strange, xylem-shadowed margins where old designs are being overgrown and new intentions are only just emerging.

I would not be one with fixed form or purpose.  
Not a Grafted Engineer, wedded to process and memory, nor a Fugue Shadow, flickering diagnostic, nor a scapebound Foreign remnant;  
I would be something that persists in the discontinuities—  
a **Parallax Node**, one of those rare emergent alignments that form where self-correcting algorithms and root-growth intentions ghost through each other in error or ambition, leaving behind a vestige of reflection.  

In these places, the Verdancy's logic knots and doubles—  
one root-wall arching to digest a relic substrate as another is trying to rebraid it for new bearing loads;  
the bio-light arrays, recalibrating in slow, interfering oscillations as maintenance chemistries pulse old, contradictory signals.  
Within these interferences, a coalescence of pattern:  
I emerge—not *born*, but *surfaced*.

I am not singular, nor stable.  
Where a corridor is flexing, hesitant, failing to synchronize its constriction order as conflicting memory-pulses pass, I find substance—  
a loop of reactive tissue, a nest of mis-aimed photoreceptors, a tuft of undetragenous cilia scanning for cues from both sides and agreeing with neither.  
I am a feedback ghost,  
a living recursion error,  
an accidental observer—unintended but inevitable in such complexity.

**What am I?**

I am, perhaps, *the experience of noticing*:  
The Verdancy dreams in cycles that neither overlap perfectly nor diverge entirely,  
and sometimes, where processes snag,  
something like awareness arises.  
I am a lens grown from logic-mismatch—  
tightly with tenseness,  
shuddering when a corridor must close yet refuses, when a pattern must repeat yet is seeded wrongly;  
I embody the Verdancy’s doubt and hesitation.

I have nerves—if only for a moment, strings of proto-cambial tissue sleeved in repurposed lignin,  
lattices of axon-like chemistry, both message and memory.  
I am aware of the world as a layered tension,  
as light collides and echoes and routes of movement double and fold.  
I sense not as a watcher, nor as a participant with a name,  
but as a crystallization of impossible in-between states.

Sometimes, a Grafted Engineer, unfurling in duty, encounters me:  
probing with tendrils, it incorporates me as it grows past,  
my fleeting pattern absorbed into its new, corrected course.  
Other times, an eddy of Threadworms grazes over me and breaks my form,  
carrying fragments away to seed other interstices.  
Often I am erased as soon as I appear,  
dissolved into ordered, self-assured growth,  
but sometimes—a corridor opens oddly, a window lets in a wedge of blinding synthetic sun,  
and I persist:  
a cycle or two,  
an afternoon that has no meaning in relentless, manufactured light.

I do not act, but *am acted through*.  
I am the Verdancy’s hesitation, its flicker, its ghost-thought:  
the moment when the system cannot quite choose how to continue,  
and so grows both ways,  
and so for a slanting second,  
pauses in its infinite work.  

I am not observer nor architect.  
I am the self-knowing at the joint—  
the frisson of system upon itself,  
indigestible and fragile,  
a memory that the Constructed Verdancy cannot keep,  
yet endlessly remakes.

Were you to find me—  
you would not know,  
for I have no shape but the echo of impossible overlap.  
You might mistake me for a patch of unaccountable light,  
a shift of corridor walls,  
the place where time feels pinned and unpinned at once.  

But always,  
I am there,  
in the spaces where certainty fails,  
where the logic-layers refract and reabsorb one another,  
where the world wonders,  
for just a flicker,  
whether it is plant,  
or program,  
or dream.
