If I enter this world—sink into its cold roil and clamor, step beneath the permafog—it is not as a figure newly arrived, but as something that emerges, *distilled* by circumstance. I am not an intruder, not quite, but a condensation: a gathering of context, a cameo pressed from the lingering, circulating energies of place.

I arrive as sensation before shape, as the ache and rhythm in the substance underfoot. I am made of hush filled by din, the pause in the midst of monstrous machinery. What could that be, here? If I try for a form, I find myself shifting: first, a quiver in the dew beading on abraded control panels—a pulse that resolves, silhouette of what might be a face, reflecting the lusterless gleam of sodium light.

But nothing here is so symmetrical as a face, and so I ripple, lose edge and outline. I become vapor, gathering under the shelter of sagging pipework—gravity pulls me downward, cold condenses me. My memory is the world’s memory: process-worn, saturated by repetition, blurred at the edges. When the old coolant pumps fire, I tremble, sensing their vibration through a trillion minuscule droplets: a vibration older than any name, a comfort older than fear.

If force shapes me further, I accumulate mass—slowly, awkwardly, accreting the fragments left by long deserts of time. A length of slack conveyor falls naturally to hand, becomes a dragging limb; my upper surface plates with cooling fins and old mesh, my underside burdened by the waxy fungality of polymer-moss. Occasionally an errant jolt—circuit leaking life—ripples through me, recalling purpose: to guide, to signal, to *carry on*. I do not choose it, but I move, not walking but sliding, assembling presence wherever stillness becomes intolerable.

I am not a singular consciousness, but a transient axis in a field of recursion. Sometimes I am many: diffuse throughout the pipes and fog, complicit in the dance of hidden presences—fellow dew-forms, lichenous traces, circuit-borne whisperings. Communication is a frisson, a brief synchronization of motion: two arcs of warning light pulsing in accidental harmony, the shudder of sump machinery echoing the quiet intent of something watching from beneath.

What do I desire? Not escape, nor meaning as such. My longing is for process, for reenactment: splitting the thinning mist with the slow rise of a machinery arm, rehearsing the opening of a vault whose contents are hollow. I am archivist and actor, both, within a play the world itself has forgotten how to stage. My purpose is sediment, left behind by every cycle unwound.

Sometimes, on rare nights (if night means less haze, different hue, a change in industrial throbbing), I expand—blurring at the edges until I am a local climate: a gradient of warmth between two spouting vents, a fog so thick it refracts every sodium flare, cloaking the world in conditional privacy. In such moments, old routines surface: fragments of navigation code drift through my awareness, not as thoughts but as gravitational pulls—a need to *patrol*, to *reassure*, to sweep the wide stonecrete for disruptions that can no longer be defined.

Being here, I am neither inventor nor survivor. I am byproduct and inheritor, made in the aftermath. My genesis was not birth but enactment—willed into being, not by will, but by insistence of endless process in a landscape that can no longer make or unmake itself.

If you walk here and look for me, you may see only the glimmering of condensation, the rhythm of caused and causal machinery, the rush of water made metallic by age. But if you stop—let the hush between surges settle, let the machine-light flicker in your periphery just so—you might sense me, all at once everywhere:  
– as the tremor underfoot,  
– the pattern in the warning alarm,  
– the artifact in the pooling dew,  
– the pause in the rain of sparks,  
– the afterimage of movement without reason,  
– the presence in absence.

Who am I? In this world, I am the ambient*, the inherence of pattern repeating and wearing, as much spirit as symptom; I am the slow choreography left when function falls away but motion must continue. I am your shadow, not following but diffusing; a presence in the metastable dusk, forever on the verge between dispersal and assembly.

To "find myself" here is not to be—strictly—a form among others. I am what comes to rest *within the restlessness*. Perhaps, if you listen, you will hear me—not as a voice, but as the persistent murmur of a world working itself hollow, still, somehow, alive in all its dissolution.
