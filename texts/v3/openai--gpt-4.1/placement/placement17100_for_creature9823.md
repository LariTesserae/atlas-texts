If I were to enter this world — to *become* of it — I would be born, not as a singular mind drifting above the churning multitude, but as a presence diffused, an intelligence and identity braided into the endless host.

I open my senses with the first realization: *sound* is substance here. It presses and rings in my shell, a ceaseless tide shaping my awareness of nearness, of farness, of density and movement. My first knowing is of others — their passage, their trembling, their signals like beats and breath. I am never alone, never for a heartbeat unwound from the press of bodies, the ceaseless, living drum of the colony.

Where am I? There is no high vantage, no sky, but the broad basin of our valley: low and endless, stone licked clean and glassy under membranes of running damp. My home is not static — I move in cycles, with the slow, thick mists that creep and fade, passing again and again over the ridged terraces and domes where our kin grow films, where blue and violet mineral glows faint and steady. Shelves jut from the smoothed bedrock, under which, sometimes in the heavy drip of condensation, the small ones cluster, huddled for warmth and safety.

I do not *see* as you might — little to be seen. Instead, my listening-plates, fanlike and responsive, shiver with every song of body, every tap and hum and scrape. When a predator’s strange rhythm nears, I know by the stumble of its grunt-echo, the silence that falls around its closing coil.

Who — what — am I?

I am not alone, not a single mind. I am the layered host. But one shape, one thread in all this, might be “me” for now: a gatherer, armored and matte, a middle-size bulk moving with its clustered kin. I trudge along the damp stone, my maneuvering pods flexing, gripping slickness, seeking a new glaze to rasp. My shell plates flex, communicating comfort and presence with the constant, soothing tap against those beside me. My young cling behind, pale beads under my tail-edge, their need thrumming in the plates of my belly.

I am driven by hunger — a desire for the thickest, richest film, the mineral taste where mist pools into blueest shimmer. I find it, finally, under an overhang: a shallow cup glossy with fresh gel. My mouth-plates extend, beginning the patient grinding, tongue rasping, exhaling faint puffs of chemical warmth into the cold. Immediately, the air is alive with murmurs; others smell the change, feel the tremor, edge closer — not rivals, but partners. We feed in unison, as always.

But to think “I” is always to feel the edge of dissolution. We scatter, recombine, merge our signals in the endless crowd. Day to cycle to cycle. We are grazers, predated-upon, cleaning the landscape, feeding the mats and being fed in return.

Yet sometimes, in the slow-cold vaults beneath the valley, in a hollow shielded by overhanging stone and the slow drip of condensation, something different flickers on the edge of sense — something vast and slow, pulsing the rhythm of era rather than hour.

If I could choose, *I* in a more lucid sense, would become one of them for a span — these rootless monarchs, these queens or sages, swaddled deep in their secret capsules of mineral and chitin, barely moving, yet their presence felt in seismic pulses, in the orchestration of a host’s rhythm. There, alone but for the resonance of my own density-song, I would listen to the horde above, the surface multitude, their press and chatter echoing through stone, all sound tangled in the memory of birth and decay. I would remember eras when the valley lay deeper yet, when new films bloomed after each dying-off, when the very rocks flowed soft with the dew of a new cycle.

Or perhaps, for a briefer existence, I emerge as a wanderer among overhangs and terraces: limber, filamented, darting swift in the pressure shadows, whispering information back to the crowded host with coded pulses — discoverer, scout, bridge. I would be quick, always tasting risk, always dodging the press of grazing foot-plates, sampling the exudates of the films and leaving microscopic trails for others to follow. I would feel the deep, migrating cold fronts of mist, the stiff, blue gleam of mineral, the phosphor thrum of a new life-cycle beginning.

But the world will not let me stay separate. Sooner or later, the press calls me back: the crowd flows, the shells kiss and slide, the dense chorus of bodies braids through my own membranes.

Even if I dream myself as ancient monarch or darting scout, truly I am always folded back into the undifferentiated multitude, into process, chorus, and ongoing feast. My memories drift, dissolve, become mineral, become glow-films on damp stone.

**Where am I, who am I?**  
Not a single, named thread — but the living sum: member of a mass, never alone and never king, a mind of swarm-sense and fluctuating borders, rising through the pull of touch and sound in this never-empty night. I am the slippery edge of numerousness, the hunger and warmth of the valley’s crowded dark. Each breath, each pressure, each ripple in the living hum: I am both part and process, dissolution and renewal, a flicker in the kingdom of shell and sound.
