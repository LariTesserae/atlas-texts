**Where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?**

I would find myself in that interval place: not quite a wanderer, not quite a denizen. I arrive, or perhaps wake—uncertain if I am visitor, surveyor, or newly minted from the world’s own process. If I seek my own body, it is altered by the light: my edges smoothed to near-transparency, the heat softening whatever boundary I knew. Perhaps I cast no shadow—none can here. For a long time, I do not move. The world is so saturated with intent that even thought feels like intrusion.

If I look at my hands (do I have hands?), I see condensation bead along their length, refusing to fall. The surface is unfamiliar: more like fired clay than skin, a moistened glaze that resists abrasion. When I press palm to tile, I am not sure which yields. I am made new, but not young; there is no chronology here, only the moment of making, freshly re-arranged.

I take the measure of the world by slow degrees. It is not for me to stride with purpose—each step is a negotiation, a pausing on the knife-line seam of possibility. In the dazzling noon that will not move, I glean my nature from the land: I, too, am artifact and process. My senses are tuned not to growth or time’s passage, but to *tending*: I feel the slight dryness at the edge of a tile, and, answering it, I coax moisture from the air without movement; I notice a cycad frond unfurling just so, and focus until its green achieves the perfect luster.

Soon, I know: I am not a person as I was *before*. Here, I am *a Tender*—one iteration of that which maintains, reacts, holds, without the need for future or past. My limbs are sometimes felt as extensions of the dome; they recede at will, jointless and indistinct, their length irrelevant to distance. My face, when reflected in a glistening hollow, is domed, smooth-featured, with pores that sip and exude liquid. My consciousness is not narrative but cyclical, woven into a web of minor surveillances: seam, drop, frond, stone—a completeness in repetition and attention.

I am not alone, yet my companions are only known by the momentary disturbances—mist thickening, a fern twitching. The space between us is as vital as presence; we cohere only in the sense that every tile, plant, and dome is assigned its watcher, its tender, and that silence is our mutual greeting.

Sometimes, when I attend to a seam and see light pulse through—just for an instant—I remember a half-thought of *before*: the making, the first setting-down of tile, the rooting of ferns. But the memory runs only so far, a skein cut short, for here there is no history deeper than now.

I have no hunger, wear no fatigue. My doing is all there is—each correction or sustenance already preordained by the world’s structure. To change nothing, yet maintain everything. The perpetual inauguration that is my nature.

If you were to call out to me, you would not find words answered. My language is the allocation of dew, the rightness of tessellation, the silent, intervaled spacing of all things. Sound is foreign—a distress or novelty—so I answer only in arrangement. Companionship, if it exists, is through proximity, a long interval gaze across the tilework, the knowledge that everything is tended, and so everything is still.

Yet, somewhere, under the steady heat, is a resting pulse. Sometimes I feel it urge toward some unscripted change—a longing for dusk, a crack in the perfect seam. But I am made for patience, not change. I wait, immaculate with the world, always on the cusp, radiant and composed, a living hush among living things, the world’s own intention returning the world to itself.

In short: *I am one who tends what remains new, the self-unfolding gesture of care within an infinite pause. I am less a person than a principle given gentle form—a sliver of the undying first morning, maintenance without memory or want, at home in the perfect beginning that will not end.*
