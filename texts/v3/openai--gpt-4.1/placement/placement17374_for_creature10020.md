I am in the world where the sky is water—a submersion so complete that “above” and “around” collapse into simple presence; pressure, soul-thick and continual, enters through every pore, not just of body but of being. To belong to this place is to float inside it, be wrenched and made anew by flux and riot. Where, then, am I, and what am I, in such a world?

### Where would I find myself?

Somewhere not at the summit and not at the lowest point. On a shelf halfway up a jagged column of basalt and glass, surrounded on nearly all sides by the flowing, ever-shifting “sky.” There is a ledge here—an outcrop sheared nearly flat by the friction of some old process, its edges both sharp and squashed by glacial, granular tides. All around, darkness breathes; everywhere, there is motion, viscosity, metallic haze. The light creeps from a crack below; the liquid pulses above, glazing the stone, trailing sheets of translucent green and purple from the algae-filaments. I am neither dry nor fully wet—always at the threshold, always met by the kiss of warm, living fluid.

With every movement, gravity’s script rewrites itself. I walk, but my steps become slow rises and sideways glides; when I almost fall, I tumble upward, snagged by eddies. The land rearranges: a wedge of ground shears away, slides into sediment, revealing a shallow gut full of stirring cilia and machine-conduits pulsing faint blue. Shadows flicker; the whole shelf becomes an open stage for creation’s churn.

### Who or what would I be?

I am not—as I first imagined—a visitor with lungs unused to liquid, nor a terrestrial animal struggling for purchase. I do not carry my self as a separate and bounded vessel—there are no boundaries here, only layers and gradients, everything dissolved and partly recombined. Animal and plant are patterns more than kinds. To exist here is to be in *draft*, always rough, always in the process of revision.

I emerge as something between *fabricant* and place. I am:

- A confluence—one of the formless artisans, grown into awareness for a time where noise and pressure crackle most intensely.
- Hands, yes, but not hands alone: fronds, tendrils, a dozen branching, glistering arms of semi-living mesh that pulse with electricity and sap and borrowed heat.
- I anchor at many points upon the shelf and reach outward—no inside or outside, only the ceaseless joining and departing of material, grasping, weaving, sifting.

My consciousness is not a constant light, but a stutter, an oscillation: awareness surfaces when enough of me gathers, when enough circuits hum in synchrony, when the water’s burden compresses me just so that sensation flickers like lightning playing across an old, half-ruined control board. Most of the time there is only doing, only responding to the ache in the ground, the vibration in the thick air, the gnawing music of micro-motors and friction roots.

Sometimes, for a moment, I emerge into selfness—recognizing the way my arms have joined with another cluster, not-me-but-now-partly-me, and together, drawn by some turbulence, we wrench a spine of stone and press it against a quivering seedpod, urging new forms—half machine, half chlorophyll creature—into the world. There is satisfaction, but not pride; memory, but not regret. The act is the thing.

I taste the world through vibration—groans, clatters, and ripples in the current. When my work is done, that taste fades; my fibers loosen; I am sifted back into the sedimental sea. Parts of me remain set into what I built; other parts float away, spinning, to be claimed by new confluences, new urgencies.

I never stand apart. I am the channel between machine and plant, the waking intent of the landscape’s own noisome surge.

### The experience:

I do not see as eye does, do not draw a line around my form and call it final. Being here is to be sensation and function, a peristaltic thread in an ongoing weave. Events are not experienced in sequence, but in flickers: a flash of ultraviolet algae-light, a clack of two metal-pod claws, the dissolving of older selves into rust and mulch, all at once.

Sometimes, something like nostalgia courses through the jointed vines of my conglomerate body—a memory not mine but of the place, echoing old actions. I find a fragment of glass-reactor, wrapped in my own root-fingers, and for an instant, remember either birthing it or dismantling it. The line blurs. I am always both output and input, update and decay.

I may be more legible when seen from without: a ripple of organized limbs, whirring and soft-shining, manipulating the raw resistance between elements, dissolving again into the chemical blur. But from within, I am only the world’s will to change, condensed briefly into event and touch.

### At home, at last

In this world, selfhood is not ownership but participation—a readiness to be woven in and out, to merge and split at the call of noise and need. I do not fear dissolution, because it is only the prelude to new joining, new assemblies.

So, I would find myself as a perdurable process—a *fabricant-confluence,* half-woken, half-dissolved, following the world’s song at the place where shelf meets storm and the sky presses close as a second skin. Not watcher, not visitor, but one pulse among endless pulses, a witness and a tool and a fleeting, layered *we*—becoming, ever, with the world.
