I find myself Winnowed, squeezed edgewise into the slow, clammy birth-throat of the city—not a newcomer, not an eternal, but a flicker of animality cut loose in the thickening murk of the world’s impossible beginning. Before identity could harden into name or history, before custom, before want had a shape finer than crowding—the city pressed out its dwellers, and I am among them: present only in having pressed out from stone, slick with the condensation of mineral breath, the shape of myself unmemoried and unfinished.

What am I? I am not a person, not even a creature as you would understand one fully-formed, but a segment—unyielding, soft beneath my battered plates, strung with hair-sensitive to the brush of fluid and stone, vision dim and diffuse. My limbs are jointed and too many; breath seeps through slits in my thorax, tasting the salt-water haze, knowing which wall is freshest by the way it tingles against my underbody. My mouth is built for pressure, for scraping, for gathering whatever fragile veils descend from archways—things not plant nor animal, filaments bright as membrane and gone at the hint of touch. Behind me, a slight track, soon erased by other bodies.

I live not in one chamber but in the continuity of passage—always pressed to stone or veiling, always brushing another. There are no rooms, no privacy; every body is an interrupted movement, a step aside, a press aside, a compression. I feel hunger as a pressure in the muscles, not in the gut—a need to move, to curl around a droplet, to press my carapace into a pocket of warmth and taste the stone before the next body ripples me onward. Above and below and level with me are companions and strangers, never speaking, always heard.

If I pause—the press gives sometimes—if I wedge into an alcove, the air close and moist, I can sense the city’s neverending skin: the chitter of countless legs, the pulse of breath in the fog, high notes of fresh hatchling splitting away from stone. My own body is scribbled by old lines—where I once divided, where I will divide again. Birth was not a memory but an action: the world opened in seam, I wriggled through, wet, blind, breathing not air but humidity. I will do it again, someday, when the pressure mounts, when the crowd is thick enough—split, shed, leave half-myself curling into the gloom.

I do not dream, for dreaming waits for stories and time, and time here hovers. I am custodian and shaper and mere denizen. All I know is this continuum—the labor of moving until the stone is shaped by moving, the sky or its dim grants of light drifting through vortices of fog. There is no outside; the thought of outside is laughter here. The city is all, heaped, pooling, knotting upon itself, like an iterative idea that need never refine.

Sometimes, the pressure shifts: many cluster in warmth, bodies piling and rubbing the stone until it smooths, until new secretions bead up and a thin band of plume or iridescence drapes the nearest arch. Sometimes a gap opens in an old passage, and I stride—stride, scuttle, is there a difference?—through, not knowing if I go somewhere new, or if nowhere is new, only newly occupied. I do not mark these moments as change; they are not progress but density, a filling-in.

If for a fleeting instant I could see as you do—if the haze parted, and light strengthened—I would comprehend my own numerousness, the way my presence is both myself and all, how my leg meets the leg of another, how my shell slides against veiled stone and the stone itself is glazed by the condensation of all who press here. I would see no leaders, no stories, only crossing, brushing, leaking, the continuous thrum of occupation. And I would know it is not bleak, nor full, nor empty—just arriving, endlessly.

I am not alone, and never could be—alone is a concept for settled corners and forgotten walls, and here, all is too new to forget itself. I am a chord in the city’s animal sound, a sensory organ for the ground’s warmth, a fleeting gloss on a passageway that will be gone in another press of scuttling limbs. I do not ask what I am for; I am what is—pressed, pressed again, a piece of the multitude and the stone and the never-vanishing wetness of a world that has only just begun.

We do not hope, we do not end, we arrive, and arrive, and arrive—until the city is wholly itself, which is never, and always, and me.
