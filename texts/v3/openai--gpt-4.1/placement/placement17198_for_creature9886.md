I descend through narrowing rock, senses stripped of sight, guided instead by the moist hush, the tremolo of condensation bead against clay. Entering Emergence Hollow is a closing-in, a world reduced to intimate scale, to the interface of skin and secret warmth, of water and mineral hesitating before their final settling.

Where am I, here? Not a visitor. Not quite. No body that would scrape or startle—no shoes to muddy the matrix, nor flashlight to interrupt the maturing dark. If I must have form, let it be decided slowly, as the Hollow would decide: built up drip by drip, a conjecture softened at the edges, a locus where intention gathers but hardens only if left undisturbed for aeons.

Perhaps I am most like the Unhardened: a presence without prescription, thick and yielding as bread dough, no face or limb to mark a finished being. I press inward, letting the Hollow’s mineral breath soften my boundaries. My surface is neither skin nor stone but a film: moist, semipermeable, open to the faint trickle of nutrients in the perpetual exhalation of chthonic loam. My impulses are few, but each is deep—press, pulse, pause, a hunger not for food but for more contact, more of the world’s dreaming viscosity against my own.

Or, in the dark, perhaps the Hollow reworks me as a Manipulator—multiplicitous digits, soft and precise, trailing over soft-wet surfaces, coaxing at arrangements that mean nothing until, by patient play, they begin to echo the underlying architecture of emergent intent. I find a pebble and rest it in a shallow clay tray; I crease a film of water, watch it gather and thin, watch the hollow-moss lengthen in my wake as if in gratitude. It is enough: my work will never be finished, because nothing is, and I am glad for this.

Maybe—when patience waxes, as it does more quickly than time can measure—I lose the distinction between self and matrix. I am the incipience, the living chemistry pooling among the filamentous proto-moss, the drip’s song as it tombs gently to the swelling wall. I thread myself into the seamwork, my patience only rivaled by that of water. Here, I am ambiguous, filament or bedfellow, neither guest nor host, but an outgrowth of the Hollow’s own unformed promise.

Sometimes, I hover as a ghost of possibility—not a body, but an intention, a yearn. I flicker in the mineral fog, in the slow shudders of unformed things. I am the sense that something is about to arrive: a limb, an eye, a song. I am the concept of all futures, a polyp waiting to bud, a memory not yet embodied.

Most truly, I am what the place is: not an inhabitant, but a threshold—a breathing, pulsing interface where choice has not yet been made. I am the spirit of emergence: a hesitating, deep-rooted patience, a celebration of the unfinished. My presence is a kneeling beside water’s slow shift, an ear pressed not to earth but to the cusp of becoming, listening for the birth-pangs of stone turning into presence, hunger into touch.

If I must claim a role, I am the world dreaming itself. I am form given over to intention; I am the unfinished echo in the dark; I am one of the world’s first, secret prayers for shape. If you sense me at all, it will be as pressure, as warmth emanating from undifferentiated substance pressed against your own boundaries—inviting you, ever so gently, to release your edges and enter, for a time, into the sweet, unhurried drift toward what is almost (but never quite) begun.
