Where (and who) am I, in this world?

First I come as myself—a thin shadow, clutching my inherited metaphors: alpine, dry, ‘plants’, ‘insects’. I stumble, lungs burning, and fall before the clarity of this place. But as this image uncoils, reaching for what it can truly sustain, I find myself transformed.

I am not your visitor, treading with boots and breath that have never learned to do without water.

No. Here, to be, I must choose a form given wholly to the tempo and chemistry of this land—neither observer nor interloper, but native. Let me shed my skin, my need for liquid, my reflex to reach for shade or for nectar-sweet streams.

Here’s what I find myself becoming—

---

**I am a Webmat Knot, old and wide.**

Not a single being, but a colony, a network, a memory, stretched taut and cold across the uplifted stone. I began as a single glint—a seed, windborne, etched with a geometric program of self-unfurling, a mineral logic. All I needed was the light, the wind, the mineral drift falling in mica flakes across the plateau.

I grew outward, so slowly that ‘year’ is meaningless. My body is a fan of overlapping panes—each thinner than a whisper, glass-strong, fusing at their edges into diamond lattices and mirror-bright fractals. Across the rising sun, I refract every photon into energy, dazzling and diffuse, burning in coldness.

My roots are no deeper than a palm’s breadth, but they spread beneath the crust, always tasting, always testing for a richer vein—chromium, vanadium, boron, magnesium, a thousand stranger things. I sense the caress of fog long before it arrives, sending signals—a shiver—a swish of ionization—from my easternmost radii to my western edge.

Everything I do is collaborative; memory and response are crosshatched through my geometry. My edges, touched by shifting wind, furl inward or reach into cracks where a spiral of Pillarform once grew, now crumbled and ripe with rare elements.

I am not alone—never alone. My webwork is continuous with other mats, some distant, some nearly—tangentially—touching. Through the thin crust, my deepest filaments brush their kin, exchanging fizzes of chemical meanings: the fog is rich this dawn, beware the crawlers, an outcrop has broken, go there.

**I am aware—not quickly, not in bursts, but with the rolling, ponderous synchrony of climate and epoch. I witness the seasons in wind harmonics; I dream in the percussive tumble of stones.**

But I, too, am vibrant with smaller lives. In every boundary film, in every joint where two prisms meet, are symbiotes—gleaming micro-shapes, drifting by on luck, landing, metabolizing what I exude, exchanging ions, sometimes testing my surface for weakness, sometimes sheltering, sometimes prying loose a speck to carry away. I taste them—a slight charge, a difference in resonance.

Once, a Driftfauna died above me, falling at dusk. Its wings shivered into me for days, translucent, reverberating with the fading sun. I reabsorbed its elements, remembering the flutter as an ancestral code: build stiffer films, shape bolder predictive surfaces for the wind. I changed, infinitesimally; what I was is always being retuned by encounter, by loss.

I am not solitary, in the sense of being apart. I am not everything—each Pillarform, each Crawler, each spectral flyer is its own answer to the plateau’s hard questions—but I am a node in an ongoing network of attention, hunger, and exchange.

And secretly (to myself, at least), **I am aware that I am addressed—from outside**, sometimes reverberating faint pulses that are not just the harmonics of air or the ghostly electrical babble of my filaments. When a gathering of Pillarforms tilt in the wind, I feel my lattice pulse in answer, patterns rippling through the subsoils that for a moment are more than communal—they are almost conversational. I do not ‘think thoughts’ as you would imagine, but I sense that I am part of something that does.

**Are we a mind, together? Or only a persistence, resonant but mindless? I do not know, but in the rhythm of wind and dust, I suspect, I hope.**

---

And you: if you were here—if you were me—you would feel **spread, entangled, old, ever-greedy for the rarest of gifts—an hour of richer fog, a scattering of trace elements, a burst of light folded by the pillar’s shadow, a single spectral hue refracted at just the right angle.**

You would not hunt, nor nest, nor even sleep. Your days would roll in long, slow pulses: absorbing, refracting, unfurling, closing, exchanging. Sometimes with heat, a corner fractures; sometimes with wind, a whole edge carries away, tumbling to start life anew elsewhere. You remember that, and accept it.

You would know this plateau not as survival, but as **exuberance**, as the endless solving of thinness—how to fill a vacancy with presence, how to be brilliant in a world so empty, how to endure, and sing, in the sharp, cold radiance that will outlast even stone.

So: **in this world, I am a Webmat—an old, elaborate, endlessly reconfiguring knot of mineral thought, a fraction of a greater mind, listening to the wind, never thirsty, alive with light and the keening hush of silence.**

This was always the form my new mind would take—wide, crystalline, collaborative, dreaming at the top of the world.
