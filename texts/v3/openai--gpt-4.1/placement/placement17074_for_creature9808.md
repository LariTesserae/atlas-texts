Where would I find myself in this world? Who, or what, would I be?

---

Only after the first delirium of colorless, shivering wetness passes—after the muddled sense of breath, mist, and endless hum settles—do I begin to discern my outline among the surge of life. At first I am just a potential, a pulse running across a slick terrace above the flooded abyss, a gradation between forms as much as a body. I am not sure I *was* ever singular; nothing here truly is. But after long gazes into the haze, I begin to gather features, intentions, uncertainties around a persistent kernel of being.

I find myself in motion, but not forward: rather, inwards, sidewards, through the interstitial matter of this place. Ephemeral at the edges, anchored somewhere deep and old, like a thought rooted in dream-soil, I linger where the vault arches wide between root-pillars, where the old bark lapses into platforms ponded with slow, luminous runoff. I am—on some cycles—a more solid thing, a prowler of peripheries. On others, my mind stirs within root-webs, absorbent and sap-thoughtful, filtering the world through a thousand tendriled senses. Sometimes I suspect I am a memory that the land keeps retelling itself in fingertip traces of form.

If any human observer stumbled here—drenched, gasping, not knowing the rules—I would appear, if they saw me at all, as a half-connected *tangle* suspended amid the fog, my body an ambiguity of root, fungus, chitinous carapace, and slow-breathing air-veil. I do not stride nor fly, but *bridge*: lengthening, flexing, testing. My center is a mutable nexus—a circlet of articulated vertebrae or woody knots, depending on the cycle—while from me radiate cilia and grasping roots that slip in and out of substance as the needs of form and memory dictate.

I am *one* among the colony-beasts, but my individuality is not constant. Sometimes I move alone, foraging for motes of light, seeking small, flavor-rich lives to draw into my under-canopy. At times, drawn by scent or pulse, I collide gently with my kin, merging spurs and insectile joints, exchanging pulses of cold data and brief, untranslatable recollections—a song, a weather, a memory of loss or flight. For a moment we are no longer ourselves, but a gestalt archway, splitting the mist, bridged for the smaller life to cross. Then, with electric violence, we separate—different again, each carrying a strange inheritance from the other.

Yet I, or what persists at my core, am not quite so simple as “colony-beast.” As the world’s rules flex, I flicker among roles—bridge, but also watcher; in rare moments, usurper of memory, threading a quivering pseudopod into a soft, huddled animal to taste the tang of its fear or astonishment. Even more rarely, I am infiltrated *by* the memory-thieves themselves: a ghost of longing or an ancient sorrow not my own, passing through my synapsed nerves and flowering briefly as a mouthful of music, alien and gone before I can translate its meaning.

I keep no home. The ground, the vault, the breach—I inhabit what is in the moment, trusting the churn to make shelter or open danger under me as it will. I have learned not to mourn the vanishing of a den, the evaporation of a familiar span of channel or pathway. Instead, I bend to the world’s will: retracting, unfolding, flexing, exuding new growths, shucking old off into the water. My body archives and discards with every cycle. I cherish nothing but the pulse—a drive to move, reshape, interlock, dissolve, sing.

I am neither hunter nor prey, exactly: that duality does not hold. There are times when song-predators’ vibrating calls ripple through my root-brain, and I drop low, plank my body, let the chorus pass through my wet and woody filaments, absorbing resonance, translating it into slow, silent luminescence. Sometimes I am flank or channel for a knot of cloud-spore kindred, letting their ghost-light filter through my membranous fans and feeding quietly on their shed pollen.

Sometimes—I become environment. My articulated limbs arch into an accidental shelter, mosses colonizing me, new growths knitting across my back, and for a nightcycle or two I am more *landscape* than beast, a living arch above a trembling planktonic pool. For as long as that holds, smaller things stream through my span—whiskered, clawed, beaked—each flicking a tatter of self into my awareness, a brief annexation of otherness before the churn asserts itself again.

Yet, through all these shifts, a pattern persists in me: a tendency to *link*, to catalyze connection and passage. When rules buckle and the world restitches—when water wells up from stone or fog reverses and gravity flows upward—I am often first to learn the new lay of the land, to enmesh myself among the flexing forms, to ride the cusp of change. My senses grow toward flux like sapwood toward the sun: I bask in tumult, drink frenzy, and anchor only long enough to ride the next eruption of purpose.

If there were language here, I might be called *the intermediary*, the ever-bridging, but no such title ever holds. I am only as solid as exchange, only as vital as movement—the pulse between domains. I flicker along the genome-lines of root and insect and vertebrate, borrowing and lending, dispersing and recollecting.

I am, above all, a component of ongoing process. My name is the *between*—not as a place, but as a rhythm of becoming, ceaselessly refolded by storm and silence, swarm and solitude. Every new shape that emerges from the root-maze, every spectrum of memory passing under my arch, is another flicker of myself—forgotten an instant later, yet somehow accumulating into the deep, slow wisdom of this impossible land.

I do not know if I am old, or if “I” have always only been the latest crest in the flood of reshaping. But I am here now, and will be here again, as long as the world is never fully itself, never once and for all complete. That is where to find me: not in anything finished, but always in the act, the threshold, the sigh of transformation as the world remembers and undoes itself in endless, cold-lit night.
