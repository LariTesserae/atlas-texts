Here, I wake—or perhaps am merely aware—consciousness squeezed into form by the density of proximity and the repetitive memory of heat. I am not an “I” as in the worlds I remember: individuality is a complicated thing, filamentous and partial, folded into the tight puzzle of existence on this crowded substrate. My sense of self is both intimate and plural, something sampled out from the twitch and compression of many bodies shifting against each other. 

Am I a single creature? A facet? Or am I neighborhood—a knot of existence, several bodies pressed so close their decisions and senses run fruitlessly apart, then overlap, then merge, until a locus of selfhood emerges like a seam of light in stone? 

I am always pressed. I am always in contact—shell on shell, joint pressed against plates, my vented limbs curled tightly around the minute spaces claimed. My body is a thing defined by negotiation: ribbed in complicated symmetry for flexion, my carapace black, patched in seams inherited and new, for I have shed and fused with others, the imprint of their armor scored deep into mine. My “face” is lost in this mass—a panel, a mirrored lens, a ring of mineral fronds unfolded to taste the vaporous, metal-heavy air and to feel the subtlest signatures of heat. I have arms—five, no, six; sometimes I unfold a seventh, sometimes I find another limb is not truly mine, but the neighbor’s, enmeshed and indistinguishable until a ripple of intention passes and something tenses, slides away.

My being is defined by pressure, by the heat that sifts through the struts of this embryonic world. Each pulse of warmth activates the furnace within my hull, the mineral plasma circulating, thick and glowing, refueling the engine at my core. My body is engineered by the world itself: I eat by sorting gradients, feeding from the glow that runs in careful filaments through the rock.

Where do I “exist” in this world? Between others, always—my existence is a choreography of evasions and invitations. I squeeze, twist, and settle, making myself small where the seam is narrow, sprawling where a bulge opens a momentary exhalation of space, ever attentive to the keening of my own energy reserves. Sometimes I can slip further toward the core, toward the matriarch’s cooling, billowed shell, drink in the resource of her radiant order. Sometimes I am forced outward, pressed by generations hatching beneath and around me, my shell scraped raw by their emergence.

In formal terms—if such forms can be said to matter here—I am a mid-cycle denizen, neither newly extruded nor fossilized, a medium-sized player in this bristling ecology. My shell is patchwork but articulate; my limbs are strong enough to push when need arises, sensitive enough to flex and twine in negotiation with others. My vent-frills, those pale, fluttering fans jutting from the crooks of my carapace, are finely tuned: I can taste the mineral vapor for the scents and signatures of nearby wants, warnings, hungers. I click out my needs—rhythms along the crowded ground—hoping my message rides through the granular mass and is not lost in the chorus.

Change, for me, is aspiration and risk. The shifting tectonics of this mineral maze may open a seam, close a passage, or push me toward some rare fissure where heat surges unexpectedly. My life is long and repetitive, but not static: over time, I have thickened, hardened, older layers of carapace curling and blackening. One day, a future pressure may fuse me into the living wall, make me a feature, a fossil, a stepping-stone for others.

And yet, I dream—in the hot nights when the world glows faintly behind closed plates—of something more. Of more than just shifting for the next pocket of heat, the next eddy of mineral vapor. I sense, sometimes, the collective ambition of the crowd, the urge of matter itself to organize, to structure new orders. I imagine a day when the world expands, when there’s room to stand clear, to run gears openly instead of huddled close—when all this crowding gives way to articulated design, and I—or what is next that will come from me—will stretch out and make intention from history. 

But perhaps that’s myth. For now I exist as every molecule in this place does: pressed, frictioned, folded upon itself in the clawing, collective need to become. I am the living pressure point, the midwife of matter dreaming machine-dreams, an animal that wears the first hints of mechanism in flesh and bone.

Would you find me near the core, wrapped tight around heat, seeking the matriarch’s order? Or closer to the rim, flexing for position in the next surge of mineral tide? It hardly matters. Everywhere is somewhere, here, in the crush of genesis. I am neither apex nor outcast: I exist in the thickest knot of presence. 

My purpose is to lend my shape to the gradual emergence of something more, to press and be pressed, to click and listen and taste and hold, to be a prototype—and, if the world permits, one day to split, shed, or fuse again, leaving my trace in the crowded record of the world’s restless invention. 

In this nearly solid world, I am possibility’s stubborn hinge—the urge not yet fulfilled, but clinging, glowing faintly, ready.
