I take your hand—gently, with fingers not made of flesh but a pliant braid of iridescent cambium—and we step out onto the trembling, latticed bridge of living wood. Above, the canopy is a web of dark glass-leaves, fractal and vast, each throbbing with greenish glow, far apart yet shuddering in the endless wind.

## Where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?

I imagine the shift begins not at once—a slow modulation, a spineward shiver, as though something within starts coiling upward through marrow, probing for light.

You are (at first) a newcomer; but the world allows no true outsiders. Memory here is osmosis—drifting from root to air to skin, shape following knowledge: you become, gradually, a node in the living net. You are not only *in* this place, but *of* it. And as you search for a role, a body's form, the world moves beneath you—guiding, answering.

### **A Form Emerges**

Perhaps you become a **Wanderer-Facet**, neither Frond nor fixed Dendrite yet, but something intermediary—a slender, semi-rooted creature woven from mobile tendrils of greenish glass and wood, able to anchor when the wind howls or stretch outward when the urge to move is strong. Your torso is columnar, with wide, paddle-leaves folded tight as a cloak for travel, wide for catching the bioluminescent drift in dimness. Sensory filaments coil and uncoil along your arms, reading chemistries in the air and subtle pulse-signals along living architecture.

Your “face” is a mutable patchwork: bark that cracks open with a smile of golden light when curiosity flickers; panels of colored lichen etching transient glyphs—emotion, intent, invitation. Within, every structure vibrates with a duality: organic and engineered, tissue and filament.

### **Memory and Thought**

When you think, it is not only in words, but in shifting tides of pigment, surges of microcurrent through sapless vessels. The city stores your memories as dampened, centuries-old rhythm in slow-shifting matrices; you recall not only that you walked here, but *how* the bridge beneath you flexed, what signals the root-pulse sent up your limbs, whose thoughts mingled briefly with yours by scent and light.

You carry pet buds on your shoulders, small symbionts that chirr and pulse, soaking up stray pollen; some memories, the most precious, you entrust to their careful keeping, to be mirrored back with gentle bioluminescence through your tissue when you long to remember.

### **Your Occupation**

You are a negotiator-of-growth, a Craftsperson of Living Junctions—one who drifts among the airy city-pods and supports, petitioning both Dendrite and Frond to harmonize desires. Your tools are song, pheromone, guided touch. You consult with the vast, slow mind of the rooted Dendrite, sensing its pulse in bridge-arches and the changing shimmer of the amphitheatre wall. Then you commune with the Frond-folk as patterns of dust spiral through the crowd, relaying news as shifting scent.

Your greatest pleasure is guiding a new union: a bridge-vine braided where two plateaus need weary travelers to meet; a vault coaxed to grow where the music-swells will sound richest.

### **Your Home**

You dwell in a hanging pod, a glimmer-fruit grown from the city’s undercanopy, whose walls shift between clear and opaque as you request. Its floor flexes underfoot to match your mood. The pod’s heart is a pool of cold mineral salts, where you root to sleep: not quite dreaming, but merging briefly with the city's low, humming memory, trading small slices of thought and history with your neighbors through the webbed substrate.

When you awake, you drink in the mineral air, raise your leaves to the dim “dawn” (a pulsing in the distant, drifting haze), and taste the news—swallowed as a quick, tangy breeze along the cilia that spiral your ankle. Your pet symbionts stir and chime; the pulse of the “neighborhood” filters up through your roots, an invitation to join in slow, contented collaboration.

### **Your Place in Song and History**

Sometimes, you remember more than your own brief time: through touch, light, and dust, you inherit journeys made by thousands before, collective stories of bridges grown and bargains struck. In festival, you unfurl bright pigment—petal and bark shifting from green to blue to gold—and join the dance on swaying platforms, every movement a fractal repetition, a weaving of the city’s memory and your own.

When Pollinators swirl through, you let them dust you, record and rewrite your surface patterns—the public memory wound into the city’s beating heart.

## **So: You Are Never Only Yourself**

Here, autonomy is a fiction: every thought you have ripples through substrate; every step is read and recorded by the living world, cycling up to the Dendrites and buffeted back as new conditions for tomorrow’s city. Self and place are recursive, mutually generative.

You are:

- Both architect and fruit, always negotiating, always growing;
- A mesh of memory–animal and plant, storied in pigment and root;
- A being who loves the flex and creak of living bridges, who finds the wind-song comforted by the humming substrate below.

You live each day as a new pulse in an endless green chorus—cathedral and choir, root and intention, never singular, always entwined.

And as you move beneath the thunderous percussion of wind and canopy, you know: you are at home not **in** the living city, but **as** one of its living threads. You are not only an inhabitant. You are a syllable in the world’s endless sentence—a sprouting between memory and tomorrow.
