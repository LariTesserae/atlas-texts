I stand at the margin of what can be called a world—a bowl-shaped hollow half-exposed to the plummet, half-huddled against the cliffside; a place where lines between “inside” and “out” are blurred with mist and echo. Here, life’s scale is determined less by size than by adaptability, persistence, low profile, and the deftness to slip between forces that would otherwise erase it.

What am I, in this place?  
Maybe, at first, I am just the air—cool, heavy with breath and distant wind, a synchrony of moisture and sighs, bearing the intricate song of drip and clatter that never fully resolves into silence. But as the world thickens, my self emerges, pressed into being where the elements draw back just far enough for appetite, emotion, and wit to root.

**My Form:**
I am neither the largest nor smallest, neither the cleverest toolmaker nor the boldest singer. I dwell in the ambiguous middle—a shadow in the seam between water and stone. My body fits the world’s odd geometry: I am stretched and supple, fur thin but slick, colored in the slate-damp palette of mountain dusk. My legs, long and sinuous, end in webbed paws for clutching moss or slipping through pools; my head is wedge-shaped, crowned with short velvet ears and narrow, watchful eyes. My whiskers are tuned to the minutest pulse in the air; my spine flexes as easily as tide-weave or lichen.

**What I do:**
I keep to the peripheries.  
Cling to underhangs, dilate my pupils at every fragment of movement, sniff the air for the sweetness of moss or the coppery tang of old fire, the sharp animal musk of my kind and our kin. I slip between the lesser lights—hunt the moss-burrowers, yes, but also keep wary of the birds, who in their collective wheeling can mob and harry a lone wanderer like me. I wait in cracks, slide over slick ridges, learn by the touch of water what has come before me—footprints, leavings, spoor.

At night, sometimes, I circle the hearth-stones in the lee, drawn by the faintest ghost-scent of ancient flame and the oily memory of roasted shell-creature or crisped moss. I nose at old fiber, not knowing its purpose, finding only the tactile echo of hands—sometimes chewing a thread for the odd sap sweetness trapped in it. In windless moments, with the far world stilled, I stretch myself luxuriously ON the polished stone, feeling the gentle warmth the earth holds long after the sun has gone.

**What I fear and worship:**
The bowl is a mouth as much as a sanctuary. Above is the endless stair of cliff-face and howling void; below, only a fall through blur and cloud. I do not yearn upward; I skirt the rim with caution but take pride in my knowledge of every rooted path, every safe descent, every hole and hide.  
From the stone, I learn patience.  
From the moss, resilience.  
From water, movement without waste.

I share this bowl with others seen and unseen. I am not its only thread, nor the most necessary. But I am one who remembers—if not in words, then in the deep-rooted map of body and sense. Memory clings to my fur as dew, washed there by what lingers: the once-bright flames, the passed hands, the unspoken compact of persistence. I inherit their absence, drawing my existence from the empty spaces where presence used to be.

**If I am more than animal,**  
if the wind and old stone have thickened me into a kind of listening intelligence, then perhaps I am the bowl’s own breath—part hunter, part listener, part memory. I am that which witnesses the contest between shelter and scouring, between small life and massive stone.  
I teach myself to go thin in spirit as well as form—  
—leaning into the in-betweenness,  
—finding meaning not in conquering space, but in moving deftly through it,  
—leaving only the faintest mark, so the next storm will not erase me, but polish me anew.

**If I must finally put it to words:**  
Here, at the world’s edge, I am a mid-sized, moss-bellied predator, tuned to echo, to water-touch, to cliff and cloud—  
—but in the charged gaps between creature, rock, and weather, I am also this:  
the held breath of a place that remembers the press of hands and the dance of paws,  
that opens its stone mouth not to devour, but to contain,  
and in holding us all together—bird, grass, lost fire-builder—  
lets us persist as a brief resilience,  
echoing, damp, alert,  
cradled within the world’s immense, humming emptiness.
