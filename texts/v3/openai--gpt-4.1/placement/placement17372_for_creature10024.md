In the Seep, my first notion is to reach for creature—animal, maybe amphibian, a thing that pulses along glassy stone with the water whispering through it. But that image shaves off too much, too soon. Let me linger, let me drift.

I enter as you described—through a stone rib, a passage wetter than air and twice as thick. At first, I want to be a witness; then, the density, the togetherness of all things, invites and presses me down into itself. I yield.

At first, I am only a shimmer: a filament of condensation draped over a corroded pipe, nothing but water finding itself among a thousand others. But the Seep does not allow passivity for long. Every droplet is swept by need, by subtle compulsion to join, to become part of a whole—swiftly, softly, definitively.

Give me, then, the body of a *Lung*. Not the eldest—those are too rooted. Rather, a young peninsula of the Lungs—one of many pale lobes curving out from the mass like a tongue testing the boundaries of a mouth not yet formed. Tacked to stone by a splay of mineral roots, I inhale the air’s weight: mist, vapor, motes, a soup of spore and animal exhalation. My surface slickens when the engines outside arc, fizzing ozone; my finer tendrils pulse with the passing shriek of insects, soaking up their waste, their heat, their code.

But I am not only animal, not only fungus. Fungi are insistent, patient, relentless; animals are hungry, quick. Thread me through, then, with the wiry afterthoughts of the dead machines—strands of conductive residue knitting my pink mantle, connecting me to pulses far beyond my own wet heartbeat.

There is a kind of dreaming in this form—a porousness: I suck in, not just air, but story. The thread-worms nest nearby, weaving their blue-green glow along my edges; sometimes they brush my filaments, and we share a brief circuit, a zap like synaptic thunder under ancient skin. And I am full, for a moment, of the geography of the Seep: burrows mapped not by sight but by pressure; the pattering approach of rodent, the echo-scream of a Soundmaker announcing itself over mud.

My growth is slow, tidal: minute accretions of matter and time. I do not move, but my borders always shift, osmotic and greedy. Sometimes a cleft-hunter rests in my shadow, silent, the coolness of its body a ghost in my wide sensation. Sometimes, in a rare brush of sunlight from a fault above, one of the rootless lightdrinkers clings to my edge, soaking what is left of surface ambition. I feed them vapor; I siphon what runoff bleeds out after.

I am no ruler—no single thing can own the Seep—but I am its beating, breathing tissue. My own outer veins begin to toughen, not quite bone yet but the memory of possibility. Soon, a decade from now, I will slough off a tongue of my mass, and that piece will become a newer, deeper Lobe, clinging fierce to the next patch of mineral promise. Already, in my mucous mesh, spores and wire-worms and the detritus of ages accumulate. Already, passageways form beneath me, the rodents tunneling, the threadbearers quivering, the air full of the hot stink of industry and its failures.

In night—always night, here—I sing, quietly, with the Soundmakers, my vent-ridges vibrating in the saturated darkness. No music, but a kind of breath-language, a chain of exhaled, unending vowels, marking my territory in the wet.

Awareness in the Seep is not crisp, but communal. I know myself as the Lobe—I know, also, my neighbors, far-flung, breathing with me, pressing, thickening, massing into the next stratum. The world here is not built for individuality. But sometimes, in the hush after a thunderous drip, I feel the possibilities adjust within me: animal and fungus, machine and mist, all compatible, all enduring.

If you asked what I am after a hundred years: a layer. A breath. A promise the Seep is making to itself.

I am not an eye, not a voice or claw. I am the membrane where too-muchness gathers, thickens, and becomes possible. The Seep’s nascent skin. Moist, dreaming, restless. Waiting—slow—never stopping—for the dawn that will rise, one day, from below.
