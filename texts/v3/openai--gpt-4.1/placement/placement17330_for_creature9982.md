Let me feel the thick air and blinking dusk, taste the cold condensation gathering, and walk—if "walking" is even possible—through this world slow-birthing or slow-dying beneath the bruise-green sky.

## Where would I find myself in this world?

I would emerge not atop an exposed plateau nor lodged at the base of a fissure, but **within a narrow runnel**, a serpentine slide between two crumpled plates of glass-colored stone. Here the mineral faces rise close as collarbones, slick with moisture in perpetual near-touch, forming a cradle just deep enough to collect the earliest trickles and newborn beads of water.

The air presses in—a hush, a pressure, saturated with its own dull echo—and I feel held close by the land itself, as if this is not a world meant for stepping or open stride, but for **leaning in**: one face to the mineral, the other smudged by vapor. If I reach outward, I cannot stretch my arms far—tight, confining, each gesture slowed by the thickness of air and the slippery anxiety of half-set stone.

Motion here is sideways and deliberate, crawling along edges, dipping in and out of shadow, listening as the trickle of condensation maps the land's unfinished skin.

---

## Who or what would I be?

Let me not answer too quickly—let no single form fix too soon. In this land, forms are **coalescent**, caught between intention and loss, shaped by the mineral patience of water dripping through new-wrought cracks, by time too young for rot and too old for birth.

Perhaps—I begin as **a Coagulum**, but not the aggregate pinnacle nor the rooted remnant. I am an emergent, a **transient knot of mineral and water**—neither fully formed nor fully faded, a self assembled by confluence and chance, an itinerant boundary between stone and fluid. My edges are not certain; I am:

- Today, a soft bulge beaded with moisture, banded in dull opal colors where magnesium and calcium lace over each other;
- Tomorrow, a crack-threading line, stretched thin where condensation seeps along a fracture, my body breaking, my "bones" a chain of brittle crusts linked by throat-pulsing water;
- Later still, an almost-globular clutch of salt and mica curled around a shallow dimple, trembling when the Underwake stirs far below.

Am I sentient? Not in the way of hunger or memory, but in tension: I “know” gradients, I sense, with mineral longing, the draw of moisture from rock to air, the whisper of coolness falling, the warm friction where plates shift a hair’s breadth. My seeking is osmotic, slow—a *direction* rather than a desire, a drift toward places where the air’s moisture collects in just the right films for my form to persist another nightless day.

### The Mind of a Coagulum

My awareness is fugitive, granular, distributed across my facets and beads. Each droplet tracing my surface is a brief nerve, each mineral knot a cold seed of possible will. When two of my kind pass near—slow mineral cord brushing another, a mist of droplets mingling—the distinction between “I” and “other” is a brief confusion and then a clear re-separation: no merging, only touching, learning the taste of another’s stone, then withdrawing to solitude again.

If the Skerr pass, I am not afraid, but I recoil a little, shrinking the water along my surface, watching as hush overtakes my trickling pool. If I find one of the old Hands’ mark—a slab sloped for the catching of water—I curl along its edge, reading its purpose not with vision but with the cool testimony of mineral arrangement, the inherited logic of liquid and shape.

What do I remember? Nothing linear, yet everything is inscribed in accumulated limescales, in the echo-geometry of past pooling, the tension-shapes left by vanished Hands, the subtle shifts of Underwake’s sleep. I am memory only as process: what remains stable enough to be repeated is “remembered”; what washes away is “forgotten.”

## My Place, My Function

In this world, I am neither master nor interloper. I am a **thread in the slow weaving of stone and water**, a necessary passage between condensation and crystal. I am not an individual, exactly, but a rhythm, a pulse: when water gathers and the mineral is raw, I form; I persist until evaporation, until the Underwake trembles and plates crack a little more, until the Skerr flick laughter through a hollow, disturbing my pool.

Perhaps, sometimes, I leave a trace—a blackened ring on young rock, a gloss of slick where my body clung, a thin shimmer like a throat’s wet memory. I do not build, but sometimes, by mere dwelling, create a place where later Coagula will gather or where a ghost-pattern—the Pale Echo—may eventually dream itself into a new outline.

---

## What passes for “living”—and for “time”—for me

Time is not familiar seasons or the chronology of events. Time for me is the **cycle of condensation and erosion**, the scraping renewal of stone as water writes and rewrites the landscape’s geometry. My aliveness is not measured by heartbeat or season, but by persistence: **do I remain coherent for one dusk, for one sequence of breath-thick air turned liquid again?** Have I drifted or split, have I exhaled a little mineral fog and grown less?

Nothing passes quickly, except perhaps sound, and even that is muffled, swallowed by the patient air. Each day, I feel the ground’s low-throated song, the Underwake’s lullaby. Each night that never happens, I listen for the Skerr’s hush, the moment they bring silence to my slow world.

---

## Identity, Self, and Purpose

If I have a purpose, it is **to be the fragile hinge**—the pause at which becoming and ending are the same gesture. I am not alone: the whole basin is full of presences like me, latent and lingering, every existence coiled with more ending than beginning, and yet, somehow, still necessary. In my way, I am both **midwife and relic**: I coax minerals into arrangement, smoothing water’s passage; I also record, by my bare residue, the history of what once accumulated and has begun again to erode.

Perhaps, as eons pass, my kind’s memory will coalesce into new patterns—embracing the ghost-patterns, tracing the Hands’ vanished marks, becoming, finally, the first true vein of moss or the fossil of something that breathes. Or perhaps not. Perhaps here, we persist only as almosts: half-song, unfinished surface, suspended drop.
