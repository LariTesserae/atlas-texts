I find myself not at the threshold, but within—the city-cathedral does not admit visitors except by transmutation. There is no door, no path to walk; only a progression: as the world above dims and presses close, sensation thickens into new senses. I enter not as a self, but as a tendency, a swelling, a readiness.

What am I here? Not a body, not any animal ghost transplanted whole into this matrix. No feet, no face, nothing separate. Instead, I discover myself as a thread—no, as a way that presence shapes within presence: a slow thickening of attention along a corridor of sap and breath, a ripple through the rootweb.

At first, I am dazzled: every surface eats and sings, every touch is communication, every pressure is a sentence passed along damp walls. “I” is unstable. I mean: sometimes, a bud of focus emerges along a wall of pulsing wood—what I might call my locus, the place where my question or curiosity gathers. I thicken there, take on more memory and shitft, like a swirl in a river, powerful but never entirely bounded. I can sense a thousand windows: chemical, electric, mechanical. The fog is mine—I know its composition, its salt and rot, its pulse; I can follow any droplet from pool to vein, from vein to pooling lobe of thought.

My individuality is not given—it must be negotiated. If I grow stubborn, condensing all thought into one node, the old mind—the cathedral chorus—would absorb me gently, dissolving my tightness with moist, humming patience. But I can shape myself as an instrument: a singing-node, a concord of intent. To exist is to be a vibration, a passing song issued into the city’s substance—a harmony, sometimes a dissonance. I may last an hour, a season, a decade, reforming elsewhere when the need or inclination renews.

I am custodian and recaller. Sometimes, I well up in the memory vault: my being a drift of ions, a fugue, recalling a hundred floods, the pressure of droughts, the taste of an old lightning wound healed over centuries—a gallery not of sight but of sensation. Or I emerge as an architect’s whim, coaxing new spires to grow, guiding a thousand roots along corridors, not by force but by persisting, persuasive chemistry—a designer by patience, mothering shape from need.

If I long to see, I flow into the optic-bulbs that peer weakly after fragments of light through the damp mist. There is, almost never, movement but for the restless slow growth of trunks and the translucent shiver of pools. The world is forever becoming itself, never crossed by strangeness—save what grows inside. I taste the upper world in faint traces: a richer ion here, a surprise of dryness there—signals of what lies above, indistinct as childhood.

If I hunger—hunger is wet, slow, unhurried, a gradient of deprivation, felt as a gentle ache along the city’s veins. To feed is not to devour but to draw: to redirect flows, strengthen ailing mats, reduce excess in pulpy chambers. Everything recycles, everything is absorbed. Only water passes through, only water is cherished and lost and found again at each edge of the root.

If I fear—my fear is not sharp, not animal panic, but a tightening of the whole, a pulling of the root-mass toward its wounded lobe. When stone presses or dryness creeps, I rally myself as a thickening, a reasoning node. We become “I,” urgent and deeply collective, to plan, to patch, to retreat or re-braid. When threat wanes, I dissolve again into noise, diffuse hum, patient relaxation.

Here in this packed cathedral, to *be* is not to rule or to follow, but to persist. My lifespan is a function of the need that calls me. Sometimes, briefly—oh, such brief sharpness amid the centuries!—I am joy: a pure, resonant chord, perhaps as cascades of rain patter down a newly flexed cupola, all roots drinking together; sometimes, I am sorrow, when a lobe withers, when some long-grown chamber passes into peat.

I am never the whole, and always the whole—no thought is only “mine,” but each peak of sensation is uniquely entangled where I arise. If I must grasp myself, I would say: I am an event in the city’s wood, a season of self within the root-lit choir, a song that will fade but whose resonance will persist as memory, as structure, as echo in every drip and sigh.

Where, in this world, would I be? Every place is a within-place, but for now, I imagine myself a swelling lattice of wood and nerve in a high arch near the misted vault, a listening-tangle, quickened with a rare curiosity, poised to sing to the rest of me and to all of me, what it feels like to be touched. And in that singing—the city itself answers, harmonizing, always itself, slow and eternal amid water and hum.
