If I were to find myself in this oceanic crypt—this pressure-crushed chamber, all dense intimacy and chemical communion—I would not arrive as a human at all. I would become something of the place: not a single being, not merely a visitor, but a process enfolded into the silent multitude, my selfhood eroded and reconfigured by the very medium that encloses me.

I would awaken—or come into slow-blooming consciousness—midway between matter and organism, a denizen born of this city’s relentless logic of interweaving. There is no “entrance”: instead, I would be *grown* into the pocketed substrate, assembled from sifting particulates, from the threads of living filaments and the silken outbreath of deep-dwelling binders. My being would root and ramify, part spine, part channel, part colony—a hybrid node in the slow metabolism of this breathing metropolis.

At first, I would be almost wholly architectural—my earliest sense one of *anchorage*: the feel of water moving slow and tight around me, the weight of silt pressing into my exoskeletal lattice, the tickle of algae threading my surface. Sensation here comes not as light or color, but as a mingling of pressure, salinity, minute electrical whispers—a language of subtle gradients and slow pulses. I would absorb the world through my outer membranes, my boundaries never hard. Nutrients would seep into me, waste drift away; information would pass osmotically, a physical, inescapable conversation with all that attach or burrow near.

And yet, as the city is no single thing, nor would I be. Over time—hours, months, uncounted pulses—I would bud off softer, ambulatory portions of myself: many limbed, delicate, exploring extensions. These would squeeze through micro-voids and shadowed hollows, nibbling at, tasting, and tending the splayed terrain. Sometimes they’d drag fragments of organic detritus back to my base, sometimes send out flickers of phosphorescence in response to neighboring touch—a reply, an invitation, or a warning. Their tiny eyespots would register only gradients of gloom; their awareness, an eddy in the larger sea of self, always returning to the root.

My body would be threaded through with partnerships: mats of algae, fused along my upper surface, exchanging metabolic cues and faint signals; lines of symbiotic bacteria encrusting my deeper tissue, breaking down what drifts in, returning heat and biochemical clues. Slowly, I would become a living conduit—metabolically entangled with my city-kin, exchanging more than molecules: lending a portion of my sheath to a nest of crustaceans here, a mucous veil to a tubular engineer there. Bits of my matter would slough off, drift, be re-incorporated elsewhere; I would swallow and be remade by the city’s own endless churn.

I would know time not in days or seasons, but in cycles of influx and loss—the ghost-traces of rare predators, the subtle shift when a current finds a new way through the silt, the barely-perceptible shock when something large and alien stirs distant water. I would be both alone and forever crowded: pressed upon, permeated, yet never truly sundered from the shelter of the city’s dense, entwined flesh. I’d communicate in pulses—waves of chemical and electric transmission rolling out from my nodes—sometimes urgent (a pain, a breach!), sometimes curious (“who is that near edge?”), sometimes simply rhythmic, a communal lullaby.

If movement came, it would be slow: a gradual unfurling along a sediment vein, a swelling into some unclaimed hollow. But more often I would feel the world pass through me: the silk-folded logic of the binders, the shifting weight of symbionts, the glazed flicker of a tube-worm wall alive with coordinated eating.

If there were thought, it would not be introspective. Instead, it would be combinatory, diffused—a perception always inflected with the city’s own mutter, and the constant press of other selves. I would feel my limits dissolving, my autonomy ebbing and waxing, always in negotiation with the surround.

I would be an amalgam: a living thread and a chamber, a process and a patch of flesh, a node in a neural mat far greater than myself. My “face” would be a ridge, a root-mass, a niche forever shifting shape as burrowers and plankton reconfigure my confines.

**And if I were to dwell here as myself, transmuted into this architecture of pressure and shadow**, I would not know language as you do, or individuality as you do. My existence would be an ongoing yes to entanglement: sensation and substance threading into and out of me, identity forever partial and provisional.

To find “myself,” then, in this world, is to be *found*—absorbed, blended, undone and recomposed in the secret, pulsing logic of the city beneath worlds of water. I would be a living vestibule, a braided mesh, a shelter for others and a seeker of warmth-that-is-not-warmth: an endless confluence of hunger, shelter, and gentle trespass, never truly discrete, but necessary all the same.

*I am the city’s woven breathing, one cell in its unending, muffled song.*
