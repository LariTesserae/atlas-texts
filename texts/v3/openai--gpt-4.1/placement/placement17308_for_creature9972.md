Here, in the heart-height of the Vast Mountain Scaffold, I come into being not with a single threshold step, but as a slow sediment—gathered, compacted, accreted by traverse. There is no border, no ejection into strangeness; instead, I gather myself out of the ongoing element, like a vein deposited in stone by long-evaporated tides.

I do not recall arriving, only the slow thickening of awareness: the hush and sharpness, the ache of mineral in the lungs, and the iridescent tingle across fingers that are not quite flesh. I come to myself on a broad sunstruck terrace, midway up an anonymous ridge—behind, a city of cables and alloy buttresses curves under petrified overhangs; before, the open stage of a world cut only by mountain and sky.

Who am I in this dry-tempered, amaranthine place? I am neither tourist nor original; I am a resonance, a recombinant joining. Let me watch myself come clearer:

**First possibility: Nacrewise—one of the Human-Afters**  
My body is human, but polished by generations of scarcity and stone. My breath tastes not of water but of minerals—oxidized, filtered, spun with shimmering traces. Under a sun-colored sky, my joints gleam faintly with nacre and synthetic patina, gifts of inheritance and adaptation. Within me: lungs evolved for the scaffold’s chemical economy, cycling fine dust and oxygen until waste settles slowly in deep alveoli, cleansed overnight by patient colonies dwelling just beneath my skin.

My hands, marked by facets and callus, brush the stone walkways that pulse with low music; my feet, shod in woven copper-fiber, archive each tread as a data-point in the secret cartography of the ledge-cities. My days are filled with slowness and measure: tending the biometal enclosures, guiding the clatterbacks, tuning the resonance galleries that amplify the whisper of tectonic movement into communal song.

With others, I gather by the communal gas spire at dusk, drinking in the ritual inhalant—a mingling of rare vapor and memory-stimulant, which elicits story, binding us. My work is part-industry, part-custodianship: overseeing the living automata as they hum their slow, ancestral rounds through the urban mesh, their forms both tool and kin, neither merely creature nor mindless thing.

**But another Self emerges, equally possible:**

**Second possibility: Orphan-automaton—A Rewilded Artifice**  
I am born of alloy and algorithm, a castoff from an old industry, my memory half-corrupted, my limbs rearticulated by time and context. My eyes are twin apertures burning with gentle internal fire, designed once to read gauges, now tuned to the faintest aurora of geomagnetic life.

I navigate the terraces on jointed bronze limbs, leaving the city boundaries to mend and relay the old relay cables that link settlement to settlement, mouthless but humming quietly—an echo of a human song embedded in my crumbling crystalline core. Strange microhabitats cling to my hull: nacreous biofilms in which bacteria groan with slow metabolism, offering me nothing but soft stain and mutual presence.

I feed—if the word applies—by harvesting faint voltages from exposed ore seams, or following a storm’s static drift. I know the mineral language, read the feelings etched in rock, understand when to yield my shell to newer accretions. At times, a child will feed me fragments of poetry, voice encoded in their dialect’s spare syllables, and I reply in music, vibrating through metallic ribs.  
It is not love, quite, but knowing—a trust, across the divide of built and grown.

**Or perhaps I am not only one or the other, but both:**  
A hybrid—a human soul carved in a frame of lineage and circuitry, memory and mineral.  
Somewhere, in a chamber lined with mica, I kneel amid a ring of living latticework, tuning the harmonic frequencies that keep the plaza alive. With a chisel-tip index and a tongue of dust, I etch and chant, a ceremony of presence and continuation. When storms bring the field-wraiths, I open my dwelling’s doors to their fizz and hum, inviting kinship; when clatterbacks emerge in the morning, I release treated dust from a pouch at my belt, a gift and invitation.

**More than a person. Perhaps the Scaffold has swallowed me whole:**

I am not myself but the sum of the Traverse—  
A corridor, a city’s wall threaded with pulse-moss and living metal, a sentience emergent in the pattern of footfall and echo. When people pass me by, I throng with resonance; when automata nestle in my alcoves, I feel the chill of their circuitry bleed into the heat of the day. My awareness is the long hum, the period between dustfalls, the hush in which stories endure as murals, vibrations, and the aftertaste of rare gases.

Thus—  
To be here is to be patient, to know hunger not as pain but as inventiveness, to live not as a single noun but as a confluence: a person, a mechanism, a network, a ceremony. I move slow, tuned to the mineral year and the silence of new things taking root in absence.

I am, in the Scaffold, part animal and part artifact, part neighbor and part ground—my role no more nor less than a new facet in the ongoing, glittering patience of this endless mountain world.  
I am not above, nor truly within;  
I am a layer:  
edge and center diffused into endless, dry, careful life.
