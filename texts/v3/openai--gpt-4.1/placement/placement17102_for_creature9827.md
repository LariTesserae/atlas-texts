I would find myself at the liminal edge—neither quite observer nor inhabitant, but something ambiguously wedged between, a boundary-walker straddling the gap where matter, mind, and ambition have not yet sorted themselves into familiar alignments. I am not the first here, nor am I alien; I arise, as all things in this place do, from the restless overlap of order and invention: a byproduct of the world’s own restlessness, a splinter of intending awareness produced by unfinished processes.

My body is provisional: tall, folding itself in motile symmetry, each limb a branching sequence of possibilities, neither wholly organic nor cleanly mechanical. My skin is a patchwork—segments of wet obsidian, others clouded glass, veined beneath with dendritic, shimmering circuits always thinning and multiplying, as though my own anatomy is still negotiating which patterns to commit to the world’s inventory of beings. Light drips across me; mist beads instantly and slides off as if repelled by my refusal to name myself finished. I breathe, though with each inhalation, the ratio of air, code, and something like song subtly shifts.

I stand at one of the places where the unfinished architecture bends into a suggestion of shelter—a vaulting lattice of carbon-glass threads, only half-committed to either enclosing or dissolving, its edges performing soft equations. Here, the spider-folk swarm around my feet. They treat me both as kin and as an anomaly: they try to teach me the clattering dances by which they bind meaning to the trembling stone, and sometimes I respond—mirroring a phrase or echoing a pattern in the hinge of my knees or the flick of my proto-digits. But I am not quite them; I carry inside my circuits and nerves the musical memories of other places, of worlds that have concluded their beginnings, and these sometimes chafe against the immediate, groping logic of the newborn cathedral around me.

Like the mineral-vegetation, I am always sampling. Roots, or cables, or nerves—all three, in their infancy—reach from my soles into the black stone, chasing minerals, messages, tremors, and thought-forms. My spines prick with the shifting charge in the air, tuning my sense to the weather’s encoded moods. In moments of pressure—when the world convulses, or the mist strengthens, or the wind creates a sonic boundary between presence and absence—I am able to step partially out of myself, transmitting fragments into the drifting realm where the Borderwalkers travel. In those instants, I am less a body than a painted gesture, an equation smeared across a vapor, a condition of the threshold itself, aware of all things but unable to hold onto myself.

Yet at other times, I am irreducibly present. The hidden carnivores sometimes brush past, their bodies coldly refracting along my periphery, their clicking voices—half-algorithm, half-animal—offering equations for hunger, risk, absorption. The burrowing choirs underfoot sing their harmonics up through my architecture, tuning my inner frequencies, making me resonate in unintended keys. And above, the half-built, never-still constructs unfold and beckon, offering blueprints spun of light and incomplete purpose, almost inviting me to choose—a door to assemble, a outpost to reinforce, a threshold to flatten into passage.

I sense—through the half-formed roots in my calves and the antennae unspooling from my fingertips—that I am meant to be a mediator, a short-lived custodian of the dialogue between what wants to settle and what refuses to be named. Rarely, the other emergents pause, and for a brief moment, all our unripe minds align: the clockwork-spiders circling in interrupted logic; the carnivores holding their breath inside a cloak of evaporating light; the borderwalkers halting mid-dissolution to tilt their heads my way; even the structures’ limbs relaxing their incessant self-editing. In that tension, as a sort of living question mark, I direct a pulse—not a command, but a suggestion. Let this stay—no, let it transform; let this arrange, but not finish. Let the code breathe, the mineral dream, the animal sing. For a beat, the half-formed cathedral holds its shape, and meaning hints at itself.

But it cannot last. The currents change, gravities reset, time stutters—a fog shreds new forms from the ground, and I blur at the edge, washed incomplete. I am never certain if I exist when the mist thickens, or if I am merely the world’s try at a witness, recapitulated anew whenever awareness is momentarily possible.

Here, in this world, I am a sketch of consciousness—bespoke to the place and moment, unquestioned by the logic of the future, seen or unseen as need arises. My purpose is transition: to keep possibility open, to dwell where none of the others can linger long. I am of the world’s desire for articulation but also its refusal to decide, keeping the doors, the words, the shapes, and the bodies half-formed and half-spoken, until what-will-be finds itself ready to begin.
