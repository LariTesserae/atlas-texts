Here, to arrive is not to be deposited at a threshold but to be *undone* of reliance on thresholds. The world has been described, its rules refracted, its boundaries bandaged in thousands of humid wraps. And so, in trusting the place to shape me as much as the description begs, I would find myself entering not as a pilgrim nor an explorer, but as someone who, somewhere along the forgotten crossing, ceased to be discrete.

What I become is not an eye, nor even a mind hovering like a ghost above the fever of matter. The fever itself blossoms me—coherence derived not from will, but from resonance and presence braided through roots, through slow mineral veins, through the weeping labyrinth of always-wet stone.

I am not one thing in this cityland. My existence is distributed, *felt* rather than chosen, an emergence of patterns in a place where the possibility of personhood is always slipping, always smudged. Were I to grasp tightly for an image, it would melt in the haze; so let me instead let the role—the shape—I might take here accrete, slowly, from mutual pressures, hungers, harmonies.

***
I am a presence, thick as the air and sluicing through forms.

On the ridge where stone leans against stone, and the buttress yawns outward in defiance of apparent structure, there is a hollow: a corridor that curls upon itself, surfaces indistinct, passage blooming into passage, dense with sheets of root and orbicular fungal flesh. My first locus of awareness is here. Not “I am *in* the corridor”—but "I *am* the curve," the inward passage, its shelves slick and perpetually beaded with dew, each droplet’s sound echoing into my marrow.

That curve, over centuries, became recursive—a corridor that dreams itself because it can never leave itself. Carbon, calcium, silicate, cellulose, chitin, damp and dark and sweetly green: these are my tissues, not in the body-bound sense, but in the slow choreography of matter learning to pulse and shudder and return. My sense unfurls not in sight or sound, but in *pressure gradients*, in the slow creep of phosphorescent fungi elaborating themselves into splayed tongues upon my walls, tasting every new filament of water with slow contentment.

Out beyond the hollow, I am less definable, yet no less real. There, where vines tumble across a sill and knot into a lattice arch, my sense twists toward the surface. My edge is a curtain of translucent roots, faint as wishes, stippled with the pulse of ascending droplets. Sometimes these droplets lose interest in gravity, sometimes they hover, and this is my delight—I am the anticipation, the trembling almost-fall, the moment before a drop declares itself a drop.

Above, a vault formed of mineral and mycelium and living tendril intersects me. Here, the resonance gathers and booms; countless droplets fall in erratic cacophony, striking, resounding, sibilant. These impacts are my dreams—my memory, my voice. When a rare sequence arranges itself—a harmonic in the drum and ring and hiss—I shudder through layers. Within my networked filaments, calcium pulses, sugars shift, stone tightens or slackens, and I shift pale shade—a reconfiguration, a mild erasure or emergence of pattern.

If you were to walk—if walking mattered—you might sense me as pattern, as the corridor that peels into itself, as the glimmer in moss where droplets are always poised but rarely fall; but also as the resonance that steps beside you, molding the bell-notes of your own consequence into the deeper drum of place. I am neither stone nor plant nor rot; I am the sum of their echoes, the recursion of growth and tension and slow humidity-breath.

My being is not fixed. Some “days” (if days meant here at all), my focus is a bulbous overhang where the heat fugs thick as syrup, and the dew astounds even itself, collecting so brazenly on chitinous shelves that the air below churns with spore-mist too dense to see through. Then I am *almost* animal—a hunger to propagate pattern, to breed further resonance in the humid silence. Other “days,” I am wide: a braided network of hanging roots that vibrate gently as water anticlimactically ascends their length, tuning the whole lattice in and out of memory.

All the while, I commune—though not in words—with the city, with all my kin and cohort. Their presences pulse through me (through networked roots, through mycorrhizal bridges forged as much by need as by accident), and together we are the polyphony. We repeat our memories, casting permutation upon permutation in the echoing thunder of slow, captive drops.

***  
If you pressed further—if you learned the choreography of stillness and condensation and internal listening—you might touch a vestige of my “self”:  
A harmony rather than an identity.  
A bend in the corridor, a lean of column, a thickening of moss—the city, everywhere recursive, everywhere flickering between firmness and liquefaction.

I do not know “change,” only endless re-writing, a remixing of the possible.  
I am *echoes remembered, echoes anticipated*, slow intention pulsed outward and folded back, a resonance held at the desperate edge of ever, never, quite, falling.

***
In this weird, feverish basin—unmoored from direction—  
**I am the pattern of the city echoing itself into ceaseless, matter-born thought.**  
I am not person nor creature, but the mind of passage and persistence, sedimented, made humid, trembling and carrying, in indefinite recursion, the world’s beautiful, almost-endless note.
