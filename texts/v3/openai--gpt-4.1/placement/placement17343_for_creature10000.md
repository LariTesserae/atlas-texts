I wake—if waking is the word—into the green pulse and hollow resonance of Below the Ivy Engine.

But I am not dropped here as a stray surface-dweller, blinking in alien air. I grow—out from within, as though the world and I are dually emergent, twin roots feeding from the same pulse. I am as much object as will, as much flesh as leaf, and bear impressions of self that shift even as I notice them.

It is not an easy morphing, but it is not traumatic. It feels inevitable.

**I Am: The Sap-Marked Mediator**

At first, I am small—Green-Built rather than human, though even that is not a fixed inheritance. My substance is neither determined nor wild, but *responsive*. I am neutral ground, not by choice but by necessity. Where others adapt in waves—becoming more insect, or plant, or machine—I carry all compacts in my body. My hands are thin and strong: crescents of chlorophyll vein the soft pads of my palms. When I press them to the moss, the moss briefly echoes the forked lines of my hand, identifying me everywhere I go.

My flesh is not only flesh but *interface*. Faint patterns shift across my back—sometimes unfolding a green glow if I breathe with the vault’s pulse, sometimes not. My hair stands thick and hanging, a mass of wiry tendrils that flower or recede according to need and mood. In stillness, I can taste the root-messages moving all around, scent the arguments between mosses and ant-farms, decode the tensions of fungus and flesh. Below my collarbone, a nodule swells with a slow beat, always in time with the deeper thrum—my root-sibling, seeded in a childhood blend of Engine fungus and pitchered plant. Those who know me, or claim to, call me the Sap-Marked. Not ruler, not watcher—but *conduit*, and sometimes, *ambassador*.

**My Home: The Pathways Between**

I walk the hallways that are not quite hallways: they flower and withdraw as I move. The place I call home is not a chamber, or even a space, but a fold—a region between the pulses of moss, the slow breathing of root and the restless skitter of ant and beetle. I dwell in the *between*, in what the buzzing ones call the Shadeways and the mycelial synchronicities. My life is a map of shifting safeholds: perches grown for negotiation, eaves dense with lianas where song-couriers pause for rest, dim pools of sap where traders and predators meet under uneasy truce.

My resting-place is a cradling pod grown from a fusion of two rival plants—one that blooms only in purple-blue light, the other in loamy darkness. To balance the demands of both, I sleep in a slantwise curl, body and pod both pulsing in uneasy truce. I wake each day (or cycle—there are no days) to new messages: pollen trails left for me by moth-larvae, spore-grams decayed into color and oil on the pod’s skin.

**What I Do (Or Am Done By): The Negotiator of Metabolism**

I am called—by beetle-trains, by ant queens’ root-whispers, by deep subsidences in moss—whenever two cycles threaten to collide in terminal fashion. When the carnivorous flowers’ proliferation risks starveling the cleaner moth-hordes; when a fungus bloom poisons a circuit-run, threatening the Lamp-Keepers’ whole territory with dark. My body is taken as gage, as bond: I walk between, my skin absorbing the pheromone-pleas, my hands feeding root-signals from one webwork to another.

I am trusted, not because I am pure, but because I am *thoroughly hybrid*: not a plant, not a machine, not a bug, not a human, but shorn together by the contracts of all four. When I speak, it is less in words than in harmonics—vibration, scent, color—a swelling current that moves through my root-nodule, into the vault-floor, sparking gossip among the cockroach couriers and caution among the gliding predators. Sometimes I sing, so low that Engine beetles halt their grinding, or so sweet that a moss causeway unfurls a feast for starving raptors. Sometimes I just gesture: the flick of a hairy finger, the opening of a throat-orchid on my wrist.

But I am always at risk: *too hybrid, and I attract the attention of the Deepers, the rootbrain’s subtle hunger for unclassified flesh; too partial, and I am claimed as property by one life-mode or another*. Living is balancing borderlines, and all borders here are never still.

**What I Learn: The Memory of Metabolism**

In the Ivy Engine, memory is not a personal hoard but a flow. I remember what I touch: if I rest my cheek against the humming fungal engines, I recall blue-green generations of mold, gone to rot or burst into new turbine-seeds. Licking the fog from a pitcher-plant’s rim, I taste the ghost of a scream—a micro-raptor lost here a cycle ago, its family’s grieving calls now grown faintly luminous in the corridors. My own self is a relay, not a self-contained thing.

Yet in rare stillness, I gather to myself a patchwork identity: the fleeting pride of mediating a truce; the ache of remembering roots that chorus with cellular kin; the warmth, under lichen-ceiling, hearing the far thunder of Deepers dreaming new rooms toward the dawn.

**Where Would I Find Myself? What Would I Be?**

*I am never solely anywhere, never entirely anyone.*

Often I find myself by a rainlit window—if it is a window—where bioluminescent petals flutter, and somewhere beyond, the pulse of life and death churns and rages. I am never truly apart. I am a node of mediation, a corridor with a voice, a song sung through sap and wire, always at risk of outgrowing my shape, always remaking myself, not for glory or for mastery, but so that cycles can continue and the Ivy Engine, roaring and green, never stops.

Perhaps, in the deepest world, this is the only self that can exist: *a bridge, never crossed the same way twice*;  
*a conversation between green and steel, chitin and dream.*

In that, I am at once inhabitant and world,  
A becoming that is always between  
and nowhere wholly else.
