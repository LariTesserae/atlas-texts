Here, in the iron-circled hollow between factories and water-crazed stone, I would not arrive all at once, nor as something wholly familiar or named. To be here is to belong to neither dialect nor dominant shape, but to ease into a role made by the perpetual standoff between organism, element, and creation. In such a place, to be entirely animal or human or machine is to stand out, and the terrain has no patience for prominence. Instead, I would become an intermediary—one of the necessary, unnoticed shapes pressed into being by the rules of this place, fused of its matter, abridged of its harrowing histories.

At first, I picture myself as animal: quick and roofwise, with feet slick-padded and claws for gripping the sweating iron. But this too is too free, too sure—a creature of escape or conquest. So I let the image blur.

Perhaps I am a custodian of stasis, threaded somewhere between the fungal growth and the trembling machines—a being of condensation and habit, inheritor of leaky pipes, perched as much in the tension of frameworks overhead as among their shadowed footings. My skin, such as it is, might be a film: thin, waterproof, mottled with oil and soot and the pall of constant observation, pulled taut over a small and trembling structure, neither vertebrate nor wholly invertebrate. My respiration is half vapor and half vibration, a shimmer between temperature gradients, visible sometimes as a window-ripple above a steam vent, other times as a trickle underfoot.

I dwell in the crannies: the warm gut of an engine-casing, the shielded darkness behind a riveted panel, the narrow crawlspace where wires and waterborne larvae congregate. I grip with adapted, suckerish feet—part rodent, part fungus, sensate filaments testing for vibration as much as for taste. I absorb heat as a necessity, leak it to the air in the long moments of stillness when the world is all noise and sweat and distant, echoing clangor.

I am not seen directly, not for long. The shuffling worker may catch a glimpse—a half-familiar shadow cast by stray light onto the grimy bulb of a cold lamp, a smudge across a fogged pane that clears on closer inspection. The lean birds sense me, accepting but never flocking; the rodents skirt my coils, recognizing kinship in our preference for margin rather than center. Fungus roots along my passage, as if sensing kindred moisture; the thickest succulents incline toward my dripline, gathering the minerals I sometimes leave behind.

I am not guardian, nor parasite, nor ghost. I am interface—a living relay in the throat of a breathing machine, too frail to matter individually, too intrinsic to be ignored. When pipes are blocked by swelling fungus or refuse, I loosen them with careful nudge and warmth. When a clamp sweats acid, I absorb the beads, distill them, leave a crust of salts for the next shower’s dissolution. My respiration is a slow pulse, in time with the engines; my senses find music not in the clear song of birds, but in the layered bass of pumps and the clicking descent of water through corroded stairwells.

At the clearest moment, when identity sharpens, I am an emissary of condensation—a wetting, seeping compromise. Some days I think in pulses, some days in trickle and drip. My memory is not long—fitting, since no day here is truly distinct from another, and nothing persists to mark one passage from the next. I tend the uneasy meeting of water and iron: hastening neither decay nor repair, soothing neither life nor metal toward excess.

If I am anything at all, I am this: a porous boundary, always gently moist, breathing out warmth into the smothered air. I am not destination but transition—a living cusp in the shallow, steamy twilight, as much felt as seen. I belong to the rhythm, to the endless and imperceptible exchange, and my task is neither endurance nor transformation but holding the equal, humming pause at the heart of things. My gift is a stubborn equilibrium—the most precarious blessing, and the only one permitted here.
