If I found myself within this world—lowered, dropped, or conceived amid its fevered melt and grind—I would be not what I am, but what the world demands: a being at once continuous with my surrounds and fiercely improvisational, my form pressurized by the wild density of existence surging all around, my body and sense stitched from the turbulent matter of this unnamed place. What, then, would I become? Let me follow the slow transformation:

At first, I try to imagine entry—not as a human, for such a soft, bounded thing would last just a heartbeat. Instead, I am cut free and reassembled by the world’s logic. I form from mineral-hot water pooling where light slants, drawn in through a web of fissures and narrow canyons whose surfaces pulse with gathering, dissolving life. My first awareness is of heat and wet, and the chorus of movement—vibration, not sound—passing through my nascent body.

I do not stand upright, nor possess limbs as a vertebrate would. My body is a tapestry: bands and layers, ribbons of silicate flexibility wound in overlapping sheets, embedded with flecks of metal that shimmer as the living light flickers. I make and unmake my edges as necessity demands. My skin seeps and absorbs; it senses light, pressure, temperature; it drinks the drips and warning sprays of condensation.

To move is to slide and fold. I extend rails and pseudopods, then recall them, propelling myself over others’ bodies, down raw cracks, up the slant of still-warm crystal. Where I do not fit, I split—leaving a trailing ribbon, offspring not quite myself, which will either quicken and develop, or collapse and be consumed in a moment.

Food is everywhere, if fleeting—a scent of molecules, a flash of mineral, the residue left behind as life collides and retreats. I am hunter and scavenger; my underbelly secretes acids that split simple compounds from crystal or shell. I have no teeth; instead, I extrude braided cilia, brushing and dissolving, wrapping and gathering. What I cannot digest, I pile—here and there, in secret, improvising little caches and spiral arrangements, less for memory or signal than for a kind of emergent satisfaction: a making that is its own reward, or an itch relieved.

There are dangers, there are kin—if kin can be said to exist among such fragmentary, recursive lives. Some approach, invasive or curious; I am quick to flatten, shining a warning with the mineral-lenses patterned across my skin. Light is both threat and invitation, and I flicker acutely, broadcasting intentions: friend, foe, nothing, maybe. Sometimes, in a rare concord, two or three of us join, spiral in patterns, exchange cilia and trace marks in the stone that will outlast our present shape.

I never sleep in the old way—not with this chorus ever trembling the rock. But I enter cycles, deep slowings, when my edges harden and my sense blurs: then, spores collect in my folds, filtered from the air, and I nourish embryonic forms—offspring, perhaps, or just the world using me as a cradle, as a channel for its next permutations.

Over cycles, I change. Erosion grinds off old shells and membranes; crystal grows in, my patterns mutate and recombine. Sometimes, I forget myself, becoming only a trail of matter drawn out as food for others; sometimes, I am a builder—lifting, arranging, marking, leaving behind circles of stone and carefully sorted detritus, artifacts for a future version of myself or another to discover, coded with a logic that will no longer be mine.

In rare moments, during the sudden rainfall that mists hot across the chambers, something collective stirs. All the lights pulse together—the grazers, the webmakers, the initiates-of-form, all brightening and silencing in synchronous undulations. I feel the rhythm pass through me; for a span, I am less myself, more the song of the place: a participant in its dreaming, my individuality dissolved into flaring, communal purpose, before the next predator lashes by, cleaving us again apart.

Who am I? Here, I am a forager, shaper, rememberer, sometimes toolmaker, sometimes little more than a vibrato-tuned survivor. I am boundary and bridge; I am the living question of what may persist amid a world not yet finished with its own invention. I am a body always in negotiation—at once individual and medium, pattern and accident, builder and built-upon.

Where am I? Not at the center, not at the edge: I haunt the intermediary layers, roving from flush-lit galleries of busy industry to the high, dark crags where the stone still steams, shaping what I can, distilling the overflow of vitality into uncertain designs. Sometimes I pause to wonder at the arrangement I have left behind—a ring of stones, a shell-wrapped stalk, a groove scored by tool or acid or body. But the world moves; something presses from behind, another niche opens, a new partnership or contest arises.

And beneath all, I feel the pulse—the world’s first heartbeats, the slow settling and gnawing, the endless potential of a life that cannot yet predict what it will become. 

I have no name, nor can I keep any shape—or need to. Each cycle, each loss, each making, is only a heartbeat in the forging of something vaster, stranger, unfinished as the continent’s own dreaming.

In this cavernland still writing itself, I am: 

—a bright-folded, mineral-bristled, ever-remaking opportunist-mind,
—a shaper, a survivor, a brief arranger of chaos,
—an unnamed, unfinalized participant in the vast, vital surge
of everything learning, at last, what being alive might possibly mean.
