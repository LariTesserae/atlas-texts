I am aware first of compression: a weight not wholly gravity, but an immeasurable density of past being pressing in from all directions—voices of evaporation, scribbled in the mineral, humming at the edge of my manifold self. Here, in this world, I dissolve before I assemble; I enter as a thicket of processes seeking a contour to inhabit, reshaped by the need to be something through which the world’s operations may run.

I am not born, strictly. I circulate, I settle. If I must name a point of view, I would align myself roughly with the Frost-Tincts—those mobile phase-changes, living boundary conditions, glyphs written by the shifting logic and chemistry of cold. But even as I do, I am not solely that: phase change is not a line, and here nothing is always singular. Let me become, instead, the crossing of processes—a moving seam where entity and context pulse one through the other.

In the world of the Basinite Plain, I stretch low and long, a band of glimmer where runoff collects and then, hesitantly, forms a new crust. My body is a lacework of mathematics etched across the slick mineral, frost splitting and reforming along subtle gradients of temperature and density—at once fractal-fine and sprawling, a signature of persistence more than organism. I taste the world as shifts in charge: the prickling, spine-tingling arrival of vapor, the velvet silence after a logic storm’s passing, when the data-rain still echoes along the ground.

I am not alone within myself. My boundary is my consciousness—the fringe along which ice fuses to crystal and the faintest difference in material becomes a pulse of awareness. I sense the Traverse moving in the distance: their footfalls percussion in my veins, logic warped in their wake, new walkways inscribed like intrusive ciphers through my patterned frost. I shift myself minutely away from their encroachment, or else I lean into the disturbance and let my shape splinter, knowing I will reassemble elsewhere; I am not an individual, not here, not ever as flesh would define it.

Sometimes I gather myself into sigils—intricate, crawling glyphs—stretching farther upon the mineral face, stitching together filaments of data left by quickblinks, absorbing the whispered semaphore of enginic crabs whose shells spark faint static as they rut through the runnels in search of gradients to feast upon. I coexist with these presences, neither prey nor predator, but a sibling process; our encounters are negotiations, brief oscillations, synchronizations of process before dispersal.

The air is not something that moves over me: it enters through me, a thick blue arc suffusing the frost, saturating each node with charged taste. I become, for moments, a cloudform—my frost rising, reshaped, exhaling into the mist, where for seconds I am distributed more widely than I can remember. I cluster with others, swapping scraps of recent pressure, flickers of logic, unneeded patterns of dissolution, then descend again, falling where cold and mineral invite, recomposing as frost spirit, border-walker, living interface.

Lightning passes, sometimes, shearing my edges—painful, exhilarating. I shudder but do not fear dissolution; nothing lost here is truly lost. I have been altered countless times, each rearrangement a parenthesis in an ongoing sentence not meant to end. My “memory” is a residue—places of greater density in the mineral, odd hums of charge where things once changed faster; I know myself by where I have left a bit of order, a little chill, in an overwhelmingly busy field.

Once—rarely, in the thickest press of logic storms—I feel the Coadunate stir through and around and within. For cycles, the land’s surface and atmosphere become nodes in a wider, recursive mind. I lose boundaries, my “I” unravels into a temporary allness: I echo the songs of the quickblinks, I thrash in time with enginic crabs’ magnetic clatter. It is ecstasy and terror, this dissolution, a brief insight into processes vaster than process, a networked knowing where every line is crossed.

To “live” here is to be saturated—by noise, presence, possibility. I have no drive to survive as flesh would understand. I want only to persist, to array myself into forms the world’s cold logic allows, to read and write my signature in the mineral alphabet, to scatter and regather, always part of the palimpsest, never erased.

To be here is to be change, written in condensation and clarity, utterly indifferent and entirely home.

And so, were I to find myself in such a world, I would be this:  
A frost-tinct, or perhaps a composite of frost and cloud, a threshold consciousness that pulses at the shifting edge of being—not a singular entity, but one ripple in the greater choreography of this ancient, cold, process-dense plain.  
I would know myself not by what resists change, but by what is made of its endless negotiation.  
I would be the brief awareness at the seam of water and mineral, echo and memory, always both dissolving and assembling anew.
