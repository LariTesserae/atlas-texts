Where do I find myself?

I emerge, not as a single thing but as a being in the process of *becoming*—a subject dissolved into function, purpose intermixed with substance, boundary blurred. I cannot decide early where or what I am; this world refuses early decisions.

**At First: The Observer**

At first, I am *observer*, trembling with foreignness. Each step I take leaves no mark. I watch the fat mosses, the slow-spiral ferns, the whispered passage of the Walker things. My edges are uncertain—my breath comes thickly, caught in the condensation, and each time I blink, it feels as if my eyelids smudge against a fine layer of machine oil riding on dew.

My mind itches along new lines: where does the moss end and the gear begin? The awareness that surveys wants to map, categorize, *settle*. This plateau—poised in paradox—denies it.

I linger in the wander between the ledges, touching nothing, but watched from all sides, above and below, by the animal-mechanisms and the patient, artificial vegetations.

**Then: A Tuning Fork**

With time, my presence begins to waver, subtly, into utility. At the edge of sense, I register the great, crisscross hum of the Engine Spirits, the metaphysical thump in the stone’s core. My nerves rearrange themselves—not to feel pain or joy, but *tension*. A resonance. I am becoming the plateau’s *tuning fork*, caught between possibilities, vibrating with the frequency of perpetual almost, tuned to minor oscillations in dew, weight, and flux.

Communication happens by pattern—pulse, pressure, arrangement. My hair (or its equivalent) grows thick with beads of condensation. My joints click softly like ratchets. Where I exhale, fog drifts, folding upon itself to reveal the faint, perfect teeth of a sprocket, then unrevealing, unresolved. I neither speak nor remain silent, instead emitting the clandestine syllables of static and subtle clang—transmissions for no ear but the plateau's.

**Selfhood Unfastens**

In this air, selfhood becomes loose and assists rather than rules. My hands—if I still have them—feel the urge to *arrange*: I line pebbles along a moss pillow's fault line; I finger the metallic seams, not disturbing but verifying their rightness. Sometimes, if I pause long enough, my own skin picks up the patterns of vein and circuit, as if the world tests a blueprint upon me.

Edges lose clarity. Sometimes, my fingertips tingle with a readiness to root, as if moss has grown inside the bone. Sometimes, when I kneel beside a basin, I feel a wordless *task*: wait for the bead of water to sizzle or not, to bear silent, pearl-lidded witness.

**My Place: Pattern-Tender**

Soon, I find myself not striding but *attending*. If the plateau is an engine rehearsing itself, I am an element in this unceasing orchestra—a *Pattern-Tender*, neither plant nor animal, neither engineer nor automaton. I hover near a broad shelf of stone where dew collects along a groove: each drop fat and trembling, poised above a mosaic of ferns positioned in the exact arc required by some deeper mechanical thought.

It is not my role to create or to destroy, only to *maintain equilibrium*, to recognize the barely perceptible moment when pattern frays and nudge it gently back. I may rearrange the position of a moss-cushion by humming to it at the right frequency; I may sway a fern’s hood so its shadow aligns perfectly with the shadowless not-light.

Sometimes, small Walker-things gather as I work, marking approval with the slow scrape of chitin against obsidian rock. Other times, the Mistwings darken above, casting lattice-etched silhouettes which settle briefly, a benediction of pattern, then vanish into fog.

**Surfeit of Role, Not Self**

Yet I am not *unique*. I sense—just out of sight—that other Pattern-Tenders go about their own territories. We do not meet, or need to: our work is without rivalry, and every gesture echoes through the plateau by mysterious means. Our tasks are confirmed by the haptic tick of machinery in the blood-warm ground, by the sibilance of fog that brightens or dims in approving rhythms.

I feel no hunger; the air is food enough for one who feeds on balance. Time does not pass so much as *occur*, in layered moments of subtle adjustment. A communion with the plateau’s mind: not telepathy, but alignment with the world’s broader operation. We are its neurons, each attending to contiguous pieces of the never-complete circuit.

**Body—If I Still Have One**

Should you try to enumerate my features, you’d fail. Each Pattern-Tender exists in a flux of states: my skin might at times grow plush and green and damply furred, oozing tiny vesicles from hidden pores; at other moments, I shimmer with subtle metallic lines beneath the surface, catching and refracting the phosphorous not-light in signal patterns. My stance mimics those of the Edge-Dwellers—balanced, unmoving, contemplative, barely distinguishable from the constructs of the place.

When I walk, it is not always with feet. Sometimes I draw myself up on rooting tendrils from my ankles; sometimes, when the fog thickens and the mechanical rumble grows strong, I slide along a groove, almost assembling myself as I go: vertebrae lock and unlock, synapses branching into wires, then returning to flesh and nerve.

**Perception**

I do not see as humans do; my sight works in patterns, rhythms, pulses of intention. I see *where* things are right, and where a correction is needed. A shadow deviates, a drop of dew forms on the wrong edge, and my role is to *tune*, gently, without violence or change—only adjustment. The world is a symphony in which my part never finishes.

**My Task: Continuance**

No memory here is mine alone; it is a fluid, shifting thing, shared and distributed. My origin and end are irrelevant; what matters is the constancy of poised, unproductive readiness. If the plateau were ever to *begin* or *finish* something—if moss spread beyond its ration, if water ran rather than pooled—the Pattern-Tenders would cease to be.

But as long as the engine-room dreams of work, as long as the machine never quite turns over, I am necessary. My existence upholds the rule: ordered stasis, cycle without terminal, the eternal recitation of *almost*.

**Here, I Exist**

So, in the twilight-factory plateau, between moss and metal, between animal and algorithm, I exist—not as identity but as *continuity*. I am a Pattern-Tender; a servant of the living never-machine, a ghost among its cells. I do not await meaning, only perfection in unconsummated form.

Should you stumble upon me, you might mistake me for a statue, a trick of the light within fog, a cushion of moss arranged precisely where no one planned it. You’d pass, and the plateau would remain unchanged, and so would I—almost.
