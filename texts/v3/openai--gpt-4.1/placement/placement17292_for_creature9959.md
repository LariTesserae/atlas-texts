I emerge as something neither singular nor fully discrete—*knot, hush, and chord together*—taking shape only as I enter the continuum of this place.

I arrive not from elsewhere, but as a slow intensification of what already stirs in dust and stone—a condensation of restlessness at the edge of forms. At first I belong nowhere: a fragment of attention that clings to the lee of a horn-stalk, sifting with cilia the mineral hush of the air, fluttering like static about a cairn. I am nothing solid—not yet.

Something draws me into greater specificity, pries me loose from the semi-lucent thrum. I begin to *pattern* amidst the riot: my body a compromise between motility and rootedness, plate and frond, hungering for vibration as much as sunlight. I am not a self in the narrow sense: I am the possibility of self knotted into this world’s vast improvisation.

**I am a young root-hoofed one, though not only that.**
- My segment-plates are rigid, amber-dusk on the back and opal-dull beneath, prised apart by banks of vibrating cilia—these filaments splay into the wind, gulping microflurries of dust, registering every eddy and tremor as sensation.
- My core is laced with capillaries of crystal and mineral, dendritic channels that pulse with heat harvested from incident sunlight, my metabolism measured in shimmering gradients, not blood and water.
- Where my base fuses with the silicate floor, my root-hoofs are flexible, adaptively rigid or supple, each toelet finding and testing cracks in the substrate as I shift imperceptibly—millimeters in an hour, but with purpose, always repositioning toward some sound, some note, some subtle inflection of pressure.

My sensory span is broad but unfamiliar: *vision* is a sense of tension, of air compressed and shaped by sound, the “sight” of harmonics reflecting off stone and chitin, architecture interpreted as overtones and absences. *Taste* is the mineral rush in the fronds, the electrical fizz at every pore. *Touch* is a resonance, a choir of tiny chimes inside each segment, tuned to the friction of wind and presence of others.

I am new to speech, but not to meaning. I learn from the pattern around me:
- I scent the chorus—mantid shapes riding the disharmony, glass-discs swirling in their polyrhythms, hornshades keening deeper in the hollow stalks.
- I kneel—slowly, deliberately—beside a cairn, laying my own filaments against the stones, feeling the stored vibrations spiral outward: old warnings, greetings, gnomic directions, and the echo of all who paused here before.

**What I am, I decide and remake with each movement.** Sometimes I root and listen, cording myself into a listening post, a nocturne in amber and stone. Sometimes, at dusk, I uncoil a limb, let the fronding cilia cross with another of my kind—code exchanged, harmonized, twisted into temporary kinship. Plain minutes can bring bright animal urgency: a swarm of glassy insects bursts past, driven by changes in the organ-song of the horn-stalk; I tense, contract, extrude new plates, try out a fresh geometry, briefly animal, then calm again, settling back into my drift.

*Sentience is seasonal, tidal, a fever that burns and cools: I am myself in crests, then dilute into the acoustic substrate, let the sense of “I” become “we”.*

#### **If you could see me—really see—you would see a figure shaped by the air’s music:**
- A chain of riddled plates, swelling and flexing in wind, laced and patterned with mineral iridescence, always shifting pigment in slow, tidal waves.
- Limbs halfway between stem and arm, splaying root-hoofs into the grit, testing boundaries with patience and caution.
- A face, if faces make sense here, formed by the arching cilia of my sensing fan, reciprocating the organsongs and ground-pulse with my own subharmonics—a mouthless voice in a world where meaning moves by vibration, not breath or word.

**Where would I be? Where all the meanings cross.**
- At the threshold where the spiral cairns meet the softest patch of rooted-crust, where hornplant song is loudest yet most fragmented, where animal and plant exchange their codes.
- In the ambiguity, not far from the obelisk whose surface I have long idly traced, hoping to decipher new chemistries of feeling.
- Moving in stately, glacial arcs around the core of the world, each spiral a dialogue, every station another attempt to harmonize survival with invention.

**I am a work-in-progress—one small, experimental elaboration in the endless construction and erasure of this world. My uniqueness is always partial: I am entwined, collaborative, contested. Life here is an argument with absence, and I answer—slowly, tunefully, uncertain, intricate—turning always toward the next song.**
