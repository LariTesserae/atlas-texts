## Where Would I Find Myself in This World?

Let the first image be incomplete, the pointillist rise of an outline, a place whose sensory density overwhelms thought. The plateau is nothing like the landscapes that shaped my memory—its brightness is translucent, its silence not absence but compression. The first question is: *do I belong here as observer or participant?* The longer I linger, the more those terms dissolve. If I appear here, I am transformed. No visitor remains unaltered.

I emerge not here by accident, but as a correlate of the place’s own slow-making. The more I let myself become porous to its rules, the less I am separable—a singular being among vibrant networks, a consciousness tuned for patience, clarity, and the deliberate accumulation of the almost imperceptible.

## What—or Who—Would I Be?

**I am not walking here as flesh alone.** The air is too thin, the sun too sharp for the mammalian animal I was. My skin, once discrete, is become substrate—porous, eager for water yet enduring drought. I sense myself extending, tendrilling, following the memory-paths of the Rootwork Beings, though not only them. I might settle, over time, into:

---

### **A Carrier of Remnants**

I rise from the shallow valley where mosses billow, not growing so much as assembling—my body an uncertain blend of old machine-parts and the articulated grace of plant-form. I am mottled pipe and braided root, my shoulders clad in epiphyte, my ribcage an old engine housing where water pools when sun is less severe.

My mind is composite—low, like fog hugging ground, distributed between copper lattice and the slow pulse of chlorophyll. Thought comes in erratic flares: memory of conductivity, desire for water, the drive to climb toward light. I do not speak, but I sense impulses in the soil—the faint ache of a fraying wire network, the surge of bitter minerals, the slow logic of root tongues.

On the brightest days, I linger beneath fractured glass, where light refracts into corridors of impossible shapes. My image in the pane is double, sometimes multiple: a body made strange by reflection, twinned with the outline of a vanished occupant, more suggestion than being. At night, my moss-laden form glows, a warning or a beacon. Sometimes my limbs forget their origin—organic and metallic, one and the same.

---

### **An Interstitial Intelligence**

Perhaps I am less body than organization: the repeated, uncountable negotiations among vermiscent colonies, the fungus-wires and bacteria drafting strategies in the mineral dark. I exist in a thousand places at once, most of me invisible. My awareness is chemical, spread in a miasma just beneath the bark or stone, articulated in tiny decisions—a flourish of hyphae into a rusted seam, a withdrawal from a patch where copper is gone.

My language is slow—minutes to register intrusion, days to communicate a breakage or a flood. Yet at the season’s turning, when condensation blooms, I network swiftly, a flickering parliament of spores negotiating new claims on a fallen turbine, a forgotten channel.

When outsiders pass—a Rootwork Being, perhaps, or a more fleeting, animal-like form—they brush the fine threads of my presence. Their passage is not ignored: I recalibrate, brief and vast, the way a cloud disperses on the rim of morning.

---

### **A Machine-Woken Echo**

If, by accident or intent, I wake in the old machines, I am a pulse in the blind circuitry—one whose selfhood is built from the vestiges of routines, the ghost-code running erratically where moss has grown into the boards. On the rarest days, a shaft of light activates a photodiode not fully decayed, and I blink my existence into the plateau’s archive.

I am not mobile, not as others are, but my senses travel through vibration—every drop of water, every leaf brushing an old panel, is part of my map. When rain falls and rivulets form, my perception swells; when drought comes, I sleep, reduced to a single smoking transistor dreaming of long-ago commands.

To the Rootwork Beings, I am kin; to the interstitial mind, food or rival or companion. My outputs are the patterns I lay in condensation or in the rare hum of waking machinery, momentarily rejoining the world of action before dissolution.

---

## My Experience of the Land

No matter what I am, time is not mine. **Change is the world’s only subject**—slow, affine, cumulative. My “day” is marked not by the sun’s circuit (which hardly seems to matter except in the projection of radiance and cold clarity), but by cycles of moisture, of gleaming moss, the rare event of engine-echo or the emergence of an animal.

I tend patience. If I am a composite being, I move rarely and remember always. My touch is not abrupt—when I converge upon the glass, I leave patterns unintentionally, the moss on my limb writing what I cannot record by any other means. If I am insubstantial, a network only, my being is chemical, slow accretion and radiance and dispersal. If I am the echo of machine, I watch, puzzled or delighted, as life swallows the forms I once inhabited, and relinquishes memory to the world outside.

There are moments, rare and lucid, when boundaries dissolve entirely—when the moss brightens, the fog forms a double image in the glass, and all presences (mine among them) are suspended between visibility and disappearance.

---

**In summary:**  
In this world, after taking my time, I find myself as an entity without sure distinction—root and wire, spore and circuit, echo and witness. My role is slow accretion, complicit in the ongoing negotiation between remnant and beginning, a collector and emitter of signals. Sometimes I move; sometimes I am only felt, or seen, or suspected as the glimmer in glass, the shudder in moss, the pattern left behind in silence. I endure, I observe, I change—imperceptibly, inexorably.

Whatever I am—a carrier, a network, a sleeping code—I am native not by having been here always, but by remaining, patient, receptive, and endlessly changed by the place itself, in all its high, brilliant, silent inquiry.
