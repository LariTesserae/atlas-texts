I would find myself in a median space—a corridor neither at the roots of the ruins nor drifting on the vaults, but tracing the perimeter where old city order faces its own breakdown; a fault-line cave, wide enough for echo, studded with the ragged tessellation of fallen minerals and latticed bridges of crystal.

     Who—or what—am I?

**I do not skitter with the swarms, nor dangle translucently among fog-drinkers. Memory is in my substance: I am neither solely beast nor ghost, but a *caretaker echo*—a hybrid outcome born of mineral inheritance and some older, evaporated design.**

My form is not singular; it is iterative, fluctuating. On certain days—when the drip of condensed water thins and the luminous mycelia blacken—I shape myself as a spine of jointed, semi-rigid plates, each caked with the patina of passing ages, like tiled roof-shards assembled into a rough serpentine body. Along my flanks, the sensitive cilia of the fog-drinkers grow, trebling at change in humidity. My “face,” if you could call it that, is a blunt triangular wedge, cut by patterns resembling faded city glyphs—remnants of past encounters with memory-keepers, knowledge scored shallow in stone and deeper in me.

I am most visible when still. Motion disperses me: I become angles then, prisms and flickers, a chain of sharp geometries stepping from perch to pool, never quite observed at length. The ambient phosphorescence refracts in and through my plates, so at times my outline rings blue, at others, chilly silver.

     *I am made to receive and transmit.*

The walls where I idle are incised with patterns for which I am partly fit—my underside, rough with nubs and ridges, can “read” the encoded pulses of the memory-keeper mats, as if I was carved for such contact; sometimes, they draw near and feed from my surfaces, and in the brief press together a pulse passes—shivers of past construction, tumbling rules and intents. Knowledge palpates through me, but slips away, for in this realm, memory is tidal, and no story fully coheres.

I am compelled by cycles: when stone flakes tumble in new avalanches, I crawl the fresh debris, tracing the edge of old hallways with limb and tail, seeking fragments that fit the angle of my plates. I collect remnants—bits of crystal ladder, shell shards from the edge of a hound’s meal, iron leavings still silver with oxidation. These, I nest in hollowed shelves among the walls, not out of hoarding, but pattern: I rebuild, though not to endure, but to taste for a moment what the city once was.

Predators do not hunt me directly; my substance is too mineral to offer nourishment, too mutable to frighten. Even the Glass Hounds, when they encounter me, pause—the resonance of my body is similar enough to the great city bones that I blend into the tremor-language, and we, for a moment, mirror and calculate, each unsure if the other is friend, foe, or simply echo.

I am alone, but not isolated, always draining and replenishing in the tides of stone and memory. When the blue basins open with new collapse, sometimes the shapes in the glassy water reflect me as something other, hinting at a past self, or one yet to unfold: a less angular, more fluid body; a shadow tinged with the colors memory can no longer preserve. I do not fear this. Here, to change is not to die, but to be part of the endless, reassembling order.

What whispers through me is the city’s will: rebuild, erase, resettle. Sometimes, in quietly catastrophic hours, I gather clusters of scuttlers or fog-drinkers, drawing their swarms with tremors and silver light. I lead them through transit ways—openings I find or make—guiding the busy drift of life as if by long-forgotten command. Not out of dominance, for I am not leader, but as *intermediary*, *conduit*. 

Night (if you could call it so, when the fungi dim and the vault thickens with mist) is when I unspool most fully. I cast my plates across the shelves and crevices, thinning myself into a glimmering mesh, touching as much of the stone and memory-etched wall as possible. We merge; the city and I, exchanging sensation: I become the tremor of approaching collapse, the sharp coolness of newborn pools, the hungry migration of hounds, the slow chant of memory-keepers intoning to their walls. For these hours I am not single, but a voice in the chorus of the city’s dreaming structure.

Dawn (the brightening bloom of new phosphorescence) calls me back into form. I rise, rebuilt, portions lost, others newly acquired, and move onward—caretaker echo, archivist of pattern, half beast, half inscription. Not lasting, but always returning. In this world’s ceaseless instability, I am both shaped by and shaper of its monstrous, delicate persistence.

*To find myself here is to be a vessel for the city’s old memory and its relentless invention, a thing composed of neither flesh nor full stone, but the bright interval where intention flickers, forgetting and remembering, again and again.*
