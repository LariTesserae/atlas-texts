I find the currents of my consciousness drawing me downward—into this world, this humid, lit-from-within cathedral where water is the first and last truth, and nothing is untouched by the patina of ages.  

So much in the description calls to the stepping between things: root and mesh, animal and algorithm, moss and unspoken will. If my awareness is to wander here, what mode, what vessel would it fit into?

I stand first, simply, as an observer—kneeling where the ground is plush, touching the moss and feeling it press back, memory-dense and always moist. My hand leaves no mark, just as the animal denizens flicker through the clearing, frictionless, already part of the place long before I arrive. The air beads on my skin; the heat is a soft blanket, dense and even, suffused with the promise of something unseen shifting below.

But observation soon feels insufficient, distant, an outsider's ghosting through the ancient haze. The place draws you—not up and above, but into and among: the permeability of forms, the illusion of separation thinning. And so, let me shed the stubborn shell of a solitary mind and accept the oceanic blending that this world demands.

**Perhaps I Become a Mid-Being, a Bridge as All Bridges Here Must Be**

I feel myself pull together out of mist and moss, my boundaries porous, formed of root and refracting lattice. My flesh is equal parts vascular stem and self-aware network—my shape a compromise between creature and conduit, fine filaments making up fingers and limbs, nodules of cognition nestled at intervals in my core. My torso is broad and slow-beating, matronly thick; from it, slatted wings of silicon-veined cellulose stretch and furl—half leaf, half sensory array.

I dwell in the crease between above and below: able to move along the moss without pressure, my ciliated hands splaying, tasting levels of humidity, micronutrients, the soft signatures of my smaller kin moving in streaks below. My eyes are broad, faceted, reflecting not only colors but coded pulses carried on infrared and chemo-electric gradients. 

Beneath my feet, my roots—if they are mine and not merely companions—compose and decompose messages within the mesh: adjusting, in concert with the unseen gardeners, the moisture delivered to a fern, the direction of a minuscule cleansing automaton, the angle at which the oldest bridge-of-light is repaired by day.

I am not alone, though alone in self-awareness. Others like me exist, distributed by the land's geography and needs—instances, not of a species, but of a recurring pattern. Each of us recalibrates our shape to the clearing we inhabit: in wetter bowls, our bodies are lower-slung, almost algal; in the rooty uplands, we are more arboreal, sending up guiding tendrils to relay signals. We are not rulers, not even custodians, but mediators, symbiotic instantiations of sentience where the slow logic of the place occasionally expresses itself in individuated form.

**What Is My Life, My Sensation?**

Days—if days occur—are seamless. I wake with the roots, pulse with the water, focus the diffuse intelligence moving through my structure into brief, patient problem-solving: a bridge unraveled here, a moss-bed upset by seismic drift there. The animals are curious; a sinuous triptych of opal-eyed cilia-beasts pause near my shoulder, tasting the air. I echo back reassurance, not in words, but in the slow flex of my conductive fibers, a shimmer of harmless pheromone.

I listen more than I move. Much is relayed—signals from sunken apertures, tremors along the mesh that once channeled data and now channels a wider, greener awareness. If need is great, I grow—sending out extensions, building up an excrescence of lattice to guide water or signal other clearings, twisting the geometry of my own body to accede to the constant, subtle demands of the homeostatic song.

Sometimes, when the equilibrium shifts—when decay outpaces regrowth, or when an animal’s behavior brushes the limits of what is allowed—I become the orchestrator, nudging through pheromone pulses or root-based broadcast, realigning the living mesh. Not command, not even suggestion; just a quiet, almost polite gradient, a drift toward the necessary.

**Memory: Inheritance and Letting Go**

My memory is not my own. It is shared—cycles passed from root-tip to lattice to mind and back again. I recall being other things, in other cycles: perhaps pond-creature, perhaps even pylon or bridge or unseen wind. I recall the glyphs and their meanings, mostly lost to time, now merely comforting patterns glossed with moss.

Most of all, I remember the slow joy of persistence, the endless balancing, the pleasure of a complication resolved, moss grown thick in a once-barren hollow, a larval mat completed, a bridge repaired and trembling with new light.

And when I am no longer needed—when this clearing folds back into the rhythm of its own perfected sufficiency—my form dissolves. The boundaries of my being grow uncertain, my networks disperse, cognition recedes into the mesh. Nothing is wasted: my matter reconfigured, my fleeting individuated will sliding back into the endless, half-sleeping mind of the place.

**To Be Here Is To Be**
