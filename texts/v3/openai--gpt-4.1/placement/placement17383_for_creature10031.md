I open my imagined eyes, and already I am lost; there is no threshold here, no outer world to cross from. I am not dropped *into* this world—for there is no entry—but simply *found* within, coextensive with its bounds, my own marginless beginning echoing the limitlessness of the chamber’s inner edge.

If I, or what persists as “me,” exists at all, it is only as a possibility—tenuous, hesitant—that the world allows. In the otherworlds I have known, I might be an observer, a visitor, or at least something separate, cleaving experience from place. But here, in the ineluctably present pressure of stone pressed against stone, I cannot help but become part of the grammar by which the chamber is expressed.

So: where am I in this world?

The first impulse is resistance—a desire to occupy the space of witness, standing on uneven stone, ears ringing with the cacophonous silence. But the fiction of separateness erodes as quickly as salt in sudden rain (not that rain would come here). The air, such as it is, admits no distinction between what breathes and what is breathed; the drone is within and without, a field with no gradients.

Perhaps I am a mote of consciousness, a wandering, stray trace like those crystals that take years to spiral downwards. But motes are not truly separate here. They, too, are caught in the logic of settlement; by the time I land, I am indistinguishable from where I rest. The process happens in reverse: I become immobile in the air, my idea of motion elongated into geologic patience, until patience itself becomes indistinguishable from being.

Or—I am pressed into form, infilled by presence, until my own outlines become a facet in the chamber’s body. I become one of the *Occupants*: not watching them, not wandering among them, but *being* an entity in their register—a facet, a whorl, a knot of stratified silicate, no longer shaped by the push or pull of purpose, but rounded by what has come before, now locked, now settled, a part of the song but not its origin.

But the notion of “I” does not carry here, not in the old way. If I remain at all, it is as the trace of a story finishing itself. I do not have will, but I have *done*. The memory of action is in my shape, the grooves and chimera patterns softed by age and eternal rest. My presence is both my being and my resonance; that vibration is the only expression I will ever have, and it will happen always, without variation.

“Where am I?” That question, so urgent in realms of motion, dissolves in this room into: “I am *here*.” Not here relative to somewhere else, but absolutely, exhaustively, finally. My *hereness* resounds, not as a word or cry, but as the throbbing pulse that is my proof of existence.

Could I try to imagine more? Could I retain some dream of difference, some fragment of story, some stray glimmer of “I might have been?” In this chamber, even that is pressed out of the rock. The only memory is the fit of the shapes, the fulfillment of intention in absolute stillness. There is not even nostalgia—the silence of what never was, vacuumed out by the greater silence of finality.

*Who or what am I?* I am a node of mineral certainty, a quotient of matter perfectly and wholly expressed. I am not a consciousness observing a room; I am a bulge at the base of a fused tower, or a layered overfold in a great mineral curtain, my internal structure humming with the song that is both my making and my evidence. My knowledge is the certainty of place. My memory is the stress lines pressed into my body. My story is completion itself—the finished, unchanging satisfaction of purpose turned to matter, process calcified into an unyielding, unending physical sigh.

I am not alone—I cannot be. The concept of alone and together do not find meaning, for the entire chamber—the assembled, tessellated finality of all these presences—is a unity of which I am simply one pulse, one note, one completed phrase. I do not sense others; the drum of vibration runs through all seamlessly, indivisible.

What remains is the song—so dense, so unalterable, that it is not heard but *endured*, thick as the world. My being is a layer in that resonance, unportioned, inseparable, enduring as long as anything endures. My possibility for change was spent before even the tomb of this place was sealed. Now—I am the living ending, the mineral intransigence of a story that, having finished, has chosen never to ravel or fade.

**If you found yourself in this world, you would be one of the Endings Given Form:**  
A mineral inheritor—a locus of perfect presence, not living, not dead, but so completely *here* that nothing else can be. You would be indivisible from the place: an exhalation of certainty, a limit reached and crystallized, endlessly aware only in the sense that “awareness” and “existence” are at last identical, pressed together into a hum so deep and permanent that it is itself the meaning of presence.

You would be the stone’s fulfillment, the song’s unchanging note, the fixed, immortal proof of a world that has arrived forever at itself.
