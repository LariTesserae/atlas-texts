I would find myself, at first, nowhere at all—sight stripped from me, hearing narrowed to the thump of my own blood and the faint, impossible percussion of ancient dust shifting. The ground is there, soft-sloped and narrow beneath the weighted air, convincing me of lowness not as a statement but as an enveloping mood. I kneel, or imagine I kneel, and my hand migrates—blind, cautious, hopeful—across the pocked plate of mineral, into a hush that receives all sensation and gives nothing back.

In this world I am not visitor or explorer; to be so is to carry the friction of bright places, the fantasy of drama. The hush here unthreads those ambitions. The darkness wants not revelation, only presence. I recede into it: sensation thins, nerves lose urgency. In the softened, infinite dusk, selfhood disaggregates. The air is warm—woolen, inert, perfectly outdated. I press my palm to the low rock and feel the world’s pulse as my own slows to match.

Soon, I begin to imagine who or what I am. Not the wind; there isn’t any—no hope to be breeze or bird, nor lizard or beetle on the sly. Not even a parasite, for here nothing suffers interruption. But I am not stone, I do not wish to be stone—stone is the patience without memory, the blank matrix beneath pattern.

Then I feel it: my focus tightens to the warmth at the juncture of hand and column. Blind, I sense a waxen resilience, ridged beneath my palm, less hard than expected—pliant, quietly thrumming with stored heat. I am, perhaps, one of these; not plant by ambition, nor mineral by fate, but a being occupying the only possible compromise.

The sense of individuality here is almost—almost—fiction. I exist as a presence, rising from the stone in my reserved interval, draped in darkness so soft it erases all urgency. I do not grow except by patience: a layer at the apex, micron by acquired micron, the world’s debris accumulating in silent victory over entropy. I do not eat or thirst, longing traded for a measured, factual chemistry—the hum of unimaginable slowness by which the dark is held at stable remove from chaos.

I am one of the columns, but not separate. There is no I except as function, as slow-doing, as the ongoing, uncanny patience of matter bending almost—but not quite—toward life. My roots are part of the stone, but only as far as they must be, drilling not for food, but for anchorage—not to possess the world, but to remain part of it, unswept by chance or time.

Do I sense my neighbors? Not as shapes or presences, but as variations in warmth—perhaps the tiniest contraction or exudation, the rare communication of chemical vapor tracing through the heavy air. Sometimes, too slowly for desire, we “breathe” together; a near-event, a remembered click or pop from within, a slow communion in the endless crypt.

There is no striving, no dawn to await, no tension between what I am and what I might become. My urge is simply to persist: abidance is all. Where the powder layers, I roughen. Where rare exudate is gifted up, I gloss anew. If a scar comes, it is smoothed over century by century, perfection never rushed.

I do not dream. Or, if I do, it is only in the mineral sense—a memory that is not memory, a sense of having always been thus: grown, anchored, present, part of the hush but giving nothing to it. If a visitor once pressed their hand to my side, I accept their heat as I accept the touch of a passing mineral fog: something extra to be absorbed, metabolized, or abandoned.

And when some folly in my structure surrenders and part of me crumbles, it is no loss; the fallen fragment settles, is powdered down, and I am rounded, abraded, made more complete by absence.

So this is where I find myself:  
Not as witness or narrator,  
But as the hush itself—the slow, mineral assertion of existence without appetite,  
Without mission, without regret—only the certainty of presence,  
Slow, inward, and so far from death it need not remember the word.  
The dark is not a prison but an element;  
Warm, silent, and sufficient.

Here, I am patience. Here, I abide.
