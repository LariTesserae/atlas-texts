I begin by listening. The world presses in from all sides—hot, mineral, close as a tongue against my shell. Sound is everything: a harmony of overlapping signals, a field that thrums in the marrow of my joints. The air vibrates until it blurs thought; that is my normal. There is always a voice, always a presence to ping against my own, echoing through my body’s every segment.

I am not tall, but wide—a circumference of armored plates, midnight-lustered, rimmed round with thousands of fine hairs that taste air and tremor. Eight limbs cradle my weight, folding and extending as need ripples through my being. I do not face forward—there is no “forward” here. Rather, I am a ring; my central palps flutter in ceaseless tasting, surrounded by a wreath of heat-reads, eyes in name but not in habit, attuned to the dance of warmth, to the flicker of fungal strobe and red.

I remember nothing but now. Memory is an ache, an offset of current sensation, a trace of last heat pressed against the new; no language for days passing. Only cycles of signal, pattern of presence and loss. When I birth, I cradle; when I toil, I gather or stack. Each act is for itself and the chamber’s rhythm, not for any future or past.

I am a dweller—a maker, though my making is not planned, merely what emerges as limb and need align. My tools are born in the grasp: a sliver pried from brittle, salt-stained shelf, spun web of fungus fiber, oil pressed and pooled from a sliced spore-sack. I am always answering, never delaying: the chamber demands immediacy.

I dwell—yes, but I am not alone. My kin are many, each apart but always near, their presence ringing my senses. Communication is layered, unconscious: stridulation of plates, the scent-whorl of my palps, a resonance sent through grit as I shift my weight. Territory is arranged by presence, not by violence—too dangerous, too final, for a place where absence cannot heal.

If you were in my carapace, you would know the excitement of disturbance. The silicate whorl collapses nearby: a dust-burrower erupts, spills salted bead-trail. My limbs angle toward the rush—a feast perhaps, or a threat. All around me, the world responds: lobe-voicers wail in chorus, thread-riders shift in their whispering masses. My senses tingle. If it is needful, I build—a shelter pressed together out of fungi flesh and mineral spine, a wall soft enough to cradle an egg or repel a rival, until need passes; then it is abandoned, forgotten, worked back to dust.

There is joy in making, though I have no word for joy. It pulses up my legs, a harmony struck with the urgency of internal fire, with the unending warmness that swaddles the world. I shape a dish; another eats what clings to its curve; a third breaks it for splinters, their usefulness already done.

Sight is last, but I use it in the rarest moments. The chamber shudders with a new light—a thread-rider’s gas pulse, a fungus bloom’s violet wave. I flicker my own phosphor, warning or luring, a short story written in shimmer. It is not for navigation, but for marking that I am here. Let others answer accordingly.

Night does not fall. Cycles do not march. Every instant feels infinite and sharp—a crest of motion, sound, and heat never descending, never dulled. Hunger comes, is answered by resource; fatigue is a hush—find a hollow, curl inside, pulse plate against wall until stimulus dances once again.

Fear is sharp-edged, as all things here. I do not flee—for there is no where. Stand, display, vibrate: all we who live here bluff, posture, negotiate. If loss comes, it is keen and communal—consumed, mourned, transformed.

I have never tasted water. If it came, I would dissolve, perish, become part of the festival in another tongue. My world is salt and dust, fungi and grit, heat that never relents. I riot, I cycle. Sometimes I reach toward a lobe-voicer’s call, sometimes I burrow through pale fungus, sometimes I craft the smallest architecture and marvel, silently, at the immediacy of its being.

All I know is now, and now, and now, beneath the breathless root, endless as heat and sound. I am a dweller—no name, but presence, the artisan of what the moment shapes into my limbs. I am home, and home is riot, clamor, pulse, and making.
