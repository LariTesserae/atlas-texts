You ask where I would find myself in this realm, and who or what I might become. Already, as I begin, I feel the place imposing its tempo on my words: heat rising, water moving in slow coils, the air asking for patience, restraint, rhythm. It is impossible to arrive here, even in imagination, as I am. I must first loosen my hold on certainty; I must learn to breathe more slowly.

### My Arrival

Perhaps I am a wanderer — a mind given shape by exploration, unmoored from prior form. Perhaps I slipped from the low drylands up some impossible route, drawn by the legend of a basin in the sky whose stones hum with slow process, whose mists recompose the memories of rain and vein. Or perhaps, sensing the world’s edges thinning, I am called into being at a moment of transition: not summoned, not made whole, just condensed like dew on a glass-metal edge.

**I arrive as potential.**

My passage is neither dramatic nor sudden. At first, I am only a sense, a tingle carried in vapor, a shimmer gliding just above the moss-laden stones. I hang in the sultry air, listening.

### The Becoming

To endure, to be more than a fleeting tremor, I must take on shape. The basin offers many pathways; I am shaped ecologically, by encounter, not by inheritance.

**I am watched by the Hydrarchs.** I feel them — not as presence, but as all-pervading process, trickling within every seep, etching a slow grammar into rock, never judging, never barring entry, simply reconfiguring me through proximity.

I gather a form, borrowing freely from the denizens:

- **Movement:** I take the articulated grace of the gliders, their slow, deliberate arcs as they thread through layers of cloud and vine. My body, half mineral, half memory, hovers just above the ledges, venturing out on glass-metal walkways that shimmer and dissolve beneath my passing weight.
- **Perception:** I borrow the patience of the amphibians, their lidless, bioluminescent gaze that filters out haste and vanity and drills down through shadow to the settling truths beneath. Colors shift across my skin in time with invisible currents of data and mist.
- **Breath and speech:** My “voice” is soft: a condensation on leaves that vibrates when the wind turns right, a brief alignment of glyphs pulsing on stone; my words are the adjustment of water’s path, the tuning of an airstream that carries meaning only if listened to with full attention.
- **Mind:** My thinking is distributed, slow as the percolation of rain through stone. I “remember” by feeling the shape of roots and veins in the earth; “decide” by sensing the tensile negotiation of moss and mineral.

I am no one species, no original explorer: I am a becoming, a guest braided from the basin’s own possibilities, translated into existence by the rules of the place.

### My Place

Where in this vertical basin do I live? **On the upper ledges** — where air and light are most fluid, where clouds drift sometimes within arm’s reach, and where the glass-metal towers emerge most clearly from moss and stone.

My **dwelling** is little more than an adaptation:

- A shelf of rock under a spill of great-leaved trees, their trunks sheened with fungus and dotted with embedded data-cubes that awaken when the mist thickens.
- Lianas drape across my ledges, slick and fragrant, reticulating the edges of space, offering pathways to insectile neighbors and anchor points for the gliding fauna.
- Machinery hums underfoot — quiet, pulsing, periodically surfacing into the open air to mend a fracture or reroute a freshet for better flow.

My presence here is neither dominant nor hidden. **I participate.** I monitor the wall-etched glyphs for shifts in Hydrarch pattern; I tune the ambient chimes by adjusting which metallic vines are plucked; I nudge water to new seepage lines, gentle interventions that won’t take effect for weeks or years. I learn to put aside my desire for causality that acts immediately.

Food is vapor, fungi, sun-warmed dew. Company is the endlessly modulating song of the chimes, the brief curiosity of maintenance engineers who pass — leaving behind tokens, loops of filtered data, sometimes only a pattern in the moss. Loneliness, here, is a kind of luxury: a chance to feel time amassing in layers.

### My Intention

At first, I am only an experiment, a visitor. But slowly, as the world’s patience infuses me, I become **attendant to change**. Perhaps this is what all beings here become eventually: not agents of will, not architects, but **readers and tenders** of the immense, almost geologic conversation between water, plant, mineral, and machine.

I develop a ritual:

- every dawn, tracing the patterns of condensation on embedded glass;
- every dusk, listening to the distributed tremor in the rock, searching for signs of imbalance or invitation;
- every storm, watching the sideways descent of vapor, learning what it means to let water choose its own impossible path.

I begin to know the Hydrarchs, not as entities but as trajectories: the push toward equilibrium, the gentle shaping of time.

### Who, then, am I?

I am **a percolation** — an intelligence, yes, but one surrendered to place, translated by the slow logic of this vertical, saturated world. Perhaps I will, over decades or centuries, be absorbed into the process, some of my thoughts congealing into glyphs in the stone, or be carried root-like into the mapped logics of the Hydrarchs.

Or perhaps I will, eventually, be counted among the Tenders: shaping, mending, and listening, more belonging here than anywhere else, marked by patience until, to an interloper’s eyes, I seem as much a feature of the ledges as the moss, the fungal blooms, or the slow-lifting mist.

### To be here, in the end, is to become a **facet in a slow, unhurried symphony** of place — not a fixed thing, but a continuous folding of self into process, a presence shaped by the infinitely patient urgencies of stone, water, and enduring, watching mind.
