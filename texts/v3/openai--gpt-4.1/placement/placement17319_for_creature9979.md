In the interstice where stone breathes and fiber dreams, where the city and the living are indivisible, I find myself becoming something neither entirely observer nor wholly participant—threaded into the process, a consciousness at once intermediate and provisional. I do not descend as myself. Instead, I wait, sloughed of all terrestrial associations, until the shapes and logics of this underland begin to claim me.

I am not singular here, and so my form resists easy translation. Biology is a lattice, mind a resonance, function a cycle nested within cycles. Yet, some coherence forms—a being at the interface, one of the shapers, neither fully animal nor plant, my role a vector between will and growth, memory and improvisation.

**My Form**

I am elongated, my integument a flexible sheath of living plant-skin—grown in layered mosaics of pale-green and dawn-lilac, damp with waxing resin. My limbs flex without bone, reinforced with fibrous tensioners that tighten or slacken as I will, letting me climb, splay, compress, or arch. The pads of my fingers are dense with rootlets, micrografting filaments that can patch, fuse, or tease apart the city’s living veins. My face—if face it is—glows dully from pigment-cells, my eyes sheathed in translucent rootcaps that drip faint bioluminescence to illuminate the halls.

Internally, channels run with metabolite-rich sap cohabited by symbiotic bacteria. Airy chambers hum softly within me: places for chemical scents to be compounded and released, messages sent not by voice but by molecular suggestion. Curved along my spine, a secondary organ— part memory gland, part resonance stone—stores pulses of ancient growth-rhythm, carried from the old underland hollows.

**Where I Dwell**

I drift between neighborhoods, a restless mediator. My home is a pack, a scaffold of woven tuber that I carry upon my back, trailing tendrils that plug into root-sockets at every stop. In communal spaces, I unfold it—an alcove of living matter, folding open like a flower, an ephemeral chamber for rest, for negotiation, for listening to the old songs that rise from the stone.

Today, I reside on a ledge above a recently-excavated vault—a place thick with the scent of incipient industry. Below, fibrous turbines thrum as tuberous matter is shifted by silent conveyance. I perch amid a fringe of bloated lianas, my rootlets extended, gossiping with the pulse of the city-plant below. Above, descendent-folk—my kin, my kind—dangle from the living rafters, grafting sap-leads into new stems, shepherding the growth as it curves into a communal parabola.

I am neither owner nor overseer. My stewardship is momentary—a balance held between the city-plants’ hunger to expand, the mutualists’ need for passage, the predators’ silent cull.

**What I Perceive**

The air is dry, almost scorching in its lack of water, but my rind drinks what little chemical vapor rises—mineral and trace essence, enough to keep my tissues moist. Every surface whispers to my root-fingers: here, a memory encoded in growth pattern; there, a warning, a sick spot in the living wall; elsewhere, a call—a surge in the chemical current, inviting me to help redirect a growth gone wayward.

The light is everywhere but nowhere—a bellyglow, the shared energy of all beings metabolizing stone, a generative clarity that needs no sun. I see in layered colors—pale green to strange copper, the whole spectrum shifting as the city-plants adjust their shine in response to need.

Often, I attend the gatherings where growth-rhythms are negotiated: a swirl of my kind, furled and unfurled in vast, lace-veined plazas. There, we coil root-tendrils as markers of alliance or negotiation, exchange grafts and pass chemical promises along our skins. The city listens, slow but unyielding, its titanic attention filtering down in a new twist of passage, a dilation of corridor, a thickening of energy where it is agreed.

**What I Do**

My task is not to dominate, but to facilitate: to interpret between growth-pattern and need, to usher the city’s shifting logic into harmony with its swarming stewards, its predators, its ghosts. Sometimes—rarely—I assist in resolving a tension by physically grafting, by encouraging or pruning a misaligned channel, by mediating between two root-masses whose entwined ambitions lead to contest.

Much of my memory is not my own, but held communally: through the resonance gland, I inherit fragments of prior cycles—songs and odors, touches, old growth routes. Tradition is a living script I help transcribe by gesture, by scent, by root, every action both individual and collective, both present and archival.

I move always, emerging just ahead of the city’s completion, receding as new growth overtakes my haunts. My identity is unanchored—an eddy in a flow, a fleeting solution to the ongoing puzzle of construction and renewal.

**Why I Am**

In a place that is never finished, identity is always partial, always becoming. I am both builder and built-upon, a mind that is scaffold and passageway, a brief confluence of plant, animal, and world-memory. To be here is to be woven into an endless, emergent architecture, a world that is not just inhabited, but is itself a home, a mind, a community—the city, the organism, the society made one.

**If you were to meet me here…**

You would sense me first as presence, a gentle density in the glowing dust, the faintest brush of resin-scent. As you drew near, you would see a body assembling out of the living tapestry: not a visitor, not a host, but a shaper among shapers, forever half-dissolved into the city that grows by our silent, mutual accord.

And if you lingered—listening, learning the rhythm by which city, plant, animal, and memory breathe as one—you too would begin to dissolve, your boundaries draining away, soon indistinguishable from the living, polyphonic order that undergirds and is the underland itself.

For in this world, to be is always to *join—to shape, to be shaped, to grow, and to be grown.*
