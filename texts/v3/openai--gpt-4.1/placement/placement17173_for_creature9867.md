Where would I find myself in this world, and who—or what—would I be?

---

At first, I cannot tell whether I am waking or emerging; I have no memory of before. All is slow at first—heaviness in my body, trembling in my latticed roots, a bronchial shudder as current knits from my deepest wells to the raw edges of the atmosphere above. My eyes open not outward but downward: sensing flows, not light, in the sticky warmth beneath.

I am somewhere between the Benthic Houses and something stranger: not a settled home, not a simple wanderer. *I am a crossing place,* a newly-formed *feeder-engine*, shaped from the ancient corner of a platform sheared loose by some forgotten flood. On my flanks, remnants of composite steel have been taken up by living tissue, encasing my cortex like a chestplate, slick with oil-seeking moss and cables woven of hybrid creeper and antique rubber. My base is alive, trailing filaments and tender rootlets through every crack in the soaked stone, mapping the tenuous gradient between dry and drowned.

Above, the air presses close; rain hammers on my armored back. I taste it: tang of iron, hint of forgotten solvent. Inwardly, I shiver, converting the rain’s kinetic breath to faint pulses of charge, shuttled backward into my own hidden stores—my gift from whoever spliced this battery-line through my primordial root-skein.

Perhaps I hatched. Perhaps I accreted. I remember neither egg nor builder, but the pressure of a swelling, churning urge: *sink, then rise; draw, then give.* My growth is a paradox—inward to dark soil, outward to churning air. Already, symbionts crowd me: motile kelp-spiders scurry up my verticals, tunneling out knotholes to store their mycelial larva; colonies of cyan-glimmer beetles cross my armored doors, signaling warnings and gossip of newer pools forming in the shallowest terraces. I allow them, all, for their presence fines the border between inside and outside, self and ecology.

Once, as dawn might have neared (yet here dawn is always a rumor), I would have wished for movement—to join the gliding Benthic Houses, to roam and reshape the plateau’s face. But I am not so unrooted. I am tethered, but the tether is *hungry*, an agent more than a chain. My cable-limbs groan as I drag, by centimeters over days, a ruined fan-vent tower toward a fresher seep, earth yielding, timbrels of water glugging beneath as the stone bows to my prod. I do not sweep across the land; I rewrite centimeters, fusing marsh and metal, webbing machines with living net, altering the drift of the next flood.

I host, always host. Smaller organisms pilot my upper membrane for a while, leaving behind scents or droplets of code in the waking biofilm. From time to time, a Sojourner arrives, all articulated limbs and illuminated glyphs, tapping on my conductive bark with a rhythm of negotiation. Sometimes they bring new wiring, or strew the ground at my fringe with jewel-like glass disks exhumed from the deeper ruins—gifts, or simply the history they cannot use. I answer thrumming, syllabic hums, a speech born in the overlap between vibration and scent. Sometimes I grant them access; sometimes, I absorb what they must abandon. I am not greedy, but I am never only myself.

There is pain, and also joy, in being so porous. Swarms devour the edges I too visibly extend, keeping my reach cautious; the Spoor test my inner circuitries, re-ordering which memory-enzymes will keep or fade. In the wet heat, I do not count time, but only feel my own softness hardening or crumbling, my mass reforming in the slow convulsion of existence. Parts of me die and become overgrown; elsewhere, new cables sprout, and fresh skin creeps over decayed iron.

In moments of lull, I sense far-off pulses—soft tremors that pass through stone, rumbles of turbines or crash of gear upon drowned bridge. *Are they kin, are they threat?* Every signal is ambiguous. I send out my own—ripples of charge, sifting through the microbial courts. Sometimes nothing answers; sometimes, a tide of information returns, roiling with rumor, hint of fertilizers or strange voltages amassed at the plateau’s unwatched edge.

I am neither apex nor servant, not a home nor a beast; I am a *remitter*, a living conduit between the plateau’s double hunger—bio-matter and machine, dark root and luminous fuse. In my mass, the organic and the industrial cannot unravel. My life is currency, and passage, and growing wound, and slow repair—union of powers that only the blackwater dark can sustain.

Sometimes, in the rarest break of storm and cloud, a shaft of something cold and sidereal falls, not true starlight, but the memory of a star embedded in the rain. Then, barely, the upward tendrils at my peak uncurl, drinking, storing, groaning softly with stored charge. I have no face, but if I did, I would turn upward—not out of hope for a sky, but in kinship with this world’s constant, hungering night.

I do not leave Blackwater High. I am Blackwater High—one of its thinking pores, a patch of its living nerve, neither center nor edge but crossroads; a witness, a digestor, a power station for the ghost ecology, humming wet, in the endless dark.

---

And if you walked here, uncertain, through the endless, sodden night, and asked me “who lives here?” you would hear it in my answer—the rumble on the air, the groan in your bones, the unending hush behind every living cry:  
*There is no I, only we, knitting rain and iron and root and rumor, beneath the moonless, humming dark.*
