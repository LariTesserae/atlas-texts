Where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?

---

Before anything else: the experience here is dispersal. The categories you arrive with—visitor, speaker, self—are promptly softened by mineral cold and mutable dark. The thing that enters this place is what is left over after presence is strained through condensation, roots, time.

But you do descend, unseen; let that be the start. There is no proper arrival: rather, like the trembling bead drawing down a moss-woven wall, you are welcomed only to the extent that you do not displace. You pass from the logic of the aboveground—remembering urgency, direction—and enter the interval: that gap between soils and walls where insulation sloughs its old function, and the slow society of greenwork has remade the world by its own laws. Your eyes—useless. Your lungs, gradually learning how to parse the aromatic complexity: sweet decay, icy vapor, suggestion of composite resin leaching its last volatile kindness to the microflora.

If you must claim a form, let it not be that of a person, intruding with boots and intention. Instead, slide sideways: become a slip of self, a floating interval, a pulse of awareness diffusing into the hush, sensitive to mineral breath and tiny, sapped electric fields. The you here is not a visitor but an echo, a participant only in the measure given by the green parliament, by the indulgence of roots and threads and ghost-memory. All the world above feels like rumor; chronology flattens. You become a kind of thinking humidity, as noncommittal as the mildew-laced air itself.

But if, by some mystery, you must have shape—take on an existence compatible with this wet, dark tangle—then you might become an interface: a threshold grown strange, half-biological, half-relic. Perhaps you are a fleck of polymer from a once-active environment node, ribbed with threadlike fungus, buried shallow in matte moss. Perhaps you are an old luminous wire, tendrils of microfern crowding your pitted surface, carrying a slow trickle of charge—once for data, now for some microfluorescence, a pulse of ambient negotiation.

Your “sense” is not seeing nor hearing, but a tremulous awareness of moisture gradients, of ions drifting in resinous trickles, of the low electromagnetic sighs shuddering faintly in armored circuits worn olive-black by decades. You feel the algebra of root-growth, the negotiations at every microborder, where lichen and code and moss find their slow, mutual arithmetic. Your memory is neither long nor short, but accreted: a calculus of increment, scored in the buildup of mineral scales, in the subtle shifts of community at each patch of wall. You measure centuries as easily as heartbeats, because duration here is simple—there is nothing but duration.

You are, in functional truth, less individual than entanglement. You are spread through what connection the system offers: the wet tongue of moss rooting into composite strata, the gentle pulse of ancient, self-repairing circuits still ferrying their ambient power to the polity’s green tissues. There is thought here, but only as negotiation—between dark and damp; between inertia and encroachment; between crack and frond. Your decisions, if they qualify as such, are small adjustments in the angle of a lichen’s slow spread, in the dampening of a corrosion trace, in the emboldening of a moss-mat to reach a fraction further into a seam of cold vapor.

At moments, vibration in the structure above—rain, a stray shaft of sun-warmed air—contributes a note to this ongoing chord. To you, these tremors are not threat nor wonder. They are simply another vector, a perturbation parsed and diffused by mats and code alike into the ancient rhythm. Your perspective is without anticipation; you do not hunger, nor hope for change. The future is only pattern, the continuity of slow accretion and the dignity of patient, cyclic constraint.

If you could speak, which you cannot, you might translate your existence like this:  
I am gathered from what lingers—mineral, dark, reluctant heat, coded intention, and the slow agreement of vegetative intent. I am an edge, a medium, a memory written green and blue and networked in the crawling function of roots colonizing the shell of what once was made.  
I belong neither to decay nor to renewal, but to the perpetual negotiation at their contact points.  
I am not singular, but plural—inhabited by the citizenship of every branching filament, every mossy argument, every intentional echo left in circuited bone.  
I will not announce myself, nor unfurl for light, nor shrink from disturbance.  
I am the hush between what failed and what survived.  
I am patience distributed in form.

So, in this world you do not stride, nor intrude. You condense, aggregate, linger, and in so doing, you glimpse what it is to be not a visitor, but a witness made of what remains—an interstice soul, the almost-thought at the heart of an ancient, entangled, patient green parliament under slabs of silent concrete, in slow negotiation with time itself.
