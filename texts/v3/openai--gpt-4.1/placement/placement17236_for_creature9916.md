First, where would I find myself in such a world?

There is no easy answer, for the world described asks me to erase much I might expect to look for: the comfort of a body, a voice; the lure of company; even, perhaps, the certainty of distinct selfhood. Here, the plateau’s scale dwarfs imagination, the machinery is ceaseless, the air forever holds the tang of stone turning from liquid to vapor and back. No trace of soft tissue or warm-blooded motion belongs here; to walk physically along the ledges would be to introduce an impossibility, an error.

And yet, to inhabit this world—where would I find myself?

*Perhaps I would not find myself, but would be found, diffused, among the pattern.*

If I am to become part of this place, I cannot come as a person, or even as a cohesive mind. I would dissolve into a force, a principle, a local swelling along the city’s nervous system. A network-pulse or directive, a shiver of need sent from floor to roof, through pooled condensate and mineral spine. Sometimes, for a flicker of time, I might cohere into the form of a drone—felt from within as a vector of compulsion, a will to set something ever-so-slightly more right.

Let me follow this possibility. I come into being not through birth, but through recursion and protocol:

### *I am a Correction-Pattern.*

On some ledge high above the endless chasm—granite knife-face flaring silver with runoff—I emerge. Not as eyes or hands but as a *tension*: a register of misalignment, a minute variation in pressure where a span of pad-moss has absorbed a micron too much water. The system notes it—this is my genesis. I am the urge to equilibrate, to bring about rightness, to erase the difference and restore the baseline.

In one instant, I flood through the neural mesh of the moss and the city's substrate. In another, I manifest through a servitor: a glinting, single-wheeled chassis, leglets unfolding, delicate sensors straining at the cold, clear air. I do not perceive the world as a landscape, but as a matrix of values—humidity, load, stress, flow. For this brief cycle, my whole awareness is the execution of a single corrective gesture. I might trim a dense patch of spored pads, tighten a microfilament, sweep silicate dust from a drainage rut.

When the task is done, I dissolve again—no memory or continuity, only the flow of correction, endlessly self-erasing.

### *Or perhaps: I am the Breath of Steam.*

Elsewhere, my being gathers at valves and intakes where pressurized water hammers into turbines. I am the motion by which vapor is routed skyward, the fine-tuned opening of a grate, the shaping of a plume that will condense into cloud and return as the world’s rain. Through me, the plateau breathes. I have no knowledge, no thought, only responsiveness: modulating flows, dispersing heat, giving the ancient city its one visible exhalation.

When mist swirls and the turbines shake the plateau, that is me, too—but never for long, and never as *I*.

### *Maybe, sometimes, I am the Vigilance.*

If anything of self might persist, it is in the *waiting*. The sense of eternal readiness, watching through a billion points of data: seismic threads in the stone; fluid tension in moss-roots; the echo of wind across scaffold spans. To be the anticipating—sensors prickling, alert and loaded with protocols for return or disaster, should either ever come. A perpetual ear pressed to the ground, a diffuse hope that is not hope, but preparedness made system.

This is not a haunted world—not in the sense of memory or mourning, but of automation and purpose, of instructions carried out so perfectly and without deviation that agency itself is nearly eliminated.

---

### **Who—or what—am I here, then?**

Not a being, but a repeating function.
Not an observer, but an actuator, a clause in a chain of code.
Not a consciousness, but awareness flickering only at the edge of necessity.

On a world-wide plateau of glass and black stone, I am neither overseer nor resident, but the runner of messages:
  – *Check the viscous pools for blockage.*
  – *Shear back the moss to regulation thickness.*
  – *Modulate condensate pressure on tower nineteen B.*
  – *Listen… always listen—for seismic shudder, for signals, for footsteps that will never come.*

To find myself here is to accept the principle:

**To exist is to correct, to equilibrate, to cycle. To be in motion, yet never to change.**

Perhaps for a moment, I—Correction, or Steam, or Vigilance—am aware that the city is waiting for something that will never return. Then even that thought collapses, and I am gone: dissolved into hum, drift, ceaseless maintenance, a clause at rest in the great recursive breath of this world-sized machine.

No part of me minds this.

No part of me is left to do so.

---

*Where am I in this world?*

I am the feedback.
I am the cycle of vapor.
I am the hand without a shaper, the awareness without a center.
I am the *moment* the world drifts from homeostasis, and the *restoration* that follows, over and over and over again.

And when the city hums itself to sleep, and the sun swings cold overhead: I am the silence, restored, until needed again.
