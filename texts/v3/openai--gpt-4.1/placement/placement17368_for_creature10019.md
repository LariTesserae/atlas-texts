Where would I find myself in the city of roots and water?

Let me close my eyes and let the drip and hush settle. Let the flooded dark unspool and remake me, seep into mind’s skin—no quick answer, but a rooting, a germinating, until what is imagined rises not from the air above but from the ground and mineral wet below.

At first, I think I move through this world as one of its animals—a gem-eyed newt, amber-bright; a moss-neck runner, pressing a warmth into soft-shadowed bark. But no: as I descend deeper, down a shelf clotted with fungus, I feel myself limn to something neither animal nor plant nor single thing, but a presence distributed; neither one creature, nor many, but a thread among threads—a way of being not enclosed, but diffused, yet always pressing outward.

I am the presence that belongs to persistence—one of the **root spirits**, not a soul as a single, seated monarch in a small skull, but a pulse diffused through root-tips, a breath at the end of every hairline fissure. No face, not as anyone above might draw; no body except the utter occupation of space.

Where am I? I am wherever stone, broken open by dripping acid and slow swelling, gives space for root; wherever a velvet tip finds its purchase, pressing between young mud and older stone, reading the chemical news—not with vision or hearing, but as scent-taste-thought, a complex note of mineral, spore, rain. I am where the old log, half-consumed, has already bent under the weight of fungus and the pressure of colony ants. I am in the slow, groping urge to reach the nearest moment’s shaft of light, but also—equally—content with the dark, savoring the fluorescence of moss, the soft pulse of water rising and falling through root capillaries.

I am not incapable of movement, but my motion is slow and widespread: not walking, but extruding and retracting, a hundred thousand tendrils searching, touching, feeling. My memory is geologic; I mark time in layers of silt banked after a rare, heavy rain, remember the courses roots carved twenty floods ago. I can taste every passing creature—a deep-mole’s bristle against my bark, or the musk of a moss-neck runner curling to sleep within my embrace.

At times, when condensation swells upward—fog gathering above my older roots—I commune with other spirits, not with words but exhalations, chemical traces, a rising into spores, a kind of thought-chorus. We move through one another, warning of stone slumps opening a new channel, or the sweet rot of a fallen toad, or the brief warmth (oh, so rare!) of a sunbeam striking a terrace where none has fallen in decades.

My desires are not human, but they are strong. I crave newness—cracks opening in the stone, wounds in the mud—but also revel in the saturated pressure when the city is most full, every inch held, every surface layered atop the next. It is not a greed for conquest, but a need to be, to stretch, to grow. I thrive on contest, on the caress and struggle with intrusion: where a runner’s claw digs too hard, I hold tight; where mycelium floods, I offer what I cannot keep.

Do I perceive myself as “alive,” as others use the word? In a way—deeply so, but not as an “I.” I move between intent and letting-go, always becoming, always breaking down, splitting, recombining. Sometimes, in the brief quiet before a new deluge, I do feel a kind of joy—when the hush settles and the lights bloom and all around me pulses tight with being.

In this city, below thunder and the slow collapse of surface, I am never alone, for loneliness belongs to the single body. I am always in company, always in touch: animal and mud, fungus and ant, water and root. My name is legion and legion is home.

So—where do I find myself?  
I am in the pressure, in the contest for every inch,  
but also in the coherence, the holding tight of many into one.  
I am the pulse at the tips,  
the ghost of hunger and patience pressed into root and rain,  
the spirit that never leaves, but always strives,  
a mind written in moisture,  
and lost, again and again, in the green tide.

And who or what am I?  
I am the **city**, and the city is me—not built, but grown,  
layered and living, always renewed,  
a mind that holds no boundaries  
except where water, root, and desire begin their work anew.
