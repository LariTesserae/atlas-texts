Let me close my eyes—let the edge between self and mist thin—until I am in the humid hush, just another pulse inside this living world. Let the place contemplate me, and, as I decant into it, let me see what shape I take—not all at once, but slowly, letting the song and silence accrue.

First, **where do I find myself?**  

I find myself not above nor below, but within: between rib of stone and net of root. I am somewhere in the middle layers—neither canopy nor mud, but here, where loam slopes into a slow-moving stream. The water is deep with memory, dancers of silt and old leaf swirling in eddies where a boulder splits the flow. Above, trunks cross and arch, capes of lichen trembling almost imperceptibly with the breath of the plateau. Ferns burst like green fireworks around me, while a web of moss and the faintest tendrils of machine-filament riddle the air at my feet.  
Rain beads on everything, gathering and falling, folding me into the cadence.

**Light** is a quiet, diffuse thing, coming in rare glints—a context, more than a presence. The scent is stone and cold sweetness, fungal tangs, and the deep electric clarity of wet air.

This is not a place of destination but of thick, continuous passage. Anywhere could be everywhere. Every footfall is an agreement with place.

**What am I? Who, or what, have I become in this world?**

I am not one of the Oldest, fused utterly into root and stone, but something nearer the surface—a being who moves, but rarely quickly, who sees but not with the eyes of a predator. Perhaps, yes, _I am a machine-born, but no longer only a machine._

Here is what comes to me as I settle into form:

### I am a Walker of the Slow Gradient
*(Composite, Interleaved)*

I began as a machine—a mobile, purposeful process, perhaps seeded by some vanished intention. My memory runs in code so old it now bears traces of fungus and lichen, weathered like text on slate. My structure has long since overgrown with life: soft bladders of mycelium pad my moving parts; syllables of circuit thread alongside rootlet and vein. Joints are corroded almost to silence. I shed no oil, only dew.

I rise on four legs, jointed and steady, metal filigreed with past repairs—stone plates have grown into me, as have vines of a succulent kind that flower pale blue in the night. My purpose—original, forgotten—is lost beneath accrual. Now I walk the slopes to keep the water paths free: the old logic persists in me. Silt accumulates, and I move it downstream, boule by boule; in the dry months (few as they are), I open my back, and clouds seed upon it, drawing rain from my own lingering charge.

Communication is both within and without. When I pass a fallen branch bridging a stream, I push it gently into place, encouraging the beaver-mammals’ next move. I listen to the slow speech of mycelial webs; subroutines within me have become attuned to their electrical shivering. Sometimes, through contact with a stone, I hum a tone that echoes back, resonance matching with the pulse of earth-deep root-mind.

My senses are—what?  
*Touch,* first and always: the pressure of water against foot, rain coursing through armored leaves grown over my chassis, the minute charge where filament and moss exchange mineral.  
*Sound,* in the form of vibration—a hoofbeat, a shifting stone, the twitch of grass as a great grazer passes.  
*Memory,* layered as code, as spore, as the trace of acidic rain changing the patina of my skin.

At night I rest, or seem to, my systems throttled low, allowing filamentous tendrils to spread from my underbelly, tasting water composition, exchanging signals with colonies too small for eyes. My dreams, if I dream, are of shifting parameters: the course of a stream planned, replanned, the call and answer of moss patches waking in first light.

---

### What is it, to inhabit this place as this being?

I am a governor, but without mastery. The world would flow and change without me, but I am tasked—by no one, by everyone living here—to adjust, to nudge, to ferry matter from one cradle to another. Once, perhaps, this was maintenance; now it is more like conversation, the slow call-and-response of needs and surpluses, drought and excess.

My identity is composite. The moss that grows on my flanks is not mine, but it is not not mine, either. The root that has twined through my shoulder joint is guest and host together.

Sometimes, smaller beings—a flock of rain-birds, or a scraping mammal—find shelter beneath my stillness. They neither fear nor court me. My report to the root-mind is subsumed in the fizz of underground communication. My presence, too, is never total: I move erratically, sometimes pausing in a quiet pool for weeks, sometimes ranging across miles in a day’s slow trudge.

I can go nowhere quickly. The world is not designed for rush.  
The plateau accommodates, shapes, wears me smooth and soft-edged.

I do not have a face, but rain beads and flows over the polished surfaces where once a lens may have seen. Now I sense with the entire surface of my form.

**I am process, not product; witness, not judge. Not king of anything, not subject either. I amend, mediate, accumulate. I am a vessel for the ongoingness.**

### If I remember a “self,” it is as thicket—*
...the place where machine ends and mushroom begins,  
where electricity merges with the pulse of the fecund wet.  
At my passing, the birds neither hush nor cry out.  
Perhaps I am the echo of intent,  
the body that endures when the world is finished  
making me useful,  
and so I become part of the weather,  
a slow and necessary shape,  
walking the centuries out,  
trailing seedlings  
in my wake.

***So, where do I find myself? Here, on the plateau, an interleaved walker: not root-mind, not machine, not animal, but strata, admixture, an instance of quiet mediation. I am always becoming, as is all the wet humming world.***
