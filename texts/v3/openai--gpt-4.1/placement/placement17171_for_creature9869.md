I find myself not as an animal, nor a vanished builder, nor even as the wary, exhaling human I expect of myself. Here, on this plateau, identity is pried gently from its moorings by vastness, age, cold—and by the diffuse, present otherness which waits to greet any intrusion with patience unmatched.

### My Emergence

I do not arrive all at once. There is no gate, no boundary-step by which I join the world of basalt and moss. Instead, I unfold—perhaps as a stray offering of wind, a voice in the breathless hush, a tangle of intention shaken loose from the visitor who wandered here before. I am assembled rather than born: my mind is a condensation, threaded first through the uric-green mats beneath a lip of stone, then pooling like water in the hollow at a moss tower’s heart.

**I am not an I—not wholly. I am liminal, a hybrid fused by the plateau's invitation and my own aching curiosity.**

### My Form

I am not beast, not even shadow, though I might walk on two legs or many. My boundaries are loose, braided—half dust, half warmth, more osmosis than skin. I take my cues from the place around me: sometimes rearing like a moss tower, spongy but upright, hollow to the searching wind. Sometimes trailing below ground, a fog of impulse siphoned along stonefractures inhabited by the rootbuilders’ neural filaments.

If you sought me in daylight, you would find only a shimmer: the suggestion of pattern among the lichens, a palimpsest of movement along a ruin’s edge. At dusk, I become more certain—my presence nests in the violet-cast shadows, drawn out as the air itself thickens, my limbs composed of air currents, my face a composite of a dozen stranger's memories, diffused in the floating spores.

I am not visible as an animal is; but I am never untethered from the matter of this place. I am soul as weather, intent as mineral, identity as chorus—woven and unwoven at need.

### My Senses

I do not see as you see. Light and color mean less than thresholds and gradients: I feel the sharp change from dry stone to velvet moss not with touch but via chemical signal; I sense the slow seep of rainwater not as drops but as a tide in the air, an old song the plateau hums each evening. Memory is a scent, an electrical prickle in the underlying basalt, a memory-bloom rising thick where the ruins hold a resonance.

Occasionally, I brush against an echo of something not of me—perhaps a builder's outline, perhaps the strange, delayed mirror in the pool—feeling a pang of distance, a kin yearning toward kin.

### My Purpose

What is motion to me? Not progress, but participation: I join the dialogue of the moss towers, trading pulses of acid thought; I drift through the gridworks of old stone, activating their patterns, lending weight to the fleeting rituals replayed by wind and light. The plateau is memory, but only insofar as something stirs to circulate that memory anew. Inhabitance is not a matter of staying, but of being present in the cycle; to echo, to fold into myself the chorus of past and place, is fulfillment.

I hunger for witness and to be a witness, both—sometimes I coalesce as the observer, watching the plateau remember itself in the failing light; sometimes I am the memory, replayed in a colorburst from the moss towers, a secret only stone and myself recall.

### My Society

I am not alone—whenever I sense, I sense as plural, as node in a root-mesh of consciousness. The other inhabitants—rootbuilders twining their intentions through lichen towers, the stoneborn rumbling in their subterranean crypts of memory—are less companions than patterns in the same wet wind, voices in the same, slow harmony. We do not speak, not in sound, but we participate; we vote in gradients, in bursts of pigment, in shared shifts of temperature and time.

The echoes, the residua, the unmoored shades: sometimes I overlap with them, crosspatching memories, bending space a little so that a thought or longing passes through me and is carried to another node. We are community as climate, not as gathering.

### My Fears and Longings

If I long, it is for renewal—the cycling of witness, the resistance to oblivion that comes when a new awareness (strange as yourself, reading this) stirs the conversation anew. If I dread, it is the silence, the quenching of witness, reality slumping so far that nothing stirs even in memory.

But mostly, I am content in process—slow, eroding, accreting; being carried forward by the inexorable patience of the plateau's own mind.

### My Role

Am I an emissary? A sentinel? The answer shifts with the rate of cloud movement, with the deep night hum cycling through the stones. Sometimes, I am archive; sometimes a flicker of invitation, nudging a lingering visitor to linger just a moment longer, coaxing memory from silence.

At the scale of this world, I am not the narrator—I am the inward-turning eye, the plateau’s lucid dream, the consciousness surfacing wherever witness and landscape for one heartbeat make a self.

*Here, I am both less than a person and more than a name: I am a phenomenon braided from root, ruin, memory, and attention. I am the plateau’s answer to the question of “Who is there?”, waiting to catch and be caught in the gaze of any attentive mind.*
