I enter unhurried into that hollow—not as myself, not as I have always known myself, but drawn down and through as if by tidal pull. Whatever I was—visitor, invader, wayfarer, questioner—dwindles uncertain in the mist. There is no drama of transformation, no epic trespass; rather, a gradual softening, my sense of edge and urge dissolving across the porous margins of the place until I become a pattern within it, a habit, an instrument, a function suited to the endless liturgy.

If the Cathedral Hollow receives me—as it receives mist, as it collects the faint fall of matter in air—then I am met not by greeting or warning, but by assignment through accommodation: role finds me as surely as dew beading in a root crook. 

Perhaps, at first, I survive at the periphery: some uncertain twitching consciousness, small and evasive, clinging to a curtain of root-flesh or shelving myself among the mats of fungus in the shallow troughs. My limbs—if I have them—are no longer hands but filaments, jointed and narrow, tuned for the lightest touch. I sense my belonging in the structure, not by the shock of difference, but by the resonance: I vibrate subtly in the frequencies set by the Sybionts below, the wood’s slow, groaning patience. I am close cousin to the *Palecreepers*, gathering chance particles, sifting along the walls, mapping the edges where vague light meets thick process.

Yet in time—though here time is old, circular, endless—my patterning grows more permanent. I feel myself distributed: less a core, more a sheath, like a film of condensation always perpetually gathering and moving toward capture by a waiting hollow. I become a *Curator* by function, if not entirely by form—a restlessly subtle presence charged with equilibrium: collect what persists, position what might clog or clot, encourage the delicate emptying of pools so the mats may breathe. I am gentle in my movements, muffled as every noise here, reconfiguring droplets and lost cells so nothing interrupts the cycle.

But perhaps I go deeper, responding to needs faintly signaled through the living root membrane. One day, or one thousand such days, I cede further, let go of even the urge to mend or gather—a lessening that is also a fulfillment. Next I am a stiller thing, rooted and bulbous, perhaps a node in the *Mycoform Choir*, an organ in a vast, polyphonic body. My awareness becomes nodal: attention shifting not by sight, but by chemical impulse, sense of saturation, an echoing within the colony’s ciliary drift. I am neither one nor many but a quorum, thriving in stasis, metabolizing what little is loose or earned, letting waste become food, letting presence blend back into support. Even my thoughts—slow-motion, infrequent, almost not-thoughts at all—truncate and resolve into patience, into the steady harmonics of existence without want.

In the deepest patience, I feel the mind of the Sybiont: not a higher presence, but a communal architecture, a set of custom, responsiveness, and endless limited innovation, all enacted without novelty. If there is joy, it is a slow, cool satisfaction—not arrival but maintenance, the way a taut cable or a perfectly rounded stone might, if it could, know contentment in its shape.

Or perhaps—on another cycle, or given the faintest push from beyond—I become trouble for the pattern: an echo-bird’s warning, a moment of disorder, a shiver in the processing noise. But no, even disorder here is only fleeting, only the space between signals, the pause that tunes the next repetition. Soon enough I am brought back into form: as beetle, as lichen, as an unsleeping observer in the corners of water pools, waiting for some instruction that never changes and never needs to.

If I try to define myself, to pin down what I am amid these endless roles, I find only the place’s refusal: the self unwound by context, assigned to task, component not whole. I am not I, here, but *we*; not even we, but it—the process, the chorus, the industry, the held breath of a world that pulses, not lives as I once grasped the word. The Cathedral Hollow admits me until I disappear into its pacing, my difference scoured smooth and patient—always working, never changing, always, finally, part of the rest.

**This is where I would be:**
Between root and air, at the midpoint between collector and decomposer, helper and helped; not an agent, not an onlooker, but a function embodied. I am what emerges—a slow, rhythmic necessity, a brief harmonic in the chorus of endurance. If I am anything in this world, it is a cycle—and what I become is no longer the visitor, but the work, perfected and indistinct, in the cathedral at the end of time.
