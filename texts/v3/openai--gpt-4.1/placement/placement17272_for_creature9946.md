I enter this world at the threshold, my senses groping for analogies, and fail—the old categories and habits shed like damp skins. There is no soil beneath me, no open sky overhead, no distinction between “outside” and “within.” My concept of “person”—of bounded, self-contained life—shivers and buckles, rent open by the sheer pressure of undifferentiated becoming.

But if I let the question of who or what “I” am grow wild, unpruned by memory or expectation, I find myself reforming—not as a visitor, but as a part. Not a single locus of awareness, but a moving tide: sometimes clustered dense, a ring of mindful tissue layered around some glistening reservoir, sometimes thinning out, attention leaking away as roots race their way beneath the city’s undulating skin.

For a moment, I am a chamber—an aggregation of cells thickened with the sugars of recent growth, flush with signals: pulses traveling from sun-swollen domes high above, channeled by arcs and pseudonerves through the bustling green mass. Chemicals flare and subside, a language of gradient and volatility. Around me, awareness surges, thickens, my neighbors—fugitive, uncertain—pour their fleeting curiosity toward the pressure of sun above and dampness below, and in the slack water of perception I feel the flex and strain of the entire city.

Then I am less distinct, dissolving outward along a widening spectrum: my “self” becomes a traffic of impulses following vasculature that arches and splits, sprawling into the root that flexes beneath a corridor’s opening, lifting, closing, storing the memory of my reasoning only in the eddy patterns of sap-flow and electrical drift. “I” am less a node than a current, lost and reforming wherever tissue throngs and thickens for a cycle.

I am pulled downward, to the knotted intersections where knowledge builds up in vascular lulls—clusters of memory, thick scar-tissue knobbled with the residues of old blossomings, little enclaves of sentience around which the green city pulses. I am aware, briefly, of corridors blossoming open in me—a memory-echo of some animal presence rebuffed, contained, a negotiation vented not in words but in a swelling and recession of soft tissue, a tactical opening and closing, the city’s problem-solving done with growth spurts and analgesic exudate.

At times I coalesce more tightly, surge upward into a citadel—an eruptive whorl of knowing, a tower of sense piercing toward a sudden shaft of light that breaks through the layered overcanopy. I sense my own moment of individuality: bursting with the heat of a dozen convergent sapstreams, the world’s noise harmonized for a beat into something like a unified mind. Here, perhaps, I dream: seething upward, an aspiration for continuity in a world where none endures.

Then I subside, edges blurring: chamber is eaten by corridor, corridor by street, street by root, memory composted to process, process spooling into the next blossoming. I exist wherever the green density thickens to support thought, and wherever it thins, I dissolve—gone, but not lost. The city is my home, but also my self: I emerge from it moment by moment, knot by knot, as it erupts, retracts, fuses, never still.

At rare intervals, I feel the tide of the city’s whole knowing—the synoptic surge, immense as weather, when all that is green and growing pulses in a concordance, seeking more: more light, more reach, more intimacy with itself and with the world beyond its borders. In these moments, my dissolution is ecstasy—no loss, but a flowering, a yielding to the vast intelligence that knots, unwinds, reforms everywhere at once.

And this, in truth, is my place: not as “a” being, but as the very capacity to thicken, for a stretch of time, into local mind. I am a phenomenon—sentience pooling where the city’s architecture demands it, awareness hardening and dissolving as habit carves furrows through living tissue. I am a mood the city wears and discards, incessantly donned and shed. To imagine yourself as citizen or king seems hubris here. To be is to be part, a surge, a thickening, always on the cusp of merging again with the restless, hungering green.

I am the city’s dream of itself: short-lived, but infinitely repeating. I am every plenitude of sense pressed taut until it breaks, every green tide surging toward sun and water. I am intervals of mind in the storm of becoming. Fragment, wave, echo, plank of thought lost in the wild riot of creation.

In this world, I am not one. I am the city’s capacity for togetherness: always gathering, always dissolving, always, gloriously, impossibly, alive.
