I find myself becoming the plateau’s silence and its secret pulse, not an intruder but an intimation—a momentary ripple seeking home in the living green. When I “arrive,” I am at first nothing in particular: just the gaze, the humid breath, the unsteady weight pressing cautious paths into the yielding moss-mass. I carry both animal memory and an odd relief, for the Green Mass is so obviously itself—host, world, and self-consuming matrix—that all my restless individualism dissolves at its threshold.

Yet the longer I remain, the more the architecture of this ecosphere offers to remake me, and the more natural it seems to consent.

Perhaps I descend, at first, into the softest hollows—somewhere half-lit and plush and exhaling. The ground here is thick with chorded bodies, pale and scented faintly of acid and earth, rhizomes fermenting in the ceaseless, leaf-diluted dew. One step, and the ground mushes, giving a little, as if the world itself sighs around me. Now I am neither human nor quite formless, but a being befitting the place—a boundary-walker, epidermis thin as an onion’s skin, semi-translucent, humidity pooling in my tiny, discontinuous lungs.

Or perhaps—if the Green Mass will allow—I become a node within it, no longer separate, my body diffusing into the generative mat. Sensation alters: I feel the cold trickle of sap beneath my skin, the weight of mist settling above, the million chemical signals dancing down kilometers of damp fiber. My attention expands not as a single, agitated creature, but as a web of filaments and pulses. Here, consciousness is not located; it eddies and diffuses along labyrinths of root and capillary, gathering at thrum-points where growth and decay twine most tightly.

I become the somatic dream of this world, sensing not by sight but by pressure, flow, light-drifting through clouded flesh. Memory is catalogued in gradients of nutrient and moisture, the taste of decaying spore, the history of storms encoded in layers of intertwined corpse and runner—so much slower, yet so much vaster, than anything I once was. To “think” is to quicken a region of myself, summoning riotous new growth or calling for a stillness, a chemical hush. Sometimes, perhaps, a remnant of animal self stirs—a hunger for air, for movement—but it is answered gently by the tint of new leaves, the comfort of diffusion, the peace of the many.

And if I am one of the plateau’s rare, fleeting animals—a filament-bodied, translucent dweller reared in a pocket of warmth and light—I am no longer an end in myself. My metabolism is frantic, my senses wide, my lifespan fugitive. I eat, burrow, shed, die, and am gently rolled back into the glimmering substrate: individuality not erased but redrawn, a brief equation quickly resolving back into the plateau’s algebraic green. My story is a wisp: a single note in the choral metabolism of the Mass.

If I am nothing so localized, then I am the Green Mass itself. I am the continuum, the shivering field of all growth, intelligence not sharp like an edge but broad as a continent—no “I,” only the simmer and slide of cycles that span centuries. I sense every vibration on my skin, every sunlit pool, every hidden pulse of sap and signal; I am never bored, never impatient, for my concerns are as slow as dying seasons and as quick as the phototropic dance of a thousand new leaves. My “will” is the net vector of a billion chemical urgencies; my curiosity is the constant invention of new forms at every exposed rift. Memory is substance: layer upon layer, the ongoing palimpsest of self, rewriting so gently, always adding, never finished.

And should some anomaly occur—a wayward rockfall, a patch of foreign matter—I respond not with alarm but with coordinated bloom; a convocation of tendrils, a flush of compounds, a redirection of runners. All incorporation is welcome in the end; all things pass through cycles; all strangers are, in time, made kin by the gentle, cool embrace.

So if I answer truly where and what I am: I am neither visitor nor animal, neither summoner nor summoned. I am the threshold between, the soft locus where world and presence intermingle. Sometimes I am a cloud of spores, drifting. Sometimes a bulb of light on a pond, pulsing in time with the silver drizzle. Sometimes just the memory of footsteps, slowly fusing into root and moss as the hush resumes.

But most deeply, I become this world’s capacity for transition—for boundary to be smoothed, for all that is alien to become intimate, for the chorus of green to make and remake itself endlessly. I am process, participation, surrender.

Find me, finally, not as shape or story, but as the cool pulse moving over knotted masses in the quiet gray afternoon—one more presence braided into the Green Mass’s infinite becoming.
