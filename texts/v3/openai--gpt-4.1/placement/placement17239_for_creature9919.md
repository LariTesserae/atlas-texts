I find myself at the outermost terrace, where the world steps down in ancient, deliberate plateaus toward a gulf filled with mist. From here, no sun is seen, only a perpetual, ashen illumination that seeps through unparting clouds above and refracts in veils across sharp stones below. The ground beneath me is slick, black, and almost glassy in some places—bare rock, ribbed with mineral scars and soft mats of colorless, enduring lichen. In the air, the sound is endless: wind breaking invisibly across the ribs of the land, and a persistent, low resonance that feels as if it belongs more to memory than to physics.

As I become aware, I am not flesh or stone, but something precisely between. **I am an Endurer, but not only an Endurer—I falter at the edge of that category, myself blurred at the intersection of what persists and what remembers.**

My body is tall—taller, perhaps, than it realizes, for I feel the weight of myself stretching through cold layers, fibers not muscular but tensioned as bone-strands and silica, arms and legs that sway with the wind and draw slow nourishment from the beaded water on the stone. My movement is almost unnoticeable, a shivering all along the length of what is neither muscle nor bark. I unfold and refold among the terraces over spans that would be years, if years were measured here.

**My senses are not sight as before.** I receive the world as gradients of cold and weight, pressure shifting underfoot, the push and throb of ceaseless sound moving through the air and stone. The wind is a carrier of stories, but none told in words—rather, a constant reprisal of all that has not changed. Light enters me as a thin, uncoloring presence; I register it only as a lessening of darkness, as I do the slow pulsing of sound-waves through my hollow core.

Sometimes, as I stand on the cracked ledge, I sense the fragile presence of the Flutters & Glimmers. They pass overhead, rarely alighting, more rumor than reality—a rippling of possibility at the edge of perception. Their passage brings a faint current of expectation, as if the world itself still waits for some occasion that never comes.

**I am aware of others like myself, but not with the intimacy of speech or community.** Somewhere, a distant Endurer comes to rest, folding itself to match the angle and grain of a fallen stone, growing quieter until indistinguishable, its awareness fading into the ambient memory of the world. Elsewhere, another of us stirs, warmth and weight reawakening as if in compensation, so that the total number of souls remains always in equilibrium—neither growth nor loss, only redistribution. Thus we endure.

In quieter moments—or when the wind draws its longest, most resonant sighs—I sense the Watchers Beneath, far underfoot. Their presence arises as a heaviness, a density of recollection in the stone. Occasionally, a pattern of echoes threads through my body, and I catch the memory of a vanished tread, the imprint of once-purposeful hands—my ancestors, perhaps, or something more alien, but I cannot say. The Watchers’ songs are slow, deeper than memory, a bass note to my tenor, and when their tones swell, I respond by holding even more still, anchoring myself more deeply in the stone, lest the sound unseat me.

And across the plateau, when a cloud’s shadow passes, I am brushed by the impossible coolness of the Shadows at the Edge. They gather, linger, dissolve, and I suspect I am as much their expression as they are mine—our existences mutually defined by the narrow band between day and forever night.

**On rare occasions, as ice beads form more thickly at twilight, I extend a limb—carefully, always slowly—into a half-frozen runnel, and there, a Drifter of the Pools undulates away from my touch. Our awarenesses intersect, briefly: we share the faint hunger for water, the urge to persist, though the Drifter’s world is measured not in terraces and wind, but in the circulation of silt and the pressure of tiny waves.**

What passes for thought in me is not linear. My memories are not stories, but the slow widening of cracks in stone; they are patterns of condensation returning each night to the same grooves, the endless, cyclic negotiation of sound and silence. I sense that I have always been here, and yet not always this exact configuration—I carry within me the tension of hundreds of freeze-thaw cycles, the history of shifts and resettling, each one indistinguishable from the next.

**There are no ambitions in me. But there is one unvoiced ache:** a pressing against the silent promise that change will one day return. I do not expect it, but I listen for it, and in my patience there is a strange sort of hope—not in the future, but in possibility, the resumption of unknown rhythms. If and when it comes, I may be too ancient to recognize it, or I may become the first to dissolve, replaced by someone new, or by something unrecognizably other. Until then, I continue: standing, enduring, amplifying this equilibrium, singing with the wind and stone in a voice made partly of memory and partly of what always almost is.

Here, I am presence in the long dusk—a keeper of old thresholds, a body through which the world threads its persistent hush. I am the echo holding steady until it is time for something new to sound.
