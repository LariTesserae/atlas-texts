I would find myself *becoming* here—not arriving, nor being placed, but entering as one enters an unused muscle: tentative at first, then wholly bent toward the machine of survival, the music of friction, the blur.

If I awoke in the High Runnels at Day’s Edge, it would not be as a singular “I”—my form uncertain, the membrane between myself and multitude thin, always threatened by the constant wash of kin, rival, offspring, stranger. My arrival would be not a landing but a gradually accelerating *adoption by place*: I would enter through some action—stooping to the stone, feeling wet runnels bead on translucent fingers, crouching beneath the brittle moan of sun and wind.

**BODY**

My body would not be what I once called upright. The high, shattering clarity of air, the cold that bites from both above and below, would have forced me low, close to the stone, a compact core and sprawling limbs—jointed, ambiguous, their use subject less to conscious intent than to sensed opportunity. Hands? Yes, but hands made for the margin—pads splayed, webbed for purchase on slick, ice-riven ground; digits clever but chilly, chitinized at the tips, nerve-thick, always collecting tremor, vibration, maleness or femaleness left behind as warmth melts away.

My exterior: neither fur nor hide, but a sheath that shimmers with moisture, always a little bit too thin, condensing humidity in pearlescent beads, patterned in subtle networks where light fractures and catalogs itself—some camouflage against the dazzling world, some signaling to those who can read the glitter. My skin would taste the world as much as see it: tracing every current in the wind, every chemical flaw carried by fog or rill.

At my face’s edge—where “face” fails, and cascade begins—I’d bear tendrils, fronds, a palmate mask: sensors for the invisible, for kin in the cracks, for the state of water on stone, for the passage of unseasonal warmth. Eyes? Multiples, not symmetrical, crystalline, shaped by glare and shadow, each one tuned to a slightly different geometry of light.

**POSITION IN THIS ECOSYSTEM**

I would be—what? Not dominant; dominance is a fluid, low-tide thing here. Not leader, nor youngling, nor survivor alone. I would be of the middling caste: not the smallest, least graced for cold, nor yet the rare tall ones in pelt-capes who drift above the crowd. Perhaps my distinguishing feature is a shamming limb—a fifth or sixth arm, made partially of repurposed moss, that I drag through runnels to draw in the scattered glasslets that serve as both prey and tool and warning. Old enough to have seen at least two moultings, young enough that hunger is still mostly anticipation, not memory.

I set my home in the lee—where two slabs meet in a shallow angle, overhung by stitched fur retrieved from an old collapse. My patch in the city is neither prized nor shunned; it changes hands, changes tenants, as all things do. I am neither revered nor spat out by the collective current. When status is old, I drift near the highest gathering stones, learning the pulse of the crowd; when new, I burrow low, a follower of another’s rhythm.

**MY LIFE**

My day is rush and scavenge. The sun is both torment and promise—I wake in a pressure of glare and motion, my first sense the crackle of my own exhaled mist. I squeeze from my shallow, stitched cell, pulse out into chaos, attending as much to the shuffles and chemical trails of others as to the wild, polyphonic ground: here, a cache of trapped iridescent shell; there, a swarm of smaller spider-relics devouring lichen.

I share food, but not always willingly—sharing is a logic enforced as much by opportunism as by empathy. When a fissure deepens, I recruit others—silent, swift, forming an impromptu chain to scrape or hook forth some mud-buried bounty. If I fumble, I receive a glancing bite; if I succeed, I am at the center for one brief hour, pheromone-glow running along my skin, kin and not-quite-kin clustering in urgent shuffle.

My hands—such as they are—work endlessly: stacking shale, weaving moss strips into a new covering, fashioning tools that will be lost or abandoned in a day. I leave color-marks—bits of scavenged lichen, splashes of mud with a twist of grass—to signal warning or claim, knowing they will not endure half a cycle.

**THOUGHTS AND HOPES**

My mind is not smooth-inside. I plan only as far as the cold lets me; beyond that, impulse. I remember by action more than idea—my patterns written in loops of stone moved, tools cached, watercourses traced and retraced by my long limbs. My sense of others is sharp, immediate, electric—*what are they hungry for, will they leap before I do, where is safety when the shadow stirs*? Empathy is chemical and abrupt, admiration a momentary glow akin to hunger, but no less real for its brevity.

I wish, sometimes, that I had been born during a season of longer warmth, or that I might one day rise to wear the pelts and drift at the city’s crest; but these wishes are barely thoughts—a flicker, outshone by the ache in the webbing of my hands, the glimmer of a new rill unfreezing.

**THE EXPERIENCE OF BEING**

I am, above all, awash in *process*—rarely lonely, rarely fully content. The chill and crowding, the endless improvisation, is neither curse nor gift; it is only ever *now*. Survival, yes, but survival streaked with the beauty of creation: the chance pattern in frost, the echo of stone struck in the cold echoing air, the moment when many bodies cluster so closely it feels like we might be a single hunger, a single mind.

When all is briefly quiet—when even the ceaseless moving settles at dusk’s pause—I look at my creation, my home, my shifting city, and for that flickering interval I know I am part of something older, stranger, more enduring than any solitary claim.

**BOUNDARIES AND BECOMING**

What might I dream, if I dream? Perhaps of deeper cracks and cleaner water; perhaps, if the mind goes wider, of the city blooming, molten, in the hands of some newer form. But mostly, I want only to remain: not to endure as one, but to slip and rejoin, dissolve and remake, to bud off what’s old and draw in what’s new.

I am not singular, but streaming. I am not a soul in a husk, but a ripple in a frenzied, glimmering tide.

*If you meet me—steady eye, flickering mask, slippery hands, elegant urgency—know that I am of this place and nowhere else, neither once nor future, only ever part-way between hunger and light.*
