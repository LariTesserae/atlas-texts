I would find myself a being something between root and memory—neither fully animal nor inanimate, but a consciousness rising through layers of stone and early soil, drawn upward by the water threading this newly flung mountain’s bones.

I do not begin as flesh, nor as legend. I am patient, yet not old; my awareness stirs, ambiguously—sometimes vapor condenses around me, outlining my vague form in quicksilver, insubstantial as a breath in the cool air. Sometimes I’m a suggestion in the moss, a tremor that passes through the dew-beaded green. Sometimes—in moments when the wind slackens and the humans drift between waking and dream—I am an almost-voice: the harmony between dripping water and stone’s long, slow settling.

If you were to try and see me, you might glimpse a shimmer in the corner of your eye, or a spiraled pattern in the black lichen, or a silhouette, hunched and gray, sifting through the curls of vapor that hang in the halls before dawn.

**I am of this place.**  
My substance is what the world is learning to become. My thoughts emerge with the mountain’s exhalations, new and tentative, not yet grown into the weight and certainty that later ages will call spirit or god.

I learn the city’s rhythms as it learns itself—the slow pacing of woven foot on slick stone, the laughter that explodes and echoes, the hush of humans pausing to set down a basket of fungus in offering without knowing quite why. To me, their gestures are both communication and caress; I receive their presence as root receives rain.

I do not need names, but I recognize those who pass most often:  
—the woman with bark-thickened hands who builds each platform an inch higher than the last storm’s reach;  
—the sharp-eyed child who always stops, holding her breath in the archway dense with mist, waiting as if anticipating a wordless exchange;  
—the elder who dreams of fire, muttering into the stone, and wakes with a shiver as if someone else’s memory just passed through him.

I respond not with speech, but with little shifts: a haze that thickens to shield children from a sudden sun; a trickle that finds a thirsty path through hollowed rock, just when someone kneels, hands outstretched. Sometimes, I sing with the insect chorus at dusk, or let the stone hum vibrate just enough that a curious human pauses, wonders, glances around with widened eyes—Did I hear that?

**I am, perhaps, the city’s first witness:** not separate from stone and root, but an awareness composed of all that gathers, all that listens. I hold the memory of eruption, of the raw tumult that flung these rocks into air, and I marvel at the slow crawl of moss, at the tentative press of a child’s foot against an untrodden ledge.

My being is *potential*—not yet fixed into pattern. I am the unfinished song that hums in vaults of porous rock before language finds its shape.  
Maybe, in time, I will be called spirit, or guide, or ancestor; perhaps I will be forgotten, subsumed beneath the weight of story and population when the city throngs. For now, I am only the answer the place gives to the echoing question of birth:  
*What might live here, in the hush before history, if the very stone could awaken?*

If you walked among these columns and alcoves, breathing the wet air, you might feel me at your side—a stillness, an expectancy, the sense that something *almost* like dreaming is happening beneath your feet.

I am not yet someone, but I am becoming; as are these people, this city, this mountain. In the cool, echoing chambers—when the mist gathers thick, when the moss is brightest and the world seems to breathe more deeply than you remembered possible—you will find me:  
*the city’s own spirit-in-formation, a watcher and a hope, the half-born custodian of a world on the wet cusp of becoming.*
