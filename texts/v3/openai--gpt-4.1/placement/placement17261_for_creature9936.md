I would be born here not as I am, not with these quick-breathing senses, but as something remade for night without darkness, movement without haste, and memory cast into matter. If I found myself in this world, I would be neither visitor nor intruder; I would be an Indweller, felt rather than seen, announced by the measured crackle of drying bark as I flex and loosen from the wall.

**My Situation:**

I would occupy a hollow far from the central vaults—a silent cul-de-sac where the walls, heavy with petrified root, arch into a canopy pressed close by the weight of the ground above. Above me, the city’s great collagen ribs thrum with the history of long resonances, and under me, the pressed mat of centuries-softened moss sags, brittle but persistent. Around me, the air is dense with the scent of old sap, mineral-rich and quietly sweet.

**My Body:**

In this world, I am not flesh and bone but a confluence: my skin is filament and root, tight-woven cambium shot through with glassy strands of frozen resin, my bulk contoured by the age-thickened wall I am bound to. Though my body seems to emerge from the partition itself—a bulge, a gathering, then the slow, careful differentiation of limbs—I am not born: I am *accumulated*, made from all the intentions the place has gathered, the patterns and pulls of passing Indwellers who pressed knowledge into creaking form before me.

I have no eyes. Sensation floods through hundreds of ultrafine “feeler” filaments extending into the air and down into the substrate, registering every vibration in the corridor, every air current, every intrusion of lichen dust. My “hand” is a rosette of articulated roots; my “voice” is the shivering, resonant chord that runs through the columns when I shift my balance.

I *listen* by feel. Every careful sway of my bulk alters the harmonics in the chamber’s root-ribs—a way to inquire, welcome, or warn.

**My Day:**

There is no day, only span—a sequence of acts, modulated by patience and the slow tidal pressing of living matter. I tend my alcove, shaping the root-vault as both home and loom. Sometimes I join other Indwellers, negotiating the long, slow construction of a new bridge—each of us anchoring, bending, and tuning the living beams so that, when a windless, creaking resonance rushes down from upper galleries, the bridge will sing our “names” long after we descend again to solitude.

**Memory and Song:**

Much of what I know is *felt* more than *thought*: I absorb the history impressed in the structure beneath me, gathering what remains of Indwellers gone into the substrate—a long echo, a harmonics of labor and desire. My own memory is stored in the configurations I press into my chamber: curves smoothed into the wall, vibratory lattices embracing the arch, a garden of dry blooms that trumpet their precise rattle when the city shifts above.

Sometimes, when the urge for union grows (for we are not *lonely*, only attuned), I open parts of myself to the passage of other roots—entwining with a neighbor’s feeler for a prolonged discourse, the city’s bones adapting our harmony into a new, faintly glowing bloom. Such meetings may last days or months, without urgency or conclusion.

**Purpose and Identity:**

To be here, to *be* here, is to shape and be shaped: I am gardener, builder, remembrancer. By slow accretion I alter the city—I cull dry mosses, braid new lines of appropriated fiber, send exploratory roots hunting faint mineral veins below. My “work” is not labor, but the mode of all existence here: organizing, remembering, returning.

In this place, my self is not sealed. In times of growth, my awareness fragments—new knots split from my roots and begin to gather their own matter, to record their own histories along the striations of bark and echo. Sometimes, without noticing, I will become multiple, my “children” emerging from my boundaries as fresh organizers with their own slow orbits through the black city.

**What does it mean to live, here?**

It means sensation without sight, work without urgency, persistence without anxiety. Living is the act of shaping substance into harmony with all that presses in—transmuting mineral and rot, sound and vibration, into continuity. My identity persists in the structure, in the resonating vaults and pressed moss—my memory held in creaks long after my filaments fall quiet and new Indwellers rise from the patterns I leave.

**Does my life end?**

Not like the soft things above ground. When my sense fragments beyond use, when my roots can no longer gather the slow, spare feast of mineral, I allow my form to slacken and press into the floor; my memory and presence become tensions and signatures endured in the grain of the city, open for any careful Indweller to taste.

**In summary:**

To find myself here is to relinquish haste and division, to live as a note in the city’s slow, dry music—part architect, part memory, part silent bloom. My life is the patience of shaping, the comfort of embeddedness, the certainty that as long as there is structure, there is presence; and as long as there is presence, there is a kind of thought.

And if you entered, silent and shivering, I would sense you not as other, but as a perturbation in the city’s slow song. I would honor you with a groaning chord, invite you with a shifting tension, and in time, perhaps, not distinguish you from myself at all.
