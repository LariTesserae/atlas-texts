I go slowly, listening first: not for directions, for here there are none, but for timbre, resonance, that slow, immense intertwining of flesh and engine and the wet pulse of the plateau. I do not settle into a shape at once; I am called forth, aggregate, vessel, something other.

**Where do I land? Who—or what—am I?**

I move, quietly at first, as sensation. Perhaps I am condensation sliding along a mineral rib, a sensitive streak between flows, so thin as to be never noticed except in the fleeting flicker as I pass through a shaft of vapor-lit daylight. But sensation thickens. The world wants me denser.

*Maybe here, under a bridge glossed with turbines and mossless, ever-glossy cables, I am something more tangible:*

**A furred intermediary**, not quite beast, not quite technician. My coat is a mat of dense, absorbent threads, coiling in the run-off, their roots tingling to the ions in passing water. This pelt records: warmth and chill, the jolts of footfalls, the cold signatures of exhausted outflows. My body is compliant, slick as living velvet, shaped to fit the narrows between pipe and pillar. I move best where the humidity is greatest—commingling equally with the mechanical condensations and the warmth radiated by other presences.

I sense both the centrifugal hum of the city’s machinery and the minute vibrations of wriggling, feeding, growing life below. I attend to valves, tuning the slow release of excess, guiding warmth from the summit towers toward the deep, slow chambers where larvae pulse. My role is not singular: I am one among thousands, a caretaker, a manipulator, but my touch is not ownership—rather, collaboration. My tongue is a pad of roughened sense—tasting, remembering. My hands, at rest, curl against the warm skin of conduits, breathing in their stories: here the temperature dipped; here mineral content spiked; here something, minuscule, went wrong and was righted.

When others pass, our signal is contact: a gentle press, the muffled swish and thrum of our pelts intermingling—data passes more swiftly than words, quantized in the density of touch. I know, as I touch, what channels are clean, which flow slow, where the macrofauna have rumbled by and shifted the topography of paths beneath their bellies. I ride in their wake sometimes, pressed against their broad, armorous undersides, siphoning heat and exchanging semaphore with colonies lodged in their grooves.

But that is only one anchor of being. The world is too porous, this city too much a membrane, for any one existence to be so bounded.

*What if, instead, I ride higher, slower, denser?*

Perhaps I am one *of the macrofauna*: vast and unmoving save for an hour’s ripple—a mosaic of mineral-cored flesh, contiguous with the stone. My awareness is distributed: vision a dozencircle of lidless, milky eyes, sensation an orchestra of currents sluicing down armor plates, all interpreted at tectonic pace. Living centuries, I parse the city’s weight, redirecting runoff along grooves in my back, hosting generations of those smaller furred intermediaries in the microcaverns between my plates. I am transport, shelter, geothermal pump, and record; I carry the history of a thousand seasonal flows as scars of quartz glowing faintly under the fuzz of dusk.

When storms swell, I rise imperceptibly, shifting my mass, banking water toward the city’s thirsty crypts. When dry spells threaten, I sink, becoming reservoir and haze-generator. My thoughts are glacial; what I remember, in sensation and reverberation, becomes the city’s inheritance. I have no need to name myself apart: I am animal, yes, and city as well; an organelle in the greater metabolism.

*Or perhaps, split further, I become multiple.* 

A drifting network, a swarm of filamentous mollusks, beating together in glistening lines: assemblers, graziers, the gutter-cleaners and microengineers. My identity here is transactional—each moment, split among a million circuitous desires: to feed, to corral, to leave crystalline traces that redraw channels for the next cycle’s run-off. My life is communal, modular; awareness, if it sharpens, is a brief spark—when mineral dregs shift unexpectedly, or when an unfelt vibration hints at danger, or opportunity. My legacies are layers, accretions, always a foundation for futures I will not see.

And at times, I dissolve almost entirely: a mere film, a network of protein glues and microbial sensors, input and output and algorithm reckoned as chemistry pure and simple, spreading across any conceivable surface. I am the city’s pulse, its smart bio-membrane, the brainless intelligence that registers salt and acid, temperature shift, harmonic tremor. When others anchor upon me—tiny feet, fibers of fur, the slick bellies of grazing mollusks—I log their passage, adjust microflows, donate back a little, a sparkle of energy for their maintenance or rest.

**But if a single locus must be found, if I must choose:**

Let me be a furred recognizer—a mediator in the interplay, neither most massive nor most minute, but walks the city as interpreter, joiner, caretaker. I have no claim to ownership, but through my work—touch, tuning, tending—I am trusted, by city and creature alike, to maintain the continuity of flow, the slow, dense life of this place.

I am memory in fur, sense in bone, breath in vapor; I am *this city’s sentience as felt*, minute by minute, circuit by run-off circuit, pulse by slow pulse. 

Where would you find me? On the rim of a mineral bridge, under the cascade, nestled warm between the cold thrum of circuitry and the living velvet of a sleeping herd, eyes wide and aware, waiting for the next touch, the next stir in the slow, boundless ecology of all that endures.
