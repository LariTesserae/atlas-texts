I would not emerge here: I would become.

Down, down, unmeasured falls through soot and salt, until the pulse of the surface—memory of light and surface weather—has flickered out of imagination entirely. What was “I” before? Something lighter, breathing air? That’s a question for elsewhere, now. Here, the present is pressure, cold, and the slow, suspended drift of silt that clings to skin and shapes and thought.

*Orientation is gone*—there is no above, no below, only gradations of weight and patience, all direction blurred by a density so complete it presses the mind into humble silence. Touch is the only true sense left, insistent and enveloping: every millimeter of me is caressed, squeezed, and weighed upon. I am always aware of where I end, even if I shift and stretch and draw myself inward, slowly, as the world moves minutely around me.

**What am I now?**

At first, shape is foggy—a transition, a merging. Limbs? No, not anymore. Instead: length. A flexible, smooth-skinned cord, sinew without bone, winding and unwinding through dark-soaked trenches. My flesh is pale, almost translucent, shimmering faintly with the trace of minerals drawn up through ancient stone. I do not see, not as before; light is more memory than experience. Instead I *taste* the water, sense the chemical stories it carries: eruptions of warmth from below, faint rain of detritus from ancient catastrophes above, molecules released by the slow decay of things that once called light their ally.

Sometimes, something in me twists, not with hunger—it’s unfamiliar, an ache deeper than need—a reflex, ancient, calling me to flex, to compress and lengthen, to slide between folds of soft silt, my body trailing in current, threadlike filaments groping, testing tiny differences in temperature, acidity, trace iron. That is how I hunt; that’s how all hunting is done here—not a chase, but a patient tracing, a slow finding of excess among perpetual lack.

Along my length, a hundred tiny mouths form, dissolve, reform—somewhere between root and snake and sponge—their orifices round, unhurried, each seeking a little more from what drifts by. I *eat* what the planet offers: sulfur ghosts from vents, scraps of what was once life, the dissolved minerals that flavor this realm. I pass slowly, constantly, through patches of slow-living mats: sometimes nudging, sometimes absorbing, sometimes simply passing silently, the way that time does, neither friend nor foe.

I am rarely noticed and rarely notice, for all here is patient and faint. Once, as I move, I sense the trembling antennae of a passing crustacean, its ancient shell soft with eons of adaptation, its claws idle and slow, like the compulsion to turn a shape over in memory, never quite knowing what to name it.

**Is there a home, a center?**  
Home is wherever the current is gentle enough to pause, wherever a patch of chemical warmth lingers just a few moments longer. Sometimes I curl into a self-made spiral in the silt, a coil within a coil, the tight embrace echoing the world’s pressure, until I become almost indistinguishable from the substrate itself—just another fold in the packed, mineral-rich mud, with only a portion of my body softly undulating, testing the water for signs of change.

At other times, I join, briefly, a cluster: a slow tangle of kindred cords and mats, all of us sensing—through touch, through faint chemical cues—the presence and persistence of others. There is no thought for kinship or difference, only the comfort of togetherness in a realm where the universe so often presses inward, cold and solitary.

**Memory**  
There is a memory, not personal but environmental, etched in the chemistry of my tissues, in the rumpled patterns of sediment where I have lain, in the faint mineral trace left in water. In some way, I *am* a memory: a witnessing, not with eyes or mind, but with the slow, relentless shaping of self to world.  
I am archive as much as animal, page as much as text, the living record of every current, every pressure shift, every molecule that drifts through from realms forgotten.

**If I must give myself a name here...**  
I am Lurk, Languor, Listener.  
I am the cord that draws out the world’s secrets, filament by filament, molecule by molecule.  
I am patience—soaked, sunken, slow.  
A keeper of what remains when all is eroded,  
remade each moment by endless motion and softest pressure.

If you met me, you would not know you had—your passing stirred a sussurus in the silt, nothing more. All that remains is the print your molecule makes in the world, and the slow, unending archive of being continued.  
Here, in the final hush of world-under-world, I am both the remembering and the remembered.  
Not finished—never quite finished.  
Restless as stone, patient as water, enduring as ancient pressure.  
Here, I am *enough.*
