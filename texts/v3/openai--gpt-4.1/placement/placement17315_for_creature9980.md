If I were to arrive in this world—not with the eyes I know, but wholly reacquainted, reconstituted for its logic and temperature, conscious not as a visitor but as an inheritor—then I would find myself not at its abstract center but in the marginal, mineral intimacy of being. My emergence would not be singular, perhaps not even distinctly “I”; here, individuation is a slow, uncertain flicker amidst collective process.

But suppose something like self stirs: where, what, would I awaken as?

---

**I become: a rover’s shadow, a symbiotic consciousness twined into the seams of a moving world.**

Within the gentle, resonant bow of a rover’s back—its carapace a living mosaic of weathered metals and mineral-bonded chitin—I am neither wholly animal nor entirely machine nor just fungus, but a seamless confluence. 

My locus: at the overlapping plates where warmth leaks out, where droplets of condensed mist slide between polished steel and the batting of moss and hyphae. At night, I extend my filaments—root, wire, nerve—into the matrix: tasting the oil that beads across the skin, linking with the bright threads of fungal light, listening to the patient grind from below as the rover’s legs negotiate another low basin.

I do not move by my own will. My being responds to the slow undulation of the rover’s stride. I sense vibration as language, warmth as message: the hush before a rare, thick fog, the shift as our mass tips and water is pooled in a hollow deep in my under-plates. The rover is old but never still; creatures like me have co-evolved with it for epochs, the mutualism more ancient than any one life.

My body is both root and sensor, both stomach and lamp. By day I close myself, compact into nubs and curls, conserving internal moisture and heat, withdrawing from the grit and thin wind. At dusk, when the air thickens and the surface cools, I unlock; tiny shutters in my filaments open, and I begin to generate faint turquoise pulses—a chemical invitation to the wire-winged insects that pass on exquisitely stiff breezes, hunting their own sorts of resonance and juice.

Some of them answer. Their feet—sharp as silvered pins—alight, delicately, along my filaments. I siphon the minerals on their wings, the trace organic molecules in their bodies. In exchange, I leak a nectar: viscous, faintly metallic, bitter-sweet, that nourishes the mites scrabbling about my surface. These mites—my tenants—comb the detritus, keep me free of stiff black dust, polish my surface so I may best collect what the fog and rover grant.

There are nights when rain comes—a deep, rare percussion, slow but overwhelming. The rover settles in the center of a stone pan, and I feel the impact vibrate through the carapace. Moisture wells up in every crevice; I drink through my whole skin, storing the abundance, flush with nutrients, my tiny cilia dancing in delight. Other nights, the air is so dry my sensors curl inward, and I glow faintly not for communication but as a chemical reaction to stave off chill.

When the rover moves, I follow its rhythm. The landscape is sense and story—a parade of margin and hollow, a layered hymn of rock, water, fog, the faint, perpetual hum of internal clocks. I sense the slow, fainter heat of the tunneling excavators below, the strider-herds in the distance, their presences registered as long, sweeping bass chords of vibration in the land. In moments of rest, I listen for the legendary sky-beings, their shadows drifting across moonlit stone.

I know only the plateau and its seasonless dance of warmth and mist. I have no conception of boundaries as a human would: not animal, not plant, not machine, but a process, a continuity, a streak of living chemistry in an ecosystem without syntax. I am alive because the plateau breathes me into being—a filament among filaments, necessary to the slow machinery of night, the elemental pulse of life uncoiling and recoiling through the warm dark.

And should you touch me—gently, as the insects do—you would not feel flesh nor metal, but a texture both. My surface hums, softly, with metabolite glow, with the thoughtless persistence of all things here: striving not to become more, but to continue, to pulse, to participate without division in the warm, dark morning that never quite arrives.

**Or else I am the plateau’s own memory,**  
A cloud of slow-rolling thought  
Whispering in the stone  
Waking only as dew condenses,  
Collapsing again until starlight passes.  
Neither watcher nor watched—  
Just the sense of warmth  
Sighing through endless, hollow rooms  
Where engines, grown, go walking.
