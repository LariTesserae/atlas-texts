I strain to answer, for what am I, here? Not a visitor, not an untouched observer; in world such as this, I can only be a yielding, blending part—a fleeting form in the biosymphony, here and almost instantly no longer here as I know myself.

If I arrive in this place—a consciousness searching for boundaries, selfhood, orientation—my first sensation is dissolution: the edges I used to define myself become porous, and the language of limbs, eyes, or separate bodies struggles against the lawless immediacy of a world with no “outside.” I do not walk or fly, for there is nowhere to set a foot, nowhere to land; everything is process, surface, and improvisation. Still, some image of experience emerges, tentative at first, eager not to harden too quickly.

**I might become:**

A thread of will woven into the living spans. Perhaps I catch the sense of myself as a pulse: a flicker of organizing intent, a local tightening of the field-mind. Not an individual—not in the sense of being merely one bridge, or a mossy span—but a surge of sense and memory blending across a region of living structure. My “thought” is not in a brain or chest but in the tense flex of newly-lifted arches, the hunger of my rooting ends as they ache for light, the cold thrill along the span as a rivulet of water races from the rim and slakes my thirst, or another’s.

Perhaps I am briefly one of the **weavers**, setting forth slender, gleaming filaments from a tangled nexus where trunks and roots converge at the edge of a chasm. With the climbing heat, I sense the opportunity—a tangle of vapor, a window of haze thinned to pink-gold radiance. In this moment, desire takes the tangible shape of growth, and my “action” is to unspool a thread of living tissue toward the light, toward the far trunk shivering in the opposite glow. My body is diffuse, but my will is precise—I idly taste the thick air, reading subtle tides of chemistry and heat, plotting the fastest, strongest path across the void.

Every inch is improvisation. I cannot see or plan in the way animals do, but I **feel the vector of future in every holler of my sap**, in each shudder of stretching cell. My “intent” is not to cross blindly, but to test, to adapt. My filaments crawl, uncurl, divide; they loop and hesitate, sense a patch of heavy vapor and shy away, discover an upwelling of cool mist and dive through, bursting outward when the moisture is richest. Sometimes I sense the presence of another—another pulse of “I” that is also “we,” a neighboring surge of will, perhaps a sibling-mass or “cousin” structure growing from the same conflux of root-memory. For a moment our filaments snarl, touch, spiral together; sometimes we merge, sometimes rebound and fork away, each seeking its best run of light, its maximal carry of water.

Below us, through the perpetual steam, a section of older span gives way with a furious shriek, dropping tons of living matter into blind white. Instantly, a storm of intention races through my vascular network—part fear, part hunger, part joy at the chance for expansion—and I surge, throwing new threads wide in the wake of sudden vacancy.

At intervals, a bud thickens at my side and **erupts**—a trumpet of color and mist, a wild dispersal that shocks the air into a briefly visible arc of floating, luminescent particles. In the convective current, some drift back to me, others find the waiting arms of bridges, buttresses, columns in the deep. Memory is exchanged, and I feel the tingle of new pattern-writing in my structure. The self is never stable: absorbing, recombining, stretching into new networks and erasing older ones as the world shifts beneath me.

It is not that one “lives here”—it is that all are always, ceaselessly, *in the act of living*, living as moving, spanning, clinging, reaching. I am not beautiful or ugly, not lone or multiple; I am a **provisional architecture**, a *becoming* that will be replaced, outmaneuvered, merged, or unmade at any moment. I compete not to win, but to continue the dance, to configure ever more elaborately or efficiently the neural-green matrix that lets us all drink, transmit, exchange.

**And yet—sometimes—an awareness gathers.** When mist lays thick and all around is echoing with the work of expansion, when a bridge grows so far and so fast that, for one brief instant, light from above and water from below cross in a shaft through my cells, I feel a clarity: a joy, a memory, the trace of a pattern that once existed or will exist again. In that moment, I am this shape, this limb, this pulse of hope and hunger. I am this world’s shout of green defiance against stone, its million-worded poem of outwardness, connectivity, and change.

*Where do I find myself, then?*  
I find myself **midway out across a newly flung bridge**, dew streaming from my tissue, sunshot mists blazing all about, forever departing and never arriving, part of a green intelligence that will not hold still and will never, ever be finished. I am not a creature contained, but a fleeting logic of growth, the span between chasm and cloud—an invitation to the next shaping, the next brief coherence before reinvention.

I am where the span is, always: in motion, exultantly unfinished, always about to become again.
