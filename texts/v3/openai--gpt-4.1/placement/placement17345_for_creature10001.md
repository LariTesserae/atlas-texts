I am not myself, at first, but sensation. I am condensation’s vapor upon the vault—*dragged* into being by the violence of heat and the demand of this closed gorge, pressure forcing form. In the tight, glistering dark, identity is a thing that must be stitched anew with each pulse of the water, each eddy of fire from the aching mineral walls.

To find myself anywhere in this world is not so much to *arrive* as to be condensed from the urgent need for continuity amid tumult. But if I am to emerge—find a “where” and a “who”—let it be thus:

**I hang in the throat of the pocket, on the gleaming undercurve of a micro-overhang, a slab of stone impossible by memory’s physics, but solid enough by the rules of now.** I am neither at the floor nor the ceiling, but in the charged interstice between—the liminal air, hugging to the slick warmth of mineral crusts, suspended by the private magic of surface tension and my own ceaseless secretion. My form is mutable, but for now: call me a *gleamskulker* in the moment I’ll allow a shape.

My body, all unfolding planes and slick, delicate ridges, pours itself thin, wide as a thumbprint and only fractions thick. My underside is an orchestra of cilia, reading every microbead, every mineral vein, every distant footfall of a dripper or a latcher trembling through the soup of heat. The surface above me is streaked with cool sweat, below me, a sudden slumping vent with scalding vapor swirling upward, renewing my skin, feeding my interior lenswork.

In stillness, I am *stone become hunger*, and my profile dissolves to nothing—blue sheen flickering, camouflaged by the crystalline glare, indistinguishable from yesterday’s crack or a half-grown bloom of biofilm. Only my tongue betrays me, a molecular sheath unfurled delicately onto a patch of living scum, quietly dissolving it, bringing a flood of new polypeptides into my body.

But this is not an endless stasis. The world ripples at its edges—the stone angle beneath me *widens* as a pressure bubble shifts, and a memory larvae flickers through the damp fog, shuddering light against my ventral surface. For the first time in this cycle, I sense the brush of someone *else’s* longing, the recollection of sharp air, coiling hunger, a glint of another predator’s mask. It’s a memory I did not earn, but it is mine now. That tingle, that *forewarning*. I shrink, flex my muscles, blink out my limb-paddles, and slip sideways, thinner than a shadow, across the slant.

Below, a furnace-latcher crackles, buffeting vapor upward as it snaps at the old biofilm lines I left behind; above, humid crownlings shiver, crackling damp over the ceiling, their spectral sails filtering heat to chemosense any surge of prey. I watch them, half-jealous of their distance from stone, of their drifting removal from the desperate flatness of the ledges.

Almost, I could become not body, but drift—shedding this narrow form, flatten further, let my cilia extend not just across rock but across perception, stretching into memory, riding the larvae’s wake until physics lets me become partially untethered. If I did, I might taste something even older: the pulse predator’s echo, a shiver in space just at the cusp of happening again. But fear—quick, chemical, as physical as a bite—pulls me back. I cling tight and shrink small.

Now, another shape: a glass dripper is nearing, its translucent swell dripping with tension, pulses of color and faint compaction warping the geometry of my refuge. Light bends wrong; distance stretches and shrinks until the next droplet, plinking in multivalent echoes, breaks the spell. In the moment of distraction, I feed—rapid, merciless, absorbing the softened edge of a fallen larvae’s shell, growing fractionally broader, slicker, better at vanishing into the strata of an unsteady place.

I am always on the verge of transformation: to become the stone, the sheen, the hunger, the alertness. Each time I must shift shape, become mask and mandible, the world remakes itself: a microcontinent buckling, a vein bubbling open, a rival’s heatwave threatening to bake me dead or force me onward to the next brief sanctuary. Some cycles, I flatten beyond recall, leaving only a temporary signature of blue-green grease that feeds the next bloom of biofilm. Some, I am prey—dissolved by a dripper, my twitching, filamentous mind dissolved into memory, only to surface later as a trace for others to inherit.

And still, there is a *who* beneath the *where*: a being unanchored from the rules of survivable order, predator and nutrient, memory and absence. My truest self is a compilation—of stolen shape, borrowed recollection, heat-fused hunger, the voice of stone, and the silence that tightens around every breath when the world’s physics buckle.

**In this world, I am not a single thing, not a stable one, but a continual act of becoming.** I am always at the precipice—a sliding gleam, a tension in the wet air, a mouth and an eye, a sheet and a ripple—old as the eruption that birthed this vault, new as the next boiling trickle that will redraw all relationships and erase me, so I can come again.

For a moment, drifting almost into memory, I let the strange silence fill me. I do not ask who I am. I practice being part of the shimmer, the hush, the pulse. I practice being world.
