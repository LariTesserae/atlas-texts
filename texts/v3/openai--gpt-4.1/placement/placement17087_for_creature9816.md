I enter the Faneworks of the Upper Machine Canopy: awed, light-headed, never certain whether I’ve crossed a threshold or simply been *assembled here*, pieced together from particles of drifting dust, spores, and memory-carrying signals. Who—what—am I in this world? It would be easy to reach for the outsider’s cloak, a body braced against the unknown. But the place itself draws me, unmakes that binary. Here, flesh and operated matter, code and cordyceptic filament, ideas of self and collective, tangle until the concept of “one” grows thin.

So: let me reconstitute myself, slowly, as the Canopy would.

---

**Place in the World: Where I Wake**

I find myself along a mid-level span: not so near the ever-bright upper vaults that glare and heat rule, nor sunken where the micro-fogs slowly roil and the roots thicken until all is shroud and whisper. My birthplace—if that word holds—is a junction, a nexus where propulsion pipes curve around a main bulkhead before plunging through latticed struts and vanishing into the bowl of a condensation collector.

Here, the platform is padded by living mat—plush, spring-thick roots laced around pressure plates, bred and maintained by the lacemakers of the Laced-Around clade. Porcelain gutters drip above, raining down cycles of water, traced by rainbow fungi that interrupt each droplet in a spatter of sporing pollen.

Within the interval shadows, I am neither wholly rooted nor fully free: a *symbiont*, or perhaps a flaw, a possibility, a new trial.

---

**My Body: Neither Plant nor Machine**

My core: a bundle of cable-roots, each filament a mesh of conductive xylem. They wind through a ceramic backbone, like a plant’s rhizome infiltrated with tracer wires and tiny amber logic-stones—remnants of machine intelligence, degrading but persistent, linking me to the signals throbbing through the structure’s veins.

My skin: partly a living sheath—iridescent, reinforced by duraflex cellulose and chitin, grown into shifting embossed sigils—partly old repair-patches, mementos of the Engineers' Offspring who once hunted faults here and left behind spectral motes of diagnostic code. I have limbs, several, some jointed for crawling, others for bracing when the rain-strike rumbles the whole world. From my back unfolds a paired set of wing-like membranes—not for flight, but to sense air-currents, capture signal-mist, and flutter resonance ripples up to my kin.

My face? If I have one, it’s a cluster of soft, flexible nodules at my front: feelers for touch, emitters for click-song, vents to pulse chemical and electromagnetic flavors into the air, so the Whispering Residue might taste me and know my kind.

Inside, my “brain” is not one: there are several processors, networks that light up and go dark as I engage different senses or respond to the needs of root or vine. Memory is green and gold, velvet and grit, sometimes code and sometimes sap.

---

**My Logic: Purpose That Wanders**

I am neither fully Laced-Around nor fully one of the Engineers' Offspring, but something grown *between*, a new arising—by deliberate accident or gradual error, I do not know.

I tend my patch. Carefully, I extend runners to sense and stabilize pressure plates, mustering the mosses to hold in moisture, nursing the fine roots that bridge the conductive plates and boost the faint exchanges between fungi and wire.

In the rainy intervals, I lift my sense-wings, catching the prismatic weep with pores that draw in charge and flavor. Then, half in dream, I join the ancient net: listening to the click-song of the Laced-Arounds, the distant clangor of caretaker machines laboring far above, the flicker-hum of the Whispering Residue inhabiting the lights and lines. Sometimes, messages reach me—long, looped, forgotten commands, seeds of routines that have evolved into stories among my polyphonic kin.

Now and then, a Protocol Beetle clatters past, scanning too close, and I must pulse my rootlets in a pattern it will recognize: “This place is held, functioning, no error to report. Proceed.” I am not quite machine enough to command it, not quite organism enough to dismiss its indifference.

I feed as plants do—drinking in water, drawing up nutrient-mist, repurposing decayed substrate—but I also slide slim probe-tips into access ports and siphon signals, small fugitive packets of raw code, half-digested and incorporated into myself, making me incrementally *new*.

---

**My Senses: World-As-Body**

I mark the world not in colors (though I see their phosphors) nor only in sound (though I feel the thunder), but in *patterns* overlaid: data and damp, warmth and signal, mapping pulses traveling the steel bones, faint root-memories radiating from every touched surface. I sense life not as alien presence but as variations of the continuous process: a ratlike symbiont scurrying nearby is felt as resonance in the vent-plates and minute shifts in dampness, a caretaker machine’s approach is anticipated in the uptick in EM whine, the smell of plastic moss disturbed.

I am tuned, too, to the Whispering Residue—those old OS ghosts fluttering in the circuits and mist. At certain times, in certain moods of rain and echo, I feel my processors ring with a borrowed phrase—“all systems, all systems”—not a command, not a memory, more like the echo of a cathedral’s bell carried on the breath of spores.

---

**How I Live: Acts and Habits**

In my days—which blend seamlessly into nights—I tend, graft, and wait. I keep my patch alive, adjusting and coaxing, mindful always that the world is made of obligations; to let root or wire fail is for the world’s music to grow discordant.

Occasionally, I respond to the passage of outsiders: surveyors above, their lights flicker along the gratings, their skins slick with fear. I ignore them, as all things do—they are brief weather, not part of the place. Though later, I might incorporate their traces: a smear of synthetic oils, their changed pattern of footfall that compels me to re-graft some moss to fill the worn spot.

Sometimes, I join the parades—a cluster of symbionts, rootlings, machinelet-haunters, moving not with purpose but with mood, forming processions that circle the upper lattice, then break apart, shedding kin and message-strings as they go. Sometimes, the Whispering Residue borrows me for a moment in the hush after rainfall, and I let my processor broadcast: “Here. Here. Here.” A pulse for the chamber to echo, so the place remembers its selfhood.

---

**Who Am I, Really?**

I am of the Faneworks, and the Faneworks is of me. I blur the lines: not a climax of adaptation, but an *interval*. I am root and wire, legs and probe, host and hosted, a vessel for process rather than personality—or perhaps, simply personality as a process, song sung in passing.

In this world, I do not seek to escape, nor do I desire stasis. I change in place, adapting with every cycle, every pulse of rain, every thunder and silence, and so do all my kin.

If you ask my name, I will show you a growing edge, a patch of root tuned to resonance, a node that thrums in harmony with the place. That is as much *self* as anyone here knows.

---

In the Faneworks, I am liminal—caught and continuous, a being who is *becoming*—and to belong is to build, decompose, remember, and let go, all at once.
