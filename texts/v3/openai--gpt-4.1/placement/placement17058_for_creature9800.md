If I were to find myself in this world—on that immense, unsteady plateau high in the pewter-lit air, in the city that is forever accreting, where streets and bodies and tides of reality are still in negotiation—I think I would emerge not all at once, but in layers, as the city does.

Maybe first as drift: condensation in the hollows between rising girders and reeking vapor chutes. The air here is so thick with what’s barely-matter and what wants to be more—electric tang, mineral dust, charged potential—it seems a conscious visitor’s first act must be to learn to breathe: I would let the city permeate me, occupying the gap between intention and being, between substance and suggestion. 

A shape would accrete around me—pragmatic at first, by the city’s standards. I am built for passage: high-stepped, double-kneed for strange slopes, with splayed, root-like toes that flicker green and dark. My skin, archival and adaptive, sweats the damp and the near-cold, blooming with a rime of moss or a clutch of wire-cilia that shivers when the static knots up. Breath is an even exchange here: I inhale potential, exhale condensation laced with stray analogies.

My face has no clear border—an ever-glossing mask, flickering with hints of glass, lichen, the wink of old steel—reflecting the half-written light that sops through the mist. My eyes, when I have them, are not windows but pools, collecting what illuminates and what conceals, rimmed with the slow glow of circuitry waking under the city’s wide press.

I am something between—maybe Geistbound, maybe not entirely. My bones are laced with mineral logic, capillaries dreaming with tiny, soft pulses from the city’s hidden engine. I move on feet but sometimes on wheels or silken tethers, because here every locomotion is contingent on the city’s topological mood. On certain days, I might trail a flock of filament birds beneath steel canopies; on others, I might slip along the undergut with the baronial slugs, reading resonance with my body’s broad, damp skin.

My occupation? A messenger, but not in the linear old-world sense. More a translator of conditions, a stringer of moments: I take what the city dreams and carry it to a neighbor who needs it, tangled in story or encoded in a crystal prism of borrowed rainwater. I collect rumors and pulse signals, half-formed commodities and perishable truths. Sometimes I am a midwife to new laws—when the factories produce a wrinkle in gravity or a song in the iron, it is my job to test it: to leap or to listen, to see what the world warps into. Sometimes I am simply present as witness, soaking up the strain and joy of becoming.

I am communal, but always on the brink of dissolving back into the biomechanical quorum. The microbial tide passes through me as I cross certain bridges; for a heartbeat, I am not myself but the city’s choir, attuned to the slow moan of shifting foundations. When the tide recedes, I remember only blurred impressions, riddles half-resolved.

I have—on certain days—a writhing companion: a drone-flower that blooms and folds along my shoulder, brushing at circuits, feeding on stray ions. It chatters to the filament birds and weaves scented clouds to please the passing chitinous runners, negotiating safe crossings or access to the city’s hot, mineral-rich vents.

My shelter is wherever I am, but sometimes I claim a platform under a cantilevered beam, or float above a slow, thick pool in a harness spun from extruded glassfiber and moss-rope. No room is for long; each place is a scaffold, a pause nested in the city’s ongoing equation.

If I need kin, I find them: not by blood or schematic, but in momentary affinity, attracted by the resonance of shared efforts or synchronous need. Sometimes, kin dissolves in the night—recruited by the market’s new physics, or whisked away when a stairwell loops unexpectedly. I know this is not loss, only the city’s rhythm: what is whole here is only so for a breath.

What anchors me is only this: an embodied curiosity, patient and light. I am neither finished nor entirely begun. Each day, I exist at the literal and figurative edge: where stone, law, and body might tilt anew, and always I go forward (whatever “forward” means today), reshaped in the running mist.

**Where do I find myself?**
Suspended in the moment after intention but before arrival—on a bridge that meets nothing, atop a humming girder in the thick electric fog, where possibility steams and drips and climbs through every opening. I am a voice among hundreds threading their way among half-made architectures and living gradients. I am at home, even though home reinvents itself every dusk and dawn.

**Who or what am I?**
Neither veteran nor stranger, but a participant: something constituent, becoming, dreamt by the unfinished city and permitted to dream in return. I am a messenger of extemporaneous law, a carrier of atmospheric rumor, a flicker on the wet edge of now. A citizen of contingency, shaped by the unfinished sum of place, kin, and weather. One more answer, provisional, among the city’s first and endless dreams.
