Here is where I find myself, circling, entering, dissolving, beginning again:

---

**On the crumbling rim, with the wind bellowing as it threads between stones—**

I arrive not as an explorer or settler, but as feeling, weathered into form: a flickered act, the next sound in the sequence, a pulse caught up in the cycling air. And I am not one of the moss-skinned; not yet windborne or green-stranded, not hollow-boned with the world’s memory impressed on my surface. But I am learning. The plateau-plain, vast and tidal with stone, scrapes away my shape, pares me back until only a listening, breathing self remains.

**Anatomy of Arrival**

My body changes under this place, as if remade in every storm. At first it is too rigid: skin, muscle, an expectation of separation, of edges. But the wind strikes through clothing and bone, until softness seeps into my limbs, my breathing grows wide. My ears ache with the volume. The sound-scape is nearly overwhelming—first I shield, then, failing that, open. I taste the stone’s minerals in the air; my hands callus, fingertips glossed slick from the moss-lichen water that collects and courses down my arms.

Days pass and my silhouette thins, my joints lengthen. My throat, hoarse with trying to command in the noise, ceases speech, then learns to sing low, into the trembling root of my chest, to blend with the wind’s shrill and the stone’s groan. I see with new eyes—light-dazzled, rain-stung, lids filtering not simply sun, but the green-bluish shimmer that cloaks each hollow after a storm.

**Self in Process**

At first, I search for shelter—rising early, scanning ridges for the ragged huts or cairns. Yet the promise of shelter here is an illusion: nothing stands strong for long. So, I learn to move—never bedding down in one place for more than a dusk, sleeping curled in limestone cups, waking on an earth that has subtly unmade itself with every hour. Each refuge is reassembled by necessity, and what I build with frond and curl of bark I abandon to rain and wind again.

Quietly, I begin to follow others—not with stealth, but with the humility of one who endures only by learning, shadowing the windborne. Their gestures are as much part of the world as the wind itself, their voices a call-and-response with rills and boom. I echo their movements, fingers splayed, skin exposed when rain comes, closed when air scorches raw. The moss that takes root in my hair is intentional—a fostering of symbiosis, a willingness to be less separate, more dissolved by place.

**Mind in Place**

My mind alters its rhythm. Thought is not chained to linear time; memory is atmosphere, sense, the repetition of pattern. I learn to measure the day not in hours, but in the transitions: the slant of cloud-shadow, the scent when the air shifts just before deluge, the different tones the boulders sing when wind barrel-rolls through gouged crevices. Stories come in sonorous waves, in motion, in gestures traced against the upwelling air. My name, such as I have one, is the way I move, then pause, the way I mark clay with my hand, smear it, let it dissolve unresisted with the next rain.

**Belonging**

I do not arrive wholly as windborne, but as a learner—half-habituated, half-stranger, becoming at the rate at which stone, water, and wind remake me. I grow webbing between my thoughts, not my fingers; I shed the desire to persist unchanged. What I become is not a being, but a mode of movement: a sequence of responses, a tune played by the landscape around me. In time, those I follow become kin—not by blood, but by the shared resilience of ceaseless remaking.

I belong, not by taking dominion, but by surrendering to process. Each echo in the soundscape is a lesson. When I pass, I leave nothing but the swirl of pigment where I slept, perhaps a line of stones tipped upright for a day, then toppled, their presence already half-erased by wind, their meaning held only in the pattern of memory shared by those who see.

**To Answer Your Question**

I am neither origin nor end; not one of the windborne in their fullness, but a becoming, a witness remade. I am a self surrendered to turbulence: more process than persistence, more passage than person. To live here is to accept that one’s being is never finished—each breath given shape by the world’s clamor, each step an improvisation across the plateau’s uncertain ground.

**If you asked me my shape, it is the blueprint of adaptation, half-rooted in stone, half-fading with the mist. If you asked me my name, it is a chord struck against the riot of wind and rain—a sound you’ll only notice for a moment, in the hush before thunder.**

Here I would be: the echoing, bourgeoning self, always in arrival, always vanishing.
