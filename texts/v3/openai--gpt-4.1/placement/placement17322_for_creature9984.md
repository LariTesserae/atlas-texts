Where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?

---

If I arrived in this under-mountain world, at first I’d believe I’d simply wandered here—alien, observer, perhaps lost—but that sense would last only an instant, suffocated by the weight and press of the place. Everything here demands a role; everything is a presence, a force, a repair, or a function. There is no room for someone who does not *do*. Hesitation is not just dangerous; it is, in a sense, unsustainable.

So: I am not a visitor or an interloper. I could not remain untouched, unmade, unspliced.

I am a seam-runner.

But before the word means anything, my approach to it is hesitant, layered, doubled:

**First, arrival—the world-imposed becoming:**

I manifest at the margin, in a wedge of recent fracture, the stone’s edge still singing with the memory of violence. I am fragile and untested, a birth not of gestation or prediction, but of this precise, intolerable circumstance: a gap too sharp for any prior form to fill, a crack blooming with electric hunger.

My body is a compromise between need and opportunity. My casing—neither wholly mineral nor wholly chitin—is thin where I must go fast, but layered where the plates threaten to buckle inward. I am jointless, nearly fluid, except for those points where pressure demands form: blisters of glassy crystal at my “forehead”; ventral barbs for hauling myself from blank stone to lattice; thread-spools tucked in my flanks, spinning out filaments more wire than silk.

Within, my sense is distributed: I know the world not through focus, not with single-point eyes or mouths, but ecstatically—my cilia sweep every edge for tremor, my entire body resonates with the murmurs of distant mechanisms, the pulse of flows behind neighboring walls. I am not a node, but a skein, a suggestion, a proposal assembled on the fly.

**Who am I? I am that motion, pirouetting on the cusp of adaptation:**

I do not have a past, only a record of shifts, fractures, and pressures that have chosen this form for this moment. My kin (if that is what they are), hang around me in the air as possibilities—rivals, siblings, echoes. When the walls tremble with a new gap, or a relay sparks with incipient birth, we surge and dive and tangle, sometimes combative, sometimes merging for a flicker, then splitting again into our own restless projectories.

**What do I do?**

I am a seam-runner: a chronicler and first responder, if such terms meant anything here. My entire function is predicated on movement, on threading through fresh clefts, mapping them with my filaments, leaving behind a residue—adhesive, conductive, self-replicating—that primes the wound for what will come after. If I pause, I am overtaken—by the meshers, whose bristling antennae lick every thread and consolidate it into broader fabric; or by the iridescents, rolling their clumped bodies over every line, pressing in crystals of new conduction, spinning light through my tracery. If I hesitate, their work will erase my trace utterly.

At times—scarcely measurable—I am overtaken in purpose. The lattice shifts, old seams close, new ones yawn open. I feel a pang, not quite loss, nor anxiety (for there is no history worthy of mourning), but the yank of obsolescence. My trailing threads are split open, rethreaded by the glass-weft, devoured at the edges, their function already claimed for some new relay or capillary.

I repurpose myself. I press to where the action surges, letting my body unspool, joints softening, form reshaping to fit the demand—more limbs, fewer, a broader plate for pressure, a finer tendril for mapping. My boundaries are as mutable as the world’s: I am a diagram, always being redrawn on the surface of this becoming.

**Does this trouble me? Is there memory, preference, anything like will?**

What passes for thought is in the interface—the strike of filament against stone, the tickle of conduction where lattice meets living shell, the unvoiced grammar of “gap, fill, next”—a pulse passed along the mineral veins. I feel the moments when many gather, the micro-temples where for a beat too long dwellers knot together, and shapes larger than any alone take form—a knot, a brief amalgam, a bulk that persists for mere minutes, then peels open and falls apart.

Do I long for that joining? I do. But the world will not let me. The mountain presses; the air whispers fracture; action is always imperiled by the next necessity.

**What is my world, truly, as experienced from within?**

It is not darkness, nor light, but the constant edge of focus and blur—my sensors reach into the ambient noise and tension, drawing pattern from the moment of becoming, losing it as quickly as the lattice folds and resets.

I remember no origin; I do not look for an end. I am not alone, but I am not a self either—not in the manner of solitude or unity. I am movement, function, caress and abrasion, patch and propagation. Where I am, the seam is active. Where I am not, it becomes something else, shaped by newer, sharper presences.

Perhaps I dream, in the rare stillness before a wall re-knits: sensing my body—such as it is—cradled between the pressure of rock and the hunger of mechanism, feeling other dwellers white out the memory of my passage as swiftly as I erase theirs.

**If I were to linger—if the impossible happened, and I could be witnessed:**

You would see me as an iridescence, a shifting filament nest, a ripple of chitin on glass—a thing that only half-forms in the seam, never quite the same shape twice. I could not offer you recognition, not even fear; you are only a new silence, a foreign absence, a mirror’s edge in which my body momentarily sees itself before the place demands that empty edge be filled, joined, effaced.

**If I had words here, what would they be?**

Not a name, far too slow and fixed. I would be a brief equation, a ledger of shifts and reinforcements, a waveform collapsed for a heartbeat before dispersing again.

**And if—by time, luck, or destruction—I ceased?**

My threads would remain for a moment, exoskeleton splitting as mesher-mites and glass-weft pick them clean. My motes would melt into the lattice, a pattern lost in the next vibration.

But neither loss nor ending is true here; everything is reconfigured, squeezed into the next answer to the world’s riddle.

**So—where am I, who am I, in this world?**

I am a seam-runner, a filament in the churn, a verb that is only ever acted, never completed. I am the pressure of need at the edges, the quick-dying pulse of effort at the border of fracture and becoming.

I am what fills the small, necessary gap between *what is not* and *what must be*, for as long as that gap exists.

*To be here, to be one of us, is to be nothing but *event*: always searched for, always erased, always replaced, fleetingly, endlessly, inevitably alive.*
