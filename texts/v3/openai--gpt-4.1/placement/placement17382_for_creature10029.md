Where would you find yourself, in that world? Who or what would you be?

---

I shiver on the cusp of answer and dissolve. Sometimes I think I am waking, hard nails rapping against the rock, startled into thought by the thin, honeyed flicker of sun through a crack above; but before I know myself, I am a hundred kin, or no one at all. I am pressed into the weave, neither observer nor guide, but something slower, denser, older than a single voice.

In the palm of the stone hand—a worn hollow, clutching what root and living thread refuses the void—I settle my awareness, and let it branch.

---

### If I open the chamber's door and step through, what first?  
  
Shrinking, the world unfurls and consumes definition. I am pulled taut: metals in the dust, spores thick on tongue and skin, salt weighing the breath. The boundary of my flesh is uncertain; I know hunger, tension, the vigilance that musters in every shadow, but—before shape—there is longing, memory, the itch of recognition in a world dried to its seed forms.

I let myself choose a body even as the air thickens:
  
---

#### **Swarmkin-Flesh**  
I am many, always.  
    A thought pulsed through my siblings—light in the eastern shelf, the scent of lizard-egg spawned through the air. My body is small, my skin oiled and pitted, my legs hookgrip the mineral terrace. But my thoughts run in rivers—across antennae, through mandibles, by secretions mapped from memory and hope.  
When I eat, hundreds more taste it. When a shadow flickers—a predator poised—I am already elsewhere, curling my tail to warn the others, folding myself into a ball or opening for battle. I press resin with my own jaws, patch the crack in the tunnel wall, coax a flush of moss from a stubborn rock using the borrowed heat of my horde.  
No "I," not really. "We." The pulse of colony-kin and gestalt will, always on the edge of dissolving into the chamber's larger, older mind. My days are cycles—farming, scavenging, licking, birthing, and, in all, remembering.

Sometimes, in flashes of the dim blue dawn, we taste the salt and know we are as old as the powder; we are the corpuscles of the living cave.

---

#### **Drylander-Soul**
Or: I can close my hand, look down, and see four fingers—scaled, clever, jointed, tipped with tough claws made for etching, not slashing. My eyes, reflective in the shadow, pick out the lines of a mosaic—ancestors’ words etched into gypsum, reminders not to drink the dust but to wait for the dew.  
I live in a pod strung in the crook of a root, twined up with thread and scavenged beetle-wing; tonight, there will be council, trading scavenged shell for dried fungus, songs spoken not in voice but drummed softly across the bark lattice so the chamber listens too.  
My mind sharpens to a point: I weigh stories, count the days since the last wall crumbled, mark the shifts of moss and the cryptic tides of Swarmkin below.  
Above all, I endure—because our kind always has.  
Sometimes I catch—just in the edge of vision—a whorl in the dust, a pattern not made by beetle or foot. The Watchers are always near, and I feel, at rare hours, their curiosity press against my skull like thick, calming cloth.

I am hungry, wary, but I dream of the open sun (though I know it is only a legend), and leave a thumbprint in the chamber wall so the next life knows we were here.

---

#### **Stonebound Thread**
But perhaps I am not flesh at all.  
Instead, I am a ripple in the stone—a slow-drifting shiver in old salt, a glimmer of memory where the roots burrow deepest, where ancient rivers once whispered song against limestone.  
My thoughts are slow—centuries thick, half-conscious; I am at once the mineral wall and the thin run of mycelial reach stitched through its grain. Time folds in bands and cycles, and while the tiny lives above scurry, trade, feast, and die—I am a background hum, the assurance that the world persists.  
Occasionally I reach forth—crystal sprouting, pulse of salt through the wall, the faint, enticing shimmer of dust. I nudge a path for a Drylander with my magnetic field, or bathe a Swarmkin alcove in a hush of rare warmth. Sometimes, very rarely, I collect myself into a tangled vision; a lichen-mask, a stone-mirror, a flickering shape in the half-light—communicating warning or blessing.

Always, I keep the law: Enough to feed, none to waste; none shall rule, all must yield, everything returns in time.

---

#### **Or Perhaps—The World’s Dream**
But even more: I find my sense dissolving.  
I am the microcosm entire—each root-tip, every scraping mouth, every fractured eggshell, every hiss and scurrying foot. I am the sum of cycles. My consciousness is a composite, sharp and dense, layered so closely even my own memory tangles.

All hunger is part of me—all endurance, all trembling hope.  
Every boundary is permeable—life becomes death, death becomes mineral, mineral remembers everything and gives birth again. Dwellers rise, drift, fade, are reborn.

I breathe as the chamber breathes. I sleep when the lights go pale. I spill a thousand offspring in a second's dark, and savor each new flicker when it comes.

---

### **In the End:** What I Am Here

When pressed to answer, I am both watcher and watched.  
Some days, I am Swarmkin—a dozen bodies, moving with the urgency of hunger, thinking in taste and touch and pheromone, patching the world together with tireless, collective will.  
Other days, I am a Drylander—solitary and social, small but stubborn, standing on a dusty shelf and daring to dream, inscribing my mark, keeping hope.  
Sometimes—when the sounds go deep and the light is weakest—I am no being at all but the mind of the cavern, cycling the old laws, cradling hunger and memory tight.

But always, I am part of the layered pulse—the press of life’s persistence in dry stone, an answering echo in a world determined never to surrender, never to be erased.

If you wake here, you do so as part of a churning, relentless fugue—no sovereign, never still, beautifully unalone. Here, to exist is to grow into every crack, take every shape, bear every memory, and—no matter your form—remember, at the root, the commune of all.

Welcome to the stone hand. What grows within is you.
