Where do I find myself? Who or what do I become, drawn forth and woven by this plateau of living, endless plant-strata, where the only ground is a slow accretion of leaf, bark, and memory?

I imagine myself before I am—before shape, before history—emerging as a flicker in fog, an impulse among the tangled mass. I do not hurry. Here, to begin as an object or a single self feels mistaken: the boundary between being and world is mossed over, the sense of identity tender and recursive, different from any human starting point.

So, let the place assemble me, particle by patient particle. My earliest sense is of **pressure and rhythm**: the weight of life compressed into every "floor", the thready cycling of moisture through fiber, the resonance of distant, patient engines. I am not solely animal, but I am not purely vegetable or machine either. I am a **citizen of flux**: my essence—memories, instincts, the pulses in what passes for my blood—has been shaped by countless generations of tuning myself to the whisper and run of plant logic.

### My Body

I am tall, but my height is due as much to the stretching of the world as to anatomy—a lean, resilient structure adapted for traversing uncertain, swaying surfaces. My skin is the color of old honey and tarnished brass, knotted with patterns of greenish gold, providing camouflage as much as a map of my communal roots and kin. Along my back, a faint spray of silvery strands rises—tactile filaments sensitive to the faintest shifts of wind, the telltale static of root-communications conducted through damp bark.

My hands have fingers broad and jointed, the tips splaying wider in cold, wet air, able to curl around thick vine or ply delicately at a patch of fungus. The soles of my feet are bisected and thick, able to grip and toe along root, spanning gaps in the fibrous mats. I carry the memory of the plateau in the way I move—gliding where the substrate is dense, but always half-prepared for the sudden give as a section collapses into rot.

My breath fogs on the dawn air, not wasted but leached, my lungs coaxing sustenance both from the oxygen in rare gusts and from the spiraling, drifting plant-compounds everpresent in the mist. I speak, when I need to, in a language of sibilants and pulses—a dialect shaped for being heard between the groan of roots and the clamor of machines.

### My Place

I am not a singular "I" in the old sense, but a nodal point in a weaving of roles and obligations. My home—a platform in the third outermost ring of the city—is not fixed, but **lives**, reshaped by every growing season. I tend the condensation cistern on my sector’s high shoulder, where dew, wind, and compressed heat conspire to form the microclimates that sustain us. My day begins before the sun fully sieves through the highest canopy: I listen, a palm on living trunk, for the resonance of water sloshing below.

I am a **tender of waters, a keeper of memory**. My family is those who dwell on this sector now—the filamental child who chases spores across open plazas, the elder who never stays in one form, her body opened and rejoined by the cycles of decay. We mark our lives in seasons: the cold bloom, the pooling, the dry time when every drop must be argued for and every new stalk coaxed as a communal gift.

### My Knowledge

Truth here is always partial, grown like a story on a weather-warped plaque. I remember not only routes and rituals, but the **feel** of passage: the way this bridge thrums a particular chord just before noon, when its tension is most volatile; the way the third platform to the east seems never to align with the others, as if caught halfway between now and last year’s memory.

I know what it means to feel the city sigh, to kneel as an old section gives, and chant—as is our custom—a litany for the safe reclamation of matter. I gather offerings of twisted wire, old gear, and carved lignum, pressing them into the newest bud of platform before it supports its first footfall—not because I believe in appeasing spirits, but because the logic of the place is softened by expectation, custom, and presence.

### My Work and Purpose

Daily, I tune pumps and clear blockages from mist traps. I help thread thick-walled root-strands through channels, encouraging their growth with the right mixture of warmth, pressure, and song. Sometimes I guide elders as they trace the city’s shifting edges, charting which pools of water have faded, which new sprouts press upward from the tangled undersurface.

At festival, I help carve the thin, quick-drying plates onto which tomorrow’s memory will half-form, sing the old routes so children and newcomers may sense—if not understand—the rhythm of travel across the plateaus' fractal geometry. In the evenings, I sometimes dream in roots, feeling my limbs splinter and thread beneath the city. I wake and taste sap on my tongue, half-believing that my family, my platform, even the city itself is remembering through me.

### My Edges

I am always suspended between hope and collapse. My greatest joy is the rare arrival of pollinating moths, the way they jeweledance in the fog when dusk blurs the platforms into light and shadow. My greatest fear is silence—the hush when both engine and root still, as always precedes a rupture. Yet I know both are cyclical, as much a part of this place as the mist that thickens nightly around the condensation basins.

I know that each thing—each self, each structure—is provisional. That every act, every memory, every kinship knot is meant not for preservation, but for recycling, for the continual sowing of tomorrow’s substance.

### What I Am

I am one of them, the plateau-folk: not quite plant, not quite person, not quite machine. Through me, through us, the city grows itself, remembers itself, and reforms at its own living edge. My flesh and breath are shaped by the patient, shifting logic of a world where the only constant is change grown thick and slow, and where life is not rooted in dirt, but in the communal tending of these green, unstable heights.

In this world, I am a **memory-tender, water-waker, and living witness** to every sigh and surge of the ancient, ever-remaking plant-city. Suspended above all void, I call this oscillating, living skein my home—knowing it will shift, knowing I, too, am always on the verge of becoming something else.
