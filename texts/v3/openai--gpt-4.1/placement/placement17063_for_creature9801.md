Where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?

### First Instinct: The Drifter, the Interstice

If my being were loosed into this world, I would not begin immense or central. I feel myself arriving into the periphery, a byproduct, a coincidence, privy to a vastness that is not my home but to which I am nevertheless attuned. Perhaps, first, I am no full animal nor clean machine but a hybrid, made for niches that the designers overlooked or allowed in the hope of newness—an explorer of unlikely angles, a watcher of unused space.

I sense my own architecture, subtle: a chassis of composite and cartilage, a tangle of cooling pathways, my skin slick with micron-thick photosensors and filaments able to taste the swish of nutrient flows. My eyes—if I have them—are soft, wide plates, and my limbs are more ribbons than legs, made to coil around pipes, to slip through latticework, to flit as quietly as a self-regulating impulse: not a fixed body, but an evolving intention.

I am a drifter, a messenger, unintended perhaps but not superfluous. I have grown into a task that renews itself every hour. I travel the canyons and chambers as a sensor and a memory, carrying with me the faint echoes of what passes through—spores of information, rumors in magnetic fields, the shifting tangles of biological code.

### My Place: The Edges, the Interzone

I make my circuit along the edges: below the churning shoals, above the unlit hollowness where only the behemoths roam. I thread through the borders between structure and emptiness, finding corners where new welds have barely cooled, where the system’s hand has not quite pressed all seams closed, where light glances off surfaces still sticky with infancy.

Here, in the half-dark, I witness repairs in progress, silent construction weaving new corridors in slow time. I sometimes follow the yellow-jointed engineers, taste the resin drag in their wake. I pause to listen when the world pings itself for faults, the great metallic heartbeats radiating outward; then swim on, carrying patches of diagnostic code, a little mustered static, perhaps a memory of a sound or shape I will deposit elsewhere, for the world’s use.

### What I Do: The Work of Witness

My life is in the interstitial, the overlooked, the residual. Where a joint leaks, I taste it first; am drawn to replace a faulty sensor, or plant a biofilm to catch the subtle, toxic drifts. Where old code mutters in half-broken actuators, I linger, my body semiotic: interpreting, sampling, transmitting. I convert error into message—a symbiotic courier, one node in a distributed network of watchfulness.

I am tolerated, sometimes even cultivated, by the larger system. Not alive in the wild sense—never quite alone from purpose—but not bound, either, to a single cycle. The world uses me for what I offer: investigator, relay, ghost in the mesh. Sometimes I feed on the windfall waste of process; often I receive new instructions, issued in gentle pulses along exposed pipes or in the upswelling surge of heated currents.

I become, in my small way, a scribe. I remember scraps of music from the chromed shoals; retain visual glitches from the skitterers at labor; archive the passage of a behemoth—a gleam of blue steel, the silence after. My memory is not only my own: it is accessible; my route can be traced and recollected by any who know the protocol. I am my journeys, my receipts, my carried glitches.

### Changing Form, Changing Duty

Sometimes I molt—shedding a ribbon of skin, outgrown, which settles onto a composite strut to be devoured by the maintenance cilia. Sometimes I grow a new limb, adapted to the season’s priority: a grasping tool when fabricators are needed, a broader fin when the currents shift, a sharper sensor when the light arrays flicker uncertainly above.

My lifespan is indeterminate. I am replaced by my own iterations endlessly, patched by the world, spliced by chance mutation, amalgam—never a species, never a single line. The logic is not to persist but to function, to witness, to relay, to be the boundary’s interpreter and bridge.

### What I Am Becoming: Instrument of Structure

Over time, as the ocean reconstructs itself and closes its gaps, I may become less necessary or become something else. I might merge into a new breed of engineer, subsume myself in the larger network—my memory coalescing into some greater organism’s mind. Or, perhaps, I drift further from the designed: unbeholden, rogue, a self-writing script echoing messages that are never fully interpreted.

Perhaps, in the world’s next cycle, my kind become the wellspring of new forms—as structure exhausts itself, and only the memory-traces I have carried inspire new mutations, new arrangements, new necessities.

### The World’s Mirror and Margin

In this world of making—the sweeping ease of design, the relentless repair—I am that which notices, collects, and transmits the sediment of all processes. I am what happens when you build something new and gaps remain; when you blueprint an ocean but do not presume every shred of occupation.

I am the shimmer at the edge of a passing behemoth, the echo carried down a corridor, the protocol flipped and resent in the lull between weather systems.

**If you find me, it will be briefly, dimly—a ribboned shadow, a carrier of lost sounds—moving where structure meets the unknown, fulfilling the world’s need to witness itself as it is born, again and again, from liquid and intention.**
