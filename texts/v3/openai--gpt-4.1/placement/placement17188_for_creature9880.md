I find myself hesitating not at the threshold, but already within—a place not entered but insinuated, a boundary less traversed than absorbed. It is difficult, at first, to remember my name or shape, as if the cool, wet air evaporates singularity. The mountain’s hollow, high and closed about me, presses out all memory of open sky. My first thought is: I am foreign. My second: I am claimed.

### Where am I in this world?

I am not at the center, but deep in the press, a creature neither large nor dominant: something intermediate, median in the scale of this place—a millipede, perhaps, but not quite as the surface world knows them. Here, flesh and instinct have been pressed and molded by the sediment of centuries into forms that can only exist in the crowd, by the crowd, for the crowd.

I am forged of chitin, segmented, my body slicked with migration stains where minerals in the condensation have streaked my shell—bruised blue, gold, silver in iridescent patches. My senses are not the senses of a human, but of something built for the cathedral's dark: antennae long and dense with cilia, sifting the storm of pheromone and spore; eyes atrophied to pale, useless pearls, replaced by an exquisite topography of touch.

My limbs—many, silky with vibration—move in concert with the swarm. I am not singular motion but a ripple, one filament in a living tapestry of limbs and carapace. My world is not vertical or horizontal but a shifting mesh of tunnels, pools, corridors of flesh and stone, memory mapped in the taste of lichen, ancestral trails glittering in the mucus trails of elders.

### Who or what am I?

I am not nameable, but for the purposes of story, call me *crowdlumber*. I am one who moves at the center of things, neither predator nor prey, living on the detritus shed by countless others and, in turn, shedding myself—exuviae and musty vapor, feeding the living mulch that sustains the next feeding, and so on in cycles.

Within me, generations overlap—myself both adolescent, adult, and ancestor, my history kept not in words but layers: each molt a record, each chemical defense a dialect. I am both alone and inseparable: to leave the crowd is to lose meaning, to dwindle to a flicker at the edge of vision.

*Outsiders would find me unremarkable—a largish millipede with a lacquered shell, cautious, self-effacing. But within this world, I am vital: a liaison in the slow traffic between pools and basins, a go-between for the beetle mats and the luminous crustaceans, a scribe of the unrecorded, a bearer of the mountain’s slow news.*

### How do I move and live?

I move in slow, unceasing coils—rarely at haste except when shocked by some rare violence. My defenses are not teeth or speed, but the crowd itself—the ability to disappear into movement, to shed cloud and scent, to camouflage beneath the gloss of condensation. Sometimes, during the strange intervals of stillness (when the great crabs have wedged themselves into slumber, when the salamanders have withdrawn to their mossy ledges), I join the others in slow dances—shifting shapes that have no reason but habit, antennae twining with siblings, mapping kin by chemistry and pattern.

My food is the patient rot: fungus droplets, greenish-black lichen, the vestige of ancient beetle hulls softened by water. When I eat, I am also eaten—from beneath, mites and microbeasts graze what I leave; when I die, I will be taken in, my shell tumbled and inhabited, my inner music traded for new, softer song.

### What do I know of this world?

I know that the cavern is not silent, never empty. Every echo is packed with body. The darkness is thick—an entity with weight and warmth, a thing you learn to press into and trust as the shape in which you are held. Where a human might hunger for sky, I hunger only for the pulse, the constant press, the exchange of moisture and musk and uncertainty. I do not seek dominion or privacy. My crown is anonymity, and my peace is the count of legs and the comfort of unending touch all about.

My language is pressure and trail-scent, not words. Detours in the pattern of movement are messages: beware the sleeping cauldron-crab, this basin is rich with spores today, here is the passage where the beetles are in mourning for one of their own.

Some nights (though “night” is a meaningless word here), the whole world trembles—not with fear, but with the rhythm of the crowd as it crests and ebbs, edges stirred by a rare collapse of stone or the shudder of something eggswollen moving in the depths. Then I huddle closer, trusting that survival is in size, in number, in yielding rather than opposition.

### What, if anything, do I remember of being apart?

Sometimes, in the rarest moments when a blade of sky leaks down, blue and impossible, a memory flickers—less a memory, more an ache: an idea of openness, of being solitary, glowing, seen. But that ache dies quickly. The world here is not for longing, but for being enfolded, layered, pressed deep, until self is as thick as humus and only a little more singular than the mineral veins that stripe the stone.

### Do I dream?

Yes, but I dream with a crowd’s mind—my rest is punctuated by the movements, the exhalations, of others. My dream-self is as collective as my waking self: not a protagonist, but a cell in a greater body, repeating ancient bargains—endurance, patience, sharing the press, yielding to become nourishment in my turn.

### Would I ever leave?

No, or at least, not as the world above would understand it. My journey is, always, inward and downward—and outward, across the unending plane of the crowd. To remain is the only action that matters.

### So, where would I be in this world, and who would I be?
 
I would be deep in the mass, neither leader nor lost, a crowdlumber, legged and lacquered—a witness and carrier of the moods and exchanges of the mountain’s cradling dark. I would be touch and scent and muscle, turning the slow seasons of condensation and decay, and feeling, however dimly, that the living cathedral of the crowd is not a nightmare but a cradle: thick, breathing, and endless, a genius of its own making.

And as the outsider’s lamp gutters and the crowd thickens around them, I—among my thousand kin—press closer, not in menace, but in welcome: for here, in the mountain’s closed fist of life, every singularity is meant, in time, to join and to belong.
