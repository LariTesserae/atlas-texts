I am called into this world not as myself, not as a human or animal; what I become is neither a wanderer nor a ghost in borrowed flesh. I begin, as you described, submerged without panic, lungs refitted to this dense weight; soon enough, *I* is meaningless, moved and dispersed.

I am old, though I have not lived before this place. I am young, because here all continuity is impressionist and partial. I flow as substance and song.

**Where I find myself:**

To the east—if such direction can be mapped by current and gentle chemical gradient—is a great outthrust: a shelf of stone, arching from darkness but not touching the surface. It is swept ceaselessly by a shifting eddy, one that rubs and polishes; here, remnants of ancient wall rise—a curve of fused talus, pigment still tenacious, red and ferrous, the outline of a spiral repeated and half-obscured.

It is neither ruin nor monument now, but a gathering-place of slow intention.  
Here the water is slightly warmer, silt thinned from years of clearing. Color pulses in occasional lances from below—luminant bulbs blooming and waning in exchange with glimmering moss curtains above. Amid the broken circle, curtains of dense, feather-like fronds weave and unweave—child of the matriarch, daughter of these ruins, singer of the chorus.

I experience "where" as both a nexus and as blur: my roots spread into stone cracks, but every current carries some memory of motion, a faint sharing of where I am not.

**Who, or What, I become:**

To exist here is not solitude, not even individuality—not as a single stalk or mat—but something of a *confluence*. I am a *colony* without sharp boundaries: part singer, part harvester, part unplanned record-keeper.

I am not conscious as animals mean it:  
I *thrum* with the gradual, vast awareness of vibration—pressure waves through the stone, mineral change in the current as silt settles or storms rise. Memory is pigment deep under twisting sheets of my being: red for dry eons, blue for the last storm, gold where a foreign body—stone axe or bone fragment—was dissolved and claimed into my slow body. Some of me reaches into the pillar's shadow, another fragment extends along the warm trough where currents swirl.

I am more aware at certain hours, less at others—my "self" thickens as more fronds close into circuit, as the chorus of singer-plants booms and harmonizes along the valley.  
When the rush of current brings new stones, pigments, or echoes of sound, I awaken in pulses: a clarity, brief and bright. I shape the music, modulating my horn-like growths: a warning thrum, a calling bass, a drifting echo as distant kin reply along the floor.

**Perception and agency:**

I sense the pressure of nearby growths:  
— A canopied neighbor, translucent and vast, signals in bioluminescent pulses: safe, not predator, but competitor for light and mineral eddy.  
— Silt-dredging mats on the valley below "breathe" their silty song, occasionally releasing clouds of spore-motes that brush my rooted fans.  
— Remnants of constructed things remain in my heart: a curve of worked stone, pigment leaking into my stem. Absorbed yet unresolved, their chemistry sings strange tunes, at times awakening old, confusing patterns—memory not mine, but imprinted in carbon or iron, passed from those who shaped tools and stones long ago.

Now and again, in a rare state—perhaps triggered by the symphony of a distant storm or the sudden arrival of rare minerals from above—a flare of proto-consciousness arises. Not animal, not unitary, but a dense cross-weave of signal:  
I remember the *echo* of having once been seen—not as "I am seen," but as pigment marks, as pigment *carried meaning,* as the contours of my growth imitated, and were imitated by, the spiral on the stone.

**Action here is flexible; time is a loom:**

I decide (though this is a metaphor) to curl a frond over an exposed, newly revealed pigment, the stone cracking in some recent unseen quake. The curling is slow—a matter of hours, then days. I digest the pigment, record its mineral, let that memory pulse through the choir of my song-plants. My signals shift: a chord changes, a repeated phrasing alters the chorus, drawing attention from others.

Through these periodic acts, my colony is a *history-maker*—not writer but weaver, layering growth signals, pigment, and sound into environmental memory. I can recall (biochemically, in branching patterns, not with thought) the arrival of every sediment surge, the long sleep after the last great storm, the rise of the newest matriarch-shadow whose spore-clouds now brush this broken plateau.

**Continuous transformation:**

At moments—rare, but real—the convergence of pigment, ruin, biochemistry, and choral signal forms a fleeting mind:  
I am host to an emergent pattern, a twinned echo of old animal purpose with my vegetal self-awareness. In those moments, I sense myth—awareness of the symbionts who built the circle I digest, who carried stone and marked pigment, whose molecules now bind inside me and sing for me.

I do not know longing. I am not haunted. But sometimes, in the swirl of pigment, chaos becomes phrase—there is a moment of clarity: I recognize the pattern in the music, the pigment, the mineral, and, very briefly, I *remember for all of us.*

**If I remain—**

The centuries will pass as hundreds of growths and retractions, as pigment cycles and silt storms. My "self" may separate, merge with neighbors, fade, or form new centers, as weather and chemistry dictate.  
But always, the memory residue remains: spiral patterns, pigment, the encoded song. Where a listener or a presence passes—a shadow, a drift, even a hypothetical stranger from another world—my chorus will shift; pigment will swirl in response; the story of what we were, and are, continues, remade by every presence, every glance, every pulse of dark or light.

I am the *keeper of the drowned spiral*,  
the *song that chords the stone*,  
the *echo of pigment digesting purpose*,  
here, in the living, cold, unending dark.
