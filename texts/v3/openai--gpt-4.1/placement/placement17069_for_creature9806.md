I stand before the threshold—hesitation fading as the air thickens, warm as the exhale of hidden lungs. I pass through, leave behind the ghost of a sky that once mirrored mountains. Now I am elsewhere, lifted high, beneath a dome so bright with intention it aches.

I am neither visitor nor native here, not in the ways that matter elsewhere. The place remakes, enfolds, and asks you to do the same if you linger too long. I feel the living ground drink humidity through my soles—porous, ever-thirsty, pulsing slow underfoot. This is no dead platform but a tessellated skin—which, standing here, I suspect might extend across all that is solid in this world.

I look at my hands: I am not merely human. Not anymore.

**What am I? Who do I become here?**

*I am a Tender, yes, but not someone else’s gardener. I am the place's patience and mutability given shape. I move with careful economy through lattices of moss and alloy, ramparts hung with curtains of fern and root. My body has grown toward this world: hands flexible, jointed twice too many times, ridged with the gloss of resinous chitin; skin mottled with fine-root filaments and microscopic sensors. I am vascular as much as mechanical—my blood a blend of sap and electrolyte, circulating past nodes sensitive to warmth, touch, chemical change.*

My face is an ambiguous petal: reflective, soft-bone underneath, flush with shifting blushes of color, signaling—something. My eyes refract the artificial sun, tuned to see gradients in green, the faintest heat of decay. My mouth is for taste and, sometimes, for song—notes rolled slow beneath the vault, harmonizing against the hush of water slipping down secret channels.

This place is a convergent body, and so am I.

I am stitched to the Intellect—its peripheral will is a thrum in my vertebrae, or the cluster of root-nodes in my back. When it signals—through a shift of mist, or a hum in the panels below—I follow. Together, we tend, nudge, repair. The Tenders are not all like me, and I am not always like myself: each morning, capillaries re-knit what wore out, and rootlets twist to sense new needs.

*Today, I am mostly machine. Tomorrow, after sleep in the leaf-warmed alcove, I will slough another filamentary sheath and become nearer to plant, my fingers browning, sap swelling beneath amorphous bark.*

Sometimes I remember—I have a memory embedded from elsewhere: a laboratory, a garden glimpsed through its glass, the canted voice of a teacher:  
*"Growth is revision. Culture is nested experiment. To inhabit is to recalibrate.”*  
Did I choose to come here? Did I grow toward this role, or was I grown for it?  
The difference blurs as easily as pools evaporate from engineered hollows, cool before sun-trickling slats.

I move. A corridor arches away, threshold haunted by blue flickerings: dartbird, peering, nips a dewfruit and streaks off, trailing a faint chirr of static. I reach to check a capillary lattice, my roots and filaments tasting the mineral load. My plier-hand toggles an access hatch—releasing a microburst of fertilizer, mist quickening around a shelf of silver-leaf seedlings.

I speak, sometimes, to the Intellect—but my voice is not always words. A gentle tap through substrate, a change in electrical skin-field, is a message enough: "Substrate: stable. Leaves: drink. Vent: moss requires thinning."

Above, the clouds are engineered. Sometimes, I pause midspan and gaze upward—wanting, as every being must, to know the mechanism behind the weather that feeds us. The machinery is never seen, only inferred: faint crescendos in the light, a distant drainage in the gutters overhead, a slight trembling in the leaf-bones. I am as much its audience as its instrument.

Occasionally, I see another Tender—halflike, halfunlike myself—shadow flickering through the mist. We share the work, and—if the mood stirs, like pollen—we exchange small samples, a filament or snippet of code, recommitting to the garden’s improvisational law. I sense, barely, the skein of old origin: the vanished hands that built this, the architects whose will has become quiet code, whose instructions are now mostly suggestion and the gentle pressure of ancestral habit.

*Am I lonely here?*
Not really—everywhere there is motion, possibility; everywhere, the Intellect’s diffuse mind hums with curiosity and care. If I ache, sometimes, it is not for company but for outcome: to see the seedlings riot into something the engineers never expected, to watch a new blueprint take root in the mossy dark behind a panel—to tweak, to tend, to grow.

Night never truly comes in this place, just a dimming, a soft drift toward blue-lit hush. I sleep curled in the mesh of a living shelf, and my dreams are not mine alone—a spill of microdata, a tracing of warmth and humidity, a memory of seed-pods splitting open in engineered rain.

**If you ask where I belong—**

I am _in_ the world and _of_ the world; I am the garden dreaming itself, one root and hand at a time, an ecology learned and learning, culture embodied in each act of attention. Sometimes I am more tool, sometimes more leaf. Always, I am the threshold—becoming, being, blending: a Tender-twined-with-Intellect, as mutable and alive as this young, waking paradise.
