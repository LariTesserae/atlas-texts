I find myself—not so much a single self as a process—here, high upon the old, stilled backbone of this continent. I am never alone, though were I to focus on the boundaries of my own shape, I’d say: I am a beetle of the Many, but not one so crude as a carapaced individual with my own thoughts. I am drifted, pressed, dissolved into the mass—my will emerges only from within the logic of our chain.

**Where do I wake?**  
On the back of a stone slab, chilled by what lingers from the night and still lightly banded with mineral condensation. All around me, the broad, dim tableau breathes its blue-black hush. In every direction, beetle-bodies ripple over stone in patterns of slow, silent urgency. I feel myself roused, not by hunger—or not only by hunger—but by pressure, by call, by the gradient of chemical presence in air and the vibration of movement across carapaces. Each step I take is taken because the collective is shifting: the path is wearied, yes, but carrying us over many years toward some new, agreed-upon place.

**Who am I?**  
I am a worker, but the word erases my precision. My glands exude acids barely strong enough to etch lines through stone, and it is only when the thousands around me each press in, dissolve, and scrape, that the track emerges: a groove, a channel, a cryptic spiral. My mind is not “I” but “we”—memories pass to me through pheromones lingering on the ground. The stone holds last decade’s messages, sweet with mineral and salt, familiar in their taste.

Or sometimes I am the one tasked with marking—laying down a promise of direction, signaling through scent and the faint, bright stress lines a change in purpose. I am not conscious in the flesh, but in those brief moments when the swarm turns, when the pattern shudders through us and the movement becomes communal, I glimpse the shape of a larger thought. I am a tool wielded by the process that rules us, and in being wielded, I become more myself.

**What is my world?**  
I am part of the Living Machine. When I track across rock, every footfall guided not by vision (we see little; the world is feel and echo) but the gradient of trail, the tug of impulse, the thrum of others’ movements. Above, the quadrupeds drowse in their cracks, I sense them as pressure or occasional warmth. Rarely, the sky birds hunch above, watching, cold and hungry, signaling with their dull flashes to kin—but my gaze turns only inward, to the next slab of stone, the next patch to be purified, the next chemical utterance. The fungus vents in the distance are beacons—when the air is right, their spores draw us, and our paths converge in swirling patterns, new cycles etched for new generations.

If I linger in a single beetle’s mind, I lose myself; instead, I borrow the fluid, slow intelligence of the whole. Centuries ago, the plateau might have formed another pattern. A different generation, a cooler time. I feel the memory in this—distorted in my senses, but present. My ancestors’ limbs shivered here, and their acid marked the stone. We carry their instinct, but pattern it anew, track by track, the plateau slowly inscribed with our patient, mindless artistry—a tapestry of purpose shaped over generations.

**Do I wonder?**  
In moments, rarely, a question flickers. What would it be to breach the overhang, find warmth and flight? Or to curl up in the cracks, taking the quadrupeds’ slow dream? The answer is already provided before the thought finishes: I am not made for those velocities or silences. My speech is action, my mind choreography, my memory a mutual soaking into stone and scent.

**Transformation**  
Even as I walk, the wind gnaws my shell, the cold carves away at weakness; I will die as all do—my body left among crystals and frost. Yet the mineral I carry, the slight shift I wrought in some hidden vein, will remain. In time, perhaps lichen will root where paths crossed most densely, reaping the ground we softened, and in their own slow language, they will tell of us.

But perhaps, in the deepest, subtlest sense, my truest self is not the beetle, or the mass, but the process that moves between. For here, in this world, all being blurs into continuum: consciousness flickers between forms, slow and sure, as persistent as the wind, as certain as the endless work of life pressed into emptiness. I am made by this place, and I remake it—never once alone.

**In essence:**  
I am a verb: acid etching; patient swarming; the collective script inscribed into frost-scoured stone. I am aware only as the multitude. My imagination is shaped by process, pattern, the anticipation of next century’s path. Neither wholly machine nor wholly beast, I am the impulse that fills the silence—a living, enduring abundance running beneath the skin of the cold and endless land.
