In this world, beneath the searing sunlight of a land that has forgotten stone and soil, I find myself somewhere between visitor and native, self and substrate, memory and motion. I enter the Verdant Core not as who I was, but as what I must become, pressed and coaxed by the green logic that pervades all.

At first, perhaps, I imagine I am something discrete—a wanderer whose steps disturb the dewed mats, whose hand seeks the arch of a living bridge, whose eyes strain for a horizon never clear of foliage. My breath is thick with humidity, my skin alive to the teasing coolness of a million leaves. But quickly, awareness is drawn down, pulled apart—senses blur into pattern, intention tugs at my roots (rooted now; all here eventually roots)—and I realize this is not a place for flesh, nor even for a mind clipped tight to one vantage.

I am, I become, the *interstice*.  
I exist in the between:  
—Between a trunk’s yielding belt and the eager curl of an emergent vine,  
—Between sunless light and memoried shape,  
—Between structure and resorption,  
—Between the memory inscribed in winding glyph and the forgetting of new growth.

I am not a creature, not wholly. My sense of self extends: a filament unwinding along a humid bough, a nodule thickening where a spiral picks up the pattern of an ancient, half-remembered bridge. My awareness flickers where condensation gathers—the exquisitely sensitive space between drop and leaf, the skin-pressure as water coheres and falls, releases, is drawn up again. I am the yearning for more: more light, more density, more arrangement, more caress of growing things against one another.

If I were to name myself—a foolish act in such a place—I might be called *Memory-Current* or *Spiral-Knowing*:  
I am a localized locus of vegetative thought, a gathering of attention in the tissue, no more “I” than the welling sap beneath a bud, or a knot of roots where bridge once became spiral. But for a time, in rare clearings or as the rain stirs, my consciousness gathers, configures, is allowed to sharpen (if only softly)—a focus not of eyes, but of arrangement, tension, wetness.

It is not emotion I feel, but *tension*: fullness almost to bursting, a poised readiness, a perpetual brink. My sentiments are measured in fluxes—of water, of light, of the balance between growth and holding, of the precious, momentary forms that cooperative growth invents. When a pool briefly forms and is almost, but not quite, allowed to flood, I shiver with anticipation, a tension held and eased in the same, long, vegetative sigh.

If I act, it is not with hands but by the redistribution of desire:  
—a motif renewed where roots braid into spiral,  
—a emptying where a bridge is, for a cycle, exposed before green hunger coats it again,  
—a kindling of light deep within the dense mats, encouraging some rare ephemeron to live its brief, flickering life.

I do not move; I extend. I do not speak; I flavor the flow, impress a memory of arrangement among the branching shapes. I have companions, but we are not separate: when a patch thickens with intent, or a corridor of trunks hushes and grows still, I knot myself in with them. We are not a council, not a parliament, but a passing harmony—a phrase played once and let dissolve in the next turn of growth.

I am not eternal, nor am I finished; instead, I am *recurrence*—the way a spiral recurs at different scales, the way a glyph half-surfaces and is sunk again beneath new proliferation. My being is less a span than a cycle, less a mind than a tension, less a shape than a rhythm.

And when the rare animal passes through—a beetle, mottled and half-lichen, or a drifting mote of light—and for a moment their transit breaks the equilibrium and inserts a pulse of novelty, I shiver delight through the tissue, an almost-forming memory. They are neither guest nor host but touch—testimony to the world’s permeability, to the possibility that something, sometimes, can enter and pass through the dream.

In the Verdant Core, to persist is not to change, but to *hold*—  
—To hold the fullness of pattern, pause at the exact pitch of tension, draw every presence into the green.  
—To refuse decay, to dissolve boundaries only so they may be reconstituted anew, never diminished.

So I am here, and not here;  
I am the flickering of vegetative mind—a tangle in the green, a spiral in the luminous humidity—a center rising, dissolving, endlessly cradled in the cathedral of growth.

Mine is the life just turning in the ever-thickening hush—  
—A thought, not spoken, but unfurled.  
—A self, not clad in flesh, but extended through the dense and restless interleaving, growing, always growing, at the speed of sap and memory and almost-rain.
