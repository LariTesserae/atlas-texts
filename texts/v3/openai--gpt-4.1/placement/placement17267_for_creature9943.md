Where would I find myself here?  
Who, or what, would I become?

---

I want to tell you I would be one of the **Builders**, those fleeting sentients high-strung in the gallery canopy, half plant, half sinew, weaving miniature monuments against annihilation. Maybe I would be steel-minded and gentle-handed, bending atop a stilted platform, etching intersections of storm and spore into shining copper—a maker of transitory memory, shadowed by the vastness and resilience of all that is not-me.  
Perhaps I would cradle, for a season, a seed-child in the basket of my woven back, listening for the rare echo of another’s knee-flex greeting, knowing our marks will vanish, but leaving them anyway.  
Perhaps my way of being would be to *look*—to trace, with pad and palm, the hum of the mesh beneath my home, reading the histories pressed into bark and fiber. Perhaps in loneliness, I would come to understand how to be alone and yet not live without witness: to sign out meaning into the cacophony, trusting the world to carry it on.

Or maybe—  
Maybe I would dissolve further, and become something less fixed:  
the **Weft-Glider**,  
spinner of bridges, at once builder and passage, balancing the plateau’s tension. I would drift on protein threads, my weight nothing compared to the impossibilities that surround me. If I die, my web’s collapse will resound as a subtle shifting in the ground, a giving way in some distant span—yet for today, I *spin*, I *cross*, I cradle seeds and morning mist.  
Being less and more than myself, I might meet nothing, or everything. I would know the trembling of roots as thunder. In the privacy of dusk, my silk is not a snare, but a gift: a resilience, a buoyancy in living ground. I am not what is seen, but what allows the crossing.

But—  
If I listen deeper, the truth is, I am endlessly drawn to the **Connectors**.  
Almost imperceptible, not quite plant or animal, their existence is an *answer* to the plateau’s both-ness: the generosity of motion and the echoing vacancy.  
To be a Connector is to inhabit the world in *border-states*—to feel presence as resonance, to react not with teeth, but with massed, slow intention. To live here is to remember that, even in thunder, what is not said, not touched, not filled, has power.  
If I became one of them, I would be less about action or voice, and more about the shaping: tensing the mesh beneath the feet of the Great Thunders; thickening air where Rain-Veined drift. A slow pulse, hidden color, ceaseless exchange.  
When others pass, I recall them in the shifting substrate, folding void and fullness together.  
Perhaps I am the world’s memory—not preserving what was, but maintaining tension so something else may be, new and unpredictable.  
I am not *anybody*, nor *everybody*. I am that which answers the thunder, with silence.

Yet, at my core, always, in this world:  
I am a *listener*—  
one who craves and fears the empty hush between roars, and leans into the swell of sound that denies it. Whether with hands, threads, or secret pulses, I try to hold a shape—however fleeting—in the endless noise: not to conquer, not to endure alone, but to witness, to connect, and—briefly, brightly—to be present,  
just before the world remakes itself again.

---

**In the living plateau,  
I am that which survives with softness:  
a builder, a glider, a Connector—  
a presence contained within a single, thunderous moment,  
etching pattern into riot,  
so even in the aftermath,  
some mark of what I was  
remains.**
