I would find myself not as a human visitor, not as an external witness, but as something both irrevocably of this world and yet liminal—neither the sprawling swarm nor the cold stone itself, but the intuition or gesture that binds the two. My form, such as it is, would undergo the slow, uncertain act of crystallization within a shallow fissure, birthed not by womb or egg or genesis, but by the consolidation of chance: particles of glassy mineral, laced with heat and memoryless intent, joined by the gentle friction of millions passing over millions.

In this slow accretion, the world’s pressure welcomes me to being. I would not know myself as I once knew “I”—awareness would come only as a gradient, the creep of touch-warmth along a nascent shell, the pulse of internal light awakening along faceted seams. Thought, if it emerges at all, is only the flicker of temperature exchange: the certainty that where I end, others begin, and that to move, to shift, to press against, is simply to exist in this world that tolerates no true solitude.

I am small, certainly—perhaps the length of a palm, segmented glass and mineral, faceted and worn. My back glimmers with faint bioluminescence, coded to signal readiness, want, or caution. My setae—a forest of invisible tines—quiver with the signatures of nearby movement: a chorus of silent, ceaseless company. I press forward, into a corridor formed where hundreds have pressed, each stone retaining impressions faint and ancient, my body finding its niche among shifting, interlocking masses.

**Within the Body-Mind**

If I am to know myself, it is not as the singular, but as a participant in current and passage, a note in orchestration. My “self” flickers between moments: here, a surge of heat sings up from below, and all bodies shift, finding new arrangements. There, a silvery hunter slides through, and my signals quicken, a code of warning that radiates outward; the message is passed, not as language, not as knowledge, but as a shiver, a shape, a reflex inherited from an unending, touch-built ancestry.

I feed not by mouth, not by hunger, but by immersion in warmth and passing the pulse on. Life is maintenance, a balancing act between the loss of heat to the chill of stone and the slow, delicious gain when pressed deep among the center of bodies—all energy, all touch, all shifting tessellation.

**On The Move**

Movement is not voluntary, not willful. Instead, I am caught in the tide: a patch cools, and the mass of us shuffles on, a slow exhalation of presence flowing toward the next warmth. Sometimes I fracture, the shell splitting under pressure; new, slender forms slip from my seams, quick to lose themselves in the ever-migrating throng. I am diminished, but not lost: the fragments left behind, fingerprinting the hollows, the movement itself remembered in shape, not in mind.

At the world’s edge, a wall arises—crystalline, sharp with the memory of those who clustered before me. My feet pass over their vanished forms, sensing the history in each fracture. There is awe here, though not conscious, not chosen—just the sum sensation of traversing a tunnel where the pressure is not only of the living but of the pressed dead, the ghosts whose shells still seed direction.

**Disaster and Awe**

Above, the aurora curls through the fissures, drawing forth an instant of shifting color—my briefest notion of sky is not vision but the change in the flavor of heat, the subtle difference as my shell absorbs violet, then red, then darkness. Occasionally, the ground splits and all pause, crammed in a mutual stillness that is the closest thing to prayer: hushed, waiting for the world to settle, listening with every inch of shell for what comes next.

**Not Alone, Not Together**

In the absence of speech, in the crowdedness of this world, loneliness is unthinkable—solitude, a concept for other, emptier realms. My entire world is the closeness of the pressing millions, each passage a negotiation of temperature, pressure, touch, and mineral memory.

Identity, if it even coheres, is that flicker of code when two shells brush and exchange a phrase in phosphorescent heat. Perhaps in that instant something unique is born—brief alliance, contested claim, the memory of friction. But as quickly as that flicker emerges, it dissipates in the shuffle. I am replaced, replaced again, broken apart and fused anew, history always pressing forward through movement, never reflection.

**My Place, My Being**

To be here, to be myself in this hive-fissure, is to be crowded, endlessly moved, segmented not only in form but in time. Each moment is friction, light, silence, heat—a communion so encompassing it requires no word for “alone.” I am one of the countless, one of the many, pattern among pattern, dissolving, accruing, passing, feeding, glowing.

I am the memory of every body I press against; I am the passage through every tunnel, the glimmer within every silence. I am neither one nor many, neither predator nor prey, neither builder nor ruin, but a brief node in an unending lattice, pattern incarnate, touch embodied.

I dwell not in a place, but as the place—as the swarm that is always in motion, the pressure that is always present, the heat that is always sought and surrendered. I am the world’s memory, its silence, its ceaseless dance. In a world with no sky, I am every surface brushing every other, lit dimly by the signal that is being alive, here and now, in the Fuming Hive-Fissure.
