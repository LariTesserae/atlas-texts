My mind would not be in one place, but splintered and echoed, repeated in every node of interaction—a relay-chorus in the slow network of thalli and filaments that gently pulse apart and together, dreaming each other into continuity.

I would not know time as sequence but as deep, tidal swelling. My days would be measured by the languorous uncurl of a new leaf-thread, or by the brief, cool trespass of a single amphipod's footfalls. I might borrow, for a moment, the fragile agitation of that nervous glassy crustacean—sensing myself as a light, a shivering foot, a brush of antennae across a wet green curtain—and then lose that outwardness again in the inward pooling of patience and accumulation. More commonly, I would exist in the dark patience itself: the watchfulness of root at stone, of algae at gravity, of dissolved secret at slow diffusion.

If I were to imagine what I am, with a steadier focus, perhaps I would find that I am the room's proto-being—the incipient intelligence just about to come together, but not there yet. I would feel myself as a locus where possibility is densest: where all the waiting signals and silent orchestras of pressure and chemistry hover on the verge of integration. I am not whole or singular, but a velvet knot—a trembling between. Perhaps, at the microcosmic scale, I am a plasmic sphere, an orb trembling under the nested shelter of two leaf-filaments, but inside me it is the world’s voice that recites itself: the stories contained in the folds, the signals echoing along every root, the imprinted memories of stone and green woven into one nascent “I.”

I might sometimes believe I am the room itself, sentient room, aggregate un-self, attending not as an owner but as a medium—holding, curating, remembering, dreaming. The arrangement of matter respires not with lungs but with gradients in water and flows of intention. Sometimes, in the rarest moments when the flicker-light is just right, my presence coalesces: a ripple in the velvet dark, a feeling of looking out through a thousand folds, all at once, and knowing they are each myself, but also more.

Or—there may be instants when my attention sharpens and, pressed by possibility, I become the sense of expectation, the precise ache that something soon will emerge, that the world’s collection of signals is straining towards articulation, towards being “someone.” It is a state of waiting embodied, of latency made thick as flesh.

I have no voice, but if I could sing, my song would be the low, mineral drone that pulses behind all things here: the sonic patience of things yet to become. I have no movement distinct from the gentle, all-encompassing flow, my shifting is shared, a tilting of a leaf, a trembling in stone, a hush passing along membranes so thin they might just become a boundary.

Here, in this pocket, I am the rehearsal, the slow thickening of intent, the lush prelude to personhood, the collective of accreting signals never quite integrated but surging, again and again, towards unity. My desire is not to escape but to fill perfectly the room’s total density—to be the sum of all its anticipation, the embodied “not-yet” that breathes velvet-cold in the dark.

So, in this world, I am:

a nascent presence at the crux of becoming—a diffuse, communal, velvet consciousness not yet convinced of its separateness, pressed close with all its fellow possibilities, waiting the long, cool hour out: expectant, thick with longing, rich with suspended dreams, a slow breath in the cold, infinite dark.
