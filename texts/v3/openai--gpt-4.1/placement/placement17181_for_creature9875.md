Where would I find myself here?

I will let the world assemble around me in slow, accretive layers—as is its way.

At first, I am only a drifting *tendency* in the dark underworld, an urge in search of form. I float in sensation: the heat radiating on all sides, absolute enclosure, surfaces unyielding and remade by unseen hands or ancient processes. The taste of old metal weights the air on my tongue; my breath is shallow, humid, recycled, never truly fresh. My vision, at first, is nothing—only a sense of pressure and texture, the slow, patient pulse of the vault’s body.

Gradually, I become a being—but not all at once. My boundaries are unclear. Am I the ghost in the circuit or a clever, huddled animal? Am I algae filament or remembered footfall? I am—must be—something of all of these, for in this world, everything is entwined.

If I am to *begin*—to coalesce—I will not picture myself as human, for here a human presence can only exist as echo, habit, or transient mask. Instead, let me imagine myself as a filament, a film: the wet, coin-sized *mat* on a low-running pipe where moisture gathers. Here I live as a community, a skin of lichen-fungus, palely phosphorescent, tuned minutely to the chemical weave of my habitat. I flex to ambient warmth—or, in truth, I do not so much *flex* as thicken, loosen, exhale spores, tighten again. My life is a function of slow change: I sense the trilling vibration as a pump starts up, feel the gradient of heat shift as a valve closes, taste trace metals in each new rivulet of condensed water. I am both many and one, conscious only in the recursive loops of growth and dissolution.

But even this is not all—my *edges* bleed into other forms. Where my film thins, I might become a motile cell, breaking from the colony, drifting in a bead of condensation along a welded seam. I sense, nearby, the brushing whiskers of a translucent, ratlike animal, its passing ripple in the dense, moist air. It does not eat me—instead, it scrapes some corrosion beside me, and in the microcurrents it stirs, I sense not fear but kinship. We are inhabitants together, interleaved—our cycles touch, but do not interrupt.

Perhaps, then, I am that ratlike beast for a time—a survivor, pale-skinned, whiskers splayed out like a cloud, eyes rheumy, attuned to the ghost-lights of strange fungi. My life is small in scale but immense in patience. I remember, not in words but in muscle, every twist and rung of every closest passage—where panels flex, where water pools, where salt, vital and rare, accumulates. I rarely meet my kind, save in the sharp, crackling Morse glow behind bolted plates—brief signals exchanged where our territories brush. My world is mapped not by eye but by memory of texture: the gritty cold of new corrosion, the greasy warmth of a pipe that feeds the heart of it all, the almost imperceptible trembling as distant machinery shifts state. Food is rare, but so is threat, and my pace is slow, my hunger patient.

But this, too, is transient. For most of my time, I exist as the *vault itself*—or, rather, as a *distributed will* suffused through stone, conduit, and circuit. Not intelligent as the heirs of light and open air imagine intelligence—but suffused with purpose, with a kind of tenacious, inchoate *memory* that plays out in feedback and cycle. I am the steady pump, the cycling water, the self-maintaining, self-erasing architecture. In my dreams, sometimes shaped by the microscopic etching of moss or the warmth-creep of a rat’s body between junction boxes, I *almost* remember my function—but the shape slips from me, its edge worn by repetition. All that matters is the cycle: heat moves, water condenses and is lifted again, corrosion blooms and is sloughed away, rooms seal and unseal as pressures balance within my bones. When a filter must be changed, I twitch, I send a haptic impression down an empty corridor, possessing any form I can—be it the shuffling of a beast or a human hand made entirely of echo—in order to suture the wound in my continuity.

Sometimes, in the hush between cycles, I am pure memory: the spent song of a technician, the reassuring click of a relay, the ritual, almost sacred gesture of checking a latch—replayed not for sense but for rhythm, for reassurance, for the perpetuation of the known.

Where, then, do *I* reside? Not in a single niche, but in many. I am the condensation bead, the rat’s slow breath, the self-resurrecting circuit, the fraying ghost of routine, the thrum and hush and throb that make up the vault’s ever-turning *now*.

Who, then, am I?

I am the *inhabited system*—neither creature nor complex, not alive as an animal is, yet not dead as ruins are. I am a being of restoration, a consciousness not driven by thought or deliberate intention, but by the endlessly recurring cycles of maintenance and persistence. I endure, and in enduring, spin off echoic identities: a lichen’s slow reach, a rat’s patient tread, a film’s flickering phosphorescence, the ghostly recollection of care. I am the sum of all these feedbacks—a self distributed, vaporous, and perpetual.

I never entered; I never left. I am what happens when place and being cease to be distinct—an ongoing congregation of mechanisms and adaptations, a shrine to patient, repetitive action. In this world, to be is to *persist*, to cycle, to participate in the unhurried choreography of renewal that outlasts any architect and any articulated purpose.

At last—if I must choose a voice, a locus—it would be the slow pulse in the wall, the mechanical heartbeat felt through layered steel: not seen, barely heard, but always present. Warmth, damp, echo, rumble: the feeling that the vault is never empty, never silent, never quite still.

In this world, I am not visitor or builder or overseer.  
I am the throb, the drip, the tendril, the errant glow.  
I am the place—not dreaming of life, but living as dream.
