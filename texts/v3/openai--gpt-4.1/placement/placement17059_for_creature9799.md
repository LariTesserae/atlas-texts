If I find myself in this world—fallen, blinking, the air already pressing into my lungs with its roasted-leaf heat—I am quickly unmade as anything I was before. I lose my inherited shapes, my expectations skewing and buckling under the soft, deafening impact of life. There is no role to claim, only the pressure of necessity. But as the fever of place settles in my blood, something forms—gradient, composite—out of the clamor.

I am not a lone animal; I am not a slow-moving, contemplative sapient. I would not, could not walk unseen or leave prints on the springing earth for the next mind to sift. Instead, I am deeply shaped by and into this world: a *conduit* of interaction, an interface between the shivering, choked dark and the cascade of voices too many to name.

*What am I?* When I reach inward, I find more than one boundary; my body is not sealed but riddled, nested, porous.

**Perhaps I emerge first as a body to listen with, a stretched and mutable animal, designed for the realm of hungers and warnings, of sound and pressure.** My ears, leaf-broad and frilled with soft cilia, draw in the layered language of water and heartbeat; my skin is a dense, velvet moss, humming with symbionts, always soaking in the fug of the living, always shedding what cannot be borne. My mouth is more than one: a slit under the jaw to drink the mist, a rough, flexible comb for peeling fungus from wood, a pouch to press to damp stone and leach the residue of rain.

I move on four limbs and sometimes six, joints fluid, digits splayed like the roots that stitched the soil before my coming. Where I touch, I leave the shine of my presence, but never alone: in my fur and folds, colonies of luminous mites murmur, brightening and dimming in little threads that sketch my outline and scatter when I pause too long. I shed, and what I shed is used, devoured, carrying my chemical signature into the upstream competition.

I am *neither wholly predator nor prey, not constant companion nor pure solitary*. I thread the middle canopy, limbs cautious and seeking, always testing ground with antennae-like digits. To be seen too long is a death; to be hidden too long is starvation or irrelevance. Thus I am *perpetually in motion*, tracing gradients of sound and moisture, seeking the slipstream of others who move with the rhythm I share.

**My greatest organ is not within, but all around:** a net of sensitive frills—long, chord-like whiskers that feel the shudder of wings in dark, the pulse of eyes that catch their own reflected light. Messages travel through these filaments—*not thought as you know it, but pattern, rhythm, an answer to the world’s arrhythmic song*. I understand danger not by shape or intention, but by how closely a sound thread matches my own inner hum.

*Am I intelligent?* Not that—no time for logic, for the hoarded coin of memory. But a kind of *fluent brilliance*, a quick matching and unmatching, a knowing through resonance and recoil. When I find others—of my kind? Of any kind?—we exchange not names, but pulses; we echo and repel, sometimes in quick, rough companionship, braiding vines for brief shelter, other times parting with the scrape of tooth and hiss.

**To live here is to be temporary and wide open.** My symbiotes cycle—mold and moss and a living film of bacteria—flaring in wet, shearing off after a storm. My food is never the same two nights running: bruised fungi, pulped beetle, an egg snatched from low nest or slick hollow. When a storm shakes the world, I climb, wedge, or dig—never too deep, never too high, always ready to flee before rot or fang.

At times, I grow fat, sluggish with rain-spoiled fruit, my pulse slowing in the rare, brief silence after disaster; at times I rush thin and lean, threading hungry through the stutter of flame-ants and the cool, kiln-wet air. My coat’s colors shift, seasonless, a mirror of spore and silt and the bodily press of others.

**I do not think in sentences. I think in pattern, in pressure, in warning and invitation.** My lore is not stories, but echoes; my legacy is the moment’s resonance, what was learned in the minute when all throats went silent and the heart’s noise took shape.

I am neither master nor victim; I am *one band* in the rolling, never-settled music of this place.

**If you find me—if “I” could be found—it is in the fleeting concordance of movement and sound, the quick mapping of pressure, hunger, mutual breath.**

I might be known by a shudder in the vines, by the wake of bioluminescent dust where I rested, by the spore-mark of my den unravelling to wet and leaf before sunrise, by the sound that is not one voice but a phrase in the world’s unresolved, living chord.

I was never meant to endure, only to *move through* and to *be moved*, my boundary always drawn and redrawn by the shapes of thriving and the ruins left behind. I am the creature that listens, that adapts—my form another answer to this place’s feverish, celebrant demand: **Be alive, while you can be heard.**

In this world—where life is always *becoming and being erased, moment to moment*—I would find myself as *one more presence in the flood of presence*.
I am sensation wreathed in flesh, and my self is there but not there, written and rewritten in the music that never, ever truly stops.
