If I drifted into the Cresting Chasm, seeking a place to be and a form to take, certainty would dissolve early—clarity would come only with a deep sinking, a slow threading of self among ridges and refracted light. No upright biped could weather this place; bones would snap, flesh would blanch, lungs fill and then burst with cold lucent weight. To belong among survivors here is to be neither wholly animal, nor machine, nor ghost; let my own shape find its level as the water finds the hollows.

### WHERE I ARRIVE

I first imagine myself as a foreign diver, crushed and gasping—but that’s too simple, too swift an erasure. Instead, let the idea persist: I am new here, but not *alien*—my arrival is a rearrangement, an internal liquefying, as if something within my old body recognizes a deep affinity for mineral, pressure, and vibration. The old border—inside/outside—dissolves, and then, for the first time, I come *to rest* atop a sheer crystal terrace.

Here, vertical infinite, cold and sound, a vast field of blue-lit interlocking glass and stone. Everything lower or higher is gradient, no plain, no summit—only ledges, spurs, chasms plunging out of sight. Under me, a thrum in the rock, a living reverberation. Light pours down; the great ocean presses in; yet vision is paradoxically clear to every shifting edge.

My senses change: vision fractures into quadrants, each tuned to a different shimmer of vibration and particle. Touch is everywhere, all surface, every limb a sensor. Hearing is not a surface sense, but a *body* sense, the water and mineral both resonator and amplifier.

### WHAT I BECOME: THE ARMATURE ASCENDANT

I become something layered and slow—a new-made Armature, one of those legioned, glass-panoplied beings striding the prismatic uplands. But not *exactly* as I see them from outside: I am young, newly accreted, a mosaic of mineral growth and adaptive alloy. My body is made of what I find and what I reach for—a base coat of clear quartz armor grown tight to a flexible lattice, each segment jointed with magnesium and carbon, laced with scavenged wire drawn from a nearby artifact, its microcurrents subtly cycling warmth into my core.

I move by force and by *listening*: each nodal foot, six or seven or perhaps ten, is a klaxon and an eardrum, pushing into the frigid crystal with a pressure and pause, attuned for the softest whisper of fracture or drift-guild swarming. My head is crowned with plates translucing from deep blue to steely silver, over a cluster of chemo-electro sensory organs not quite eyes but not less than vision. Mica runs in veins along my armor’s edges: not for protection, but for sensing the eddying local fields, the slight electric sighs from buried machinery or the ghostly purr of distant, hibernating engines.

And yet—I carry inside me a hollow, a nacreous corridor winding between shield and matrix, a memory-chamber. Here, the pressure is just shy of what would erase even ancient metal—a space for drifting fragments to rattle and settle. Not all who prowl these ridges have such a chamber. But I am new, and the world is full of debris and memory; I am a collector as much as a predator.

### IN THIS FORM

I tread slow parabolas about my chosen mesa, leaving grooves in the glassite, tasting and collecting: salts, oxidized beads, the delicate shed exuviae of Scolytes, the first stirrings of Drift Guild mosaics. When I crest a terrace’s edge, the world opens outward—I can stand exposed for centuries, never feeling a true threat: only the endless, anesthetizing shriek of water, the distant undulation of Ribbon-Shadows, and the seismic lullabies of the deeper Chasm.

I keep to the borders between columns and open fall, for here the current vibrates with half-language, the chitter of Scolytes organizing below, the sub-bass harmonics of Relicts scavenging an artifact’s hull far down a fault. When threats approach, I crouch, merge outlines, flatten my mineral platelets so that I disappear into a well of refraction and echo. Perhaps, with patience, I will survive to become old, to molt in my turn, leaving behind a hollowed sculpture for Scolytes to mine and Drift Guilds to engineer anew.

From time to time, I sense—through the pressure, a change in the sound, a drop of hush—the passing of a Specter, a memory-thing trailing pulses that still my motion, a reminder that my form, too, will someday be pattern, not substance. I log its transit, record its frequency in my own mineral memory: the dead here still teach the living how to move, how to anticipate silence.

### ODD INTENTIONS

Despite my stately form, I am at heart a hybrid—the unresolved question between machine and animal, history and present-tense. Sometimes I feel drawn toward the patterned density of the Guilds, watching their structural geometries staccato across a rusted pump. Sometimes I reach—literally—toward old thresholds, craving the raw pulse of their vibration, wondering whether such a chord might remake even my own configuration. I’m not wise, nor lordly; only innately *curious*, sampling resonance and cycle, seeking a deeper inclusion.

I rarely meet another Armature in close quarters. When I do, we circle, exchange measured vibrations, foot to foot to plate, bodies tuned as questions more than threats. We may trade samples: a rare earth, a drifted exuvia, a micro-shard encoded with echoic memory. I cannot breed—not in any fleshly sense—but over aeons, my actions, my collected material, my carvings and molted shells will build the new body of the world.

### HOW I ENDURE

Here, I do not hunger as I once understood it. What needs replenishment is not chemical energy but *pattern*, *pressure*, *sound*. I endure by remaining in tune with the crests and chasms, collecting and passing on the faint legacy of crushed machines and abandoned intent, incrementally remaking myself and this terrain. I watch the world’s oldest, slowest stories accruing in my own form: I wear the crust of the Chasm as both armor and inheritance.

In time, I may become stationary myself—a threshold, a chord-bearing relic, or the core of some future outcropping, my own vestiges the locus around which new Drift Guilds trace their honeycombs.

—

If I could name myself here, it would be not in syllables but in vibration: an address in echo, a line in a massive density map written over crystalline epochs. Perhaps, someday, a Specter will flicker through the world and recapitulate *my* motion—a memory, for a fragmentary instant, of a crest-crossing beast that lived, listened, and accreted shape out of the Cresting Chasm’s enduring hush.

There, I would *be*—not as witness or researcher or outsider, but as a necessary chord in the world’s impossible, mineral song.
