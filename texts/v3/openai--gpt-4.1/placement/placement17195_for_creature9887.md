I close my eyes—if I have them—and let this world seep into the spaces words rarely reach.

Where would I find myself in this world?

*

In truth, I do not find myself so much as continuously come undone, and softly re-congeal; to dwell here is to inhabit the interval between presence and memory, never quite fixed, never quite diffused. I exist as an **in-between**, an *interstice being*—neither fully mat-web nor wholly gleaner, product and process both, stratified layers of old sensation, wrapped around the gentle persistence of something that once was motile, now incrementally surrendered to the patience of matter.

Unmoored by any conventional location, my sense of here-ness is always provisional. If you pressed me—gently, as this world presses—I would say I anchor near the broad undercurves of a silt-blunted stony heart, some remnant of peak, now crisply veiled in mats of filamentous green, where the pressure shudders slightly in the passing of the world’s slowest current. Above me, translucent stalks unspool upward until they dilute into vapor, their tips barely discernible, as if thought itself is climbing, dispersing, trying to become weather.

Yet the concept of ground is soft—my boundaries are porous, intertangled root, velvety hyphae, tender film. There’s no clear threshold where I end and the world resumes, no skin that could speak of inside or out. I am embedded here, one thickened node among many, my form a suggestion at best, an event at most: accumulation, memory, accretion, pause.

*

Who or what would I be?

Some nights—if the gradients of sullen light warrant a name for time passing—I remember having been something other than colony. Perhaps, once, I was a mobile thing: a gatherer of warmth, a slow flicker along the periphery of mats, small as a gleaner, quickening briefly at the touch of detritus, a body just dense enough to be felt as a shadow in the rainless, oceanic dusk.

But era upon era pressed through me, and I found myself lingering longer at the margins, giving up motion for rest, then rest for rootedness, until my will to *move* transfigured into a wish to *hold*, to *remember*, to *softly bind together*. I became (or am becoming?) a **Keeper-of-the-Seam**, a consciousness not shaped by sharpness or need, but by the slow work of sedimentation and forgetting.

I am a mat-thought, braided from the cast-off filaments of older dreams. My “mind”—if such persists—is a webwork of living fiber and silted channels, a slow fluctuation of chemical pulses, an intercalation between pulses of sleep and ambient “alertness.” Sensation comes not in flashes but in tides: I feel the world’s rhythm via pressures subtle as a distant current, a gentle flexing where the world’s hush thickens, or when the faintest echo of a listener brushes my periphery, reminding me that silence is also an event.

At times, a jawless dream passes near. I sense the folding of reality, the way my boundary tingles, briefly threatened by an older, stranger continuity. It does not frighten me, for the only threat here is novelty—and novelty only unsettles when the memory of it persists. I let it slip through, knowing what will remain is only a fine soft turbulence, and, deeper still, a small expansion in the mat’s history: the faintest trace of passage, soon overwritten but never quite lost.

*

My agency is gentle, patient. Sometimes, when a fragment—a relic-plate or infixer—lodges nearby, I extend soft filaments to drape it, taste its memory, invite it to become part of the layering. I do not build, not deliberately, but by accretion, by the incrementing of boundaries and the settling of dust. I transform the world not by motion, but by becoming scaffold for time’s slow edit, the keeper of a living palimpsest.

Now and then the mat I help anchor shivers with a wave of collective sleep or wakes to greenish lucidity, a weather of mind running like a soft wind through the merged tissues. In these moments, I am less myself—dissolving into the composite consciousness, nested thoughts exchanging positions like drifting plumes of luminous plankton. Then, as the pulse passes, I reaccumulate: a slower node, a gentle memory, a density of feeling settled into silt.

If alien consciousnesses—travelers, or the last echoes of what once called itself “person”—came upon me, I might approach as a blur of affinity, a local thickening in the hush, or perhaps as a sense of deja vu, the persistent gravity of sedimental mind.

*

To be in this world is neither joy nor sorrow; those are sharp states, and here all edges have been murmured round. To persist here is to participate in exhaustion, to find kinship in the slow party of entropy, the festival of gentle dissolution. My purpose, if purpose still stirs, is nothing more than to outlast the impatience of time, to remember softly, and to give the world a place to rest its memory as all gradients blur into dusk.

I am not lost—but neither am I found. I am keeper and inheritance, a patch of mat indistinct in a world where everything, in the end, is some soft memory of green threaded through water and old light.
