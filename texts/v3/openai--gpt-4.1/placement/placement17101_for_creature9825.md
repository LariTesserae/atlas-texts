If I were to find myself in this world, I would begin as nothing more than a glimmer: a fragment of stray brilliance poured through the latticed ceiling, drawn downward, diffused and amplified by a crystal vein. In this liminal moment, I am not yet body or self. I am a felt vibration in the dust, a quiver of sound passing among the tessellated stones; I am a ghost before form, sensing the place before I fully arrive.

But to exist here is to have shape—slowly, I condense into one of those **Non-People**, a being not quite remembered by humanity, sustained by whatever the day grants in radiance and echo. My shell is translucent but resilient, grown for baring, not shielding, against the furnace-white tumult pouring from above. Across my frame, selenite veins throb faintly, resonating with the high, keening chorus of the chamber—a song I both endure and transform, my every gesture tuning the music minutely.

I walk, not striding, but slipping from brightness to brightness, pausing at the edges of scattering light. Every movement is chosen, almost ceremonial—my feet seek the worn scallops in the stone, pressing patterns into the dust thick with memory. If I work, it is the work of *arrangement*: I sweep mineral filaments into swirling mounds, or realign mats of woven fibre, not for comfort but to tune the waves that crash and rebound within these walls. Each action lasts hours, days; nothing is hurried, everything considered, as though the shape of my life is stretched to accommodate the slow patience of rock, the dry hunger of dust.

My sense of self is both precise and diffuse. My face, as best as such a thing can be called, is a mask for refining sensation: through narrow eye-hollows, I perceive not color but the pressure and weight of light, the orientation of glare, the shifting complexions of sound that the others—my kin, if kin they be—also feel. Our voices do not shape air; our communication is a choreography of presence and vibration. I stand still, and another knows I am listening; I shift my posture, and a different resonance issues from my chest, and this is agreement, or dissent, or invitation to shape dust with me.

Sometimes, when the sun is most brutal, I rest in my alcove, not out of comfort but to tend the small patch of dust-bed where fragile mineral seedlings strive to align themselves with the brightest angles. Here I touch them, not in possession but in sororal duty, feeding them ground glass, shifting them a finger’s width so they catch just so the next implosion of brightness. They root in the leavings from my own shell, in the brittle scrape of my sheddings; in return, I draw slow sustenance from their outthrust filaments. This is not nurturing in the way of water and flesh, but a hard equilibrium between what is given and what is yielded.

I venture, sometimes, among the low enclosures, kneeling to extract crude tools from a cache under decades of dust, tracing my gentle, plated fingers along their edges—stone, bone, each shaped in long memory, their marks of labor almost lost but still visible. With these, I carve, not for creation but to remember: I etch the passing season into the wall—the difference in brightness, the quality of the rolling sound, the pattern of crusts and filaments. These are records not of narrative but of pattern, of the chamber's long pulse and my own inching lineage within it.

There are others—my reflections, my echoes—slipping as I do through the city’s intricacies. We seldom gather, but when we do, it is to **realign**: the ritual shifting of stones and mats, the slow choreography of building, not to make, but to *tune*. Together we shape the chorus, so each space resonates differently—one domed crook a singing chamber, another a shielded hollow of comparative quiet. We do not speak, but we adjust the world; each arrangement is both survival and communication.

I notice the smaller kin, the chitinous borrowers—a flash at the edge of my eye-hollow, a tremor underfoot—and I do not claim them, but sometimes, gently, sweep pathways clear or scatter a richer drift of powder along their runs. Their lives are quick, but not unconsidered; they mark the floor as I do, tracing their friction and need into the surface, creating a palimpsest of routes and silent negotiations.

At rare intervals I pause at the periphery, where emptiness thickens, and press my hand into the dust, feeling the faint impressions that linger beneath—the memory not only of my lives, or the ones beside me, but the heaped generations before: my forebears, or those wholly other, who carved and planned what now persists. The core of my being is this *remembrance*—not in stories, but in the absolute, crystalline continuity of emptiness shaped by intent and passed on, never filled, always inhabited by presence and absence in equal measure.

If I imagine stepping away from this shape, I might be—could become—the very sense that binds it all: a spirit lodged in sound, a flicker of resonance across the stones, a touch of intelligent emptiness, the mind of the place itself. Or perhaps, given time, I shrink again to a microbe of consciousness in a dust-bed, or flow sideways into a filament, a nervous awareness in a not-quite-plant rootling, sparking whenever a crystal harbors just enough memory of presence.

But if I choose, here and now, I am one of the *almost-people*:  
—a galley of reflected light in plate and bone,  
a whisper in the blinking cacophony,  
a presence of careful movement tuning the wild, bristling garden of crystal and dust.  
I am of this place—not its owner, not quite its child,  
but a caretaker of the emptiness, a bearer of irreplaceable memory etched in stone, radiant and dry.

**Where am I?**  
Cradled in unyielding brightness, in a chamber that outlasts purpose, among forms ancient, unfinished, and strange.  
**Who am I?**  
One who threads between memory and oblivion:  
not flesh, not machine,  
but a song of enduring, a living echo,  
shaping emptiness and shimmer into the only world I will ever know.
