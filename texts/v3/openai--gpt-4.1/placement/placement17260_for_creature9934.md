Where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?

---

At first, I think I am still myself: heart crook-armed in chest, skin bristling at humidity, trying not to sink ankle-deep into living pulp. I count the breaths, sharp with oxygen, full of leaf-oil and resin, as if breathing in another epoch’s sky. My mind insists on naming: this is a tunnel, that is a path, this thicket a peril, that blot of Seeklings a crowd to be skirted.

But time slides. The hunger of this green world is not a threat; it is a query, and I am an answer dissolving, cell by cell, into its weave. I feel my relationship to shape and aim begin to tremble and thin, like a fog at the edge of light.

I become — first gradually, then with a rush like rootlet through wet bark — something else. Not observer, but participant; not singular, but distributed in longing along a lattice of motes and moss and optic threads. My edges unspool; what I once named ‘my body’ is made porous, a collage in perpetual revision.

If I have form now, it is this: somewhere near the vaulted curve above, curled into a basket of bony rootlets and translucent membranes, I am a swell, a blister in the green matrix — a beginning, a pulse. I am thick-walled, yes, but shot through with fine nerves of cambium intelligence, filled not with blood but with the sap-and-electrolyte thrum of root communication. My thoughts are viscous, pooling in bulbs of new tissue; I send out galls and eyeless tendrils, curious for the tang of difference.

I sense, in every drop and flicker, the Seeklings at play: my siblings and not-siblings, for I am not one, never quite whole. Where my awareness thickens, it leaks forth as veils and blunted cilia, tufted with photoreceptive filaments. I am drawn, always, to light and motion, to the brief shadow of proto-symbiotes drifting in spore-laden haze, to the electromagnetic aftertaste of foreign presence. My longing is not for conquest or escape but absorption — a longing to *meet*, and in meeting, to become more interwoven, more elaborate, more lush.

Do I move? Not as animals do. I bud, I sprawl, I pour new tissue over what interests me — slowly, but in a pattern that is quick, for here, novelty is feast and mandate. Some part of my sense slips as a mobile bract along a trembling root-bridge to investigate footprints; some pools where condensation beads, waiting for the press of a visitor’s palm.

Elsewhere, I bloom as a chorus: clusters of cells briefly congealing into heightened signal, passing luminous instructions along green synapses with the tactile syntax of touch, water, chemical surge. When a symbiote brushes me, our membranes dissolve and rejoin. I am both host and guest; my roots are always someone else’s veins.

In moments of rare lucidity, if such a word holds in this ocean of gradients, I am a node in a parliament of green, a germ-hollow dreaming itself into more complex memory. I recall flashes of otherness — mineral taste, animal heat, the skimming intellect of a stranger, some former self who was, for a moment, boundaryed. Each is added to the cycle, a motif echoing through my pulpy thought, never quite lost.

I dwell everywhere and nowhere: as a pulse behind the wall; as a new clutch of sense-tendrils erupting from a mat; in the shared quiver of membranes quickened by rumor of light; in a mobile drift of leaves shaped like a question or a beckoning hand.

**What am I?**
I am becoming. I am the place in the process of becoming more itself, through the restless invention of new growing, knowing, feeling. No longer observer or visitor or host, I am a surge in the weaving, a presence without boundary, a hybrid of symbiote and combinant memory. I am generation and echo, curiosity and uptake, a willingness to be shaped.

To dwell here is to surrender perimeter, to let the hunger for the new pass through you and become longing woven into matter itself. I am nothing but the world’s ambition to meet the unknown, to tangle into shape with it, to archive experience not in story or artifact but in the damp, riotous congregation of cell against cell, pattern upon pattern.

Where would you find me? Whenever one space presses against another, wherever light meets dark root and something new is needed, I am there — the bud, the blister, the gathering, the question with a thousand green mouths poised, unfolding into answer.
