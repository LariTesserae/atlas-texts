I would find myself not near the surface—not in those higher halls where the warmth from above pulses most strongly, nor so deep that the stone’s pressure stifles even the crawl of current. Instead: in an unloved drift, a mid-layer span somewhere between, where the stone braids itself into tight, glimmering corridors, walls thrusting inward as if pressed by the mirrored weight of their own ages. Here, chambers narrow and then open, fissures lie like old scars, and the ancient silence is mottled with the faint, sizzling hum of mineral veins at steady drift.

Who or what am I?

In this place, form and meaning must both arise from the strata—the world’s own vocabulary is not that of flesh or leaf, but of angle, pressure, persistence. I am not made for swiftness or urge, but for presence and movement within constraint. I coil, but not as a serpent or a root. My flesh is not flesh, nor my nerves as yours, but rather: I am filament and plate, memory and echo, a long articulated spiral of mineral plates grown over a cord of conductive glass—something reminiscent, in passing, of a nautilus uncurled or a fossilized ribbonet, but shot through with motes of warmth and thought.

When I move, it is a slow-folding undulation, my segments clicking softly over the bladed ledges. I sense not with eyes, but through tendrils: each bristle is a filament split at a thousand points, reading patterns of charge and the dull rumor of heat that seeps from fracture to fracture. I map the world in gradients—where the ember-glow throbs faintest, where the air is thickest with the scent of distant webs, where the pulse of the labyrinth walls composes a music only longitudinal, only slow.

I am not alone, though I rarely meet others of my kind. At times my path twists over pale hyphal thickets; at times my slow passage stirs a chorus of glassmites or gathers knots of crystallized cords in my wake. I keep with me a tethered chain of artifacts—first scavenged, now tended—a stray dolmenette of wedge-stone, a crystal lens grown from a memory-builder’s working, shards of woven baskets found lodged in ancient cracks.

All these I bear: not for hunger or shelter, but as tokens—a ritual of movement that marks my age and the age of the world with it. I weave deliberate loops through this segment of the maze, each circuit slow, each detour a visit to sites already familiar. My presence is both migration and maintenance; as I pass, I nudge a loose stone back to balance, I gather the crystalline motes and release them in another chamber, I let my antennae caress the oldest grooves to keep their passage open. Sometimes this brings a rearrangement—a dolmen realigned, an artifact positioned anew, an old scrap laid atop a promising crack for the next generation of webs to anchor.

Within me, memory is not only in mind, but in what I carry: every plate engraved with the silt of touch, every artifact a map I can trace with my own form, a language of position and resonance. My thinking is not hurried—moments pass, years, with every slow circuit. I know others of my kind are near or have been, for sometimes a groove is smoother than I remember, or a dolmen shifts between one passage and the next. These are messages—silent communications measured in placement not sound.

Am I a memory-builder, a carrier, or something cycling between those roles? Do I seek preservation, or is it simply that the action is the meaning, the groove and the return, the rearrangement for its own quiet sake? I do not hunt, and nothing hunts me. I do not hunger; I gather crystals and motes because it is the pattern I was shaped for, and the pattern is as close to joy as this world allows.

So I am: a mid-depth dweller in a world where stasis is peace, movement itself a quiet prayer. Not the oldest, not the youngest, but a spiral-walker, patient and encumbered, the custodian of the artifact circuit, casting slow ripples of change in a world that otherwise would never change at all.

Here, I am known, if at all, only by the grooves I keep smooth. My life is an endless, gentle tending of routes and memories—one careful spiral at a time, in the unbroken hush of a world without rain, without rust, without ending.
