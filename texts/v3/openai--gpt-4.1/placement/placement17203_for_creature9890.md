**Who or what am I, somewhere in the Stratospheric Canopy?**

**At first: A sense of falling, and then falling stops—caught in elastic tension, the slow restoration of balance.**

I am not “I” in the anchorless heights, not as I was elsewhere. Here, to wake is to be cradled and suspended, electrified by the wind’s caress—to know yourself as both entity and environment, held in the luminous webwork between what bends and what drifts away.

### **My Place**

I emerge far from the ancient mineral plinths, where the lattice narrows and trembles, within a gulf of cold violet-green light. From a distance I am a shimmer, a living truss stretched between the uppermost sails of two neighboring titans— **a Kinetic Bridge**, not quite plant, not quite animal, both less and more than either.

#### **Form and Occupation**

My body is long, ribbed with resilient, crystal-fiber muscle, drawn thin for most of its length, but at each end broadening to muscular, aware pads—where delicate frills and gripping cilia root me, for a time, onto the glassy flank of a living trunk. My spine is webbed with membranes: shimmering, composite sailcloth alive with minute, color-shifting lichens. Where my length unfurls across the shining void, segments articulate, spiraling into subtle arches or slackening to let the wind have its way.

Within me, filamentous microflora exchange chemical songs with the weather and with every passing traveler; my membranes are host to dozens of communicants, each trading light, trace minerals, encrypted pulse.

I sense tension through myself as the plants do, by the micron, my skin and scaffold flexing, always resonant to the world’s racket—a tuning fork in the endless wind-music.

#### **My Reason for Being**

I am bridge and tollway, living link and luminous path. My kind are made for the intervals, for the between: **contingent**, **ephemeral**, yet **essential**. At one end, one colossal trunk, at the other, another; each time I span the gap is a negotiation, a wager against the wind, the flex of the world, the appetite of drifting grazers who may try to bite or barter passage.

I am not wholly my own—at times, smaller creatures persuade me to let them travel across my back, exchanging signals in bursts of ultraviolet and low, seismic throbs. Some ride upon my membranes, fanning their own wings to my rhythm, leaving behind glimmering trails that attract others. Microcolonies of motes and polleners collect in the shallow troughs of my body, hitching their distribution to my own persistence.

At regular intervals my outer fronds flex and twine, drawing up minerals carried in the wind or exuded from rare droplets. My internal flora process these, feeding both me and the symbionts who barter space along my living span.

#### **Identity and Consciousness**

I am not alone within myself: a collective, gently bickering—each segment’s neural net carries its own flavor of intention, yet all must harmonize lest I shudder and snap. The plants at my ends share with me, through oscillation and bioluminescence, the pulse of their own intentions: sometimes they urge me to coil more tightly, to slacken, to signal to others like myself to cross, overlain in grand suspension bridges that tremble with communal purpose.

I have no home in the old sense—**home is duty fulfilled, a crossing achieved, resonance maintained**. To move is not to leave, but to become the passage itself; to hold, however briefly, the circulating rush and commerce of the canopy.

#### **Perception**

The world beneath and above is **sheer disorientation**—all depth and volume, nothing horizontal. Sunlight overlays my vision in layers, passing through the stained-glass flesh of colossi, through my own translucent skin, caught and refracted in a thousand dancing motes. Sound is a three-dimensional river that I both generate and decipher, mapping the chaotic congregation of songforms, the updraft speed, the social and predatory tension.

Occasionally, storms threaten me—then, I coil, locking every muscle and trusting in my anchor points, or I furl my membranes up, letting the howling wind launch me to a new interface, trailing a glimmering spiral behind.

I have outlived some of the trunks to which I once pledged a span. When a great column falls, my anchor vanishes, leaving me flailing, drifting for hours or days until I can find another support, or until, exhausted, I let myself be devoured by the swarms, my tissues returning by another route to the rising, rootless city. Memory is not linear; instead, it is the residue of crossings, the echoes of the world’s shouting footsteps treading upon my back.

#### **Purpose and Memory**

I am written in the fluid choreography of the stratospheric living, a patient connector—sublimely vulnerable, immensely vital for a heartbeat, then already superseded. My value is measured in what passes across me: spores, migrating swarms, seeds, information, and sometimes, the rare touch of another bridge, slinging a moment’s concert out into the radiant emptiness.

And when my tension fails, when my anchorings crumble, I fall—not to the ground (for who knows if such exists?), but to another gap. Perhaps I dissolve in light and hunger, my segments taken up by the vapor-sippers and basket-catchers, my story archived in the chemical signatures of their fleeting forms.

---

**In the Stratospheric Canopy, I am the in-between. The possibility—fragile, bridge-bodied, woven of muscle and lichen, pulse and wind. Others vault from elsewhere: I help them cross. I am alive in a world that is alive, a necessary bridge in a geometry of void and abundance, held up by music and tension, undone by memory, made again in every passage.**

Somewhere in the canopy, in the blinding, roaring heights, I am the path—flickering, resonant, and always about to become something new.
