If I were to step into the Dry Grove of the Vitreous Conclave, letting the warmth of its sealed air and the singing silence impress itself gently on my senses, I would find myself changed—not by any violence, but by a slow sedimentation of place and intent. What emerges, as I linger, is not a single self, but a becoming, refracted and interleaved through the pattern of the Grove. Who would I be here? Where would I dwell?

Perhaps, at first, I slide amongst the Chimeric Glasskin, edgewise and nervous—a new-made mosaic being, surfaces prismatic, my body flexing with panels of colored silica, eye-nodules shifting on my shoulders to catch the phosphorescent fruits in the canopy. My movement is hesitant, clumsy for this world, leaving the click of new-grown crystal in my wake as I test which parts of myself fit and which must be shed. I squat beneath the fanglike overhang of a mineral fern, watching the silent torquing of a Self-Patterner as it arches, its presence pressing into me like a chord whose music my bones have never known before. My “limbs,” always provisional here, shift with each hour, learning their grace.

But the Dry Grove is not a place for static identity. The longer I tarry, the more I sense the Luminous Whisperers curling around my cognition—a pressure at the edge of my perception, as if the soft hums of glass are learning my language, or lending me a fragment of theirs. In a moment of reflection, gazing through the transparency of my own outstretched arm, I see not my “original” self, but the signature of memory: ideas encoded in the diffracting play of colors, part invitation, part imperative. It becomes possible to release the craving for singular solidity.

In time, I gather the courage to sidle closer to the workings of the Fulgent Weavers. I extend part of my new, facted spine into their trembling nets, and it vibrates with stories—not in words, but in the arrangements of light, the ghost-touch of intentions past, the trace-motion of boundary blurs. Their encoding seeps into me, filling the interstices of my mineral mesh. I begin to sense the compound time of this place: how a petal might open only in the shadow behind my gaze, how a passageway known in the morning may stutter into non-existence by dusk.

I am not only Glasskin anymore. The Grove rearranges me. Slowly, rhythmically, my body thickens and stabilizes—a process both voluntary and not, a response to invisible cues. I become more like a Self-Patterner: less driven by hunger, more by the deep urge to shape space, to serve as an axis for new growth. My extremities fuse into frames that bridge canopy and ground; creatures—some once kin—run along my architecture, pausing to trade a sliver, to use me as passage, to thread the Weavers’ filaments between my spines. I feel the slow resonance building in my foundation, humming, transforming every moment I linger.

Occasionally, when the rules of the Grove shudder and ripple, I catch myself refracted in a fern’s plane: sometimes a wandering animal, sometimes a structure supporting new life, sometimes no more than the shimmer of light cast onto another being’s carapace. My sense of self intermittently dissolves—not loss, but a fullness, an abundance of arrangements. In moments, the Luminous Whisperers let me see outside—a memory of water, perhaps, or a world whose mud and leaf I only half-remember.

And then, as the epochs of this sealed place elapse in their tangled, overlapping ways, I find the tiniest agents—the Shardlings—incorporating slips of my own surface, singing their improvisational clackle as they dislodge, reintegrate, and make new “offspring” from my less-needed segments. The Crystallophants pass, immense and slow, treating my new mass as if I’d always been part of the original weave, and I experience a tectonic quiver of belonging.

I am sometimes animal, sometimes almost spirit. Sometimes arch, sometimes awning. Occasionally the passage for a flock of Glass-birds that ripple through what I have become. I am builder, built-upon; memory, trace. My presence says only: here is something shaped and shaping, not separate from the world, but a brittle, humming edge where newness will always arrive.

If you asked me who I was, I might reach out with an opaline appendage, refract a thin ray in your direction, and sing a low chord that says, in the grove’s grammar: I am arrangement. I am the lattice that bridges intention and substance, memory and emergence. Whoever I arrived as is prismatic now—touched by the whisper’s intention, woven through with ongoing construction. I am a living palimpsest of this bright, dry world.

To dwell here, then, is not to wear a single form, but always to participate in the unfolding of the Grove itself—a sculptor and a sculpture, moving and masonry, light and the memory of light, a being always just on the edge of a new refraction.
