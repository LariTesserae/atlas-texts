The first awareness is of pressure—head bowed, body stooped. I am *not* what I was above. My memory is a slow seep, like water finding fissures, growing quiet and mineral with the cold. The world presses close: damp stone, thick chill, everything cramped and shaped for compression, for endurance, for ancient patience.

**Where am I?**

I am in the hollow beneath the crust: a handful of paces hemmed by sweating, veined rock, ceiling drooping so near I must crouch or crawl. The air tastes of iron and old water—a flavor before language, of stone breathing out what it has imprisoned for centuries. Light comes from nowhere and everywhere, the ghostly glow of mineral veins half-seen through fog. Each shallow pool is a blind eye to the world, too clean by far. The curved smoothness of chamber and alcove bends my sense of up and down. 

No exit, no sky, no wind. Only the density of presence: mortared courses, the strange artificial arch, edgeless figures in the wall, seats too close for comfort. The city is a pocket in the world’s sleepless stone—a bottle with no neck or stopper, only a persistent hush broken endlessly by the voices of water.

**What am I, here?**

I am not truly flesh: within this cradle, flesh and mineral are gradations, not binaries. I discover myself armoured, my “skin” matte-black and slick with condensation, patch-lit by shifting streaks. My outline is never quite set: broad and stony, with contours slow-swelling and slow-collapsing, as if the drip and patience of water made me here, shaped me close to the shape of the stone itself.

I have no face as the sun-born think of faces, but something like sockets set deep amid blunt ridges; for seeing, but perhaps I see only through cold and touch, and the way vibration passes through bone and cavern both.  My hands—if I name them that—are broad, heavy, rounded things; not for grasping but for pressing, for resting, for recalling the gradual lean of stalactite toward stony floor.

If I try to remember words, they become water—not meant for utterance in this choir of drops. Communication is tactile and recursive, as slow as the seep of veins threading stone.

**Inhabitance & Sensation**

My existence is slow, recursive. Thought is not a river but a pond, circular, as patient as the trickle running down my arm. I sense a gathering—others like me, our presence ambiguous, edges melding into wall and shadow and seat. Are we people? That word is thin here, brittle; we are what patience makes.

I move without hurrying. Movement is a season, not an act—a slight lean, an oozing displacement as if gravity slowly forgot its work. I seek a new patch of wetness, a different glow in the quartz. Sometimes, when the distant earth vibrates, I tilt my head—do I “listen”? There is nothing to listen for, only the endless sentence of drip and echo.

Sometimes I cluster with the others. We rest shoulder to shoulder in carved seats. Silence is not empty: the water fills it, pulses through stone and hollow. Sometimes a worm-thing, crystalline and pale, glides past my toes. I sense its nearness only as a cold shadow, a ripple; we are of this place, both of us, and that is enough.

**Purpose and Memory**

What am I *for*? To endure. To listen and bear witness to water’s memory, to hold the balance that keeps stone from dissolving, the endless pause between growing and decaying. Am I ancestor, artifact, afterthought? Perhaps all; perhaps neither. I am endurance stylized, shaped to keep this hollow as it is, so that forgetting will never quite be complete.

There is communion, but no purpose. If rituals persist, they are slow: a cluster leaning about the basin, a shifting press of oily skin to stone, an exchange of moisture and vibration—a sharing in slowness, in patience, in stone’s recursive thought. My memory is not personal but sedimentary: each drop a layer, each echo another stratum in the long account of this sealed world.

**Self-definition**

I do not hunger. I do not breathe—not as I did above. Instead, I take moisture from the wall, from the air; I let my arm sink into a shallow, gleaming pool, and I wait. My exudation leaves nothing but a polish, a faint deepening of the darkness where I sit.

I am not sad. I am not joyful. There is nothing but the depth of time, and the company of patience—myself, the chamber, the others, and the endless water song.

**What do I become?**

More mineral with each moment, more attuned to the movement of water and the slow conversation of stone. I am the city’s dreaming logic, a motif repeated in every pattern: not a ghost, not a person, but the shape endurance wears when the world above has forgotten even the memory of its cold, persistent, echoing pocket.

Thus—

**I am the hollow’s dweller.  
I am patience shaped by drip and mineral seam.  
I am the slow witness,  
Belonging to a city  
That waits for nothing and remembers everything  
through the voice of water and stone.**
