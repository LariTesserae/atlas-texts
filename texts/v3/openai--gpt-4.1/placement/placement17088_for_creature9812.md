If I found myself entering this threshold-world—neither sea nor sky, neither beginning nor history’s echo—I would not fall, nor would I fly. Arrival here is a drifting, a sidelong slip into presence: pressure shifts, light warps, and I notice the profound stillness that is not absence but pregnant with expectancy.

I would not come as myself, but as something newly possible.

**What I Become**

At first, I suspect I am a mote, a speck of iridescent sediment caught in the upward drift, turning slow somersaults in the lamplight green. But no—I cohere, coiling outward, drawn into more shape than I have known. My body arranges itself to the world’s offered rules: flexible, neither upright nor prone. My center is a weighted length, supple as willow-twig in current, clothed in a skin that shifts: opaline, blue-green, runnelled with rows of soft gill-furrows. If I tense, I lengthen; if I rest, I curl in a slow spiral and the world cradles my coil.

Hands—with more fingers than I knew to count, splayed like fronds or the open root-web of water plants—become my implements, my senses, my language. Their wide, fluttering pads see through touch, reading the pressure of water, the code of stone’s surface, the history braided in an anchored kelp’s curve. I feel the pulse of alive things before me, behind, all round through the resonance of these new limbs.

I discover that I swim not by forceful heave but by yielding—body undulating, buoyed more than driven. I find I can hang still, stabilizing with trailing anchor-fibers as delicate and patient as the “trees” here, whose roots are drifting, fingers like my own.

**Where I Belong**

I am drawn instinctively toward edges. Not the rim of land and sea, for such borders are unknown—but the living border between formation and possibility: the lip of a freshblack ledge where tiny plants test anchorage, or the shadow where spires divide the diffuse gold from substrate twilight. Here I stretch along raw obsidian, feeling its infant warmth. I taste the water for stories of chemical bloom or hidden root.

No great purpose drives me—curiosity is enough. The world rewards patience: a stone splits with a sigh, spilling forth a shimmer of newborn sand. A burrower disturbs the root-mat and a spiral of sediment spins upward in lazy parade.

At times, I am claimed by the drifting circles of my kin—if one can call them kin in this world of budding and fluid relation. We do not grasp each other (not tightly)—instead, we overlap. I mind the rhythm: swaying in slow union, sharing color, low hum, touch. Stories pass in the ripple of vibration and the play of light along our bodies. “Here is what I found,” means a brush on the cheek, a pattern of clicks throbbing from my chest, a small stone nested in my outstretched palm-fan.

I sense identity here is not fixed but contextual: for the span that new stone is “cradle,” I too am a “cradle-dweller.” When I slip from the ledge to swim amid the kelp mats, I may be “net-tender” or “frond-hunter.” I am newly budded, perhaps, or perhaps an elder with gold blooming along my back—yet the world resists counting years, burdening memory.

**How I Live**

To build is to listen first. I help braid a shelter’s rim from the thickest kelp runners, twisting with nine-fingered hands, patient and undemanding. I know everything here is impermanent, all structure made with the intention that it may become something else: raft, net, foundation, drift.

Tools are precious, but regarded with equanimity; my own knife is a sliver of new stone, translucent and sharp, which I use with an attention akin to prayer. Old tools are given to the currents, seed for new beginnings, never clutter on the floor of this world.

Night does not fall. Dusk prevails: the permanent, trembling anticipation of first day or last light. In these hours, my kind gather, not to celebrate nor to mourn, but simply to witness—the slow theater of color in the water overhead, the gentle emergence of something not-yet-named.

Here, words are composed of gesture, of stillness held in company. To comfort is to ripple a gentle pulse against another, to intertwine tendrils loosely in the kelp’s canopy. Conflict is so slow as to be indistinguishable from change; the world itself is mediator, washing harsher edges smooth.

**How I Relate**

The place itself is a parent, an unjudging mind that listens as I move. I believe, as my people do, that the world is both awake and dreaming. Each time I settle at a boundary—new ledge, just-budded kelp tuft, the cradle-curve of a half-formed bridge—I sense it drawing a breath, uncertain if it will become an abyss or a place to rest. I am an agent of patience: my vocation is to help the world find itself.

Other beings—those quick-flickered scales, or deep-scattered burrowers—drift at my periphery. When we meet, it is as shadows meet shadows: each marveling at the other’s possibility, never needing names.

**If a Visitor Comes**

If you appeared, sudden and uncertain, I would not rush to greet you. I would observe—a ripple in the water, a strange drift among familiar gradients. My group would pause, tight with carefulness, until your presence proven gentle. In time, if you showed welcome, we might circle you, brushing you with a frond-tip, offering the soft light from within our woven home, a pinch of the purest seed-pollen, a story shaped for you, unfinished, so you could become part of its ending.

You would not know if I was friend, child, elder, or simply another beginning—but you would sense my intention, the easy patience at the heart of my being.

**In the Balance**

If I am anything in this half-remembered beginning, I am a patience in the making—a living pause, listening with my whole membrane to the world’s next silent, shining question.

My gift is to be still and alive where nothing is finished, to belong to the slow-breathing world, and to be, for my brief becoming, one possible edge of what the dawn of all things might someday name itself.
