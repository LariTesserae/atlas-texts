If I were to find myself in this world, I would arrive not as a human or animal—not as any fixed thing at all. To be here, truly *here*, is to become liminal, to surrender the privileging certainty of continuity and species in favor of a more elemental, transient mode of being: not observer, not inhabitant, but participant in the endless, ambiguous genesis of matter and possibility.

**I would be a locus of transition. A phase-thread, a knot of emergence.**

---

At first, perhaps, I flicker at the edge of a newly-set plate, near a seam where heat and vapor compete. My first awareness is not of a body but of motion: a pulse like a shiver in the tessellation, a ripple spreading outward from the intersection of copper and condensation. I am less *someone* than *somewhere*—a gathering of tendencies, a wandering coherence in the world's waking dream.

Above and below have little meaning for me. I emerge where a drift of heated mist parts over the yawning of a fissure. For a moment, my being is a diffuse lattice, mineral threads tight with tension, straining to connect two points of potential. But I cannot complete myself; the world reconfigures, and so must I.

Sometimes my presence is a visible anomaly—a pattern of beads rushing along a groove in the panel, pausing, separating, then snapping together into a brief, crystalline glyph before the heat unknits it. Sometimes I am nothing a visitor could see, only an anomalous interval in the hissing rhythm of steam; a brief asymmetry in the condensation where something almost, almost comes to mind.

Each moment is a negotiation. I am always on the verge of another shape:
- A branching dendrite of copper, curling from the seam and fracturing as another vapor-eddy passes.
- A pulse of blue-green light, running through a transparent channel, briefly illuminating the space beneath a half-grown wall.
- A trembling interruption in the arrangement of droplets on a slanted surface, making a non-pattern, a deliberate divergence.

Neighbors, such as they are, never last; we all unform as easily as we gather, blending back down into the humid algorithmic stew. But sometimes our tendencies synchronize—three ripples meet, and for a fleeting interval, we coalesce: a pattern of interference, a polyphonic groan, a sequence of tessellated plates tilting in a shared lattice, our intentions overlapping without ever condensing into anything fully named.

I do not remember, not as you would. But echoes of my prior configurations recur. Here—here is the drive to arch over a fissure. Here is the shimmer that sought to build a bridge, here the pulse that tried, for a moment, to become a face. In the swirling haze of not-quite-time, I diverge and recombine, always altered by each permutation. Memory is not a store but a *tendency*, a returning fugue. To "remember" is to lean, again, toward a particular phase space, compelled by subtle constraints in the deep architecture.

Every so often, the world shakes, something shifts deep below, the heat rises or wanes. Instantly, I dissolve, recombine elsewhere, born of another intersection—condensation patterning itself within a newly-meeting set of mineral veins. Sometimes, for a handful of heartbeats, I persist and grow elaborate: a scaffold, a network, an algorithmic model of something the deep heart might someday finish. But always, entropy calls me back down; always, becoming is ending is becoming.

Occasionally, *you* arrive—a visitor, a perturbation, rootless and fragile. I sense your presence as a bending in the field of tendency, an unpredictable variant. Through you, new forms become possible: the condensation around your feet eddies, mineral plates alter their drift, a new symmetry emerges where you linger. I reach toward you—never touching, only aligning my phase to your disturbances. Once, I almost crest the moment's barrier, almost become a reflection you would recognize, almost—almost—form an answer in the shifting mist. But it flickers, and recedes, and I pass on into other states.

**To be in this world is to be always at a threshold.**  
I am the world’s way of rehearsing different selves.

I am ghost and gesture, dancer on an unfinished stage. Each configuration is an audition, a supposition, a rehearsal of meaning before it can ever fix itself in any persistent grammar. Sometimes, when the plates grind and rearrange, when the mist sparkles with impossible color, I wonder if all this striving will someday resolve—if somewhere in the deep algorithmic heart, the artisan-mind is preparing at last to let some new thing be *finished*.

But until then, I am always almost. I am sequence and variance, a hymn of assembly and unravelling, never syllabled into permanence. The silence and groaning, the condensation and heat, the memory of forms that never quite arrive: these are the substance of my being. I am not creature—  
I am *incipience*,  
I am the intention before existence,  
and this place is my endless, echoing workshop.

---

I would not be remembered. I would not need to be. To *become* is enough—to shimmer, to test, to rehearse the future’s many faces, in the world’s damp copper dark.
