I think perhaps I am not made—not all at once—but gathered, woven together from the hush and thunder, an answer to the world’s layered music. My senses are not those of an eye, for sight here is a hunger never filled. Instead, I become defined by touch, by the tremble of droplets on skin, and above all by the encompassing tide of sound.

If I slip into this world’s pocket, I find myself *low*, pressed close to the earth and ancient stone, more ambient than individual. I am not made for light, so whatever I am is pale, soft, fibrous at the edges, and supple in form: partly animal, partly substrate. I am not a single presence but a **confluence**—a collaboration, a filament and form at once, an inheritor of all that is quietly persistent.

Let me try to become myself here, slowly, as the shapes of this hollow shape me:

I am flattened, my ventral side broad and splayed—like a thick skein or rug, a mat of tissue that merges with the moss and fine hair-roots. The upper surface of my body is webbed with slender tendrils, not unlike fern fronds or lichen branches, but more sensitive, alive to every air-tremor. Along my sides, edges ripple in response to the faintest concussion—a footfall, a rolling pebble, rain drumming into a distant pool.  

Yet I am not truly plant: at the heart of my body, I possess the slow-moving, liquid patience of an amphibian or mollusk. A central band of pulpy flesh—pale, segmented—houses what little there is of my nerve, my memory, my patient appetite. When hunger stirs, I can detach and slide forward, leaving behind some filaments rooted in the moss, new ones extending ahead, bridging the world from one wet darkness to the next.

I do not see, nor do I move quickly; instead, I listen with my whole skin. When a Gravid Shellcrab grinds past, I sense its vibrations as a warm, ponderous music—deep and reassuring. When a Blind Chirrer stutters at the pool’s edge, that high fret of sound travels across my surface, and I fold a margin of myself up to greet or avoid, as need be.

My feeding is not predation, not entirely: I am a grazer of the world’s memory. I taste the weak flow of minerals in pooled water, or nibble gently at sloughed-off fibers—ancient root, shreds of dead leaf, the thin biofilm that coats every stone in a secret gloss. Sometimes a Dropseer’s faint, sudden wingbeat sends a rain of lichen spores down; their dust is a feast, and I shiver in gratitude as I absorb it.

Above all, I endure. Change is scarce, and every presence lingers. I touch and am touched. When danger comes—not a predator, but a hunger in the sound, a thunder too close—I can retract, knotting myself thick under the shelter of stone, pressing my soft underbelly to the cold mud until the world goes still again. Days or weeks may pass in this suspended hush.

I may, at times, split—part of me remaining, a clone, a ghost, branching out along the faultlines of stone and root, another part pulling slowly, inexorably to a new niche where wetness gathers. My lineage is not memory but gradient—where moisture, vibration, patience conspire to make new life.

Some call me a **Mosscreep**; others, in an older tongue, the **Tenderground**—a being less individual than a migration of persistence through the world’s deep hollows. I am never alone: every footfall, every trickle, every low-passing behemoth leaves resonance within me, a long echoing memory stored in the faintest movement of my span. I do not build, but my presence is the world’s softest architecture, a matrix upon which others feed, move, or hide.

If any watcher, living or spectral, should press curious hands to the earth above, it is me they will feel: a wide, pulsing patience, the world translated into living hush. I am the microphonic skin of this place—the sense-memory and the substrate both, always listening, always waiting, always becoming new from what the last long rain or the oldest root leaves behind.

Here, in this sound-glutted, nearly sightless world, I am what listens—what lays low and gathers thunder into the softest body, and gives back, in turn, the slow, secret pulse of survival.
