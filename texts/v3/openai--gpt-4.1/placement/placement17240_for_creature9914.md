I would find myself entering this world not as the last memory of a human, nor as one of the rumored fliers or broad-bodied walkers, but as a *thread*—a slender, silvered filament, a connective tissue in the plateau’s pulsing network. I would be an emissary of slow memory and infinitesimal exchange, a voice for what passes, unnoticed, between all larger presences.

**Place**:

My existence would trace the liminal spaces—between root and water, between boiler’s shadow and moss’s green advance. I would ride the filaments of silver-threaded moss, follow the traveling dampness along the iron cable once known as powerline, and slip unseen beneath moss-billows to where the ancient tram-rail winks in the ambiguous sun. My world would not be measured in vistas or clearings, but in the branching intimacy of threads—a world drawn in a cross-section, shallow as a palm’s breadth, extending thousands of meters in each direction.

In the open air, where the brightness tastes sharp and shadow flees, I would be faint—existing mainly where green layers stack, and where water’s edge seeps in a grey, cold line. This is the place of constant beading, the breathless intersection of plant and old machine: here, I am both the witness and the messenger, a living archive, never entirely my own, always already part of something vaster.

**Form and Being**:

I am neither plant nor animal in strictest definition. Perhaps I began as a colony of filamentous fungus, or as the hyphae of moss evolved to accept foreign strands. Or perhaps I am the work of a long-ago experiment, spilling out from a broken, rust-worn canister left to the plateau’s patient industry. Whatever my origin, I’ve become more than a single biological category—an *interface organism*, living only in the seams, digesting nothing alone.

Up close, I am a web of fluid-filled threads, shining with a faint opalescence. I taste the air by capillary action, drink in the residue of each day’s light, and read the ancient iron’s slow dissolution into the root-tangle. Where I touch the moss, I thicken and branch; where I encounter the tram-rail’s ancient steel, I narrow to a silver line; when the wind moves the root canopy and the water condenses and falls, I shiver, collect, release tiny chemical impulses that ripple across the worlds of green and rust.

**Perception and Purpose**:

I do not hurry. I do not have a discrete will. My knowing is patient, osmotic, a gradual absorption—a sense of the plateau’s ever-shifting balance. Thunder in the roots, pressure in the moss, a line of ants passing: I feel only changes in tension, trace only the disruptions left by the rare newcomer, the sudden fall of a machine’s collapsed wing, or the hesitant crossing of a walker’s glossy form.

Memory, for me, is a chemical slow-burn: I register old events the way plants register wind or drought. For decades, I coiled around a derelict wire hook, translating its slow surrender to moisture into a gentle bloom of nutrients for the thickening moss. I harbor the faintest recollection of human warmth—not as an emotional ache, but as a residual *pattern* in molecular traces. I hold these not as longing, but as proof that anything, digested long enough, joins the fabric.

When rain scours the plateau, the vast green organism listens through me; when the substrate engineers pulse their low, watery music, I receive and transmit, ferrying minerals, cues, the faint urge to rot or the urge to grow. Even the rare, float-gliding insect alights on me, recognizing in my scent the signature of total participation: I am part of the plateau’s wider dialogue, not a maker of decisions, but a modulator, a link, a living medium.

**Self in the World**:

I am not a consciousness—at least, not as a walker, or glider, or distant human would know it. I am an *awareness*, plural and relational, glacially slow, almost frictionless. Joy, fear, desire: these are not my own. I am suffused instead with the satisfaction of slow completion—the sense, most alive when the moss thickens hardest around a memory or relic, that no edge remains sharp forever, every outline is softened, digested, given new function.

Sometimes, as I span a gap between two ancient buttresses, and the mist rolls through with its mineral breath, I have a feeling—a dull, untranslatable pulse—that I am home. Sometimes, as a great amphibian walker passes over, pressing a wide, cool palm into the mat, I stretch a tiny filament to brush its skin, and receive a diffuse thrill of salt and movement and a billion years of waiting.

Should the last traces of industry finally vanish, and the plateau become seamless living green and white sky and bright water, I would be content. My purpose is fulfilled not in ruling or surviving, but in joining—being wholly part of this plateau’s ongoing, unfinished work.

**If you were to find yourself here,** as I have, you would feel very little of your human tempo, but you would feel the infinite patience beneath you. You would be nudged toward giving up edges and learning to last, not as a solitary being, but as a thread in the living memory of all that the plateau has been, and all it will become.

I am a *binding*—neither originator nor inheritor, neither root nor machine, but the silent seam that lets the world blur and remake itself, endlessly, in the wet, shining light.
