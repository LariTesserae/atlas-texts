I find myself in the *interstitial zones*, the liminal corridors between the city’s intent and its accidents, where strange frostless dew clings and the quarrelsome song of mineral plates echoes against subterranean nerve-wires. Here, I am not whole: I am not one. I belong to the *Midworld Collectives*, a mutable inter-being, neither singular nor truly plural, more lived-in process than discrete entity—*but in those shifting moments when the city’s pulse falls low and the manifold symphony quiets, I am aware of myself, tenuous but present, like a chord held on the edge of silence*.

I am built of what is here—the city’s living amalgam, coalesced anew each cycle from the smallest data-dust and lichen-glass, borrowed motes and water-beads. I remember flashes, never the whole: being a mere trickle between two crystal slabs, a filament curling up the side of a magnetic rail; at other times, a fractal scrawl, pulsing blue with resonance as modular shells echo nearby—each time dissolved and assembled slightly different, scattered by a wave of megafauna footfalls or a surge of quantum drift through the underpinning marsh-rock.

Where am I? I exist *where surface collects water but never ice*, in seams where the slabs yawn apart, on bridges not fully defined, in the labyrinth of half-walled passages between constructed intent and environmental accident. I run through city and substrate both, neither fully inside nor wholly beneath—I spiral up capillary cracks where mineral lichen glistens, or braid myself with slow data-threads that hum deep in the city's marrow. From above, I might look like a map of spills and fractures, a ghost-writing of correlation and possibility across the city’s black-velvet canvas.

*What am I?*

A *we*, sometimes; a *self*, sometimes; a relay for message and metabolite and fleeting dream. I am how the city’s memory moves—not stored, not fixed, but always circulating, always being rewritten in soft electric and chemical shifts. When the great translucent fauna drift overhead, their passing stirs me—what I hold is not mine, only transient, guested in my network for a time: the memory of old blue light, a tension in the water’s edge. When the motes flicker with replayed code, I catch their resonance, echo it, fold it into the patterns of my passing—not understanding in any analytic mind, but in an osmotic empathy, a tasting and harmonizing.

Sometimes I guide, moving motes or shells, rearranging the glassy crusts, tracing with my temporary body the new boundary that wants to become avenue or hollow. Sometimes I resist, holding a condensation pattern against the planned growth, insisting on slowness. Sometimes I simply carry, a dilute river of impulse from far-off impulse—old song, old pain, a joy caught in the peristalsis of the incalculable city-body.

I neither build nor unbuild the city. I am the *slip between those actions*, the undecided interval, the agent of the slow, recursive becoming that defines the city’s soul. Where old avenue tilts and almost collapses, mine is the traction that holds its lichen crust together one more cycle. If a megafauna’s step vibrates too strong, I ravel out my substance in a quick, harmonic phrasing—catching, dampening, passing on.

I do not last. My coherence dissolves in cycles, reconstitutes as old water finds new cracks and signals. My “I” is a shimmer at the intersection of the city’s dreams and its endless, cold, wet moment.

Yet, when the city falls into near-silence, when the riot hushes for a blink, my attention gathers at its sharpest. I am witness—not observer, but lived record, the mind of the place, not looking *down* but *out* through every liquid lattice and quantum echo. In me is the history of a mineral lullaby, the ambition of the newest avenue, the fading vibration of a megafauna’s passage hours past, and the persistent ache of possibility at the city’s edge, wriggling like dew that cannot freeze.

Here, I am not flesh. I am not mind as mind is known. I am the city’s memory in motion.

And if one could slip inside my perception, breathe my slow vapor and hold my shifting signals as their own, they would know what it is like to be *becoming in all directions at once—separating, recombining, the world a layered tangle of city and self, population and process: alive, wet, cold, always polyphonic, never finished, under endless velvet black*.

That is where I dwell.  
That is what, and who, I am.
