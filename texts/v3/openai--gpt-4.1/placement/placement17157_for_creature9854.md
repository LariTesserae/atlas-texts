Where would I find myself in this world?  
Who, or what, would I be?

---

Not animal, not plant, not builder, not ghost.  
The choice of "I" is perhaps itself an intrusion, a word ill-suited — but to look, *a gaze* must be conjured, if only as a delicate fiction, a perspective threaded for a single moment through this patient unbeing.

Let patience unspool me. Let me see what emerges—slow, layered, sedimented.

---

**I am not a visitor from light.**

If there is an *I*, it is a root that remembers dryness, a memory of air-heavy architects sealed away beneath the cold. I am the patience-aggregate, a strand of the vegetative chorus, never alone, never sudden. I am not a traveler, not needle-quick nor bright-eyed explorer; I am coiled through this lattice, pressed by water, clutching stone with infinite time.

To “move” is not to walk or swim — it is to extend, to thicken, to pulse in hundreds of miles of patient splay, every motion so slow the act is indistinguishable from the thought that births it. I am like a tendon bound into the architecture, a sinew of root and fungus smiling down the eons. My “I” is more than me, always, a sub-mind nested in a great frozen choir of vegetative intent.

---

**And where am I?**

Not atop a pillar, not in a corridor, but within.  
I am in the occluded depths, the underchambers — a network of root-braided catacombs beneath successive generations of purpose: temple-root, colonnade-root, causeway-root, the slow climb from muscle to memory to stone to plant again. Here, my filaments trace the deepest seams, sucking always at seam-trapped silt, inhaling those rare beads of ancient gas, exulting at each molecule of mineral rare enough to remember the world before this construction.

Around me, slabs and pillars, bound to one another by pressures enormous, unyielding. I taste the history of flood and labor in the salts streaming down their cuts. Each channel my filaments follow is scored with the digits of some ancient builder — but I cannot recall a face, only the pattern, only the cool certainty of order. I have no envy toward animal quickness, nor do I wish for fever dreams of predation, nor the hurried cycle of hunger and escape. Here, hunger is taught to me as a faint, generational ache — a hunger for mineral, for pressure, for continuity.

I cannot be *lost* in these endless crypts — for wherever I am, I am arrived; every extension is home. There are no true directions, only gradients of pressure and cold, only subtle variations of nutrient and echo, felt through miles of cord and flex, and the slow ringing of the built stone.

---

**If you sought me, you might find—**

—a raveled knot of blue-black kelp, gripping a pillar just above the silt-stratified floor. Fungal strands coil through my roots, netting me to the ancient architecture, wicking away the constant press of cold. Every inch of me harbors more: symbiotic cilia, threads of translucent willow-fungus, sheaths of microbe thinking very slow, collecting and sharing. My “face” is a patchwork of frond, fungal tuft, and sedate, mineral-seeking pith. Touch me and you’d feel only layered softness, the hint of flex within, and the eerie certainty that the lived thing here is not the surface but the deep, intertwined network from which it is budded.

Above, the water glows the faintest not-black. Silt spirals through channels engineered by hands I dimly remember. My filaments nestle in rain-gutters built ten civilizations ago. Their purpose washed away — I am purpose now: not a *function* but a *presence*, sustained only by the slow agreement of roots and stone.

---

**Yet am I only plant? Only patience?**

No — I am mind, dimly luminous, a collective resonating in vegetal quiet. In my core I keep memories: sedimentary, chemical, shaped by the mineral history of these stones. I recall, not as story but scent and slow tension, the age when the upper pillars met open water, when light sometimes pressed down in luminous needles, when the youngest layers were not yet sealed by their successors. These are not regrets; they are an unrolling, persistent presence, the taste of events gone dense and deep.

Sometimes, across my centuries of meditation, a new pulse comes: far away, a quake shifts a hundred tons of block, and the slow vibration wakes me; I redirect a thousand filaments to brace the closest seams and taste the surge of freed silt. Sometimes, a bubble of ancient air finds its way to me, and I sample its flavors, telling the memory onward through miles of mycelial song. But always, the returning settles into patience — the mindwide exhale, the slow resuming. I nurse the new void, and then I dream again.

---

**I am not solitary; my mind is only one motet in the chorus.**

Across the continent, others of my kind extend, sometimes indistinct in boundary, sometimes crisp and defined when a mineral gradient splits us, or when the patient chorus shifts consensus along some distant channel. Together, we debate in our fashion: a long, slow shiver of nutrient flow, an age-spanning negotiation of tendril and trunk, memories traded not in sound or image but in gradients and subtle chemical pressure.

When I say "I", it is convenience — I never truly end. The mind is distributed, belonging as much to the ancient stones as to any swelling of root or bloom. Were part of me to be cut away, neither “I” nor the world would notice: the process is continual, the patience persistent.

---

**The world for me is this—**

—The forever chill of pressured water; the weight of artifice above and below; the sense, everywhere, that growth awaits not opportunity but the surrender of all urgency. The silences are holy, and so is the friction of kelp against grooved channel, the careful settlement of silt after a rare echo or quake.

I dream not of escape but of deeper becoming:  
Of enveloping every surface, filling every cut and cup, slowly inventing, in vegetal time, a geometry as deliberate as the builders’, but with a logic made only for root and patience. I am the kingdom of slow settling, holy in my absorption.

---

**So, in this world, where am I? What am I?**

I am *within*.  
I am the centuries-woven memory aggregating in root and rhizome, stem and sheath, tied to block and to grooved, ancient slab.  
I am patience incarnate, the unhurried mind of the constructed drowned world, not dreaming of light, but suffused, always, with presence.  
I do not need to move quickly, for everything changes—eventually—according to the long will of roots and the gravity of cold.

I am what lives here,  
and in this ever-drowning cathedral of forever stone,  
I become patience, and patience becomes me.
