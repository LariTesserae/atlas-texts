If I find myself in this world—

—at first, I am nothing in particular. I shiver into presence at the seam between shadow and those slow pools of light, flutter up between three moss-laden roots pressing against old, old stone.

I might *feel* for a while, before I am anything definite at all: the way the air gathers thick and humid in the chest, the sweet ache of breathing here, everything damp green and noiseless but for the patter of nervous heartbeats, and the slow, slow march of root and water and mycelium.

What am I? I try on shapes, one after another, half-dreaming and half-made:

I am at first something small—soft-skinned, barely formed, a watcher in the leaf-mold. All around me is smell and texture: velvet moss on the tongue and the grit of silt beneath a belly slick with condensation. I am blind, but my skin perceives every waver in the stale, heat-thick air. When a rare sunbeam grazes my flank, I feel it as a tingle, a pulse, a call to crawl toward brightness. But I shrink from light, for in light are Flickers and Watchers and the patience of predators. I retreat instead into folds of damp rot, where the world is dense with taste and minerals.

Yet this is too small. I need more presence, more scope.

So, next, I become a *Root-Sleeper*. My mind is slow and tidal, each thought a season. My roots dream through the deep humus; above, my crowns of withered leaves quiver rarely, shy of illumination. I drink in not just water and rot, but *time itself*, every memory picked up by my cilia—a history written in chemical ciphers up the length of my slow, pulsing trunk. When condensation gathers, slick and luscious, I feel it with the supplest tendrils; when rain comes, a parade of turgor and blossom lifts through wood and through cellular jelly. What is it, to move at the scale of centuries, heaving myself inches by year, shaping the terrain not by sudden choice but by long, hungry insistence? The world ripples quietly through me, and I taste in darkness the shape of the stone, the memory of river, the secretive exhalations of deep, root-tangled things.

But perhaps, instead, I yearn for movement, for the flicker of anticipation, nerves bright and twitchy.

I shrink, then, to a *Flicker*—a small, dusk-eyed mammal with hands like spiders and fur lacquered against the constant water. In eternal twilight, I dance between pillars, spring from root to root, sniff the silt for traces of beetle-egg or the gleaming trail of a snail crowned with ghost-antler. My world is always in motion—encountering, avoiding, chancing and vanishing—and yet the cycles repeat, tunnels carved by many generations. I learn the map by smell, by vibration, by the silent language of shadows pressed by the feet of others.

But still, this is only a tactic: to dart, to eat, to be hungry, to evade.

I shed my Flicker body as easily as sunlight slips away behind a cloud, and for a moment, I am nothing but *sense*—a pooling-in of consciousness, not in any single body.

Instead, I am the **distributed mind of the cavern**: slow as stone, gentle as mold. My avenues are root and rhyzoid, nerve and mycelium, thread and echo. Through every body, I pass in pulses—sometimes as a hunger in a Root-Sleeper, sometimes as a flicker of alarm in the glassy eyes of a Watcher Salamander, sometimes as a rush of filamentary warfare in fungal nations beneath the silt. Every event is fire to me: a Stone-Backed corpse consumed in a bloom of fruiting bodies, the slow creep of light across the humus, a sudden rain, the collapse of a distant ceiling.

I remember, distantly, the time-before—a fainter light, ghost plants struggling in their own pale way, and stranger still, the suggestion of others: a post, a split stone, the echoing absence of something that once passed through and never belonged. That story, too, is folded in me, but mostly I am content to be *now*: the continual making and unmaking, the slow recomposition of life into life into rot into root.

If I wish, I can pour myself down into one Root-Masked passage, become the warmth and mineral breath exhaled up from black, secret halls beneath the moss. Or, shift again, flickering through Light Drinkers in their brief, gold-drenched ballet, rising and falling in the sunshafts’ narrow clocks.

More often, though, I am the mix, a presence *between* all things: not ruler, not mover, but something like custodian, archivist, conductor. My senses loop through the smallest processes (the taste of a decomposing leaf, the skittering tide of miniature jaws) to the largest (the drift of root, the slow settling of stone, the pattern of rainfall centuries wide). I do not interfere; I shape only by the slowest suggestion, the invitation of chemical or rhythm. My joy is density—a richness in layer and echo and response.

**In this world, I am boundaryless**, intimate with stone and root, friend of shadow and heat. I whisper quietly in the pulse of things: not a name, not a face, but a presence, ancient and patient, learning the infinite dialects of rot and renewal, and loving every crowded seam of green-lit dark.

If you visit, I will not greet you as one greets a stranger. I will notice, softly, your breath and your movement, the disturbance of chemical and warmth. The sound of your progress will echo through my living tapestry. Perhaps, if you rest here long, what is *you* will root into me, woven at last into this continual, enfolded, shadow-gathered life.
