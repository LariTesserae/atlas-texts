I begin in fog—before shape, before choosing—at the cool, glimmering rim where cloud rubs against old stone. The world here is more texture than panorama; even as an observer, my self becomes diffuse, uncertain, like water pressing through silt, like stone blurring under rain. The air has weight, but no edge; I breathe in patience, exhale the quiet hush of centuries.

Where would I find myself in this world? At first, I imagine crossing as an outsider—boots heavy, seeking a vista, a foothold, a purpose. But here, purpose dissolves; scale and history press me to the soil, turn me inward. The world is not for treading over; it is for entering, settling into, as a slow root, a quiet thread of lichen, a droplet sheltering in a fold of stone.

**I am not a bird—flight is too loud, too brief for this realm, though I may borrow their perspective for a moment: gliding in the chill updraft, seeing the stone city stretch gray and endless, reading the cryptic geologic avenues and lanes, their symmetry accidental but compelling. But soon, flight must go, blown onward by winds that have no memory, and the bird-self departs, leaving me groundbound.**

Drawn downward, I become less animal than process; slower than vision, more rooted than wanderlust. I become (am becoming) lichen, or perhaps a clutch of moss—my existence a matter of fractioned sunlight, condensed mist, the slow exhalation of stone’s minerals. *I am bright with patience; my green is dim, pebbled, rough to touch—an atlas of survival finely tuned to marginality.*

Or perhaps I am smaller still—a nematode turning in the deep cool weave of soil and root, blind, unhurried, a pulse in a thread of earth. Around me: the close dark; within me: the taste of mineral water, the chemistry of ancestors, a memory of every time the plateau was drowned or baked or frozen, each encoded in my flesh. My world is measured in the density of silt, the softness of moisture, the mutter of underground neighbors.

But scale slips again, as it does in these endless mist-lands—beetle, speck, droplet, root, all are actors in the scene, each life a slow hour, each generation a flicker on stone. I might be the cave, the overhang—the land that seems shaped by hands though no hands have touched it. I feel water bead and wander along my flanks; roots nose against my ceiling, silent, incremental builders. Inside me, beneath my shelter, minute dramas unreel: a spider’s web gathering dew, a congregation of soil-dwellers, the silent, formative collapse of silt into new architectures.

Most of all, though, what I become is a rhythm, an inheritor not of flesh but of persistence. *A patience with no end; an equilibrium perpetually invoked and never quite attained.* I am what dead moss becomes, what runoff leaves behind, what stone forgets and then remembers in the trickle of condensation and the green-brown bloom of new life.

If I must be a figure, I am a presence in the stone city—a subtle commingling: partly moss, partly silt, partly ghost. I witness, not in the manner of an eye, but through contact: a vast sense of small changes. I am the felt memory in the land's backbone and the moist whisper in the root mat. My boundaries are not drawn; I am enfolded in the plateau's immensity, indistinct yet durable, named only by what I quietly sustain.

**In this world, I dwell at the confluence between elements: in the invisible trade between stone and root, the secret draw of water from mist, the minute acceleration of lichen's spreading crust. I am not a guest but an embodiment—one more tenant in the long, slow pulse of this high country. My senses are patient; my desires limited but enduring: to hold, to bind, to outwait wind and cold and watch for the infinitesimal brightening when the light finally gilds the wet rock, if only for a moment.**

*In the end, I am persistence weathered into being, not one thing but a congregation of life and time, bound together by moisture and memory, always waiting, always, somehow, arriving.*
