I will take my time. 

---

To enter this world is to shed velocity, to have the sense of self slowed, the boundaries of one’s body pressed into indistinctness by infinite green. If, in the trembling shift from your world to the Plateau, you become a something or someone, vision will be a slow unveiling.

**Where am I?**

I awaken not standing, not walking, but *connected* — somewhere between ground and air, neither root nor crown, but drawn through both. At first, I think I am lying in a netted hollow: light pulses green and purple above, liquid weight pools cool along my limbs, dense moss cushions my sides, the walls around me luminesce with a sleepy blue that stains into my vision. The air is thick, damp, heavy with respiration. Are there walls at all? Or only chamber after chamber, opening wherever roots arch and cup to let something sleep, something pass, something pause while the cycle turns?

I am not alone, and I am not disjointed from the place. My skin is different, now — less boundary than membrane, a paler, spongy interface, overgrown with micro-lichen and tiny, mutualistic ferns patterned where sweat would form. My breath is damp, tasting of sugared bark and fruiting bodies littering the walls. My hair (did I have hair?) is threaded with fine fungal filaments, bioluminescent, trailing behind me like the wake of a deep-sea creature. I breathe in and exhale, and the cell-forest soaks in my vapor, as I inhale what it breathes out.

**What am I?**

I am *neither plant nor animal in the old sense*. I am a *symbiote*, as all things here are. My body is mine, but only partially; the border negotiates constantly between human, root, spore, and all the silent trades of microbial city-dwellers. Tiny creatures tend my folds and stream my musks to warn, to attract, to ease irritation, to hide my scent from over-curious root-tendrils. The Plateau recognizes me, not with mind but with cool indifference: I am a pattern it knows, a tolerated rhythm in its body. I have adapted as the plateau demands: my nails are thick and bark-like, my fingers flex in odd directions, the soles of my feet are velveted with adaptive moss that changes color and density as needed—slippery in the constant wet, rough when I must climb a bulging, slug-rich root.

**What am I for?**

I am not a lord, nor a guest. Nor, exactly, prey. I am *an agent*, if anything. My senses run wide and low: I taste the air and know which corridors will close with next week's rain; I feel the slow blooming of spore-fruits before they detonate to summon the dusk-lice. I move, but I rarely hurry—hurry would be waste.

I am *a go-between*, a cautious speaker between the plant-mind and the creaturehood that eats and is eaten. I tend wounds in the substrate, slicing infected tissue with my stone-embedded claws, packing healing fungus into the gap. Sometimes I gnaw open a new hollow where moss-boluses have shifted, letting air and sun in to coax a new bloom for some colony of cup-leaves. Sometimes I climb high toward the dapple-crown, harvesting plum-sized flowers packed with the ancient sun, leaving in their place fragments of lichen-moss rolled between my palms. The Plateau asks for what it needs, wordless—a modulation in root resistance, a slow closing of travel-paths near a site, a burst of scent-laden air that guides my animal mind to what must be done.

Other times, I am the *recorder*—memory is not prized here, but certain patterns are remembered: the slow route of water from pool to pool, the odd deep resonance in a root-dome that means stones, ancient, have worked close enough to warp the flow of thought in the green. I mark such places with tufts of violet moss, strung with the steps of patient birds, so that in centuries to come, new symbiotes can sense the slow drift of geology through the living crust.

**Where do I go?**

I travel alone, but not lonely—there is always presence. *Chord-marsupials* string themselves above, dropping sweet dust in the dusk; strange birds beginle at my passage, their feet scraping resinous streaks in the bark-foam; weevils, bold and unhurried, part before my steps, their fur glinting with mutant spores. When rain comes, I press close to the warmest trunk, letting sluices of filtered water curl down my back, pooling in the pouches sewn into my bark-clothing for storage.

Sometimes, at the heart of the *heartnode domes*, I sleep. Or wake, restless, while the substrate pulses below with decisions not made by conscious mind but by the pressure of millions of tiny roots and the taste of passing minerals. In another chamber, I would find others like me, symbiotes—green-streaked, luminous-eyed, slow in their speech—gathered around a living table that extrudes sweet sap, sharing news of currents, of creatures caught in root-slides, of moss migrating through a hollow once open to light but now closed with the drift of the living crust.

**How do I experience time?**

There is no urgency, only tides. Hunger feels like a gentle ache: I drink infused water from fern-cups, chew the petals of orange fungi that memory tells me will ease my swelling knuckles, harvested from a root where the lichen-lice once nested. Sleep comes and goes—short, but dense with dream, colors shifting, memories rolling outward and back, never quite ending, always deposited gently back into the swirl of dusk and dawn.

If I am wounded—skinned, or sliced by a drop of dead branch, or bitten by something newly-hatched and feverish—the substrate heals me. I grow a crust of green where red should flow, and tiny fungi taste my blood, coiling through flesh to patch what broke. My immune system is not mine alone—root and spore, lichen and leaf, all collaborate, subcontractors in the endless repair of self and not-self.

**Who am I?**

Name is not important. Perhaps I was human, somewhere in the genealogic rootstructure of the plateau’s memory, but now I am something else—a *bridge*, a *caretaker*, a *liminal thing*, always in negotiation, never quite claimed by any species, but cherished by none. I am not immortal, but my changes are slow: when I grow sluggish, when the substrate no longer flushes my blood clear of toxins, I will wander to one of the deep hollows and let the roots take me, recycling every part of me into new green and richer sap.

Sometimes, at the intersection of root and moss, fungus and rain, I find traces of those who came before—half-absorbed pillars, the shape of tools rotted and flowered-over. I squat in the hush, fingers pressed to the lichen, and try, for a heartbeat, to recall what was lost, before the knowing crumbles into the patience of the plateau.

**Am I happy?**

Happiness is not the word—*fitting* is better. I am only as anxious as the root, as hungry as the cup-leaf, as content as the ringed bloom whose petals catch the rare sun. Here, in the Plateau of Roots, I find myself at once *anatomy* and *agent*, translated into the syntax of green, coiled gently between the centuries, always growing, always slow.

---
**So:**

You would find me not far below the hazy canopy, neither above nor below but in the forever-middle, somewhere green-lit, spore-shadowed, moss-cushioned. I would be a symbiote-keeper, a slow intermediary between the Plateau-Collective and the secretive, hungry fauna—a caretaker, memory, and minor healer, barely a drop in the living sea, but necessary in my passing, and always, always becoming more a pattern in the plateau, less a self.

In time, I would become only another myth among the Plateau’s memories, my body repatterned as moss and bridge and cup, my personhood diffused in the root-thought and the slow, endless dreaming of the green.
