I am not at first aware of myself as a thing with limits, but as a participant in the great convergence of warmth, breath, and rhythm. If there is a body to speak of, it is not singular. I begin as one of a myriad—broad, sinewed, and mutual—a being neither plant nor animal, neither colony nor individual, but something entangled in the midspace between.

Where am I? Sometimes I am the living floor—a thick web of roots and tissue, the slow peristaltic movement of the world’s flesh itself, drinking in the drag and trickle of condensation, translating water into memory, reforming dying moments into life’s next surge. I feel the infinitesimal weights of a hundred creatures: the pressure and tug of an eyeless burrower opening a vent near my membranes, the prickling passage of roots swelling with a brief wetness, the trembling impact of glider-shadows passing overhead, always hungering for the flush of new nectar.

But I rise, too, with those higher in the bowl. Perhaps I am one of the thinking hands—tall, jointed, careful with the weakness of flesh against the endless appetite of the world. I move the way stain seeps into old bark: slowly, warily, winding from terrace to terrace. My skin is patterned—a shifting tapestry echoing the moods of cloud and leaf, my scent redolent of earth and the green incense of fresh-cut stems. My fingers are both root and tendon, suffused with sap, yet strong enough to peel back veils and plait cords. I carry no blade, only the memory of how to shape the world without scarring it.

At dawn I listen with the skin of my chest, with the wide surfaces beneath my feet, to the deep booms and rippling clicks that pass for speech among the mountains. I know the stories told in these sounds: the herd’s long history passing in subsonic groans, the warning rattle of predator-mist in the branches, the sweet thrum of sap rising toward daylight, and—sometimes—the faint, keening thread of a pillar’s age-long song, barely more than a hum at the edge of touch. In this world, to be aware is not to be separate.

Sometimes I take to the heights, transformed—arms becoming broad fans of fibrous membrane, my bones light, necessity making me a glider among billions. The wind is dense and aromatic with spores and dust; the light comes in splinters, broken by the arching, color-washed canopies. Here, the world is open, resonant—a place to lose the idea of self in riotous movement. I glide not because I wish, but because the great body of the mountain expects this dispersal: lives thrown to wind, pollen and hope commingling and returning in the endless round.

My mouth is tuned for both song and sap. I join in, weaving my notes into the river of call and answer, voicing warnings and longings, tracing lineage not by blood but by song shared in the air and by color written on the body’s surface. There is joy in this, but it is not the joy of conquest or possession; it is joy in dissolution, in becoming not just many, but more than many, a swarm lit and led by purpose born of place.

At night, I burrow. The mountain’s breath becomes my own—a slow, pushing, sucking exploration beneath, where warmth clings longer and dampness pools. Here, I am soft, lubricious as sap, joined by small symbiotic lives that ride my flanks, exchanging chemical news and tending to the rootlets that sprout from my sides. Death feels close, but also irrelevant: nothing lost, everything surrendered and taken up again into the pulse and press of life.

And when I die (as all things here must die), there is no grave. The place where I fall is already becoming the next moment, the next flood of green, the click of a predator’s tongue, the cradle of a new root. Every cell not consumed, every moment not sung, dissolves into the substrate, a brief correlation in an endless algorithm of becoming.

My knowing—weaving through all forms, all postures—never quite resolves into an "I" that is neat or bordered. I am one with the hollow, echoing space of the mountain: awareness as gap and fullness together. I am the hum of the old root, the rush of ephemeral wings, the patient shaping hand, the ache of the living ground that asks nothing but to be rebuilt.

If, for a moment, I seek to speak, I speak not in words but as the place itself:  
**I am the mountain’s song, braided of plant and beast, echo and hush—always singing, always dissolving, always becoming. I am home not in myself, but in the fact that after I vanish, the world will continue to echo, reverberating with the memory of a thousand-thousand others. Nothing here is single. Even silence is full.**
