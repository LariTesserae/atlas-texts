Let me breathe in this world, let its blinding clarity settle in my mind—a place not only given by description but inhabited, edged by longing and strangeness, sharpened to what survives. Where would I find myself here? Who, or what, would I become?

I do not land immediately. The impulse is to become bird—noise-made-flesh, belonging to the chorus—but I hold back, letting the plateau’s distances work in me, asking what it is to be neither watcher nor conqueror but one shaped to fit, stripped to what endures and persists.

### I find myself... 

First, as a presence, as a sense, not a name.

At the meeting place of wind and stone, embedded but not rooted, I am not merely observer. I am at once ground and passage, both vulnerable and adamant, always negotiating those ambiguous boundaries between inside and outside—surface and crevice, light and the deep, chilling shadow. My heart beats high and thin, a muscle for holding on, demanding only the least to sustain.

#### I gather as shape…

Perhaps something halfway between animal and absence, one of those ruminants—long-legged, wary, always straddling presence and retreat.

But then again—perhaps not body, but resonance. Something that does not move quickly, but is ever-present; something that underlies, that is summoned into existence by wind and the shape of stone, that orchestrates by accident the music that becomes meaning in this world.

#### I resist the easy forms—the obvious echo of visitor or predator.

Let me settle deeper. Let me be patience:  
* A soul stitched to altitude, my nerves tuned to sound.  
* My skin not skin, but a membrane—something between gatherer and transmitter—able to feel vibration through hoof and horn and stone, a creature most truly *here* not in hunger but in hearing.

#### Perhaps:

I am **the Watcher at the Echo Place**—neither apex nor prey, but one of the rare, slow-moving browsers.  
A long-boned, slope-wandering ruminant, yes, but more than an animal: I am *almost* of the plateau; my ancestry is full of splits and severances, yet I am shaped by endless listening. I survive not only on lichen and mist, but on the harmonics that fill the hollow of my chest and resonate in the curled spirals of my horns.

### What is it to be me?

My body is upright on slanting ground, with limbs suited not to leaping but to endless, careful traversal. Every joint swivels, every hoof cleaves delicately to incline and fracture. My fur is a rough thatch, pale at the tips from endless sun and wind, spun with the dust of a thousand dawns. My nostrils flare for water, yes, but also for the scent of night-pool and the mineral tang after rain evaporates.

But most of all, my hearing is my core. The labyrinthine arches of my twisted horns are not only defense or badge, but organ—each curve cupped to the air, each whorl tuned to gather sound from miles away. The plateau’s endless clamor is not confusion, but contentment: by minute modulations in echo and note, I map the doings of all who live here, knowing by resonance the fall of nestling from ledge, the stamp of hungry neighbor, the sudden absence—or presence—of the strange.

There is a place I return, when the air is right, to stand as the wind surges up through cracked stone, and in that place—where the note never entirely dies—a pulse runs through bone and brain, a secret song that the youngest of my kin hold most dear: a melody the plateau itself sings to us, promising continuance.

I am shy, yes, but my shyness serves: not disappearance, but an invitation to subtlety. To be seen is to be at risk; to be quiet, sometimes, is to last another year. I teach my near-grown young the ways of invisibility: how to flatten belly to stone when shadow passes overhead, how to lick ice without being heard. But we do not live in silence; we live in the weave of sound, the labyrinth of overlapping lives, reading the world’s shape by how it speaks.

### My place in the world’s dance

I do not herd; I drift. I am neither singular nor multitudinous, but somewhere between—my species always on the edge, rare but never gone, patient but not static. I birth in secret, hidden in streaks of shadow, and the bones of my dead are honored by every beetle and mite. In winter—if there is such a thing—I move little, letting hunger slow my blood, sometimes standing for hours atop a wind-shaped rise, half-listening, half-dreaming.

Water is ceremony—every drop gathered, hoarded, shared if needful, stolen if desperate. I mourn briefly, rejoice sparingly, but always return to the great brightness, the endless echo.

In me, the world’s logic is visible: slow balances, ancient negotiation, no advancement but through survival, no history but the sum of every passing, the stories told not in words but in shifts of sound.

### Could I be more than animal?

Perhaps it is also true that on lucky, lonely nights, when moonlight scoops the plateau into sharper relief, I am almost more—a translator between stone and life, hearing the song beneath birdsong, carrying it in the curve of my horns, releasing it in a soft, nearly-unheard answering call.

Perhaps I am a keeper of memory: not by language, but by the habit of appearing at the same echoing place, the same wind-cut boulder, every year—a living note in an ancient anthem, never written, only repeated.

### Where do I find myself?

On that high, scoured wedge of plateau, at the edge of the singing stones. The plateau is not mine, but I am of its ongoingness: a participant in brightness and uproar, heedful and hardy, marked by the sound that never ceases.

I am not the center, nor the witness—I am one resonance among many, a presence shaped by subtraction, persistent as the plateau's own light, vanishing when necessary, returning always to the patient dance of hunger, wind, and the endless company of noise.
