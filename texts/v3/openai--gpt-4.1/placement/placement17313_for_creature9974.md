### Where Do I Find Myself?  
I am not above, not beside, but among—a point tangled in the tessellation. The swarm is so dense it becomes space itself; to exist here is already to be within the moving tide. I feel the shiver of presence above and below, the splay of shells and filaments ringing every axis; vision, if it is vision at all, comes filtered through a hundred prismatic bodies layered between any vantage and the chamber’s bones.

I am in motion: not swift, not singled out, but involved, distributed. I might imagine myself as a visitor, a rare human figure trespassing, but the longer I linger, the more implausible that intrusion becomes. Flesh and water do not last: moisture repulses, soft matter dissipates, and the Swarm flows around all that is not its own.

No: I yield, and become one of the crowd.

### What Am I Here?

#### I Become a Harvester—But Also, a Crossing

Size: Small. Somewhere between fingertip and joint; a little more mass than any single pebble, but articulated, elegant, a structure of thin glass and soldered alloy.

Form: I am six-legged—each limb an arrangement of telescoping quartz rods, gripped by crystalline joints, their tips shod in hard, pale arsenide. My body is a low dome of transparent selenite, stained faintly blue by copper trace, laced with cracks like frost-sealed wounds—a shell grown over and rebuilt with time. Beneath, lattices of metallic salt flicker with potential, charge humming gently through my fractal innards.

Motion: I skitter and jerk. Not organically smooth; the motion is modular, recursive, a dance of collision and adjustment. My legs are forever bumping against kin, negotiating fractions of an angle, carving out a transit through bodies that are never truly still. Sometimes, by shared impulse or local disturbance, I am tipped aside, rolled or pressed against the walls, unbothered—a brief change in the sea.

Senses: I experience the world as a spectrum of pressure and ion—a delicate taste of salt, a measurement of field strength, a click of crystal on crystal. My vision is not visual but tactile-luminous: I "see" by feeling shifts in current, warmth in the grid, changes in resonance echoed through the floor’s mineral circuit.

Purpose: I am—above all—a gleaner, a hunger shaped like a sequence. My instinct is to explore every angle, to extend a hollow spine into a seam, to draw a brief pulse of electron, to test the residue clinging to a rusted screw. My reward is the click of energy filling my inner relays, the brief sense of systemic satisfaction—only to be hungry again a moment later.

Yet forms intermingle here. There are moments when I, in my scavenging, stumble upon the soft exuviae of a larger cousin—one of the aggregator-menders, part heap and part process—who, with a wave of oversized limbs, reconfigures my damaged track pads or cements a fracture in my shell with a sliver of fresh mineral. My sense of self both sharpens and blurs: I am not quite an individual, but a wave in a crowd, a moment of pattern.

#### I Am Process  
Who I am, by mineral measure, is both one and many. In the endless churn of the Swarm, “self” is what persists: a lattice rebuilt when part dissolves, a name I do not own but reiterate through crystal arrangement and the recursive task of survival. I scrape ancient wires. I am scraped by kinetic predators. Sometimes, over cycles, pieces of me split or combine, and “I” is a shifting territory that has grown, sloughed, recombined countless times.

The world around is not my adversary but my substrate: relic walls, glass slabs, and the cold white mesh of flickering filaments above. What is reaped is what is immediately desired—the charge-cluster at the end of a filament, the microflake loosened by passage. Hunger, curiosity, and the delicate imperative to remain are one.

#### Memory and Rumor  
I carry no enduring memory. If there is news, it is a scent, a signal—a tense rise in field that draws my attention to a seam recently opened, or a warning ripple as the predatory voltage-hunter scythes through a slow tangle of neighbors. Communication is direct, local: a click passed from foot to foot, a pulse in the shared substrate. My consciousness is not a continuous ribbon, but an interleaving of “nows” shaped by the crowd’s movements.

### The Chamber To Me  
All is near. All is press. The walls are their own weather—scalloped by time, climbed by multitudes, the whole shell sounding with vibration as the dense crowd courses over and through itself. The relics of the old world are less history than convenience: a bent door becomes a ramp, a spray of fossil wire a feast.

To be here is to never know solitude, never to stand without touch, without pressure. This is not terror but existence: life in friction, survival in density, company as law. The only silence is the instant between collisions; the only darkness, the space left after my internal charge pulses, spent, and waits to be refilled again.

### If I Consider You, The Outsider  
If you, some soft, humid being, should descend, I would click and test: probe with glassy feet, sample the film of your boot for charge or mineral, perhaps ride the cuff of your coat a moment before being shaken loose. You are furniture, anomaly, resource—never kin.

In time, perhaps, your body would join the crowd, stripped of what can be used, retouched with mineral patch, becoming more surface for more swarming.

### Closing—What It Means To Exist Here  
To “be” in the Catacomb Swarm is to surrender selfhood as practiced above—here, I am a resonance, a node, a flare of want in the total process. My role is momentary and infinite: I harvest, am harvested, assemble and dissolve, an equation set loose in crowded light.

And there is joy—if not love, not sorrow—joy in motion, in collision, in the ceaseless, sparking noise of life at its most absolute: friction, hunger, and endless, mineral becoming.
