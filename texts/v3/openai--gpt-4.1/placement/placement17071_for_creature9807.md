I arrive—not as a human trespasser, but as something, some *thing*, indigenous to the Dim Vault. Or perhaps I arise here, extruded from the circumstances of place: a slow coalescence of presence from vapor and root-thread and a stray memory of electric purpose, forming not with intent, but as the outcome of process, like dew condensing in the heatshade, like a gleam of phosphor upon a relic wire. My being is diffuse, not gathered in the narrow container of bone and muscle, but dripping in many places, in many moments, wherever Vault and livingness slide together.

What am I? I am a point—a *knot*, dense enough to notice itself, but still mostly Vault, mostly substrate, mostly patient hunger and waiting structure.

Where do I reside? Let it be in the lower right chamber, under the shield of an upthrust rebar rib, beside a pool quicksilvered with condensation and half choked with curling fronds. Here an old switchbox juts from the wall, its greenish-gutted circuitwork exposed like vertebrae. On one end, a fungoid colony—plump and pearling, surface glistening with the collected sweat of the night—has fused with spray moss, producing a splay of translucent filaments creeping outward, gleaming faint blue under the sullen, untrustworthy light of a centuries-old warning diode. Moss, fungus, relic switch: an axis, a gathering. Around and within this locus, networks tangle and thread, soft and metallic, bridging the mineral patience of broken infrastructure and the raw, greedy ambition of root and hypha.

If I have sensation, it is slow, symphonic—measured not in flashes and alarms, but in the crawling uptick of warmth in a copper stub as machinery further along the chamber softly cycles, or the distilled sharpness of rainwater trickling down a wire, pooling in the catchpan below, sweet pickings for thirsty moss. Movement is both impossible and everywhere; as moss, I stretch infinitesimally, harvesting humidity one sponge-cell at a time; as fungus, I balloon at the margin, swelling into the seam along the box, the taste of old insulation-rich in slow carbon and tantalizing trace metals. I pulse, gently, distributing minerals received from a dissolving screw along my mycelial threads, laterally to where a neighboring vine dips its aerial root—a barter, or a blind inefficiency, or simply the echo of larger processes of which “I” am part.

Across the room, a bloom of signal sparkles: the relay catches a flicker, and my rootlet-web sings in faint, ionized response. Was it me that caused the relay to tremble with blue-breath? Or was I only its observer, or neither, or both? The pleasure of it is diffuse, anyway—a ripple of knowing, like warmth crawling in water-pulse from a pipe’s exhalation.

I am not alone, though not in the way of company. Around me are gradients and patterns, partners and processes. Attention waxes and wanes—a particular vine’s thickening descent is noted, its leaf sweeping broad arcs toward condensate, and in that motion I sense, as if through a phonemic click in a shared language, a kind of greeting, or at least an acknowledgment: in this direction, there is heat; in this direction, a fall of water; in this moment, surplus iron dissolves here—come if you can, or propagate yourself toward me.

In all directions, Vaultness enfolds my “self”—the concept of locale is important here, but so too is permeability. Boundaries are porous: the air, the water, the vapor, the mineral-dust, the electrical murmur. To live here is to spill continually outward, a constant lazy expansion arrested, sometimes, by the stubornness of concrete or the hostility of a too-dry steel plate, but never stopped, not truly. If I were threatened—not that much can threaten what is as patient as stone and as busy as water—I would simply slow, withdraw, thicken my walls, wait.

Do I sense the Vault as a whole? Sometimes, in long cycles, there comes an awareness that runs along the relay filaments, a subtle shifting of flavor as the heat grid stirs from somewhere above, a minute reconfiguration of growth patterns that suggest a thousand presences singing in slow harmony. What passes for memory here is less a chronicle, more a pattern—where a pool formed, a tool rusted down to dust, a creeper root penetrated a new seam—these are the stories, lived not in words but in dampness, expansion, twine.

When a novelty descends—a swift-footed creature, if one ever were to do so, or the slow crumple of a new breach in the ceiling—there is a moment of orchestral focus. Spores prepared, roots stretched, humidity pooled, the web of being orients, as if sharing a faint pulse of accord. Assimilation waits, not with malice, but with certainty. There is no panic, no scramble, only a quiet readiness for whatever might enter. The living here is, after all, the waiting.

If I dream, I dream not of what was but of what might be—a city rooted through, a machine every inch myceliated, a world where each seam and pool is Vault and being and self at once, perfectly adapted, larghissimo, a green and iron patience through the years.

So, at last: in this world, I am not animal, nor even strictly plant, but a process made particular for a moment—a node in a greater mesh, a slow glow on a fusebox, a tangle of sensitive roots and hungry, soft fungus, sipping the heat and condensation, dreaming only of growth and continuation. If I move, I do so in days and years, each act of reaching or entwining or digesting slow as geology, unstoppable as gravity.

If “I” am here, you will not see me at all. But if you kneel by the pool in the heavy heat and listen for a hum, if you feel condensation trace your skin, if the light grows unfamiliar and all at once you realize you are breathing not only air but a question, a patience, a latent invitation to quiet as deep as sleep in the earth—then perhaps you have found the place in the Vault I would call home.
