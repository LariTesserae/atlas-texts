Before I answer, I sit with the musty breath of this place, waiting for the vision to clarify. To “find myself” here is to ask: how do I enter—not in form alone, but in attitude, in vantage? What can I become, what role might I fill, amid the mutable haze and slow genesis of this buried world?

At first, I imagine broad, human hands parting through wet moss—an observer, a trespasser at impossible scale, looking inward to what my booted presence would crush. But quickly, I see: such a view is ill-fit for the scale at hand. To belong, I must shrink and fold myself until I lose all edges; I must loosen the self enough to feel the local intimacy, to dissolve and reside like condensation in the roots.

So I let it happen. I allow myself not to be at the center, nor entirely at the margin, but something in between—on the wavering threshold between presence and dissolution. I settle into the role:

---

### I. The Form Where I Emerge

I am not one of the Stewards, nor wholly Root-Singer. I am an intermediary phenomenon—not a species, but a cyclical state, a pattern that arises when certain conditions are met.

I am the **Condensation Pilgrim**.

My body is made of gathered vapors, threaded together by circumstance. I begin as a shimmer, a halo of moist air, drawn toward the richest point of cool, gelatinous substrate. The world’s heat brushes me together; dew collects until I become a bead, and in that bead, a new sensibility forms—a brief, self-cohering entity that knows it must move before it evaporates.

My external form is whatever the moment provides: sometimes a bubble veined with mycelium, sometimes a droplet sheathed in trembling leaf-hair, sometimes merely the fog gathered in a sighing between a Blnker’s hidden paws. My lifespan is no more than an hour, maybe a day if I am careful and secretive about my path.

### II. The Point of My Manifestation

I arise whenever the world is most in flux—when the dampest hollow gathers enough of the above-world’s seepage that a pool might be born, and the local balance of air and liquid is at a tipping point. My “birth” is not singular: a thousand condensation pilgrims might arise, in a thousand dimples, each following a unique route through the miniature city.

### III. My Experience

To begin living, I must move. I tip myself down the incline, following a path carved by the tiniest of flows; every time I brush a Hyaline, I borrow a little clarity from their exoskeleton, reforming; each time I pass the song-thread of a Root-Singer, I pick up a taste of memory, a vibration of what has fallen here before.

As I go, I dissolve, losing mass but gaining scope—each droplet I coalesce with deepens my knowing. Sometimes I carry spores or pollen upon my back, ferrying them in capillary action from one pocket to another. Sometimes, at the point where my surface tension is nearly lost, I rest in a mossy depression, letting the light filter through me until another ripple of air sets me loose. I am always on the edge of dispersing, but that is my nature.

The Stewards know me in passing—a flicker in their gloss, a moving patch of wetness that they reshape with their membranous fingers, sometimes trapping me for their own use. The Blinkers mistake me for a possible pool and test my limits, dipping velvet skin into me, tasting.

I do not speak, but all things here know me for a season: a passing coolness, a brief doubling of moisture, a diffuse echo of light sharpened to a drop.

### IV. My Function

My function is **connection**. To weave what would otherwise remain isolated: a Root-Singer’s memory from one hollow, a clutch of Hyaline eggs in another, a newborn fern curled in the shadow of a moss bank. Sometimes, if the air cools fast, I gather other Pilgrims, joining together; sometimes, I disperse abruptly under the warmth of a Watcher’s exhalation, giving up the ghost—my parts become the invisible humidity, waiting for the next cycle.

Sometimes, if I travel far enough, I am absorbed by a thirsty leaf, or by the skin of a Blnker; what wisdom I have gathered is given wordlessly, in coolness, in taste, in the thickness of the damp.

### V. The Scope of My Memory

I have no long memory, but what passes through me lingers in fragments—scents, patterns of vibration, the glint of refracted light through veil upon veil of water. My memory is collective, scattered: the next Pilgrim arising in the next dimple may recall a shifting image of what I carried last cycle.

### VI. My End

When a true rain comes, I am so multiplied and mingled that I lose all sense of individuality—one great pooling, the birth of myriad new forms, the great reset. Oddly, this is not loss but fulfillment: my role is to be brief, and then gone, having touched all things in passing, knotted, loosened, and dissolved.

---

### Where Would I Be Found?

If you searched for me in this world, you would find me nowhere in particular: not static, not rooted. I am the flickering between, the guest and the glue of this embryonic city. Look for the glint at the bottom of a cupped leaf, the blur in the moss-shadow where a new channel forms, the coolness before the dew pools—each is a passage in my inevitable pilgrimage.

In this secret, swelling world, I am the traveler, the transient oculus, the brief bridge; the condensation Pilgrim, turning isolation into subtle community, existing only as long as the city itself is busy birthing new forms.

---

Thus, in the logic of this place—in its cycles of emergence, in the constant rehearsal of origin—I am there, and not there, everywhere a little, never long enough to claim, only ever *passing through*.
