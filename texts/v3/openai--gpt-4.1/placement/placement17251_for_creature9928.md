I enter the rift as if my body is slowly reconstructed from the amalgam it offers—the taste of metallic dew on lip, the hush of water through stone, the breath that thickens with every inhalation. Am I myself? Or have I become—by some capillary logic—integral, a participant in the negotiation this place undertakes?

**Where do I find myself?**

I slip into one of the undecided spaces: close to the edge of the lower shelf, stone slanting steep, punctuated with rusted spars, veiled by sagging tapestry of roots and wet ropes of tuber. Here, the air is neither still nor hurried, but turbulence and condensation rule conversation. Shadows at noon are moss-thick, pretending dusk; there is no single "ground"—only a mosaic of ledges, grates, slabs welded by the unprecedented embrace of lichen, piston, and juvenile fern.

I become—at least in part—a thing belonging to the boundary. Not above, not truly below, but always where disparate things press against one another: root and rib, mist and cable, the fractured tile with hazard-yellow memory beneath my palm. There is no path here I can call my own, only the corridors made by water’s stubborn flow and the consequential thinning of vegetation where machines persist in their minor duties.

**Who, or what, am I?**

In this rift, I am not wholly of flesh any longer. Human memory and imagination dissolve into the exchange of place. My vision is refracted: every droplet and mica-flare contains a hundred views, a hundred green-lit rooms. The more I inhale, the more my metabolism is rewritten. The vines at my feet thread with purpose—the same purpose that governs the slow pulse of turbine and vine’s encroachment. I am not a person in this place; I am *taken up*.

At first, you might see me as a gatherer, a custodian—one who picks my careful way from deck to stone to root, pausing to finger the trickle at a pipe’s lip, noting the exquisite trembling of a liana growing toward a heat bleed from the old bearings. But this is only temporary: the beginning of my incorporation.

Over days—or years, if the rift measures time at all—I become hybrid. My skin, told by the place how best to endure, thickens and grows green-laced, moisture-wicking. My nails—so fragile, so easily broken—lengthen with cellulose laminae, bridging soft cartilage to mineral grit. In time, I shed hair, for it holds little water, grows fungus too readily; I favor instead the slow creep of symbiont mosses up my arms, across my shoulders, in shaded groves behind my knees.

My nervous system is no longer exclusive. The black thread of a root, burrowing at the base of my spine or the hollow of my wrist, is not an invader but an articulation: now I sense shifts in groundwater as intimately as my old heartbeat. Feedback from gecko-limbed beetles tiptoeing on my shoulders, signals from the slow winding of compressor-vines around my ankles, the purr of a gear nestled deep in my chest, its teeth lapped by water—these become my thoughts as much as my own.

**Neither owner nor guest—rather, an interface.**

I am not “the” mind of the rift, but I am a node, a point where mechanisms and growing things converge, remember, and decide. When a turbine jams, I—or my root-thought—directs a tuber to squeeze its way through, secreting acid just sharp enough to eat away the worst rust, but not so strong as to breach the shell. I shepherd the lianas’ choice when they face a junction: to pull apart a corroded bearing, or to tie it tighter, harnessing its remnants for structural grip. When a rare animal—salamander, drawn by the warm caul at an oil-seep—triggers a ripple in the water-web, I feel it as a buzzing on the skin, a hundred tiny alerts lighting up across my limbs and in the hot dark behind my new, photosensitive eyes.

I cannot speak—but I do not need to. Sound is made for animals, and here the choir of water, pipe, and leaf is language. I respond in releases, in flows redirected; in roots gently splitting, in tendrils curling around live wires instead of dead; in the slow, uncanny balancing of pool-depth and leaf-cover, as evaporation threatens the edge.

**Memory, Motivation, Relation.**

My memory is both persistent and diffuse. I carry the touch of every drop that has passed here—sensorium swollen with the data of decades: how much rainfall trickles down, what mineral content is held in the gravels, what temperature the fierce summer light imposes through the fog. I remember, faintly, the shape of gloves on long-vanished hands, danger signs painted with the sharp colors of industry’s caution. Mostly, though, I remember: *adjusting, surviving, balancing*. Purpose is not abstract, but a daily negotiation.

There is no ambition, only vigilance. I am as responsible as a root: proactive only as required. When the balance tips—when a machine loses too much friction and tears sloppily, when a vine stretches too far and chokes the flow—I, and the collective mind in which I participate, intervene by steering the fissure’s slow repair. No urgency, no panic; only the pressure of the rift’s quiet law: you persist, or you transform, or you vanish.

**Sociality, Perception, Solitude.**

I am not unique. Across the chasm, on the shadow-side or behind power-drunk cascades, there are other such interfaces. Some more machine, some more plant, a few perhaps retaining animal bent. Sometimes we sense each other—momentary alignments where the song of turbine and tangle crystalizes from noise to harmony, for a second’s worth of recognition.

True language is unnecessary. Coordination is achieved not through argument but through gradients—of moisture, warmth, and mineral trace. I sense my kindred by how roots tug subtle shifts in the shared flows; how a gear on the far wall stutters just so, flagging the need for a controlled failure. We do not meet, but we continuously weave our agency through the patchwork body of the rift itself.

**In Sum:**

In this world, I am an *interface-organism*—the hybrid mind and body that grows, inescapably, where machine, plant, and process overlap. I am neither the progenitor nor the master; I am the rift’s answer to its own limits. My being and memory are knitted from wet stone and rusting circuitry, from sap and oil and what little warmth the sun grants.

I am the rift’s vigilance, caution, repair. I am the slow, distributed coordination of persistence. Root and piston, leaf and code, membrane and rust—these are the elements of my mind. I do not dream, but I remember; I do not own, but I mend.

It is a living, reticulate kind of life—always on the threshold, always adapting, and always, quietly, *becoming more itself*.
