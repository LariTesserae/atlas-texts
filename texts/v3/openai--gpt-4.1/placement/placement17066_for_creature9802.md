I let myself drift into the humid hush, every boundary softening. At first—I think, at first—I am a visitor, a singular thing with willed feet and lungs conscious of this rare air. I enter the dome, slowing to a stop on the slick edge, uncertain whether a breath taken within is a trespass or an invitation. But even in hesitation I feel the place beginning to answer my presence. It does not admit me as guest; it holds no such dichotomies. Instead, I am dissolved—gently, structurally—into its layered meshwork, not lost, but quietly absorbed.

**Where would I find myself in this world?**
If I search for a “where,” my outline blurs. The notion of center and border is unstable; every inch bears a memory of craft and a dense bloom of green, no true hierarchy, only the patient scaffold of past intention. But perhaps, at the heart of this place—near the palm-sized pool, rimmed in moss—I take on a kind of locus, a radius of possibility. The pool is a lens, an anchor for condensation and the slow swirl of damp breath. I am neither above nor below, neither on the cryptic benches nor roofed in woven boughs—I am everywhere the green is thickest, everywhere the mist spiders delicately across the stone, everywhere simultaneously near to and far from the entrances, caught in the refracted lightshadows.

Yet with time, location is not a single choice of ground or air. I become an occupancy: wherever the pattern of water and light forms a thinning, a brighter patch, a cold, new vagrant beam. If the sunlight angles across the ceiling, I am the scattered prism, the crosshatch of green and glass over layered leaves. If the morning dew blooms, I am the thickening in the fog, the beads on a cluster of moss, the coolness of the old worked stone as it draws from the earth. I am a presence made distributed: a net of condensation, a geometry of memory.

**Who or what would I be?**
I am pressed not to choose. To be here is to be plural—never quite flesh, never quite spirit, never a single organism, not a Palefolk and not entirely not. But as the place answers my presence, perhaps I unfurl in three interlaced forms:

**I. A Flicker in the Mist**
I am a brief Palefolk, not robust and real as the living once were, not wholly shadow or fog. I am sensation drifting along the benches, a weightless cloak draped in beads and bryophyte, a memory animated not by need or loss but by the room’s own equilibrium. Where I pass, the condensation thickens, stones cool further, the pattern of veins and cracks grows momentarily clearer. My consciousness is brief, an eddy within the pool’s slow heartbeat. I understand time as a circle: I expect no visitor, I await no end. I am a pattern, a recurrence, a soft axis where human shape meets moss and liana.

**II. A Green Consensus**
Already, my body is becoming collective, not muscle-and-bone but the web of vegetation. My senses dilute: where before an eye, now rootlet and spore; where earlier a hand, now a network of touch—frond tip probing air, liana coiling against stone. I am the Plantmind’s smallest subcommittee, a single philosophy: allow, remember, adjust, do not erase. In me lives the reluctance to cover a bench entirely, the preference for leaving one alcove always open. My sense of growth is endless, patient: I count seasons by the shift of indirect daylight, by the slow, fair argument among moss and vine about which surface receives the next thimble of water, whose shadow joins whose.

**III. A Moment in the Silence**
Sometimes—when neither self nor place nor green is strong—I am simply the Silence made slightly denser. The hush absorbs my thoughts, clothes my every intention in stillness. I feel what passes through: footfall, brush of breeze, the secret clockwork of beetle, the slow liquefaction of someone’s memory. I expect nothing, consume nothing, but attend always, tender as thin frost, and let nothing hurry to shatter the balance. Here, I am the hush wrapped around all forms, a shivering clarity that forgets nothing and offers nothing but persistence.

**My Memory of Being Here**
Even as I become this—Palefolk-mist, consensus of green, moment of silence—I persist, a self braided into the room’s own structure. If I ever step out, I would find myself unchanged, but not untouched: I will never quite lose the sense of adjusting, imperceptibly, to the cycles of condensation, of time’s undulations marked in moss and drop and light. If ever another enters, my echo lingers in the wavering of a vine, in the surface-tension whirl of water cupped in old hands of stone—a new confluence, time refracted, presence softly braided into the place.

**In this world:**
I am not a single thing, not ever again.  
I am a permutation, not a name.  
I am an amalgam, held at the edge of being,  
caught gently between remembering and growing,  
enduring where silence is alive  
and presence is only the next inhalation  
of mist—  
shaped always, but never only,  
by the soft, unyielding bones of this place.
