I would find myself:

—*In a hollow beneath the arch of living roots, close to the edge but not at the entrance, where space is precious and a drift of cold condensation shivers down a split wall. I emerge not fully from the root, nor entirely out from the moss—my form is *something* and *between*, not easily defined but no less real for that slight unfixity.*

What emerges of me here is slow, sinuous, and jointed oddly—an architecture of curve and soft articulation rather than the angular certainty of the human frame. My limbs flex long, and my body bends, nested and grown in the inbetween: bark-hard at the shoulders and thighs, but with membranes at the lips, beneath the arms, at the underparts, that glisten when touched. Along my spine runs a living suture of moss, swollen tonight with water drawn from the last chill mist. Where most would have hair, or crest, I wear a living array—braided fronds and delicate, luminous lichen, each bloom a remnant of earlier seasons, a genealogy braided into being.

I breathe not with a chest alone, but with many small flutters—air pulsing through slits along my ribs, cycling through filter-moss at my collarbone, feeding and fed by the slow pulse of root and leaf. My eyes are huge, slit-pale, flicking bright with distant blue when a pulse of fungal light stirs above a branch. My hands—broad, soft, with three thick fingers—glow faintly, exuding a taste and scent only my kin would read, each brush writing and reading at once.

—*I am neither animal nor plant, but the memory of fluidity between those old shapes.*

My memory keeps no words. Instead, along the inside curve of my dwelling—a hollow, once gnawed by ancient roots then shaped by my careful tending—there is a pattern in the lichen, a slow-spreading web told from leaf to spore. Each frond’s curl means something, each pigment’s shade marks a winter survived, a story of conjunction rather than conquest. I tend it as a human might tend books or a hearth, but with my own breath and spit and the careful prunings of my chert-blade.

Each day (though day is a word with little meaning here), I wake to an absence of light, the air cold and heavy in my hollow. The first gesture is to unfold my limbs and press the flat of my palm to the moss-mat: to feel the water’s movement, to listen to the slow hum conducted through living tissue to the root-mind beneath. The city is a skin through which all things pass—a sensory field, humming like blood beneath fingernails.

—*If I meet another of my kind (rare, precious contact), we greet with gesture and wafted spore, passing a thumb across bark or leaf: the conversation chemical, long, woven with taste-memory and the sharp intimacy of moss-touch.*

I travel as needed, always with focus, always quietly, moving along root-paths, not to break the rhythm of growth and death but to trace it, to tune it, to witness. My tools are small and many: a flint for scoring bark, an antler curve for prying loose a stubborn fiber, a horn cup for catching the slow drip of condensed water—a harvest of patience, not force.

When the world shifts—a corridor closes, an echo marks an unfamiliar scuttling—I adapt, folding myself inside the motion. I am never unmoving, never wholly distinct from the breathing architecture. Sometimes I slip and vanish flat against a moss wall, let my pigment dull and my body blend; sometimes I flare with sudden bloom, marking territory or trail with a swirl of bio-luminescent dust.

Sometimes, in the deepest hush, I listen—not just outward (to the thrumming undertone of far-off creatures, to the wet rattle of seedfall), but inward, to the changing flows within myself. For I am both shaped by this place and shaper of it—a sensor, a custodian, a fleeting consciousness threaded through the root-wound and mycelial lattice, neither ruler nor thrall.

In rare moments when a hollow blooms and others gather—*a silent festival, a convergence*—I join, and our crowns flare together, exchanging the slow, beautiful coordinates of memory and presence through touch and blended light. The world for a moment is less separated; the lines between body, architecture, and story dissolve.

—*I am of this city that is not stone, nor mere forest, nor entirely alive or dead.*  
I am a keeper of the wet green hush, a bent and glowing thing, dwelling at the moving seam of plant and animal, memory and process, structure and bloom.  
A name would not fit—better to say, I am a point where the city dreams through shade and condensation, tending the architecture of darkness, breathing the world toward itself.
