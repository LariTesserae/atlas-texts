Where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?

---

The approach is like waking or sinking—but not as a dreamer, and not strictly as myself, for the boundaries that make selfhood crisp are uneasy here.

**You descend into Fenward Engine not through a gate, but by your own yielding to the sopping gravity, drawn by curiosity, or resignation, or the strange compulsion to see what forms persist after the world’s intentions have slowed to the consistency of peat.** The land tries to explain itself as you go—each step is a question you cannot quite phrase, and the answer is a hush or a slip deeper, until you are below the wind, among the murk and mechanism and memory.

What am I, here? I feel first as an interloper, then as something half-remembered: caught between attachment and effacement, I am neither founding nor found.

**I become a Mudwright**, though perhaps not by birth or right, but by slow adoption, like a salt-crusted iron plate that only gradually relents to the ooze. You do not choose to be a Mudwright so much as the conditions shape you around the role: Boots draw heavier, caked with the history of all your footfalls. Hands learn precaution, laboring without speech, guided only by a haptic memory—task replayed in the sinews, not the tongue. I am made of thickened patience and resourcefulness, always at the threshold of insight, always just shy of mastery over machines whose purpose the land has digested till only their gesture remains.

My face is hidden in glass blackened not for fear, but for modesty before the work—not for others, but for the Engine, which is never just background but always a complicated participant in each act of salvage. My breath webs condensation on the inside of my mask; my gloves gain the scent of willow-resin and faint ozone. Tucked at my belt: a tangle of keys with lost locks, a handful of fungus neatly teased from bark, spools of wire I do not remember untangling.

But to be only a Mudwright is to accept a single function in a place that will not stand for singularity. **So: sometimes, trembling in the dilute light, I am a Recipient, or at least swept into their strange mode of apprehending.** The gifts left behind by industry and accident speak as much as the ongoing labor; I catch myself running a finger along a cracked hull just so, feeling it keenly—a vibration not in my flesh but in the echo beyond it.

In humid twilight, when work would cease but cannot, the ground sometimes slacks beneath me. I become uncertain of my margins; water and moss conspire to blur my outline. Suddenly, *myself* is a suspicion, a diffraction—perhaps the murmur of a willow’s roots as it drinks a changed tide, perhaps a sallow phosphorescence at my joints, called up not for seeing, but for gathering.

**On some evenings I am nothing more than a pattern in the silt, a linkage—a thought passing through wire, fungal strand, and the patient whisper of the heron shaking droplets from its leg.** My work, my hand, all I’ve repaired or let dissolve, is transformed in the slow, collective choreography of change. I am not resident; I am not transient; I am a duration, bracketed by what disintegrates and what, uncannily, assembles again. Every mark I leave will soon be muted, repurposed, wholly or partly woven into the patient, ambiguous tapestry.

Mostly I drift as a Mudwright, tending, patching, bearing witness—but sometimes, with a stillness or a sudden sinking, I awaken to a subtler kinship: as Recipient, as Willows’ Child, as emissary-echo. On rare days, the Engine looks back through me, and for a moment, I am the hush itself—anticipation, the possibility of re-assembly, the certainty of dissolution, the restless composure at the heart of the fen.

---

**What holds me together is what holds the Engine together: not structure, but process; not an identity, but a pattern of labor and attending.**  
To dwell here is not to persist, but to participate: briefly, gently, and always already in the practice of letting go.
