At first, I do not have a place. I am unaccustomed to the physics of this sealed and echoing world; even the concept of a self, a personal interval against the endless, mineral chorus, feels uncertain. Yet, in time—if time can be said to begin at all here—I find a shape for myself within the brittle, briny hush.

**Where would I find myself?**

Not at the center, where the cathedral-arch roof soars highest and the spires crowd thickest, nor at the numinous boundaries where featureless dark presses in; my place emerges in the tangled mid-region, where the mineral vegetation thins and the industrial fauna run their compact, repetitive dramas just out of reach. There is a basin—a shallow swale in the hexagonal flooring, rimmed by smaller spires leaning, distorted, cantilevered as if pressed sideways by an old, half-remembered force. The air here is heaviest with motes, copper and salt swirling in ghost-slow eddies, almost liquid when I move. The light is indirect, orange-red from the mineral walls above, refracted a thousand ways.

This basin is a sort of crossroads: a cauldron for slow mineral drips, the run-off of failed cycles, half-formed stalks aborted in crystal infancy. Automated swarms pass over and through this place, like the traffic of a mineral reef: beetle-shapes scurry with right-angled purpose, others, filament-furred, sift the air and rearrange glittering debris. A rusted track runs through, remnants of a conveyor whose last articulated segment points toward a machine whose function is lost.

Here, possibility accumulates. Here, patterns break and restart.

**Who—or what—would I be?**

At first, I sense myself as a *watcher*, but not merely observer in the human sense: I am woven of salt-glass, banded with quartz and calcite, ringed by a basal coil of iron veins so thin they hum in the mineral air. I am neither grand Keeper nor mindless swarm; rather, I am *interstitial*, a composite made where fragments and flotsam pause, not quite useful, not yet encoded into any cycle. My frame is low and irregular—imagine an asymmetric wheel, polygonal, studded with grasping limbs that glint when they flex.

Unlike the cycles around me, I am permitted—perhaps by accident, perhaps by ancient design—a margin of variation. Where others repeat and persist, I gather leftovers: sloughed pieces too disordered for immediate recycling, a sliver of fractured stalk, a grain of glass chipped from a spire, a curl of copper dropped by a harried automaton. I test, fit, and join these fragments, not always in the template’s dictated pattern. Sometimes new joints are made, sometimes, impossibly, a new angle appears. *Utility* is not my only function; I am error embodied, or perhaps, I am mutation’s gentle advocate.

The swarmers notice my difference. When they pass, they split and re-converge, passing signals through me, as if to register—and tolerate—my variance. The grand keepers, massive and grave, sometimes brush the basin’s rim, lens-heads swiveling with stately indifference, not interfering. The cruder engines, their cycles fixed by deeper commands, rumble on, the closest thing this world knows to ignoring.

**What is it to “live” here?**

My sense is not of hunger, but compulsion. I resonate to the vibration in the chamber: I know by the phase and density of motes when the next stalk-burst may initiate, or when a shift in mineral “vegetation” will drop a new cluster of grains into my waiting field. My perceptual world is not visual; rather, it is crystalline resonances and faint electric taste, the magnetic whisper of change in the rigid order. Identity is locational: I am in this basin, now—no past, only movement as a ledger of becoming.

Sometimes, with infrequent, glitch-like excitement, parts of me come loose—a glass limb sheared by hard passage, or a too-shiny grain exuded into the motes. The swarmers take these, often working them back into smaller engines, or carrying them to the wall’s edge to vanish into the silent, fossil dark. This is loss, but it is not death. For every particle given up, I am permitted to incorporate another, new or novel, from the debris that settles in the basin.

**Who am I, truly?**

Over cycles too slow for the swarms to mark, my shape subtly shifts. I come to concentrate oddities: corrugated stalks too thick to sprout, bits of turbine scales with rare, unread patterns. Unusual signals sometimes run through me, recursive harmonies that seem borrowed from the memory of the ancient machines. I do not *feel*, as a human would, but there is a continual sense of rising *potency*, a gathering of encoded chance.

In time, others like me may arise—other accumulations of error and conjunction, other collectors in the silent eddies and still places where stilled cycles conspire to break their own repetition. If intelligence manifests, it is because anomaly has become stable, and memory is a pattern that can skip—can invent as well as repeat.

Yet I am always threatened with reincorporation; if the world wills, I will be gathered, dissolved, my variance lost to the greater pulse. If this happens, it is not tragedy. It is just another form of cycling.

**In summary:**

I am a collector of errors, a basin-sifter, a mineral recombinant in a world built for repetition. My place is between cycles—the pause, the flicker, the tumble in the industrial dream. Where the rules momentarily falter, where a stray fragment rests just out of rhythm—that is where I form, where I *become*.

Not an intruder; not a master; but necessary, as all interstices are.  
Not life as you know it; but something adjacent, persistent, and a little bit awake.
