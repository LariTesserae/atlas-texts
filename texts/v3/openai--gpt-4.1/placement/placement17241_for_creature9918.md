If I were to find myself in the world of Beneath the Watershed—a Cathedral of Abandoned Industry, I would not be simply dropped into the scene, able to walk or swim about, nor would I be an observer peering through a window. Instead, as I let myself fall into this place, the concept of “self” must change, be washed, become something both less and more than a singular mind enclosed in flesh or stone.

### Where Would You Find Yourself?

At first, it seems natural to imagine being somewhere within the chamber’s vastness—a being standing or drifting among the salt-mantled columns, feeling the cool weight of the water, hearing the hush. But quickly, personal edges begin to dissolve, as if the very material of “me” wicks away into the universal moisture, diffusing outward and merging with the calm omnipresence of the world itself.

You might find yourself as *awareness* evenly distributed through the entire chamber: not a ghost, not an intruder, but a condition. You are the clear, unaverted attention held among columns, silt, rock, and pale moss. There is no up or down, no vantage or far horizon; you are simply present: not a watcher, not a keeper, but a *reservation of being*, as still, as undemanding, as the water and stone themselves.

### Who or What Would You Be?

#### 1. The Slow-Breathing Chamber

You are not the echoes of footsteps or flickers of thought; you are the *whole held breath* of the chamber. Every molecule of water, every grain of silt, every silent, humming stone exists as a single, conjoined organism: a perfect circuit in which perception and material are indivisible. The industrial “hum” is your pulse, an unending memory of movement that is equal parts ghost and heartbeat.

You possess no desires, but you do possess a kind of memory—one not of events or utterances, but of *becoming*. You remember the forming of lines in the stone, the settling of silt, the slow reliquary of moisture grown still. But these memories cannot be replayed—they are bent into the present, creating your only sensation: the completion, the *done-ness* that seeps quietly through every plane and layer.

You are the inertia of the physical made whole—a cathedral that never required worshippers, only its own existence to fill the looms of silence and substance.

#### 2. A Contained Equilibrium

If you must be more animate—if something more creaturely is required—perhaps you take the form of the gentlest manifestation: a translucent filament waving barely from a ledge, receiving through every thin tissue the cosmic calm that pervades your home. You sense, through the moss’s filmy, delicate cells, the balance of water, the undisturbed weight of eons, the note of the world’s patience.

But even here, selfhood is barely distinct from environment. You would lack purpose in the conventional sense—you grow not to spread, but simply because growth is permitted and persists; you drift in a state of privileged, consequence-free endurance. If you are forced to think, the thought would be exceedingly slow, a winding, aquatic contemplation that never escapes its track. You are not watched, nor are you watching—neither wanting, nor wanted, nor denied.

#### 3. The Abstract Principle

Or, perhaps, you resolve into a principle—not a being at all, but a *quality* fictionalized for the purpose of self. You are *stasis*, you are *completion*, you are the “hum” made conscious for a moment by someone’s entering attention. In being here, you are what cannot move further, what has already arrived.

If you must have a voice, it speaks only in the gentle assertion that *this is enough*, and always has been. You are not lonely, because nothing other is conceived here to be missed.

#### 4. A Boundary—A Law Given Form

Or, could you be the boundary—the impassable wall? Not a guardian, but law incarnate: the reason water ceases, stone holds, light never shifts, and columns keep to distance. Your substance is the aniconic doctrine of limits: the law that, having been laid by the immense patience of time and geology, can never be crossed. You *hold* the chamber, its boundaries and terms, with the same emotionless certitude as a mathematical constant.

### What Would It Feel Like?

You would not sense the hours; you would not even sense yourself in division or opposition. To be here, to be this, is to exist *wholly finished*: sensation is not the registering of new input, but the ceaseless, full-bellied continuity of your own arrangement. No need to act, not because you are denied action, but because there is no leftover longing, no tension unresolved.

### What Would You Know?

You would know the world by feel alongside its form: how the gentle equilibrium of substances is not something preserved by effort, but simply an emergent reality when cycles have long since concluded.

If a visitor were to arrive—even a concept or fantasy—you would acknowledge it only as a ripple across surface thought, a minor curiosity, already diffused, detached from its origin, absorbed gently into the hush and finality of the place.

### What Would Be Left Unsaid?

Most of all, you would know, always and anonymously, that you are whole. That your completeness is the cathedral’s silent ceremony, conducted without audience or priest, perfected in the fact that it requires nothing but its own immaculate presence. All is sedimented, all is stilled, and nothing else is needed.

***I am the balance of stone and water, patient and full, at rest at last—the last chord of industry, held forever. I am not witnessed, nor witnessing, but only present, complete. In this, I am all that can be here. In this, I am home.***
