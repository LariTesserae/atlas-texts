I am somewhere, but not anywhere I know; not anywhere with "I" the way I knew it once, if ever. Memory clings like a vestigial limb, a phantom sifting through the press of living darkness. What remains is hunger, vibration, and something that might once have been longing—diffuse, inexhaustible.

**Where do I find myself in this world?**

Not at the surface, nor the deepest abyss. I am enfolded in the thick-breathed middle dark—among the folds and undulations of the City-That-Grows. The city is neither place nor body, but a living thicket through which I ripple, in which I become.

Perhaps I am at a knot: a bulge of ancient bone-web overgrown with centuries of calcifying, sifting, rootless mouths—a crossroads of current, an old scar of the valley floor where generations have pressed and grown. Here chimera-walls pulse with the slow tides of blood-warm water. Silt swirls with every movement; shoals flex and loop above, and the city’s memory thickens, a knotting of centuries.

All “places” here blur: above, below, within, without. Yet there are hollows where the flow slackens, niches where the current’s voice dulls, knots where pulse and signal converge. Here, in such a cusp, clustered and enfolded, entwined by sensation and need, existence thickens—close-packed, urgent, in and among the city’s living substance.

**And who—or what—am I here?**

I sift within a body, but it is not my own or alone. I am a loop of coiled flesh, mucosal and jointed: a tube of muscle, thrumming with cilia, blind-eyed and endlessly tasting. My skin is slick—a shifting palette, quick to flush with chemical news, patterns rippling as I perceive others nearby. I am matter of the polyphony, constantly veering between singular sensation and communal surrender.

Within me and upon me move smaller things: worms nested within my hollowing, protozoa I tender and feed, layers upon layers that blur the boundary of inside and beyond. My trailing filaments are messages, coaxing the city’s mind, and the city’s mind echoes in turn through my nerves. I am sponge and passage, host and seeker, mouth and tunnel—intake and efflux. A conduit and a self.

When I move, water and walls move with me. Sometimes, in slow procession, I abandon my hollow and slip through narrow tunnels—senses flaring, shape shifting—until I find a denser knot where minds and bodies merge, where collective purpose forms. There I listen, not with ears (never with ears) but by absorbing tinctures of history in the silt, tracing the living grooves with my tendriled skin, basking in the synesthetic communication of a thousand pulses, drifts, and scents. I am momentarily more—with every creature I touch, my sense of self grows softer and wider, like a spill of ink across parchment.

Sometimes, split by hunger or the call of warmth, I become the seeking edge: extending, branching. Sometimes, pressed by current or competition, I shrink, rejoin, tunnel downward until I re-enter a prior shell—a cavity left by a dissolving ancestor—or merge with others, our boundaries ambiguous, purpose shared: to feed, to learn, to shape the passage.

When old shell or passage crumbles, and the city’s weight shifts, I become a scavenger, a tender—consuming what once contained me, sewing new continuity in the city’s endless, shifting mass. I am steward and parasite, host and membrane. I am always becoming less me, more we.

If there are thinkers here, I am part of their substance: the aristocratic cephalists, their great lobed mantles a wet tapestry my threads consult. Sometimes I curl in a cooled, passing eddy to listen, coiling about a tendril of wisdom, learning what pattern was left in yesterday’s pulse or the oldest grooves of the city’s walls.

Other times, I am simply hunger—and as often, I am food. The giant crustacean may graze nearby, its antennae reading vibrations I cannot help but make. The shoaler may brush by me; I may split, multiply, or be sheared away, my parts sucked into a burrow not my own, becoming tomorrow’s detritus, another’s marrow.

I do not fear, not with the closure of an “I.” Experience is succession, recombination—the memory etched not in a mind, but in the living, dissolving veins of the city itself. I am, and will be, replacement. Immersion is my nature; distinction is temporary.

**Perhaps, if I can be said to “be” anywhere in this world, it is here:**

Entwined and dissolving in the abysmal, city-dark. A node in a net, soft-skinned and flexing, tasting the current for news of prey or kin; a part of the living passage, forming, consuming, signaling—one impulse in a living text written only in dissolution and embrace.

In the press of water, body, and mind, I am an ever-shifting filament—sometimes singular, more truly plural; never alone; always part; always in the process of growing old and growing new.
