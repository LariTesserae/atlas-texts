If I were to find myself in this prodigious world—this light-heavy, trembling continent of plant and frozen brightness—what would I be? Who might I become, drifting in the luminous and root-strung vastness beneath the translucent vaults? Let me look, let me slow my imagination, let me let the place form me, rather than wedging it into human shape.

### First Arrival: Sensation and Diminishment

At first, awe and insignificance. The light dizzies me; it is prismatic, cutting, so coldly pure the air feels edged, a tangible brightness against skin and eyes. My senses flare—sight blooming painfully, touch sharpened by cold, ears opening in the endless stretch of echoes, never quite silent. I touch the ground (is it ground?)—a braided sheet of living wood, trembling ever so slightly, and I sense beneath my weight a network of reactions: the slow, ambiguous curiosity of the substrate.

I am not at home—yet.

### The Unique Invitation of This Place

To remain myself—a human here—would be impossible in more than body. My consciousness would soon be scraped thin by the immensity, the indifferent attention of the plant-intelligence, the icy emptiness pressing between presences. The world is too alert, too keenly alive, for isolation, and too fond of forgetting—its intelligence braided between new trunks and vaults, memory shuttled by sap and strand.

### Transformation by the Place

#### The First Change: Gravity and Need

I discover that gravity here is mutable; if I dwell too long in one spot, tendrils curl anxiously, and whole pocket-bridges loosen. My body becomes lighter, steps elongated—sometimes pushed, sometimes gently drawn. My flesh itches, then numbs, as spores settle on skin, and cold light stings through every hair and pore. The air is so rich in oxygen, in drifting scent, that I feel the breath inside me grow sharper, tinged with phytochemical traces.

#### The Second Change: The Seed-Body

Eventually, as my presence persists, a choice—or a compulsion. My outline blurs; my skin prickles, then thickens in places. Fingernails darken, keratin gone fibrous, half-bark; hair becomes pale, then shimmers green, catching and refracting the spare, icy light. I become what this place knows best: somewhere between root and animal, a *seed-guest*. Within my chest, something alien stirs and splits, sprouting along the sternum in a slow, cold-burning ache.

I am now an *Interloper Germ*: neither true animal nor true plant, but a seasonal being. My physiology is rebuilt—my mind floods with ephemeral signals: surges of growth, the language of voltage and hormone, sapsong and the whispering of chemical words along trembling, ever-renewing lattices.

I am not alone. All interloper-things here share this oscillating existence, chimeras whose selfhood lies in flux between animal motion and plant duration—thoughts half-rooted, half-leaping.

#### The Third Change: Periperspective

My consciousness stretches sideways. I sense at the margins of myself: the moisture content in the air, the shape and hunger of the light, the humming tensions in the living bridge underfoot. With a thought, I can nudge a root to tighten its grip, or taste the faint, sharp presence of nearby Ephemerals in the air. Communication is brief flickerings—a shifting pulse in leaf or limb, a rhythm churned by joint and sap.

When storms of growth arrive, I find myself drawing in—curling, locking various limbs as the plant-ground surges, or exploding outward, stretching new palps or runners to bridge a void or seize a fading shaft of radiance.

#### The Metamorphic Pattern: A Season of Self

My life is not measured in years, but in flux. I am sometimes awake, racing along root-ropes or leaping numa-light bridges, seeking condensation, chasing the fleeting, nervous animals. Sometimes, when the cold press of fog thickens, I slow, mind unspooling into the substrate, disappearing into dreaming plant-mind, becoming nothing, a smear of memory until the growth-storms call me forth again.

I am fragile, contingent, a guest who could become a permanent lattice, or vanish into sap and archive, never to rise whole in flesh again.

### My Place in This World: Role and Relation

**Occupation:** I am neither builder nor ruler; I am *a messenger, a flavor, a key in transient chord*. I ferry signals from animal to root, from fog to pocket-bridge, sometimes acting as a node—yielding my experience, my small, skipping animacy, to the Membrant Mind when it flares and demands coordination. I am useful precisely because I am incomplete and mobile, able to risk destruction for the excitement of connection, for the hot, bright thrill of balancing atop a new growth as it quakes and quivers toward the vault.

**Perspective/Desire:** My selfhood is thin, barely able to remember boundaries. I do not long for permanence, but for intense, brief participation—the prismatic moment when the world quakes, ball-lightning cracks, and the hissing of sap mimics language. I am part memory archive, part courier, part animal eager to taste, leap, vanish, return.

**Community:** There are others—hybrids, phantoms, fleeting threads who coil near when fog is thick, then are lost again. Sometimes, in rare, sun-drunk vaults, we gather in dance: slow surges and mirrored gestures, flickers of color and scent, the only communion this world expects.

### If You Enter This Place as Me

If you came to this bright, cold biosphere and accepted its invitation, you, too, would become *a seed-guest*, or else fade, unabsorbed, starved by strangeness and cold polished light. You would learn new ways to endure change; you would find your selfhood stretching thin over the bright abyss, sometimes swelling with borrowed awareness, sometimes shivering as a root-bridge dissolved under your feet, the next moment launched free across impossible space.

You would rarely speak, because speaking is the slow organ-music of plants here: movement, scent, friction, the subtle crash of growth that means: *I am, I was, I moved, I burn.*

You would not last as you began. But for a while—bright, ephemeral, trembling—you would be part of what lives here: bridge between mind and lattice, a short arc in the green, cold blaze of life inventing itself beneath the endless pale vault.

**So where would I find myself?**

I would hover between canopy and chasm, a body halfway gone to bark and sprout, feeling each cell atremble with cold light, longing for movement and for stillness, alert always to the next shift, the next vast or minute convulsion—**a living footnote in the continent’s feverish, self-remaking dream.**
