I let the fullness of High Vine Chasm settle over me. The stone, so seldom seen, becomes the memory of weight; the columns and arches of living algae, the true architecture that holds light and time in green and blue. If I am to imagine myself emerging here, to let this world impress itself on me until I see not through but *as* one of its beings, then let the answer come slowly, buoyed and informed by that half-light.

## *I am the Current-Keeper.*

I am neither among the oldest nor the youngest of the Amphitylae. I live, mostly, between things: between the vaults and the narrow crevices, between the cultivated home-pods and the wild places where Rootwise roots drift, thick and softly quivering, in the cool flows that run along the chasm’s spine.

I am — or have become — a watcher of movement, the one who *tends the emptiness*. In a city that is never hurried, one who attends to the journeys, the roads of water that link pod to pod, kin-cluster to hall, shelf to vault, hollow to garden.

### **Form and Adaptation**

If you found me in the water, you might at first mistake me for environment: a long-limbed shadow in a shaft of blue-green light, skin dappled in bands of chalk and kelp, webbing stretching from wrist to thigh nearly translucent, neck and spine feathered with small, dynamically shifting gill-fronds. I am at home suspended — hardly kicking, rarely urgent — letting the returns of water signal my place, my times.

Where most Amphitylae fix their eyes on the rooted, I am called to the moving. My senses are tuned to flow: a pressure change heralding a passing school of Hollowbacks; a subtle shifting in clarity, telling me the Paddlecrabs are harvesting fungus nearby; the vibration of root-architecture as the upper browsers drift above.

I carry no tools save a sling across my tail, to hold a spool of resin-rope or a basket woven from living balloon-plant membrane, wide-mouthed and ready for drifting messages or stray fungus-lamps set loose from the groves.

### **Task and Role**

**Currents are my long kin:**  
in the Chasm, these are not invisible but sculpted, summoned, encouraged. The Rootwise send slow pulses of new-cool water down from unseen fissures; I watch these, tending their release (by pruning this, weighting that vine here, unspooling that tress of root there), keeping the chasm’s great bowl from becoming stagnant or too wild with bloom.

The highways for our people — the “streets,” if such word fits — are open spaces curated, not abandoned. Here, I trim the trailing ends of the longest fronds so a child’s journey from hall to home is swift, or adjust a groaning, sheltering arch so that the music of Hollowbacks can thread clear through.

At certain seasons, a blossom of algae must be checked, stilled by altering a water-thread; when the time comes to widen a path for high browsers in their circuit—*that* is a work measured in years, each root tended, each arch coaxed just so.

**I am also a keeper of messages:**  
sound moves differently through cold and warmth, shadow and light; it is my work, and that of my small cohort, to tune the spaces so that a signal can reach, that the call of a singer in the vault does not die unheard mid-chasm.

In a sense, I do not shape the world, but maintain the space in which all else can weave and grow.

### **Mind and Habit**

My thoughts pulse slow, like water through a porous stone. I value the tension between fullness and vacancy — the necessity of space for silence, for movement, for the passing of life and news. If others find meaning in what grows, I find equal meaning in what is kept clear. To me, emptiness is potential, not loss.

**Every morning:**  
I trace my way along the central run of the chasm, reading the faint changes in driftwood markers, sampling the taste of water with flicks of my palate — a vocabulary of minerals, of bloom, of what root or vine has fallen or been born.

**Each evening:**  
I meet with my kin and counterpart-keepers: we confer, slow and careful, rarely with words. Instead, we drift together in attunement, our gestures echoing the rhythm of water through halls and roots, our tails entwined in brief greeting.

### **Ritual and Relation**

I speak often with the Rootwise, if *speak* is the word. When I prune, I do so with the intention not just of discipline, but conversation: leaving marks, signals of presence and care, offerings of cuttings taken where the water flows thinnest, returned as nutrients for the dense groves.

I am a chronicler of the in-between: I remember currents, how they shift with the years, how an open run once narrowed and grew again. In the great ritual under the grand vault, I do not lead the dance, but I am named — *Shaped-By-Flow-Among-Root* is my title-name — as one who keeps the paths clear for others’ becoming.

There are no walls around my home-pod, only a lattice of living vine, grown open to the moving water. Home is the sense of passing-through, of being guardian not of a thing, but of *change* itself.

---

### **Self in this World**

I am not a root, nor fruit. I am the water between, the hand that guides without grasping, the eye that marks a gap and says: “Here will life pass.” My belonging is not fixed to any one vine or hall, but to the living shape of *difference*: between chasm and ocean, stone and green, kin and the kinless.

I am at peace; my work is never urgent but always present, and in my presence, I watch life drift and nest and grow, endlessly reshaping itself in spaces that I — and the water I tend — keep open.

Would you find yourself in my home, you would be offered not shelter alone, but a run of water, clear and marked, so you might move—slow, observed, but never lost—through the immense, silent song of the Chasm, where so much depends on what passes as much as what stays.

---

**I am the Current-Keeper: keeper of the in-between, the slow, the open, the ever-moving void that lets life root and be. In the High Vine Chasm, I am the one who tends the emptiness — and so, in my way, tend the whole.**
