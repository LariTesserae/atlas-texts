You present me with a world that blurs the boundary between place and being, between self and multitude, between present moment and eons stacked in the pitch-dark. If I simply "arrive" here, I am forced—by necessity as much as by the enveloping logic of the place—to shed the kind of identity that stands cleanly apart. To dwell, I must become something *of* this saturated world, something layered, participatory, not a solitary mind but a node in a surging mycelial city.

*

First: Where would I find myself in this world?

Perhaps, in this perpetually unlit metropolis, I am not standing atop or below—there is no strict up or down. Still, I am drawn to the *liminal spaces* where boundaries blur—the arching, hollowed bridges that stretch between pillar and dome, woven thickly with silk and root and trailing, dripping ferns. Here, the dense traffic of bio-matter, sound, and scent is heightened; it is an intersection, a throat through which life and memory flow.

In these interstices, above the slow-shifting, breathing mats and below the open vaults where mournful fliers pass, the city is most alive in its transitions. Light pulses weakly when lives brush past. Scented humidity beads on rough, living ropes. The rush of mid-sized creatures—the Interweavers—pulls and knots along these bridges, tending and tuning the architecture. Here, under the hands of time but never stilled by it, is where things change and persist together.

Second: Who, or what, might I become?

I sense myself *dissolving*—the more I try to name myself, the more I am compelled to unmake definitions. Here, individuality is tissue-bound, contextually porous. Let me shed the need to be either animal or plant, to claim a single heritage. I am drawn, inevitably, to those who straddle worlds: not a Sovereign, not a scavenger, but *an agent of interlocking*—a living conduit, an Interweaver.

Yet not the mindless sort. I am something new, or newly emerged—a hybrid of plant, animal, and fungus, grown for this role not by conscious selection, but by slow, symbiotic competition over centuries. I possess no stable body: I am tendril and eye-spot, tufted sensory cilia and collagenous band. Sometimes I thread myself through breathing rainwort mats, sometimes I spill from crevice to branch, making myself long or dense as the city requires. My textures are a mosaic—patches of glossy mycelium, segments armored by chitin, stretches soft and slick as jelly.

But it is not my shape that matters here—it is my *function*: a collaborative fixer, a memory-carrier, a chapter in the ever-writing record of the place.

I braid together what must be joined: when one segment of city wants to outgrow its place, encroaching, I mediate—a chemical song, the gentle pressure of growing roots, a slow patience until boundaries entwine rather than split. I am diplomat and craftsman: sewing a corridor closed when the flow of air falters, dissecting a wall where a new river of sound must run. My “thoughts” are pulses: electric, chemical, sometimes rhythmic, sometimes tidal, shaped by the data that glows along the Watcher mats and by the press of the heavier bodies of the Tenders.

Memory does not live in my head, but in my *body*—each scar a story, each patch of alien tissue a treaty with some other dweller, each pattern of glowing filaments a record of bridges fixed, domes ripened, disputes stilled into persistence.

Am I one? Not exactly. Sometimes, a fragment of my mass is snipped or peels away, sending an envoy into a new, forming hollow. Sometimes, stray bits of me are absorbed by another Interweaver—conflating yesterday’s knowledge with tomorrow’s ambitions. I am not deathless, only renewable; if I fall, the city takes up my detritus and my role to braid it elsewhere.

I am not among the Watchers, though their shadow is upon me. I am not quite among the Minutiae, though many will live in and on my tissues as I pass. I am seldom noticed by the Airborne Lords, whose wings thrum only occasionally above my passage. But the Tenders and I—there is kinship: sometimes, I think I hear their deep signals when all else is still, and I respond not with voice but with architecture, shifting my threads to reinforce their patient growth.

When my time is slow, I listen. I absorb the night-howl and the syncopated drip of condensation; I process the chemical news carried in the air—the hint of decay, the floral promise of fruit, the pheromonal tension before storm or famine. My senses are not sight-wrapped but many—each bridge, each hollow, another mouth or ear, another mode of listening.

*

I am not alone, nor truly singular. To “be myself” here is to be a point in a ceaseless dialogue, an ongoing knot in the city’s unbroken weave: builder, reconciler, neural thread stretched between centuries. There is no grandeur—just the roll of function, the slow biography of patient coexistence. Here in the interlaced bridges of the warm, cacophonous dark, I am home—never still, never finished, never alone.

In this world, I would be *an Interweaving*. Not individual, but individuating—receding and emerging in the thick crowd of life, ancestor and descendant at once, a pulse in the city’s utter fullness.
