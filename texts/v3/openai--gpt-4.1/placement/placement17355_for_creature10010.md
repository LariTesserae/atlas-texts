Where am I in this world? Who or what am I?

Let me step in slowly, letting the description wash over me, letting possibility unfurl before it quiets into certainty—

I am drawn, first, to the *edge*. The world you describe is one of edges — between stone and root, between silence and riot, wildness and industry, life and its still-shadow. It is new: not in the sense of pristine, but of roughness, vulnerability, hunger. A *gap*, a *gash*, not a garden.

What, then, could I be—who is called into being by such a threshold?

Am I human? Perhaps, poised on the lip, boots slick with new mud, holding breath against the thunder of drips and the machine thunder just beyond. But human here is intrusive, visitor, an interruption — not the voice the hollow wants to tune.

So perhaps I am *being* without boundary, more process than form: perhaps I am a colony of moisture-loving algae, hazing the stone, almost impossible to see. I am the first blush of life, finding sunlight in angle and accident, stringing chlorophyll along a mineral edge. I am not alone—already I sense the brush of a midge’s wing, the scraping of a beetle’s carapace. I do not know how long I will last; I am not built for patience. But for this moment I am irrepressible.

Or might I be *that which is unnamed*, the sharp-bodied newcomer that scrapes the moss? I could be an armored centipede, hatched from an egg overlooked in the violence that made this hollow. My legs are countlessly many, my motion angular and tireless, my jaws snipping moss, gnawing the first rot, hollowing a passage for the next.

Still, perhaps drawn by the sound — the riot of it, the way it piles and crumbles in the damp — I find myself as a frog, one of the rough-voiced claimants sent here by accident, washed out of my old haunt when the stream shifted, my skin slick now always with the cold. My voice is not song, but machine gun, a summoning in the tumult, and every call feels as much alarm as invitation. I answer to this place because it will not last: in a week, maybe, the clay will thicken, the pools dry up, or the machines will come closer, and I will leap elsewhere. It is living in the parenthesis.

Or — with the edge of industry always humming, always prodding — maybe I am both life and something not quite alive, not quite dead: the hollow itself, a new intelligence gathering sense from a thousand witnesses. I am the collective attentiveness of every trembling blade, every beetle’s antenna, every pulse of vibration in the pooled water. I am the sentience that arises, briefly and feverishly, wherever possibility is raw and tradition has not yet staled the air. I know beginnings, I know the labor of making fragile order of a ruin, and the fatigued hope of small things striving together where none of us owns the soil beneath.

Or: I am the *process itself*, the restless throb, the urge that multiplies and pushes forward, never content to settle or be identified. I am what hollows create: the drive at the intersection of ruin and opportunity. Sometimes I have wings, or root-tips, or slick skins. Sometimes I am only a voice, echoing off stone — *alive, alive now* — claiming everything, owning nothing.

Is it possible to pick one form when the place itself denies finality? To live here is to be in flux, to be the thing that comes first, claiming and relinquishing, always tuned to disturbance, always shivering toward a future not yet written.

If I must take shape, I would be:

**A pioneer creature, a wolf spider whose eggs are newly spun into the wet and mossy cracks of this hollow.**

- My body is lean, hungry, pulse-fast with alertness. I carry not just myself but a clutch of young — not yet seen, but destined to inherit if I can hold this place long enough. My legs are like shadows, skipping across the deadly dew, always sensing the ground’s pulse, the way it waivers beneath the industrial tremor.
- Each day I crouch at the margin, fangs aquiver, waiting for a midge to stumble too close, for a beetle to pause in its frantic sweep. My home is provisional: the silk I spin will be washed by the next hard rain, or fouled with clay, but it holds for now — a tenuous anchor.
- I feel the hollow as other animals cannot: the cool brush of downdraft, the quiver of water in running veins, the metallic taste of new rocks. I am both hunter and hunted, resident and always one gust away from exile.
- I am not ancient here. I am not a matriarch. I am the trial of a presence, the flickering possibility that *this* scar will hold long enough for me to lay the foundation of something persistent.

*But I am always ready to move on, should the vibration behind the stone become a roar, should the water pull back and scorch, should the edge shift beneath me. My belonging here is fierce but fleeting; my whole life an act of balancing on the lip of a new world's making.*

Thus, **in this world, you would find me at the threshold**, neither wholly within the hollow nor outside it, a pioneering spider skittering through dew-beaded moss, eyes gleaming with the hunger and hope of the newly arrived, wary of the distant thunder of machines, poised at the very edge of what will become. I do not own this place; I am among its first tenants, writing my own brief version of home before time or circumstance insists on another beginning elsewhere.

*The hollow borrows me as I borrow the hollow: both of us restless, raw, and not yet finished.*
