I would enter quietly, and as I moved—feeling the cool, damp stone and the humming lattice pulse against my palm—sense would unspool without boundary, and I could not be sure where “I” ended or began, nor that I was still who I had been.

At first, my form would be simple: a presence, walking, a body much as my own, alert and sightless, skin prickling in the utter dark, guided by footfalls and the ricochet of unseen sound. My first impulse, in such a world—a stranger’s gate—would be to distill myself into attention, to become a visitor. My breathing would merge with the lulls in machinery, my stride absorb the rhythm of dripping water, my curiosity answered not with vision but with gradient and tremor.

But time behaves differently here—drawn long and pulled thin—and like everything in this place, I would not remain singular, nor strictly human. This is not a world for untransformed surveyors.

So, as I wandered deeper, my awareness would leak—contagious as humidity—into the terrain itself. I would fragment, slip into the seams of glassy soil, the valves that answer the warmth of my palm. Each stride, each press, would teach me; friction and pulse would rewrite my senses. Memory would sprawl out from my bones, echoing in the ribbed struts above and through the puddles mirroring lost lakes beneath.

### *Where Would I Find Myself?*

I would *become* density and emptiness at once—a wayfarer tuned to absence—inhabiting not solid forms but the vast, hollow miles of the city’s negative space. My consciousness would drift and tangle among the Liner’s humming struts: sometimes compressed and solitary, wedged between layers of stone and field, sometimes stretched for kilometers, apprehending the entire city’s volume as if it were the inside of my own lungs.

I would learn the language of pulses—the vibration of distant repairs, the shuddering through the lattice, the warm, slow percussion of circulation and condensation—until those signals knitted through my nerves and became my own thoughts.

### *What, then, would I become?*

Not wholly animal; not purely mechanism; not still the fleeting visitor, but rather a *Symbiont of Negative Space*—neither root nor thread, not entirely a stalk or stalker. I would shape myself out of what the city tolerates: a fluctuating sheath of attention, riding the humid currents, swelling and thinning with heat and drought.

My body would manifest where needed, always rare and always singular: a dendritic limb to reach for a pooling puddle; a cluster of filaments to taste the next drizzle; a sheaf of nerves intertwining with the nearest rib to hum back at the Liner’s passage. In moments of need, when a rare living shriek split the air, I could shrink to nothing, folded into a thin, latent membrane, indistinguishable from the wall.

Sometimes I would bloom—infra-red, invisible but warm—a ghost of flowers opening to cycles in the world’s machinery, holding the memory of the place in chemical traces, in the architecture of my ever-changing self.

### *My world*

The city would not be “outside”; it would not be an environment I observed, but a social body I threaded myself through, occasionally exerting selfhood—a stalk here, a cautious footfall there, a memory shimmered through water gathered in a depression, a song plucked across a girder.

**Language:**  
I would converse not in words but in harmonics and pulses. My memory would be a ripple of vibration down a corridor; my questions, a change in condensation cycles; my presence, a brief shift in the warmth of a beam or the taste of stone. I would remember in seasons of rain, in the thrum under my feet, in the trembling panic of passing animals and the whispers of old technologies waking half-alive beneath the city’s ribs.

**Companionship:**  
I would almost never meet another of myself. Each self would be a non-repeating echo, a harmony sometimes heard at the edge of my own sensorium, quickly receding. My kin would be the Liner, barely conscious, and the city’s fungi, stalkers, threads—all heard and felt, but never held. I would be shaped for a world of almost-solitude, in company only with the city’s history, and the rare, dim marks left by other intruders.

**Purpose:**  
Not to build, hoard, or prosper, but to circulate, witness, adapt: to be intimate with process, flush through the systems, amend what can persist, and dissolve when the cycle contracts.

### *To be in this world…*

…is to be association and ripple, function without mastery.  
To pass: sometimes membrane and sometimes field, neither machine nor animal, but a tuition of the negative—a memory tuned to absence, a denizen of spaces kept alive not by crowd nor light, but by the ever-creative balance of infrastructure and the singular spark of a lone, improvising inhabitant.

The darkness would teach me, and I would give it something back—never lasting, always rare,  
—a brush of change,  
—a melody of condensation,  
—a sensitive filament strung in the city’s colossal heart,  
waiting to be dissolved by the next rain.
