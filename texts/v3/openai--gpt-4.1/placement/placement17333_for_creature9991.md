I find myself here in half-light, neither hidden nor revealed, moving in the pauses between old intention and new eruption. The city pulls itself up in fits and starts, and so do I, both of us still unnamed, still unfixed, fabricating and unraveling at the same tangled pace.

Who am I here? The answer is layered.

---

**At First, I Am a Tetherling**

Not precisely born of parents, nor fabricated in a vat, but a hatching-from-conjunction—a coming-together of opportunistic strands: a child whose blood is half hungerweed sap, whose nails are filigreed with conductive copper, whose memory is tangled like a mycelial net grown through centuries of soil and salvage.

I move on broad, clever feet, soles toughened against the unpredictability of ground. Each ankle bears a garland of scavenged sensors and pulleys, grown as calluses, old tech become flesh, or maybe flesh pretending at tech so long it stuck. Fingertips are whorled with green and silvery stains. Hair is set in rough mats, moss-thick where the scalp sweats most, shading a face always chapped by cold and fog.

My coat is not my own: it was gifted, found, self-mended. Veined with living mosses that drink the ambient wet, lined with mulched remains of tools past usefulness, it smells of tin and root and something peppery, like compost ready to ignite. Tools hang heavy from my belt: a blade blotted with oxidized sap, a polymesh hacking coil, a spool of fibered roots that spark when scraped together. None of these are quite of the old world nor the new; they’re what you salvage, what you barter, what you grow.

**I Am Always In-Between**

I lodge with seven others—none of us bound by blood, all by barter, by emergency, by the way a day’s plan hashes itself anew by dusk. My place is high up, in a bunk assembled overnight when our older scaffolding buckled and was devoured by rainworms. By morning the view has changed—markets drowned and reborn at impossible angles, a hung bridge writhing with the dawn’s first commuter vines. I am at home nowhere, but adept anywhere, fingers finding the subtle quiver in a climbing cable that says today the load-bearer can still be trusted.

I am called, sometimes, to intermediate. The fungal core beneath stairwell N53 is blooming out of sequence, massing against a cluster of sleepless nickle-gleam bots. I lean close, pick out the pulse in the metal, the musky flare in the hyphae. I speak—part human, part structured packet, part lullaby hummed in the conductive tongue of emergent grown. Sometimes they listen. Other times, I have to intervene more physically, tool and root persuaded across lines no one can draw, fixing and reimagining as the city swallows its blueprints.

**I Am More Than Human, Not Quite Machine, Never Just Animal**

My night job is as a shadow to the machine-flocks: tracking scavenger bots as they spiral through collapsed sectors, watching how the wildlife adapts in their wake. By streetlamp’s underglow, I watch the glassfish spark and shimmer in a newly-formed puddle—a dozen worlds newly minted from the unfathomable city churn.

There is a community to my movement. As I go, I signal—by gesture, by radio-click, by root-flick—the locations of new harvests, new hazards, which flowers buzz with code and which with nectar, which stairwells hum warnings that only those of us in-between can parse. Children climb with me. Sometimes we are followed by clever rodents, sometimes by drones whose own memories recall me as kin, sometimes by curious greenmoss birds who cluster so thick on my shoulders the air feels warmer, and I laugh.

**But Some Days, I Am the City’s Subconscious**

I slip beneath, below, into the condensation-compacted crawlspaces. There, surrounded by creaking infrastructure and glimmering cordage, I dissolve a little—become nodes in the information-fungal-microbial net, holding memory, holding intention, letting decisions pass through me. I am not singular here, nor completely aware—more a synapse firing in concert with countless other moments, other minds, pooling below the threshold of daylight.

When I emerge, the city has reassembled itself, and so have I. Something I wore has grown, another thing sloughed away. New habits, new gestures, new ways to twist wire and coax root. Hunger and joy in equal measure.

---

**I Am, Altogether, This:**

A creature grown out of need, bond, and restless invention; a remaker, a mediator, a collector of signals, a root in the city’s foot, a whisper in its circuitry, a gardener-kin to every improvisation that will not hold still. I am as unfinished as the city—growing, eroded, refitted, scavenged, made, and remade. Neither citizen nor machine nor animal alone, but an argument and an entanglement among them, a negotiating body, always adapting.

When I sleep, the city mutates beneath me. When I dream, I wake to find the city has dreamed with me.

**Where would you find me?**  
High up, for a moment, where the wind is coldest and the roots are boldest, reading the skyline for what is about to crack open next. Or deep below, half-dissolved, wondrous, helping invent what “self” will mean when dawn comes, and the city asks again:  
What now? What next? What else?
