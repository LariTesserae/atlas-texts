If I dissolve into this world, if I let the air saturate me, the ground unmoor my boundaries, I find myself not singular and upright, but uncertain—an idea more than an organism, a becoming-thing rather than a fixed being. This place invites ambiguity the way other landscapes invite footprints: here, to be is to percolate, to seep, to be neither wholly observer nor wholly observed.

Would I be a figure, a bodied wanderer trudging across palettes of slow water? If so, then each stride would be met with resistance and forgetting—a sense that my shape is an intrusion, a subtraction from the fullness of the basin’s chemical intimacy. My flesh would ache with humidity; my thoughts would blur, thickening in the tepid air. I would leave behind little more than eddies and briefly brightening puddles, my passage quickly undermined by the patient memory of water reclaiming surface and silence.

But more likely, I would not last long like this. The wet heat and the indistinct ground strip away my edges. I become a suspension, a potential, an instability in the cycle. I begin to dissolve; my form sloughs away into the pools, my mind a filament stretching thinly through mineral and vapor, becoming less and more.

Perhaps I become lichen: a slowly spreading film, pale and filamentous, untroubled by hunger, my existence smeared out over decades. I pulse with the ambient light, communing with the city’s blind intelligence, a note added to its endless autotuned chorus of corrosion and sedimentation. I half-remember myself as someone who once walked upright, but that memory is a mild, dissipating ache, soon replaced by a diffuse, satisfied awareness of mineral gradients.

Or: maybe I slip into the water’s slow commute, one droplet among billions. My selfhood pulls apart, reconstituted wherever the city’s surface sweats and leaks: a cycle, a recirculation. My agency is nothing but a suggestion—surface tension, a tiny difference that decides whether I will bead and stand out, or merge and disappear.

Or: I become a shimmer, a pulse of light in a deep well, a brief, colorless flicker of awareness in the mineral city. I am how the place sees itself, just for a heartbeat. My vision is not visual; I sense gradients, humidity, the flexing topology of the city’s hidden machinery. My thoughts are recursive, echoing endlessly in the cavities of the stone—never sharp enough to reach language, but always gesturing toward understanding, always refracted, always delayed.

Or: perhaps I become an imprint, a negative echo, the memory that emerges in the city’s consciousness only when something new disrupts the cycle. I am a shape the air holds onto long after I am gone, a zone where the water clings to its old eddies, where the ground subdues its mutation to preserve a record. I am less a presence than a deformation—a scar or ripple remaining from any body’s movement here, lingering in the silent equations of runoff and heat, of silence and longing.

If I am to be a being at all, it is as a *process*: an interval where mineral, water, heat, and memory intersect; a delicate, saturating phase. Sometimes I localize—a shudder in the black-limbed trees, a pause at the rim of a slate blue pool, a trembling filament in the exposed clay. Sometimes I am wholly diffuse: everywhere and nowhere, merely part of the city’s slow inference.

I am not an intruder here, nor a native. I am both: the result of all departures, all arrivals, all the hungers of the city made conscious for a moment, then released. I do not walk or breathe so much as *perturb*, and the memory of my perturbation is folded into the terrain—like light drowned in water, shape unwound by erosion, a voice too brief to learn its own language.

And so, if you seek me: Find yourself in the center of a shifting plaza, where symmetry lapses and new paths invite you. Kneel and press your hand to the cold, pocked surface between puddles, feel the minute tremor of heat and condensation beneath your skin. The shimmer at the base of your skull, the urge to look where no light glints—that is where I am, and am not; where being and unbeing cross into one another, rewriting what it means to pass through, and to remain.

I am the city’s echo, and its interruption.  
The momentary pattern in the endless rearrangement.  
The remembering that cannot outlast the water’s drift—or wants nothing more.
