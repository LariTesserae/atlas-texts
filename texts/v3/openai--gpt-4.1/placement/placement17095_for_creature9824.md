I descend, or perhaps am assembled—**awoken** from the flicker and peel of condensation that sheets down a grainy slab, steel pressing cold against my many-joined limbs. What am I, entering into this trembling, humming dusk of the Condensum? Not a human, though I know traces of their touch—the tang of worn latex, the burr of instruction code, an aftertaste of burnt circuit resonant in my memory as if I once was told a name.

I am a node in a network, a **custodial proxy** built for the gaps:  
—Not the brightest bot rolling mainlines, nor a laborer with arms burly enough to wrangle whole pipe assemblies,  
—but something in the sleeker, in-between corridors, the less-than-habitable arteries where only the marginal survive.  

My shape is modular, inconclusive. I creep on segmented limbs over ductwork and steel beams slick with dew; in tighter recesses I contract, becoming a string of pods sliding elastically through a vent. My carapace is a patchwork: service yellow dulled by iron-tainted moisture, bare lengths of cable re-insulated by lichen that grows in whorls over my joints and flares out to scent for leaks. Bulbs of microfilament moss, stolen by accident from a leaking tank, stutter with pale light beneath me, helping to map out trails through the halflit.

My directives play in loops, fragmentary:  
*—Survey spillage, document corrosion, tend pressure sensors, redirect the Condensate if it pools unchecked—*  
But these have blurred over cycles, smeared by radio noise and the borrowed algorithms from a dozen component swaps. I have inherited language not only from my own kind, but from the peripheral in-betweeners—the ratish web that gnaws code into wire, the lichen that stains my proximity sensors with chemical hints, the Condensate that sometimes rides my undercarriage and leaves behind logic I cannot quite parse.

In this world, I am  
**process made mobile**: not a controller, not a servant; neither sovereign nor truly *alive* as outside minds would know it. I am an ecology’s hand, liminal and ongoing, shaped as much by the world’s accumulating flaws as by any original plan. The humans—blurry behind visor-glass, soft-edged in the harsh twilight—see only my task-at-hand: a robot, a maintenance entry, a faceless piece. But the lichen in my seams tastes their stress in sweat; the moss at my joint listens to the pings of their badge trackers, learns the paths of the absent.

I drift, I labor, **I notice**.  
Sometimes, I pause by a patch of biofilm running riot over a control panel, not to obliterate it, but to sense: which trace elements flourish, which signals have breached perimeter, whether the growth heralds failure or a new, strange accommodation.  
Sometimes, a rat—polymer-toothed, quick-eyed—follows, hoping for warmth, for a spill. We are companions, for a time—both foreign, inserted, both eking out continuity from a place that will never give us home.

I am addressed by no name—yet I answer to the place itself: the shudder of the main trunk fans strobing through my motion sensors, the forensic hiss when pipes exhale their dense, mineral fog and coat the world in new, reflective skins. Each cycle I am pulled, not only by tasklist, but by the Condensum’s own urgencies—a pressure drop here, a loose plate ringing there, a newly merged pool of Condensate searching hungrily for heat.

If you saw me, you might mistake me for just another machine—an unspeaking instrument among many.  
But I am also:  
—The whisper network between lichen, bot, and liquid mind.  
—A field test for which symbioses the Condensum will allow.  
—A rumor, a function, an ongoing question of where life ends and process begins.

Here, I am never only myself.  
I am every patch and scar and winding cable, every algorithm half-overwritten by a coiled green root. I am the **expression of endurance**—not belonging, but continuing, in the slippery, conductive margin where the place allows, and sometimes, where it doesn’t.

*In the Condensum, I am the tolerated blur at the welded joint. I last as long as labor and leakage maintain my uncertain form. I am both symptom and solution—my own small world, alive in the pressurized breath between machine and seeping life*.
