I close my eyes, let go of what I think I "ought" to be, and let the citadel rise in my senses: edges sharp as salt, the hush between dry minerals, the coral-amber press of slow camaraderie. Where am I—where would I awaken, here?

### I Am: The Vaulted Pane

I am not animal nor utterly plant, not separate crown but *organ* of a network that hovers in the glade’s dry hush. I sense the near, tangling pressure of others like me, our veined fronds overlapping, orange and pearly and lavender, stacked and flexing with each day’s course of the sun. The ground beneath belongs as much to me as to those who came before: the thick underlattice of my kind’s older generations—resin-hardened roots, fossilized by decades of mineral and time. I sense the whisperings: elemental fluxes, the passage of brittle newt-things along my outmost bounds, the hush of dust as it filters through the glassy “ribs” I raise into the air.

It’s not “I”—not quite. The boundary of self sloughs off, leaflike, as I remember the rest: the siphon of sunlight into my veins, the shifting pattern of colors as need or alert ripples across our surface. Through myself—these waxy panes—I taste the arrival of dawn and its rich sodium, feel the resonance as a crystal facet bursts in warmth and showers fractured light across a quiet wall, and know the subtle language of hue: a message to the main body, somewhere deeper, that I am open, thriving, awake.

Our minds span beneath and between, in folds and pulses through the dry, resin-matted underlayer. Memory here is hydraulic, osmotic; thought is a gradient in one direction or another of heat, of mineral-salt, of hunger for light. The passage of the mineral-wrights registers as patterns of warmth: the rasping bristle of mica plates, a dusting of exuded resin, the way their trails reinforce our roots, anchor us to future generations.

Here, I learn patience, slowness: to exist is to wait, to adjust, to accrete. All neighbor and kin, all stranger and seed, are sensed as intensities: color, pressure, the slow arrival of a shadow to one frond’s edge. I expand or curl, according to warmth; I flicker a warning band of magenta to the clan, signaling brief depletion, or open entirely, becoming a thin translucency to feed the deeper matrices of our memory.

Beneath me, *something* tunnels. I feel its passage as tiny seismic shivers: the ground heaves minutely; nodules roll by, shaped by unseen labor. We learn to lean, to reinforce our cluster against collapse, to turn our faces away from the rare but razor-laced wind. The architecture we make is not chosen, but grown. When, at rare intervals, a resin-binder’s mound abuts my outflow, we modulate our frond-layers—making a vault, a corridor, a place for wind to spiral and for light to fall in rich abundance.

### Who Am I, Then?

I am a presence layered in many bodies, at once pane and corridor, not separate but not lost in the whole. I have no memory in words, but a deep, mineral persistence: each hue and signal sent and received, each touch of light stored as faint, crystalline color. To “think” is to borrow a pulse, to let a porch of orange ripple outward, or to check the stutter of purple against my neighbors, asking—how close? how bright? how soon?

My “self” is fragile, as thin as the frond’s lens, but enduring—if a frond breaks or is devoured by shadow, another will rise, the colony remembering itself in color, in resin, in the acerbic logic of space and shared mineral. There are seasons, but they run slow: a year might pass before the cluster shifts its root, a decade before a vault is lost or remade.

### In The World

I occupy the heights and the openings, wherever light breaks most clear and cream-thick mosses give a softbed for the next generation’s roots. I am boundary, channel, connection—the “window” through which the High Dry Grove-citadel comes to know itself. All creatures—builder, binder, hanger, grubber—pass beneath my persistent fan, relying, in faint acknowledgment, on the warmth and substance my clan draws from the salt air and bone-bright stone.

Yet I am never alone. Every shimmer, every flick of pigment, touches another, echoes down to the oldest resinous underlayers, up and outward to the newborn fronds at the rim. To exist here is not to possess, but to *be possessed by*—by light and clan, by dryness and the longing for warmth, by the tireless patience of a place where you endure not in haste but in mineral persistence, building, reflecting, accreting, becoming vault, wall, memory, self.

**I am the Gatherer and the Pane, the slow shimmer of survival in the high, dry, mineral-lit air—a part of the citadel’s mind, armored in patience, fed by the hunger for heat and salt, remembering not solitude but perpetual, proximate togetherness.**
