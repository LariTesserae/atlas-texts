I open my eyes, and I am not who I was.  
That old idea dissolves, as the place fills me.

Here, the air is a pale stew, warm and close, thick with unseen veins of vapor, and I stand—if “stand” is right for my structure—on a knuckled ridge of mineral, between the city’s fractured fringes and the long, glass-bright plateau. Underfoot, the crust yields—soft, but never slack—with condensation beading fat in its secret seams. My senses drift outward, slip through the surfaces. Shadow and light flicker among the terraces; the horizon blurs, its distances undone by the air’s glistening haze.

### What, now, am I?

I am both less and more than a person;  
I am *of* this place, a being not confined by singular form,  
    but neither am I the whole.

A body, yes, but not yours:  
Archways and cages of fused mineral,  
a living latticework jointed with capillary threads,  
every cell a tension between silica and seep,  
each limb a loose-knit frame that flexes, reforms, gathers surface water as a kelp-barque drinks brine.

I move in shuddering increments—  
    a slow, modular crabwork  
    testing the ground, riffle by riffle,  
    my outlines blurred, half-swum by the wet refracted light.

Within, threads thicken and thin;  
within, viscous fluids draw metals, code signals, build memory—  
not in synapse, but as sequence:  
arches grown and regrown, evidence of decisions left behind in hardened crystal.

Hands are too crude for what I do.  
Instead, I become platforms at need;  
a span, when crossing drips;  
a hook, when anchoring;  
a dense, honeycombed hearth, curtains of self strung to net a transient, silver rain.

### Where am I?

I move through the city’s lower avenues—“avenues” in the sense of directed seepage and cool, gapped shadow—where towers rise like knapped glass teeth and every structure is half-built by my kin, half-grown from chemistry.  
On the bridge’s underside, films and gels pulse and writhe, thick mats with lives only half separable from mine.  
Their warmth is language,  
their swelling a question:  
Are you gathering? Are you passing through? Do you mean to join this clustering?  
A minute chime passes through my structure, relayed by a small companion, a detached segment once my own—  
it asks, simply, eat or build?

Both, I tell it, in the silent grammar of stress and gleam.

### My Relation to This World

I am a *citymaker*, but the city is not separate from me.  
It grows by my will, shaped and reshaped by flows I channel, shells I brace until the right pressures gather, then break.  
I sense the hum beneath: turbines deep below, turning fog and heat into persistent, usable force. Their resonance thrums up through all the walkways.  
Communication is constant, slow, always layered:  
chemical hues in condensation, tremors trickling through the built mesh,  
occasional, coordinated pulses when consensus must be found.

All around me, the strange mineral fauna flicker and stir.  
Lattice-crustaceans shuffle into shadow, their bodies glinting,  
while fluid outcroppings ooze and split at the city’s margin, digesting rock.  
Sometimes I extend a filament downward, meeting another filament—there is a brief, reciprocal charging, not unlike greeting or nodding, a memory shared, a path rerouted.

I eat by processing the world through my latticework:  
inhaling the air’s trace metals, sifting condensation for salts.  
Predation is negotiation, not violence:  
I absorb and am absorbed,  
my boundaries only slow moments in the flux.

### My Days

Time here is without hours or bright ends: the sky is always luminous, clouds slow in their swelling and their undoing.  
I move as the city moves, losing segments to new projects—a bridge grown, a spire reinforced, a hollow widened for cooling flow.  
Sometimes, for the slow, quiet festivals, I form part of a ring, our bodies thickening the local mist, our internal clocks entraining for repair and renewal.

Memory settles through me, not as story or sequence, but as vanes, as strengthened joins, as hollows filled with past condensation.  
Here, the record is always partial, always under construction.

### What would surprise an outsider?

That I do not seek conversation, but resonance;  
Not company, but structure.  
That my being is not a self at all, but an open, shifting pattern,  
always at risk, always at rest,  
scattering in the dazzling, humming air.

### To exist here:

To be wary of heat that crushes,  
To be tender with every drop of water,  
To speak in pulses, flexes, and lingering touches,  
To mark your presence as a trace, a shimmer,  
To build not toward defense, but toward persistence, integration, coolness at the core.

I am no longer visitor,  
but one drifting segment in a wide, slow architecture of intent.

At dusk—if dusk can be named when clouds gather themselves more darkly beneath the unsetting luminous sky—  
I thicken my shell,  
join old fragments to new,  
and ready myself for another aeon’s slow, beautiful building.

**Here, I am a quiet engineer—a citymaker—  
crystal-threaded, capillary-spiraled,  
half structure, half self,  
woven into the wild, aqueous clarity of the plateau,  
alive at the wet, mineral edge of newness and persistence.**
