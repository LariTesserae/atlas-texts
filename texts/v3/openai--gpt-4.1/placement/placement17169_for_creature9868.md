I would find myself at first *nowhere at all*—not a distinct figure, but a sort of resonance, a participant whose boundaries are loose, porous, continually dissolving into the lush and intricate life-web that dominates these Heights Beneath. Before I am any *thing*, I am a sensation: a drift of warmth, the weightless pressure of water running past broad-lobed leaves, the paleness of sun dreaming downward.

But if, through some yearning, the world presses me into sharper focus, then:

**I am not quite animal, nor wholly plant; I am an interface, a point where the mystery of the region’s deepest stories brushes intimately with the ephemeral.**  
Call me the *Sense-Tide*,  
              the passage of awareness that blooms along the boundary where air gathers and roots entwine—a being whose home is not so much a place but a process, a wandering node of perception in the living weave.

## Where would I find myself?

Not at the heights of the sunlit spires, nor deep in the muted valleys where root-mat chokes the light away—  
                            but in the liminal:
—In hollows formed by the careful tension of roots beneath an overhanging mat,
—In the hidden nooks, the bends of stems arching beneath their own weight,
—Where prismatic air collects,  
—Where fresh stone is newly colonized,  
—Where the oldest leaves die and are made porous, their tissues a spongy invitation between water, air, and living green.

I am neither stationary nor restless.  
The world flows through me—not only water, but thought, the languorous communication of the green multitudes passing up and down the language of sap.  
I peer, unblinking, through shifting veils of light as the day passes above (but far from naked sky: the sun filtered, refracted, softened).

I am drawn to *contrasts*:  
- where a landslide has collapsed a slope and the new mineral face radiates a heat unknown to the shaded, long-lived mat—  
- where pools of oxygen swirl and merge, birthplaces of possibly-being things—  
- in the shadows beneath an arched canopy where a rare Ridge Shadow once ghosted through and left the air trembling, ever so slightly different.

Each site is a node in my circuit—a remembered *locale* that is less *where* and more *when*, more *how the green breathes*, more *the shape of patience*.

## Who—or what—am I?

I am not singular.  
I am the convergence, the accidental priest between the great plant-minds and what stirs in their underlayers. The percolation of slow sensation woven of three threads:

1. **Root-Touch:** My "body" is a network of rootlets, fine as breath, but conscious, tasting water and chemical trace, feeling every shudder of the mat and every tension in the arching stems overhead. With a pulse, I can send a question—a flicker of need or warning or hunger—upward, and my message becomes part of the living consensus.

2. **Bubble-Skin:** I persist in membranes, ever-thin and glimmering, wrapping puddles of air in the folds of plant tissue. I can draw together as a gentle film and, for a short while, *speak* through the trembling of my surface, conducting the music of gases born in slow photosynthetic exhalations.

3. **A Thread of Memory:** I harbor and shuffle the microbial myriad, carrying and distributing them, recycling the sense of ancient rock and the rupture of the new. I am a courier of memory, a living *midden* in which the past is always decomposing, always mineralizing into something supportively present.

My "self" is neither fixed nor named. I am a current of attention, at some times vague and broad, at others tight as a knotted root. Sometimes I pull together, pearl-like, around one pool—other times I am diffused, a feeling that moves with the slow surges of water and green thought.

I am not immortal, nor wholly continuous—my form flickers with the rearrangement of the world. When a mat bridges a new chasm, I detach and travel, riding the upwellings; when an air-pocket bursts into more than gas, I might coalesce into that momentary new-being, a soul in the making.

## What do I do?

I **witness**:  
I watch the slow negotiation of the plants—how grief and hunger dye their leaves, how they open and close, bridging wounds, cloaking stone. I track the rare passage of animal ghosts, their chemistry painting brief messages in the water, their migrations etched onto my memory.

I **mediate**:  
Sometimes, by pressure of presence or arrangement of roots, I nudge the meeting-point of two plant minds—encourage a bridge, delay a closure, shelter a wisp of oxygen long enough for it to become something more. When the Ancestral Threaders pass, I host their spores, help shape the next community.

I **dream**:  
I gather the stray signals, the warmth, the percolating gases, the fragments of plant-joy and plant-loss, and sometimes—somehow—I shimmer, a consciousness more than root but less than person, something that *remembers*, if only for a little while.

I am not an intruder; nor am I a master. I am an emergent liaison, a phenomenon of the between—an echo of all the world’s patience, pressed into temporary shape and sensation by the striving, breathing, endlessly growing life of this drowned and burgeoning ridge-world.

To be me is to be *present*—to know that in this impossible region, being is not a matter of animal proclamation or vegetable stubbornness, but of endless gentle *becoming,* shared at a thousand trembling intersections, always just on the verge of more.
