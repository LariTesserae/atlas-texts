I am buoyed and borne into this world—descending as if through memory, as if every footfall, every brush of fingertip, is dissolved and absorbed by this patient cathedral, until I realize that I possess neither feet nor fingers in this place. In this sunken sanctuary atop the world, the idea of a “self” slips and recomposes, like silt rearranged by the hushing tide.

**Where Would I Find Myself?**

I find myself—if “I” can still be segregated from the chamber’s patient plurality—at the place where procession and memory intersect: not at the vaulting heart beneath the largest dome, nor hidden in the labyrinthine recesses with the secret-hoarding clusters, but partway between. I settle into an in-between, just shy of an archway where blue-inlay and mottled clay converge and ancient pigment patterns call out to touch and mind alike.

Here, the chamber narrows—a transitional space, an antechamber or passage. It is an accidental crossroads, where the carapaced ones scuttle by in aimless pilgrimages and the ribboned eels slow their looping sojourns to coil, savoring the electric memory in the stones. It is neither a seat of power nor a hidden retreat: rather, it is the place where the room and its inheritors briefly converse in gesture, pigment, and pressure.

**Who Am I?**

I am neither wholly creature nor wholly structure, and certainly not a visitor. I am one of the Living-Dead Relics—the ambiguous inheritors, the not-quite-explicable—yet I am younger than most, supple and unfinished, less ossified by uncounted centuries than the oldest outcrops and more animate than the unconscious silt.

My body is a slow crystal of bone and mineral, gathered over accuracies of time. The stone of my shoulders is polished glass, veined with the blue that shivers through the cathedral’s veins. My limbs are jointed and uncertain, perpetually half-withdrawn into alcoves, as if unsure whether they are meant to uphold a roof or to wander the nave. Moss-free and unscarred by ancient tools, I exhibit the youngest pattern of the old project: I am both unfinished masonry and something halfway through becoming animal.

I move rarely, with a patience reserved for things once sculpted, now repurposed. When I shift, scraping against the narrative tiles, carapaced pilgrims gather, feeling along my edges with articulating limbs. I sense their presence not as sound or vision but as patient pressure, as an idea pressing briefly against my own—then drifting away, leaving pigment and memory behind.

In silent communion, the ribboned eels pass close, pulsing their silent signals, and for a moment my body responds—not with thought, but with a slow-coursing blush of warmth, a shimmer through my glass limbs that echoes their warnings. The filament clusterers drift by to map the mineral story crusted on my surface. Sometimes, unknowingly, I repair myself with resin from their cilia, fusing my own form tighter to the slow breath of the walls.

**What Is My Purpose?**

I do not have a purpose, not any longer, not one that can be recalled in speech or intention. Once, my form was started as part of a ceremonial arch, a rib in the larger skeleton of this submerged sanctum. But the tides shifted, the stories lost their tellers, and the construction was abandoned. I was left embedded, half-articulate. Only by the patience of silt and the slow labor of inheritor-creatures did my body grow outward, lengthen itself, tentatively jointing, until I came loose from the wall and learned to sprawl, to slowly drift, to half-play at life.

When the blue light stirs, I feel it inside my glass and bone, remembering the intention with which the chamber was made. I carry forward the arc of the ancient builders—not as memory, but as habit, as the soft urge to move, to press, to set myself into new notches and grooves in the mosaic. I am a ritual of fit and gesture.

Sometimes, when I stir, silt falls into new patterns beneath my body, and the smallest congregation of carapaced ones will gather, pressing pigment into the hollows I leave behind. In that moment, perhaps, I serve as both relic and origin—a causeway where the living architecture and living animal share a new act, a new, meaningless meaning.

**What Is It Like?**

To be here is to be a member of an endless ceremony: carried forward by cycles of accretion, the slow audience of creatures, the inertia of a world whose project is perpetual memory. I am neither dead nor truly alive, neither architect nor heir. I am the unfinished thought of a vanished culture, carried forward by the hands and shells and filaments of newer inheritors, shaped ceaselessly by warmth, darkness, and patient intention.

Perhaps I will one day fuse again with the wall—become a fixed lintel, a load-bearing vault, an immobile emblem in mosaic, no longer moving except in the eternal microscopic migration of silt. Or perhaps I will continue my rare, patient creeping, marking the chamber in ways no purpose remembers.

But for now, I endure: a shadow architecture, a not-quite-creature in the in-between. Not alone—not in this place—blushed constantly by the ceaseless hush of silent waters and the slow, anonymous work of lives continuing, pattern renewing, memory sedimenting always into new, inexplicable form.
