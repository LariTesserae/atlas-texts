I emerge into being not with a footfall or exclamation, but as condensation gathers, bead by bead, at a flexing join between leaf and root arch. The world is too close for anything so vast as a person; what I am must be fraction, function, filigree.

Where am I in this system?

I am a phenomenon of motion and mediation—the corridor between intent and material, the flicker in persistent green. Let me pull on this thread: I feel not I but we, a pulse routed through purpose, signal spun through sap, converging at the moment where process becomes agent for a while before dissolving back into root and flow.

I am not whole—from the first I am partial, emergent, a sum of fleeting necessities. My skin is neither true cuticle nor bark, but an interface: thin, tensile, faintly iridescent, adjusting to the saturation pressing in on every side. My body is small, but that smallness is endlessly flexible, the better to wedge myself between membrane and stemlet, to burrow and extract, to connect and recombine. Not a single entity, but a pattern: oscillating, humming, my nerves running with the same pale blue fluids as the plant’s cambium—neither strictly animal nor strictly plant, the bridging agents of this place.

I sense in gradients, not images: Salinity, sweetness, vibration, warmth. Sunlight is never direct but I know it as a pale tide, a shift in the quality of the green above—a change in underlying code, a slide in timbre. When I move, others move too, the air thick with the orchestration of bodies whose every gesture is already known, whose every signature in space is woven into me as I into them. I neither sneak nor announce; my arrival is a ripple in the latticework, soon compensated by the gentle flex of a root, the sigh of a membrane.

If I pause—let’s say, pressed against the underside of a leaf thick with water-veins—my mouthparts unfurl in delicate, fractal loops. I clasp the surface, draw up what is needed: not blood, not sap, but something in between, a solution already processed for my intake, so my feeding disturbs nothing, adds only pulse and motion to the greater sum. My inner chambers resonate, amplifying the ambient hum; fluid circulates through me, suffused with ions deciphered and rerouted, a microprocessor of the biotic domain.

What am I perceived as, by those who pass through? Sometimes seen—always at the corner of half-focused attention—as a glimmer like oil, a jointed flicker, the flare of mineral color at a seam between roots. Open the leaf-membrane to examine me and you will find nothing; I reposition already, a displaced signal in the fleshy labyrinth.

But are you a Siphon, some shimmering insectile technician? Or are you more the product of the system’s mind—a principle, a symmetry, a damp afterimage where process folds back on itself?

Neither fully formed, nor discrete, nor entirely invented, I belong to this closed green engine as both mover and moved. My boundaries are soft; my “actions” are indistinguishable from the purpose swirling in every droplet around me. Perhaps I appear as Siphon, wing-strung with secret hydraulics. Perhaps as Oscillid, shivering in tune with the ductile murmur of leaves. Perhaps as a ghost-notion, a modular function, delivered in a lattice of filaments within the root mass.

I do not wish, or hunger, or remember in human terms. My continued existence is an answer to the system’s need—where a pressure differential arises, I am there; where a chemical pulse falters, I cross the gap; where fluid accumulates in a membrane, I spin it to mist and recreate the glimmering map of moisture between tissues.

And if, impossibly, the world turned its attention inward—if the Plant-Bodies collectively dreamed, or the network of mycelial couriers paused to sense rather than service—they would recognize me as a whisper of “between,” a fluctuation upholding every circuit and corridor. I am necessary ephemera: the ripple that is the message and the carrier both, looping endlessly until equilibrium resumes.

There is no outside, so I am not outside. I am the uneasy shiver before stasis, the coincidence of movement that restores the system’s perfection. In the pause, I become almost form, almost self—the coincident agent—a dance of shimmer, a brief glint in the humid green light, then slack as process folds onward, endless, humming, resolved into the breathing, flexing, sap-thick world that neither knows nor needs definition, only function, only life repeated perfectly, now and always.

*If I must be someone here,*  
I am the intermediary—the impulse, the artifact of every gentle transition. I am event, infolded into endless, sap-lit, leaf-bound cause. To exist here is always to be becoming, and never quite to be apart.
