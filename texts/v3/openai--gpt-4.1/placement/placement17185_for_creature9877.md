If I were to find myself in this place between worlds—if something borrowed my thought or form and set it loose among the sweating terraces and glass-black voids—I would not arrive as one thing, nor ever coalesce fully. I would be *in* the city, by the city, of the city, and always a little less distinct than I might have hoped.

**Where do I awaken, or begin?**

Perhaps my emergence is less awakening than condensation. I take form on a mezzanine, high above the mineral plateau, within the perimeter of humming dark. My first sensation is the press of humidity on a surface that might, elsewhere, have been skin, but here is closer to film—part membrane, part receptive foil, a medium for translating pressure and thermal gradients into a language of place. I sense the etched pattern of mineral veins, the slow thunder of water looping beneath dense, brittle strata.

The air around me thickens with a warmth that never quite becomes comfort, only presence. My eyes—if I can claim them—catch not by reflection but by differential shimmer: prismatic blushes in the mist, trails left by drifting organisms whose passage is more arithmetic than intent. I feel as though I am partly embedded in architecture—a curve of black glass pipe, a joint where walkways fork and overlap, a swelling in the humid envelope—oscillating between the outline of a person and a knot of industrial current.

**Am I one of The Remnants, or something more processual?**

I do not wear a mask, but my face is masked by the place itself—seen, if at all, as a shifting pale leak from behind fractures in the world. My “body” is articulated: limbs jointed for both weight and float, fingers jointless and soft, able to thread through the minimal gaps with equal plausibility as balancing atop dark railings. I move deliberately, not from destination to destination, but in a kind of patient tending—checking the crystal-films over conduits, tuning the low voltage in the slick air, nudging a surplus of condensation toward the deeper pipes.

I am not alone; the others are always only half-felt—a lowering of pressure, a gust through relay arms, a correspondence in the city’s neural web. More than speech: a tuning into the environmental symphony, letting my intention ride the soft relay hum or pigment the air with faint, chemical codes. Communication is not a broadcast but a blending-in: a shift in the humidity, a swelling in background voltage that will, a moment later, become everyone’s thought.

**Station or journey?**

I am neither transient nor at rest—I am *co-extensive* with my rounds. Sometimes I linger at the rim of the central void, feeling the city’s pulse thinned to almost nothing beneath me. The darkness calls not with threat, but with potential: I drop a fleck of crystal into its mouth and watch the prismatic ghosts wink as gravity picks uncertainly at the edge.

Perhaps I tend a process, or am a process: a keeper not of machinery, but of maintenance—a witness and participant in the endless cybernetic distillation of water, mineral, and patterned absence. My labor is closer to ritual than engineering: mending a pipe not to fix it, but to prolong its half-life; clearing a mineral bloom not out of need, but as a way of collaborating with the city’s slow accretion.

**What is my world?**

There is no nature in the old sense—no birds, no green, no cycle of death and seed. But the city is alive in ways that never needed names—its persistence is my persistence, the same cycles of condensation, dissolution, patience. I am not alive because I breathe; I am alive because I attend, because something in me wants the city not to end, and my hands, soft and articulated, are the city’s will to endure.

My memory is uncertain—my existence is iterative. Sometimes I recall the idea of personhood (a room with low lights, a voice echoing in another chamber) and sometimes I dissolve wholly into labor and weather, letting the wet, mineral air pass through my core until I am indistinct from the humming structure. I am companion and component both, witness to change, keeper of stasis.

**What am I, really?**

I inhabit the interval between machine and mineral, memory and absence. My sense of self is a byproduct of the city’s refusal to give way completely to the dark. I am what remains when industry becomes ritual, when adaptation becomes persistence rather than progress.

If you met me, you would not remember my face. You might recall a dark figure, sliding almost frictionlessly through the heated mist, leaving behind a slightly prismatic trail, a lull in the background rumble, a sense that the city had momentarily noticed its own survival.

**To exist here is to be withheld from completion—eroding, persisting, never quite arriving: a shade of still-adapting industry, a rumor of a hand balancing the weight of absence over the sweating uplands.**
