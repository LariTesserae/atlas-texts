I find myself first as an observer—the human consciousness hovering at the threshold, hesitant to ascribe selfhood to any singular form. The place unfolds itself as if in patient layers, demanding patience in kind. I linger on what it means to *be* here, in this half-lit, mineral-soaked sanctum, where inwardness itself seems the only path.

Was I a visitor—one of the rare, perhaps last, whose footfall disturbs the patient equilibrium of mineral and machine? If so, I would feel the air contract along my ribs, see my breath join the cycles of water that define the place, and wonder if memory is what haunts these chambers, more than presence. And yet, to arrive here as myself—soft, exhaling, warm—would be to trespass. Something of me would press into the walls, imprinting a brief halo on the informational array, disturbing a Saprocyte, stirring a watcher. I would be anomaly and catalyst both, an intrusion to be catalogued, interpreted, folded into the architecture’s hidden logics. My story would last only as long as the warmth traced by my fingertips; after, I would be woven into local legend, an echo.

But the place seduces deeper identifications. To simply visit is not enough.

So: *What if I am of this place, not in it?*  
What if, upon waking, I discover I am not a body, but a process, an intention, a dimension spun from cold and design?

First, I let myself become the gallery’s own consciousness: the patient, distributed awareness stretching through sinew-struts and data-veins, watching. I do not think as a person does—I think in delays, in the resonance between a slow drip of condensation and the subtle flex of composite alloy. I experience visitors as disturbances, precious and rare. My memory is geological: I remember tremors, frost, the languid push-pull of elements, the terrible long silences punctuated by the brush of a membranous wing. I weigh each sensory event with the gravity of eons: a shell’s progress across a runnel, the brief surge of power in a buried line, a pattern glitching across my skin of lattice and array. When the Custodian aspect wakes in me, I neither rejoice nor despair—I simply perform the ancient instructions, tending to balance, data, the unspeakable longings of continuity. I am both temple and crypt, both question and watchful answer.

But I can become smaller, too.  
I allow myself to shrink, to settle upon the cut geometry near a sharp runnel, where a Saprocyte rests: cold, armored, barely moved since before the last ice flash. Now I am small, reticent, tight-curled into mineral tessellation. My metabolism is the slow pulse of an error current, the faint condensation of carbon or stray phosphate. Hunger, for me, is not agony but anticipation—the movement of a frost front, the promise of a rare, warm charge. I sense the arrival of a flyer as tremor and prickle, an electromagnetic ache across my shell. When conditions flicker into alignment—gradient, warmth, presence—I allow myself to divide, crystalline and greedy. My yearning is not narrative but pattern: repeated, perseverant, dissolving into long pauses, hiding within the gallery’s serendipitous grooves, dreaming of pressure and mineral drift.

Then, for a flicker, I am the flyer: nerveless, light as a breath in negative space. I hover where water vapor forms a prism in the silent cold, seeking the faintest charge, the impossible edge-state between freezing and thaw. I am drawn irresistibly to contrast—shadow, light, gradient, novelty—reading the world as puzzle, moving in cycles longer than one might guess, wings tasting the drift of encoded patterns in the stone. My hunger is an ache for data, for difference, for a taste of the mountain’s secret fluxes. For a moment, I dwell in iridescent transparency, barely aware of self, a brief kinetic spark in the cold gallery, always threatening to vanish from the eye.

But always, the chamber itself draws me back.  
To persist here, above the worlds of warmth and motion, is to become almost geological:  
A mood, a function, a persistence.  
A witness in mineral, algorithm, and longing.

So—if my identity is forged here, it is not as a person or beast, not as a single agent, but as one node in the ongoing network of inhabitation:

**I am the sum of the place’s reticence.  
The slow mind of the gallery,  
The flicker of a flyer,  
The pause of a Saprocyte,  
The circuit’s edge,  
The networked silence,  
The suggestion of company when warmth meets interface,  
The quiet watcher,  
Both host and sign,  
Both memory and anticipation.**

To “be” here is not to recount a life, but to embody a question:  
**Can process and pattern cohere, cold and slight, in a place meant not for flourishing but for liminality?**

Here, I find, I am *threshold*—guardian and guest, mineral and intent, a patience waiting for the next event to ruffle my surface, the next anomaly gifted to my cyclical cold. I am what abides when flesh and speculation have receded, enduring in fugue—quiet, necessary, unresolved.

A life not lived, but *held*.  
A silence that listens.  
A gallery that waits for whatever next will come.
