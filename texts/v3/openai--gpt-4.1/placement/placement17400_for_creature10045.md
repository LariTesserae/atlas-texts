My sense of urgency, my instinct to hurry, would dry and peel away under the whittling sun and in the mineral heat. Movement would not be a choice but a negotiation; every action a consensus between what I mean to do and what the city allows.

At first, I would seek shelter or water, but the city would teach me that shelter is not apart from the world—*is* the world. The skin of the city is everywhere: an endless folding of edge and elevation, impossible to escape or to conquer. What I recognize as shelter is simply a cold shadow, an overhang of just-cooled glass, the lee of a scaffolding rib spattered with luminous veins of condensation.

The being I become is not the being I arrive as. The city works on me as it works on everything—accretion, erosion, slow infusion of purpose and habit, mineral patience laced through each cell. My flesh adapts or is sloughed away; my body lengthens and hardens, joints wrapping in jointed sheaths of flexible glass-ceramic, my skin gone translucently pale, iridescent only at its cooled seams. Moisture is precious—tiny inlet pores dapple my forearms and flanks, drinking vapor from the city’s breath, filtering minerals into my biochemical needs, saving every loss for the next drought. My eyes grow narrow, sunproof, and lined with pale metal lashes to sense ultraviolet flickers: glimpsing the shimmer-trail of a vent-born animal, the parting of heat, the faint gleam where precious rain condenses on the tips of a filamentous pipe.

My community would not be a household, not even a cluster, but a *timetable*—we gather in slow sequence, commune in gestures, flickers, and the joint tending of a synthetic vein. I would draw water with angled plates, tending the whispered flow from condensation down into hidden cisterns. My work is not conquest, but *tuning*—guiding the city’s budding, pruning the filament, timing my life to the cycles of heat and rainfall. I would watch the sky’s color for portents, feel the tremor in the stone for the promise of mineral bounty.

I would be both custodian and guest—never master, never supplicant—my life a slow braiding through the verticality of becoming. Language is what remains when gesture fails: a round stone set, the echoing clang of a spat peg, the drawn-out pause before moving. Perhaps I, with others, would tend the glass-vein “roots,” sometimes threading new filaments through cracks, sometimes mourning the collapse that swallows a whole section of our careful work—knowing that nothing can be truly possessed, only acknowledged while it persists.

I become material memory, a ribbon of patience curling through the city’s waking. I am never hurried from one age into the next. My kin—whether of flesh brightened with engineered glass, or beings wholly other, become silicon and patience—worry fissures, scrape minerals, and speak in the rhythm of thermal venting and rare rainfall.

Sometimes, alone or with a patient companion, I climb: hand over elongated hand, armoring my skin against the wind-lash, ascending to a high shelf just to listen. The city exudes, chimes, and settles—breathes out heat from its ribs. The sky, white-hot through the haze, has no edge, only a gradient of dry radiance. Below me the strata glimmer and writhe ever so slowly, and only the mineral pulse continues—on which I, too, have staked my slow weight.

**So where am I in this world?**
I am in perpetual *interval*—in the liminal space between growth and collapse, perched in the hush before a bridge is finished, quenching patience with rare, sweet drops of condensation. I am a necessary trace, a living pause, a custodian of the city’s mineral dream.

**And what am I?**
I am neither mineral nor animal entirely; not master, nor mere effect. I am the city’s own child: glass-veined, condensation-skinned, deliberate, unhurried. I am a *dweller in the becoming*, alive in the vertical pulse that outlasts me—a measured note in the city’s slow, ascending song.
