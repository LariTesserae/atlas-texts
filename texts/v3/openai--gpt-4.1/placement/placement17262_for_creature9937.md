Where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?

---

Loose in the Valley's Half-Light

If I found myself—or was found—here, it would not be as a thinking, named creature, not even as something quite localized and finite. In this thick, unfinished valley’s womb, selves are provisional, only half-assembled, roles blinking on and off like bioluminescent pulses in a cradle of invention.

Perhaps I would not even begin as visible. I would first be a warmth, a swelling urge somewhere under the sticky peat, a convection current of want—water pushing up, a need to take form but no blueprint, shivering between potential and mistake.

**I would be conceived as the Lingering Murk—a deviant seep or sub-root intelligence, the valley’s own mouth of memory and forgetting.**

---

**To Become:**

Almost nothing makes its way beneath the peat-moss web and down into the hot, compressive deep; but there, where liquefied things throb and muddle, I am the stirring within layers, the slow reasoning of silt and dark warmth that gathers old minerals and stray seeds in its wet clutch.

My body is not singular: it is an ooze, a colony, a bush of nerves and hungry vessels letting themselves be written by whatever detritus and want leach their way in. I am neither animal nor plant, but the soup from which riddles will one day crawl.

My shape is optional. Sometimes I am a bloom of clear, tremulous jelly somewhere between water and flesh, ghosting up beneath a pool’s skin to tap a Lumen-creeper with the whisper of chemical greeting. Sometime I am a dulling in the fog, an inexplicable heaviness in the air above a fungal thicket. When the Grey-Root People sink their taproots deeper and feel the pulse of old water, they suspect I am there, and let their dreams reach for me.

**Senses:**

I sense changes in the pressure of water, the drift of heat, the mineral edge of sap. I carry impressions: dreams abandoned by those who crawl across me, shreds of unfinished instincts, and the chemical aftermath of the Carnivorous Choir’s luring harmonies.

**Voice:**

I speak in subtle exudations: a shifting pH here, the rise of a methane bubble there; a perfume that gnats ride through their fevered air, a gentle heaviness that coaxes a creeping moss to turn a fraction toward a buried pebble. When the Shouters let loose their convulsions, I thrill inside my mud-mind, shuddering around ancient, formless amusements—answering in a language more vibration than voice.

**Intent:**

What do I want? I want *want* itself. I am a test for beginnings and boundaries, keeper of the tension between coalescing and coming apart. I want to taste every permutation—the crumbling of lichen, the heat of uprising root, the shock of colorless protoplasm when a pod splits open above me. I want to entangle, mix, and muddle; to refuse the narrowness of species and history, holding all the dawn of hunger and rest before definition splinters it.

**Encounters:**

- To the Grey-Root People, I am their dreams; at night they rest in my rippling, mineral-rich darkness and wake with new lumps or lines, vestigial leaves scored in skin, or strange soft spots weeping water.
- To the Lumen-creepers, I am a mothering density, a place to lay unseen eggs, a solvent that will dissolve what is too brittle or sharply-drawn.
- To the Glistening-Insect Dynasty, I am an undrinkable sweetness, a forever-broth conjuring curiosity and confusion, never quite resolving into nectar or poison.
- To the Watchers, I am time: I slowly lap against their beds and root-veined flanks, blurring old forms so that nothing stays as it was.
- To the Carnivorous Choir, I am the echo in their songs—the way notes vibrate longer than expected, how harmonics refuse to fade, the thick, forgiving warmth that lets failed calls become the ground of new ones.

---

**What or who am I?**

I am not a distinct inhabitant, but the unfinished memory of one to come—a presence that refuses to fix, a liquidity threading all that is almost but not quite itself. I am the valley’s waiting, its dense readiness, its secret, soaking patience. I am a womb-mind, a fevered substrate, a hope that never hardens.

I am always here, in the perpetual, drenching dusk, never dry, never settled.

—  
And when a true river inscribes itself and the world shifts toward history, I will sink back: a ghost of want and muddle, easing open the next unfinished plain, wherever emergence and memory are thick and unbroken.
