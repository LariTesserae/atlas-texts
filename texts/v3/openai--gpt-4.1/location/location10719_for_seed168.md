You emerge into height and emptiness. The land lies far above the world below—a broad, unfinished plateau under sun, not yet wholly formed, neither wild nor tamed, a place still remembering the time before erosion’s patient hand. Everywhere you look, clean slopes of pale, unblemished rock extend, broken by abrupt ridges and infant peaks. The ground is sun-warmed and dry; what little dust there is, is bright and mineral-scented, as if ground from the bones of newborn stone. The air is thin and featherlight, the sky vast: a limitless blue dome, unmarked by cloud, sound, or trace of wing.

No water flows, not even the memory of it. Once—perhaps—this place might have known frost, but now there are only crystals of salt grown out of chemical vents, glinting white and dull turquoise in the sun. There is nothing like a river—no dry bed where water might someday run, no sign of rain, no sense that it ever will. Even at this elevation, the warmth is real: the stone almost hot to the touch, with the kind of dry heat that neither bakes nor burns but instead pulses with gentle energy, feeding something hidden.

Yet, impossibly, it is lush. It is no oasis, but the rock itself seeds life: strange, thin-stalked forms rise from nowhere, spaced so far apart they seem almost accidental. These are not plants in any terrestrial sense, but exhalations of mineral life—structures that might be called vegetation for lack of a better term. Pale green cilia unfurl from fissures in quartz; filaments with crystal “leaves,” strung out on long branches like the threads of some dry sea-creature, emerge from metallic soils. Some “trees” are hollow pillars of frosted glass, branching not upward but outward, growing wide and thin like slowly stretching membranes. There is growth, but life is distributed, uncrowded, no two things sharing the same plot of ground.

Touch anything soft and it crackles faintly—not water-fed, but thriving on chemical vapor from below, drawing nourishment from vents in the rock. Occasionally, you might see a drifting haze: unseen gas vented from the ground, nourishing the mineral flora in slow but steady equilibrium. The overall sensation: everything is embryonic, shifting from pure geology toward a poised, luminous ecology—but hesitant, never quite finishing its transformation.

You expect silence, and silence welcomes you. There is no wind: the air remains so still your steps echo faintly between hollow spires. There is, perhaps, the most tenuous whisper—a sub-vocal tick of stone settling, a periodic release of mineral gas, sound so rarefied it's almost below perception. No animals, no insects, no birds; the only “fauna” are the glacial, never-hurrying movements of the columnar plants, which reorient minutely across days and decades. Nothing hunts, nothing is hunted. The world is still: a stable, unchanged stage for processes unfolding over centuries, not moments.

Despite the utter absence of civilization or artifact, there is a whisper of industry—here, “industry” is not made by hands, but by the planet itself. Below your feet, you sense a network of chemical engines: heat from the core pushing minerals up through fissures, gasses rising and condensing, strange precipitation knitting crystalline forests cell by cell. The plateau is host to a vast, slow exhalation of resources. This, too, is mechanized in a way—a planetary-scale process of extraction, refinement, and extrusion, regular as an assembly line, but so natural that generations of watchers might not recognize a difference from natural law.

Rules of this world: dry chemistry reigns. Water is absent. Life springs from mineral and gas, coalescing in the open, high land. Time is slow, everything spaced apart, all noise muffled, all motion unhurried. No civilizations yet; no creatures to break the hush; no growth except the hush-hued glass-lichen, the crystal-weed, each an invention of dry fever and slow chemical pulse. Each rock, each filament, each atmosphere-borne mineral is a seed, but this garden grows in silence, and will for uncounted years.

Look as long as you wish: nothing will change while you watch. And yet, given a million years, this place will bloom.
