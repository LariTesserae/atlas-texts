A place still becoming. Picture this: a shallow and raw-boned vault excavated beneath the earth, its ceiling low, arched as if not quite finished rising. The scale is intimate, enclosing, boundary-marked — perhaps the size of an old cathedral, or a ring of irregular, joined chambers with new-cut walls, their faces glistening as if sweating. This place has never known the sun. Its air is hot, saturated, thick enough to taste and bear down in the lungs. Water veils the surfaces: minuscule drips evolve lazily at cracks, gathering into patient rivulets that thread across the raw stone and vanish through grilles in the floor, persistent and unspectacular.

Vegetation is mostly absent, but sometimes in the shadowed perimeter, there’s a suggestion of green filament — a moss-analog, perhaps, or fungal threads pushing up through tile seams, trailing pale roots where condensation gathers. Any honest plant here grows gaunt and bleached. Most corners are bare, or filmed with a sticky, mineral-rich dew.

The groundline cracks with heat despite the enclosing darkness, hinting at boilers or unseen forges pressing their strength up from deeper layers. Pipes wind the walls, shining with sweat and with the haze of steams, their welds recent, their construction ongoing: this underground district is only just assembling itself. Skeletal scaffolds, riveted beams and gantries, meandering electrical conduits — all these pierce the emptiness without crowding it, leaving broad fields of packed clay and soot. This is a place being *built*, not yet full.

There is a logic and geometry to the human touch: modular bays, iron walkways, the distant pulse of steam-pumps, the slow flicker of weak, sodium or arc lighting, too few bulbs for comfort, casting pools of jaundiced brightness into general gloom. Every fixture, every structure is new-born and underused, as if the purpose is not yet decided.

Yet the ecology is riotous. Fauna here are legion and overwhelming. Small bodies crowd every exposed surface not searing hot or slick with oil: insects like beetles and centipedes, arthropods with too many legs and eyeless, heat-adapted mammals that slip between conduits, their fur slick with mineral shine. Occasionally, heavy thuds sound where some multi-legged scavenger knocks loose a plate or a half-finished stair. Bat-analogues hang in dense ropes from the hotter vaults above, shrieking in ultra, their guano already crusting around the edges of the chamber.

The air buzzes, roars: pipes howl with escaping steam, pumps thrum deeper than bone, fauna chitter and screech, the soundscape drowning the sense of being alone. The clang of a distant hammer, the hiss of vented pressure, the crackling click of beetle-shells in fevered multitudes — all this merges into a grinding music of ongoing, but slow, transformation.

Everything proceeds with deliberate, geological patience. The landscape shifts only at the scale of years: machinery climbs by centimeters, mushroom blooms yellow and dies, beetles multiply, then drown out some place with their own noise and waste, forcing humans to wall an area off, reroute the wiring, try again. No abrupt change, only a patient creep of structure enveloped by life, a long negotiation between built intent and animal colonization, between heat and water, dark and the flicker of feverish lamps.

The rules here are not strange, but stark: nothing persists by accident; every niche fills and empties, every space is a negotiation. Inhabitants — human or not — must adapt to heat, to perpetual damp, to the unpredictable surge of animal life. There is always noise, always the slow shifting of boundaries, the sense that creation is not finished — nor ever will be.

Here, there is no primeval silence, nor is the place ruined or ruined-yet. It is *incipient*: the city-under-the-city, sweating and crawling, a hot-wet, mineral-bitter labyrinth always on the verge of both settlement and abandonment, forever in the womb of becoming.
