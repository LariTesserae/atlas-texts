Let’s take stock, and let it grow:

There is air here, cool and clear, and dazzling light—sharp at this elevation, with shadows carved by brilliance and utter clarity. You might think, at first, that you stand on an alpine plateau, but let go of the idea that such places require snowmelt, or that life here must depend on water drawn from hidden streams or vanishing dew. This is a place that has already forgotten water—a world that has lived dry, breathed dry, evolved dry for so long that every living thing is woven from minerals, or drinks the wind, or feeds on sunlight directly.

Above you, the sky is an unfamiliar cobalt, thinning at its highest points into almost-black, as this plateau never falls low enough to approach the breathable comfort of ordinary mountains. The elevation is continental—a high world upon a higher world, a desert in the sky, but one spacious as oceans, horizon folding into itself, rolling in gentle undulations, the bones of ancient continents ground and rearranged over geological ages.

Here, erosion is etched deep: long ridges of pale, friable stone, exfoliated and planed by epochs of wind, shaped not by rain but by persistent, dry abrasion. Plates of mineral crust crack and tumble down slopes, accumulating in flaky drifts. Ravines and gullies line the plateau, sharp-edged and abrupt, their sides varnished with time and atmospheric chemistry—streaked with greens, blues, strange metallic grays.

And yet, the land is not bare: sparse, yes, but not empty. Something lush unfolds, but this lushness is in the persistent, remarkable colonization of life forms not anchored by water at all.

The vegetation is overwhelming in its adaptability, not in its density: each organism, spaced far apart, forms its own oasis of vitality. Great pillar-like trunks of silicate or carbonate material—translucent, twisting—rise out of the stony soil, bristling with fronds or spines designed to capture wind-borne nutrients, or perhaps to harvest trace elements from the thin, mineral-rich fogs that sometimes pass. Lush, here, means each presence is elaborately alive, built to thrive where truly little is given.

Between these luminous trunks, mats and cushions of crystalline growths cluster—low, glassy, leaf-like structures that refract the sunlight and shiver at the smallest breath of atmosphere. Some are web-like, stretching ribbons that span gaps between high outcrops, their surfaces shimmering, almost iridescent, responsive to the angle of the light.

No streams thread through this world, no pools reflect that radiant sky; all moisture is rare, locked in chemistry rather than flowing free. From time to time, a dry wind gusts through, carrying micron-sized particles—nutrient, hazard, or seed, depending on the life that intercepts them.

Fauna are rare—scant traces, soft movements. Occasionally, in the quiet, a soundless shimmer will shift behind a pillar, or a flicker of movement will reveal a mineral-bodied insect or a drifting, gossamer organism hovering in the gentle updrafts, crystalline wings trembling but almost voiceless. Larger creatures are scarce: perhaps a six-legged form will appear, camouflaged against the blue-white stones, only to vanish in the labyrinth of mineral trunks.

The silence here is profound, but never absolute. From time to time, the wind sings, keening over stone and through the latticework vegetation, producing harmonics that travel great distances: a music of minerals, faint but clear in the thin air. At intervals, a crash or distant rumble as crust shifts or pillar falls, echoing out into the luminous emptiness.

Beneath it all, the world is busy: mineral roots exchange elements, organisms signal one another through subtle chemical clouds, predator and prey act out an intricate, mineral-bound dance—growth and grazing, hiding and hunting, cycles slow by our standards but brisk in the context of such a frugal ecosystem.

There are no ruins, no footprints, no artifact or tool—if intelligence exists here, it is constrained by its materials and rewrites every rule of biology or physics known elsewhere. Rules here bend toward balance with absence, flourishing with almost nothing, finding lushness in space, light, and mineral alone.

You could wander for leagues and see only a handful of organisms, each its own miracle of design, painted with the palette of an alien chemistry—a world entirely itself, unmade and ongoing, sharply refracted in the high, cold, mineral-bright brilliance above the sky.
