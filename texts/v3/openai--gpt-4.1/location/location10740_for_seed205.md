The world opens in a haze of heat and a thunderous, living roar.

**TERRAIN**  
You crest a ridge, legs prickly from the warm mist that’s always clinging, and stare down into impossible scale: a broad, rolling upland, as if a plateau were stretched taut with tension—utterly massive, not jutting with peaks but undulating so wide that distance dissolves and land becomes sky. Yet there is no earth you see, not truly—**what you walk upon is all of it alive**.

Every surface is *vegetation*. Plant fills the breadth and depth, the substance and the skeleton of things. There are towering, columnar forms like trees at a thousand times familiar scale, but with trunks coiling into broad, sinewy bridges and fan-palms as big as argosies arching overhead. Vines the girth of riverboats wind from their heights, trailing silvery, beaded leaves like mobile curtains that twitch with animal life.

Yet this is not a jungle. It is **open**, **spacious**. Each grand organism is set apart, gallery-like. The ground is not a layer of soil but a dense, springing mesh of rootworks and thick, fibrous mats; at your feet: kilometers of tangle, yet between the titans, there is vastness—a void of open air, distances that dwarf cathedrals. The plateau’s substrate is plant, but given form by weather, time, and its own indelible will.** Old, mighty trunks arch and bulge where floods or winds have reshaped them, surfaces grown leathery, scarred, and smooth by millennia of weather. In gullies and hollows, not soil but spongy mats collect pools—standing water from rain or condensation, shimmering in the heat.

**MOISTURE & HEAT**  
There is little running water—no rivers, rarely a trickle—but **pockets** and **veins** and **hollows**, low-lying places or crooks of vast boughs, brim with humidity. Rain falls sometimes, quick and loud, running in great beads down ridged bark and leafy slopes, vanishing as mist as soon as it hits the burning ambient air. *The air is hot, thick, and intermittently wet—sometimes oppressive, sometimes merely warm steam.*

**SOUND**  
The **sound** is an ocean: not the silence of forests, nor the hush of desert, but an unending clatter and shriek of living things. Segmented, carapace-clad forms scuttle in torrents over roots and bark. Chitinous wings beat like drums. Giant insects and scaled, many-limbed things warble, bellow, and clash. There are predatory cries—deep, throaty ululations, like foghorns, that roll across kilometers. All of it: overlapping, competing, at scales from the microscopic shrill to the orchestral. Even the plants participate—trembling, shifting, rattling their immense leaves as sudden gusts, or the passage of something vast, move through them.

**FAUNA**  
Life is not just prolific but **overwhelming**, **dominant**. Among the plants (and often, inside them), the animal world is riotous. Multilimbed, sometimes translucent, sometimes armored, the animals are as varied as they are loud—the size of mice, bears, wagons, and beyond—specialized to the arboreal, the aerial, the pocket-pools and sky-bridges. Some flowerlike creatures hover, trailing stingers or nets. Massive sloths of moss, or six-eyed camouflaged predators, move with uncanny slowness in the open air—then vanish, sudden as lightning. You see carcasses, sometimes, fallen through the fibrous mats: a moldering bloom, a skeleton gnawed by hundreds, disappearing in days.

**ARCHITECTURE & PEOPLE**  
But here and there—so rarely they surprise—you find the faint **marks of sentience**. A stilted platform grown from entwined roots; a single, small structure: woven from sinew and vine, perhaps a shelter or lookout, perched high for survival. No cities—only scatterings. Tools: bone, stone, and occasional hammered copper, left near ancient water-pools or in the shadow of the largest trunks. Simple figurines carved from seed shell, with stylized shapes of something—maybe even plant, not animal—held in reverence.

**ATMOSPHERE**  
Above, the sky is open, but always tinged with haze—the light **usual**, bright enough, but filtered through vapors. Weather moves in slow, gentle cycles; storms sweep sudden, friction sparks setting off blooms of spore and color as whole swathes of canopy flower in heat and rain.

**DYNAMICS & RULES**  
This place **breathes**—not as a forest, but as a living, shifting network. The plant-mass is old, balanced but never quiet—sculpted by cycles of growth, predation, scavenging, and death. There is no true dirt—the notion of soil is replaced by decomposing vegetal substrate, the air thick with rich, green scents but never stifling. Fauna keep to high trails and canopy, mostly avoiding the hot, open voids between trunks.

Here, the **laws** seem exaggerated: distances grow the longer you look; scale confuses, with tiny pockets supporting epic drama and monsters silent in the next shadow. Everything is in **motion:** predator and prey lope, leap, or fly; structures built by hand are temporary, always threatened by weather and noise. Time feels different—stretched thin, then compressed in hurricanes of noise and movement.

**WEIRDNESS**  
It is a kingdom not of plants or animals but of the in-between: shapes and cycles and interactions that never resolve, surface and form and process that never stop being strange. From some vantage, it is beautiful; from others, a sustained, thunderous challenge. But it is always alive—with a violence and plenty that you feel on your skin as well as your eardrums. And even in a world made from living things, **emptiness lingers**: vast, vibratory spaces between presences, between each thunder-crash of life. Even here, you realize, room is made for something else.
