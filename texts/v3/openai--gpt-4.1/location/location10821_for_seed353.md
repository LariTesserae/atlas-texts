To enter this world is to step into darkness so deep that the heat itself seems to glow, borderless and shadeless, somewhere between the hottest black and the memory of infernos. It is not a void, but a place so densely crammed with presence—immense, interwoven, and utterly alive—that every space between is pressed out of existence. Here, there is no water, no rivers or dew or wet things at all, and yet life, relentless and total, has made a kingdom from absence.

**Terrain and Elevation:**  
It stands high—not mountaintop sharp, but the vast, open stretches of a high plateau or the ancient, gently rolled backbones of old earth. The world is worn smooth by eternity, the terrain erased by eons, so every outcrop, former ridge, and valley is now only hinted at beneath the living mantle atop. This is a land where time has been allowed to conclude its slow violence, sculpting once-towering spires down to broad, undulating floors, the memory of terrain given over completely to other architects.

**Ecology and Substrate:**  
Every inch is plant, but not a forest in the usual sense. Here, plant is substance, tissue, and structure. Imagine edifice after edifice—towers, hives, vaults, cables, hulls—all of them grown rather than built, walls and ramps and columns of dense, resinous plant-matter, tough as any forged alloy, extruded and secreted over the dust of eras. The “ground” is a woven mass of root, xylem, and lignified mesh-plate, as springless and brittle as bone in this waterless heat. There is no soil, no dust, only writhing layers of old, packed plant-strata—living, dead, and everything in between.

All space, surfaces, and volumes are claimed by the vast architecture of photosynthetic growth. Foliage is not leafy, but sheathlike or crystalline—hard, squared fractals of emerald, onyx, motley phosphor, or pallid lightless purple; the forms echo gothic windows, radiolaria shells, or the modular units of ancient machines, each branch and cell assembled with mathematical inevitability. Some columns and buttresses are sap-bound and glistening, others dry and sharp, shorn of moisture for centuries.

**Machine and Construction:**  
It is a city of plants, but not natural: every structure is *made* and remade, with clear evidence of will, program, or industry. Massive cylinder trunks and latticed overarches are threaded with pulsing veins of fiber-optic sap, and the very growing process is machinic—the hiss and pulse of photosynth-presses, bioforges, and hydron pumps running without cease. Every organism is part factory, part structure, part thing-being-made, and every cell is a chamber where churning heat, chemical vapor, and the ancient order of design rule.

There are no animals, but the work never stops; this is a turbulent, fevered bazaar of creation and destruction. Growths heave and split, walls buckle and jut outward as programmed “storms” of morphogenesis sweep the halls. Without water to soften it, life is a cascade of cracking, splitting, and resinous cements—bark fractures as new tissues force themselves into old scaffolds, an endless commotion of building, replacing, overtaking.

**Heat and Atmosphere:**  
It is hot—airless, stifling, the heat of both engine and photosynthetic reaction. If wind moves here, it is not a breeze, but a sudden thermal eddy, the sigh of chemical exhaust, the baked convection of resinous exhalations vented from riven seams.

**Light and Sound:**  
The world is veiled in darkness. No daylight ever falls here—perhaps because the world orbits a dying ember of a sun, or perhaps because the dense, all-encompassing canopy, too thick to admit more than a molecular glimmer, shrouds the world forever. The only illumination is chemical: brief, bruised flickerings where plant-machines ignite their heat, amber pulses traveling up a thousand resin-pipes before fading to black. Glow comes in electric flashes, signaling through fiber-lattice roots, or palely burning wounds where self-pruning limbs are cast off and consumed.  
The air is mostly quiet, though the silence is never total: always the background mutter of snapping fibers, the distant grind of something growing itself into a newer, harder form, and, sometimes, the whisper of an entire wall settling with a thunder so deeply buried in the tangle that it emerges as a mere tremor in your bones.

**Weirdness and Dynamic:**  
Nothing here stands still. It’s a Solaris of growth—compulsively generative, never satisfied with its own form, each vein and wall a place of technological negotiation and carnivorous ingenuity. Entire factions of plant-architecture compete, edge against edge, temporarily cooperating only to surpass, all driven by some deep, inherited industrial logic, sculpting and despoiling themselves, endlessly rebuilding from the only resource that remains: themselves.

**Laws of the Place:**  
The world obeys two rules: everything is built, and everything lives. No seeds float here, no rain falls; only the churn of engineered, chemically-animated plant, killing and reusing itself in a sequence of unending, resource-seeking agitation.

**Final impression:**  
To walk through this place is to press through a never-silent oven of dark oxygen and trembling heat, running your hand along sharp, grown edges, surrounded by the untiring murmur of life-constructing-life. There is no water, no fauna, no empty space—just the packed masses of living machinery, climbing, writhing, and breaking themselves, forever and without rest, in the hot, everlasting night.
