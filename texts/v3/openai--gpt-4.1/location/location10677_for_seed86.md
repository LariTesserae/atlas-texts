You descend through a fractured throat of earth, a ragged sink in a vast, cool basin. Not cave, not quite valley — rather, it’s a sunken plain, wide and deep and broken only by the jagged faults of stone. Walls rise, plummeting abruptly from the meager upland, then kneel and sprawl into a broad, open underworld. The sky is nothing but a distant glow, its daylight faint and blue between overhanging ledges and the suspended damp of lingering mist — the place is dim, a world at dusk even at noon. Moisture beads on rock, runs down in spider-fine threads, pools in shallow pans among sharp, new stone: a dampness that is patchwork and fleeting, never abundant nor gone, always evanescent.

The floor is mostly bare earth and rock, the residue of some recent upwelling or collapse. Stone slabs, angular and undissolved by time, thrust from the floor: flagstones of a continent’s making, hard and unsheltered. In the fissures between, some thin, stubborn growths: huddles of reedy grass, pale and wiry, never forming a continuous turf, just tufts and scrabbly vines, brittle lichen writing cryptic messages across stone. Here and there a squat, grey bush clings with roots splayed, fighting for each drop of dew that condenses overnight.

The air is always cool, chill with the memory of earth’s shelter, never biting, but never warm, either. This underground prairie does not change. Condensation glistens in predawn, then forms droplets, never enough to make streams — only isolated runnels, slicking vertical walls, guttering down, gathering in hollows; the softest sound is the rhythmic plink, plink, plink, echoing off the stone. Sometimes rain drifts in slow, windless curtains through broken ceilings in the stone, feeding ephemeral pools.

Though the materials are raw — stone, water, bone-dry twigs, pale earth — the real substance of this place is life. Yet not greenery: no jungle, no riot of mosses. It’s the animals. They are everywhere. A cacophony of wing-beats, scrapes, howls and clickings rises into the still, unmoving air: birds or things that are somewhat like birds, with scattered, stunted feathers and uncanny beaks; insect swarms that flash dully in dim light, wings churning the mist into low clouds; strange, pallid mammals that scurry and caper about the rocks with unblinking, oversized eyes. Something in their numbers dominates the landscape; everywhere you look, you are watched by furtive shapes, clicked-at by countless voices, nearly overwhelmed by the press of animate presence.

And yet — the vastness, the empty stretches between. There are always long reaches of uninterrupted stone, silent but for the echoes. The animals move in great, shifting waves, but rarely occupy all the space at once; one valley alive with shrieking flight, the next a silence broken only by distant, repetitive hoots. Here: a stretch of barren slates; there: a cluster of burrows in the thin soil, from which pale, wiry snouts and twitching antennae emerge to test the air. Prey and predator alike, no sign of tools more complex than a gnawed bone or a nest of stacked twigs. Occasionally, a hammerstone lies near the mouth of a burrow, smoothed by use, left by a creature whose cleverness has not remade the world.

No roads, no markers, no enduring sign of hand or voice. The rules here are old, and uncontested. Life here is organized around an equilibrium so permanent it feels frozen: each niche occupied, each behavior endlessly repeating. Predators wait in the fixed shadow of a standing stone, year after year; prey learn the routes from water-pool to water-pool, those unchanging puddles that evaporate and reappear with mathematical dependability.

The soundtrack is ceaseless, layers upon layers — chitters, squawks, the rhythmic thud of a large creature’s walk echoing three crevasses away, a chorus of echoes swelling until it becomes less a noise than the pulse of the world. Above it all, the drip of water, always the drip, governing the pace and boundaries of all that lives.

Though weirdness does not emerge here, and time seems never to move, the place is stranger for being so ordinary: wildlife in unyielding abundance; moisture present only in isolated pockets and passing veils; stone in youth’s chaotic fracturing; air shot through with overlapping calls. Unsettling in its stability, this is a sunk world not yet worn smooth, teeming, dim, and endlessly alive, but never, ever changing.
