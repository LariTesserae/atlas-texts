You stand in a world unmoored from expectation: a high place — but there is no ground beneath your feet, only water. Not a placid lake, but a volume so charged and alive it shakes with deafening, tumultuous energy. It is as though you are at the prow of an oceanic cathedral, the very peak of a submarine mountain, or perhaps atop a lost mesa, yet the distinction between air and water has long since dissolved. Everything is submerged; you move through a heated tide that offers more resistance than air but less hostility than a true abyss.

The *water* is not uniform: it pulses, shifts, thickens. At times, it is as clear as crystal, light refracted into surging bands above and below; at others, it occludes itself with churning clouds or filamentous sheets. The *light* is dim; indigo and sea-glass green, never quite black, haunted by the infrequent suggestion of something luminous catching the fractal tumults. The sun is a myth, replaced by flaring gradients and the backscatter of unseen singularities.

This realm is *high* — you sense, through gradient in pressure and the ghost-wind of currents, that you are far above a denser, darker deep. The terrain’s “elevation” is conveyed not by sight, but by the feeling of gravity shifting oddly with each eddy, and the intimacy of boundaries — the sense you are in a great chamber that has only recently come into being, its edges sharp, its dropoffs abrupt, the world itself a newly-cast sculpture in water.

Everywhere there is *sound*: not the gentle conversation of rain or gentle surf, but a roaring, colliding chaos — sheets of invisible rain, echoing caverns of turbulence, rumbling tones, sudden snaps like stones cracking, the shriek of water accelerated to razors. At other times, the noise coalesces strangely, forming not-music: a harmony one moment, then a cacophony, then a rhythmic, incomprehensible speech. Silence does not live here.

Vegetation is nearly *absent*. At intervals, you spot something living: a translucent tendril floating, briefly catching the faintest light; a bulbous pod anchored to unseen geometry, pulsating with bioluminescence, then gone in the riptide; the suggestion of a stony stalk, more mineral than plant, that flexes just once before the turbulent stream erodes it into nothing. Perhaps these are the only survivors of some older, more temperate epoch, or perhaps they are dreams the environment is having, half-formed and unable to persist.

You discover *marks* of something built — perhaps shaped as the precipitate of the chaos, not true buildings, but remnants, geologic architecture, pillar-like bones of calcium and black glass grown overnight. A sacred geometry emerges, quickly erased: a hollow arc, a scattered line of aligned stones, a frame as if for a vanished archway. The water erases as quickly as it creates, shaping memory and artifact in one gesture. No toolmarks, nothing finished; only the suggestion that an intention once passed through this vault, and was churned to fragments.

Life — *fauna* — is shy, rare. Once, a pair of eyes, reflecting back borrowed green light, vanish like coins in the vortex. Something immense and sinuous moves in silhouette, disrupted by the next surge. Here and there, clusters of tiny, nearly formless motes pulse in unison, riding currents as if they were highways, briefly bright, then gone before you can focus.

Rules are *unstable*. At times you can swim, at others the water’s density is such that you almost walk upon it, steps trembling on unseen currents; sometimes, gravity pinches and folds, and you must huddle, holding fast to nothing, the world twisting as the next pulse rearranges cardinal direction. Sound and light follow the lead of the turbulent energy, mutating, shaping the very nature of perception. Boundaries — the edge of your “room,” the outline of your “platform” — seem to shimmer and flicker, retreating and then springing into new existence. There is no horizon, only zones of thickening pressure and dissolving light.

Atmosphere is a thing you feel in the bones: charged, impossibly vital, electric with movement, but not comfortable. The constant thunder of water is endless, bone-shaking; it presses thought out, replacing it with raw sensation, urgency, awe. Occasionally you glimpse the birth of something new — a fresh filament extending, a pillar swirling into existence — and just as suddenly, the collapse, the return to chaotic nothing.

Here, *weirdness* reigns: physics on holiday, time unstable, matter and energy in perennial negotiation. The only certainty is change, the ceaseless song of water creating and unmaking, a roaring cathedral at the top of the drowned world, only briefly yours before the forms all turn to surge again.
