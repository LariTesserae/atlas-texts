The place is a high, broad, freshly-formed plateau—a geologic newborn, as if lifted recently from the world’s crust, its surfaces sharp but already acquainted with the burden of water. The land stretches, a region-sized tabula rasa, where distance is always visible and presence is scattered, never thick. Breathable openness reigns: the atmosphere is clear, the horizon is near and far at once, the scale midway between the intimacy of a village basin and the sweep of a broad upland chain.

**Terrain & Water**

The ground is stony, its bones pale and young—jagged edges and unworn facets, a land untroubled by centuries of softening rain. But the water is here, everywhere: the stone weeps thin sheets over its faces, collecting in shallow pans and runnels that traverse the plateau. Pools gather in sudden dimples; the traces of yesterday’s deluge linger on the surface in mirrored patches. Mist seeps from the land at dawn and dusk, condensing and evaporating in a cycle imperceptibly slow, making the air gentle and humid, yet never oppressive. Edges between mere dampness, shallow sheets, and solid stone shift gently but rarely; the water is always present, patient but unmoving.

**Vegetation**

Against the flood and on the margins of pools, plants have found their place, but with restraint. Here and there, broad-leaved stalks—deep green, almost waxen—rise from stone cracks and the softer earth that collects in hollows. Mosses and creeping ground-cover flank the runnels like ribbons, their density nowhere overwhelming, but always present where water lingers longest. Here and there, robust shrubs arch, their roots clutching at the high mineral content, their presence scattered by the newness of the surface and the spareness of the soil. Everything that grows here is lush in form and color, yet sparse in number—a flowering, but one that minds its distance.

**Atmosphere, Sound, Light**

The air carries a warmth that never truly leaves; the sun visits with steady illumination, casting shallow but distinct shadows around each stone, each stalk. The wind is nearly absent, muting the world. Sound is rare: only the quiet patter of condensation falling as new droplets, the soft burble where water finds a steeper path, the distant rill of overflow echoing down to unseen, unreachable valleys. The world here is never truly silent, but it never rises above a gentle hush. No bird or animal disturbs the open air; there is only the quiet dialogue of water, plant, and stone.

**Settlement & Human Presence**

Scattered across this plateau are clusters of primitive structures—town-sized settlements, but never crowded together, their boundaries respectful of each other, divided by pools and bands of saturating green. Everything is built by hand: low walls of slabbed stone, roofs thatched with the broad-edged leaves, elevated walkways bridging waterlogged ground or spanning sharper crevices. The tools are simple: stone hammers, lacquered wood for digging and cutting, perhaps copper used sparingly, shaped in direct proportion to what can be worked by hand.

Paths connect settlements—unpaved, but trodden stone, their routes winding by necessity around the pools and fresh escarpments. Central spaces—maybe assembly grounds, open-air workshops, places for quiet gathering—reflect the order and balance of their builders, always on ground a little higher, a little drier, always with space left between each habitation as if in deference to the land’s rawness.

**Rules & Ecological Order**

This world is in equilibrium, unchanging. The rock will not erode for ages yet; the water finds its persistent, self-renewing course, neither flooding nor receding. No animal walks the land—no birds, no insects—so the work of pollination must fall to wind, to the water, or perhaps the careful hands of those who live here. The plants grow in slow, steady cycles, never encroaching much upon each other, never deadened by drought or frost. Each settlement persists, generation after generation, without ambition to sprawl.

**Sense & Mood**

It is a land of fresh beginnings, bounded calm, a permanence that feels as if it was always meant to endure. Quiet attends every act—human voices, when present, are low, nearly subsumed by water’s dialogue, blending into the soft hush that defines the plateau. There is room for every presence; no one is crowded nor alone. Time does not press forward here; the newness of the land seems suspended, a world where things have just begun and might always stay that way, still, open, washed by stone-born water, green with the memories of rain.
