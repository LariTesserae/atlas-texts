Here is a place where light never truly touches down, not sun nor fire nor even bright starlight — darkness suffuses all, thick and without edges. Anything seen is guessed, never known; the air is warm as a heavy woollen shroud, the sense of warmth more felt on skin than seen in any reaction of the world around. The ground is low, lying close to some unseen planetary datum, perhaps a basin or a vast shallow depression, and there is no sense of drama in the rises or falls, just the certainty of lowness. There is no running water, no trickle of hidden springs, no scent or humidity, no evidence of the shaping force of fluid — only the slow press of aeons has lent this place its forms.

The terrain is mineral—smooth stone and broad plates of a pale, soft, easily-weathered rock, pocked and pitted, sometimes dusted with an odd, clinging powder that creeps into seams and depressions, accumulating without ever drifting or blowing. The forms are settled, worn-down to rounded humps, flat shelves, and gentle inclines, like the interior of a vast fossilized tongue or the bedding planes of some lost canyon; everywhere is a hush, a breathless waiting.

Sparse vegetation punctuates this stone field, but not as leafed plants or trees. Here, the living things are few and widely spaced, emerging from the rock almost as if extruded. They resemble thick, rubbery spines or columns, faintly glimmering with a dark, waxen glossy sheen at any chance of stray phosphorescence, each isolated from the other by a respectful interval. They sprout single or in clumps of two or three, their surfaces ridged or pebbled, always looking inward at themselves rather than stretching out. Their color is impossible to tell in the dark, but if you feel them, they are warm and pliant, exuding the dry scent of something surviving on little but dust and chemical hope.

The atmosphere is close, motionless, and sound is a memory — only the faintest shifting of powder on stone, or the small, impossible click of expansion or contraction when the rock surrenders a sliver of itself to time. No wind stirs, and certainly no animal moves through this place — there are no insects, not even the patient waiters of a real desert, no scurrying, no birds, nothing with a nervous system but what little primitive flora still abides.

If there were rules or ecology here, they would concern slow patience and the minimum expenditure of energy. These plants, if they are plants, do not “grow” as we know it — perhaps they accumulate mass only when rare mineral fogs drift through, or perhaps they metabolize the slight warmth in the stone. They do not need water, or else have outgrown the need in the callousness of time. They do not bloom or fruit or die with season — for, here, there is no season, and night is endless. Their biology might speak more to mineral or synthetic life; their roots are holdfasts drilled down into ever-drier stone, and their metabolism one of waiting for chance molecular offerings: atoms stripped from bare rock and slowly assimilated into new growth at the apex, so slowly that the form remains essentially static to any creature with a life lived in sunlight.

The scale is humble, so that after a short walk in darkness — your hands perhaps brushing the next waxen column to orient yourself, stepping around their wide bases — you would sense boundaries: the edge of this mineral room, the subtle rise where bedrock shelf meets a discouraging wall of solid matter. It is not an endless plain — think of a cathedral crypt, or the hollowness beneath a dry sea, a chamber neither natural nor shaped, but long-settled and complete. No sign of construction, no tool-marks or pits, and no artifacts hint that tool-users ever dwelt — here, the primitive “technology” is only that of the plants, perhaps producing primitive exudates to cement themselves to stone, or to cast off failed side-shoots now fossilizing nearby.

The darkness is perfect — not menacing, just elemental. No weirdness threatens, no unknown laws. Stability reigns in every dimension. The place gives the sense that it has been this way for longer than anything you know — that, barring catastrophe, it will remain as it is for epochs more, stalwart in emptiness and silence. Touch it, and warmth answers, touch a column and it yields slightly; listen, and only the rasp of your own breath or the shifting of powder will answer.

This is the hush of a world that has found balance and now simply abides, every absence and surface tension pressed gently into place, under a never-broken darkness.
