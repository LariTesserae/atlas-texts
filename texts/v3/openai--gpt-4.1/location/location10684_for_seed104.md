There are no horizons here—only gradients of suspended water, thick with life and memory. You float, or drop, or drift in a place that seems not so much to have depths as to be depth itself, the vertical dimension vast and intricate as the buried histories of worlds. Sun or star, whatever fuels warmth here, is a rumor: the light is sullen, diffused through churning particulate layers, its own colors lost to a shimmering, subtropical density. It is always raining, or rather, there is no “above” for rain to fall from; the whole world is an ocean, but not exactly oceanic, more like the atmosphere has surrendered all pretense and become water, pressure mounting gradually as you ascend rather than as you descend, the whole elevation schema inverted.

It is warm, sensuous, humid to the core of being—evidently high up, though the distinction between surface and shadowy seam is academic. Perhaps once, epochs ago, this place held mountains; now, only their blunt, spherical hearts remain, smoothed into mythic pancakes by eternity. Slowly pulsing ridges, stones softened to nearly organic roundness, hold the tangled anchor-limbs of a vegetation never wholly out of the water, or in it: some unfurling stalks reach upwards until their fronded tips dissolve visibly into vapor, silently streaming into upper currents of the endless aqueous sky.

Vegetation is abundant—so much green and blue it stains the liquid dim—yet less like discrete plants and more a composite mat of rooted, floating, draping things: mats of jellylike algal slime, great veils of luminous plankton that slowly rise and drape in curtains, towers of braided vine-like structures whose petals rotate in slow, planetary sweeps. Things do not “grow” here; they increment, press, overwrite each other in stratified compositions that are at once immense forests and dense reefs, writhing around the softened relics of former highlands.

Sound is rare—a hush so complete your own presence becomes a contradiction. Sometimes, the soft crackle of shifting weed-mat, the distant whump of vegetative recession as a current stirs. Tiny bioluminescent motes swarm momentarily, casting pale green shadows, before vanishing into dusk.

Fauna are few and far between. The shock of seeing movement becomes an event: a translucent, jawless thing, not quite fish and not quite thought, threads its way through the fronds, leaving a wake of barely-palpable entropy. A silicate-armored crawler, ancient and monstrous but slow, traverses the smoothed slopes, disturbing nothing and announcing no claim.

You surrender to an atmosphere of oldness. Every surface, every root and folded gill, is nearly spent; geological features have become more suggestion than reality, boundaries between stone and plant and water blurring into forms that approach—but never quite merge with—each other. Erosion is the lifeblood: everything is worn down, dispersed, carried sidelong in widening boluses of slow turbulence.

Across the landscape are puzzles, artifacts: the odd hexagonal plate embedded in softened sediment, a single column rising only thumb-lengths above the waving growth, stippled with spirals; hints of design, but so time-sanded it is almost uncertain if anything here was ever “built” or if the mind keeps seeing intention in deep time’s random layering.

The rules of this world are less like physics and more like memories, processes carried over from before the flood, before dissolution. Weather is constant but ambiguous: flows and reversals of current, vertical jets and quiet undertows, internal waves pulsing vast so slowly that life itself suspects only rhythm, not change.

The world itself flags and tunnels at its limits, reality unreliable—the pressure feels inconsistent, the sense of direction blurred. Sometimes, as you move, you come upon yourself from a strange new angle, or you sense that distance and angle are shifting relative to some logic entirely vegetative, layered, or mnemonic rather than geometric. This is more than a submerged plateau or a drowned mountain-chain—it is the settled quiet after a cosmos-wide flood, in which density is not threat but inevitability, life clustering in peace with time, and the silence is not emptiness but the softness of all that is worn down, drifting, contoured to a kind of underwater memory.
