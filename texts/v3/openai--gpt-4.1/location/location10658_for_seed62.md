The **place** unfolds at a high elevation—a singular table-land, a sloping expanse of uplifted earth, young and full of raw intentions. The surface is knuckled and sharp-shouldered, where rock ridges rise as though the land were flexing, stretching itself upward with embryonic determination. Valleys lie shallow between the ridges, fresh scars where water trickles after rain, but mostly dries and withdraws to hidden pockets—clear pools in deep ground hollows, beads of moisture caught in crevices, a terrain of intermittent dampness.

Despite the dry-stretch, **vegetation is copious**—lush, almost overwhelming in its vigor. Moss sprawls thickly wherever stone remains cool and shaded, and ferns thrust themselves upwards beside swollen, waxy-leaved shrubs with saturated greens and blue-purples. Stems and leaves interlace, competing for space, pressing against one another with a layered density—one can never see the soil directly except at the bare peaks and the gouged gutters of newly formed gullies. Vines braid around boulders. Even in this brightness, with the sun pouring mercilessly overhead and throwing the shallowest shadow, dampness hides, just barely, beneath protective leaf and stone.

**Atmosphere** is clear and intense—high-altitude clarity, the sun magnified and brilliant, scattering cold luminescence despite the brightness, leaving the air cool and thin. The world glows, glasslike, but without heat. Vapors rise only where water pools and quickly vanish into dry air, caught in brief, glittering condensation. Light reflects in luminous halos around every wet surface, painting rainbow beads on moss.

**Silence** dominates here—no birdsong thrums through the grove, no insect chorus, no calls echo off stone. The dense vegetation muffles what little sound the sporadic wind carries. Once in a long while, the faint grind of pebbles shifts, or a bead of water falls from a leaf into a hidden pool, the sound small and secretive. Every other activity—growth, decay, formation—happens so slowly that it hardly registers to the senses except across seasons, years.

**Industrial remnants** dot the land. Not in clusters, but here and there: a rusted engine half-buried in moss, the skeletal rib of a transmission tower marking a distant ridge, a concrete spillway split by vines, its former purpose now unclear. Metal plates, and lengths of pipe vanish into knees of undergrowth, so coated in lichen and leaf that they seem almost organic—a latticework of the old world and the root, a strange and slow conversation. Sometimes, in the most unlikely places, whole walls of thick glass, fogged and tilted, reflect the forest with uncanny fidelity, hinting at lost observatories, power stations, or research posts. All the structures are quiet, seized by the brooding density.

**Fauna are rare.** Sometimes a flicker at the edge of vision—an animal, perhaps? Or maybe a machine, remnants with slumbering cores persisting in the undergrowth, moving only when unseen. The few creatures that live here must be subtle and sly: the glimmer of translucent wings in the sunlight, elusive shapes sliding between stems, leaving faint, ambiguous tracks. Perhaps life is more fungal, more mold or bacterial, or is entirely internal—hidden colonies operating beneath bark and stone, invisible to the outsider.

**Rules of the world**:  
- Light is almost a physical force, exposing everything, intolerant of secrets except those buried deep.
- Engines and plants coexist, their boundaries soft, sometimes merging.
- Change is slow—the vegetation might swallow a structure by centimeters over a year; a pool of water might evaporate only as the decade turns.
- Silence is both law and invitation—what noise exists is precious, deliberate, or startling.
- This world is patient; it endures, collects, accumulates, barely yielding anything to haste.

**Weirdness creeps in quietly.** The structures sometimes repeat improbable patterns, as if their positions form unknown constellations across the landscape. Reflections in the old glass hint at features not present—a tree, a figure, an unknown shape, lingering only in the play of sun and water droplets. The moss glows a little, not just green but strangely, with feverish hints at night. The rare animals you might meet are neither entirely animal nor machine, but something quietly negotiating both.

From any low summit, entrants see the **region extend**, a half-valley, half-plateau, rimmed by young, defiant ridgelines, every surface bristling with growth and the faint memory of civilization. Every sense is engaged—by the density, the brightness, the strange, relentless patience of a place that is lush, echoing, and almost—but never fully—alive.
