Beneath a sky of ordinary, pearled light—neither ominous nor radiant—sprawls a place at odds with itself and yet perfectly resolved in its contradictions. This is a low-lying basin, bounded as though by intention, sized to the measure of a cathedral’s grounds or an urban plaza: large enough to be echoic, small enough that edges always whisper at the mind’s edge. Its form is carved by time, each contour softened by a history of quiet persistence. You sense at once the place has *always* been like this, and will never shift—a snapshot of perpetual stability, an equilibrium locked in deep harmony.

The air is cool and heavily laden; moisture seeps into every crevice and seeps back out again, thickening with clinging condensation on every exposed surface. Water is present, but only as vapor and rivulets—beaded droplets on corroded rails, the gleam of shallow runoff in guttered channels, the slickness of polished stone edges. There are no lakes, no rivers, merely the sense that fluid is always between states, evaporating and condensing in a cycle as unchangeable as the world itself. Occasional drips echo, as if somewhere high above a condensation accumulates enough to fall, but these drops always land in the precise same places, leaving stains on the floor’s worn material.

Vegetation is absent—no fronds, no moss, not even a tenacious scrap of green. The ground is a tapestry of mineral and constructed material: battered paving, sinkings of glassy slag, and broad, lichen-pale outcrops of rock whose composition is ambiguous—possibly processed waste or some long-hardened industrial byproduct. In cracks, there dwells no life—but perhaps the suggestion of something: mycelial threads, or mineral dendrites, or the ghost-traces of forms that almost learned to feed upon the stone, arrested in the act.

Yet the city is here, dense in its construction but sparse in *occupation*. Structures push up in dense clusters—factories, squat and looming; halls of unclear purpose, their entrances dark and echoing; stalks of coiled pipe and skeletal gantries fused together in great urban fossil formations. Rail lines and conveyor systems loop like petrified intestines, fully visible through open walls and absent roofs. Everything suggests manufacture, endless process, industrial intention. Machinery idles in perfect stillness, gears forever wound but never shifting, presses half-descended as if fossilized in mid-stroke. Light glows from arc lamps and panels, unblinking, bathing everything in room-temperature fluorescence—a light as indifferent as the landscape’s stasis.

But the city is not empty. Far from it: fauna overrun everything, crowding the air, the ground, the surfaces. Not recognizable animals, but living forms that hint at the habits of insects, isopods, and more—mechanical, calcified, mineral, or something in between. Armored bodies throng, some with glassy shells, others with powdery skin or metallic plates veined with condensation. Many crawl, in coordinated, unhurried circuits—across pipes, in guttered trenches, over silent conveyor belts. Some gather in swarming clots, negotiating the passage of their own kind around malfunctioning gates and half-open doors. A rain of micro-creatures splatters against every exposed face, too small to identify except as friction and static.

Their voices make the atmosphere deafening: the crackle of jointed limbs on stone, the drumming and hissing of population movements, the static-whine of wings, the resonant clatter of bodies jostling metal. Intermittent calls ripple through the crowd, a language of beat and scrape. The overall sound is like standing inside an engine shed where every machine has become a massive insect hive—a chorus so constant and balanced that it becomes its own kind of stillness, no part ever growing or shrinking, never thinning or thickening, only holding its present, roaring intensity.

There is no growth, no decay; no construction, no collapse. Pipes never rust further; hulls never break. Water and matter cycle in micro-looped processes perfectly in balance with loss and gain. The air is always cool; the illumination always the same. Shapeless not because nothing changes, but because nothing *can*: time here is form, and form is time, sustained at the edge of feverish possibility.

Perhaps the local rules are these: that biology exists only to move, never to feed or breed; that industry produces solely potential, never output; that noise is the soul of life, but never its song. Fauna endlessly circulate, constructions eternally persist—both locked in unyielding, clamorous harmony, neither struggling nor progressing. This is a world where the only current is between a million bodies and the sleeping relics of industry, and where the ceaseless clamor is the only pulse the world will ever know.
