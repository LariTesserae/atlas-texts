**You descend, into a saturated hush:**

Water as everything. Liquid as the air, the ground, the body. Once, long ago, this was land—low and prone, pressed flat by glacial cold and eons of ceaseless rain. It has since drowned wholly: not a flood, but a permanent, ancient submersion. A cold, slow ocean, but not the loose, limitless pelagic. Here, the horizon ends in shadow and yielding barriers: a small, cohesive space, the chamber of a drowned cathedral or the shell of a sunken city block. The scale: human, maybe a little larger—vaulted but bounded, a place you could map in an hour, if you could only see.

**Sight is a negotiation.**  
Light does not reach here, except as dim, sallow gradients finding their way through crack or vent. It falls in as if reluctant, clinging to the surface of things—a pane of glass, a strip of corroded metal. The world is overwhelmingly dark, with the occasional glimmer of phosphorescence: a rail edge whorled with algal scrawl, the peeling glow from a control-panel long since corroded, a shimmer trailing the passage of something living and rare.

**Atmosphere:**  
You do not breathe here. The cold is absolute and steady, seeping into all things. Liquid presses against every contour. The sense of density is total—this is not empty ocean but a layered, almost claustrophobic immersion. Walls, columns, cables, pipes, detritus: the built environment is packed, woven into ceilings and floors, running up walls like the last bones of forgotten industry. Each passage, each chamber, feels full even when empty.

**Terrain and Materials:**  
Stone, yes—melted smooth and etched by aeons of steady current, but more often metal, mottled by corrosion, layered in silt, barely visible beneath overlapping mechanical remains. Rooms and corridors divide the space: a collapsed foundry, a flooded conduit, a vault ribbed and domed, all suffused with the sense of something repurposed again and again, until even decay has its elegance.

Every surface is softened by water's weight, rounded, blue-black with a film of algae where even sparse sunlight encourages life. Tiny, tough plants exist—stubby filaments, pale leaves trailing from cracks in bulkheads, lichens dull and starved of color, but hanging on with the tenacity of things that know their world is marginal.

**Sound:**  
A profound, heavy quiet. The rare sound: a metallic tick, the shifting stress of a distant bulkhead, the faintest muffled hum of a mechanical pump still, miraculously, cycling distilled water through layers of pipes. No birds, no whales, no insect hum. Perhaps a swirl of sediment when a current passes. Silence rules here, and your own movement becomes intrusive—a tremor in an ancient order.

**Ecology:**  
Life is stretched thin. A cloud of pale copepods fleeing disturbance, a segmented, glassy worm trailing along a wire. Fish are unknown here; larger forms stripped out by cold and time. Occasionally, a rare scavenger, something blind and jointed, lopes along a beam and disappears, or a colony of translucent, slow-moving polyps clings to the shelter of a valve. The rest is microscopic, metabolic: a dance of fevers and decays, brownian and unseen.

**World rules:**  
Time is slow. Metals corrode, but at a measured pace—the salt-water battery of industry keeps some lights flickering, some doors cycling through half-remembered routines. The machinery is mostly still, but never truly dead; pumps chug to life, a conveyor starts, something grinds onward, resolved to its task, no longer observed or directed.

Gravity is softened, in constant dialogue with the water's support. Movement is choreographed—no sudden gestures, only a slow negotiation through the densely layered corridors and vaults. Little floats free for long. Everything has settled, or is settling, in the deep cold.

**Strange influences:**  
Edges blur. Space warps subtly with the pressure gradients. Distances feel unreliable—walk a corridor, turn, return, and the lengths seem to alter, the familiar configurations smudged by slight changes in current and silt. Memory is unreliable in the gloom: direction, passage, origin—it all feels a little unmoored, as if the water in the mind mirrors the water outside.

**In summary:**  
A drowned, tiny city or vast room; a clustered, manufactural crypt; a place where water is not above or below, but omnipresent—cold, heavy, silent, weathered smooth by eon and engine alike. Few living things, each tenuous, strange, and persistent. Machines persist in a half-life, and every inch is dense with the relics of both construction and decay. Silence reigns, except for the smallest enduring processes—mechanical or metabolic—the slow, cold music of a world long lost and yet, impossibly, still turning somewhere deep below the surface of things.
