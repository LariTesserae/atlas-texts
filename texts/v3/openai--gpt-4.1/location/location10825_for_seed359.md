### PLACE: The Dry Grove of the Vitreous Conclave

*Step inside.*

#### Terrain

The air is warm but not oppressive—a comfortable, living heat, gently sustained. The ground is close underfoot, giving the impression not of earth, but of being in a chamber partly sunken, its tough, angular surface looming upward like ancient, jagged floors. The "soil" is not soil. These floors, walls, and even the ceiling are all part of a contiguous made-environment—once divided from the outside, now thoroughly claimed and rearranged. Every corner, curve, and seam is deliberate as if carved from an unfathomable block.

The room, or the grove—it isn’t clear which—stretches no further than a large cathedral. There are boundaries, not sharp edges, but densely tangled barriers of artifice and growth, where going farther is simply not… possible. Here, every surface bears the gift of recent genesis: sharp ribs, unmistakably fresh, no mark of wind or water’s slow wearing, but a shimmer of formation—like the edge of newly split glass, unmarred by time.

#### Materials

It gleams. Nothing within these bounds is made of the living mud or stone or wood of a garden world. Instead, the ground and rising structures are glasslike: clear where light can filter through, opaline where denser, streaked with subtle colors, sometimes iridescent, sometimes with the faintest internal luminescence. This substance is brittle, sharp-edged but thick and strong, a matrix of mineral that clicks faintly beneath the soft tread of those who traverse it.

The air smells faintly metallic, warm but not stifling. There is no dust; the environment is sealed, the ground never drinks, and nothing evaporates. In this dry world, water is legend, an ancient memory from a chemical ancestry left behind.

#### Vegetation

Vegetation is lush—not in the drenching, leafy way of Earth, but with an alien density. Every available surface writhes with a forest of growths, yet not a leaf depends on water. Instead, they are mineral-plants: the “vines” are flexible rods of colored silica and carbon, twining in complex braids, almost musical in composition. Flat, feathered fronds unfurl like obsidian ferns, thin enough to quiver in the slow drift of the air. Their surfaces trap the warm light, turning it to energy by processes unknown to biochemistry as we know it—photo-reactive minerals, perhaps, or living crystals.

The “roots” of these plants fuse into the glass substrate, as if they had themselves grown up from it, or were part of it from the beginning. Their branches nest in dense, interlocking layers, blocking parts of the room from view. In some places, vegetation swells in vertical tapestries, nearly touching canopy to ground. Occasional nodules, like luminous fruit, glow faintly from within.

#### Fauna

You are not alone. All around, barely visible save for sudden glimmers and chittered movements, fauna makes this grove a hive of life—if life means movement, feeding, learning, transforming. Lizard-shapes made from jointed basalt and smoky quartz scuttle in the undergrowth. Featherless glass-birds flicker through mineral foliage on brief, slow arcs, their wings catching the light in silent flashes. Sometimes a nest of angular, segmented creatures—half-spider, half-crab but all made, never born—congregates under an outcropping, their shells clinking faintly.

The creatures here are not organic, but they are not machines, either. They are made, in the way everything here is made: shaped by hand—or quasi-hand—by whatever civilization or wildness laid claim to this corner of the world. They propagate by accretion, molding tiny slivers of themselves from local material, or repairing each other with shards and filaments.

Ecologies are intricate: some “eat” light, some slowly erode and incorporate fragments of each other, and some seem to live only for arrangement—building nacreous spiral towers, moving one chip of glass at a time, over patient weeks.

#### Sound

Quiet reigns. There is no wind. Most days, the only sounds are the faintest tinks and chimes as creatures move, or the barely-audible oscillation as dense mineral-veins flex and shift under slow cycles of light and warmth. Every so often, the silence deepens, becoming a palpable, pregnant quiet, as if sound itself is held in suspension.

#### Light and Atmosphere

The lighting is normal enough to distinguish detail, yet something is off. There is no visible source for these comfortably bright rays; rather, illumination seems to be a property of the material itself or the world’s laws. Sometimes shafts of light appear to run at impossible angles, bouncing from nowhere to here, falling in thin pencils through clear mineral structures, then dispersing into nothing.

Colors are rich but never gaudy—deep greens, blues, and bronzes, always with an undercurrent of light trapped inside things, rather than reflecting off surfaces. The air moves so slowly that it’s difficult to tell if it’s moving at all.

#### Rules of the World

This is a place where causality is loosened. Sometimes, features seem to exist only when you look hard enough. The same tangle of “branches” may be wildly different when seen from another angle. Occasionally, if you stare, you catch in the reflection of a glass fern a view of somewhere else—a high place, or a dark river—none of which can fit within these boundaries.

Time proceeds, but not for all things at the same rate. A blossom of mineral “petals” may open over hours—or in a blink, if not watched. Creatures are sometimes present, sometimes not, their arrangements fluid. The boundaries of the place, once solid, pulse with potential: structures from outside might blink into existence for a moment, only to fade with a sigh, as if the local laws permit brief, negotiated excursions from deeper realms.

#### Emergence

Patterns emerge slowly. The residents—plant, animal, mineral, something else—are always constructing, always shaping. Though the place is young, it is blocked with memory, like a room where every wall is a palimpsest, every shard a trace of an earlier order overwritten by the new.

And then, sometimes: the very laws that layered crystal, animate glass, and birthed mineral vines into lush density shimmer, tremble…and for a second, reality itself is questioned. Are the “plants” really growing? Are the “creatures” truly separate from the architecture, or just lively extrusions? Do your senses report a closed, finite space, or is the rest of the world waiting on the other side of a single, quick refraction?

All is slow, warm, dense—a lush, silent grove where everything is made, nothing flows, but life—of a strange kind—thrives, constructing itself from the brittle edges of the world’s first sharp dawn.
