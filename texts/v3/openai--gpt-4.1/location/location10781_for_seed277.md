A WARM, DARK, VAST UPLAND — WHERE ENGINES ROAM

---

Begin looking not with your eyes, but your skin. The hush is broken only by far-off grinding, the shudder of distant mechanisms, the infrequent staccato of wings, and here and there the wet sigh of condensation dripping from hidden heights. You have arrived atop a continent-spanning plateau, dark and ancient, where the faint warmth pervades the night and sits upon the air: close and rich, but never stifling.

Here is an expanse that dwarfs your conception of land. It is not a mountain but an upthrust land-mass — so broad that distance is fog, not horizon, and above, the sky is cloaked in night, a true darkness, with only the odd phase of an alien moon or, perhaps, the bioluminescent trails of high-sailing creatures to lend any trace of luminescence. Most often, the world is ink-dark, the warmth rising from stone and dirt, the very ground breathing out memory as dew.

The terrain is old, settled — sculpted by eons of wind and rain into broad, swelling ridges and shallow basins, deeply cut with winding veils of rivers and gullies. Stone dominates: broad pavements of weathered rock, pitted with gentle depressions that pool water in the aftermath of rare and unpredictable storms. But the rock is softer than you expect, worn friendlier by millennia. The rivers do not crash; they wander, feeding pockets of dampness in an otherwise dry, wind-smoothed world.

Vegetation is a rare punctuation upon the vastness. Each presence is precious, sturdy, yet spread thin: low, dark-foliaged shrubs with wide waxy leaves cluster around stone depressions where mist condenses at night. Moss-plates and tangled roots clutch at the edges of rain-collecting bowls. Sometimes, in protected clefts or shadowed mini-valleys, you find a rare, stunted tree — limbs contorted by ancient wind, grown as much sideways as up, the bark black as space and coolly luminescent where sap is exposed.

But it is in the living pulse of the place that you sense the strangeness — for the land itself shelters an industrial ecology, its denizens as much machine as animal. This is an untouched world, unshaped by human or deliberate craft; yet the echo of industry is present, not as a mark of civilization but as a way of being. The fauna are multiplied and diverse, yet sparsely scattered. Some scuttle, some stride, many burrow deep by day and emerge at night to prowl the edges of awareness.

The air, when stirred, smells faintly of oil and hot metal, as if the living things here vent traces of minerals churned in their bodies. Heat is not only meteorological — many of the animals are warm in themselves, sometimes with a faint internal hum, an industrial chemistry running hot through their circulating fluids. You pass close to an immense, pill-bug-like rover, its carapace patterned as overlapping steel-blue plates, fungal blooms punctuating each segment. It brushes by, silent, the only sign its presence the gentle vibration in the rocks and a faint rattle of internal gears, a self-organizing clockwork powered by the chemical condensation of mist.

Vast, spindly herds of strider-beasts roam dark gullies: leggy, birdlike forms ringed with barbed antennae, their voices a rare metal chirr in the stillness. Predators are lean, efficient, stalking not with paws or claws but by latching onto rivulets, where the warm water draws in prey. There are burrowers too, great segmented things whose movements turn stone to gravel in ancient, slow labor; their droppings are nubs of new substrate, the only soil to feed the meager plants.

Rain comes rarely, but the mists are more frequent, roiling in river valleys, condensing along the edges of shallow pans where they catch on stone lips and drip, drop by drop, to create miniature oases. Each time, the air thickens momentarily, and you may hear a surge of animal life drawn to the refreshment, voices and movements swelling, then subsiding as the water dissipates.

No sign has ever marked this place as home to thinking hands, but everything here is in dialogue: animals adapted to the resources of an industrial world that no industry built. It is a place where oil is not refined but bred, metal not forged but secreted, gears not cast but grown. The worlds of living and machine blur into one another, not through technology, but through evolution’s strange hand.

Here, on this endless, warm, quiet plateau, darkness is rule, space is context, life is presence — rumbling, obscure, wondrously active. To walk across it is to move through darkness alive with metal-limbed shapes and liquid muscles, to feel the subtle heartbeat of hidden rivers, catch the shimmer of fungus on a steel-tough root, and hear, only sometimes, the ancient music of oilblood and slow machinery, all belonging utterly to themselves, in the deep, unlit vastness.
