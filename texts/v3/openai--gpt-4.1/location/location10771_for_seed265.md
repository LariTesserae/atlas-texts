You enter the edge of a soaked expanse just before you; the night has never left this place, nor ever truly receded. All is cast in restless, total darkness. You feel the thickened air, humid but not stifling, as if water is always near blooming from condensation, scenting the world with the cold tang of minerals and hidden rot. The ground underfoot is soft—slick with living films of algae and mud, the surface rumpled and uneven, like the crumpled skin of something recently formed and still settling against gravity’s will.

The terrain, while damp and low, is not worn down—jagged shoulders of midnight rock protrude at sharp angles, tracing fresh seams that spiderweb beneath a shallow skin of moss and shaly peat. Water beads and weeps from cracks, threading between these hummocks, forming glistening trickles that never dare to pool or move fast here. Sometimes, you hear the tiniest drip or slip, muffled as if the sound were swallowed by the ground even before it is born.

Vegetation is sparse, but the little there is lurks in clumps—knife-leaved sedge and spindly, pale saplings that reach upward, never clustered close, forever wrestling to claim each patch of exposed mud. Most of the growth surrenders to the black, their forms brittle, fungi-ridden, outlines stenciled against even blacker backgrounds by phosphorescent bloom—odd, silent signals between living things. A thin, glassy lichen etches the rock, casting the briefest shimmering auras, vanishing as soon as you look.

The scale is open, region-spanning, but the darkness compresses distance in all directions. Lighting—utterly absent. No moon, no sun, not even the comfort of stars. Instead, depths unfurl: you sense the valleys stretch for miles, riddled with hidden ridges and shadowed hollows, forming a land that extends far beyond your first or even tenth impression.

Yet the place thrums with presence—not space-exactly, but possibility. It is dense, in a way that almost strains the air: every cubic meter layered with urgent, silent lives. Here, fauna are profuse, their numbers never seen but always sensed—breathings, heartbeats, twitchings in the velvet gloom. You catch a glimmer of multi-legged shapes, eyeless burrowing mammals, silklike worms ribboning through mud, and amphibians whose skins pulse with strange, colored lights, blinking their warnings or invitations in patterned silence.

Ghostly insects hover close, wings making almost no sound at all; you sometimes feel the hush of their passing brush your skin, more sensation than noise. Larger forms move with ponderous patience—massive salamander-things, sheetlike creatures distending and contracting over the mirror-wet stones, their bodies flattening themselves impossibly thin for sleep or camouflage.

Between the living and the landscape lie the marks of industry—small, scattered, half-remembered. An abandoned, rusting rail runs atop a ridgeline and vanishes in a cleft. A pumpjack, ancient and nearly seized, creaks faintly when the wind stirs. Gleaming, riveted pipes—industrial arteries—thread through the ground, leaping open only to dive beneath puddles or vanish beneath moss. In a shallow gulley, a single engine—half-submerged, lidless—churns with a patient, intermittent thrum, powered by forces buried just below water’s surface, its electrics feeding a patchwork of wires that arc off into the vague, endless black.

Nothing here hurries. The changes in this world are glacial. Pools fill with water molecule by molecule, rock faces erode with infinite patience, even the fauna move as if unaware of time’s faster flows elsewhere. Yet under this serenity hums something not quite right: the geometry of the land does not cohere, ridges lead nowhere, water sometimes fails to run downhill, three stones spaced in a triangle become, upon closer examination, four or five. When you try to walk a straight line between two machines you glimpse, distance loses its meaning—sometimes you collide with the same watery hollow again and again, or reach a familiar horizon from a direction you didn’t take.

A faint, ceaseless distortion pulses down here; the world’s rules are slackened, ready to reknit at a moment’s notice. All is filtered through this abnormality—an endless murk that both preserves and warps. The darkness is so thorough, vision grows subjective: outlines flicker, boundaries double back, lights pulse from no source and then blink out as if they were never there. Human-built things are scattered but not quite ruined—they persist, stubborn but slightly misshapen or subtly merged with biology, as if machinery and moss have negotiated a cautious peace.

It is mostly quiet. Where sound comes, it’s soft—an engine’s distant cough, water’s faint tune, the breath and shuffle of hidden things. Any shout would be smothered, any intrusion repelled not by volume but sheer density of being. Here, time drips by, bent and patient; the land waits, knotted, leaning further each moment into its own dark, unfurling dream.
