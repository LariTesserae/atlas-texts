### The Place

#### Terrain, Materials, Elevation

**You enter the world already submerged.**  
There is no air—only the endless embrace of water, cool and pressing. Depth is everywhere; above and below extend in darkness, but you are not oppressed. Here, elevation is a whisper of memory: you drift amongst ancient, high plateaus swallowed by the ocean’s rise long ago. Craggy outcroppings and pillar-like ridges emerge from the silted abyss, arching toward dim hints of a distant surface, haunting and unreachable.

**The stone is old, worn smooth.**  
Edges are muted, forms rounded by centuries of current. Touching a ledge, it is cold—glacial almost—indifferent and solid beneath the swirling living veils. Crevices yawn with mossy tapestries, ravines slip off into ink and mist. Now and then, a ridge runs like the spine of some vast fossil—abandoned by time.

#### Vegetation, Ecosystem

**Vegetation here is strange, lush and yet sparse.**  
Plants of colors you can’t name thrive in surprising vigor. They are not the seaweed of familiar coasts: here bloom gelatinous forests and fronded things, some rising in translucent curtains, others tangled around the ruins and stones. Fragile domes, studded with nodules glowing faintly green or deep cobalt, sway on impossibly long stalks. Elsewhere: mats of fuzzy, feather-like forms, trapped bubbles flickering with the memory of light. They pulse and sway, moved not so much by fish or animal, but by hidden energies—currents, chemistry, perhaps the stone itself.

**No true animal dwells here—no fish, no crustacean, no watcher.**  
Instead, the flora are active in ways that suggest intention. A hollow-frond contracts as you pass; a spiral-shaped leaf uncurls, sending forth a cloud of tiny golden motes which glitter, then fade. This is a world of plant-driven action, of subtle, ceaseless competition, all in motion.

#### Light, Darkness, Atmosphere

**Darkness predominates, thick and tangible**—not one of void, but a living gloom crowded by movement. Every now and then, the sensate murk fractures: a swirl of luminescence blooms from a dehiscent pod; sheet-lightning somewhere in the water column flickers silvery shadows across the landscape. These moments pass, returning to a sightless hush, the darkness full of possibility.

**Mist and water mix strangely here.**  
Pockets of denser fog float in liquid—a phenomenon of cold and dissolved minerals, like clouds trapped within the ocean itself—creating curtains and walls impossible to see through, as if the landscape invents privacy within itself.

#### Sounds

**Despite the cold hush, the place is never silent.**  
The loudness is of water in movement:  
- The roar of currents forced between pillars and through ruined archways.
- The flute-like singing of water rushing in plant chambers.
- Distant, booming cracks as old stones settle or split—a patient, intermittent percussion.
- Sometimes, a rising hiss, as if air tries to escape the old earth, and is caught, endlessly, by thick water.
- The swelling undertone of plant matter thrumming at subsonic frequencies—as if together, the living world sings an unending, wordless chorus.

As your ears adjust, you sense patterns: waves of sound passing from region to region, a slow metronome to the ecosystem’s secret clock.

#### Remnants of Construction, Civilization

**Here and there, built things:**
- Lone pillars of not-wholly-natural symmetry, clad in moss-thick garb—towers, or markers, barely distinguishable from aged stone.
- Chunks of wall, daubed in fading, lichen-bright pigment—each mark made with hand and patience, perhaps marking a story, perhaps only a gesture.
- Half-buried circles of stone; stone axes left within twisted roots of water-plants; the foundations of huts whose roofs are long gone, floors claimed by sediment and time.

**Primitive tech:**  
Simple, functional—stone, bone, plant-fiber—honed by hand, bound with sinew and moss. No evidence of recent use: rather, these artifacts are part of the environment now, colonized, changed, and sometimes displayed by the flora, perfectly in place.

#### Ecological Rules & Processes

**A world in which plants are both architect and predator.**  
Here, vegetation is not passive: it moves, exudes, claims, and digests not animals, but the bones of old structures, the minerals that leach from settling stone. The cycles of growth and decay are active and abrupt, a balance long kept between the ambition of plants and the abiding stone.

**Weather is internalized, ongoing:**  
Currents surge and eddy like wind, storms of silt rise from valleys, sometimes obscuring everything, then settling. Organisms—plants and others—release clouds of spores, create blooms of color and pheromonal song. Temperature dips and rises in slow pulses, governed by vast, unseen tides.

#### The Unknown

**Here, time moves differently.**  
Not quick as animal lives, but not geologic either; forces are always shifting, adjusting, adapting. It is a cold, living, loud world—a great experiment in what life can be when left to the flora, when animals are myths, when the darkness and water are both home and sky.

#### If you stay…

You will learn the different darknesses of this place, and the pulse of its sounds. You will see how, in the absence of animals, plants have taken every ecological niche—hunter, scavenger, builder, singer, historian. And you will realize, eventually, that the ruins are less the monuments of what was lost, than the inheritance of what the living world has become.
