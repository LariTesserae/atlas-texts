Elevation holds a stark and shrouded eminence here, an upthrust plateau underscored by vanishing mountains flattened by aeons of rain and wind. Yet the world is not rock but *root*—ancient, dense, frost-pale. Trunks and boughs, creepers and mats, thick as city walls, weave a continent of wood and stem across the entire open highland, yet these living structures are spared the jumble of close forest; this is *not* a tangle, but a vaulting, splayed geometry of plant-forms, rising and furling into the air and then plunging away, as if shaped by tidal flows.

Moisture is omnipresent: beads drawn by capillaries onto every surface; the ground exudes a thin fog, flecked with refracted luminescence that is equal parts bioluminescence and reflected, filtered day. Every crevice and hollow is alive with dark, slow water, somewhere between marsh and cloud, pooling in shallow roots, brooks threading through thick moss platforms, slick to the touch and chill as untouched dawn.

It is perpetually almost-night. The sky—a low, saturated glow among scrimshaw-clouds—is blocked by endless webbing, not of spider but of flowering vine and crystalline lichen, forming a dim, uneven ceiling overhead. Natural light is scant, blue and green, always seeming on the verge of returning to shadow.

Nothing here is architectural—there are no ruins, no paths, no hints of hand or tool—yet everything is built, because *plants* shape all. Footing is a tense balance: ancient, spongy boles slick with water, or open platforms of living bark, poised above slow-moving pools. Hollow tree-pillars gape at intervals, each filled with slow-murmuring sap and the cold, sweet scent of overripe berries. Twisted canopies let in fingers of light that swirl eternally with mist and swelling pollen.

Despite the sparse spread—open, almost empty—everywhere life moves and *swirls*: wings beat the still fog, chitin grinds against bark, calls echo between the columns in strange, six-tongued dialects. Branches shiver with massed, pale creatures—arthropod, mammal, avian, none fitting any Linnaean taxonomy—moving in constant, abrupt rituals of collision, predation, spawning, dissolution. Even the rooted plants seem sometimes to *shift*, anchor themselves afresh, devour or nurture by slow, oozing processes.

What sound here: a wall of noise. The crackle of flexing rootwood. Sudden rush of massed wings. The keening drone of sap-insects chewing ancient fiber. Every so often, with no warning, the hum rises to a scream—an entire corner of ecosystem restructuring, as if by sudden industrial gust: trunk collides with trunk, roots split and refold, releasing pale clouds of spores and a crunching static that overwhelms all else. All around, scurrying things fight for footing, hunted and hunter collapsing into a single moment of chaos.

Amid all this, impossible things flicker at the edge of sight: a tree knot splits open to let a ribbon of cold, amber light spill out; gravity bends around a tangle of moss-midges; time slips, so moss regrows visibly even as something eats it. Shapes *almost* solidify—a woman's face in a flower, a train of segmented creatures climbing into a tear in the air—before dissolving, as if the world recalls itself only with effort, like a patchwork memory.

The rules are bent, sometimes sideways: water drips up. Roots pulse with cold flame and then become still. Landscape *recalibrates* when no one is looking—open valleys becoming webbed bridges, a clear pool replaced by spined coral, all in the blink of an eye. The age is unimaginable—every surface polished by time, by passage, by uncounted generations—but the churn is violent, constant, never still.

Above all, a sense of endless process: the cold, the wet, the plantlife orchestrating a ceaseless, liturgy-like churn of rebuilding and erasure. Here, evolution is still alive—industrial, mechanical, but in no hands but its own—spitting out new forms with every storm, grinding the ancient terrain ever more deeply into itself. Not hostile, precisely, but unapproachable, a living machine of plant and beast and anomaly, ancient as any world and never, for a moment, at rest.
