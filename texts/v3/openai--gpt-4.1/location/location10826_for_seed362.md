It is called the Constructed Verdancy, but names are for comfort, and there is little comfort here. The observer's gaze crosses a horizonless expanse of *plant-stuff*—not vegetation, not soil, not even air, but a woven, layered, crafted mass of living matter. Each centimeter is formed, grown to a design, though the designs fold into one another with logic only their authors—or perhaps *nobody*—could decipher. 

### Terrain & Materials

There is no dirt, no water, no exposed inorganic stone; it is all *plant*—root-matter and fiber, bark and phloem, sheets of pale-green cellulose lattices, swelling and thinning as they stretch in vast tessellations. Where the surface should be, the ground is instead a fused mat of living cellular blocks, sometimes smooth, sometimes ridged, pocked by breathing pores the size of fists. There are no rivers, lakes, nor the memory of any. Here and there, the substrate dips into ragged valleys where immense cords of root muscle loop and twist, some arching over the expanse like the ribs of a titanic skeleton.

Loose, fuzzy “mold” creeps between each layer—the waste of the system, chewed by invisible decomposers or repurposed by the ecosystem’s slow, mechanical digestion.

### Atmosphere & Light

Light is relentless: there is no day or night, only unyielding radiance. Luminant panels of plant membranes, engineered to refract and store all available energy, grow in planes high above other layers, making their own sun—light that is dazzling, white, almost synthetic, filtered through a million translucent leaves and casting shifting, verdant patterns on everything below.

Humidity is low; air here is dry, tasting almost artificial, carrying the faint scent of resin, photosynthate, and something faintly metallic.

There is a wind, soft, crossing over the plant-architecture, but it’s not made by weather; it pulses as vast trunks shift, ventilating their interiors, pushing old gases out and drawing new ones in.

### Scale & Density

This place is endless—continent-wide, perhaps planetary. Its scale confounds normal perception: everywhere, plant is mass-manufactured, iterated and overlapped, purpose-layered from lowest root-warren to uppermost photosynthesis arrays. To move horizontally is to wind among tightly clustered pillars and corridors, some a meter wide, some cathedral-large, all green and veined.

Space is dense, claustrophobic, pressing: between each mass, vines tangent and branch, pressing into every available volume; tangled heartwood halls burrow down into the green substrate, opening into dense galleries of active, quivering tissue, vascular fluids pumped silently through capillary-networks—no single patch of emptiness persists for long. 

### Architecture & Tech

There is nothing natural—every microstructure is built, by design or by code or by older intentions now lost. Trunks spiral in fractal tessellations, forming columns and support vaults; walls and roofs are spun from rigid fiber-lamellae, seamless and unyielding but alive. Above, “windows” open, not glass but thin films, modulating light and nutrient flow according to metabolic dictates.

Intertwined with the living tissue are signs of industry. Here, a fan of metallic leaves, stamped and folded for unknown machinery. There, a pipe—root or metal?—emanating a faint hum, drawing some sap or byproduct to a thicket of fern-turbines and photo-reactors nested like broom-cups. Occasionally, a rotary segment of stem-crane turns fully, shifting processed plant mass from one layer to another, always silent.

No area is left untilled—every available surface and volume is laid out in built order, alive and alert, optimized for relentless, indifferent growth-production. 

### Ecology & Life

Fauna is rare, almost vestigial. Once or twice a shadow flees among the reeds—perhaps an engineered symbiote, perhaps stray survivors. If creatures exist here, they are adapted to press between layers, feeding not on water, which is absent, but on the constant, slow trickle of plant-sap, on minerals leached from the structure itself or on strange photosynthetic secretions.

Yet life is everywhere, if strangely defined. The vegetative mass is active: it pulses, swells, shifts, undulates. In a distant tangle, the ground flexes momentarily—processes of metabolism, waste-removal, or biofabrication. Above, a “forest” of industrial plants swings to follow the artificial sun, photosynthesizing at optimized angles, sometimes shifting entire branches or arrays in synchronized choreography.

Deeper in the substrate, roots coil around rusty engine-blocks, their growth patterns influenced by ancient, ignored infrastructure, merged into pure function.

### Rules & Weirdness

Reality bends—edges blur, distances lose meaning, scales collide. Some corridors seem to stretch away forever until, an eyeblink later, you find yourself at their far end. Gravity is subtly off; within certain chambers, it seems the plant-mass *remembers* where it *wants* you to be and guides movement accordingly. Threads of memory—embedded within the genetic design, perhaps—infuse the very walls. Here, patterns repeat fractally, but not identically; in patches, shapes and colors appear that defy known botany, flickering in and out like mismatched overlays from another ecology.

The only true constant is that all is made: nothing raw, nothing untouched. Even decay is managed and guided.

### Soundscape

It is quiet—as if the air was padded in thick moss—yet not silent. Air circulates with the hush of distant bellows. Deep beneath, a slow heartbeat-vibration pulses through the ground, sometimes matched by the subtlest creaking, the shifting of a living city’s endless mass. Somewhere far above, a multitude of resonant filaments ring softly, tuned to winds that never reach the ground.

Occasionally: a distant, slow grind—the sound of trunks rotating, of matter being churned and reprocessed, but always at the edge of hearing, always absorbed quickly by the omnipresent biomass.

### Motion, Activity, and Mood

The entire ecosystem teems with invisible process. Plant-mass grows, reacts, opens, and retracts. Nutrient-laden gas puffs through vents, feeding upper canopies, while new layers are extruded and old matter orderly reclaimed. Despite the density, the architecture breathes—corridors expand and contract with purpose, new material pulsed through arterial vines or snapped into place by bio-mechanical actuators.

The place is not hostile, but it is indifferent. One stands within a ceaseless, systematic making machine, whose comfort for its own kind is absolute, and for others is merely ignorance—an endless, bright, crafted wilderness built and maintained by the logic of plant and industry, not for the wild, not for the human, but for itself.

---

To stand here is to surrender to a grandeur so total, so deeply layered in design and strangeness, that the mind staggers—an infinity of cool, dry, bright life, ordered beyond comprehension, silent except for the muffled breathing of the living world, going about its work.
