**This place is abundance in an absence.**

You enter the region through a pass, sensation darkened and uncertain. Air is thin—not biting cold, but gauzy, as if there’s less here to breathe. The ground underfoot is silicate, pale honey in color, gritted and hard as kiln-fired clay, striated by centuries of steady wind. Yet the dryness is deceptive: the whole space is dense with life, crowding between granules, bridging cracks, swarming from every refuge—a chaos-chorus against a near-silent sky.

**No water, not in memories or molecules. Life here is extruded from heat, mineral, and air itself.** The vegetation is bizarre: wax-skinned, segmented, each plant an articulated chain of rigid plates, tapering to root-spines sunk into the stone. Glossy filaments—like translucent cilia, pale citrine and deep purple—protrude from each plate’s edge, sifting minerals, abrading dust, vibrating feverishly in unseen currents, singing out through the air when agitated. Here and there, towering stalks take the form of spiral horns, ribbed and luminous, hollow cavities amplifying the wind’s song into something like organ music—deep, uneasy, ineffably alien.

The space is no wider than a small cathedral. Outer stone walls—a jagged rim, bleached by eons—contain this ecosystem, though not with precision. Boundaries are blurred, but definite: a hollow, the size of a larger human home, nestled in a crest volleyed by highland gusts. The plateau curves, curled and pooled like lacquer running down a slope, ledged with stony knuckles and splintered, encrusted roots.

In the dim light—molten amber, weak, filtered through the high atmosphere, perhaps a thick haze or a dying sun—plants and animals alike glow and pulse. Shadows are always moving, always uncertain. There is a constant, churning turbulence in the air; vortexes of dust rise, then vanish, scattering sparkling shards—crystals, pollen, chitin?—that hang briefly before falling into unseen cracks.

**Sounds press in from all sides. They are not gentle.**
- The wind is a howling orchestra, forced into and through the horn-plants.
- Chitinous life-forms (some like insectile mantids, some like living knots of jointed vines, some like armoured discs with thousands of flickering legs) swarm, skitter, snap.
- Bursts of shrill calls and rattling percussion ring out from hidden corners; the air is so saturated with noise that silence would feel monstrous.
- Sometimes, the earth pulses with a subsonic grinding, as if the entire shelf is shifting on living joints—directionless and disconcerting.

Fauna abound, in forms unfamiliar—there are crawling creatures, their exoskeletons glassy and brittle, their limbs fanning out wide. Some are perched atop bare roots, spreading vast fringed cloaks to ‘drink’ the sun’s warmth; others are embedded directly into the mineral substrate, unmoving but alive, their movements visible only as the subtle dilation of chromatophores. Some insects, or proto-animalculae, are swift, catching motes midair; some are slow, symbiotic with the shivering plants.

Yet there is a rhythm. Patterns emerge: a spiral migration around the central horn; ritualized combat or mating dances on exposed mineral shelves; sudden clannish swarming, which then recedes as quickly as a wave. The sense of relentless activity is inexhaustible, charged, but not directionless—a bazaar of energy, a tumult that is both competitive and communal, as if the chaos is itself a fragile equilibrium.

**Evidence of past or present sentience is scattered, ambiguous:**
- Small cairns of polished crystal, arranged in spirals or measured lines;
- Obelisks of denser mineral, carved crudely with abstract symbols, perhaps battered by storms or dust-gnawing fauna;
- Hand-shaped depressions in harder stone, sometimes set with rusted filaments or the remains of woven grass-like matter, suggesting simple tools, early crafts, intentional acts.

No one shape dominates. The terrain itself is weathered, aged: every surface softly abraded by endless wind and contact, nothing sharp or raw—except, perhaps, the emotional edge of this outlandish beauty. This is an ecosystem old in its own rules, settled into its strangeness, not needing water, not knowing green, yet bristling with invention.

**The rules drift close to dream-logic:**  
- Life grows by mineral and light alone; water is but a rumor in the churning wind.
- The living may root as easily as walk, and life pulses not by blood but by mineral osmosis and wind-harvesting fronds.
- Change is constant, cycles of construction and destruction measured in spirals and storms.
- Each entity carves a niche, yet the niche always shifts.

**It is hot, high, crowded by sound, but not by bodies.** One senses enormous age, relentless dynamism, and deep, deliberate difference—a lushness that has never required rain, yet, in the roar and riot, is every bit as alive.
