Water, everywhere, almost. Not a lake nor a river, but an omnipresent, restless force living just beneath the surface of things — seeping, vaporous, condensing in soft, gleaming sheets on wall and ceiling and strange protrusion. 

Picture an enclosed space vast as a cathedral, but it is not a building — it is the world entire, here. You are below the surface: it’s a chamber, a cavern, low in the world’s veins, yet open to a kind of difficult-to-place daylight that fills everything with an ambient, topaz-bright white, as if the rock itself were cotton infused with radiance. Light shines through columns of mist that drift and roil without wind, lenses of water vapor that sharpen and soft-blur the surfaces they pass.

The terrain undulates in long, low shelves of ancient, smoothed stone. Every form is softened by the endless tumbling of water and time, surfaces rounded and sculpted by filtration and seep. The ground is pocked in depressions — some brimming with a thin film of water, some dark and dry, some ghosted over with shining droplets that never quite accumulate enough to pour forth. Near some walls, the stone is black with old leaching, and here, feathery efflorescence of mineral blooms like white mold.

Vegetation is rare and precious — not green, but purples or grave-browns, and springing only in those places where small vents spill out nutrient-misted air and warmth: thin filaments curling, or hard, waxy moss-clusters with pebbly leaves, or a half-dozen starburst, rootless lichen-webs, their filigree catching every stray drop. Life gathers in islands, rarely, uncompetitive, eking existence from the chemical rainfall and the mineral soup that beads and slides perpetually through this world.

Sound is a living thing here. Water falls in slow, rolling sheets down stone ridges with a roar like storm surf; thin spray hisses in the air, and from farther caverns, irregular percussions — booms, groans, fizzing, singing — as subterranean pressure vents or flows crash open new paths. A constant canopy of noise, echoing, overlapping, shifting. To human ears it would be too much: an endless intensity, a mechanical sublime. But there are silences, too, as sound is sucked away in soft, wooly pockets between boulders or under the thin ledge of a mineral shelf.

Here and there — at first you doubt yourself — you see the marks of something built. An arch, but not like any human hand would carve; more as if the stone and the water together conspired to make passageways wide enough for some unknown purpose. Embedded in these are things at the edge of comprehension: columns spiraling in fractal whorls, faint, nearly invisible filamental wires strung between stalactites, half-dissolved matrices that catch the light and glimmer with an interior movement, as if data or energy, not fit for human reckoning, flexed through them. Sometimes a drilled hole in the rock, not for drainage, but as conduit — to what? Embedded in one vault, a shimmering lens, neither water nor glass, seems to shift quantum-fine images inside it. A sense: this place was adjusted, or is adjusting itself, with yet-unwritten laws.

Only rarely does anything more than water move. Fauna is seen (or heard) in glances: at one pool’s edge, a shell that flexes open, thin barbs bristling; a quick flash of eyes from a vent, retracting into a misty crevasse; sometimes a tremor in the lichen, hinting at burrowers whose passage you can never confirm. Life here is residue — left-over, marginal, or else so perfectly adapted it is unnoticeable, silent except when it must be otherwise.

Inside this wet-warm, echoing, reconfiguring place, the air shimmers with molecular storm. Blasts of mist, sudden local deluges, and eruptions of light or energy from the built artifacts can change the microclimate in a moment. Small currents of air whip up wisps of vapor, forming vortices that flash with colors from devices embedded in the stone: post-modern machines hum and sing, maybe self-healing, maybe not meant for you or for anything with limbs.

It feels alive, and unstable, and old — not wild, not tamed, but in a dynamic state uniquely its own, turbulence managed or directed by means you cannot fully see or decipher, except you understand: balance is an exertion, not a peace. This is a cathedral beneath the ground, given over to sound and water and impossible invention, inhabited only by its own relentless process, and the rare, careful organisms who dare to call it home.
