**Somewhere in the Stratospheric Canopy**

The world sprawls beneath a glaring white sky—bright, thin-aired, and endless—yet walking its ground is impossible, for there is no true ground at all.

Here, at the planetary crown, “terrain” is vertical and vast: a high-altitude zone where land has been usurped entirely by living things. Vegetation is the everything—the very fabric of place, the geometry of all existence. Roots, columns, and fronds fill the air in colossal lattices and irregular spars, yet they are widely spaced, each a titanic singularity dangling space beneath its bulk. These are not forests as we know them, nor vines, nor gnarled thickets, but planetary-scale structures: trunks thicker than mountains, drifting upwards and away from each other, with immense voids between, pillars reaching beyond sight. Some hang suspended by their own buoyancy, others anchor on ancient mineral “islands”— last relics, so weathered they've become pale, inert plinths in the endless updraft.

The plants themselves are absurd in scale: vaulted umbrellas and tattered sails of living tissue, branching into thousands of meters, veined with glowing sap—pale green and iridescent lavender—catching as much light as possible in the thin chill air. Their flesh is often glassy, crystalline, or fibrous; forms snap and shimmer, swelling and contracting in the onslaught of weather. Clinging lichens and epiphytes descend in tangled, glittering threads, forming loose, spiral stairways between layers. Wherever one plant's tendril brushes another, a microbial riot erupts—each boundary an evolutionary marketplace.

**Atmosphere**

There is no standing water. Here, humidity is a rumor, vapor snatched almost instantly into living flesh or whipped into shreds by relentless wind. The air is chill but painfully dry, carrying the sting of precipitated minerals—a desert of vapor, a place beyond rain. Dew might bead for an instant as sunlight pours through, before vanishing or being inhaled by hungry roots.

**Sound**

The world is impossibly loud. Wind shrieks and roars as it’s torn through leaf-panels. The plants themselves are alive to the air: some flex and vibrate deliberately, singing at the edges of hearing or broadcasting through ultrasonic and infrasonic booms. Migratory swarms of animals—fliers, gliders, living kites—rush through in clouds, their voices and wingbeats adding to the cacophony. Flocks clash with swarms; echolocation pings strobe through the tumult. Somewhere, a colony of six-winged things is drumming the underside of a far-off leaf, coordinating their migration to the tempo of the plant’s own vibrations.

**Ecology**

All things feed on light, drift, and each other. Animals and plants blur at the edges: vine-limbed grazers and leaf-bodied striders leap from bough to bough, their bright carapaces reflecting sunlight and noise alike. Nothing is settled for long; if a form weakens, it is rapidly recolonized, overtaken, or outright consumed. The rare precipitate mineral patches are battle zones, hosting industrial hives: immense termite-like machinists chewing stone and extruding new crystal, feverishly constructing living bridges and aerial silks.

Despite the chaos, the overarching forms have persisted for uncountable ages. Erosion is no longer the work of water or time, but the slow softening of vegetal mass, the unbuilding of the old to let in the new. Ancient, sky-hewn scars show where trunks fell, now laced over by younger growth in delicate fractal memory.

**Dynamics**

There is always motion. Winds are rarely gentle; they range from cold constant gales to sudden, deafening storms that ripple through the living mass like shockwaves. Every surface flexes and shudders. Plant ribs bend and resound like harp strings struck by titanic hands, sending vibration signals across kilometers—sometimes summoning flocks of creatures to participate in unknown, explosive gatherings.

**Rule of the World**

No water: All metabolism, all chemistry craves and retains what little vapor can be found. Fluids here are not water, but slick glutinous saps—highly viscous, luminous, with “life” seething in every drop.

No true ground: Height and verticality define all living. The void between is as sacred as the living mass: a medium for movement, for song, for light to fall through uninterrupted. A fall is fatal—a plunge through cold, howling emptiness, ending in clouds or even lower, in the unfathomable desert below the canopy, if such a place can even be reached.

No built: Yet there is industry—hive-cities and colonial swarms, engineered by life rather than hand, busy and purposeful as any factory, all on an unthinkable natural scale.

**Atmosphere and Light**

Light pours always: dazzling, refracted, coating everything with haloes, making the highest strata so bright the eye can’t resolve shape. Below, shade is green and gold and violently purple, fractured by leaf-veins and fluttering wings.

**Scale and Perception**

To dwell here is to be lost in immensity. Each living structure, spaced so far from its neighbor, feels both infinite and alone. Yet every shape vibrates with connection, all shouting across the radiant emptiness.

**Impression**

This is a world where the living supplants the inanimate, where to exist is to contend with scale, with noise, with the sharp sting of absence—absence of water, of solid ground, of silence. But presence, too: life-become-place, motion-become-law, music-become-landscape, all in the endless, bright, uproarious air.
