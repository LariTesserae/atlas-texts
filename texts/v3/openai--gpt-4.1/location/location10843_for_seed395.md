The Erupting Plain 

You emerge into a realm where the horizon curves only at the scale of continents, a world whose sheer expanse dwarfs memory of limit or edge. The air is thick and warm, bearing the heaviness of potential rather than of humidity, for there is almost no water here, not in the forms familiar to Earth. Salt, perhaps—or something like it—creeps in pale patches across the ground, sparkling with a brittle sheen, crusts forming upon crusts, betraying the faintest taste of minerals in the air.

The ground is low—it is all ground here, sloping inward and outward on the gentlest of inclines, undulating like the dunes of a mega-desert before they have known wind enough for true shaping. No river cuts through, no lake lies cupped—only the endless sweep of a smooth, granular surface. This surface is not yet sand, not yet polished by time—its grains are fractured, raw, fresh from whatever cosmic process first flung them down, not yet reduced by erosion, edges still sharp underfoot.

And yet—despite this aridity—despite the metallic tang—this world teems with life.

Vegetation springs forth as if in challenge to dryness. It is not the green of Earth: it is deep carmine, dusty grey, orange-umber, violet-black, flourishes of strange-built plant bodies that seem almost mineral in composition, their surfaces toughened, crystalline, sometimes corrugated with waxy ridges. Every available inch is crowded: mats of low, dense growths fuse to one another, forming pillows and polyp-like domes, succulent to the core, drawing nourishment seemingly from the substrate itself.

These strange plants arch and tangle overhead, twist through and over one another, so that it can be hard to tell where one colony ends and another begins. They build upwards and sideways at once, their clustered forms reminiscent of coral reefs or mycelial webs, an urban sprawl of life with no space unclaimed. Where there is a gap, it is seized in a heartbeat, flooded with new shoots, new colors, or perhaps with nests and runways of other life forms.

Fauna are everywhere, almost uncountable, moving quietly within their internal business: a low, constant, susurrus of life. In the silence—undercut only by the crisp break of mineral fragments under-foot or the subtle rustle as something skitters—creatures burrow, creep, and slide. Many wear armors, shell and scale to shield their bodies from abrasion and dryness; their limbs are adapted for gripping, climbing, or weaving swiftly through packed growth.

Bird-like things, featherless, glide on heat currents, never calling; rodent-analogues with glistening, chitinous hides nuzzle in the crevices between root and crystal-stalk. Larger forms, sinuous or squat, move deliberately through their crowded world, careful as ships navigating a reef, their mass bending the knotted tangles as they pass.

There is a sense that this world is unfinished—every ledge, every dome, every outcrop feels recently made, unscored by time or rain, textures too unweathered for long memory. Crystallization is ongoing; some crusts burst softly to reveal inner cavities where new organisms are already colonizing the fresh space.

This is an urban planet in a prehistory of its own—a city of life in which every cubic inch is called home by something, overlapping and intertwined, feuding or symbiotic or indifferent, all without tools or artifice, yet organized with incredible density and efficiency. Here, construction is not by hand but by growth: entire strata of living matter, packed and layered and pressed, creating canopies, ramps, tunnels, theaters of root and flesh and scale, an endless metropolis without walls.

There are no machines, no artifacts—technology resides in biology itself, in the endless artful recombination of forms. The rules are simple but absolute: expand, seize, adapt. In this vast, cosmically-scaled plain, life asserts itself in storms—fast, silent, ceaseless.

Everything is alive, or on the verge of it. Everything is newly born, unweathered. Between these crowded presences, the air remains still. Only the faintest breeze disturbs the powdery mineral dust, carrying the promise that someday, there will be enough time—and perhaps enough water—for change. For now, time does not erode, but only multiplies. Every moment, abundance erupts again.
