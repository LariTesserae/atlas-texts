Welcome to the Windscried Weeping Heights.

**Terrain:**  
You stand near the rim of a circling upland — not a single peak, but a stack of giant, scalloped terraces, each tens of meters high, stretching away into a bristling maze of knife-backed ridges and shadowed clefts. The ground underfoot is fractured silvery shale, slick with a persistent dampness; lines of harder rock stick up like fossilized vertebrae through the pebbled floor, the remnants of ancient ruptures made strange. Cliff faces arc almost vertically, more bone-white than stone-gray, streaked with icy copper and blue-green pustules where minerals stain the dewed surface.

In all directions, the land drops in tall, haphazard steps — wide enough to hold whole villages, if villages would survive here — and each shelf shelters smaller outcrops, shallow basins filled with mist, and the long-darkened wounds of vanished rivers now swelling with run-off. Here, there is no tidy stream; water seeps in endless slicks from the rock layer itself, oozing in windshield-smeared sheets, pattering to the next tier, and pocking every surface. It is a land perpetually washing itself, droplets chasing each other with a mindless compulsion until they find some brief rest in a mineral cup or along a fissure.

**Materials and Atmosphere:**  
The immediate air hums with the cold, almost spectral brightness of a world incapable of supporting leaf or flower. The sky overhead shows a diluted brilliance, sunlight scattered and refracted through prismatic haze – not clouds, but a high, suspended gloss, possibly particulate or crystal, that multiplies the light and throws spectral glimmers onto every face of stone. Mist generates easily in the cooler hollows, sometimes boiling up in dense patches where temperature shifts, then vanishing abruptly.

Breath here never warms. Instead, it chills — vapor from your exhalation breeds with the atmospheric damp, lingering in pale ribbons that dissolve quickly. When gusts descend, they rattle inside certain narrow cracks and stone-ringed pits, coaxing a piping drone that rises and falls in an everchanging harmony. Some nights, the wind chimes a rattling chorus, as if voices trapped in old stone trade secrets.

**Water, Erosion, and Activity:**  
Water here does not flood or flow; it seeps and slips, never still. The land, having long been shaved and gnawed by relentless elements, feels made not in violence but by patient abrasion. Over eons the terraces formed through the give-and-take of cold rain, freezing and thawing, and something more — cycles of vapor and condensation, always coaxing minerals up from the deep, draping them in strange, glassy skins and miniature stalactites on even the smallest overhangs. Where depressions linger, rough semi-crystalline patches shimmer, shaped by the endless play between dampness and brightness.

On rare, abrupt sunbursts, webs of condensation pearl up on every rock face, splitting the world into tens of millions of blinking eyes that see nobody.

**Light:**  
You would expect glare, but instead find an omnipresent cool brightness, the air as if washed and polished, transporting every edge and facet into uncannily perfect focus. The reflected light stratifies: direct rays hit at angles and cut with sharp-blue clarity, but much of the world is illuminated by radiant mist and refracted off-rock. Shadows, when found, are not black but veined with faint rainbows.

**Density and Scale:**  
Despite the openness — shelves, plateaus, and plunges stretch as far as you can see — everything is layered, each shelf terraced with smaller piles and dense with contorted rock forms, mineral pillars, and mysterious, spindly deposits. Tight clusters of nodules balance impossibly on slender stalks of mineral accretion, crowding together so nearly that wind and water dance in and around them, always pressed for space. Some places look like a stilled waterfall made of stone fingers.

**Vegetation and Fauna:**  
No plant, no animal stirs. The closest analogues are the biomimic shapes in mineral: spiraled nodules and stalks (precipitated by slow, evaporation-driven processes), perhaps riddled with strange internal patterns when broken. Sometimes, limp, translucent sheets of crystal hang from terraces almost like membranes — their glimmer and faint movement in the wind conjures a living unease, as if some other order of life grows in cycles entirely unlike anything known.

**Built, Tech, and Remains:**  
Manmade traces are scattered, if discerned at all — at first glance, indistinguishable from the stone. Here, a faint curve is visible beneath a slab: the low, huddled arch of a collapsed shelter, its floor paved in smoothed stones caked with mineral rime. Heaped among standing rocks, small hand-piled cairns nestle, marked sometimes with scraps of almost-plasticized bark or ancient, rustless metal hammered thin and woven into water-channels. All craft is utilitarian — low, resistant, revealed only in moments of close scrutiny. Simple tools of rock and clay sometimes found in crevices suggest attempts at shaping or collecting water, or perhaps just marking passage.

**Sound:**  
This place is never silent: wind surges and whines up the terraces; water hisses and spatters in a thousand frequencies; when the sun hits just right, rocks emit weird, ringing harmonics as if the world is singing to itself. Some stones, hollowed by erosion, take on a resonant quality — step past and a wavering hum chases your footsteps. When the height-born breezes run, sound gathers and multiplies, vibrating in the stone, making certain shelves throng with overlapping tones. It is nearly too much for the senses, a wilderness of echo and resonance.

**Ecology, Rules, and Weirdness:**  
This is not an ecosystem, but an active machine of mineral and atmosphere. The near-absence of traditional “life” is compensated by the restless interaction: mineral forms propagate, not biologically, but chemically — one accretion seeding another, colony-like shapes spreading as rainfall leaches elements from adjoining rock, vapor meeting with new storage in glistening rattles or spectral crusts. Sometimes, boundaries between solid and liquid and air break down altogether: a shelf quivers, and for minutes feels more liquid than land; a stair-step of terraces will ripple, not visually, but palpably beneath the feet, as if memory or possibility is as present as mass.

At intervals, light itself splinters and bends at impossible angles, showing glimpses of recursive landscapes contained within the stone — plateaus inside plateaus, terraces within a pebble — a vertigo of reality layered atop itself. To remain here is to feel that stone, water, and perception are complicit in a larger, less comprehensible circulation, in which your presence is a brief, observed anomaly.

**In summary:**  
The Windscried Weeping Heights are an upland world forged by water, brightness, and the patient choreography of erosion, now in a late act of evolution where animal and plant life are gone or never began. All is mineral, dynamic, and music: terraces dense with spined formations and crystalline drapery, glitteringly bright but cold, magnificent but uneasy, where the world’s own rules — of sound, matter, and boundary — seem to run thin and permit sudden leaps of impossible logic. The world is loud, active, and dense — yet empty, constantly re-making itself in a dance of seep, shine, and shiver.
