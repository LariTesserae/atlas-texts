You stand on a high plateau, and: yes, *it is a plateau,* but the word feels weak, like a drawing of a thing you’re seeing for the first time. Here, “height” isn’t so much above-sea-level as above *the void*, for there is no water below, or above, or anywhere. There is no sea for reference. The air, thick with mineral grit and stifling heat, shakes with reverb—noise as a constant pressure, rushing up endless splayed canyons and vaulting between peaks.

Look down: the terrain stretches, sprawled and splayed like the muscle of the world, a labyrinth of ash-hued spires and sun-withered ridges. Every edge is rounded. Each towering escarpment shows eons of exposure to a wind that scrapes and scrapes, patient and unsparing. But here, unlike a desert, the dust is not sand—it's a snow of metallic shards, planar, subtly magnetic, born from the world’s own primal industry. The ground at your feet even *hums* with that industry, a distant, ceaseless vibration, as if the very crust is a generator.

There is utterly no water, no plants. The only “life” that clings here comes in forms at the border of comprehension, more swarm and pattern than organism. Protein and cellulose never found their way here; instead, the plateau is home to endless hosts of crimped, chitinous things. They pour over the stones like living fractals, their movement an intricate, rippling mechanism: gears, valves, filaments that fold and shift. Some are as small as flecks of dust, and some so vast they bridge entire canyons, their outer shells tarnished to mirror the sky’s boiling pastel.

Everywhere, this biomechanical fauna dominates, in fractal behaviors: sometimes streaming, sometimes locking into lattices, sometimes—rarely—exploding into cacophonous conflict. The air is piercing with the drone of their motion: whirring wings, grinding mandibles, shriek-clicks that occasionally synchronize into a thunder that shakes teeth in their roots. If you focus, you can hear rhythm to the madness: an inadvertent orchestra.

The heat is ruinous. Sunlight glances off the metal-rich stones, painting the air in waves. The atmosphere is not our air at all, but something denser—corrosive and sharp, flecked with invisible ions from deep industrial processes slow-cooking in the bedrock. The sky is white, streaked with torn currents of faint violet and angular, impossible shadows—flaws or seams in the edges of reality, visible to the naked eye, as if the world’s geometry is occasionally stuttering. Sometimes, out of the corner of your vision, the ground seems to ripple or lurch: not with movement, but with a brief uncertainty about which reality it’s in.

No structures rise here. No human or alien hand ever built. Instead, the plateau’s immense, weathered formations stand untouched but for the works of the fauna—temporary hives or struts woven into the chasms, dissolved and rebuilt by the day, the week, the eon. The land looks as if it is *meant* to be empty, save these overwhelming hosts.

Technology—in the sense of industrial processes—is everywhere and nowhere. The fauna *are* the industry. Their movements, their heat-exchanges, their roaring herds and migration-waves: they drive the local cycles, both geochemical and energetic. Factories, if you squint toward metaphor, are realized here not as buildings but as processes: rivers of friction, storms of electrostatics, clouds of red-hot motes catalyzing reactions in the high, seethed winds. The world breathes in slow, clockwork pulses. Nothing is built, but everything is being *transformed*.

Time moves lethargically. Things change, but only at the scale of landslides or the millionfold growth of strange colonies. Cycles are measured not in seasons, but in epochs—though the great herds above and below periodically unleash chaos, the essential rhythm is glacial, continental.

It is loud, yes, but not in bursts. The noise grows until it achieves a kind of terrible steadiness—like the inside of an engine, or the surf of countless insect-wings. As you walk, your footsteps vanish beneath this ocean of sound.

You sense, too, that rules become liquid here. Probabilities buckle. Patterns are relentless, but always with deviation: paths spiral unexpectedly into one another, shadows bend wrong, colors double-back, and the wear on every rock follows not the logic of wind or weight but the secret mathematics of the swarm. Sometimes, portions of the landscape twitch or glitch, and something very large might be there and not-there at the same time.

There is no silence, no rest, no shade, but in this emptiness—high, dry, mineral—a world nevertheless flourishes. Entirely alien, hot and dense with life, built on relentless mineral industry and the overwhelming pressure of being, it is a landscape that never fully makes sense. And yet, in its completeness, it does not need to.
