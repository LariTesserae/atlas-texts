Below the scorched crust of a world that has never known rain or rivers, where no roots have ever tested the dry, crystalline matter for sustenance, there sprawls a sub-surface labyrinth on a continental scale—a realm so layered and intricate that its sheer depth confounds the mind. Upon first entering this place, the absolute absence of liquid is palpable: no dampness, no glistening beads, no echo of a pool, just the memory of dryness made mineral and dense.

The terrain is not cave so much as a seemingly endless tessellation of chambers, corridors, pits, and narrow fissures—sharp-walled and angular, as if the strata were split and shoved aside before ever being worn smooth by wind or water. Surfaces glint with warmth, the heat either radiating from the rocks themselves or perhaps seeping in from some deeper cosmic reactor, but there is no visible source. The world here is dim, sometimes lit by an angry ember glow from the stone, more often shrouded in dense shadow. Your eyes ache to resolve edges within the murk, and distant shapes fade abruptly as if consumed by darkness rather than mere distance.

Material here is a patchwork of mineral glass, compressed mantle-stone, and slow, brittle flows of metallic crystal, flawless but fragile. Rock faces fracture into sharp facets—bladed edges that catch and reflect any stray photon, giving the sense of lurking mirrors made from stranger elements.

There is life—or rather, a concentrated abundance of things that move and feed and sense, though none are plant. The air is quietly thick with living presences: some fungal, sprawling across ceilings and into cracks as frost-white hyphal webs; others far more unclassifiable, trailing jelly or slender filament, feeding off the heat, the minerals, or even the faint flows of charge that race through metal-veined stone. At every turn there is a sense of silent company—a shuffle, a slow clicking crawl, sometimes a faint susurrus of shifting aggregate masses. These creatures do not hunt or flee; the world, steady and unchanged, is a world of niche and equilibrium: every organism finds its groove amid the strata, every space is occupied, no hunger is ever desperate, no topography shifts.

Artifact, if you stumble across it, proves humble and ancient—a cluster of cut crystals stacked like pygmy dolmen, a carved impression in a wall, the half-crumbled residue of a woven carrier-basket made from strands that were once living, now calcified. Nothing gleams with active power or intent—a world of simple craft, left by presences with minimal ambition beyond the next chamber, the next day.

The air is nearly still, laden not with breezes but with a faint, mineral tang and the subtle warmth rising from stone. Sound travels poorly here, muffled by density and darkness; only rarely does a scrap or clatter echo—a dropped pebble, perhaps, or the slither of many legs along a glassy shelf. Most of the time, there is only a hush, a silence heavy as the pressure from the eons above.

The rules of this place seem self-contained, closed unto themselves. Nothing decays swiftly; there is no rust, no rot, nothing green or wet. Energies are slow and cycles measured in eons: what is lost accumulates, what is born persists, the very shapes of features remain unchanging across all memory. Where above ground might be swept by sandstorms or scorched by sun, here, the world is set—every corridor a frozen decision, every chamber in perfect stasis. The absence of weather, of flux, gives the gravity of ancient burial; but within this density, life—strange, antique, thriving—makes its own quiet, glimmering mystery, sealed in darkness and time, forever unmoving, and yet, somehow, endlessly full.
