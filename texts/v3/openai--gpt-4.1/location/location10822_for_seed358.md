**THE SEEP**

You enter through a rib of stone: a slick threshold arched like water-bent glass, with warmth pulsing up from somewhere deep in the earth’s throat. Light here never gathers well, always hovering low and heavy, like the quiet after dusk. What illumination exists slithers in thin veins down the glossy walls—gray daylight barely gathering in pools on the raw, waxen floor.

**The Terrain & Atmosphere**

You are below—well below—the surface, inside a broad subterrene that unwinds across many miles. The ground is a sopping, embryonic plateau, neither stable rock nor weighted clay, but a kind of proto-crust, puckered and swollen with groundwater close enough to taste on the air. Mist trickles and collects in every dip, beading on overhanging planes and dripping in slow, metronomic chains. Pools form and dissolve with the muck of time.

**Materials & Landforms**

This is the brink of geology—a landscape in the making. Mineral filaments stretch like tendrils along half-formed ledges, promising future stone. Buried rivers press at the crust, sometimes surfacing in sudden, noisy gushes that cut sinuous, hyperactive channels, only to sink again, leaving behind new, bright spatters of iron and salt. The slightest slope is accentuated: one shallow rise and you have a clump of glistening gravel; one hollow and you find a pond gorged on drowned debris.

**Water**

Water is everywhere—suspended, gathered, saturating the air. Surfaces sweat; the ground is swollen, slick, and exhaling vapor. Thin, perpetual drizzles thread down from gaps where the world above is only a distant, ghostly memory. Here, water is a presence more than a substance; you feel its humidity press against your skin, hear it churning and gurgling in unseen caves.

**Vegetation**

Plants are rare. Occasionally, where a glint of weak light slips in from fissures above, you find a clump of tough-stemmed sedge or a pale, swollen moss hugging the stone, colorless from lack of sun but stubborn, latticed together in clenched webs. Mostly, slick lichen and fungal sheets form low, spreading constellations across the gleaming bedrock, exchanging nutrients with the damp.

**Built Remains & Technology**

Spread thinly across this sub-surface expanse are half-submerged marks of intervention—low, corroded engine casings mounted on stone, clusters of pipes thrusting into mud, a skeletal crane slumped over an open sump filled with oily runoff. These are the debris of some tentative, perhaps failed, industry: single-function machines, skeletal and overgrown, built to extract or stabilize or redirect the waters, now left to corrode in the frequent flooding. Electric arcs flicker sometimes in the murk, poorly maintained but stubborn, drawing power from deep below.

**Fauna**

Where vegetation is rare, life has filled every other possible space. The place hums with animal presence: amphibian bodies slip between rocks, slick and quick; insect swarms riot in luminescent clouds, clinging to moisture; burrowing rodents churn the perpetually wet ground, creating honeycombs of passageways. Unseen throats croak and shriek, and, at the edge of the pools, something always moves—often multiple somethings jostling for the same patch of warm, lightless earth.

**Density & Scale**

There is no open air, but everything is *packed*: life in every crevice, vapor thick as breath, technologies jammed into any accessible pocket, and the terrain itself—swollen, brimming, barely enough space for all that needs to exist here. Yet the place unfolds, not as a chamber, but as a maze of overlapping, echoing valleys and hollows, spreading out across the regional scale—town-sized, interconnected by half-formed watercourses, echoing like a vast, inhabited lung beneath the steady country above.

**Erosion & Change**

Change here is slow—imperceptibly slow. The marshy ground settles by fractions over years. New runnels open so gradually you’d miss them if you weren’t watching for decades. Everything is in the process of being created, not destroyed: the world is accreting rather than eroding. Stone forming from mineral sludge, life probing into new hollows, machines integrating into the slime or rusting in place, becoming part of the foundation for whatever comes next.

**Light**

Dimness reigns. There are no sudden flares or abyssal depths; all is an even gloom punctuated by reflected metallic gleams or the intermittent, fizzing neon pulse of industrial apparatus. Here and there, glow-worms and bioluminescent mold bleed quiet color into a haze.

**Sound**

Sound is everywhere. Dripping water forms a constant, near-deafening staccato. Every motion echoes: the scurry of feet, the scraping of shell, the far-off wail of steel straining under its own rust, the glassy crack of mineral suddenly cooling. Machinery coughs and rumbles, reverberating in these close vaults, amplifying every move to a roar. Even silence here is audible—a thick, wet, congested hush.

**Atmosphere & Rules**

Life is compressed into every inch, pressure making coexistence both necessary and fraught. Everything negotiates for space: roots against rust, water against wall, animal colony against mineral crust. Movement is slow, adaptation relentless but patient. This world is not yet finished.

The rules are softness and persistence. New land is birthed as water and mineral and machine all struggle for dominance. A thousand things live and scrape and multiply in the gloom, crowding out emptiness, making this underworld as loud as a city and as crowded as a beehive—an embryo of world still becoming itself, patient and deafening, waiting for the day it finally breaks the surface and finds true form.
