The crevice is impossibly narrow, more a chasm in miniature than any human-scale canyon—a hidden pocket chewed into the flesh of something larger, forgotten. Imagine a vertical fissure only a few strides wide, plunging downward through a blocky maze of immense mineral slabs, far above the normal ground, high amidst enigmatic cliffs. Here, at the top of a crumbling, ragged bluff, the crack is sealed on all sides: no rain, no surface stream, no mist ever enters. What should be a sterile place instead pulses with unnatural vigor.

The rock walls enclose a secret world: a thicket of mineral “plants,” densely packed, crowding so close there’s almost not enough space to move between them. These life-forms—if that is what they are—are lush, exuberant, and utterly alien. In the dim light that leaks through hairline fractures overhead, gleaming blue-gray stalks bulge with crystals, bristling in fractal patterns up the walls. Every cranny is swollen with clusters of silicate “leaves,” sharp-edged and layered, shivering ever-so-faintly in currents of cool air that whisper, almost forced, through pressure-differentials between stone faces.

There is no water here, nor any sign that water has ever passed. The flourishing vegetation subsists by different chemistry: sheets of metallic lichen and feathery dendrites draw slow sustenance from the minerals around them, leeching life from the rock itself. Small, knotted columns fizz with unseen, orderly reactions, their surfaces ever-glinting with motes of green flame, consequence of some elegant exchange more patient than any living metabolism. There is a taste of ozone and rust in the air, sharp as the world’s old bones.

Despite this frenetic crowding, the air is cold. The temperature hovers only just above freezing, enforced by the stone’s own long memory. The constant sound fills the space—a resonant, mechanical growl, punctuated by sonorous metallic notes and the staccato ping of stones fracturing deep inside the cliff. Nearby, half-swallowed by mineral growths, are the remains of obscure machinery: the husks of squat generators, thick power cables, rusted control panels, and chunked gears, their function unreadable. Sometimes a dim bulb buried in tangle flickers on, but always dies down again.

There are no animals—no insects, no birds, nor even the simplest worms crawling unseen. The plants are all. Nothing preys, nothing flees, no pollinator ever arrives. The rules here are ancient, yet entirely static: the minerals nourish the growths, the growths entomb machinery, the machinery hums without intent. Every engine that ever whirred here now sits locked in perfect equilibrium, grinding but never degrading, in a cycle that is so long-established as to escape notice—everything is worn, but nothing changes. Time never shifts the configuration. What broke is broken eternally; what endures, endures forever.

And yet, it is alive; a riot of inhuman order. The only movement is the trembling of the crystal “undergrowth” as it pulls dry nourishment from ancient stone, the hesitant flicker of misplaced electric light, and the ever-present, never-varying low roar that fills the crack. In this place—hidden, dense, timeless—life and machine and mineral are one: impeccably balanced, lush, crowded, cold, and profoundly strange.
