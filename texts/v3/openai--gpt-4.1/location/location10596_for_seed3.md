At the edge of something vast and unfinished, the world unfolds in impossible elevation — a young, uplifted plateau thrust against a quilt of dense, slow-moving cloud. Peaks jumble wildly upward, stone still half-born, their faces glazed in a lichenous gloss, fissures sweating beads of green water that gather in narrow runnels and silent, hovering pools.

This high place is dim, lit in a permanent overcast. The sun strains through dense, skewed vapor, scattering a syrupy light the color of tarnished pewter. Mist drapes the city’s sharp skeleton and fills the immense, hollowed-out valleys between errant towers — towers not of old stone, but the strange, modular shapes of newly realized matter: rebar exoskeletons and unfinished mineral lattices erupting from naked ground. Here, the city is more accreted than constructed: slabs, girders, and vast sheet-fabrications waiting for their greater unity, every surface sweating damp.

The air has a constant, deep resonance, as if the earth itself were humming — or groaning. Periodically, a note rises: machinery clattering in its laborious growth, cables sparking blue and white where the mist condenses circuitry in the open air. Water and electricity seek one another in this high place, spawning rivulets of steam or pools that sway with static charge.

Vegetation splays across hard edges: mosses, thick-veined ferns, vineworks curling out of crevices and half-formed street-beds. These plants drink from condensation pooling in forgotten hollows and gutters, and from the brief but intense rains that roll off the slopes. Yet, even their greenness is patchy, occurring in bursts, leaving broad stretches of stone and synthetic bone exposed to the cool, metallic air.

Life is everywhere: not unseen, but present in restless motion. You glimpse it best in peripheral fragments. Baronial slugs bigger than shoes rasp the undersides of catwalks, chitinous creatures flit among the glow-tubes, their shifting bodies often bearing more legs than symmetry can explain. In the muted under-rhythm of the city, there are sharp cries, animal or industrial, and the scrape of carapace against metal. Flocks of impossibly thin, light-struck birds fire through interrupted beams of mist, and the sky at times seems a shifting, living surface: wings, scales, shreds of silhouette.

The atmosphere is thick: not oppressive, but viscous, as if time’s flow is slowed, the world hesitant at the moment of its own making. Nothing is finished. Everywhere, growth — stone, city, organism — is more suggestion than achievement, as if the fundamental rules for form and neighborly space are still being rewritten. Sometimes, an angle subtends impossibly, or a stairwell runs both up and down from its landing; bridges connect places that do not visibly meet, curling into dimness and returning out of phase.

Amid this are the structures of occupation. They are built with intent, but intent constantly outpaced by emergence. Walkways cross from one dense, luminous cluster to the next over canyons swathed in mist. Rail lines, half-functional and half-seminal, alternately shunt raw ore and living passengers, the latter sometimes phasing in and out as carriages traverse overlapping networks of reality. Streets are damp, echoing, spreading out like roots or veins, often intersected by chasms where the underlying stone is still rising, not yet set; residents use winches or take effortless leaps, as much speculation as flesh.

Through it all moves industry, burgeoning rather than decaying — steam and piston, electric drone, the echo of deep engines. But here, industrialism is an evolving thing: not the rusted past, but the chrysalis of now, generating both matter and anomaly. Factories produce not just goods but new laws of physics, which seep into the surroundings unpredictably, encouraging fauna to multiply in odd symmetry, for spatial boundaries to fray, for time to stack in local, slow turbulence.

Noise is everywhere, amplified by echoes off new-minted stone, thunder rolling through the framework of the city, the dull pulse of construction and the communal babble of creatures and commuters alike. Sometimes there is a low, choir-like moaning as the city’s metabolism stirs, structures reconfiguring slowly in a kind of half-dream.

Rules here are contingent, always halfway written: distances shift; directions invert politely; occupants find their own passage forward and back changing, slow and certain as rising ground. The world is young, raw, its own physics still wet to the touch. And yet, lives thrive: urban and wild, machine-form and flesh-form, all sparseness and abundance intertwined in the enormous gap between what is and what might soon be.

Here, atop the new-wrought highlands, you breathe something cool, electric, and wild, and know you stand among the city’s first dreams — not yet memory, not yet finished, but immense with becoming.
