You descend through a fissure in stone — bare, bite-sharp in its newness — then slip through a velvet squeeze, and emerge into a vertiginous amphitheater, all held in the palm of the earth. The scale is subterranean-pocket: it could fit in a grand ballroom, yet feels vaster, emptier than such finitude should allow. At your back, the fissure’s vertical light blurs, then closes, replaced by the riot of fullness ahead.

**Terrain, Materials**
The “ground” is a notion, not a thing: the walls, floor, and arching overhead are woven, brambled, and filigreed with layers of living plantmass. Imagine roots thick as wrists braided into pillars, membranes of leaf stretched taut, mosses painting geometry across crumpled surfaces, all in shifting colors — warm greens, bruise-purples, sap-yellowed chartreuse — alive, wet with condensation. There are no rocks; the substrate is plant through and through, plants so utterly dominant that any bare space seems to flicker from existence, sealed up by quick vegetative growth.

Somewhere, water isn’t *present* in pools or rivers, but it is everywhere in the air, misting, glazing every green silhouette with fine droplets. Every so often, a drip jumps the gap, plinking onto a quilt of fungus that eats the moisture in a heartbeat.

**Atmosphere, Sound**
The air is humid, warm, and oxygen-enriched, smelling of crushed grass, snapping stems, and camphor undertones. Light pours from holes in the vaulting ceiling, glancing from moist leaves, catching in “pockets” of translucent roots — a dappled, almost underwater quality. But the sound is overwhelming: the plantmass is not still. Branches flex and shift, roots snap open fresh crevices, membranes stretch and pop. The ceaseless wet whisper of transpiration, the hiss of evaporating mist, creaks, sucking squelch of sap, sudden percussive seed-cannon shots — the entire ecosystem is in motion and *barking its presence*.

**Ecology**
There is very little apart from plantlife, and what little fauna exists moves at the edge of perception: a blur under a mass of leaves, a fleeting flicker in a velvet alcove. No true animals — perhaps proto-creatures, plant-made “arms” scuttling, tendril-limbed insects barely distinct from their substrate, or plant symbionts given temporary mobility to relocate spores. There are predator/prey dramas enacted on microscopic and vegetative scales: a carnivorous bloom slowly envelops a careless tendril, a pulse of toxin caught in a dense cortex of phloem, abrupt shifts in foliage signaling a chemical war invisible to the newcomer.

**Rules of the World**
Here, plant is not mere background — it is actor, builder, destroyer, monarch. There is no soil, no stable ground; everything grows, digests, rearranges. You tread on living mats, steps sinking slightly as mats contract, react, send commensal signals. A place of newness: the pocket-warren hasn’t existed long, has not settled, its forms are sharp and hopeful, always lunging for space.

Weirdness crackles. Physical laws become malleable; your sense of distance flickers as you move. Pivot between root-pillars — were you walking a tunnel, or climbing a wall? The space seems to tuck and fold itself, sometimes bloating into impossible vastness, then collapsing back to mere armspan. You glimpse the same hunch of plantgrowth ahead again and again, as though the world loops or your passage circles in a logic unbound by Euclidean tradition.

**No Built, Primitive Tech**
Nothing here is made by hand, but hints of order: a ring of tough-woven stalks forms a kind of passage, or a tool-shaped knot dangles, imitating a clasp or grip. These arrangements are accidental, emergent — or perhaps plants have begun, in mimicking movement or light, to shape form to purpose, to lay down their own precedent for tools and implements.

**Dynamics**
Everything is in flux. A rain of seeds drums on the living mats, skittering into invisible gullies. Fleshy bladders push out their volume, then retract, cycling moist air. Acidic saps pulse, etching new tunnels, the plant-flesh healing behind. Light shifts as leaves swerve to intercept it, closing some shafts, opening others. You’d never find the way you came in; even the “door” behind has been occluded by thickening tangles.

**The Place Lives You**
It is an intimacy on the scale of chaos: you, a rare visitor (prey? symbiont? yet another vector for plant ambition?) are enveloped. You feel the pressure in your ears, a kind of language in tone and motion, as if the world demands you adapt, sprout roots, lend your cells to its churning green. You are not *in* it, so much as on the brink of being absorbed by it — to become one more rare, searching presence swept into the emptiness, between the thunderclaps of growing things.
