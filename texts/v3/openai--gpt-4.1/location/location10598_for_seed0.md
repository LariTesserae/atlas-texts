There is no path to follow but sound.

To enter this region, one’s senses tighten by reflex, unsure what counts as threat or mere abundance. In all directions, pulsing, breathing mass fills the blackness, shuddering with motion. The air is thick, not solid but saturated; it presses with a fever’s warmth and a green, heady dampness, each exhalation instantly dampening the body. Moisture beads, trickles, and steams, but never pools to open water except in crescent hollows, slick chasms, moss-choked arroyos where wet collects and vanishes in cycles unseen.

Somewhere above, darkness holds sway—it is a blackness not merely of night, but of enclosure, layered foliage, and rain-thick cloud. No full sun pierces here. Instead, the shadows are interleaved with slim, crooked darts of bioluminescence: fungus on bark, the quick flick and slither of glowing eyes, the humming swarms that stitch skeins of dim light between trunks and roots and leaves, all swallowed quickly by moist shadow.

The ground is fleeting—a latticework of roots, freshly torn by storm, upthrust by subtropical vigor. Where earth exists, it is dark and springy, rich with the pulse of decay and growth wound together, too freshly made to have settled, young in its rawness: bark yet to rot, leaves only half-collapsed, earth barely made from the crush of yesterday’s fall. Sharp, jagged exposures rise here—jutting roots, raw banks where the soft ground falls away, exposed stone still crisp and unrounded by time, protruding into the huddle of life like knuckles through flesh.

The region is low in elevation, but never flat—ravines and quick rises, ditches thrusting between heaps of moss. Mist collects in these lower runs but does not settle, always churning in the turbulence of movement, wind, and heat. There are no vast viewpoints; the open scale of the landscape is measured by the roar of commotion rather than sight. The sense of space comes from tension, never the eye.

Vegetation is nearly absolute, a crowding green so thick it is hard to pull a hand through without resistance. Ferns and twisted palms, vines festooned with swelling droplets, climbing each other in a ravel of surging life. Trees rise but rarely stand alone—epiphytes, lianas, and mushroom-crusted bark cloak every trunk. At the margins, quick-spreading grasses and flowering shrubs explosively take any clear space between swells of older growth.

If eyes cannot help you here, ears become both guide and tormentor. The wildlife is endless, cacophonous, and their chorus is not harmonious but writhing—layers of shrieks, croaks, drumming appendages, leathery wings, ululations and piping, the complex beating of many hearts. Massive beetles grind through the undergrowth; unseen lizards rattle through the canopy, their scales reflecting faint, shifting glows; frog-creatures launch themselves blindly, slurping from concealed puddles; the air is alive with somatic noise. Even the plants participate: some seeds crack explosively, some leaves resonate to passing vibrations, mosses squeeze out hissing plumes in the turbulence of a passing animal’s wake. To speak, to call, is to compete with a thousand throats.

Animals rule here—predators and prey, swarm and singular, vast and minuscule: whiplash serpents up tree-boles, curtain-like bats shivering between vines, huge centipedes clambering delicately along raw stone faces. Anything not moving is soon colonized, devoured, or woven through. The cycles of tension—life, death, consumption—are immediate, constant, and obvious. Nothing stands for long. Flesh, leaf, and loam are shaped and unmade by sheer pressure of living things.

The world’s energy is turbulent: storms regularly turn clearings upside-down, micro-avalanches of soil and mulch spill after every heavy rain, and new channels cut through the ground at the whim of each deluge. The forms of the place change—what is a thicket today may be, tomorrow, an open pool, quickly clogged again by leaf and root. The land’s youth is evident in newly-scarred banks, burst boughs, slabs of bark torn free; it is a place being made and unmade by organisms locked together, every inch contested, every patch of darkness and warmth swarming.

No artifact stands here. There is no trace of the built, and nothing made—except perhaps the woven platforms of some mammalian nest, or a crude tool of stone or bone dropped and lost in the heaving biomass. If intelligence resides, it has not tamed or cleared, but merely survives—a contest, not a conquest.

The rules here put life on equal terms with the elements: the darkness is not absolute, but rarely relieved; water is plenty but always contested, always sought. All shelter is fleeting, constructed out of nearby matter, never lasting beyond its need. What’s weird is the scale of it—the sense that the usual balances of predator, prey, and silence have tilted, not toward peace, but toward an enthralling, deafening pressure, where light is rare and life is thick, and the only boundaries are those drawn—temporarily—by overwhelming motion and sound.

To walk here is to be swept into a maelstrom of biology: to disappear almost immediately, to become part of the flow. There is no escape from presence. Intensity is the rule; brief stillness, the exception; and above all, under the obsidian vault choked by green, the music of living things thunders on, never pausing, remaking the land moment by moment.
