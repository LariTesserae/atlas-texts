*Above you: taupe cloud-glass, veined with moisture, sifting brilliant diffuse sunlight across a high, cool plateau. No wind. Light pools everywhere, slick and dazzling—refracted, reflected, caught.*

## Terrain

You stand upon a *constructed* land. Nothing here belongs to geology: the ground is a tessellation—seamless yet tightly packed—of gray and pearl plates. Each tile vaguely square, but not standardized; their borders knit by careful fitting rather than machine precision. The surface is faintly convex in places, pooling tepid, shallow water in unexpected basins—like palms offered upward.

Elevation undulates: low, abrupt swells, sharp-edged ridges, contoured ledges rising a meter or two before flattening, never rolling or melting away. Everything seems fresh, as if constructed yesterday. No smoothing, no wear—edges snap against the horizon, lines of demarcation are crisp, shadows are razor-fine.

The land extends beyond—the scale of a scattered township, or the interior of a vast empty forum. Some clusters of angular constructs reach up, conical or stepped, a few tiles higher than the rest; others dig in: shallow, angular hollows, filled with accumulated water, shimmer and pulse beneath the optics of the bright sky.

## Materials

All is mineral here, but not stone as you know it: a composite of sandglass, quartz, clay mixed until it gleams but does not sparkle, crafted by hands or hands’ analogs, not machinery. Surfaces have rough, primitive scoring—tool-marks, pressure marks, sometimes even crude geometric marks or glyphs scratched in looping lines. Everything bears the patience of shaped matter, cold-fired, air-dried, nothing alloyed or fired in modern ways. Rarely, muddy pools gather in bowl-like depressions, their rims indented from repeated hand-lifting of water. Sometimes, pebbled glass nodules or hard, bone-colored slabs have been set in tiles, perhaps as measure or marker.

## Atmosphere

The air is bright, thin, and wet, every breath dense with the promise of precipitation. Droplets cling to every angle and flat, crawling silently across the super-saturated surfaces, collecting into long slow tears. When droplets reach a tile edge, they trickle over, drop by drop, feeding the little pools that never quite empty, never quite flood. Condensation is constant—the plateau is swaddled in a cloud's edge, cool but not cold, the sun’s brilliance muted and dispersed by the misty overlayer. No sky is visible; everywhere overhead, shining fog.

## Sound

Nearly silent: the only sound the faint, intermittent plink of water as it drips from joint to joint, sometimes an echo from a hollow. Rarely, a distant trickle as some pool overtops and overflows down a channel. No wind hums across the tiles. Your footfalls are almost lost in the damp, glassy hush.

## Ecology

There is no green, no animal motion, nothing familiar to a living world: no moss or lichen, no birds, not even windblown seed. Instead, sometimes on the crest of a well-lit ridge, thin films appear—sheens of unconscious, mineral life, a self-organizing lattice that refracts light a little differently, rippling softly over centuries. In deep cracks, trickles of water breed slow-glass, growth so gradual it remains invisible, etching lines that hint, faintly, at self-repair.

## Rules and Weirdness

All *is made*, but tools never rose past the crude. The world’s density comes not from empty space filled, but from an obsessive overlay of presence: tiles packed so tight, with no earth, no wild interval, the very atmosphere seems ordered, compressed by the hand of construction. There is a logic to it, a geometry or pattern—alien and simple, yet more pervasive than anything an individual mind could achieve. Maybe there is no one here now, maybe there never was. Maybe the land built itself, tile by tile, in a kind of mineral urge. Or maybe the world was built in memory of something; the only record, these tessellated, shining plates.

At regular intervals—measured by some inhuman logic—markers rise: not pillars, but squat, primitive forms, their faces etched with the same looping glyphs, some worn faint by water but not by touch.

## The Feel of the Place

You walk, your presence bending no grass, stirring no creatures, changing nothing you tread upon. Light is constant and all-defining; water, ever-present, seeps and drips and accumulates, the world perpetually fresh-washed, perpetually new. The environment’s slowness is patience, the silence immense but never total, a place constructing and reconstructing itself in cycles quieter than rainfall.

Here, density means fullness: nothing is wasted, everything is deliberate, space is entirely occupied by the intention of placement. Yet all intention is primitive, slow, and inhuman—making a beauty of restraint and repetition, an ancient order to the mineral rain.

### To linger here is to enter a kind of communion—not with life, but with making, with iteration, with slow, mineral memory in a world where nothing is left to wild chance, and still water collects, condenses, and persists.
