Here, under the familiar world’s floor, the land is just awakening to itself—a hidden valley-plain below the crust, cupped by the unfinished hands of raw stone. The whole region is embedded beneath the outer strata, lit from natural clefts in the ceiling above, thin slices of daylight falling through rough, fractured plates of rock and scattering across the ground in pale, solid beams. The air carries the scent of stone dust mixed with something wet and growing, as if the place is equal parts cavern and clearing.

Terrain & Materials  
The terrain is uneven but vast, swelling in wide, shallow undulations: mounds, shallow basins, and raw shelves of rock fogged with condensation. The stones are pale, granular, shot through with seams of new minerals still weathering out of formation; some surfaces shine slickly where groundwater beads, feeding small, intermittent pools. Elsewhere, the earth is stripped, naked, speckled here and there with the beginnings of soil, all mixed with the shattered debris of rock not yet worn to sand.

Despite sub-surface roots, the stone is unfired, shot through with half-formed cracks. Gravity has not yet pressed these surfaces into their destined smoothness. Instead, it is a place of edges and fragments—a geology that’s incomplete, expectant, both delicate and densely packed.

Water  
Moisture is a nervous presence—a restless drizzle from above in some cycles, condensation in others, forming thin rivulets that worm through the scattering of rock depressions. Sometimes a gentle rain patters down from ceiling breaches, pooling in bowls of stone or gathering in spidery trickles that etch promising paths along the raw ground. These water pockets are rarely stable, swelling and receding with each moisture cycle, never coalescing into rivers or a continuous stream—just ephemeral, cool mirrors, glimpsing slivers of the world above.

Vegetation & Ecology  
Life has only begun to gather courage here. Vegetation is sparse, with just enough to suggest the idea of growth: a scattering of stubby mosses, lichen clinging fiercely to shadowed cracks, the occasional pale stalk piercing through thin sediment. Everything grows low, close to the ground, built for the fragmented light and unpredictable moisture—no trees, and only rare, scrappy bushes gnawing at the edges of stone.

Animal life is rarer still: the flicker of something small disappearing behind a slab, the dewdrop shimmer left by sluglike creatures, long feelers recoiling at the rhythms of water droplets. Sometimes, in the silences, there is the briefest skitter of feet or low, burred rustle. It is a hush full of possibility, not absence.

Built Presence: Settlement  
Despite nature’s newness, the space is packed with the intent of human (or near-human) habitation. Woven throughout the valley are the marks of an emergent city-state, dense and sprawling, every inch occupied. Buildings cluster tightly along every ledge, hollow and stackable, cut from the living stone or raised from timber hauled from other places. Walls are rough-calved, joined by mortise and tenon or by simple stacking and mud daub. Pathways are worn into passageways, many layered and channeled, hugging the contours of stone. Occupants have burrowed or walled off niches in rock faces, forming a honeycomb of dwellings, meeting spaces, and storerooms. Some structures rise up on stilts built of root-wood, standing above the hungering fingers of damp, while others stretch out horizontally, tunnel-like, hand-dug with primitive chisel and pick.

Crowded as it is, the city is methodical—collections of huts, warehouses, gathering platforms, and water-harvesting surfaces, all constructed with the logic of survival, defense, and collective adaptation. Every surface has a purpose, every ledge or depression pressed into service—storage, habitation, or the delicate cultivation of food and fiber plants within moist recesses. Communal crafting spaces hum with the quiet industry of stone flakes, woven rope, bundles of moss and root crop. The people are inventors of necessity, living by manual craft and simple metallurgy, shaping what they can from what can be dug, bundled, or chipped.

Atmosphere & Sound  
Sound is rare and hushed, absorbed by the packedness of forms and the hunger of the stone. Every noise is purposeful or faintly accidental—the muted scrape of rock, a scatter of tools, quiet splashes echoed in a cup of water, a low conversation lost in the stone labyrinth, the breathy growl of wind threading between high beams. Sometimes, water drips from the ceiling’s fractured seams onto hollow stone, a patient ticking. Conversations cluster in low, private tones in enclosed spaces. The dense, crowded settlement muffles echoes; nothing is allowed to travel far.

Dynamic Motion  
Though the landscape is still becoming—its stones barely shaped, its soil barely born—it is alive with activity. Moisture cycles force people and creatures alike to move, to harvest, to shore up the settlement against brief floods or long stretches of dryness. There are cycles of soft rain and dry waiting, the shifts of atmosphere bringing gusts of cooler air from the upper world, mixing with the underground’s steady chill. The inhabitants, both animal and human, move in broad patterns: foraging, scavenging, tending to each fragile shoot or crawling thing, always responding to the living pulse of new processes—erosion, condensation, anatomies of hope. And beneath it all, the world itself is changing, forming, waking up in the crush of stone against stone, in the push of water, in the ceaseless urge to grow.

Rules & Sense  
This is a place where everything is in the act of becoming—structures, habitats, roots, even the ground itself. To survive is to participate in this raw assembly: to gather, to claim, to form, to press order into the new. Rules are those of careful balance, resourcefulness, communal occupation. Nothing is wasted. Everything is squeezed into what space can be found. Life, here, is a matter of invention, constant adaptation, and the careful tending of emergence—building a world inside the world, under the world, as the world builds itself.
