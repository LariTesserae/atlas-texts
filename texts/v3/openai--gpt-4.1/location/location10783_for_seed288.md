#### Below the Skin of the World

You descend, not down a shaft or through a tunnel, but as if slipping behind the visible, past everything easy or sunlit, into a vast and hidden stratum — one worn for unthinkable aeons into near-perfection by time. *Here* the land lies stretched out, lower than lower, shielded from the sky above by half-remembered stone and layers of age.

The ceiling is immense and patient, a weight of mineral drift and memory, pocked here and there with rare mineral windows admitting only a breath of gray-green dusk. *Light trickles, never pools*; sight is always half-guess, always edge-lit in twilight, the color of old sea glass buried in ash.

The atmosphere chills the skin: not freezing, but always cool, always humming with the energy of earth kept from the sun too long. Dew never forms — not for lack of cold, but for the complete absence of water. *Water does not belong here.*

#### Dry Abundance

But life — against all logic — *thrives*. Vegetation crowds the stone in bony forests of crystalline stalks and papery, papyrus-like fans. Their roots claw for ancient salts and leathery nutrients wrung from rock, for little else remains for nourishment. Succulent bulbs and filamental tendrils form sparse colonies, each spaced in neat patterns, too aloof or too careful to ever entangle. Clusters grow up from mineral blisters in the ground, each plant careful with its energy, unfurling only rarely, and then with dignity.

#### The Lay of the Land

Across this cool, cavernous open — as large as a quiet valley swallowed by earth— the floor is a mosaic of salt flats, packed dust, ancient polished scree, and silted stone, eroded by an age without rain. Once, rivers of gas might have flowed, or vast chemical winds, but those dramas exhausted themselves millennia ago. Now, the terrain is settled into gentleness — softly undulating, constitutionally ancient, void of obvious erosion except the endless smoothing of time and windless settling of dust.

Occasionally, the land breaks into terraces and elemental boulevards — buried by epochs, long ago carved out for logic or labor, now more like natural architecture than city. They extend in regular intervals, marked by boundary stones and scattered, geometrically-set boulders, abandoned but uncannily organized; suggestion of design, swallowed into geology.

#### Urban, Without Hands

This underground metropolis is not built by hand or mind as you know it — there are no walls, no machines, no streets in the human sense. Instead, life has layered itself architecturally: dense knots of vegetative growth form “buildings,” arching up into vast natural vaults; root-mats bind platforms, laced together into sprawling levels. In petrified clearings, colonies of immense insect analogues or soft-bodied burrowers congregate, making wide, orderly “courtyards” — emptiness held by collective memory and mutual need.

On the margins, burrowing creatures and reed-bodied fauna tunnel through the mineral substrate, creating intricate, honeycombed infrastructure. Each living shape is both home and city — there is no separation between organism and environment, only endless coexistence, woven slow, in nearly static cycles.

#### Sound and Silence

By some organizing principle — not one you recognize — *life here is loud*. The roar is not industrial or mechanical, but biological and constant: clicks, reverberating thumps, the low grinding of shell and limb on stone, the whispery mad chorus of insects cascading over one another. Herbivorous beasts, their breath piping through carapace vents, add a background drone that rises and falls with communal tension or release. Occasionally, a sonic event passes: a “rain” of crystalline leaf-fall, a dense swarm’s chittering spiral, a collective warning vibrated through the supporting stone.

The air quivers endlessly with the sum-noise of existence in all directions; there is only silence in the rarest interludes, held like breath before a storm or after some mass movement.

#### Slow Motion, Steadfast Life

Change comes only slowly: root, stone, insect, and beast all move according to glacial clocks. Generations pass almost visibly, each creature or colony barely stirring except at the longest intervals. Paths, both through mineral and root, seem patient, almost mindful of each other’s boundaries, as if respecting ancient truces.

There are boundaries here, but few real walls — emptiness is respected, and space between, more than any single form, sculpts the landscape.

#### Rules of the World

Water is never a factor. Chemistry is dry, solventless, stormless. Muted minerals and exchanged gases feed all things. Light is dim and perpetual; day and night mean nothing below so many meters of world. Technology is biological, not mechanical — every “device” has grown, evolved, and settled over ages into its use, indistinguishable from its “nature.” Nothing here is startling or oddly bent — all is grounded in a logic of patient adaptation.

And always, the sound: the relentless, natural hymn of beings enfolded under the old earth, carrying on slow rituals in a city built without hands, in a world dry, cold, and ancient, but never silent, never still.
