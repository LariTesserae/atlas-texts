**This place is known, by those who track it from afar, as the Cresting Chasm: a world suspended between heights and hollows, submerged not below but *within* a world-ocean of frigid clarity.**

---

### TERRAIN

Imagine a supercontinent: a sprawling, crystalline tableland, raised upon massive, prismatic escarpments that rear like the broken shell of some impossible mollusk. The *ground* is not ground in the usual sense, but a field of mineral outcrops, glassine ridges, frozen conglomerates. Sharp, young cliffs splay outward, unmarred by time, their edges photogenic and unnervingly pristine; occasional ledges and buttes jut into a void too vast to see across.

Yet even as the plateau runs to the horizon, all of it is deep beneath an endless ocean — an ocean not of tepid or tropical water, but of gelid clarity, light refracted blue and silver, cold to the core. The pressure is immense, yet the land itself seems adapted to bear it; mineral pillars thrust upward tens of kilometers, like rooting stalagmites, separated by canyons where water moves but only rarely stirs.

Nothing *soft* remains. There are no soils, no plant remains, not a sifting of silt — just bare, cold rock, new-cut as if by some planet-mason’s knife.

---

### MATERIALS

Every surface is stone, glass, or crystalline alloy; mineral sheets fracture to form pseudo-terraces underfoot. In some places you find streaks of oxidized metal, spreads of rare-earth nodules, and the glint of prismatic salts adhering to brittle overhangs. Deep fissures breath out faint, oily bubbles that shimmer in the thick water before collapsing in silence; everywhere, dull reflections play along exposed surfaces with the steadiness of a world preserved in lucite.

Along the higher outcrops, sharp grains — tiny, mechanical — collect in sheltered pockets, picking up the faint, vibratory residue of human industry.

---

### ATMOSPHERE & LIGHT

Here, light is not absent — it is instead omnipresent, filtered through kilometers of water but rarely dimmed. The world-ocean, though deep and cold, is uncharacteristically transparent, so that illumination pervades every crevice. The quality of the light is crystalline, glaucous: a permanent twilight, sometimes brilliant, sometimes eerily soft, never truly dark.

Particles hang suspended, inert, never settling — as if arrested at the moment of their falling by some subtle equilibrium. There is no fog, no blooming algae, no organic haze — the water is too pure, too cold, too vacant for life to chain together into webs.

---

### SOUNDS

The emptiness is not silence. Instead, there is a nearly *deafening* soundscape — the world vibrates with the unyielding, tectonic groan of mineral under stress, the faint resonance of superstructures flexing in the current, and above all the ceaseless, overwhelming rush of water battered through narrow gullies, split across glassy ridges. Large, slow sound waves propagate for kilometers, rebounding endlessly in the empty corridors between pillars.

Encounters with fauna are marked by the thunder of movement: massed schools, sometimes shoaling in unison, percussion-like, sometimes echoing the brutal arithmetic of industrial machinery. Some of these sounds are artificial, relics of ancient engines, half-buried turbines or decommissioned mining rigs lying silent — until pressure equalization or passing creatures stir their rusted mechanisms and produce a metallic clangor that ripples outward, pulse after pulse.

---

### ECOLOGY

No plant, no macro fungus, no green or brown thing. Life here is muscular, armored, mineralized, or ghostly, thriving in the perpetual cold. Great segmented invertebrates, gigantic and blanched, swarm across glassy slopes; some drill into stone in slow motion, chewing out intricate tunnel networks, holding fast to the stability of the rock. Schools of semi-luminescent, ribbonlike animals, glass-bodied and flickering, undulate through sheltering fissures in their thousands, their kinetic energy gelling the water with deafening, periodic vibration.

Other creatures seem almost mechanical, carapaced in metallic sheaths — whether grown or scavenged, no one can say. Predation here is not pursuit, but proximity: vast, filter-feeding bulks hover, waiting for swarms of tiny arthropods to brush against their ciliae — a flicker, a brief acceleration, a sudden cloud of exuviae, and the drama is done.

Some inhabitants seem to have adapted to exploit the scattered artifacts left from a vanished industry: you might glimpse crustacean forms manipulating a corroded pressure-vat, or swarms nesting amid a cluster of pipe-joints extruding from the naked bedrock.

---

### BUILT ENVIRONMENT

There are marks of thought, of action, yet there is nothing planned in the surface world’s layout. Instead, artifacts lie scattered: a half-sunken magnetic rail, still intermittently humming with ancient current; a corroded aerostat anchor, now colonized by encrusting animals; a deepwater engine block, immovably wedged in a gulch. Some of these structures still tremble faintly with residual energy — others, inert, stand as hard points around which tides and fauna organize themselves.

No settlement survives; whatever industrial endeavor once staked this place did so in isolated nodes. All that remains are fragments — meant perhaps for extraction, or construction, or observation, now long repurposed by the ecology.

---

### RULES OF THE WORLD

Nothing changes here, not truly — the crust fractures, pillars ascend or tilt, but it’s all at geological pace, almost imperceptible. (The industry that once razed and scored these surfaces is long dormant.) The fauna migrate only within strict, unviolated patterns; currents are slow, and the overall silence, despite its volume, reveals no real chaos. A world held in equilibrium by the pressure and cold, by vacancy and stasis. Since the flood — as legend might say — all has found its level and stayed true.

There are no seasons. No cyclic famine or bloom. No weather but the inexorable, planetary drift of the deep currents — so massive that they are more like landscapes themselves, shifting only imperceptibly across centuries.

---

### SCALE & ATMOSPHERIC WEIRDNESS

The world’s vastness enfolds all else: it feels more like the inside of a hollow planet than any ordinary ocean. You could spend a thousand years traveling and see just a single corner of a single mesa, so distended are the scales, so empty the volume between. Gravity and pressure adhere to a regime of their own, distances confuse the senses, and geometry becomes less about lines and more about echoes.

Here, between matter and nothing, pressure and vacancy, all things endure. The ocean is a frozen moment, filled with movement and sound but not with life as we know it — only with the silent record of something that happened long ago, and the echo that endures in water and stone.
