Welcome to the Faneworks of the Upper Machine Canopy.

**The Place**

You arrive inside a monumental chamber—scale enough to house a cathedral, yet smaller than a city square—a bounded space, yet one that feels infinite in the way an echo endlessly ricochets through caverns. Here, all ground and wall and vaulting overhead is constructed; not a single bit of natural stone or unshaped earth remains, only the layered, time-darkened bones of built matter. Vast, ancient, corroded metals interlock with glassy inlays, ceramic strata, and a bewildering puzzle of conduits and flywheels. Some of these architectures recall the ribs and vaults of gothic naves, others the irregular outgrowths of vines, yet all is clearly constructed—planned and made over ages so long the plan has been forgotten.

**Terrain and Materials**

You move through open lanes and near-vertical chasms, hemmed in by latticework that rises forty, fifty meters overhead and by domed alcoves cut deep into the walls. Footing, sometimes, is along wide gratings; other times, you walk on walkway-bridges of cleverly jointed stoneware, or on the spongy mats of engineered root systems that carpet the pressure plates below. Every surface bears the touch of industrial process: weld lines, embossed inspection markings, stenciled zones half-erased by time.

Any illusion of emptiness comes from the vast airiness between these features—the architecture is drawn apart, so the bulk of the room is nothing but exhilarating, vertiginous blank: open air and echoes. The world is hollow. Nothing jostles, nothing crowds.

**Water and Moisture**

Pockets of water collect in porcelain gutters and crystal bowls hung from the labyrinth ceiling, each fed by dripping condensation that weeps from pipes and coils overhead. Every few days, rain mists down from the upper grilles—a scheduled emission, driven by machinery far above—then is gone, leaving beads along every surface and veiling the chamber in ephemeral curtains. Even in the long in-between, the air holds a heavy, humid promise; only the thick-clad, ever-thirsting plants keep the air bearably dry.

**Vegetation and Ecology**

Against expectation, engineered verdure flourishes everywhere. No wild trees here, yet columnar vines climb the ironwork, their leaves fatty and phosphorescent-veined. Fungal caps bloom along seam lines. Mossphytes—creations of patient gardening, not evolution—spill from ornamental planters, bridging glass to glass. Giant pollinating insects drift the void like lanterns, their wingbeats lost in the greater roar.

The ecology is relentless, irrepressible, almost a parody of lushness. Yet each thing has its assigned place: plants that nestle into wall-hollows, roots that dangle in mist, animals that creep unseen but their rustlings and snappings announce a ceaseless hidden presence.

**Temperature, Light, and Atmosphere**

Heat seeps softly everywhere: a warmth with the edge of metal and stone, an even, lived-in temperature. Air circulators far above keep things moving with a patient, diffusive sigh.

Light comes from modulated lamps threaded through the superstructure, set on circadian timers—enough to create day and night, though never startlingly bright nor truly dark. Bands of daylight slant sideways through slats, prismatic edges bleeding color along the edges of porcelain and steel.

**Sound**

It is deafening: a thunder that never abates. The principal tone is the drone of ancient ventilators—a ceaseless, colossal exhaust, modulated by the shudders of pumps and the periodic clanging of load-shifting carriages trundling unseen rails in the dark above. Rain, when it comes, rings the whole chamber like a bell struck in slow motion. The rattle and rumble of biomass handling, the sudden shriek of a water release, the electrical buzz of trembling contacts—all are amplified by the curved, engineered surfaces. Living things must shout or go silent. Many of them—machine or animal (or both)—have found ways to communicate in frequencies outside ordinary ears’ reach.

**Fauna and Inhabitants**

Life abounds, though in pockets. Large insectile creatures, armored in mirrorlike chitin, crawl the steelwork and vanish into inspection hatches. Pseudomammals with cable tails and many-legged forms traverse ducts and pipes. Each animal is exquisitely strange, shaped by or for the place—descendants of deliberate introduction or unforeseen adaptation. Some have plastic feathers or resinous fins. Machines—self-maintaining, clanking at their assigned tasks—constitute almost a second fauna: wire-haired, box-bodied, some mimicking the movements once belonging to vanished animals.

**Weirdness and Sense**

Everything here obeys its rules, yet the logic is dreamlike—a harmony between organism and engineered intent, but the music has mutated far from its sources. Here, a turbine whirs to flower bright, silent moss. There, a root cluster unlocks a gear-latch; a beetle reroutes a drainage valve. No surface is passive: each plate and panel has meaning, end-use, or secret. Time passes slowly, with change written not in sudden events but in the green thickening of a wall, the slow swallow of a beam vanishing into epiphyte.

**Rules and Coherence**

No patch of ground is wild; nothing is untouched—yet the hand that made things moved with patience, and allowed time to take over. All is hybrid: living, breathing, decaying, rebuilding. There is no owner, and no border; there is only quiet domain.

Outsiders visiting this “forest” must adapt to void and din, to intervals of rain and trickle, to a sense of everything being constructed and inhabited and yet, somehow, utterly empty. This is a world within a world—a shell of vast design and brilliant entropy, teeming with purpose that even its makers could no longer decipher.
