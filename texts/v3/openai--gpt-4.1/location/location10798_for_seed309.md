**Below the Ivy Engine**

Somewhere beneath a patchwork city—so the surface dwellers would guess, if they ever guessed at all—the rootworks spread: a warm, perpetually dim subterrene, saturated with the musk of living cellulose and slow, metallic exhalations. This is not the damp, wormy underworld of soil and stone, but a vault carved wholly from and by green—except the green here is a riot of color, scents, and shifting purpose that outpaces any surface forest.

The walls, floor, and vast, girder-like columns are the vascular bulk of plants: living, breathing architecture, where structural vines coil into supports thicker than columns in any human palace; where mosses engulf every footfall space, soaking up condensation that beads constantly upon lichen-ribbed ceilings. Architectural forms seem familiar for a moment—arches, corridors, naves, halls—but quickly dissolve into growths. Doorways become the mouths of massive pitcher-plants lined with motile filaments; window-wells expand into blossoms wide enough to swallow a child.

Within this cathedral-sized pocket—the scale of a theater or half-forgotten town square—emptiness is as significant as substance. There is air, but always tinged with photosynthetic tang, thick and lush, pulsing in rhythm with sap flows. Space yawns between the major trunks and vine-cables, where only starlit bioluminescences stray, revealing swaying bridges, drooping tendrils, steam-punkish fungal engines, all laced with plant wires and cyclical machinery.

Despite the enclosing plant-mass, moisture here comes in pulses: a gutteral drip from dew-laden overhangs, or rain distilled from nowhere, falling for minutes through the midair, feeding pocked pools lined with root-hairs that tremble as unseen things disturb the water. There’s the ever-present condensation, humming faintly as it sweats from every living surface, pooling and then wicking away before true wetness can accumulate.

And in all the spaces between, utter cacophony: from behind dangling moss-curtains, swarming chitinous lives hiss, chatter, scream, and grind. Overwhelming fauna fill the voids. Much of it is insectile: endless beetle trains moving goods on their backs, moths with segmented, ringed antennae the length of serpents, shuddering with static. Fat, many-jointed rodents scurry through xylem-pipes and leaf ducts; membranous-winged things thump dull against the flesh-walls, hunting smaller prey in ritualized, cyclic patterns. Colonies of micro-raptors hunt in packs across the moss, their calls like whistles run through gravel.

Predator, prey, symbiote, parasite: all cycles run fast and furious here. You might step onto a velvet-moss causeway and watch it contract around an unlucky bird-lizard; a second later, the moss spits out only bones, that are quickly ferried away by patient, sap-drinking scarabs. Carnivorous flowers suck the lifeblood from beasts—and in their turn, are torn apart by swarms of flying mammals, who drag the blooms up, spiral into dark oculi overhead, and vanish.

In the depths, machines are not so much built as grown—through patterns guided by forgotten engineers and constantly reinterpreted by the plant-flesh. Pipes pulse with engine heat and plant-sap at once. You recognize turbines, but they flower and seed each autumn, uncoupling from their root-braces and crawling elsewhere. Streetlamps arise from stalks of bioluminescent bamboo, swaying in the moisture-laden breath of air exchangers. In corners, immense engine-beetles grind their way through renewable compost, generating a hot hum and erratic light—a power grid maintained by mutualistic flora-fauna assemblages.

Society, if such a thing exists, is one of constant negotiation: the inhabitants—be they human-descendants, hybrids, or utter stranger-things—move in constant awareness of territory and truce, for any patch of ground might shift, contract, or swarm. Territory is mapped more by living networks than clear walls; "homes" are grown and portioned, then deconstructed at the season’s end.

The rule here is: adapt, or be taken. Everything is active: the plants broadcast chemical signals that shape creature behavior; the fauna, by their presence, stimulate further growths and blooms, some beneficial, some deadly. Rain might announce a festival of spawning mites; condensation might herald predatory mold. All rhythms are entwined, industrial cycles becoming the very metabolism that animates the cathedral of green.

And above it all—a constant, loud, layered sound. Insect turbines, beetle clicking, carnivorous plant snaps, the guttural murmur of shifting root—like the pulse of a heart ten times bigger than any beast. Sometimes, deep beneath, a thunderous echo moves through the root-arches: something huge, perhaps never glimpsed, cycling beneath, forever sustaining—or consuming—the world above.
