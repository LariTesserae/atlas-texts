Below you, above you, all around you—fluid presses in, cold and heavy, its taste ancient on the tongue even as your lungs ache from breath withheld. There is no up or down here, no vast plain or looming peak; just the steady, iron embrace of a world drowned long ago. This is not a place of gentle tides or surface weather, but a soaked, post-everything realm, laid far beneath what you once might have called ground.

**Terrain:**  
The landscape is more absence than presence, but even so, it is dense—unbelievably so. Rock, silt, and the compressed bones of unremembered eras make a substrate so old it has gone soft, scoured to sameness by an eternity of water’s pounding. Smooth, glacial plains and silt-choked trenches run for miles. Caverns gape, their arching mouths half-choked by sediment, whispers of lost rivers now filled utterly with deep-brined sea. Every surface is shushed to roundness; corners have long since silkened into ellipses by relentless, grinding current. Scattered boulders are rare and strange—each is a relic of an ancient fall, standing out as curiosities, still-fretted by the pressure.

**Material:**  
Sediment is packed tight, unyielding, hundreds of meters deep—beds atop beds of the world’s exhausted bones. There are layers upon layers, pressed so hard together that the oldest have become new, strange stone. The water itself is not clear but dark—a thick, shifting presence, holding silt and age in every droplet.

**Atmosphere:**  
If you could call it that: the air is gone, replaced by darkness thicker than any cloud. This darkness is tactile; it brushes against the skin with chill, salted weight. Here, light is a rare, alien thing. Occasionally, a hint of blue-glow leaks from a fracture in the stone or stirs in the wake of some distant creature, but the light is devoured almost instantly by gloom.

**Sounds:**  
The realm is nearly silent. No wave breaks, no bird calls. Only the slow rumble of shifting silt and the far-off grind of stone on stone, born of the pressure’s endless rearrangement. Once in a long while, a sharper crack echoes—the collapse of an ancient arch, or the slip of a long-buried flaw.

**Ecology:**  
Vegetation is nearly absent; now and then, a slim moss threads a surface like spider silk, photosynthesis abandoned for feeding on the chemical ghosts of planet-deep vents. You find the ruins of what were once roots: long, pale, almost mineral things, now fossil shadows stalked through the gloom.  
Fauna, too, is rare. Finned shadows dart between cracks—blind, pale, with translucent flesh. Their hearts beat slow as centuries, pressing through narrows, scavenging what little lost matter trickles down. Now and then a crustacean, more shape than animal, flexes methodically through the silt, antennae trembling, always moving, remaking its world one tiny grain at a time.

**Rules of the World:**  
It is a place untouched by invention—nothing made by hand, no tool, no art, no construction. Nothing but what the world itself has remade, and has kept remaking, through cataclysm and stillness both. A pre-technology eternity: even life, minimal and survival-driven, bears the mark of beginnings.

Yet nothing, and no one, is ever still. The water is frantic with currents, swirling and knotting in unpredictable turbulence. Sediment leaps, drifts, collapses, is swept up and resettles. The very ground undulates minutely, settling toward a final, impossibly distant rest. Chaos echoes in every molecule, even as the overall shape of all things is flat, ancient, eroded—nearly finished, but kinetic, shivering at the smallest touch.

**Further Feelings:**  
Imagine being here: you float, held tight by density and cold, the weight of the deep crushing yet soft, packed all around. The silence is near-absolute, but the sense of unstable motion is constant—never dramatic, but always present. The darkness is not empty: it is packed with the memory of what came before, the ghost-forms of lives past, and the knowledge you have entered one of the oldest, most exhausted, yet forever-restless places in all the world.

This is the drowned heart of the planet: ancient, turbulent, always shifting, yet stripped to its deepest, most enduring bones.
