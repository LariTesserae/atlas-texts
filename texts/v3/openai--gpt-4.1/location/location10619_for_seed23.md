**Landscape: The High Runnels at Day’s Edge**

Close your eyes: faint wind presses cold, thin, bright air against your cheeks; light blazes sharp and metallic even behind your eyelids. The place thrums with a tension compounded of both silence and an invisible, frantic pitch of motion. You stand on high ground, a plateau whorled by time, its stone carved and bracketed by millennia of racing water and ice. The ground is old, familiar with scars, polished where feet and claws and hooves have passed.

**Terrain & Materials**

The land here is a broad ledge—just under a mountain's shoulder—spread with a haphazard confusion of standing stones and low terraces. The plateau surges like a tide caught mid-pulse, its weathered grey-and-bluish rock showing smooth runnels where centuries of runoff have cut tracks. Water is everywhere—not in open pools, but beading upon the stone, running in myriad silvery rills along every crease, weeping from fissures, gathering temporarily before plunging in miniature cataracts into cracks. The earth is sludgy, peat-black in shaded hollows; mosses (pale and crusted with frost) cling desperately upon the north faces of rocks.

Rarely, a copse of bristle-thin, yellowed grasses (no woods—no trees survive at this elevation) straggles around a particularly sheltered depression, the blades rattling with a sound like chitin.

**Atmosphere & Light**

There is brightness beyond warmth. The sun—high and raw, white as burnished ice—glares down from a sky unbroken, washed so pale it seems to seethe. But the cold is absolute, winter in the bones of summer, frost constantly recoalescing at the shadowed thresholds. The air rings clear, every breath is a spear of cold brilliance, the kind that seems to pierce the blood itself. Mists sometimes gather from the humidity, not as lowering fog but as sudden breaths of diamond vapor, torn to tatters by the ever-present upslope wind.

**Sounds**

Silence of a rare density sits here. Not emptiness, but silence layered upon silence: the hush of movement too small or too swift for the human ear, the whisper of water sliding over stone, the ticking collapse of ice, the tiniest skitter of claws or soft pads unseen. Any larger sound—a call, a shout—seems not merely muffled but rebuffed, as if the landscape itself refuses intrusion.

**Ecology & Fauna**

Life gathers according to strange logics. The severe cold and lack of shelter should suggest scarcity, yet everywhere the eye lands, motion: writhing tangles of blind, glassy creatures nestle in the dusk of overhangs—half-spider, half-crayling, their bodies slick with condensation. Large, translucent crustaceans with iridescent shells scuttle from shadow to shadow, diving down watery cracks to avoid predators that never show themselves fully, only as slick movements in periphery.

Small, wingless things with fungal antennae—armored, multi-legged—crowd every surface, combing moss, tearing at lichen, their numbers so dense the ground at times seems to ripple. Occasionally, something larger lurches past: an ungainly, pale-muzzled mammal with paddle-like paws, fur gleaming wet and slick, or a tripodal bird-thing with glowing yellow eyes and a call so high you feel it in your teeth rather than ears.

The ecological energy is frantic, competitive, relentless, every niche contested, every square hand-span host to three or six or nine varieties of oddly-jointed, frost-adapted scavenger, parasite, seeker. There is no lull; new life writhes forth at every meltwater crack, and the dying are devoured the moment their struggle slackens.

**Built Environment & Organization**

In the lee of the larger rocks, beneath overhangs or carved into dense peat banks, strange constructions—urban by density, primitive by design—cluster in labyrinths. Thin, stacked walls of water-smoothed shale bend in arcs and loops, creating the effect of an ever-morphing lichen colony, only built by hand: walls, platforms, crawl-spaces, and passageways—roofed with stitched pelts, interwoven reeds, and more of that tenacious, pale moss.

The “city” (if you call it such) is less a village and more a hive—passages loop over and under one another, each barely wide enough for a crouched figure, some so low only the smallest denizens can travel them. The built world grows as its inhabitants do: expanding, reincorporated, dissolved, and patched over as quickly as ice forms and melts.

Communal areas—if such divisions exist—are open to the air, but hunched and layered, draped in frost-dappled furs, with pits where water trickles gather, and wide, flat stones used for gutting, cleaning, mending. There is no permanence; the raw materials—incessantly eroded, scavenged, replaced—give the impression the settlement is a living structure, trembling in motion: adapting at a pace indistinguishable from frenzy.

**Technology & Rule of Life**

Technology here is direct, adapted to cold-withered hands: flint, knapped stone, bone, wood so rare it's scavenged from traveler’s packs or drifted up from valleys below. Tools are many, small, multi-purpose: scrapers, awls, hammers, levers, clever single-use objects crafted for a specific day’s task and then abandoned to rot or be repurposed. Cooperation is seasonal, the boundary between tool and ornament is barely there—many things are tribally encoded with meaning, function, taboo.

Life follows rules not of plenty, but of opportunity—a moment of warmth, a sudden flood of meltwater, a gust that shifts snow just enough to uncover prey. Everything is momentary, turbulent, and if a pattern ever forms, it is immediately pressed and warped by change.

**Weirdness & Permeating Strangeness**

Here, time seems both ancient and unmoored. The city waxes and wanes not by years, but by flows—of water, of creatures, of the angle of light itself. The abundance of life offset by the sparseness of green; the storm of forms press so thick and so strange that you are always stepping almost upon a living thing, yet never seeing the same one twice.

And every surface, every patch of ground, echoes with a restless agency—rocks weep water, “streets” shift with the tide of bodies, and the very whisper of air at times seems to echo, to hold meaning, to pulse with a message just beyond reach. You sense, with that animal certainty, that this place is more awake than sleeping; not malevolent, not welcoming, but intent, ever drafting itself toward some unknowable phase.

**Boundaries**

Beyond the rim of this small world, cliffs drop precipitously, sheer and forbidding, to lost cold valleys below. On every side flows—of water, of life, of wind—form a kind of living wall. The city is walled not by stone, but by the threat of exposure, the promise of freezing, by the relentless torrent of creatures, all striving, all rippling nervously in the bright, diamond-cold light.

Step softly, and watch where you place your hands: the High Runnels at Day’s Edge are never quite the same from one sun’s circuit to the next, but always themselves—frenzied, writhing, silent save for cold water and the faint, constant susurrus of things alive and moving beneath the brightness.
