It is a place large enough for storms to swirl without reference to the rest of the planet—a biosphere that folds itself into darkness and cold, pressed high against some ancient plateau, continent-sized and utterly unlike the lowlands. Here, elevation gives the cold an edge: the air bites, never quite freezing but tinged always with the chill that slides from black-choked heights before dawn. In this perpetual twilight, there are no wide rivers or lakes, but water breathes in silent intervals—condensing thick on fibrous surfaces, dripping into hidden hollows, collecting in pockets before vanishing to vapor. The moisture is patchwork, unpredictable. You might stumble into a misted vale or catch a burst of rain rattling hard against vast living spires, but more often, beads of condensation course slowly down vine-wound cliffs.

**Vegetation is total.** Plant-life composes the very mass of this region: not in dense, choking thickets, but in continent-spanning structures, spindles, and voids. The dominant flora gather and rise, forming bridge-arches of slick lignin, walls like the bones of titanic trees, struts and platforms layered with moss so dense it mutes some footfalls, but doesn’t nullify them. Roots and stems twist into latticed ridges, rising endlessly through fog and shadow, crossing empty gulfs. There are caves inside trunks large as city blocks—most of these spaces echo with emptiness, but sometimes pulse with the heartbeats or calls of creatures. Plant-matter is not so much ground as possibility: it becomes bridge, shelter, spire, tunnel—a living architecture draped in creepers and lichen, spangled with droplets that catch the rare gleaming of bioluminescent fungi. 

This place is **hollow and vast**, its scale always threatening to make you feel small. Between the mighty plant forms yawns echoing darkness—hundreds, sometimes thousands, of meters open between arch and stalk, canyon and creeper-bridge. Fog collects and disperses with a will of its own, shifting luminous clouds drawn forth from nowhere and dissolving into cavernous volumes. Rare sounds—an animal’s call, the grating snap of an untethered vine falling, the muffled rumble of a night-storm—can travel leagues before meeting opposition. Voices here are lost, not because there’s no place for them, but because the volume devours noise until something else, louder, supersedes it.

**Sharpness and youth** define the terrain's face: these structures feel freshly lifted, brutally emergent. Plant-towers break from the ground on impossible angles, less eroded than torn forth, their surfaces marked by the recent struggle of growth. No gentle humus; edged, fibrous, living seams reveal wounds and scars, raw and unweathered. Sometimes you see where an entire ridge has split, revealing the quick, pale marrow of the plant’s interior. Water, when it gathers in these valleys and cracks, slides fast and away, carving nothing yet—merely passing, unanchored.

**Cold hue dominates—**not absolute night, but a darkness flecked with bluish greys, the touch of distant starlight, and the incandescent flicker of life. The air is often filled with a loud, shifting chorus: the soughing of wind stirred to violence by wide temperature differences, the crackle and hiss of clashing frost and dew. Animal life—abundant—echoes through hollowness: things with resinous voices, wings that beat in unison, clicking herds of limbless grazers moving from condensation pool to trickling seep. Half-glimpsed creatures nest in the knots of the titanic trees, leap from one unfathomable arch to the next. 

Some of these animals are familiar and some not: long-legged striders that feed on the mosses, tiny gliders that chase the bioluminescent floating spores, and great burrowing worm-things that keep whole arches trembling. All must prize mobility—the vastness between food and refuge is measured in desperate spans. 

**Sound is unbidden and omnipresent**: the wind’s roar, the crash of plant limbs torn loose and plummeting to void, the clamorous song of complex lives. Yet, for all its noise, the place can grow suddenly, terribly silent: the shift before a storm, the moments when some great predator suspends breath and motion, and the very architecture seems to wait.

**Built marks are rare and crude:** a fire-scar at a joint where three vines knot together, a windbreak wall of lashed boughs, the remains of a kiln in a deep plant-bole, bones lashed with fiber, simple lattices woven across chasms. Sometimes you find half-formed tools—chipped ice-axes of plant-glass, moss-slung cradles, huts grown rather than built—suggesting a people or creature that adapts to the living substrate, carving, weaving, never dominating.

**Tech here is primitive,** outstripped by ecology. No metal, no smelted fires; everything is hand-fashioned from the world itself, scavenged or coaxed from the living, dying, or already-dead material that forms the only available substance. The rules here are old and direct—survival, movement, balance against emptiness and cold, attentive listening to the shifting songs of plant and animal alike.

**Weirdness tints the place just slightly off-center:** not so much impossible, as unfamiliar in logic. Gravity here seems adjustable—some leaps linger a little longer, some falls slow a touch before impact. Weather is peculiar: a pocket of warm fog may surround you for minutes, then vanish into frostbitten breezes. Plant roots pulse if pressed—living but not sentient, or perhaps simply tuned to the passing weight of all who walk above them. 

This high, hollow world is alive in ways that baffle the senses, endless and empty and deafening and bright, all at once—plant and animal, structure and void, tumult and silence, cycling together at the top of existence, draped in endless, living darkness.
