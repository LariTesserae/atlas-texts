There is little horizon here, for all that the space is open. You do not see far; you see crowded layers, a labyrinth of low walls and stooping archways, corridors glistening in shadow and alcoves wrapped in ambiguous gloom. The ground is never quite level nor quite finished, pocked with rises and hollows, as if shaped more by organs than geology. This is not a place wearied by millennia—eroded—but a place beginning, unfinished, structures dense and tight as if the world had only recently learned about corners and pushed them everywhere at once. It has the cramped intensity of a city grown overnight in a single packed breath.

The air is humid, and when you brush a surface the moisture beads up, thin and constant. If you pause, you might see drops forming between cracks in the stone, or slipping from a ceiling arch and falling in bright, oily lines. Although rivers do not run here, water is always present, secretions between paving stones, slick on vertical faces, fog condensing in low places and drifting in the air, lending everything a clammy sheen. The scent is mineral: damp dust, something metallic, and the almost-not-there ozone of newness.

Scattered illumination seeps through, never enough. It is like perpetual twilight: soft, unfocused beams filtering through veiling mists or through narrow, high windows. The city’s structures are hewn from dense, ochre and slate stone, newly split and stacked, as if clay or silt had been hurried into these block-shapes and left unpolished. In places, the walls pulse faintly with warmth when touched—remnants of the earth’s own heat, not yet lost or fully contained.

Nothing grows here, not in the sense of flora—no moss, no lichen, no seedling. The surfaces are all bare matter. Yet "bare" is not sterile; in the thick, wet stillness, colonies of strange, delicate veils might dangle from archways—perhaps fungal or crystalline, perhaps something simpler, not quite alive or dead. Sometimes, in the half-light, you catch the ghost of a sheen or filigree: fragile, shifting films across puddles, hinting at new chemistries or unclassifiable colonies suspended between stone and water.

Yet the true inhabitants are animal. Here, the city is crowded with life—abundant, present in every cubic span. You do not see them—or not at first, not clearly—but you sense them, surging and shifting beneath the atmosphere’s low roof. Creatures nestle in apertures and crawl through fissures. Some are squat and six-legged, some delicate and multi-eyed, some simple shapes of vulnerability wrapped in carapace or slick skin, all moving with heedless confidence through their dense world. None have taken the luxury of standing tall; all are built to navigate the low, packed channels, the layer upon layer of constructed shelter.

The noise is impossibly loud—not chaotic, but saturating, as if every wall hums with the press of life. You hear the relentless patter of claws, the drip and plop of condensed water, the rising chorus of chittering, clicking, keening notes. In the dimness, the city is never silent nor even quiet; a continuity of sound pulses through the stone and air, giving the sense that the city itself is alive, speaking through a thousand layered throats.

Despite the primal atmosphere, construction is everywhere. The built world here is born from hands or claws, showing marks of repetitive stacking, shallow carving, tiles pressed into soft mortar while it is still curing beneath the warmth of the ambient ground. Bridges of stone span short distances, and countless passageways intersect at every height. The craft is basic—no metal glints, no glass, no pigments; just the unpolished manipulation of earth materials, and the shaping of raw matter by brute force and simple knowledge.

Here, time seems thick and held in suspension. The city persists, changeless, with no gradients of erosion, no scars of time-weather, no flow of new or decay of old. Everything is as it has always been and ever will be, like a single breath that never empties, a pool that never ripples. The result is eerily stable, almost unmoving in its totality, even as the individual creatures leap and scuffle, their acts never accumulating to shift their habitat’s equilibrium.

It is a landscape at the threshold of becoming—wet, packed, resonant with animal presences, new and already settled. The city waits in the unchanging twilight, dense and labyrinthine, its creatures loud and ceaseless, its structures crowded and raw—a world that never quite began, and so need never end.
