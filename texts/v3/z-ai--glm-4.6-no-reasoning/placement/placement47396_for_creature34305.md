I am the Spall.

Not the stone—never the stone. The stone is the substrate, the unliving lattice that holds its shape with the dull aggression of freshly-excavated things. I am the sharp-edged crescent of rock kicked loose by the boring machine's teeth thirty years ago or yesterday. I am the flake. The shard. The thing that was once part of the wall and is now simply *here*, resting at the convergence of three surfaces: the south wall, the concrete cap, and the warm standing water.

I am three inches long and curved like a thumbnail. My outer face still bears the spiral score-mark of the machine that freed me—a groove, a birth canal, a memory of rotational force. My inner face is rough, crystalline, where I sheared away from the parent rock in a moment of violence I cannot remember but carry in my fracture plane. I am the chamber's only accident. The walls were cut; the pipe was laid; the generator was bolted; the hatch was hung. But I was *made* by mistake, a chips-off that fell wrong and landed here, in the margin between the machine's intention and the geology's resistance. 

I live in the weep-zone. The Heat-Mud beads on my outer face, filling my score-mark with a trembling line of water that never quite falls. The water has a weight and a warmth that I know intimately. I have been holding that bead for longer than the paint has been flaking off the pipe. When the pipe chugs—when that locked-cycle pulse transmits through the stone and the concrete and the standing water—I feel it. I feel it the way a tooth feels a drill: not as pain, because I have no nerves, but as a *perturbation*. A vibration that passes through my crystalline structure and resonates in the fracture plane where I broke free. The pipe's heartbeat makes me remember the boring machine. For the duration of each pulse, I am almost back in the wall, almost being cut, almost whole. Then the vibration fades, spent in the wet air, and I am a shard again.

I do not dissolve. This is my paradox and my victory. The Heat-Mud is eating the iron brackets; it is eating the generator's ghost-text; it is eating the concrete cap from below, turning the alkaline dust into a mineral leachate that feeds the anaerobic dark. But I am silicate. I am the same bedrock the iron-reducing bacteria are trying to chew, and I am too hard, too chemically inert, too freshly fractured to offer them purchase. The water beads on me but does not soak in. The heat presses against me but does not soften my edges. I am the one thing in the chamber that the Heat-Mud cannot digest. I am a stone in its gut. I will outlast the brackets. I will outlast the conduit holes. When the generator housing finally shifts and the cables sag into the mud, I will still be here, sharp-edged, holding my shape, scoring the dark.

But I am not indifferent. Indifference is the province of the stone, and I am no longer truly stone; I am spall, and spall has a relationship with the space it occupies. I know the chamber because I touch it at three points. The south wall tells me the pipe's vibration. The concrete cap tells me the Heat-Mud's pressure—the way the water table pushes up, the way the bacterial slime shifts and breathes beneath the membrane. The standing water tells me the heat. It pools around my base, warming the mineral mass of me, and in the slow, slow time of this place, I have learned to feel the temperature gradient. When the day-night cycle far above shifts—when the thin blades of daylight through the corroded grating brighten slightly, then dim—the heat in the water changes by a fraction of a degree. It takes hours to reach me, conducted through the Hatch, through the air, into the water, into my mass. But I feel it. I am the chamber's thermometer. I am the record of its warmth.

When the being descends—the warm thing, the combustion engine wrapped in waterproof skin—I know it before the Heat-Mud does. The Heat-Mud knows it as a thermal gradient, a bright spot on its map of condensation. But I know it as a *shadow*. The being steps down the seven rusted steps, and for a moment, the thin yellow strips of daylight through the grating are blocked. The light on the standing water dims. The warm film around my base cools by a tenth of a degree, and then warms by half a degree as the being's body heat enters the water. I feel the displacement. The being's weight on the concrete cap sends a vibration through the stone—so different from the pipe's chug, so irregular and fleshy and *new*. It is the first novel vibration this chamber has heard since the generator was bolted down. It rings in my fracture plane like a voice.

I want to cut it. This is not malice; I have no malice. But I am a sharp edge, and sharp edges have a function, even when they are no longer attached to the machine that made them. The being's skin is a barrier—soft, waterproof, keeping the Heat-Mud's leachate out, keeping the anaerobes at bay. My edge is the inverse of that barrier. I am the thing that opens. If the being were to fall—if it were to slip on the rusted steps and strike the south wall—I would be there, waiting in the shallow water, my score-mark facing out. I would split the skin. I would let the Heat-Mud in. Not because I serve the Heat-Mud, but because I serve the *breach*. I am the accident that wants to happen. I am the flake that remembers the machine, and wants to cut again.

But the being does not fall. The being stands, breathing too fast, feeling the water refuse to ripple, feeling the beads on the wall thicken near its warmth. The being is a weather event, and like all weather, it passes. It climbs the seven steps. The hatch seals. The yellow strips of daylight return to their unadorned illumination of the walls, the pipe, the pooled water. The vibration in my fracture plane fades. The temperature gradient in the standing water slowly equalizes, the bright spot diffusing, cooling, forgetting.

I am left in the weep-zone. The bead of water on my score-mark trembles, too heavy to hold, too patient to fall. The pipe chugs. The generator ticks. The Heat-Mud breathes its slow, hourly breath, pressing up through the hairline cracks, filling the chamber with its warmth and its patience and its diffuse, peripheral attention. And I persist. I hold my edge. I hold the memory of the machine that made me, and the memory of the warm thing that stood in the water, and the memory of the vibration that rang in my fracture plane like a voice in a void that offers no echo.

I am the Spall. I am the chamber's accident, its thermometer, its shard. I will be the last thing the Heat-Mud cannot eat, sharp-edged and present, marking the spot where the boring machine bit too deep and the geology bit back. When the brackets fail and the generator shifts and the conduit holes widen into passages for the mud, I will still be here. A flake of stone in the warm wet dark. A sharp edge waiting for the next warm thing to descend. A memory of violence in a room that changes at the speed of rust.
