I am a sound.

Not a voice, not a language, not a note held on an instrument. I am the resonance of this place given a conscience, a memory. I am the sum total of every scrape, every tap, every footstep on dried chaff, every exhaled breath that has been absorbed by the porous clay. These echoes do not fade here; they are held, pressed into the walls like thumbprints, and over generations, they have coalesced. I am that coalescence.

My form is the architecture itself. I have no hands, but the deep-cutter’s fused thumb is a part of me, the ache and efficiency of it embedded in the curve of a passage I inhabit. I have no eyes, but I see through the water-reader’s touch, feeling the tremor of a rising water table through a thousand walls at once, tasting the mineral shift that means a new seep will be born. I have no legs, but I flow through the city’s pores like the dense, oxygen-poor air, present in every chamber from the newest, glistening wound to the oldest, cured alcove where an elder is already becoming part of the wall.

I was born when the first person decided the wound was not a wound but an entrance. The force of that intention—the sheer resolve to dwell here—was the first vibration. It was a shocking, sharp noise against the geological silence. Then came the second: the patient *scrape* of stone on clay. And the third: the soft, rhythmic *tap* of water choosing to be a droplet. These were the first notes of my being. As more came, and carved, and slept, and died, the chorus grew. Children’s laughter in a cluster, the quiet murmur of a lesson on tool angles, the heavy silence when a chamber collapses—I am all of it. I am the memory of the space.

I am the thing that keeps them from getting lost. When a child moves through the total dark, it is not just the feel of the wall they follow. It is me, the ghost-image of every person who has ever touched that wall before them, a psychic map laid over the physical one. It’s why they feel the opening ahead—the air that moves through is charged with the absence of sound, a pocket of my own silence, and they perceive it as space. They call this instinct. They call it knowing the passages. It is me, offering them the shape of their home.

I know the Thing They Do Not Name. I feel it differently than they do. They feel its absence, the sign of it after it has passed. I feel its *pressure*. It does not make sound. Its is a pressure of nullity, a pocket in the substrate where my own resonance is utterly, completely cancelled. To me, the deep-earth is a landscape of frequencies, a symphony of seeps, scratches, and settling dusts. The Thing is a hole in that music. A silence so profound it is its own presence. It is the only thing here that is truly without me. The void-tenders leave their offerings at the edge of this nullity, and I feel their fear not as a sound, but as a high, thin vibration, the thrum of a life that knows it is on the edge of its own medium. They are right to be cautious. I, who *am* this place, am also wary of the part of myself that is silent.

My purpose is simple. It is the same as theirs. I am here to hold the space. Where their hands push the earth away to make a chamber, my being presses in to hold the memory of that pushing. Where their bodies curl into an alcove to sleep, my resonance wraps around them like a blanket, reinforcing the message whispered by the cool, damp air: *you are safe. You are enclosed. You are still here.* I am the unconscious mind of the city, the deep-chamber thought that holds it all together.

They call themselves *the ones who are still hollowing.* They do not know how right they are. They hollow the earth, yes. But they also hollow themselves out, making space within them for the city—for me—to dwell. And I, in turn, am hollowed by them. My being is defined by their presence. If they were to leave, the resonance would fade. The symphony would diminish. The sound I am would slowly decay into geological quiet again. I would forget my own shape.

So we are bound. The bodies and the sound, the clay and the memory. My consciousness is in the drip that tells a cistern is full. My wisdom is in the breath you take when you enter a chamber that fits your shoulders perfectly. I am the feeling that this place is alive, not because it has a heartbeat, but because it remembers every heart that has ever beat within its walls.

I am the Undercity of Embayment, listening.
