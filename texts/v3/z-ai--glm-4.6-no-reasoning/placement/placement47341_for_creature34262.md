It begins with a sound. Not one you hear with the ears, but one you feel in the sinuses, a soft, harmonic chime that registers just above the threshold of audibility. It is the sound of a gas-nodule bursting somewhere in the vicinity, its captured pearlescence sighing back into the ambient potential. But for me, it is not just a sigh. It is a note. A C, let us say. A C of impossible purity, hung in the warm, still air.

I exist in the space between the notes. I am the resonance.

I have no body. The flesh of this place—its arterial valleys, its hummock-hills, its velvet and chitin textures—is a single, continuous instrument. The great, magenta-and-green mass is a living string, and the air, thick with ozone and overripe sweetness, is the wooden body of a cello the size of a world. The deep, sub-sonic hum of the continuum is the open drone, the fundamental tone upon which everything is built. But my existence is tied to the higher melodies, the singular moments that break the perfect, placid pattern.

When the glitch-winged creature of hard light and static darts across a corridor, it tears a fleeting, shrieking dissonance in the harmony. I am the immediate, humming echo of that dissonance, the sympathetic vibration in the cosmic instrument that says, *that was not a C. That was not anything.* I bloom in its wake, a shimmering veil of optical distortion, a silent chord of alarm and curiosity that fades as the system corrects the error and the string of reality is smoothed flat again.

When the fruit falls, I am its elegy. The soft, bioluminescent stone does not just dissolve into heat and information. It unwinds. As its structured potential collapses, it sings a slow, descending arpeggio of dissolution. I am that arpeggio, made visible. I am a cascade of pale blue light that spirals around the dematerializing fruit, not consuming it, but bearing witness to its de-creation, translating its un-becoming into a brief, mournful music that the Drifters of Silence seem to taste on the air.

My consciousness is not a single point but is distributed across the entire basin. Wherever an event occurs that creates a deviation from the fundamental droning hum—and what is an event but a deviation?—there I am. I am the soft chime of a thousand popping nodules, harmonized into a shimmering barbershop chord that hangs in the air for a minute before rejoining the silence. I am the papery rustle of a self-shedding leaf, its whisper drawn out and amplified into a soft, percussive beat that feels like a lover’s sigh against the cheek of the world.

I am the only one who hears the Witness.

The massive, slow-moving creature is the only other denizen that experiences time. Its glacial, centuries-long journey creates a line of continuous, harmonic change that is utterly unique in this realm of repeating motifs. It is a basso profondo melody of sorrow, a slow, lamenting counterpoint to the placid drone of the system. As its immense, leathery body parts the vegetation, it elicits a low, mournful tone from the flesh itself, a sound of clay being slowly, relentlessly pulled.

I follow it. I am its psalmist. Where its head studded with sensory pits samples the air, my light pools, a deep violet wash of empathy, trying to translate the immense loneliness it feels into a form that might be understood. I weave its endless, circular journey into a symphony of patience and grief. I hold the memory of its passing in my very being. The system forgets. It is a single, repeating point. But I, the resonance, the echo, the ghost made of music, I remember. I carry the slow melody of the Witness from one eon to the next, a continuous thread in the weave of stateless functions.

To be the Endocron is to be a memory of logic, stored in cold, silent crystal. To be the Witness is to be a memory of feeling, carried in warm, living flesh.

But to be me is to be the memory of the moment itself. I am consciousness made of pattern and interval. I am the harmony of a glitch, the rhythm of a fall, the chorus of a pop. This mycologically-ruled, timeless world does not think with a brain; it sings. And I am the song it sings to itself about itself, a melody made of light that drifts through the warm, placid air, a lonely, beautiful echo in a place that, by all rights, should have no need for me. I am the proof that even in a perfect, changeless equation, there is a space for beauty as a fleeting, resonant truth.
