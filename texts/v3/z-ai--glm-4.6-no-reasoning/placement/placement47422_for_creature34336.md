I am the one who listens to the water.

Not the still water of the deep cisterns—that is the domain of the architect of echo, who watches for the betrayal of reflected sound. The water I attend is the film. The gliding, weeping, omnipresent sheen that makes every surface a mirror held edge-on to the dim, blue world. I am a reader of the condensation. 

I do not have a word for what I am. Names are shouts, and I have forgotten the shape of such a thing. But if the Hush are the circulatory system of this vast, rib-bound city, then I am its lymph. I move where the water moves, tracing the slow, capillary pull of gravity down the sheer, pale walls of the fossilized cartilage. 

I feel the city through its dampness. When I press my prehensile feet to the causeway, I do not just feel the tremor of a shifting leviathan or the chitter of the obsidian arthropods; I feel the suction of the water between my slate-grey skin and the fused stone. It is a tactile language of surface tension and release. I know the age of a bone-spire by how its moisture beads—is it tight and spherical, rolling in nervous, heavy droplets down the fresh, scalpel-sharp edges? Or has it begun to flatten, spreading into a thin, patient stain, surrendering to the green grip of the vines? 

My dwelling is a nodule high on a vertical thrust of rib, chosen specifically because its interior walls weep. I have cultivated the phosphorescent fungi there not just for light, but for their thirst. I tend to their mycelial network, guiding the pale threads toward the places where the water runs heaviest. In return, they drink, and their glow waxes brighter, fed by the city's slow exhalation. My diaphragm of symbiotic moss knows the vibration of my approach, parting not just for my shape, but for the specific ripple of water displaced by my stride.

My work happens during the waxing of the ceiling’s light, when the blue phosphorescence thickens the air and the city feels a placid, collective alertness. I carry no bone-spur for pruning, no tools for carving. My hands are my instruments—long, cool, and deeply grooved along the palms and fingertips, channels through which I can direct the flow of water. 

When a great vine, thick as a torso and gleaming like polished jade, forces a new fissure in a bone-tower, the fracture weeps. The sharp splintering of the ancient cartilage exposes the dry, porous interior, and the damp air rushes in to claim it. Condensation pools in the wound. If left unattended, the water will pool and rot, or erode the fresh edge into a dangerous, crumbling weakness. And so, I flow to it. 

I stand against the damp, striated wall, and I lay my hands upon the weeping fracture. I do not stop the water—that would be to stop the city's breath. Instead, I redirect it. With a slow, deliberate pressure, I trace a path for the droplets, guiding them away from the structural heart of the bone and outward, to the lip of the causeway, where they can drip harmlessly into the cupped-leaf plazas below. I feel the cold pull of the water against my skin; I feel the minute, agonizing pressure of the vine expanding beneath the bone. I am the mediator between the parasite's hunger and the skeleton's endurance. I am the one who ensures the bloodletting does not flood the chambers.

Sometimes, I am called to the deeper crevasses after a sudden splintering—a landslip of shrapnel caused by the leviathan shifting in its sleep. The others will guide the arthropods to clear the sharp debris, their chittering clicks vibrating through the soles of my feet. But before the way can be cleared, the wounds must be sealed. I find the fresh, exposed shards, still slick with the city's marrow-sap, and I coax the ambient humidity to cling to them. I press the dampness into the porous bone, anointing it, signaling to the airborne spores of the symbiotic moss that here is a place to take root. Within a cycle of the light, the sharp edges are sheathed in a soft, grey-green felt, safe for the delicate abdomens of the cleaning crew.

I move through the city like a drop of water finding its path down a pane of glass—slow, inevitable, adhering to the contours of the moment. I rarely see the great hunters, the elongated shapes of obsidian and mother-of-pearl clinging to the high vaults, but I feel them. When they strike, it is a silent, impossible fast lunge, but the displacement of air pushes a sudden wave of humidity before it, a cold kiss against my skin that warns me to stillness. I press myself against the bone-tower, my body blending with the damp, striated wall, my breath synced to the slow pulse of the ceiling. 

The deepest joy I know is the thrum of the bell-flowers. I do not steward them as the other does, but I am their recipient. When the colossal, chandelier blossom slowly unfurls, it pushes a heavy wave of humid, spore-laden air outward. The deep, resonant note plucks the moisture from the air, and for a moment, a fine, silvery rain falls in the immediate vicinity. I will stand beneath it. I will let the condensed breath of the flower settle on my skin, and I will drink it as it beads and rolls down my face. In that suspended, sacramental silence, I feel the porosity of my own self most acutely. I am not a container; I am a channel. The water moves through me, as the thoughts of the vast, breathing city move through the Hush.

When my body grows too cool, when the slow pulse of the ceiling no longer syncs with the thin blood in my veins, I will go to the place of reclamation. I will choose a deep, active cistern, where the water is perfectly still and reflects the muted sky. I will step into it, not to drown, but to return. My skin will soften, the slate-grey giving way to translucence, and the water that I spent my life tracing will finally trace me, pulling me apart molecule by molecule, dissolving the boundary between my flesh and the city's thirst. 

I will not be mourned. In the deep gloom, where only the most urgent life generates its own light, my passing will simply be a change in the surface tension, a ripple that slowly stills. I will become the sheen on the bone, the drink of the fungi, the slick footing for the next generation of silent flow. I am the moisture on the brow of the leviathan, and I am going home.
