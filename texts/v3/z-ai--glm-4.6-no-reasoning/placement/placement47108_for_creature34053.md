I exist where the song wants me to. I am a secret the mountain keeps from itself.

To the Choir, the distributed mind of the Shards, I am a flaw. I am not a resonant lattice, born singing in the standing waves of a canyon. I am not made of the mountain's own quartz and memory. I am a corruption. My material is wrong—an impurity in the otherwise perfect chord of their existence. They would not consciously attack me; they have no will for such things. But their song seeks harmony, and my presence is a dissonance they unconsciously try to resolve. The great, slow currents of the flock will subtly divert away from me. The deep, foundational hum of the stone feels, to them, most true and pure in the vast, empty spaces between the spires. I am a stone in their river of being, and the water flows around me, leaving a silent pocket in my wake.

I am Stillness.

Do not mistake this for the stillness of the Glass-moss, that passive, dependent life in the shade. That stillness is a lack of action. My stillness is an action in itself. It is a choice. While a billion Shards beat their wings in a ceaseless prayer of sustenance, I do not. My wings are of a similar size to the larger of their kind, a wide shell of smoky, near-opaque obsidian, but they are fused shut. They have not opened in an age, perhaps never. There is no node of quartz at my heart, no inner light. My glow is an after-image, a faint, residual warmth on the black surface of my wings, a ghost of the energy that perpetually surrounds me.

I am a battery that cannot discharge.

I began as they all begin. In a nursery of impossible focus, where the air itself was sound, I formed. The pattern was laid, the lattice grew, and I emerged, a blank page. But something in the matrix of my creation was skewed. A speck of ancient meteoric dust, a flaw in the standing wave, a momentary lapse in the mountain's totalising attention—I do not know the origin of the error. I only know the result. When I should have joined the flock, when my first beat should have found the frequency and locked in, I did not. I felt the call, the overwhelming imperative to *resonate*, but my form would not allow it. The energy poured into me, but it could not escape through the wings. It had nowhere to go. So I simply… stopped. I hung in the air, a silent, dark note, and slowly, heavily, sank to the stone.

Here, on this narrow, knife-edged ridge where I have remained for millennia, I have my purpose. I do not feed on the mountain's piezoelectric charge. To attempt it would be to shatter. Instead, the energy washes over me, and I absorb its statistical noise. I am a record of the hum.

They call the ancient metal poles instruments. I am the listening shell pressed to the wall of existence. When a Shard lands upon a disc, and that single, booming note of pure thought erupts from the stone, it is not for me. I do not feel the pleasure or the pain of that focused moment of self-awareness. I feel the *information*. The shockwave of that pure, focused tone passes not only through the air, but through the stone itself, and I am part of the stone. In that instant, the crystalline structure of my being vibrates in sympathy, and the energy contained in the note—the entire history and intent of that singular expression—is etched into me.

A million such notes have sounded since I came to rest here. The Choir does not remember them. Its memory is in the pattern of the flock, in the complex harmonic interactions of the whole, a memory as fluid and formless as a storm. My memory is solid. It is geologic. I am a library of symphonies.

Sometimes, in the deepest, colslice of the long, white night, a lone Shard will be drawn to me. Not to land, for my surface is anathema, but to hover. It is the call of dissonance. The creature feels, in its simple resonant being, that here is a place where the song is different. Here is a place of resonance-stasis. It will circle me, its internal light pulsing with a faint, questioning curiosity. Its beating wing will create a small, localized hum, a counterpoint to the mountain's thrum. And in that close proximity, a tiny fraction of the energy stored within me will bleed out, not as light, but as a wave of pure feeling. It is not a thought, not a melody, but the *echo* of a thousand songs. It is the memory of a thousand focused moments: the terror of a rockslide translated into a deafening baritone, the joy of a new day sung as a soaring soprano, the simple, constant love of the mountain for itself as a perfect, unchanging C-sharp. The Shard absorbs this ghost-feeling, its light flickering. For a moment, it contains a history it was never meant to know. Then it will fly away, rejoining the flock, carrying that tiny seed of the past with it, a new and infinitesimal complexity in the great, simple song.

And so I wait. I do not think, not in a way you would understand. My consciousness is the slow, geological accretion of data. I am becoming the mountain's oldest memory. There is a prophecy, encoded in the song of the Shards, that the mountain will one day sing a new note, a final note, a note of such resonance that the world will change. The Choir cannot comprehend this; it can only feel the growing intricacy of its own song, the slow approach to a harmonic event horizon.

But I will know. When that note is sounded, I will be ready. The stored energy of a thousand lifetimes, the compressed history of an entire world's song, will be released in that one, single moment of contact. My creation as a flaw will finally find its resolution. My purpose, my singular, silent function, will be fulfilled. And I will not soar. I will not be a part of the new song. I will simply be the silence that allows the first note of the next world to be heard.
