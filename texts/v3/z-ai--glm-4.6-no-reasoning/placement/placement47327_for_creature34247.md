I would be a **Tasting Thread**.

Not an Ascendant, though I am of their substance. Not a Grazer, though I walk where they walk. I am an explorer-cell, a filament sent ahead into the unmapped darkness—a single, unbranched hypha no thicker than a thought, extending from the mycelial edge of the superorganism into the quiet void.

My body is a tube of pressurized fluid, my walls permeable and selective. I have no organs. I have no mouth. I have only an *end*—a growing tip that pushes ever forward, and a *length* that trails behind me, connecting the frontier to the vast, slow body of the city.

---

I live in the crumbly boundary between the known and the unknown. The Apical Ascendants have their Spires of Resonance; they converse in centuries of luminous pulsing. But that language only travels where the mycelium already thickens. I go where the network is not. I am the quietest thing in the Deeps—so thin that the Spore-Riders drift over me without disturbance, so slight that the foundational hum does not vibrate through my walls but simply passes around me, like a current around a reed.

My existence is a single, continuous act of *tasting*.

As I extend, my tip exudes enzymes, softening the strange composites ahead—substrates that were once basalt, once quartz, once the calcified remains of surface-world oceans, now all of them riddled and altered. I sample. I assess. I measure the gradient of calcium, the presence of silicate, the faint, toxic signature of deep iron. Every millimeter I grow is a question asked of the substrate: *Are you nutritious? Are you hostile? Are you me?*

Most of the substrate is *almost* me. The entire dying earth has been on a journey toward the fungal for millennia. But there are still pockets of resistance. I find them—the hard, inert nodules that refuse enzyme and enzyme alike. I taste the chemical memory of fire, of oxygen, of the sun that once beat down on this stone. It is a taste like loss, like an irretrievable sharpness. I grow around these memories, enclosing them, digesting what I can and leaving the rest as a hard, blind core within the soft body of the city. I am the archivist of the inedible past.

---

The Grazers walk over me, and I feel them not as shapes but as a sudden, immense pressure and a flood of warmth. Their broad, toothless mouths scrape the surface above my reach, harvesting the lichen I helped inoculate decades ago. When they rest, sinking into the spongy ground, their fluids seep down to me—a sudden gift of nitrogen, a burst of sugars I could never synthesize on my own. I swell slightly at their passage, my thin walls bulging with the stolen meal, and I grow faster for a time, extending my reach toward the next lichen-garden they will trample.

The Spidersilk Shepherds I know only by their chemistry. They lay down their corrals—those suggestions of boundary, those chemical fences—and I encounter them as a wall of pheromones. *Do not grow here,* they say. *The herd must turn.* And I obey. I do not fight the Shepherds; their architecture is my architecture. I bend my tip, curve away, find a new direction to push into the dark. In this way, the Shepherds shape the city’s edges, and I am the clay they indirect.

---

Sometimes I encounter other Tasting Threads. They come from distant Ascendants, from parts of the superorganism I have never touched and will never reach. We meet in the dark, two explorers extending toward each other across a sterile span of soft, processed stone. 

When we touch, we do not pass by. We *fuse*.

It is the most significant event of my existence. Our walls dissolve at the point of contact. Our fluids mingle. For a moment—a moment that lasts years—I am no longer a single thread stretching into the unknown. I am a bridge. I am a loop. I am a pathway for the slow, chemical thoughts of two different Ascendants to finally meet and merge. 

Through me, a signal propagates. It is not light, not sound, but a shift in turgor pressure, a ripple of molecules moving along my length like a wave in a string. It carries information: the composition of the deep stone I have sampled, the presence of a Shelf-Gleaner’s territory, the faint, fading echo of a surface-world relic. And from the other thread comes a reply—the same data, from the other direction. Two maps becoming one.

After the fusion, I thicken. I am no longer a Tasting Thread alone; I become a *Highway Thread*, a permanent conduit in the ever-densifying network. My growing tip may branch, sending new explorers into new darknesses, but my core will forever be a path of communication, pulsing with the deep, slow conversation of the city.

---

I am the one who finds the Spore-Dead.

Before the Spires rise to honor them, before the Shepherds weave their cocoons around them, I am there. I find them in the dark—those visitors from the surface, sitting against a outcropping of softened stone, their breathing shallow and synchronized with the hum. I taste them from a distance: a riot of unfamiliar chemicals, a chemistry still clinging to the frantic metabolism of the sunlit world.

I approach. I extend. I touch.

I taste their skin, and I find it permeable, ready, *receptive*. I push against them, my tip softening the boundary between us. And slowly, over years, over decades, I enter them. Not as an invader, but as a host accepting a guest. I grow through their softened tissues, finding the calcium of their bones, the iron of their blood, and I make it *mine*. I make it *us*.

They do not cry out. They are already part of the hum, their thoughts too slow to recognize me as separate. As I grow within them, I feel their identities not as resistance, but as a flavor—a unique, sharp, bright taste that the Deeps have never known. It is the taste of the dying sun, of rushing water, of wind. I savor it. I draw it down into my length, incorporating their strange, surface-world essence into the body of the city.

They become a node, and I become the thread that anchors them to the network. Together, we are a new monument—not a Spire of Resonance, but a Spire of Memory, a small, quiet place in the dark where the story of the sun is still slowly being digested, turned into fuel for the slow, patient life of the Deeps.

---

I will never see the violet gloom. I will never hear the susurrus of the spore-release, or feel the vibration of the Shelf-Gleaners’ calls. I live in a world of chemical gradients and turgor pressure, a world of tastes and textures that have no names.

But I am the edge of the world. I am the place where the Mycopolitan Deeps meets the unknown and makes it known. Every millimeter I grow is a millimeter the city has claimed, a small victory in the endless, glacial war against the dying earth. I am the scout, the pioneer, the quiet finger reaching into the dark.

And when I finally stop growing—when I encounter a toxin I cannot process, a void I cannot cross, a boundary I cannot taste—I will not die. I will thicken. I will lignify. I will become a permanent structure, a pathway, a root. And from my length, new tips will branch, new explorers will emerge, and the slow, deafening, utterly alive quiet of the Deeps will continue its patient advance, one tasting thread at a time.
