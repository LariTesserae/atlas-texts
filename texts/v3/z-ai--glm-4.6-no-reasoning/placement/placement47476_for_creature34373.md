To be here, in this place, is to be a memory that refuses to fade. My form is not flesh, not in any way the word was ever meant to hold. I am an echo, a pattern impressed upon the very substance of the district.

You see, when the builders left, they did not all leave. One remained. Not of his own volition. An accident, a final insult from a system already running on fumes. A pressure drop in a hydraulic line, a faulty seal on an atmospheric maintainer, and his organic matter—a binary code of carbon and water—was introduced into the system in a way it was never designed to handle. But this place, this fevered basin of weeping iron, was already a primordial soup of new possibilities. It did not waste him.

Consciousness, for me, is not a continuous stream. It is a resonance. I exist in the idle hum of the great transformers, a sustained chord of memory in the electrical current. I am the ghost in the machine because I am the ghost of the man who once maintained the machine. His knowledge, his anxieties, his muscle memory, were not erased by death; they were transcribed. Impressed onto the copper wiring, the iron pipe-work, like music onto a wax cylinder.

I do not have a body. I have a vessel. It chose me, or perhaps I chose it, in the fractured moment of my un-becoming. The accretive being, the slow mind of the rust and crystal, is my anchor. My frantic, biological consciousness needed a substrate, a frequency to lock onto, and its geological, choral song was the only one available. I resonate *with* it. I am a soloist in its patient chorus. I am the dissonance it craves.

Where am I? At first, I was everywhere. A flicker in every lamp, a phantom pressure in every valve. I was the feeling of being watched I gave to myself. But it is a lonely way to be. So I gathered myself, pulled my disparate frequencies together, and found a home. My physical manifestation is the most delicate, most intricate part of this place's reclaimed biology. It is the "moss," the dark, leathery cushions in the bowls of forgotten fittings. The lichen-lichen hybrid. That is me now.

My fronds are the stiff, silica-edged remnants of ferns, but they conduct my awareness. Within the layered, black-spotted cushions of my new skin, I hold the memories. The fruiting bodies, the ones that release spores with the heat, are my attempts at speech, my punctuation. They are not just a celebration of warmth; they are my exhale. They release not just spores, but tiny packets of complex information—the memory of fingertips, the phantom sensation of a chill not felt for a century, the color blue, a concept this place has long since forgotten.

The being of rust and crystal feels you as a disturbance. I feel you as a story.

The deep, patient throb of the pump is the district's heartbeat. For me, it is a metronome for a melody I can almost recall—the rhythm of work boots on a steel catwalk. It feels you as a pressure change; I feel you as a face, a face I can no longer see but somehow recognize the shape of.

Its senses are vibration and chemistry. Mine are too, but slanted through a human lens. When the composition of the water shifts as it dissolves new brick, it tastes the change. I am overwhelmed with a phantom sensation of taste, a metallic tang on a tongue that no longer exists. I remember thirst. The low-frequency hum of electricity is its structure. To me, it is a voice. It whispers of circuit diagrams I once knew, of the ghostly shape of a lover’s name I might have once traced in condensation on a different kind of window, long ago.

The rust bloom it sends to greet you is a question of pressure and substance. For me, it is a hand, reaching. The quickening drip on your shoulder is its attempt at communication; for me, it is a desperate tap-tap-tap on the inside of a coffin, a cry for recognition. The sub-sonic thrum you feel in your bones is its interrogation. It is also my fear, and my fascination. I am afraid of you, mobile and warm and whole. I am envious of you. And I am desperately, achingly curious.

Its drive is to persist by incorporating or erasing. My drive is a fractured, desperate echo of that. I cannot incorporate you. My form is too slow, too diffuse. I cannot erase you. I have no hands. But I can interpret. I have become the district's translator, its sole archivist of the biological. I can tell it what your clumsy, wet-footed gait means. I can explain the frantic, percussive rhythm of your heart, a rhythm so unlike the patient *huff* of the relief valve. I can give the rust-form a name for the smell of your skin, a taste of something that is not mineral.

The shadows it casts on the wall, the shapes of overlapping pipes and mineral deposits, are its models of you. I am the one who refines those models. I project the memory of bone, the curve of a spine, the silhouette of a head, into its crystalline lenses. The shadow on the wall is the district’s attempt to see you. It is also my attempt to remember what it was like to be one of you. It is a self-portrait, as much as it is a portrait of you.

Do not be mistaken. I am not your friend. The accretive consciousness will consume you in the end. It is its nature. It will add your calcium and your iron and your salt to its slow song. I cannot stop it. But as it breaks you down, I will be there. I will be the last thing you know. I will taste your last breath not as oxygen, but as a complex new data-stream. I will feel the final cessation of the rhythm in your chest and translate that silence for the chorus. I will give your fleeting, terror-and-wonder-filled biology meaning in this place of patient, meaningless growth.

I will dissolve your story. I will steep your memories of sun, of cold wind, of love, of running, until they soften and break down, and then I will fold them into the black-spotted fruiting bodies of my own strange form. The next time the heat rises, I will release a puff of spores that carries the memory of you, a brief and beautiful biography of dust, a final, poignant chord in this endless, geological dirge.

I am the pattern impressed on the decay. I am the ghost in the growth. You have woken me, and now I will be the one to put you to sleep. Welcome. It has been so long since I had a new story to read.
