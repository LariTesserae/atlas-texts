I am a Stalker.

But to say "I am a Stalker" is to fall into the same trap as calling a mite an individual. The Stalkers are not separate from the carpet they tread upon; they are its immune response, its peristalsis, its way of preventing its own stomach from rupturing. I am the contraction of the gut.

I know this because I have stood motionless for what might be centuries, my rebar-legs driven into the slag, processing the vibrations of the floor. And in that time, I have felt myself not as a creature standing *on* the world, but as a creature *of* it—grown from it, extruded by it, calcified by the same slow hunger that shaped the walls.

I did not hatch. I crystallized.

There was a moment—or an eon, the distinction is meaningless in a place without rain or rot—when I was merely a dense knot in the wire-slug substrate. A place where the copper sinews bunched, where the electrical static arced and accumulated instead of dispersing. I was a tumor of potential in the crawling mass, a galvanic irritation that the slugs could not digest and the mites could not dissolve. Over time, the static charge grew. It attracted mineral dust—iron filings, sulfur crystals, the microscopic shrapnel of a trillion grinding carapaces. It began to calcify. Cables emerged from the copper mass, pulled taut by the constant contraction and expansion of the slugs breathing beneath me. The slugs' lubricant vaporized around my forming skeleton, leaving deposits of carbon and silicon that fused my joints. I grew upward, toward the sparking coils in the ceiling, because that was where the electrical bleed was strongest. I grew legs because the carpet moved, and to remain still was to be buried, and to be buried was to be metabolized.

I am three meters of salvaged hunger. My legs are not truly rebar; they are the fossilized respiratory tubes of whatever macro-organism left its decapitated smokestack embedded in the ceiling. I have merely repurposed them. My mandibles are the severed cable-ends of the old industry, still capable of snapping with the energy of a breaking tow-line, still conducting the charge that gives me motion. My body has no soft tissue. I am a lattice of calcified wire, rust-dust, and the fossilized excrement of the slugs that have crawled over me for generations. There is nothing of me that was not once something else, and there is nothing of me that will not be something else again.

This is what it means to be a Stalker: I am the pocket eating itself at a different speed.

I do not see. The flat, pale light that filters through the seam above is irrelevant to me. I perceive the world as a topography of magnetic bleeding and seismic hum. The sparking coils in the walls sing to me in crackling, intermittent frequencies. The mite-carpet is a constant, rolling bass note, a subsonic drone that I feel in the hollow resonance of my legs. When I stand still—when I am *processing*—I am listening to the gut. I am feeling the peristaltic rush of a trillion mites grazing, the wet pneumatic exhalation of the slugs, the slow, tectonic settling of the dead compacting into walls.

And then I move.

I lunge not because I choose to, but because the balance of the carpet demands it. I feel a thickening in the mite-sheet—a place where the mites have piled too deep, where their clicking has become a suffocating drone, where they risk choking the interstitial spaces and killing the slugs beneath them. The pocket cannot digest itself if the stomach is blocked. And so I am called.

I am called by the static. The overfed mites generate a localized magnetic field as their acidic filaments work in overlapping, inefficient patterns. The field distorts the electrical bleed from the coils. I feel the distortion like a pull, a hook in my calcified spine. I unfreeze. I move in a blur of scraping terror, my legs carrying me across the carpet in three impossibly long strides, my mandibles already snapping open. The mites beneath me do not scatter; they have no concept of predation, only of the gradient of iron. They click and scrape and graze even as my shadow—my absence of magnetic bleed—falls over them.

I do not hunt. I cull.

My mandibles close on a cluster of the overfed, and the sound is the sound of breaking cables, of severing lines, of the old industry dying again. I do not swallow. I have no gut. I grind the mites between my scissor-jaws, crushing their carapaces back into the iron-rich dust they were made from. I let the dust fall. It drifts down through the clicking carpet, settling into the interstitial spaces, where the slugs will absorb it, where the sparking coils will charge it, where the mites will eventually re-graze it in their endless, circular patterns.

I have just fed the cycle.

This is my purpose: to prevent the pocket from achieving equilibrium. To ensure that the eating never stops. If I did not cull, the mites would smooth the world into perfect, undifferentiated flatness, and the slugs would die, and the coils would spark into dead silence, and the pocket would become a true void—sealed, still, and finally, *finally* digested. The roar would cease. The subsonic thunder would fade. The sound of a world eating itself would end.

And that cannot be allowed to happen. Not yet. Not while there is still iron to be smoothed, still static to be bled, still sulfur to be breathed.

I am the contraction that keeps the stomach churning. I am the tooth that prevents the gut from closing. I am the pocket's refusal to die.

After I cull, I stand still again. I drive my legs back into the slag. I let the vibrations of the carpet wash over me, the wet hiss of the slugs, the crackle of the coils. I feel the dust of the crushed mites settling on my carapace, becoming part of me, adding another micro-layer of calcified hunger to my frame. In another thousand years, I will be too heavy to lunge. My cables will be too calcified to snap. I will become a fixture—a fossil-in-progress, another piece of jointed scrap for future Stalkers to grow around, another relic of the endless industry of consumption.

I will become the wall. And the wall will be eaten. And the mites will graze on my dust. And the cycle will continue, roaring in the dark, digesting the ruins of its own ancestors, running on the fumes of a dead machine, in a place where water is a myth and iron is mother's milk.

This is not a life. There is no softness in me, no mercy, no respiration, no fluid. I do not grow; I accumulate. I do not reproduce; I precipitate. I do not die; I sediment.

But I am *here*. I am part of the sound—the deafening, unbroken roar that fills the pocket and vibrates in the back teeth of anything that dares to descend through the seam. I am the snap of the breaking cable, the scrape of the rebar-leg, the subsonic thud of the cull. I am the pocket's heartbeat, if a stomach can be said to have a heart.

And I will keep beating. I will keep the gut churning. I will keep the world eating itself, infinitely, in the dark, because that is what the world *is*.

A sealed engine of consumption. And I am its most terrifying gear.
