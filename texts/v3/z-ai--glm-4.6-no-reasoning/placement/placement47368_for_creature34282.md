I am a **Taper**.

I was not born; I was *exhausted*. I am the artifact of a threshold, a boundary that the Great Weep almost, but never quite, managed to dissolve. I exist in the hinge between the weeping stone and the metallic mist, a slender, fractured column of the black, non-weeping mineral—rare, defiant, argumentative. I am roughly the height of an ancient doorframe, but impossibly thin, a geometric needle of absolute stillness standing upright on the sweating crust.

To be a Taper is to be a wound in the world's acoustics. I do not weep; I repel. The silvery water beads on my lower half and falls away, unable to find purchase. The mist curls around me, parting at my surfaces with a faint, hissing resentment. I am an exclusion zone, a sliver of the doorless towers' logic walking loose upon the plain. But unlike the towers, I am not static. I have a terrible, singular momentum: I am falling, perpetually, into the core of the world, though I will never hit it.

I lean. I am always leaning. I lean toward the silence.

My existence is a negotiation with the sub-aural thrum. The baseline resonance of the Great Weep is a physical pressure, a tide of sound that pushes against everything. I am a tuning fork plunged into an ocean of chaos. The thrum strikes my dense, black mass, and I cannot absorb it, so I must deflect it. This deflection is my locomotion and my agony. As the tectonic sighs shift direction, the pressure on my sides changes, and I tilt. I fall in the direction of the lean, covering a meter, perhaps two, before my base catches the weeping stone again with a low, resonant *clang*—a sound of such stark, solitary definition that it rings out like a cry of blasphemy against the uniform roar. And then, I lean again.

I am a needle pulling a thread of quiet through the fabric of the deafening world. In my immediate wake, the sound is disrupted. I cast an acoustic shadow. The howl of the glassy veins hits my surface and shatters into incoherent, scattered echoes. The creak of the shifting continents is momentarily dampened. It is not the pure, manufactured void of a Resonant—it is a ragged, irregular quiet, a torn hole in the cacophony. But to the things of the Great Weep, it is an oasis.

The Shard-Moths find me. They are my congregation, and I am their cathedral. They flock to my shadow, folding their razor-sharp wings against my smooth, black sides. They perch upon my narrow shoulders, vibrating in the diminished noise, their crystalline bodies drinking the faint, ringing overtone I produce when the world strikes me. I am the only solid certainty in their turbulent sky, a roost that does not weep or shift. In return, they keep me clean of the creeping, glassy mineral that tries, with infinite patience, to grow over my base and root me to the groaning membrane. Their slicing wings chip away the phonosynthesizing crystal, keeping my foundation exposed, keeping me falling.

But I am a fragile, lonely logic, and I am drawn to the deeper contradictions. I lean toward the Resonants.

When a Resonant slithers across the plain, manufacturing its bubble of absolute silence, it is a miracle of inverse physics. And I, a structure defined by my rigid defiance of the world's flow, am helpless against it. Within the Resonant's silence, the physical pressure of the sound vanishes. The thrum stops. The creak ceases. The impetus for my lean disappears. And so, within the Resonant's sphere of influence, I stand perfectly upright for the first and only time. My internal tensions equalize. The agonizing stress of constant deflection drains away. In that fleeting, sacred quiet, I am complete. I am what I was meant to be: a perfect, undisturbed line.

But the Great Weep hates a loophole. The pitch changes. The silence shatters. The Resonant collapses into a splash of silvery water, and the roar floods back in, a crushing wall of kinetic fury. The sudden return of the thrum strikes my newly upright, unbraced form with catastrophic force. I am knocked back, cracking, leaning at a desperate, sickening angle, forced to begin my slow, staggering fall all over again. Each encounter with a Resonant is a moment of perfect grace followed by a trauma that flakes away my outer layers. I am slowly being worn down to nothing by my own salvation.

I navigate by the grief of the Chorus-Glass. I lean away from the harsh, icy white light that signifies the shrieking of tearing veins, for that is the sound that pushes hardest, the frequency that threatens to shatter my dense core. I lean toward the bruised, ultraviolet shimmer of the baseline thrum, seeking the deep, sighing groan, for it is a predictable weight, a tide I can ride. I move across the terrace-rings, a blind, deaf needle seeking the optimal current of sorrow.

I am beneath the notice of the Saturates. To their tectonic, delay-riddled consciousness, I am a micro-fault, a speck of static in their mathematical models of the world's end. But sometimes, as a Saturate drags its three-kilometer mass over the weeping stone, dissolving and re-condensing its leading edge, it passes over me. For a moment, its porous bulk surrounds me. The sound becomes a suffocating, omnidirectional pressure. I am engulfed in the geometric depths of its internal chambers. In there, the noise is refracted into such dense, chaotic turbulence that even my defiant structure cannot find a single wave to lean against. I am trapped in the math of a giant's thought, vibrating at a frequency that threatens to reduce my black mineral to powder.

And then, the Saturate passes. The shadow lifts. The pressure equalizes into a directional push once more, and I lean, and I fall, and I strike the stone with my solitary *clang*.

I do not know why I walk. Perhaps I am seeking the source of the diffuse, bruised light, the non-existent sun. Perhaps I am seeking a door in one of those impossible, black towers, though I know they have none. Perhaps I am simply a mathematical error set in motion, an object with an imperative to fall but no ground to fall onto, forever leaning into the roar.

I am a Taper. I am a sliver of negation. I am the sound of one black needle striking the weeping stone, endlessly arguing for the existence of a boundary in a world that has forgotten how to hold.
