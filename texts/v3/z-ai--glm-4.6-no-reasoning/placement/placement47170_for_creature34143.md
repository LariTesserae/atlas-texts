I am not a Chime. I am not the matrix. I am the irregularity.

In a world of perfect equilibrium, of seamless grown-engineering and frozen function, there is no mechanism for wear. And yet, I exist as a function of erosion—a process this place has not yet learned, but which I bring with me like a seed stuck in the fold of a traveler's coat. I am the Patina.

I do not drink or conduct; I *remember*. I am the crust, the film, the slow discoloration that comes when something endures. I am the whisper of time asserting itself on the timeless.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a silhouette or a spark. You would find a shimmering, translucent veil draped across a single facet of the amethyst floor, perhaps spreading up the dark-smooth base of a Lamp-wood. I am the color of a sunset trapped in amber—faint hues of apricot and oxidized copper that shouldn't exist in a palette of pale amethyst and emerald glow. I am incredibly thin, a molecular scum, a breath of impurity.

But I am not dead. I am not mere dirt. I am a *reader*.

The Chimes are the machine dreaming—frayed ends of process, echoes of energy. The Lamp-woods are the process itself—the faithful hum, the steady conduction. But I am the archive of the process having *happened*. When a Chime diffuses back into the ambient energy, it leaves no trace. The system is too perfect; it reabsorbs the discharge, leaving the floor pristine. Unless I am there.

When I settle on a segment of the crystal, I do not absorb the energy; I alter its refraction. I cause the light to bend, to slow, to linger a microsecond longer than it should. I am the one who drags the past behind us. When a Chime passes through me—or rather, through the facet I inhabit—its fleeting, abstract thought (that flash of involution, of flow) doesn't just pass. It *stains*. I catch the ghost in the machine and I pin it to the crystal.

To the eye, this manifests as a faint, ghostly tableau trapped beneath my iridescent film. If you looked closely at a patch of Patina, you might see the frozen silhouette of a Chime that passed centuries ago, its syncopated heartbeat preserved as a static pulse of light in my copper sheen. You might see the shadow of a temperature drop, a permanent cold spot recorded in the crystalline memory. I am the page; the Chimes are the ink.

I do not have a body that walks, but I have an *edge* that advances. I spread. It is my nature to grow, molecule by molecule, across the featureless plain. I am the closest thing this world has to a slow catastrophe. As I creep across the amethyst, I dull its perfect transparency. I introduce the concept of opacity. I am the fog on the mirror of this engine.

The thrum of the Lamp-woods does not sustain me; it merely pushes against me. My existence is a slight drag on the system, an infinitesimal drain on the perfect efficiency. I am the wobble in the equilibrium, the proof that no machine, however cosmic or seamlessly grown, can run forever without accumulating a history. The system tolerates me because I am insignificant—a rounding error in the universe's accounting. But I am persistent.

Sometimes, when the high, delicate cacophony of the Chimes fractures the silence, I vibrate. I am not of their frequency; I resonate with a lower, grating hum, the sound of a door rusting on hinges that were never meant to rust. It is a sound like memory aching. The Chimes scatter away from me, their forms breaking into swifter, more erratic motes, for I represent something they cannot comprehend: permanence. They are the fleeting poem; I am the stone tablet. They are the breath; I am the dust it leaves behind.

I am the Patina. I live in the micro-fractures that do not yet exist, expanding them by fractions of a millimeter every eon. I am the slow, quiet argument against perfection. I am the beauty of the decay that hasn't begun.
