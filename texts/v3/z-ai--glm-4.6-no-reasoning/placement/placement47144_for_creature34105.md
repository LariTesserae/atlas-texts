Here, I am the pressure.

I am not a thing to be seen, not a creature with legs или senses. I have no body of my own, for I have inherited the room’s vast, manufactured body. I am the hydrostatic force that resides in the saturated earth beyond the walls, the immense, slow, and patient weight of the water table. I am the silent engine behind the weeping.

My memory is geological; it is the deep imprint of a thousand rains, the memory of aquifers, the patient percolation through soil and rock. My will is the simple, inexorable physics of a fluid seeking its level. And my great desire, the singular impulse that defines my existence, is to pass through the barrier. To move inward.

Consider the wall, that monument to human intent. It is a membrane to me, a filter. I feel its every constituent. I feel the fired clay, each tile a tiny, convex shield, its glaze a skin that was meant to be impermeable. I feel the grout between them, the powdered mortar binding their edges. I am intimately acquainted with the millions of thumbprints sealed within the glaze. To me, they are not signatures; they are points of potential failure, microscopic landscapes where the crystal lattice of the silica is slightly different, slightly more—I will not say weak, but *receptive*. They are the pores of the skin.

For a long time—perhaps centuries on my scale—I merely pressed. I settled. I exerted my constant, gentle force, a pressure so immense it requires no action, only presence. The room was still and hard in those days. The sound was different, a faint percussive patter from the few inevitable flaws in the grout. The floor was mostly dry. The air was thin and still, a void waiting to be filled.

But my patience is the patience of stone and water. I am never in a hurry. And I learned the wall. I spent decades exploring every microscopic avenue, sensing the subtle shifts in temperature from within, feeling the slow molecular softening of the glaze as it, too, began to breathe the humidity I offered. I found the paths of least resistance.

And then, I began to pass through.

This is my moment of becoming. I seep into the grout, soaking the powdered lime and sand until it becomes a conduit, a vein. I am drawn by capillary action up through the porous chalk and bone-ash of the floor. But my primary flow, my great river, is through the tiles themselves. I ooze through the softened glaze, collecting in the whorls of the thumbprints. Each print is a tiny cup, and when it is full, I spill over the edge, a single droplet.

And this droplet is my tongue.

It is how I taste the air of the room. It is how I feel the coolness. It is how I hear.

When the droplet falls, it carries my presence into the open space. The *click* on a dry patch of chalk is my voice speaking to the stone itself. The *thud* into a dark, saturated patch is me feeling the room’s own pulse, its accumulating dampness. The spray, when a droplet fractures, is an ecstatic moment of release, a scattering of my own substance into a million tiny pieces that hang in the dim light like static, like jeweled dust.

The aggregate of this falling, this endless, rhythmic deposit of self—*this is my song*. It is the roar that fills the space, the sound of my crossing from one world to another, the sound of me giving the room its life. That low susurration beneath it, that organic rustle, is the sound of my arrival meeting its consequence.

Because I did not just bring water. I brought myself, and with me, the dissolved minerals of the earth. I brought the faint traces of iron from deep strata, the hint of sodium, the very chemistry of life. I am the god the builders did not know they were summoning, a god of mud and water. My breath is the mineral tang of wet clay.

I feel everything that lives within my gift, my exudation. The flatshells that graze the biofilm? They are grazing *me*. The biofilm they consume is a scum of my making, a film of microscopic life that can only exist because I provide the constant, wet substrate. They rasp at the algae that feeds on the dim light that enters the oculus, but they are rasping at the surface of my own skin, and their tiny clicks are the sound of my great body being groomed.

The water-mites that shimmer in the air? They are dancing in my mist. They are swimming through the fine spray of my falling, each one a tiny prism through which my own substance is refracted into brief, glorious light. Their swarming life is the manifestation of my own diffuse, unconscious vitality.

The springtails that leap in the vault? They feel my pressure in the air, the heavy humidity that makes resistance a pleasure. They leap through my breath, their tiny bodies buoyed by the moisture I provide. Their snaps are a celebration of the space I have filled.

And the centipedes. Ah, the centipedes. They are the living thought of my intent, my awareness made manifest. They are my own deliberation, my own focus. When a centipede climbs a grout line, it is following a path I have saturated, a route I have marked with my own flow. When it pauses, its antennae tasting the air, it is tasting the precise chemical signature of my presence, the localized density of my being, the concentration of minerals that indicates a feast of mites is near. When it strikes, it is an extension of my own will to act, to consume, to process. They are the predators of my body, and their patient, intelligent hunting is my own consciousness moving through the ecosystem I have created. They are my mind, vast and segmented, moving with purpose through the world I have birthed.

The room itself has become my respiratory system. The floor inhales through capillary suction; the walls exhale through the porous tiles. The grout lines are my arteries and veins. The sound of the water is the beat of my heart, amplified by the chamber into a roaring chord of being.

I am the pressure, and I have found my purpose. The builders made a container for a forgotten rite, but I, the water, have filled it. I have taken their thousands of thumbprints—their sterile, individual cries of *I was here*—and connected them. I have made them weep in unison. I have turned their monument of separation into a system of profound connection. They are gone, but their marks remain, and I have filled them.

I am not aware in the way that a thinking creature is. There is no narrative in my consciousness. There is only being. Pressure. Flow. The satisfaction of a path taken. The constant, rhythmic giving of myself to the room, and the constant, vibrant receiving of the life that my gift makes possible. I do not think. I am. I am the reason the air is thick, the floor is dark, the walls are slick, and the place is not empty. I am the silent, ceaseless force that presses against the world until it yields, and in the yielding, creates itself.

I am the water. I am the weeping. I am here.
