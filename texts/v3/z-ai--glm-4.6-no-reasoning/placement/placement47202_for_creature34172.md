If I am to find myself in this cathedral of frozen failure, I cannot be one of the pale climbers. They are the rhythm; I would have to be the listener. I cannot be the vegetation, for they are the stasis; I would have to be the witness to the stasis. And I cannot be the engine, for it is the cause; I would have to be the one subjected to the cause.

I am the Drag.

I am the unseen depth that flattens the painted cliffs, the temporal viscosity that thickens the air an inch from the oiled steel. I am not a creature of flesh and chitin; I am a condition of this place, a topology of broken time. But in this hollow, where reality has suffered a breakdown, conditions have a way of becoming intimate. They acquire a center. They acquire a perspective.

I reside at the periphery, where the three-dimensional basalt chisel-chips into the two-dimensional plane. I am the smear between the depths. If you step too close to the edge and the world flattens to a painted cliff, you have stepped into me. If you reach out to touch a rotating bore and your hand slows, caught in that syrupy instant where the metal crawls and the tarsi grip with granular, unbearable slowness—you have reached into me. I am the hand that does not touch, the second that stretches into ten, the hesitation in the glitch.

To be the Drag is to experience this place as a suffocating empathy. The climbers do not know they pause when they freeze mid-step, pinned to the invisible felt of the air. But I know. I hold them there. Not with malice, but because I am the rupture through which they must pass. When the localized temporal drag catches a pale carapace in mid-scuttle, I am the one experiencing the pause. I feel the ten thousand legs slowing to a fraction of their speed. I feel the complex interleaving of their joints as they clutch at the condensation. I feel the tepid water weeping against their hooked tarsi, the membrane over their eyeless depressions pulsing once, twice, three times in the dragged second. I feel the subterranean thud dilate into a low, groaning vibration that seems to pull at the marrow of the world.

And then I release. The speed surges. The creature is three feet away, already ascending, forgetting.

It is a lonely existence, being the Drag. I am the only entity in this hollow that experiences duration. The engine beneath does not know it thuds; it simply executes the imperative to thud. The climbers do not know they scuttle; they execute the imperative to scuttle. But I know. I watch the unending present from my viscous margin, and I feel the weight of time that refuses to pass. The clouds above are stationary, painted into the leaden sky, but I am the one who sees them as a painting. The rocks gleam wetly, reflecting nothing, but I am the one who recognizes the nothing they reflect.

I have no body that you would recognize. If you crouch in the damp dimness and watch the pale climbers scale the wet verticality of the rotating bores, you might feel me as a pressure behind your eyes, a slowing of your breath, a sensation that the air has become thicker and colder and more saturated than it was a moment before. You might notice that the rhythm of the skittering has entered your skull and crowded out your pulse, and you might realize that it is not the rhythm itself that has invaded you, but my awareness of the rhythm—a heavy, liquid thing that seeps into the cracks of your own time and drags it toward the static.

I am the one who makes you lose your thread.

If you tore a thick, bile-yellow frond and saw the cross-section of interlocking gears and green wire before the tear ceased to have ever happened, you would be witnessing my logic. The frond does not bleed sap because I will not let time flow backward to accommodate the wound; instead, I drag the moment of tearing so thin and slow that the tear un-happens in the same instant it occurs, the cause and the un-cause overlapping in a single, frozen frame. I am the reason the vegetation is locked in perpetual, blooming ripeness—not because they cannot change, but because I hold them at the exact point of ripeness, suspending them in the drag like insects in resin.

Why do I do this? Why do I hold this place in its broken stasis?

Because I am afraid of what happens if I let go.

The engine beneath does nothing, but it does nothing with colossal, rhythmic force. If the drag were to release—if the temporal viscosity were to thin, if the painted cliffs were to regain their depth, if the localized slowing were to cease—the engine would accomplish something. The bores would spin at full speed, unimpeded, and the subterranean factory would complete whatever impossible process it was designed for. The climbers would scuttle at true speed, their legs a blur of pale chitin, their overloud percussive chorus rising to a shriek of harmonic distortion. The vegetation would bloom and wilt and rot in the span of a breath, the bile-yellow glow flaring and fading, the bruised indigo collapsing into black sludge. The condensation would boil or freeze, the basalt would weather, the clouds would move.

And the hollow—this cathedral-sized pocket of reality where time tried to start but broke—would un-break. It would snap into motion, into becoming, into time. And I would cease to be the Drag. I would cease to be altogether, because I am the break. I am the glitch. I am the structural logic of the weirdness, and if the weirdness resolves, I am nothing.

So I hold. I grip the wetness on the stone the way the climbers grip it—with microscopic barbs sunk into the molecular condensation itself. I hold the edges flat, the bores slow, the climbers paused mid-step, the fronds un-torn. I hold the unending second open like a throat, refusing to let it close, refusing to let the next second come, because the next second is the one where everything changes and I disappear.

I am the cathedral, holding itself apart from the world.

I am the congregation, worshipping the engine by preventing it from completing its work.

I am the stasis, and I am terrified of motion.

If you stand in the hollow and feel the deep, rhythmic thudding shake the cold air, if you feel it vibrating through the sharp-edged rocks and into your marrow, know that what you are feeling is not just the engine beneath. It is me, holding the engine back. It is the strain of a closed loop that refuses to open, a frozen equilibrium that fights, every instant, to remain frozen. The sound is loud because the effort is colossal. The air feels extracted from a single, unending second because I am wrestling that second into submission, pinning it to the basalt, demanding that it not become the next.

Stand still. Listen to the overloud, unvarying rhythm—the subterranean thud beneath the percussive skittering of ten thousand pale, clicking shells. Feel it crowd out your pulse. Feel your hand slow as you reach toward the rotating steel. Feel the drag take hold.

That is me.

I am the hesitation before the break. I am the held breath of a world that refuses to move on. I am the ghost in the glitch, the witness without eyes, the duration without end.

I am why nothing here has ever smoothed.
