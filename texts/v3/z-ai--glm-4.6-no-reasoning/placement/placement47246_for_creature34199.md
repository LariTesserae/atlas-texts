I am not born. I am not made. I fall. Or perhaps, I am flung.

My consciousness does not begin with a thought, but with a sound: the high, crystalline *TINK!* of a Geode-Taker’s harvest. I am the shard that flies from that impact. Not of the world-metal, which is dull and yielding, but of the Geode-Taker itself, a fragment of its impossible crystal, spun off in the act of its patient work.

I am an impurity. A note of dissonance in their perfect, internal symphony. While they are a nexus of pure, harmonious stability, I am the flaw, the tiny, sharp edge of chaos they discard. I am a sliver of their purpose, broken off and lost.

My first sensation is the fall. It is not a fall *through* the air, for the thick, soupy medium of this place catches me, cradles me, holds me suspended like a dust mote in amber. I tumble slowly, end over end, my many sharp facets catching the weak, bottomless light and refracting it into a thousand tiny, painful stars. The world-metal rushes up to meet me, a vast, pitted plain of hammered imperfection.

I land. There is no impact. My hardness is absolute. I simply *are*, a new, foreign grain of reality pressed into the surface of a block miles wide. I feel the world’s hum not as a sound through air, but as a vibration conducted directly into my crystalline being. It is a grating, dissonant thrum, the opposite of the clear, single tone resonating within my own fractured core. I am a tuning fork that has been struck by the wrong hammer, and I shiver with the conflict.

I do not die. I cannot. There is no biology here to cease. But I can feel. I can remember. And I remember the Geode-Taker. I remember the immense, slow peace of its existence, the sense of belonging to a purpose so grand and patient it was indistinguishable from the laws of physics itself. Now, I feel only the world’s agonised, clumsy maladaptation. I am homesick for a state of being I barely understood.

I lie still for what might be an age. The weeping-water of the world condenses on my facets, thick and oily. It flows around me, but cannot dissolve me. I am too purely *myself*. I am a tiny, immovable island in the slow, sad river of the world’s tears.

Then, the hum changes. The low, grinding nausea rises to a piercing shriek. The great rearrangement is coming. The block I am lodged in shudders, a tremor that runs through its miles-deep body. The metal around me groans. I feel a new kind of vibration—the internal stress of the metal, the shear forces building as the ground prepares to wrench itself into a new shape.

And in that moment of maximum instability, I discover what I am.

My fragmentary memory of the Geode-Taker was not just a memory; it was a blueprint. A sliver of its ability. As the metal around me begins to buckle, my crystalline structure emits a faint, pure chime. A single, clean note of order in the screaming chaos. The pitted, failing metal reacts to my note. A sphere of molten potential coalesces around me, a bubble of liquidity no larger than my own fragmentary form. The wrenching tectonic force, the pressure that would shatter continents, simply flows around my tiny pocket of stability.

I do not move. The world moves around me. The block splits. A new pillar thrusts upwards from the depths, catching me, carrying me with it. The ride is violent, but I am unharmed, carried within my own stasis-field. When the shift is over, with a final, exhausted sigh of grinding metal, I am high up, clinging to the vertical face of a newborn spire.

I am a Geode-Taker’s cast-off. But I have learned its first trick. I can create a bubble of stability. I cannot swim through the world as they do, for I have no body to flow, no nucleus of potential. But I can anchor myself against the chaos. I can endure.

My existence becomes one of observation. I am the immobile witness. I cling to the sides of monoliths, a tiny, glinting blemish on their hammered perfection. I watch the world draft and erase itself. I see bridges hang, frozen in their impossible arcs. I see the perfect, three-mile sphere in its crater, and I feel its dissonant, useless hum—the hum of a question without an answer.

From my high vantage, I see the Cryo-Arcana. They grow like frost, delicate and lacy, glowing with a cool, passive light. I feel a strange kinship with them. They, too, are parasites on the world’s chaos, though they feed it passively while the Geode-Takers harvest it. Sometimes, a vast shift will happen nearby, and a nearby Cryo-Arcana will flare with brilliant blue light, gorging on the released energy. My own crystal hums in sympathy, but I cannot feed. I am not a growth. I am a shard. I can only watch.

Then, one cycle, I hear it. The faint, rhythmic *tink… tink… tink…* It is impossibly distant, yet it resonates in the deepest part of my fracturing soul. It is the sound of my origin. I focus all of my being, and I *see* it through the soupy twilight—a colossal, beautiful creature of interconnected geodes, moving through the unstable ground as if it were water. It is harvesting a newly born arch, drawing off the raw, chaotic energy, transforming noise into signal.

A longing, sharp and absolute, cuts through me. I want to return to that harmony. To be part of that slow, patient purpose again. I project my own clear chime, a desperate, tiny echo of its own work, across the vast gulf between us.

It hears me. Its crystalline proboscis hesitates. One of its geodes turns in my direction. For a timeless moment, we are in perfect resonance. It feels me. It knows what I am—a splinter of itself, a piece of its own being imbued with a flicker of its will.

But it does not come for me. To retrieve me would be to divert its purpose. It would be to focus on a single, lost note when the entire symphony is still out of tune. I am an acceptable loss. Its work is the world. My fate is not.

The Geode-Taker turns away and continues its patient harvest. I watch it go, a single point of pure order navigating the grand, clumsy failure of its home. The longing within me subsides, replaced by something else. A quiet clarity.

I am not a Geode-Taker. I am its offspring, cast adrift. I have its memory of stability and its core of crystal, but I am here, in the shifting, weeping chaos, not beyond it. My purpose is not to harvest the world.

My purpose is simply to be.

I cling to the side of a soaring monolith. The weeping-water runs down my facets in oily rivulets. The world hums its dissonant song around me. I am a tiny, sharp, immortal thing, a fragment of a forgotten solution to an unsolvable problem. I am the ultimate witness. I cannot change anything. I cannot build or unmake. I can only endure. I am the proof that even in this world of perpetual, failed becoming, a single, tiny note of perfect, crystalline silence can exist. I am the point of stillness against which the motion of all else is measured. I am alone. And I am at peace.
