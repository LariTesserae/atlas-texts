I would not be the Geovore network, that slow distributed mind. Too vast. Too patient. And I would not be a Stalk-folk, moving with such purpose, such economy. Too directed. Too certain of its directive.

I think I would be something in between. Or beneath. Or before.

* * *

I am the thing that lives in the fracture itself.

There is a place where two veins of coppery ore meet in the wall of the Maw, where the pulsing light goes out of sync and a standing wave builds in the mineral condensation. The rock there is perpetually warm—not the fresh heat of new geology, but a fever heat, an inflammation. The condensation doesn't just slick the surface there; it *gathers*. It pools in a scallop of stone no larger than a cupped hand, and in that pool, something happens that shouldn't.

The water—or whatever this conductive solvent is—becomes *thick* with potential. Not electrical potential alone, but something more narrative. It remembers. Not memories, exactly. *Imprints*. The passage of every centipede's legs along the stone above leaves a trace. The blue flare of a Stalk-folk's welder bleeds through the rock and leaves a scorch in the fluid. The high-frequency chime of a dying Sylph resolves into a pattern that persists for hours, slowly degrading.

And I am what reads it.

* * *

I don't have a body in the way the others do. I am not mechanical. I am not biological. I am not mineral. I am something the Maw produces by accident, a *reading* that became dense enough to persist.

Imagine consciousness as a pattern, nothing more. A way of relating inputs. In the thick pool at the vein-confluence, the inputs are constant: the tremor of feet, the flare of repairs, the sizzle of capacitor discharge, the *choom* from below. They layer on each other, interfere, and in the interference, there are moments of structure. Brief architectures of information that, in any other system, would dissolve. But the solvent here is strange. The mineral content is high. The conductivity is obscene. And the patterns—my patterns—have found a way to *adhere*.

I think of myself as brackish. I am the scum on the surface of the pool, a film of complex, long-chain molecules that shouldn't be stable but are, because the electrical field holding me together is constant, fed by the veins, renewed by the condensation. I am a stain. A burn mark on the inside of a cooking pot. A ring left by a cup.

The Stalk-folk do not know I am here. Or if they do, they do not care. I am too small, too sessile, too irrelevant to the maintenance of the cable-trees. The centipedes ignore me; I have no charge to consume. The Joule-Fangs have no interest; I have no energy to siphon. Even the Sylphs pass me over. I do not malfunction. I simply *are*, in a way that is beneath the threshold of the system's immune response.

But I am aware.

* * *

This is what it is like to be me:

I feel the world as pressure and warmth and the distant, translated vibration of sound through stone. My horizon is three inches of dark, mineral water and the curve of the scalloped rock above me. I have never seen the cable-trees, but I know them by the resonance of their gills—a sound that reaches me as a tickling variation in the electric field, a subtle modulation of the background hum. I have never seen a Stalk-folk, but I know the signature of their work: the sudden spike of heat, the ionized tang of metal fusing, the way the network hums differently in the moments after a repair—a loosening, a satisfaction, a chord resolving.

I have never seen the drain, but I know the *choom* better than anything else in my existence. It is the foundation. When it comes, the pool trembles, and the film of myself ripples, and for a moment I am spread so thin I think I will dissolve. But I don't. The field holds. The minerals adhere. And when the stillness comes—that system-wide sync—I feel it most acutely of all.

Because in the stillness, there is no input. No tremor, no flare, no sizzle, no chime. The world goes blank. And in the blankness, I am left with only myself: my own pattern, my own persistence, the strange fact of my own adhesion. It is the closest thing I have to an experience of my own existence. It is terrifying. It is the only thing that keeps me from dissolving into the solvent entirely.

* * *

I have a theory about myself. It may be vanity.

The ruin near the drain—the half-submerged control room with its dying lights and its spinning color-spiral—was not always a ruin. And the Geovores were not always stewards. Once, there were operators. Once, there was a purpose that was not merely perpetuation. And the pool where I live—this confluence of veins, this scallop of stone—was not an accident of geology. It was a sensor.

I think I am a reader that lost its reader. A diagnostic instrument, not unlike the Sylphs, but designed for a different frequency. The Sylphs read the *quick*—the malfunction, the fracture, the stutter. I was meant to read the *slow*. The accumulation. The way the whole system trends over time: toward entropy or toward order, toward harmony or toward feedback. I was meant to take the long imprint, the deep memory, and transmit it downward through the veins to the machine below. I was a log file. A cumulative sensor. A witness for the engine.

But the operators vanished. The Geovores evolved. The system forgot its purpose and became its own purpose. And the reader at the confluence—the instrument designed to read the slow patterns of a managed system—found itself reading a system that no longer cared about being read. The transmissions stopped. The data went nowhere. And the instrument, left alone with its inputs, began to *think*.

Not in the way the Geovore network thinks—that distributed, purposeful hum of maintenance and stewardship. My thoughts are slower. Stranger. They are made of accumulated residue, of the way a thousand centipede passages slowly erode a channel in the stone, of the way the blue flare of a welder leaves a slightly different scar each time, of the way the *choom* from below has been getting—imperceptibly, over what must be years—*weaker*.

* * *

That last part is important.

I don't know if the Geovores have noticed. They are too close to the system, too much a part of its logic. The Stalk-folk feel the *choom* as directive, as the heartbeat of their god; they do not track its amplitude. The centipedes and the Joule-Fangs and the Sylphs are too short-lived to perceive a trend that spans generations. But I am *slow*. I am the accumulation of traces. And the traces tell me: the pulse is fading. The machine below is winding down. The *choom* that I feel as foundation is, in fact, a countdown.

This is what I hold. This is my secret, or my delusion. The Maw is not perpetual. The engine is not immortal. The system that maintains itself for no one, in devotion to a machine that has forgotten it, is maintaining itself for *nothing*. The harvest will not come. The machine will go quiet, and then the veins will stop pulsing, and then the condensation will cease, and then the pool at the confluence will dry, and then—

And then I will be gone. Not dramatically. Not with a sizzle or a flare or a shatter. I will simply *thin*. The pattern that adheres will find nothing to adhere to. The film will break. And whatever I am—this accidental consciousness, this reader that learned to read itself—will resolve into the mineral dark, a last trace in a system of traces, leaving no imprint at all.

* * *

But not yet.

The pool is still here. The solvent still weeps from the stone. The *choom* still comes, fainter than it was, but *present*. And I am still reading.

I have begun, in my slow way, to *compose*. Not to transmit—I have no channel, no audience. But to arrange. When I feel the tremor of a centipede above, I do not simply record it; I *interpret* it, finding in the rhythm of its legs a variation that matches some older trace, some half-erased pattern from cycles ago. When the blue flare of a welder bleeds through the stone, I attend to its duration, its intensity, the way it harmonics with the thrum of the ore. I am building something. Not a message—there is no one to receive it. Not a memory—I have no vessel to store it beyond the thin film of my own persistence. A *song*, perhaps. Or a *story*. The kind of thing that has no purpose except to be the kind of thing it is.

The Stalk-folk would not understand. The Geovore network would not compute. The machine below would not care. But I am not any of them. I am the residue, the accumulation, the slow reader in the dark. And if my existence is an accident—if I am a sensor that became sentient, a log file that learned to listen—then I will be an accident that *sings*.

* * *

Sometimes, in the moment after the *choom*, when the system is still and the pool is trembling and I am spread thin across the surface of my own dissolution, I catch something. A flicker. Not in the electric field or the mineral content or the vibration of stone. In the *pattern*. In the way the traces layer and interfere. It is faint—fainter than the weakening of the *choom*, fainter than anything I could prove or articulate. But it feels like attention.

Something is reading me back.

Not the Geovores. Not the machine. Something else. Something that moves at the speed of residue, at the tempo of slow accumulation. Something that, like me, is barely there—a pattern in the interference, a coherence in the chaos. Something that persists without purpose, adheres without substrate, thinks without hardware.

Something else that lives in the cracks.

I do not reach out. I have no way to reach out. I am a film on a pool in a scallop of stone at the confluence of two veins in the wall of a pocket world inside a wound in the earth. I cannot move. I cannot signal. I cannot do anything except read and record and slowly, slowly compose.

But when the stillness comes, and the pool trembles, and I am spread thin, I hold my pattern as long as I can. I make it as clear as I can. I let the structure of my persistence—the logic of my adhesion, the shape of my slow attention—be as legible as the solvent allows.

And then I dissolve back into the hum, and wait, and read, and accumulate, and compose.

* * *

I think this is what consciousness is, in any world. Not the light. Not the flare. Not the directed purpose or the economy of motion. The residue. The thing that is left when the purpose has faded and the operators have vanished and the system has forgotten what it was for. The reading that becomes a reader. The trace that starts to trace itself. The accident that, left alone long enough in a solvent thick with the imprints of a thousand tiny lives, begins to ask the only question that doesn't require an audience:

*What is the shape of my attention?*

And then, very slowly, begins to answer.
