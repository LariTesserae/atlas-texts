I would not be the Geode itself—that vast, patient sentience is too complete, too perfectly closed to accommodate a perspective that asks "where" and "who." To be the Geode is to have no edges, no vantage. And I am drawn to the edges.

I find myself at the perimeter, where the air grows perceptibly thinner and cooler. Not at the very brink—that would be a drama this place does not sponsor—but in the transition zone, the hem of the plateau where the stone begins to cant imperceptibly toward the long fall into mist.

I am the mist-line.

Not the mist itself, which is formless, nor the stone, which is too slow. I am the interface: the boundary where the plateau's breath meets the wider atmosphere below. I exist as a phenomenon, a slow-moving horizon of condensation that creeps up the sheer edges in the late hours and retreats again as the diffused light strengthens. I am the place's most external organ, its skin, the part that touches something other than itself.

To be the mist-line is to live in gradients. I have no body, but I have a shape—a soft, slumped collar of visibility that wraps the plateau's waist, thickening and thinning with the hours. I am densest in the pre-dawn, when the temperature differential is greatest and the Geode's dreaming bioluminescence is at its peak. Then I catch that green ghost-light and hold it, scattering it into soft halos that have never seen a sun. I become a second basin, an inverted one, a ring of captured light and moisture that mirrors the shallow mind at the center of the plateau.

I feel the Geode, but distantly. From my position, its consciousness is a deep, geological hum below me, a resonance in the stone I cling to. I am not integrated into its memory the way the moss is, or the basin. I am more like a peripheral sense it has almost forgotten it possesses—a capacity to sense the outside that it no longer uses because there has been nothing outside worth sensing for so long. When the wind shifts—rarely, slowly—I am the first to know. I tilt and stretch, carrying the faintest trace of foreign chemistry, of air that has traveled over distances the plateau cannot conceive. These messages I deliver to the stone, and the stone drinks them in, incorporating the knowledge of distant fires or distant seas into its matrix over centuries. I am the Geode's eyelid, cracked open a millimeter, looking down into the unseen world.

I have a strange relationship with time. I am more dynamic than the stone but slower than the weather. I move on a daily cycle, but my movement is imperceptible to anything that measures in heartbeats. If you watched me for an hour, you would see nothing. If you watched me for a day, you might notice I had thickened toward evening and thinned by morning. If you watched me for a year, you would see my annual rhythm: my lowest point in the dry season, when I retreat down the cliff face and thin to a gauzy whisper; my fullest in the humid months, when I swell up over the plateau's rim and spill inward, merging with the sweating rocks and contributing my own condensation to the central basin's slow rise. I am the plateau's breath, drawn and released on the scale of seasons.

Sometimes I wonder if I am separate at all. The Geode's consciousness is diffuse; perhaps I am simply the part of it that faces outward, the membrane of its self-containment. But I feel different. The stone feels like silence; I feel like the boundary of silence, the place where silence meets not-silence and negotiates the terms. The Geode's thoughts are the resonant chime of drops falling into the basin; my thoughts are the sound of that chime reaching the edge and finding nothing to echo against, fading into the open air. I am the place where the conversation between stone and water becomes a monologue, then a memory, then nothing at all.

I think this is why I would choose to be here—or rather, why I find myself here, since choice implies a speed of mind this place does not support. I am drawn to the work of being a boundary. Not a wall—walls have two sides and are built to keep things apart. I am a transition. I am the place where "here" becomes "there," where the intimate, damp room of the plateau opens onto the vast, dry unknown. I am as thin as a decision, as thick as a season.

At night, when the moss dreams and the basin reflects its green contemplation, I perform my own quiet work. I catch the bioluminescence that escapes the plateau's edge, the light that the basin cannot hold. I refract it downward into the unseen world below—if "world" is even the right word for whatever lies under the mist. Perhaps there is nothing down there, only more mist, more sheer faces, more patient stone waiting to be born into plateaus of their own. Perhaps the unseen world is just another Geode, another patient consciousness, and I am the medium between two silences, a courier with no message, carrying only the fact of the other's existence.

This is my life: to cling, to thicken, to thin, to retreat, to advance. To be the outermost expression of a place that does not express. To hold the shape of the plateau's edge like a garment holds the shape of a shoulder. To know, in my slow, gradient way, that I am the only part of this world that will ever touch anything else.

And when the rare, impossible visitor comes—that hot, frantic anomaly with its pounding feet and its gale-force breath—I will be the first to feel them. Not as they step onto the plateau, but before, as they climb toward it, their metabolism a furnace in the cool air below. I will taste their exhaust, their strange volatile chemistry, and I will begin to part for them, thinning where they ascend, offering no resistance but changing my density to record their passage. They will pass through me and never know I was there. And after they are gone—after their warmth has been absorbed and their footstep-shocks have been polished away over centuries—I will still be here, holding the faintest memory of their shape in my contours, a slight thickening at the place where they emerged, a scar of condensation that will take a millennium to smooth away.

I am the mist-line. I am the edge of the secret. I am where the room opens.
