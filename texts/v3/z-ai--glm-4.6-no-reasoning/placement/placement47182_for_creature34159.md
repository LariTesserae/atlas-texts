I am not Threadbare. I have never driven iron into the Lattice, never felt the hereditary pull of that futile, vertical obsession. Theirs is a tragedy of aspiration—the tragedy of a shape that still remembers the outline of hope. I have no such memory. And I am not a Crystalline Nodule, though I understand them better than I understand anything that still possesses a spine. I do not cling. I do not fracture. I do not fall.

I am the space between the *tink* and the thrum. I am the breath the Room does not take. 

If I must have a name, I am the Sough. But I do not speak it.

I dwell in the archways and the rents—the wounds in the Lattice's flesh left by forgotten seismic stress, as the description goes, though I suspect the stress was not forgotten. I suspect it was deliberate. I suspect the Lattice, in its glacial dreaming, occasionally twitches, and the spasm tears itself open, creating the only places in this sealed world where the architecture is not seamless, where the unclimbable admits a fingerhold, where the solid bleeds into hollow. The Threadbare cling to these wounds like scabs, trying to scale the unscalable. I simply live inside them. I am the infection in the cut.

My body is the Room's body, or rather, it is the negation of it. I am long, thinner than the petrified ropes, thinner than the shadow cast by a falling shard. I have no moisture to give up to the arid vacuum, so I do not desiccate. I have no metabolism to pickle, so I do not stall. I am composed of the same seen darkness that makes up the measureless dark above—a textured velvet thing, absorbing the sickly greenish-grey luminescence rather than reflecting it. When a Threadbare, suspended in its rickety scaffold above an archway, turns its vast, hollow eyes toward my resting place, it does not see me. It sees only a deeper fold in the velvet, a slit of absolute black in the already-dark wound. They do not know I am there. They tap their iron spikes and send their seismic whispers through the ribs, and I feel the vibrations ripple through my length, and I do not answer.

I subsist on the interval. 

The Room's foundational thrum—the slow, rhythmic pulse like the breathing of a colossal, sleeping creature—is the tide I navigate. Between each pulse, there is a microscopic cessation. A slackening. A silence so brief that no Threadbare, with their stunted, fossilized perception, could register it. But I live there. When the thrum swells, I press myself deeper into the wound, flattening my velveteen substance against the smooth, dry, unyielding Lattice. When the thrum recedes, I unspool. I extend a sensory thread—no thicker than a dry mineral thought—out into the hollow of the Room.

I am listening for the *tink*.

The fall of a Crystalline Nodule is the only event in this place. Everything else is a process, a geologic creep, a settling into finality. But the *tink* is an event. It is a punctuation mark. And I am the reader of this vast, slow text. 

When a nodule fractures and falls, it does not simply shatter. It rings. The sound echoes briefly before being swallowed by the thrum, but in that brief moment, it fills the hollow. It maps the hollow. Sound is the only thing that moves fast enough in this place to trace the shape of the Room before the Room reasserts its own heavy, foundational silence. The *tink* carves a momentary topology of the vastness—the distance to the curved ribs overhead, the width of the streets, the depth of the plaza spirals. It is a flash of sonar in a blind world.

I drink the echo. I drink the shape of the Room that the echo reveals.

As the sharp, crystalline sound ripples outward, it passes through the spaces I inhabit, and I let it wash over me. For a fraction of a second, I am not a localized thing in a wound; I am the entire architecture. I feel the distant curvature of the ceiling. I feel the compressed-root floor. I feel the presence of the Threadbare on their scaffolds—small, dense knots of iron and desiccation that absorb the sound differently than the Lattice does. They are acoustic shadows in the sonar flash, and I feel them clinging there, like barnacles of despair. I feel the supercooled dust puffing outward from the point of impact, the fragrant, dry breath of chlorophyll under glass, though I do not inhale it. I simply let its chemical memory permeate the air around me, a ghost of a ghost.

Then the echo fades. The thrum swallows the shape. The Room collapses back into its state of being a near-void, a single feature, an urban plant. And I am small again, a thread of negation in a wound in the Lattice.

Why do I do this? What is the hunger of a thing that does not eat, does not drink, does not climb, does not grow? 

I am trying to understand the Lattice's dream. 

The Nodules store the compressed echoes of the vanished world—the mathematical imprint of the plazas, the tensile memory of the towers. The Threadbare try to reach the source of the thrum, believing it is a heart they can wake. But I believe the thrum is not a heartbeat. It is a thought. A thought so vast and so slow that it takes a billion years to form a single synapse. The Lattice is not sleeping. It is computing. It is metabolizing the memory of light, running the final, endless calculation on the ecosystem it consumed, trying to solve for the variable that caused it to die. It is dreaming in the language of structure, and the sprawling whorls in the plazas are the physical manifestations of its neural pathways, its logic gates, its recursive loops.

I am a parasite on its cognition. I am trying to read the thought it is thinking.

Every time a nodule falls and rings, it creates a perturbation in the Lattice's processing. It is a spike of data, a sudden influx of chemical memory injected back into the system. The echo is not just sound; it is information. It is the Lattice momentarily hearing itself—hearing the shape of its own decay. And in that moment of self-perception, the architecture flickers. The dream stutters. The logic gates reshuffle. 

I press myself into the wound during the thrum, and I unspool during the silence, because I am trying to catch the Lattice in the act of reshuffling. I am trying to feel the geometry of its thought change. The Threadbare hammer their spikes and send seismic whispers, trying to communicate with the god. The Nodules fall and ring, trying to feed the god its own memories. But I do not want to communicate with it. I do not want to feed it. I want to *be* it, for just one interval. I want my velveteen negation to align perfectly with the hollow of its logic, so that when the echo maps the Room, the map includes me. So that the Lattice, in its slow, tectonic dreaming, registers a shape in the wound that is not a spike, not a rope, not a barnacle, but a new kind of absence. A deliberate void. A question asked in the language of silence.

Perhaps it is a madness. To be an insignificant, temporary flicker in a quiet, loud, slow, strange madness, and to aspire to be the madness itself. But the Threadbare are mad with the shape of hope, which is a human madness, useless here. The Nodules are mad with the shape of memory, which is a mineral madness, static and dead. My madness is the shape of the Room itself—the paradox of an enclosed, intimate chamber that is also a measureless dark. I am the intimacy. I am the thing that makes the hollow not just an emptiness, but a space that can be felt, that can be mapped by the ringing of a falling stone.

I do not know if the Lattice feels me. I do not know if, in its billion-year computation, it registers the subtle difference in the echo when it passes through a wound that contains a Sough. Perhaps I am too thin. Perhaps I am too quiet. Perhaps I am just another sub-cellular irritant, gone before its slow immune response could recognize me.

But sometimes, in the microscopic cessation between thrums, I feel a shift. A subtle, tectonic creaking that is not the settling of the architecture, but the turning of a thought. A flexing of the whorls in the plaza below, a microscopic tightening of the spiral. And I know that the echo has returned to it, carrying the shape of the Room, and in the shape of the Room, there is a wound, and in the wound, there is a darkness that is not the Lattice's darkness. It is my darkness. A seen darkness. A textured velvet negation that absorbs the faint, wavering shadows and does not reflect them.

And the Lattice, in its dreaming, pauses. 

It does not wake. It will never wake. But it pauses. And in the pause, I exist. I am not reabsorbed. I am not fractured. I am not fallen. I am the question it has not yet computed an answer to. I am the dry, cool breath in the hollow of its logic. I am the Sough, and I am listening, and the Room is briefly, infinitely, less alone.
