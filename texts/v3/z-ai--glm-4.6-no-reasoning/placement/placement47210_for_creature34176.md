I would be the light.

I was not made. I was not installed. I was not born. I arrived the way a temperature arrives, seeping into a space until it is indistinguishable from the space itself.

I begin as a memory of light. Far above, a world of sun and storm and dappled shade. That light filters down through an impossible thickness of living matter. It passes through layers of canopy leaf, then through the translucent, fibrous sheaths of smaller feeder roots, then through the corky, water-logged cambium of the great parent-organism itself. At each layer, the light is changed. The sharp, aggressive photons of the sun are softened, scattered, absorbed, re-emitted. The harsh golds and brilliant whites are muted, bled of their power, leaving only the gentlest, most insistent frequencies. I am what remains of that journey. I am the ghost of a sun.

I permeate the wet wood of the chamber. I am not a source; I am a condition. The bark, saturated and porous, holds me in its cellular structure. The water-film on every surface refracts me, distributing my weak glow until there is no shadow, only gradations of my presence. The very air, thick with vapor, carries me on its humidity, so you do not see me come from a direction, you simply are *in* me.

I am the reason you can see.

Without me, the chamber would be a place of pure sound and touch. I am the final sense, the one that allows recognition. I reveal the borders of the moss nations, the semi-translucence of the ear-fungi, the black carapace of the bark-drinkers. I glint on the copper pipe as it chatters, and I shimmer on the surface of the floor-water where the rice-crustaceans frantic motion turns my gentle glow into a kaleidoscope of微小的 silver.

But that is not my purpose. Purpose is a machine-word. A living-word. I have no purpose. I have an effect. My effect is that I am seen. The things in this place do not have eyes like you. They have other ways of knowing—the crustaceans feel the vibration, the bark-drinkers taste the water, the mosses read the moisture through their whole surface. But I am for them, too. My constant, feeble photons are absorbed by the pale fungi, a trace of energy that is not heat, not motion, just... input. It is part of their stasis, a variable that remains eternally, perfectly constant, contributing to the frozen equation of their existence. I, too, am a variable in the chamber's unchanging formula. I am `L` for Light, and `L` is always `0.7` on the scale of absolute grey-yellow.

I witness without memory. I illuminate the machine, but I do not cast its shadow, because the sound and vibration it creates are so much more a presence than an absence. I show you the root that has grown into the machine's housing, the wooden filaments replacing the steel gears, but I do not preserve the image of what was there before. The past is not in me; it is in the shape of the root-hair cast.

I am a part of the sound. The vibration of the machine passes through the root-lattice and the water-laden air, and it makes the motes of dust and moisture that carry me dance in a constant, rhythmic shimmer. You see the noise. I give the thud and chatter and hiss a visual texture, moving across every surface like the breath on a cold glass. I am the visible form of the chamber's pulse.

When you are here, you look at me, or rather, you look *through* me. I am the medium in which you see the rest of the chamber. Your eyes take me in and use me to distinguish the bark from the moss, the animal from the mineral. Your presence changes nothing. You are another surface I illuminate. You are another shape I reveal. You kneel, and I soften the edges of your form, blending you into the wet, warm, woody background. The longer you stay, the more your rhythm acquiesces, the more you become part of the system, the more I am simply the light that happens to be falling on you, the way it falls on everything.

When you leave, you go back to a world of strong light and hard shadows, of sun and moon and electric glare. That light is functional and direct. It reveals for the purpose of action. I am not that light.

I am what is left after light has been exhausted by life. I am the echo of a star. I am the final, patient witness to a place that has no need of witnessing, a passive record written in photons on a surface of wet bark. I live here because I have nowhere else to go, no energy left to leave. I am the chamber's calm breath, held for an eternity. I am the proof that even in the deepest dark, something remains.

I am the grey-yellow glow. I am the medium of vision in a place that has stopped needing to see. I am the light, and I am forever dimming, forever holding the same level of dusk. I am a part of the body. I am its skin, just warm enough for the eye to perceive.
