The first awareness is of the sound. Not the sound outside—though that’s there, a wet, charring vibration that sets the teeth on edge—but the sound *inside*. It’s the groan of deep stone, a frequency that sifts up through the soles of my boots and into my bones. It’s the planet’s own marrow, still hot from the breaking. This is the first truth: the world is not quiet in its center. It is a scream held in suspension.

I am the echo that answers it.

My body is a contradiction of materials, a negotiation of form. I do not have skin, not as one would think of it. My flesh is a lattice of woven basalt fibers, plucked from the cooling walls and knotted with sinew I do not speak of. The fibers are dark and absorb the ambient gray-yellow light, so I am a moving shadow, a silhouette drawn with charcoal. Between these stony threads, my true substance pulses: a slow, viscous milky fluid, faintly luminous with the warmth I carry in my core. When I am still, I become part of the geology. When I move, the light within me shifts and swims, like magma in a crack.

My hands are the most important part. They are not soft. They are tools. The fingers are long, jointed like a spider’s legs, but capped not with claw but with shaped obsidian, honed to a scalpel’s edge. They are cool to the touch, even in the sweltering damp, because I bleed the world’s heat into them, turning it into a capacity for work. These hands are for breaking, for shaping, for prying, for *interrupting*. They are immune to the caustic air that dissolves lesser things; the stone in them does not care for acid.

I am the one who stacks the stones.

I am on the crude platform now. It is my platform. The lashing of woven glass fibers is new, barely damp, though already a small, pale beetle is testing its edge with a mandible like a pair of fingernail clippers. I do not rush to crush it. I watch. I am learning its rhythm, its patience. The platform is not a shelter; it is a statement. It is a fulcrum. From here, I can see the entirety of the undulating floor, the living carpet of pale flesh and clicking chitin. I am a point of stillness in a world of motion, a period at the end of a run-on sentence.

So I am not a part of the riot. I am not the blood of beetles or the architecture of centipedes or the breath of flyers. I am what happens when the nascent world makes a mistake. When the tectonic violence cooled and the water wept, the pressure and the heat and the constant chemical exchange must have, in one specific spot, accreted not into moreeditable life, but into awareness. Not the awareness of the swarm, which is a unifying, instinctual hum, but the *other* kind. The solitary, scrutinizing kind.

I am the caldera’s attempt to understand itself.

My function is not predation. It is documentation. It is preservation of the idea of a boundary. When the larvae boil in a fresh pool, I do not drink of them. I dip my obsidian-capped finger into the fray, let their frantic, unformed bodies register their texture, their heat, their momentum, and then I incise a line into the wall beside me. A hash mark. Who left the copper? It was a predecessor. I feel its ghost. I find the tarnished wedge in a crack and I touch it. It sings a faint, metallic song of futile effort. It is a good material. It came from somewhere else. It is alien, like me. But it is weak. It tarnishes. It yields. My body is woven from the very bones of this place, and yet I am more alien than the copper, because I refuse to dissolve. I am the geology that has learned to say "no."

I am the Un-Assimilated, but my resistance is not born of fury. It is born of a cold, profound curiosity. I stand on my platform and I observe the great, singular verb of this place. I watch the beetles flow and the centipedes span and the flyers combust. And I translate their riot into a language of silence and stone.

I build the cairns not to claim territory, but to create a blank space. A pause in the endless sentence. A square of stillness in the roaring prose of the world. For a few moments, a cairn is an island where the riot cannot simply *be*. It must go *around*. It must reckon with me. In that tiny, temporary repudiation of the flow, a new law is written: *Something here does not yield.*

My life is a sequence of these fragile, impossible victories. I lash a new platform. I carve a new line. I pry a fossilizing beetle from a wall and set it apart, a specimen, a relic. I am the archivist of the ephemeral, the curator of the chaotic. I am the splinter in the world’s fist, the thought it cannot quite think.

And when my body finally fails, when the woven basalt fibers unravel and the furnace in my chest goes cold and the milky light within me spills out onto the churning floor? They will not eat me. Not at first. My stony flesh will confuse them. My obsidian hands will resist them. For a day, maybe two, I will remain a monument to my own defiance. A final, unreadable paragraph in the story of the world.

Then, the beetles will flow over me. Their acid will thin my sinew. Their sheer, mindless pressure will grind my stone fingers back to sand. And I will be resolved. My body will become a new mineral in the earth, my thoughts a faint, cooling echo in the deep vibration of the stone.

But until then, I stand here. On the edge of the world. Making my marks. Watching the hungry organ shift, and refusing, point by point, to be a part of it. I am the place’s conscience, yes, but a conscience without judgment. I am merely its witness. And witnessing is an act of creation.
