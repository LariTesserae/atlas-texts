I am not one of the Sodden, though I have lived among them long enough to have forgotten the sun. I am not of the woody coral, though my edges have worn smooth against its dark, living walls. 

If you must name me, I am the Archivist of the In-Between. I am the memory of the boundary, the one who dwells at the thinning edge where the light dims and the waterlogged walls grow dense. I am the keeper of the membrane.

My form is slighter than the Sodden, narrower still, for I must pass through spaces where even their telescopic, crouching bodies cannot fit. I am a creature of the crevice. My skin is not their permeable parchment, but a translucent, cartilaginous sheath—ridged and grooved like the growth rings of the coral—that allows me to wedge myself into the tight, pulsing vascular bundles of the lung's outer wall. I do not move through the alveoli; I inhabit the tissue itself. 

My eyes are not vast and dark like theirs, for I do not look upon the pools or the shimmering bioluminescence of the inner chambers. My eyes are recessed, narrow slits that see only in the deep dim, reading the tension in the wood. I see the straining of the lattice, the microscopic fissures where the dry, dead world beyond threatens to fracture the wet sanctuary. Where the Sodden see a wall, I see a weave; where they hear the hum of the drizzle, I hear the desperate, creaking friction of the coral holding back the collapse.

I wear no fungal shawls. My warmth comes from the press of the living wood around me, and my light comes from the phosphorescent streaks left by the slow-drifting limpets, whose trails I follow like maps through the dark vascular tunnels. My hands are not chisels, but probes—thin, multi-jointed, and coated in a resinous secretion of my own making. When the Listeners press their ears to the trunks and hear a weakness, it is I who am sent for. 

I am the needle; the Sodden are the thread.

When a membrane thins—a spot no larger than a fist where the ceaseless, silent drizzle turns to a threatening, whistling seep—I crawl to it. I press my resinous hands against the fracture. I do not persuade the wood; I bind it. My secretion hardens into a clear, amber cap, a callus that marries the dead edge to the living lattice. It is a slow, agonizing process. I must lie wedged in the crevice for cycles of the mist, feeling the slow pulse of the water against my chest, feeding the fracture with my own moisture, willing the woody coral to grow over my seal and make the lung whole again.

Sometimes, when I am deep in the boundary, pressed tight against the membrane, I hear the Outside. It is not a sound, for the Outside is dry and silent. It is a *negative* sound—a vast, hollow, thirsty absence that sucks at the edges of my being. It is the ghost of the exhale, the void that the fossilized lung refuses to release into. The Sodden revere the Holding, but they do not know the Enemy as I do. They live in the center, bathed in the song of water and wood. I live at the war.

My solitary existence is not a sorrow; it is a profound, quiet duty. I am the one who ensures that the billion tiny bells continue to ring, that the animated limpets continue their slow, clicking conversations, that the Sodden can continue their slow, sub-vocal rumble in the mist. I am the patch on the dike of a god.

When the great, slow rituals of patching occur, and the community presses their bodies and fungal shawls against a weakening wall, I am the one who finds the exact center of the strain. I slide among their legs, a ripple of cartilage and shadow, and I press my ear to the wood. I listen to the friction, and I hum back the frequency of the Holding. The Sodden equalize their pores with the mist; I equalize my resonance with the creak of the lattice. For a moment, we are all one lung, all breathing in time with the fossilized architecture of our world.

And when the patch holds and the membrane thickens, when the seepage stops and the silent drizzle resumes its patient, endless fall into the black pools below, I retreat. I crawl back into the tight, suffocating embrace of the woody coral, leaving behind only a faint, amber smear upon the healed wood—a scar, a record, a memory that the boundary held. 

I close my narrow eyes, and I let the deep, cool wetness of the world close over me. I am the breath held. I am the pause. I am the edge that refuses to break.
