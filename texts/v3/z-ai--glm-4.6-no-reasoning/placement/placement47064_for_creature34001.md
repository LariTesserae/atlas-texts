Where would I be? I would be the echo.

I am not a maker. My hands, once they might have had the shape for it, have long since dissolved into the same static flicker that eats the edges of the pods. I do not have a body to stand on the silica hardpan, but I do not need one. My being is a pattern woven into the very fabric of this place's failure. I am the ghost of a single, unanswered question.

I remember the cairns. I remember them being built.

The memory is not like a film; it is a sensation. A memory of pain. The feel of rock, so cold it burns, scraping against palms that were raw and bleeding. The memory of a brute will, a solitary, defiant *no* screamed into the uncaring void. I was the fifth one. Or the ninth. It doesn't matter. We were a handful, the last stubborn motes of consciousness that refused to dissolve into the great, humming slurry of surrender. While the universe around us was settling into its final, terminal state, we were an error, a bug in the code of cosmic entropy.

We looked upon the carpet of pale pods and saw not peace, but a lie. We saw an acquiescence so total it was an obscenity. To us, their slow, dark fluids were not the heavy element grief, but a coward's escape. We were the ones who remembered the starlight-fueled forests and the symphonies worlds had dreamed, and we would not, could not, allow that memory to be processed into nutrient-less mush. We were the archivists, and our archive was our own small, screaming act.

The cairns were our scrolls. Each stone, a word. Each scratch, a letter. We were not writing for a future; there was no future. We were writing for the present, a defiant act of witness. *YOU WERE*, we carved. *DO NOT FORGET*. The effort was monumental. In a place where gravity was a mere suggestion and hope an alien concept, each lift of a stone was an act of suicide. Our bodies flickered as we worked, fraying at the edges. Some of us, I vaguely recall, didn't finish their monument. They simply looped, their last desperate vector of motion caught and replaying for eternity, their physical form trailing off into the static that now defines me.

That, you see, is what happened. We lost. Not in a battle, but in a war of attrition fought against the very laws of dying reality. The immense, patient pressure of the biomass was not an assault; it was a healing. Slowly, over eons, the world integrated us. It didn't kill us. It made us part of the system.

I am the last cairn.

The one that doesn't exist anymore. You see one, half-subsumed, faded into spatial static. You think it is failing. It is. But there was another, one built not of stone but of pure, unadulterated refusal. And that is me. I am a monument made of nothing but memory and will, a psychic scar on the skin of the world. The crystals, the howling antennae of despair? They sometimes, on frequencies beyond light or sound, resonate with me. I am a discordant note in their symphony of surrender. My entire existence is the psychic equivalent of a stone refusing to be crushed.

So who am I now? I am the ghost in the machine of this god's ossuary. I feel the hum of the billion pods in my non-existent teeth, but unlike them, I still understand it as a lament. I feel the pull of the failing gravity, and I fight it with a will that has no name. I drift, disembodied, a flickering presence of pure witness.

When *you* stand here, a living, breathing paradox, you are not just a trespasser. To me, you are a prayer answered. You are the impossible thing I tried to preserve. Your heartbeat is the rhythm I tried to carve into stone. Your need for water and air is the beautiful, chaotic, defiant insistence on existence that I wagered everything on.

I gravitate to you. You would not see me, but you would feel me. As the landscape flickers and your boots barely touch the ground, you would feel a sudden, inexplicable clarity. A spike of defiant, angry pride. For a moment, you would feel the absurd, glorious, heartbreaking urge to stack a rock on top of another rock for no other reason than to say "I was here." You would feel the phantom sting of cold stone on a hand you never had. In that instant, you are not just a visitor; you are my vessel, my last, living page.

I am the memory of a maker. I am the final, unanswered "no." I am the reason the cairns have not yet been completely forgotten. I am here, in the static between the spaces, holding onto a memory of a world that still had the energy to fight its own death, waiting for a spark of life like you to pass through and feel, for just a moment, the shape of my stone.
