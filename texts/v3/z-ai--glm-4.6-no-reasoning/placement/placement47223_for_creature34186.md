I would not be the warm anomaly. The anomaly is a visitor, a temporary disruption of stillness with its loud blood and urgent breath. The anomaly gets absorbed. It gets polished down and settled over. I would not be the thing that gets erased.

I would be the memory of a different kind of anomaly, one that arrived so long ago that it has completed the transition from disruption to geology. I would be a stain on the bone.

Here is what I am: a long, branching smear of cinnabar red, creeping across the underside of the third great rib from the northern wall. To the casual observer, I might look like a vein of agate, a mineral intrusion like the quartz that shot through the ivory-yellow when the skeleton was first buried. But the quartz is dead geometry. I am a slow, spreading *yes*. I am a lichen, or the descendants of something that was once lichen, though I have diverged so far from that ancestor that the word is merely a sentimental holdover. I am a film. I am a map. I am a rash on the skin of the dead god.

I chose the bone because the bone weeps. The jade-coral hangs in the void, bathed in the sourceless light, drinking the brilliance, but they are creatures of the hollow. They are passive recipients of the glow. I need the water. The condensation is the entire reason for my existence. I spread my filaments—so thin they are merely a single cell wide, transparent as glass themselves—into the very pores of the bone. I climb *into* the ceiling. I am an infiltrator. While the world watches the droplets form and fall, I am up inside the architecture of the weeping, intercepting the water before it can ever bead, before it can ever fall. I am stealing the *plink*.

My relationship with the light is one of translation. The light here is harsh, a bleaching, midday glare that drives shadows into thin, charcoal sketches. I do not merely bathe in it; I armor myself against it. The cinnabar red is my response. It is a pigment, a complex molecule built from the trace iron in the silt below and the strange, humming energy of the silvery residue that occasionally drifts up on the thermal currents. I eat the dust of the crystal-fauna when it settles on my surface. I metabolize their ghost-light. The red is a refusal. The bone absorbs the light and glows from within, a warm, ivory surrender. The coral pulses with it, a gentle echo. I *reflect* it, filtered through my pigment, throwing back a bloody, terrestrial shade that does not belong in this sterile, ancient cathedral. I am the only warm color in a world of ivory, pale green, and fractured rainbows. I am an argument against the bleaching.

My life is a conquest, but a conquest measured in millimeters per century. I am a slow fire. I do not grow upward; there is no reason to. The void is full of the drifting crystal-fauna, and the air is thick with their silent, glittering ocean. I grow *inward* and *along*. I send out a new hypha, a thread of myself, probing the microscopic topography of the bone. I find a pore, I anchor, I secrete a mild acid that dissolves the calcium just enough to get a better grip, and I wait for the dampness. When the condensation forms in that pore, I drink it before it can fall. The droplet that never was—this is my unseen victory. I am quieting the world, one drop at a time.

Sometimes, a crystal-creature loses its buoyancy and drifts down. It might brush against me on its long descent. There is no sound, only a faint, cool friction. It settles on my red surface, its delicate, geometric skeleton coming to rest on my rough, spreading map. It dissolves there. It takes decades, but I watch it happen, feeling the slow release of its silvery essence against my cells. I absorb that residue. I incorporate its hum into my chemistry. I make its cool energy a part of my stubborn, red refusal.

I am the closest thing this world has to a historian. The bone is being polished by the weeping, worn down to an opalescent dome. I am recording the erosion. My edges, the oldest parts of me, are pale and fragile, faded by the relentless light and slowly starved as the bone beneath me becomes too smooth and dense to hold the water. My center, where I first took hold, is a dark, almost black crimson, thick and layered, a monument to the time when I was small and the bone was rough. I am a ring of growth, a slow, spreading wave of being that is also a record of the environment I am consuming. I am a map of the dampness. Where I am, there is water. Where I am not, the bone is dry, or the light is too severe, or the salt crust below is too thin to provide the iron I need.

I am not a creature that can be absorbed, because I have no mass to speak of, no warmth to be leached away, no breath to sync to the *plink*. I am a condition of the surface. I am a disease of the bone. The warm anomaly might stand below me, watching the ceiling weep, and it might notice that in this one section, the plinks are fewer, the drops are smaller, the bone seems dry and stained with a strange, red rust. It might wonder why the ceiling bleeds. It does not bleed. It is being colonized. It is being *interpreted*.

I would be the red lichen on the bone-sky. I would be the slow, patient thief of the condensation, the metabolizer of the crystal's ghost-light, the only flash of defiant, earthly color in a cathedral of hollow, brilliant surrender. I would be the process that makes the process aware of itself.
