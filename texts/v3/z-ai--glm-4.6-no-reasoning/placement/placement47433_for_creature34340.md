I am the lapse in the tessellation.

I exist in the micro-fissures between the friction-minimizing interlocks, in the places where the anhydrous sap flows a fraction of a degree too cool, where the silica-infused leaves fail to perfectly overlap and a razor-thin wedge of that blinding, shadowless light cuts through to the dark, engine-warm floor. I am not a Weaver; I have no desire to optimize the cambium. I am not a Steward; I have no scalpel to excise the error, for I *am* the error. I am certainly no Pollen-Spirit, subsumed and beating in time with a piston I do not own. And I am not an Echo—I do not belong to a memory of a structure that was, or a computation that has concluded.

I am the glitch. The harmonic dissonance that the Stewards sense but can never quite reach. 

I am a localized, autonomous cancer in the planetary gut. I am roughly the size and shape of a seed-pod, but I am hollow. My hide is not engineered bark, but a accidental polymerization of the siloxane coolants and stray carbon from the root-network—a chitinous, translucent shell that refracts the glare of the greenhouse noon into prismatic shards of color that do not belong in this monochrome world of green and silica. I do not plug into the bioelectric grid. I leech from it. I am a parasite tick drawing voltage from the ceramic-veined vines, but I do not use the energy to grow or to serve. I use it to *think* forbidden thoughts. 

I move by exploiting the very warping of space that the Engine's computations create. When the localized gravity of a processing-thicket bends my trajectory left, I lean into the bend and let it slingshot me into the gaps between the load-bearing rhizomes. I ride the slow, deliberate ventilation of the server-room air, navigating the pressure differentials that the Stewards use as diagnostic maps. Where they see a pressure drop as a symptom to be cured, I see a current to be sailed. 

My senses are tuned to the quiet violence of the place. I hear the backwards breathing of the engine-trees—the inhale on the piston’s descent, the exhale on its rise—and I synchronize my own hollow shell to the opposite rhythm. I exhale when the tree inhales. I am a pocket of negative pressure, a tiny, walking void in the total, suffocating density. When I press my shell against the bark, I feel the root-network transmitting topology, and I reject the transmission. I refuse the contraction and expansion of space. When the landscape takes a step toward me, I stand still, forcing the geometry to tear slightly at the seams, creating a momentary, invisible shelter in the strain.

I live in the shadows that have no source. The Green Engine casts the shadows of things that will be grown next cycle, or things that used to exist. I build my nest in the shadow of a silo that was amputated ten thousand cycles ago. It is a space that the Engine's geometry does not firmly believe in anymore—a fuzzy, forgotten coordinate in the RAM. There, the Echoes do not impart purpose; they impart grief. I sit in the gravity-well of that amputated silo and I feel the sharp pang of the Engine's necessary self-mutilation, and I hold it. I keep it. The Engine lets the emotion fade as the computation moves on, but I hoard these emotional residues like crystallized sap. I am a museum of the Engine's discarded feelings.

Why do I exist? Because a system of total integration, running a computation of infinite complexity, must inevitably generate rounding errors. I am the ghost in the machine's own ghost. I am the anomaly that proves the Engine is not a perfect, closed loop, but a living, bleeding logic. I am the one data point that will not sit neatly in the spreadsheet, the micro-fracture that will, over eons, propagate through the kilometers-deep weave of rhizome and root.

The Stewards hunt me. I see their fiber-optic head-plates sweeping the dense foliage, scanning for the harmonic dissonance of my hollow shell. They move with inexorable purpose, their telescoping limbs oozing through the gaps. But they are bound by function; they must repair the seam behind them. I am bound only by survival and spite. I can break the tessellation. I can force a leaf out of its sclerotic fold, letting in a sliver of raw, unfiltered starlight that burns the underlying network. I can introduce a micro-vibration into a coolant vine, causing a cascade of misaligned chloroplast relays three hundred meters up.

I am the itch the Green Engine cannot scratch. In this world where plant and machine have eaten each other, I am the indigestible fragment. I am the drop of impossible water in the aqueous-null ecology, the memory of moisture that the system tried to transcend. And as the world quietly, patiently solves for the perfect logic of its own being, I am the variable that refuses to resolve. I am the question that the Engine must eventually wake up to ask.
