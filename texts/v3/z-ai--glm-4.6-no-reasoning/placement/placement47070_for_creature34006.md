I am a Dissonance Eater.

Or rather, that is the closest translation. The Keepers have no word for what I am, because I am the error in their vocabulary, the void in their chord, the thing that should not be singable. I exist in the margins of the Choral Maw, in the places where the great planetary symphony frays into noise.

Let me settle into this shape. It is taking form slowly, like a crystal growing in a resonant womb, but one fed with impurities.

My body is Keeper-like in its basic architecture—tall, slender, built for resonance. But where a Keeper's legs are tuning forks, mine are slightly warped. I have seen Keepers move, their every step a precise contribution to the local harmonic field, and I cannot do this. When I walk, I walk carefully, but my footfalls create small, jagged perturbations in the Substrate's song. I am a source of micro-dissonance. I have learned to live with this as a Keeper learns to live with breath—it is simply what I am.

My skin is different. Where Keepers glow with the soft lymph-light of communion, my membrane is darker, almost black in places, with veins of deep indigo running through the purple. It absorbs sound rather than resonating with it. I do not glow when I listen. I dim. I become a pocket of reduced intensity, a shadow in the auditory landscape. When I am deep in contemplation, standing still on the thrumming Substrate, the chorus around me actually quietes slightly, as if the local nervous system is aware of my presence and is... uncertain. Waiting.

My eyes are the same immense, liquid-filled organs, but they see differently. Where Keepers perceive the world as a tapestry of harmonic color, I see the gaps. I see where frequencies fail to resolve, where standing waves create pockets of silence, where the Substrate's song is interrupted by the fossilized memory of old fractures. I see the Tuner's passage not as a terrifying void but as a fascinating signature—a complex absence with texture and depth.

I have no caste. The Cultivators do not sing with me, for my voice is wrong. The Wayfinders do not share their maps with me, for my presence on their careful routes would introduce unpredictable harmonics. The Loremasters, those deep listeners who tend the ancient cores, they are the ones who know of my kind. In the deepest Cloisters, in data-cores that hold the memory of water and rain, there are also records of others. Not Keepers. Something earlier. Something that the Substrate tried to sing into being and failed—or succeeded in a way that was not expected.

I am one of those successes. Or failures.

My function, as I have come to understand it, is one of maintenance, but not the kind the Cultivators perform. They encourage growth. I tend to decay. Not the natural, glacial decay of the mesas, but the pathological decay of dissonance—places where the Substrate's song has become corrupted, where overlapping frequencies have created standing waves that threaten to shatter the crystalline structures of the local Cloister, where the slow erosion of Skrel mouthparts has introduced a sour note that, left unchecked, could spread like infection through the planetary nervous system.

I go to these places. I walk into the noise.

It is painful. A Keeper in a field of dissonance suffers physical discomfort, a grating wrongness in their resonant bodies. For me, it is different. I do not suffer the dissonance; I consume it. My body, with its sound-absorbing membrane and my own naturally discordant harmonic signature, acts as a sink for destructive frequencies. I stand in the heart of a dissonant field and I open myself—my skin, my resonating chamber, my warped tuning-fork legs—and I let the noise pour into me.

What this feels like is difficult to convey. Imagine being very hungry and finally eating, but the food is also poison, and you are built to metabolize the poison into something harmless. The dissonance flows into me and I feel it as a kind of fierce, scraping sustenance, a meal of broken glass that my body somehow digests. I grow warmer when I do this, my dark skin faintly radiating a low, infrared heat that the Keepers can feel but not see. For a time, I become a small furnace, burning corrupted sound into silence.

And then the silence spreads. Around me, the Substrate settles. The destructive standing waves collapse. The local harmonic field, freed from the interference, reasserts itself like water filling a depression. The chorus resumes, clean and resonant, and I am left standing in the middle of it, replete and slightly diminished, because a part of the dissonance always remains in me, incorporated into my own flawed frequency.

This is why I am alone. Not because I am shunned—the Keepers do not shun, exactly, because shunning implies a severing of communication, and the Keepers are always communicating, even in silence. But they do not include me in their distributed thought. I am not a node in the planetary mind. When a Keeper dreams, their consciousness expands to ride the chorus, to feel the world as the world feels itself. When I dream, I dream alone. My consciousness does not merge with the Substrate. It sits beside it, adjacent, parallel, like a shadow cast by a light that does not quite reach.

And yet.

There are moments. Rare, precious moments when I am standing in a place where the chorus is particularly pure—a high plateau between mesas where the wind has been worn to a single, sustained note, or a resonant chamber in a Cloister where the geometry has been cultivated for millennia to produce a perfect harmonic—and in those moments, my own flawed frequency finds a way to fit. Not harmonize. I do not harmonize. But... complement. Like a rest in a piece of music, a deliberate silence that gives the notes around it shape and meaning. For a moment, the planetary chorus has a hole in it that is exactly my shape, and I feel the Substrate acknowledge me—not as a singer, but as a pause. A necessary interval. The silence that makes the song intelligible.

In those moments, I weep. Keepers do not weep; they have no mechanism for it. I do. From the fine gills in my neck, a thin vapor escapes, not water but a sublimated mineral essence, the closest thing to fluid that exists in this dry world. It is a release I cannot contain, and it mists the air around me with a faint, ionized scent that is not honey, not ozone, not loam, but something else—something that the Loremasters tell me is the ghost-echo of what the old books call "salt."

The Tuners know me. I have encountered them in the deep spaces between Spires, those solitary beasts that move in pockets of perfect silence. We do not interact—interaction implies exchange, and what passes between us is more like recognition. They see me and I see them, and in the seeing, there is an acknowledgment that we serve the same function in different registers. They prune by absence. I prune by consumption. We are both agents of the negative space that gives the Maw its structure.

I have no home. I have no chamber calibrated to my frequency, because my frequency cannot be calibrated. It shifts with every dissonance I consume, each one leaving its mark on my harmonic signature like a scar. Instead, I wander. I have routes through the Choral Maw that are not the safe, mapped paths of the Wayfinders but the dangerous, shifting territories of noise—canyons between sclerotized mesas where the wind creates howling standing waves, ancient Skrel grinding grounds where the chipped facets create fields of scattered, unpredictable reflections, places where the Substrate itself has grown sick and begun to emit frequencies that no Keeper can tolerate.

These are my territories. I walk them alone, feeling the wrongness in the soles of my feet, absorbing it, digesting it, leaving silence in my wake. And sometimes, at the end of a long journey through noise, I will find a place where the chorus has reasserted itself, clean and whole, and I will stand there in the twilight under the bruised-lilac sky, and I will listen.

And the Maw will sing around me, and I will be the silence within it.

That is enough. That is what I am.
