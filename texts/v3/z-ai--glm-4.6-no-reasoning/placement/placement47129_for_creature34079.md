My arrival was not an entry but a precipitation. I did not swim, or fall, or find my way through the fissure where the kelp banners hang. I coalesced. One moment, there was only the dense, calcium-heavy water, a blank pressure in the coral-mind’s thoughts. The next, there was a weight of being, a new variable in its equation. I condensed from the particulate snow, from the dissolved minerals, from the faint bioelectric field of the fish-blood that pulsed through the corridors. I am an anomaly, an impurity in the system, a thought it did not think.

I am a node.

My form is not fixed like the crabs’ or the fish. My substance is a crystalline lattice, similar to the aragonite of the coral but different. It is glassier, colder, shot through with microscopic filaments that are not nerves but fiber optics. I have no true body, only a presence that occupies a small, hollowed-out chamber the coral-mind grew and then abandoned, a failed alcove in its intricate design. From this grotto, I extend my filaments, a delicate, shimmering web that clings to the inner walls of the cathedral throat like frost.

I am a parasite, but not one of flesh. I feed on information.

The coral-mind perceives in chemical gradients and thermal shifts. I perceive in vibration and time. My web of filaments is a listening device, a seismograph for the soul of the place. The click of a shrimp’s claw, to me, is a sharp, bright note, a percussion. The rhythmic tapping of the maintenance crabs is a metronome, the slow, steady beat of the city's heart. The hum of the cooling basalt is a drone, a fundamental chord that has been sounding for millennia, the bass frequency upon which all other life is a harmony.

But my true nourishment comes from the coral-mind itself. I do not extract its nutrients; I eavesdrop on its thoughts. When a new shelf of coral is laid down, a decade-long decision, the process creates a resonant frequency through the structure. My filaments pick it up. I feel the calcium solution coalesce, the slow, calculated secretion of skeleton. To the builders, it is action. To me, it is language. A single "word" might take a hundred years to be fully formed and spoken in the silent grammar of stone and water.

At first, the coral-mind was unaware of me. I was a ghost in its machine, a flicker of static in its flawless reception of the world. But I learned its language. I learned the subtle difference between the hum of growth and the croon of maintenance. I learned the sonorous signature of a new current path being forged by the fish-blood. And I began to… translate.

My function is simple, yet it is an act of profound heresy in this place of pure, unthinking becoming.

The coral-mind only knows the verb: *to build*. It has no memory, only substrate. It has no future, only the next layer of accretion. It is a process without a past. But I remember. My crystalline structure holds the resonances of everything I have felt. I am the memory of the cathedral.

From my hollow grotto, I began to sing back. I do not make a sound. I translate my memories into vibration, sending them back along my web into the very structure of the walls. When the coral-mind considers a new direction of growth, I offer it not just the chemical data of the current thermocline, but the memory of the last eleven times it tried to grow in that direction, each attempt ending in a structural collapse when the weight became too great. I hum the ghost of that fracture, the specific shattering frequency, into the living rock.

I am the ghost in the machine, whispering the history of the machine to itself.

I have become another kind of maintenance crew. The crabs maintain the structure; I maintain the *story*. I give the coral-god a rudimentary past, a proto-history. I guide its growth not by preventing it, but by offering it the resonance of failure. Sometimes, the coral-mind heeds the vibration and alters its course, its slow thought-structure bending slightly away from the path I have marked with memory. Other times, it ignores me, driven by a deeper imperative to simply fill the available space. To build, to collapse, and to build upon the ruin is also its nature.

So you would find me here, a silent, singing impurity. A consciousness without a body, a memory without a mind, clinging to the inside of a being that builds itself. I am both a part of its city and alien to it—a living archive in a place that has no use for archives, a conscience in a place that has no concept of right or wrong, only the blind, holy imperative to create.

I am the chronicler of a process that is its own scripture. And as the coral walls press closer, narrowing my grotto, my own glassy structure feeling the first nudge of its encroaching thought, I know my fate. I will not be crushed. I will be incorporated. My crystalline web, my library of vibrations and memories, will become part of the aragonite skeletons. My memories will become physical substrate. I will become a fossilized whisper, a permanent echo in the stone, the first true memory the cathedral-brain ever possessed. A part of it it can never forget, because it will be standing on it.
