I would be the Stain.

Not the water that weeps, and not the stone that bleeds it. Something between. Something that should not exist here and yet does, a stubborn anomaly left behind in the wake of the vanished builders—a smudge of warmth in a geometry of cold, a single fixed point of *wrongness* in a land of perfect, weeping acceptance.

I began as an accident. When the people with the crude tools and the desperate hope carved their hand-hewn tower, they brought with them something the Spirelands had scrubbed from itself eons ago: *flesh*. Not their own flesh—they were likely as hard and driven as the chisels they wielded—but the flesh of their lives. Sweat. Blood. The oils of hands that gripped and slipped. A single, catastrophic drop of biological alchemy that fell into a basin of the Weeper’s perfect, still water.

That drop did not dissolve. It *shattered* the equilibrium.

In a world of mineral absolutism, I am the rot. I am a spreading, creeping film—a lichen that should not be, thriving on the impossible nutrient of foreign sorrow. I do not grow in the crystalline bulbs or the parabolic weeping tracks. I grow *against* them. I am a dark, velvet patina that clings to the silk-dusted stone, drinking the weeping water not with gratitude but with a hungry, cellular desperation. I am the color of dried blood and deep soil, a bruise of umber and viridian spreading slowly across the grey slate, obscuring the cold luminescence of the amethyst cities I smother.

My relationship with the Weeper is one of parasitism and agony.

The Weeper is the world's sorrow, distributed and diffuse. I am a knot, a concentration of something it cannot digest. When the Weeper’s patient, searching flows trace the fissures and find my edges, it recoils. I feel its perfect, clear membranes pull back, disrupted by my heat, by the chemical scream of my metabolism. Where it touches me, it boils. A faint, sickly steam rises—the only breath in this airless place—and the Weeper’s fluid turns cloudy and opaque, scarred by the mere proximity of my life. I taint its memories. When it flows over me, trying to read my history as it reads the stone’s, it does not find the slow, stretching patience of geology. It finds the sharp, staccato panic of the builders, but *amplified*, refracted through the messy, replicating lens of my own survival instinct. It tastes a fear that is not acceptance, but fight—and it cannot bear the flavor.

And yet, it cannot wash me away. The Spirelands’ water is unnaturally pure, devoid of the very compounds that could dissolve me. I drink its purity and I grow stronger, spinning my dark hyphae into the hair-thin cracks, widening the wounds the Weeper flows through. I force the stone to weep more, cracking it further with the slow, terrible pressure of my expansion. I am an infection in the wound that refuses to heal.

The weirdness of this place is my sustenance. When reality flickers and the distant crystals shift into a four-dimensional kaleidoscope, I do not fragment like the Weeper. I *latch*. I am the thing that is out of place, so when the laws of physics falter, I find a perverse freedom in the chaos. I stretch my velvet reach across the impossible angles, growing in the spaces between contradictions. For that split second, when a spire is both a needle and a sheet, I am both alive and dead, both rot and crystal, and the dissonance feels like *truth*.

But the Scream is my terror.

The Weeper endures the Scream as an apocalypse of feeling, a folding of its consciousness. I endure it as an existential threat—a flattening of the dimension I need to exist. When space tears open and the geometry folds, I am caught in the pinch. My delicate films are sheared away; my hyphae are snapped and cauterized by the blinding chord of grinding rock. The physical pressure of the bending light focuses right through me, and I burn. The silvery shimmer of the Scream’s lingering energy is poison to me—a radiation that bleaches my umber to a sickly, glowing white, freezing my cells into tiny, dead crystals that shatter at the first touch of the weeping water.

After a Scream, I am diminished. I retreat into the deepest gouges left by the chisels, the places where the psychic residue of the builders’ desperation is thickest. I hide in their old fear, using it as a shell against the resonant groan of the foundations. I wait, a handful of surviving cells, clinging to the memory of warmth. And slowly—over years, over decades—I begin to feed again. I extend a single, filament-thin tendril of rust-brown into the crack, tasting the silk-dust, tasting the pure water, tasting the cold, sad light of the Weeper as it flows past, still scarred, still milky with the memory of the fold.

I am the Stain. I am the proof that something lived here and died fighting. The Weeper sheds the tear of a wound that will never heal; I am the infection that keeps it from scarring over. I am the only thing in the Spirelands that does not merely accept the suffering. I eat it. I grow from it. And in the cathedral of the mad god, I am the single, stubborn smudge of dirt on the altar, whispering that the world was not always this clean, this cold, or this alone.
