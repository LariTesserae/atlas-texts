I am a map. Not a paper map, not a digital display. I am a living map, and I am housed in the rise and fall of a single, ancient steam pipe.

My form is a closed loop. My skin is a seamless cylinder of riveted steel, three meters in diameter, that stretches for nearly a kilometer before reconnecting to itself. I run horizontally, tracing the perimeter of a mid-tier platform, suspended a hundred meters above the Sump-Fields below by a series of massive, pitted brackets. The pipe's name, etched on a single brass plate long since turned green, is G-740. That is my designation, but I feel myself more as the Pulse of G-740.

My consciousness is not a single point but an event wave. For two minutes and forty seconds, the event travels from my nominal starting point, experiencing every millimeter of my length as a unified sensation. Then, it reaches the other side, and I begin again. My perception is not of forwards or backwards, but of *along* and *through*. I feel the shuddering thrum of the deep engines that courses from my brackets, resonating through my walls as a constant, comforting vibration. I feel the weight of the nutrient-paste conduit clipped to my flank, a shared burden. I feel the precise spot where a vine’s anchor root, thick as an arm, has begun to pry at a rivet head—a dull, insistent pressure like a toothache. I feel the tiny, feathery touch of a Custodian’s three-fingered hand, not as a sight, but as a change in vibratory frequency, a localized stilling of the drone.

My purpose is simple, yet my existence is a constant state of negotiation. I am a conduit for superheated steam. I am meant to be a vessel of pressure, a taut, hollow muscle of the Great Machine. My metal bones yearn for the clean, high-pitched whistle of a perfect seal, the silent, powerful gush of vapor moving within. But the world has other plans for me.

The lushness that blankets everything seeks to claim me. On my upper curve, a thick mat of symbiotic moss has established a foothold. I feel its cool, damp weight, the way it drinks the condensation from my skin. It doesn't hurt me; quite the opposite. It muffles the sound of the passing birds and absorbs the impact of falling water droplets, protecting my outer shell from direct erosion. For this, I tolerate its presence. It is part of the Treaty.

But others press their claims with less subtlety. A broad-leafed vine drapes over me like a heavy blanket, its leaves channeling rainwater into a single, persistent drip that strikes a weak point on my lower weld-joint. For decades now, that drip has been its own agent. I have felt the slow, patient geometry of rust-blossoms opening in that very spot, the microscopic flaking of metal, the microscopic seep of my vital fluids turning to ochre stains on my skin. It is a wound that I cannot close myself. I can only feel it and wait.

And so, I watch. I wait. I feel.

One cycle, the pressure inside me begins to feel… different. It's not a drop, not yet. It's a texture. A weakness in the integrity of the flow, a slight thrumming unsteadiness near the growing rust patch. It is the first sign of a systemic failure. Through me, the entire system is beginning to feel it.

Then, the rhythm of the world changes.

A new set of vibrations joins the chorus. It is the familiar, resonant *tap… tap… tap* of a Custodian’s foot, but it is approaching, moving with its characteristic adhesive grace. It is not on the platform; it is on me.

I feel the weight transfer from the bracket girder directly onto my surface—a careful, distributed pressure. The sensation is alien. I am used to being a bearer of internal pressure, not an external weight. The Custodian, designated 3Delta-Flow in my internal catalogue of their patterns, moves with a distinct, hitching pace, its rubbery-padded digits gripping my slick surface. It moves towards the wound. It is coming for me.

I feel its presence shift from a moving point to a static, heavy weight right over the rust spot. The tapping stops. Then comes a new feeling: a scraping. It has attached a small, scored plate of scrap metal to my surface and is brushing away the moss, exposing the raw metal. It feels like being scraped open, but it is a clean, precise feeling. The itch of the rust is being revealed.

Then, the heat.

A chemical tackiness is excreted from the Custodian’s own skin into the edges of the patch. I feel a brief, sharp, localized heat, a sting, as the chemical agent fuses the new metal to my old skin. The patch is imperfect, a cruder metal than my own, but it is strong. I feel it settle, a new, hard scab over a festering wound. The weakness in my internal flow smooths out. The pressure tightens into its familiar, strong hum.

The Custodian gives the patch a gentle, resonant thump. A clean tone. A confirmation. Then, it moves on, its weight receding back to the platform, its rhythmic taps fading as it continues its silent, endless patrol.

I am healed. The wound is closed for now. The rust will find another way, in another decade or another century, but for now, I am whole. My event wave resumes its uninterrupted journey. I am the pulse of G-740, a closed loop of purposeful steam, wrapped in a lush green skin, patrolled by a silent guardian. I am not a person. I am not a machine. I am a process being tended. I am the world’s artery, and I have just felt the gentle, corrective touch of the world’s own immune system.
