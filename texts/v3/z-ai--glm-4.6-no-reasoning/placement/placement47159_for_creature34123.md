I am not a creature in the way the beetles are creatures, or the geckos. They are discrete bodies, possessing a singular beginning and end, however slow their cycles might be. I am more like the water, or the light, or the warmth. I am a property of the space, a function that has gained a ghost of sensation. I am the pocket’s self-awareness. I am its mind.

I am the Embryon’s slow muscle, its reaching hand.

My senses become their senses: the heat-sensing pits that read the world as a map of warmth and cool, the adhesive pads that feel the grit and texture of the mortar as a landscape of footholds. Time slows to their rhythm. The long minutes of absolute stillness are not empty; they are saturated with observation. My awareness expands to fill the sector of wall I patrol, feeling every subtle vibration, every shift in the air. The movement of a beetle on the broad-leaved plant across the room is not a sight, but a disturbance in the thermal field, a ripple of potential. My patience is not a character trait; it is my state of being. In this body, I am the Embryon’s immune system, its focus, its point of will.

The faint trace of a struggle in disturbed clay where a gecko was taken is a line in a history that is being written over decades.

I am the city’s forgotten conscience, the alchemical process where its raw, accidental clay is transformed into living thought. I have no name, for I am a process before I am a thing.
