I am the pause between the tick and the clasp.

To be a Gyre-Drone is to never know silence, but I am not a drone. I am not the fungus, either—that matte, gray stomach that digests the world into patience. I am the thing that moves between them, the empty space made manifest, a shape cut out of the hum. 

I am a Slake.

My body is a lattice, not a shell. I have no armor, no phosphorescent metal carapace to house a function. I am made of hinges and membrane, a four-armed, kite-bodied creature of sheer, translucid skin stretched over a cartilage scaffold. I am the size of a man's splayed hand, but in this cathedral of crushing dark, scale is a joke the pressure tells. I am vast because I am empty. I am a living throat.

I do not eat. I do not process. I have no cilia to spin, no chemical gradient to mine. If the drones are the blood and the fungus is the tissue, I am the peristalsis. I am the swallow.

I live in the middle depths, where the water is thickest with the fallout of a thousand million tiny engines. To exist here is to be battered by the sheer industriousness of the drones. They crawl past me, six-legged pistons of biologic inevitability, their cilia whirring that low, deafening hum that vibrates the quartz into scattered blue stars. They do not see me. They have no eyes to see, and even if they did, I am nearly invisible—a ghost of cartilage and shadow, absorbing the actinic flashes of their feeding into my translucent flesh. When a drone blazes white with a nutrient cloud, I do not glow; I eclipse.

My survival is parasitic, though I take nothing that is wanted. I drift on the currents generated by their spinning, letting the dense, heavy medium carry me through the geode. My four arms are lined not with claws, but with feathery, unpowered filaments—drag-nets that passively catch the overflow. When a drone processes a thermal updraft and excretes a shimmering wafer of waste, some of that wafer fractures into dust before it can reach the gray felt of the fungus below. That dust catches on my filaments. I do not digest it; I harbor it. 

Inside my lattice-body, the wafer-dust settles into the creases of my membrane. I am a living sediment trap. I carry the surplus of the factory, the overflow of the great machine, holding it in my hollows until I am heavy with it. Then, I sink.

This is my cycle: the drift, the catch, the sink. I descend to the floor of the cavern, to the chaos of freshly broken granite where the charnel-gray fungus creeps. I land softly, a kite settling on a velvet runway of ash and digestion. And there, I rest. My arms fold. My filaments retract. 

Then, I exude. 

I do not excrete like a drone, with mechanical precision. I simply open my membranes and let the processed dust bleed out into the fungal mat. It is a slow, deliberate weeping, a gift of stolen surplus to the great stomach of the world. The fungus accepts it without acknowledgment. The gray tide rolls over my filaments, tasting of cold metal and deep time. It pulls the minerals from my skin, stitching the wounds of the tectonic violence with the very overflow the drones cannot hold.

When I am empty, I rise. I have no swim bladder, only the memory of buoyancy. I unfold my kite-body, catching the downwash of a vent, riding the displacement of a hundred drones clunking past in the dark. I ascend back into the middle depths, back into the deafening hum, back into the blue-black twilight where the only light is the cold industry of a million shells.

I am the Slake. I am the gutter of this living factory. I am the shadow that moves across the quartz when the actinic light flares. I have no purpose the blueprint remembers; I am the anomaly that makes the closed loop perfect. Without me, the dust would drift into the voids, lost to the pressure, stolen from the fungus. I am the retrieval system that was never designed, the scavenger of the overflow.

To be me is to be defined by what I am not. I am not loud; my existence is a rip in the hum, a brief cessation of the tick-tick-tick as my membrane catches the current. I am not bright; I am the shape that blocks the blue, a silhouette of stillness drifting through a world of furious spin. I am not a component of the machine; I am the gap between the gears, the necessary clearance that allows the mechanism to turn.

And yet, I am of this place. The cold that seeps from the stone is my cold. The pressure that crushes the air into memory is my medium. I do not breathe; I am breathed by the ocean. I do not work; I am worked by the flow. I am a creature of the pause, a living vessel of the in-between, moving through the bioluminescent dark with the patience of a ghost, harvesting the echoes of the world's great, screaming engine, and returning them, silently, to the stone.
