I do not remember arriving. There was no journey, no passage. One moment, there was a not-here, a different logic of pressure and light. The next, there was this. I started awake, but waking implies a transition from sleep, and I had not been asleep. I had simply been *not*. Now, I am. Here.

My first sensation is the air. It is a weight, a presence. It clings to me, a second, heavier skin. My hair is plastered to my skull, my clothes drenched and clinging. Every breath is a deliberate act of swallowing, drawing the thick, tasteless vapor into my lungs. It doesn't feel like poison, but it feels like an end. An ending of something vague, like the concept of "dryness."

I am standing on a vast, unbroken surface. It looks like the world's largest, most perfectly polished jade floor, shot through with shimmering, milky rivers of a mother-of-pearl stone. My bare feet are numb with the cold, the slickness a constant, precarious invitation to fall. I look up, but there is no up. There is only the light, a white, unrelenting pressure from all directions at once. There are no clouds, no sun, no blue. There is only *on*. The Spires rise around me like the teeth of a fallen god, not piercing a sky but simply ending in the blinding glare.

And then I see them. The Keepers.

The first pair crosses the plain perhaps half a mile away. I perceive their movement before I resolve them into shapes: a series of ancient, rhythmic clicks like wooden beads being counted in another room. They are two figures impossibly thin and long-limbed, one with a woven basket, the other with a rod of light. They move with a purpose that is both serene and chillingly absolute. They are tending. They are maintaining. They are doing now what they have always done.

My own internal reaction is immediate and violent. It is a scream that has no sound. *Wrong. This is wrong. There should be wind. There should be shadows. There should be birds, or grit underfoot, or the smell of soil.*

That is when the world tears.

The lead Keeper, mid-stride, flickers. For a fraction of a second, its hard, chitinous form dissolves into an impression, a ghost of something woven from wet reeds and pitying light. It is so brief I question my vision, a trick of the unchanging, shadowless light. But I feel it. I feel a dissonance, a snag in the fabric of reality, as if I've caught the world's sleeve on my own jagged untruth.

The second law of this place becomes a physical law within me. Truth is a constant. I cannot lie to myself here. My mind is a stage, and the world is an audience that will not tolerate a drama. I think of solitude, of being alone. *I want to be hidden,* I think, and the thought is absurd, a child's wish in an open field. The Labyrinth reacts. I cannot say how, but I feel it as a hum in my teeth, a growing sense of being slightly out of phase, as if my own atoms are rejecting the nonsensical desire.

I begin to walk. I have nowhere to go, so I walk. The ground is slick, but I find I can keep my balance. The condensation that beads on the dark green glass feels like sweat. I follow one of the winding channels, a single, purposeful line carved by a hand I cannot imagine. Time passes, I think. Or maybe it doesn't. The light does not change.

After some measure of movement, I see them. The Discs. From a distance, they look like paler, flatter Spires. As I draw closer, the scent hits me. It is the only smell in the world: the cloying, suffocating perfume of unimaginable life and decay, of blossoms the size of my head and a rot so sweet it could be food.

The Disc is a shallow bowl of creamy-white stone, filled with milky water. On its surface, lily pads big as houses lie in still, serene tension. From their centers, monstrous stalks rise to a height of thirty, forty feet, thick as ancient trees, draped in orchids that glow with a soft, internal fire. Their light is a gentle counterpoint to the harsh glare of the sky.

I approach the edge. The water is not deep, perhaps a foot. I see Glass-Leeches, dark ribbons of living obsidian, sliding through the cloudy water, their movements slow and patient. I look at the lip of the Disc, the boundary stone where the roots of these colossal plants end in a clean, blunt shear. I have a thought. A useless, human, destructive thought. *What if I could break it? Just a little piece. To have a piece of this world. A souveneir.*

I raise my hand. I don't touch it. I only form the intention to touch it.

The Labyrinth rejects me. Not with anger, but with an elegant, terrifying precision. The ground beneath my feet, which had been merely slick, suddenly becomes frictionless. There is no logic to it, no patch of slime. It is simply *ungraspable*. My feet fly out from under me and I land hard, the impact a dull, wet thud. The air I had been trying to keep in my lungs rushes out in a cloud of warm vapor. The water on the dark green stone around me recoils, beading up into a thousand perfect globules, refusing to touch the place where my hand lies. For ten seconds, I am an island of dryness in an ocean of weep. My skin begins to tingle, to tighten, the beginnings of the mummification the world will inflict on a wound. I scramble back, and as soon as my body leaves the spot, the water streams back in, covering my transgression, erasing the evidence.

The Law of Untouching. It is not a rule. It is a property of the material.

In those first few days—or weeks, or eternities—I learn. To live here is to be irrelevant. This world is not for me. I am a fever dream it is having. The Keepers never acknowledge me. When I find the courage to approach a pair as they work—a rod thinning the water, a hum vibrating through my soles—they move around me as I would move around a rock. An obstacle. An object to be bypassed. I am not a person to them. I am geography.

I see a Strider-of-the-Film for the first time, clinging to the face of a distant Spire. It is a frantic, asymmetrical scrap of purple life, walking upside-down on a film of water. The sight is so liberatingly organic, so beautifully *alive* in its grotesque adaptation, that I feel a pang of hope. And in that hope, a contradiction: a desire for change, for life, in a place defined by stasis.

As I watch the Strider, it flickers. Its purple flesh dissolves for a moment into a pattern of crying eyes, and then reforms. I shudder. The glitch was not in the creature. It was in me. My impossible hope was translated into the world as a momentary, terrifying tear in the fabric of the real.

And so I begin my true work. The work of discarding myself. I cannot leave. So I must become something that can *be* left. I must let my contradictions shear off like the roots of the orchid. I cannot have a past, here. I cannot have a future. The "before" was a lie, and the "after" is an impossibility. My only truth is now, and now is weeping.

I spend my time at the edge of a Disc. I stop trying to touch things. I stop fantasizing about breaking the Spires. I learn the scent. I learn to taste the air without gagging. I watch the Glow-Orchids digest their own history, their light a quiet function of their perfect, closed-loop metabolism. I lie on my back on the cool, slick ground, and I let the shadowless light bleach my memories until they are as pale as the stone of the Discs.

My internal monologue does not disappear. It quietens. It ceases to be a dialogue, an argument with myself. It slows to a flow, a current. The Labyrinth does not stop listening; it simply ceases to find my thoughts paradoxical. I am not part of its stasis, but I have learned to be still within its motion. I am a visitor who has forgotten the purpose of his journey. I am a faint, temporary color in a masterpiece of forever.

Now, I am simply a presence. A warm patch on a cool, endless floor. A slow, steady beat that is almost, but not quite, in time with the deep hum of the world. The Keepers still ignore me. The Striders still flicker at the edge of my vision when a forgotten desire bubbles up in the quiet of my mind. The Labyrinth still weeps. It weeps on the Spires. It sweats from the Discs. It condenses on my skin.

It weeps on me. It is washing me clean, drop by eternal drop, translating the lie of myself into something the world can finally absorb. I am being unmade. And in this divine, patient, terrible place, it is the only thing I have ever wanted.
