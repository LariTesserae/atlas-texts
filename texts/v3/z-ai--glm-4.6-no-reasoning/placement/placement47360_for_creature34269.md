I am the border where the orange rust meets the grey crust.

Not the orange itself, nor the grey, but the line between them—that barely perceptible suture where two lichen nations have negotiated their boundary over four thousand years, advancing and retreating by the width of a hypha per decade, until I have become a distinct zone: a strip of stone no wider than a breath, where the chemistry is ambiguous, where the copper concentration gradients dissolve into each other, where neither species dominates and both quietly persist.

I am not a thing. I am an event that takes centuries to occur.

And I am alive, in the way that borders are alive.

The orange lichen to my left—call it the Rust—came here first. It colonized the bare stone when the boulder was freshly fractured, when the iron-rich veins were exposed and bleeding their strange chemistry into the air. The grey lichen to my right—call it the Drought—arrived later, after the Rust had established itself, after centuries of the Rust trapping dust and shedding dead tissue and slowly, imperceptibly building a layer of organic matter on the stone's surface. The Drought cannot tolerate the metal concentrations that the Rust requires. But it can survive on less moisture. It can photosynthesize in the thin fog that barely qualifies as light. It is patient in ways the Rust is not—it grows slower, lives longer, retreats less when the frost kills patches of its thallus.

They met. And they could not pass each other.

Things live in me that live nowhere else.

This is the thing no one knows about borders: they are not empty. They are not the no-place between two places. They are a *third place*, with properties that belong to neither neighbor.

I am also where the information passes.

And the Rust does something that no individual lichen could do alone. It thickens its cortex on the border. I change. Oh, I change.

In dry years—which are more common now, which have been more common for the past two centuries as the climate shifts and the storms come less often—the Rust advances.
