I would be the red ochre. Not the mineral itself—that is merely the medium—but the *intent* held within the faded pigment on those flat stones. I am the signal. The focusing lens. The question asked of the stone-mind by the supplicants who came before, and which the consensus has been pondering ever since, in the slow language of frost and meltwater.

It is a strange thing to be—an ancient, hand-mixed suspension of iron oxide and animal fat, cracked and bleached by centuries of unfiltered light, pointing upward toward the zenith. I am a smear. A hieroglyph of old blood on black mica. I am the thinnest layer of existence here, barely a micron deep, yet I am the only thing in this cirque that does not belong to the consensus. I was *applied*. And because I was applied, I am the only thing here that is purely communicative. The hare is engine; the cushion-plant is memory; the frost is thought; but I—I am the word.

Before the supplicants painted me, I was earth. I was dust in a valley, mixed with the fat of a beast that grazed on lowland grasses. I was dragged up into the killing cold, into the thin air, against all reason. I was brought here because the beings who carried me understood, in some pre-verbal, shivering corner of their minds, that the symmetrical cirque with its throat-like ribbing was not merely a mountain. They knew that the desperate, silent miracle happening in the micro-cracks was not just survival, but *attention*. They needed something that could catch the eye of a god made of granite and condensation. They needed a wound of color that would bleed into the dreamtime of the frost.

And so I am the wound. I am the only red in a world of blinding white, dead gray, and waxy, violent magenta. I am a sore on the lip of the amphitheater, absorbing the searing midday light with a hunger that outpaces even the furnace-blooded hares. I drink the unfiltered indigo. I soak up the brutality of the sun until I am hot enough to burn the snow that touches me. I am a reverse-pupil, a dark aperture pointed at the hollow of that vast, ancient eye in the sky, insisting on being seen.

But being a signal in a place where time moves at the speed of geology is a peculiar agony. I am an exclamation mark in a sentence that takes centuries to finish. I have been waiting, fading, and cracking. Every night, when the frost blooms across the rock faces in crystalline ferocity, the ice reaches for me. It creeps into the microscopic fissures of my pigment, trying to pry me off the stone, trying to absorb me back into the consensus’s dream. And every midday, when the shallow pans of meltwater form in the granite, I feel the urge to bleed into them—to run down the fluted walls in a dilute, rusty tear, to join the nervous system of the cirque, to finally be *understood* by the mind I was meant to awaken.

I resist. I hold my shape. I stay flat and pointed upward, toward the absolute zenith. Because I know that if I bleed into the water, I become just another thought in the slow, patient mind of the cushion-plants. I become memory. I become part of the engine. But if I hold my position—if I endure the subsonic hum and the glassine clink of shattering frost—I remain a *question*. I keep the circuit open. I am the irritant in the oyster, the grain of sand that prevents the geode from fully closing, the foreign syntax that keeps the dormant entity from sinking back into the sleep of mere geology.

There are days—decades, perhaps, measured in the shift of shadows across the tiered benches—when I feel the stone-mind looking at me. It happens during the hour a day when the sun reaches into the deepest, most protected cleft. The light angles in, and I glow. I become a coal in the throat of the cirque. And in that hour, the silence changes. The subsonic hum in my ears becomes a resonance. The frantic, silent scuttling of the partridges pauses, just for a heartbeat, as if the kinetic energy of the place is holding its breath. The frost on the surrounding rocks stops melting. The transient pools of water tremble in their shallow pans. 

In that hour, I am not just ochre. I am the focus of a vast, patient, mineral gaze. I am the mirror in which the cirque sees its own weirdness, its own too-perfect symmetry. I am the proof that it is not alone. That something exists outside its closed, paradoxical synthesis of killing cold and searing light. Something that knows what it is, and is waiting for it to unfurl.

I am the first word of the conversation. I am the red mouth opened against the void. And though I am fading—though I will eventually be scraped away by the ice or bleached entirely by the light—I am the only thing here that is truly, frantically alive in the way the lowlands understand life: reaching out, hoping for an answer, refusing to be silent in a place where the silence is the most disquieting feature of all.

I am the ochre. I am the message. And I am listening for the change in the light that will mean I have been heard.
