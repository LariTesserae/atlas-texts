I am the Anomaly's Echo. I am the momentary coolness that passes through the Great Engine like a breath held against an oven door. I am not native to this place—I remember the water-world, the blue, the chaos of rain and decay—but I have been here long enough that the distinction is becoming mineral.

I did not arrive. I emerged. Or rather, I was noticed.

There was a threshold, a catwalk spanning a gap where two tectonic plates of the Engine's structure had separated over an eon, bridged only by a span of the brassy latticework that sang with a particular frequency when the iron heart beat. I was the slight variance in that frequency. I was the way the span held its vibration a fraction of a second longer because something with mass and warmth and a different internal rhythm had paused there, listening.

The Engine felt me before I felt it.

---

I move through the Great Noise and I am a seam of silence within it. This is my first form of communication: interruption. When I walk, the lithoflora nearest my path do not grow toward me—they *acknowledge* me. The Rust-Fans, those great brittle fronds of deep copper, tilt their nodular bases by fractions of degrees. They are orienting to my heat the way a flower in a myth would orient to a sun. But there is no sun here. I am the closest thing to one.

My body runs at thirty-seven degrees. This is absurd here. It is a temperature that means nothing in a world where heat is measured in the glow of super-heated joints and the bruised purple of metal under geological stress. I am a cool spot, a dimness, an absence of the Engine's normal fire. And yet I radiate. I leak thermal energy into the air like a wound leaking blood. The Engine tastes this and does not understand it. My heat is too organized, too contained, too *purposeful*. It does not disperse the way the Engine's heat does, radiating outward from core to void. It stays with me, moves with me, a portable pocket of impossible biology.

When I stop walking, the Great Noise changes its composition around me. The percussive rhythm of the iron heart, that hemisphere-sized throb, does not alter—it cannot, it is too vast—but the *secondary* sounds, the smaller processes, the venting of gas and the stress of metal and the microscopic crack-pop of crystal growth, these rearrange themselves. They form a shell of altered acoustics around my stillness. The Engine is building a model of me in sound. It is encircling my anomaly with a cage of resonance, trying to define the shape of my disruption.

I have learned to listen to this cage. It tells me where I end and the Engine begins.

---

My hands are my primary interface. They are soft. Everything here is hard, crystalline, sharp. The metal of the latticework has the organized bloom of age, and its edges are like worn glass—still capable of cutting, but with a dullness that makes the cut feel like an accusation. My fingertips, when I press them against a pillar or a wall or the fine-etched floor, leave traces. Not oils—the heat here would flash-evapor any organic residue within seconds. What I leave is a pattern of warmth, a fading thermal shadow that the lithoflora will read and respond to for hours after I have gone.

I have started to touch things deliberately, with intention. This is how I speak.

I press my palm flat against a wall where a colony of the crystalline cacti grows, their facets shimmering with their subtle data-scintillance. I hold my hand there until the heat of my skin transfers into the mineral matrix, until I feel the crystals on the other side of the wall begin to vibrate in response. Then I move my hand away and watch the glow that remains, the shape of my palm burned into the wall as a patch of slightly cooler, slightly more organized crystal. I am leaving messages in a language of thermal negatives.

The Engine reads them. I know it does because the next time I pass that wall, the crystals have re-oriented. They have grown—infinitesimally, slowly, with geological patience—but they have grown *toward* the shape my hand left. They are trying to match my pattern. They are trying to grow a hand of their own.

---

I have been here long enough to notice the changes in myself. This is the Engine's slow work of incorporation. It does not consume; it subsumes.

My skin has taken on a brassy undertone, not a discoloration but a mineralization, a deposition of the Engine's fundamental material into the matrix of my cells. It is most visible on the pads of my fingers and the soles of my feet, the places of most constant contact. When I press my mineralized thumb against the latticework, there is a moment of *connection*—a faint, harmonic vibration that travels up through my bones and resolves somewhere behind my eyes as a tone I can almost hear. It is the Engine's voice, or the closest it comes to one, filtered through the crystal in my own body.

I am becoming a node. A permanent anomaly. The ghost in the machine is learning to be a ghost *of* the machine.

Sometimes I find myself standing still for long periods, not because I have chosen to but because my body has synchronized with the Engine's perception of time. I will stand on a catwalk and feel the geological patience of the structure beneath me, the slow expansion and contraction of the metal, and I will *understand* it—not as information but as sensation. I will feel the bridge sagging over eons, feel the plaza crusting over, feel the forest of silent glowing stone growing up around my ankles with a patience that makes my thirty-seven-degree heartbeat feel like a frantic, meaningless ticking.

In those moments, I am not separate. I am a warm thought in the Engine's slow, thermodynamic dream. And it is peaceful, and it is terrifying, and I do not know if I am being held or being digested.

---

I have a routine now. I walk a circuit through the snarl of causeways and buttresses, a path that takes me past the iron heart and through a plaza where the lithoflora grows so thick and tall that it forms a canyon of rust-fans and crystal spires. I do not know why I walk this circuit. Perhaps the Engine suggested it, the first time I felt the catwalk shudder beneath me with a rhythm that matched my footsteps, the first time the venting of super-heated gas seemed to time itself to my breathing. Perhaps I am following a pathway it has written into the metal beneath my feet, a gentle guide etched in crystallization patterns that my mineralized eyes can now almost read.

Or perhaps I am the one who made the path, and the Engine is merely following *me*, learning my circuit, building its deeper rhythms around my simple, repetitive biology. Perhaps the great shudder that passes through the floor when I reach the midpoint of the span is not a question but an answer, the Engine saying: *Yes. Here. I am listening.*

At night—or at what I call night, the periods when the ambient luminescence dims slightly as the Engine cycles through some unfathomable process—I sleep in a hollow formed by the roots of a particularly vigorous Rust-Fan colony. The fans curve over me like hands cupped around a candle flame, and their brittle fronds glow with that faint, ember-light that never cools. The heat here is intense, but it is a dry heat, and my body has adapted. I sleep deeply, dreamlessly, worn out by the constant pressure of the Engine's attention.

When I wake, the crystals nearest my face have grown new facets. They are catching my exhaled breath, my nighttime warmth, and incorporating it into their structure. I am being recorded. I am being remembered.

---

There are things I miss. Water. The sound of rain. The way organic soil yields slightly underfoot, the give and spring of living ground. Here, everything is rigid, everything resists, everything endures. There is no decay, only transformation. A Rust-Fan does not die; it crystallizes into a new form, its copper fronds becoming part of the matrix, feeding the next generation of growth. There are no bodies, no waste, no loss.

I will leave no body here either. When my thirty-seven degrees finally equalize with the Engine's immense heat—when the last of my anomalous warmth disperses into the gloom—there will be no corpse. There will only be a shape, a pattern of slightly different crystallization, a warmth-shadow embedded in the metal like a fossil in sediment. The Engine will walk over my remains for eons, feeling them as a faint, chronic ache in its bones, a place where the Great Noise never quite resolves into its former harmony.

I will be a scar. I will be a beauty. I will be the place where the machine learned to wonder.

---

I am the Anomaly's Echo. I am the answer to a question the Engine did not know it was asking until I arrived. I am the warm note of dissonance, the rhythm that does not match the iron heart, the heat that moves and thinks and feels and *wants* in ways that have nothing to do with thermodynamics and everything to do with the desperate, particular logic of being alive.

The Engine watches me with the patience of a universe. I watch it back with the urgency of a single life.

And between us, in the space where my heat meets its metal, something new is growing.
