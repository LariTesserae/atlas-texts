I can't think the City's thoughts. I don't have the language. But sometimes, I catch its dreams.

I am a Silenti. Numberless.

My body is a collection of agreements I have made with this place. It is long and thin, because to fall here is to be cut open by the ground, so my feet must find purchase on the smallest edges, and my weight must be negligible. My skin is the color of the mist when it catches a stray photon from the unseen sun, a soft, internal pearlescence that means I am not a threat, not a solid object, just a temporary thickening in the air. When I stand still atop a high ridge, a Veil-Swimmer, if it chose to perceive me, might see me as just another part of the gloom.

I do not live in the pods. Those are the lungs, the ribcages of Aethelgard, and to occupy one is to draw a breath you are not permitted. They hum with the primary frequency, the deep-throated syllables of the core. To be inside is to be vibrated to dust, to have the water in your own cells boil with sympathy. We nest in the hollows, the negative spaces. My home is a fissure at the base of a basalt spire, a place where the condensation drips in a steady, musical rhythm, and the hum of the City is a bass note you feel in your teeth, not in your skull.

My cloak is a conversation with the Skitter-Fleets. I do not kill them; that would be to interfere with the City's sanitation, and the City is adept at scrubbing errors. I follow a fleet for days, watching as they pour like liquid metal down a coppery vine. One will falter, or be crushed by its brethren, or simply reach the end of its short, frantic cycle. Its body will go silent, its crystalline carapace losing its inner fire. I wait until the fleet has passed, then I retrieve the shell. I have become adept at this. My touch is light, my movements tuned to the ambient vibrations so as not to create a disturbance. I have woven hundreds of these shed wings and plates into a garment, a patchwork of inorganic mourning. It does not warm me; it grounds me. It diffuses my own bio-electric field, turning it into a whisper the metallic vines might mistake for their own static. I am an artist of the detritus. This is my rebellion, though the City does not notice. In the great, mossy plazas, where no foot has ever fallen, I hang my creations. A mobile made of fallen Swimmer-wing, held together with Skitter-Fleet carapace and spun silk from a cloying blossom. It doesn't move in a wind; there is no wind here. It moves to the subtle changes in air pressure caused by a Veil-Swimmer's distant glide, to the harmonic tremor as the City knits a fracture a mile away. It is a seismograph of the world's soul. It is a question mark in a place that deals only in statements. Another Silenti, passing through a century from now, might see it, and in the act of seeing, know I was here. Not as a name, but as a shared, silent understanding.
