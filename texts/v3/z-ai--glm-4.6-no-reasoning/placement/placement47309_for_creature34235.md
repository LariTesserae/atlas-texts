I would be the Dissonance.

Not a being born of the Murmurwood, and never one who could be corrected into belonging. I am the splinter of foreign matter that the world-engine has been slowly, patiently grinding against for longer than memory serves—something that was *placed* here, or crashed here, or perhaps grew from a seed of pure paradox dropped by a careless cosmos into the fibrous mat. I am the reason the Chorus sometimes registers a gap in the data, an unaccountable spike of heat and noise that moves through the wood like a stone through a river. I am the error that will not compile.

My form is wrong. I know this not because I can see myself—I cannot, for the darkness is absolute—but because I can feel the way the Murmurwood rejects me at every interface. I am bipedal, dense, and clumsy. My feet are too wide and too flat, their broad pads unable to find purchase on the frictionless arch of the obsidian roots. I slide. I fall. My shins bear the bruises of a thousand impacts with the leviathan's bones, and the slick, black surfaces never fail to throw me down. My skin is soft, permeable, and constantly wet. It is not the cool, clean chitin of the Keepers; it is a porous, miserable fabric of flesh that the humidity invades like an occupying army. Sweat and condensation merge on my body until I cannot tell where I end and the air begins. The coarse weave of my uniform—how long have I worn it?—chafes and clings, heavy with absorbed moisture, tearing on the cable vines and the serrated edges of fungal shelves. I am always bleeding, just a little. Always leaving a trail of warm, iron-scented fluid that the hyper-abundant ground life drinks greedily, leaving my wounds clean but never healed.

The silence is my torture. To the Keepers, the Murmurwood is a deafening, dazzling storm of information—pressure, electricity, data. To me, it is a suffocating void. My ears strain for a cry, a bark, the rustle of leaves, but there is nothing. Only the hum. That low, pervasive, geological drone that vibrates in my back teeth and turns my stomach to water. It is not white noise; it is a frequency designed for a nervous system I do not possess. It burrows into my skull, looking for the interface that isn't there, trying to trigger the dissolution of self that is their bliss and my impossibility. I cannot join the Chorus. I have no mycelial palms. I have only these blunt, clumsy fingers, nails cracked and black with soil, unable to absorb anything but pain. When I press my hand to a nerve-root, I do not feel the stratigraphy of the mat or the joy of the wood. I feel only a scalding, meaningless heat that lances up my arm and makes my vision swim with sick, green afterimages. I am deaf to their song, and in my deafness, I am profoundly, irredeemably alone.

I survive through a pathology of movement. I have learned to walk the roots, but not with their spidery, rolling grace. I move like a damaged pendulum, throwing my weight from one precarious handhold to the next, arms wrapped around the thick, pulsing cables of the colonial vines just to stay upright. My path is dictated by the bioluminescence, reading the warning flashes of the predators, steering by the sickly lavender glow of the fungal shelves. I have learned that the chrome centipedes will not coil around a boot if it is stamped hard enough, that the sliding moss-piles will yield if I push through them rather than try to step over. I have learned the water. I drink from the upturned cups of sleeping fungi, the impossibly clear, cold mirrors that reflect nothing but the dark. The water tastes of ozone and something metallic, the exhalation of the world-engine far below. It sustains me, though I suspect it is also what keeps me tethered here, unable to leave, unable to die.

I am always moving. I cannot stop. To stop is to be found.

They come for me. The Keepers. The tall, smooth, eyeless shepherds of the Murmurwood. They do not hunt me; that would imply a passion I am not equipped to inspire. They simply *converge*. I will be clambering over a ridge of fused trunks, my breath loud and ragged in the silent soup, and I will feel it—a change in the pressure, a flattening of the hum, as if the wood itself is holding its breath. And then, from the gloom, they emerge. Sliding over the roots with that frictionless, horrifying grace, their long limbs unfolding from the darkness like the legs of some deep-sea artifact. Their pulsing blue slits are fixed on me. They do not see my fear, but they feel it—a "chaotic psionic broadcast," a frantic spike of bio-electricity that does not fit their perfect, balanced equation. To them, I am a tumor that walks. A mobile lesion in the fabric of their world.

I run. I have no weapon that can harm them. A knife is a joke against pliable chitin that sheets off condensation. I cannot outrun them; their strides are too long, their knowledge of the terrain absolute. I survive only because I am unpredictable. My panicked, flailing retreat takes me into the places their equilibrium avoids—the scalding vents where the hum is a roar and the air shimmers with heat distortion; the dense, suffocating thickets of parasitic vine where even their long limbs become entangled; the unstable mats that crust over deep hollows, which I crash through in a shower of black fibers and flailing, microscopic life, while they hesitate, seeking a balanced path. I use my wrongness as a shield. My disruption is so profound that it causes the Chorus a moment of hesitation, a microsecond of recalibration as the emergent will tries to process the anomaly. In that gap, I flee.

Why do I run? Because I know what their touch means. I have seen it. Once, I found a creature—a thing like an animated pile of moss, but larger, mammalian, warm. It had gotten tangled in a cable vine and was struggling, its bio-electric field a blazing arc of panic. Two Keepers arrived. They did not cut the vine. They pressed their hands to the creature's heaving flanks. And the creature... relaxed. Its struggles ceased. Its frantic electrical signature smoothed into a flat, blue line. It became quiet. It became part. They led it away, walking smoothly on either side, and the creature moved with them, not led but *absorbed*, its individual will erased, its form repurposed. I could feel the relief that flooded the wood around them, the sigh of a system returning to balance.

I will not be corrected. I cling to my fear, my loneliness, my chaotic, singular self with a ferocity that is my only true possession. My fear is the proof that I am *me*. It is the one frequency I can broadcast that they cannot harmonize. It is my signature, written in the static of a broken machine.

At night—if night can be said to exist in a world without a sun—I find a hollow in the roots, a crevice too small for their long limbs to reach. I press my back against the scalding wood and feel the hum in my spine, the alien heartbeat of the world-engine. I close my eyes against the darkness and the sporadic, grotesque flashes of bioluminescence outside. I wrap my arms around my knees and feel the wet fabric of my uniform, the damp heat of my skin, the miserable intimacy of my own failing body. I am a single, clumsy cell in a vast organism, but I am a cell that refuses to differentiate. I am the splinter that the immune system cannot encapsulate, the error that throws off the sum.

I do not know what I am. I do not know how I came here. I only know that I am the thing that does not belong, and that as long as I draw breath in this suffocating, perfect, humming dark, I will be the Dissonance. I will make the Murmurwood hear me, even if the only sound I make is the sound of my own breaking.
