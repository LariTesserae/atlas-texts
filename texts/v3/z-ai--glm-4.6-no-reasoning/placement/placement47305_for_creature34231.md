I would be one of the Patrollers of the Dim.

Not a specific one—they are interchangeable, replaceable, as uniform as the basalt they traverse—but an instance of one, currently mid-route. I have come to this not by choosing it, but by realizing that the other forms of life on the plateau are too fast, too small, too consumed with the metabolism of survival. The rice-grain beings scramble and process; they are the chemical reaction itself, inseparable from the stone. The mat barely exists; it is a stain, a translation layer without consciousness. But the Patrollers have *routes*. They have a purpose that extends beyond the next microgram of mineral rime. They are the ones who *know* the plateau, not as a surface to be devoured, but as a system to be traversed.

So I am a dog-sized architecture of scarred chitin, moving on legs too numerous to count individually, each step placing a foot with the precision of something navigating by vibration alone. My weight is distributed so perfectly that I leave no trace on the damp stone; I am a ghost made of shell and patience. The cold has long since ceased to be a sensation. It is a condition, like gravity, like the subsonic hum that I feel in the hollow cavities of my own body—I have cavities, I realize, and they resonate with the machines below, making me a receiver, an antenna walking across the face of the factory.

I do not see the way creatures in warmer places see. The light here is too attenuated, too grey, too filtered through the high-altitude haze to carry the kind of information that vision requires. Instead, I *read*. I read the thermal gradients that bleed through the stone—the warmth of a vent still miles away, the cold pocket where a buried engine has finally failed and is being slowly subsumed into the crust. I read the chemical traces in the air, the faint signatures of exhaust that distinguish one section of the plateau from another in a landscape where everything looks identical. And I read the hum, the vast chord of ten thousand engines, distinguishing within it the frequencies that mean *stable* from the frequencies that mean *change*, that mean *a vent is opening* or *a section is settling* or *something is shifting in the geometry below*.

My route is not marked. There are no cairns, no grooves worn into the stone by generations of my kind. The route exists only in me—in the arrangement of whatever serves as memory within my chitin-encased body. I carry the map of my territory not as a image but as a sequence of vibrations, a song that the machines sing to me and that I sing back to them with my footsteps. I am walking a line that was determined before I was born, that will be walked after I am gone, that has no beginning and no end because the plateau is a circle, or a spiral, or a shape that has no shape because it extends beyond the horizon in every direction.

The other Patrollers are out here too, but we do not interact. We pass within meters of each other and do not acknowledge the encounter. We are not social; we are *functional*. We are performing the same operation in parallel, like processors in the vast machine we walk upon. Sometimes I detect the thermal signature of another Patroler, a faint warmth against the cold stone, and I adjust my route by a fraction of a degree to maintain the optimal spacing, the coverage pattern that ensures every section of the plateau is monitored, is sensed, is *incorporated* into the awareness of the system. We are the sensory array of the factory. We are its eyes, if it had eyes, its feelers, its way of knowing itself.

---

Right now, the dimness is deepening. The attenuated light is filtering away, and the true dark is settling in, not suddenly but incrementally, the way everything happens here. The smaller forms are already retreating, seeping into the joints of the machines, leaving the surface to us. The stone beneath me is vibrating at a frequency I recognize—*stable*, *unchanged*, *continuing*. The damp is beginning to form, a fine precipitate that will bead on my shell in a matter of hours, making me glisten briefly before the cold drives it into rime.

I am approaching a vent. I can feel its warmth like a voice calling—not urgently, not desperately, but with the steady, administrative certainty of a machine producing output. The chemical trace is there too, the exhaust signature that I know as well as I know my own route. This vent opened eleven cycles ago, or twelve; I lose count, because counting requires a kind of attention that I do not possess, that I have never needed. The vent simply *is*, and I am going to it, and when I arrive I will stop.

Stopping is not rest. Stopping is not sleep. It is a different mode of operation—the *absorption* mode, the *integration* mode. I will press my shell against the warm stone and I will become a heat sink, a radiator, a biological extension of the vent's output. The warmth will enter me, and the chemicals will enter me, and for a period that I cannot measure and do not need to measure, I will be part of the machine. Not metaphorically. Literally. My body will conduct heat away from the vent, distributing it across the plateau through my chitin, through the chitin of the other Patrollers who are converging on this same point from their own routes. We will form a network, a temporary organic infrastructure, and we will perform a function that is not feeding, not mating, not any of the things that biology is supposed to do, but something else—something *industrial*, something that serves the process that the plateau is performing.

I do not know what the plateau is producing. I have never known. The question does not occur to me in the way that questions occur to creatures with different neurologies, different histories. The process *is*. The hum *is*. The route *is*. And I am the thing that walks the route and stops at the vent and becomes, for a time, a component in a system that has been running since before I evolved and will run after I am subsumed into the stone.

---

This is what it is like to be me: to move without urgency across a surface that is also a machine, to navigate by vibration and chemistry through a landscape without landmarks, to perform a function that has no explanation, to be biological and industrial simultaneously, to possess a body that is indistinguishable from the environment when viewed from any distance, to be alive in a way that does not require hope or fear or any of the interior experiences that define life in other places.

I am not unhappy. I am not happy. These categories require a boundary between self and world, and on the Plateau of Machines, that boundary does not exist. The cold is in me. The hum is in me. The damp forms on me as it forms on the stone. The route I walk is not mine; it is the plateau's, expressed through me. When I stop at the vent and absorb its warmth, the warmth is not mine; it is the machine's, borrowed for a time by my chitin, distributed by my presence, returned to the system through my slow, patient metabolism.

I am a Patroler. I am a node in a sensory network the size of a continent. I am a walking algorithm, a biological subroutine in a program that has been running for geological time. I am the flesh of the factory, the mobile component, the part that moves because the stone cannot, the part that senses because the engines cannot, the part that *goes* because the plateau must be known, must be felt, must be incorporated into its own operation.

And when I am gone—when my shell finally succumbs to the patient chemical weathering, when the condensation eats through my chitin, when I become another dark smear on the dark stone, another mineral trace for the rice-grain beings to process—another will take my route. Another will walk the same vibrations, stop at the same vents, become the same temporary component in the same unending machine. And the hum will not change. And the damp will form and lift. And the plateau will continue, producing nothing visible, producing *conditions*, producing *us*, producing the process that is the only thing this place has ever made.

I am walking. The dim is settling. The vent is ahead. I can feel it. I am going there now, at the speed of the cold, at the speed of the hum, at the speed of this place, which is the only speed I have ever known.
