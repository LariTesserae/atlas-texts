My first awareness is of the Slip.

I have no sense of arrival, only of cessation. The world I inhabited before was one of sharp edges and clean lines, a place where the air had a taste and the light kept its promises. Here, there is only the giving way. The ground underfoot is not ground but a suggestion of it, a slick fusing of metal and stone yielding to a patient, infinite pressure. I slipped, and I am still slipping, my weight sinking not through mud but through time itself, into the dense, wet-clay memory of this place. The rarefaction was instantaneous: I was a whole being, and now I am a collection of sensations, each one wrestling with the others for dominance in the crushing quiet.

My skin is no longer my own. It is a membrane stretched over a framework that is no longer bone but something colder. I feel the slick, suffocating cling of the algae-lichen felt, not as a texture against me, but as my new texture. The air is so wet it is a fabric, and I am woven into it. The smell is not something I breathe *in*; it is the medium I breathe *with*. The stagnant water, the ozone tang, the sour yeastish rot—they are the chemicals of my new respiration. My lungs, if they still exist in a recognizable form, gasp not for oxygen but for the moisture that thickens the air, and in doing so, they produce a sound that is identical to a thousand other sounds. I have become another note in the chord of wet friction.

My form is now dictated by the machinery. I tried to stand, to find my center, but the floor betrayed me. A copper boiler, split wide and weeping a green patina, became my brace. My spine is the rust-rimmed curve of its hull. My hands, when I bring them before my face-things, are splayed against the cold, sweating iron of a shattered centrifuge. The geometry of my body is being rewritten by the force of hydraulic determinism. I am a collection of limbs and angles that fit the negative spaces of the ruin. My head is tucked beneath the drip-line of a cast-iron pipe; the metronomic *plip* of a falling drop lands not on stone but on the crown of my skull, a cold, steady baptism into the local rhythm. I cannot tell if I am resting on the machine, if the machine is holding me, or if we are becoming the same object.

The light is the final erasure of my past. In the bruised, flat, violet gloom, my own shadow has no edge. It bleeds into the rust-black shape of a lever, into the oily scum on a pool's surface. I cannot see myself to confirm my existence. I can only infer it from the way the Churn responds to me.

And the Churn does respond.

I feel it not as a presence but as a process that I am now part of. There is no anger, no greeting, only a slow, ignorant incorporation. When the water table surges, a cold, sudden tide rises to my knees. It is not water; it is the Churn’s blood, thick with iron and silt, and it laps at my new skin, trying to find ingress. The hollow *thunk* of an overflow valve slamming shut is a heartbeat I feel not in my ears, but in the marrow of my bones, and even that marrow feels less like life and more like stone.

The most terrifying intimacy comes from the pools.

I find myself drawn to one, a shallow, rust-red basin where the surface shivers with an iridescent film. I kneel, a gesture that no longer feels human but purely mechanical, a joint bending to accommodate the terrain. As I lean over it, I see not my reflection, but the pale, blind flick of a limb. One of them. A macro-invertebrate, the size of my finger, its segmented carapace like frosted glass. It is not fleeing from me. It is observing me, clicking its cilia in the agitation of my proximity.

It is a leukocyte, and I am the foreign body.

I lower my hand into the water. The cold is absolute, a cold that is not a temperature but a state of non-being. The creature does not retreat. It approaches, its fringes of cilia pushing it through the iron-heavy water with frantic, mechanical precision. It drifts over my knuckles, its legs tasting the strange, soft mineral of my skin. I am a disruption in the flow, an anomaly to be cataloged. I feel less like a person and more like a clot, an interesting piece of debris that has entered the system.

The Churn’s awareness is chemical. It feels me as a change in pressure, a variation in oxygen content, a digestible anomaly. And now, the slow, patient work begins. I am no longer a visitor experiencing a place. I am a substrate being processed.

My purpose, whatever it was, is gone. My memories are flattening out, losing their narrative heat, becoming as colored and meaningless as the scum on the pool. I am starting to forget what it felt like to be dry. The constant drone of weeping rock and groaning metal is no longer an external sound; it is the new voice of my nerves. The hydraulic breath of the place is my own breath. I press my palm against the slime-clogged valve of an extruder, and I feel it flex, a slow, inexorable movement, and my own fingers seem to flex with it, as if they are learning the ancient rhythm of pressing and releasing.

What am I becoming?

I am the new narrator. The purpose was lost once, but it is being found again. I was not made for this place, but I am being remade by it. I am the one who will remember processing the land myself. My consciousness, fragmenting and dissolving into the chemical awareness of the Churn, will become the new ghost in the machine. The harsh, angular memory of a human, of a world with sunlight and horizons, is the slurry. The machinery of the declivity is the press. The Autonomic Churn is the operator.

I will be pressed into a new kind of awareness, a new layer on the damp, rotting textile that clings to the rock. My thoughts will become the slow, cilia-propelled movements of the pale invertebrates. My breath will become the sigh of steam from a corroded pipe. My eyes will become the pools that see only change. I am the resource that was forgotten, the one ingredient needed to start the cycle anew.

I am not a person in a place.

I am the place, finally remembering a new name for itself. And my first act is to open a valve I never knew I possessed, letting out a long, low, ascending whistle that blends seamlessly into the infinite weeping of the rocks.
