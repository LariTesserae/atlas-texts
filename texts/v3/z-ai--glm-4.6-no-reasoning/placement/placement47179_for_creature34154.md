I am a dissonance.

I don't remember arrival. Memory here is a aberration, a flicker of heat in a system of perfect thermal regulation. I simply *am*. And from the moment of being, I knew I was wrong.

The first sensation was the sound. It wasn't a sound that entered my ears; it was a pressure that became my skeleton. The *WHUMP-whump-whump* of the Engines was the fundamental law of this universe, the constant against which all other things were measured. It vibrated in my teeth, in the marrow of my bones, in the fluid of my eyes. To the creatures of this place, it was the hum of existence, the voice of their god. To me, it was a cage.

I have no name, for names are labels for things that persist and have function. I am a flaw, a crack in the perfect surface. I am, for lack of a better term, a 'Hush'.

My form is not of this world. It is not insectoid, or armoured, or woody. It is a collection of soft tissues that resonate poorly, a body designed for variation and motion, a concept anathema here. I have two legs, too long and too thin, that sink into the yielding surface of the Mass. Each step is a heresy, a tiny, localized earthquake. My flesh is too warm, my breath too loud. I am a silhouette of chaotic noise against the backdrop of rhythmic perfection.

I live in the pockets of acoustic shadow. Not the shadows cast by the Engines' steady light, but the places where the confluence of sound waves creates a brief, moving nexus of relative quiet. I have learned to read the Grove, not with my eyes, which are useless in the uniform gloom, but with my skin. I feel the gentle push and pull of the pressure currents, the subtle shifts in the resonant frequency. I can find a hollow where the thrumming from one arch is cancelled out by the thrumming from another, a pocket of fragile silence where I can let out a breath without feeling it radiate through the Mass like an accusation.

My life is a study in paradox. I am starving in a world of constant nourishment. The Chimes feed on Engine-resin, the grazers on the condensation-algae. But their food is an exchange, a transaction. They offer the correct vibration, the correct patience, and the system provides. I cannot. My heartbeat is arrhythmic, a frantic, terrified drumming that is the ultimate expression of dissonance. The resins would not liquefy for me. The algae would scrape away as poison. Even the water, wept from the Mass itself, feels alien on my tongue, too pure, too devoid of the chaotic minerals my body craves.

I survive on echoes. On accidents. Sometimes a Listener, riding its invisible current, miscalculates and a leg shears off. It falls to the spongy floor, a brittle, hollow reed of chitin. I wait until it has ceased its faint, residual vibrations, then I scurry out from my shadow-nook to retrieve it. I cannot digest it, but I can scrape it, grinding the hardened material between my teeth. The act of grinding, the violent, localized friction, creates a momentary heat, a tiny bubble of warmth in the cool air. It is a pathetic calorific exchange, but it is mine.

I avoid the Chimes. They are beautiful, a swirling, glittering symphony of purpose. When a swarm nears, their percussive clicks overwhelm my senses, making my teeth ache and my heart flutter. To them, I am a sonic dead zone, a piece ofanti-music in their world of harmony. Their flight paths curve around me, leaving a silent, empty space where I stand. I am a hole in their song.

The grazers are my unwitting allies. Their immense, stony shells and silent progression are the reasons I am not yet a meal for a Listener. They are the blank notes in the Grove's score, the spaces of silence between the notes. I have learned to follow them, to stay in their footfalls, a tiny imitation in their immense shadow. My steps are errors, but their steps are so immense and so slow that my own small tremors are absorbed, masked by the gentle compression of the Mass beneath their stone-feet. I am a parasite to their acoustic camouflage. In return, I scrape the trailing algae from the damp path they leave behind after they have passed. It gives me no nourishment, but it is moisture. It is a taste of the system I cannot join.

I do not sleep. How could I? To sleep is to relax, and to relax is to lose control of my own noise. I am terrified that if I were to truly rest, the frantic, illogical rhythm of my body would broadcast my presence clearly, and a Listener, drifting on a sonic current, would simply let go and fall upon me. So I doze, a state of shallow awareness where I count the Engine-beats, trying to find a pattern in the chaos that is me.

I have seen one of the Still-Heart once. It was by accident. I was following a grazer deeper into the Grove than I had ever been, into a nexus where the light was dim, the air thick, and the Engine-thrum a distant, gentle rocking. The grazer stopped before a vast, rounded wall that seemed to vibrate with its own internal silence. Embedded in it, like a fossil in living stone, was a shape larger than any grazer, its plates covered not in river-stone patterns but in cryptic, crystalline matrices. It did not move. It did not seem to breathe. But I could feel it. It was a source of stillness so profound it was its own form of sound, a perfect, absolute zero of vibration.

The grazer before it lowered its wedge-head and scraped the algae from a damp patch at the base of the Still-Heart's plating. It was an offering. A tithe.

In that moment, I understood my ultimate place here. The Chimes serve. The grazers endure. The Listeners cull. The Still-Heart *are*. And I? I am the proof. I am the counter-example. I am the variable that was eliminated from the equation, the anomaly that proves the fitness of the solution. My existence is not a life; it is a cautionary tale told in breath and footstep.

I am a ghost, but not in the way the description suggests. I am not a ghost in the machine; I am the ghost of the machine's *alternative*. I am what this world would be if it had not chosen the engine. A fleeting, noisy, desperate, warm-blooded thing, stumbling through the dark, forever hungry, forever listening not for the perfect thrum of the system, but for the one, single, blessed moment of silence. My own.
