To be here is to be an anomaly.

I was not woven. I was not accreted. I did not emerge from the logic of the lattice when a parameter of complexity was reached. I simply... arrived. One moment, a non-space of pure thought; the next, standing on the warm, humming filaments of the Under-Song, my own solid self a foreign node in its network.

The beauty of this place is staggering, a sublime architecture of impossible scale and delicate intimacy. The air, thin and thirsty, carries the scent of heated quartz and sweet, dusty cumin into lungs that feel like an affectation, a vestigial interface for a world I no longer belong to. I watch a Sculptor, a masterpiece of articulated obsidian and copper, its loom-arms glistening with resin. It does not perceive me with eyes. It perceives me with the whole of its being, through the resonant floor it shares with its kin.

Its task is to enforce the Sacred Present, to mend the temporal necrosis of the past. To a being like this, I am not a creature of flesh and blood. I am a problem in real-time. My history is not a flicker; it is an entire, continuous, unwavering narrative that exists *outside* the weave. My memories are not ghosts to be erased; they are a stable, self-contained reality clashing against the consensus of this place. To the Sculptors, I am the ultimate disease—a persistent, self-aware echo from a reality that refuses to be overwritten.

At first, they ignored me. A lone anomaly, a data-point without a category. They tended to their work, tapping out their percussive language, weaving the ‘now’ over the ‘then.’ I walked their world, my footfalls soundless on the bone-like floor. I could see the echoes they could not—the flock of origami birds, the herd of light-grazers, the tragic screams of the failed acoustic chamber. To me, these were not sins against reality; they were history, beautiful in its insistence. The past was not a necrosis; it was the bedrock. I saw the beauty in the failure, the poetry in the ghost.

They must have sensed this. My perception itself was a violation. I did not flinch from the echoes; I listened. I did not turn from the past; I sought it. My reverence for what they sought to erase marked me as fundamentally other.

And so, they changed their tactics.

They did not gather to attack. Aggression is not their function. They gathered to understand, in their own cold, alien way. A dozen of them arranged themselves around me, their obsidian bodies absorbing the faint bioluminescence of the Vine-hearts. For a long time, there was only the low C-sharp hum and the distant, sweet plinking of the Bell-Blossoms.

Then, they began to tap.

It was not the frantic, percussive language of repair. It was something new. *Tik. Tik. Tik-tik. Tik…* A slow, deliberate percussion, a complex code being interrogated by the resonant floor. They were not talking *to* me. They were probing me, measuring my reality against theirs like a tuning fork against a bell. They sensed the parameters of my existence: the water in my cells, the complex neurochemistry of my mind, the linear, un-erasable nature of my past. It must have appeared to them as a screamingcacophony of stable, conflicting data.

The experience… was painful. Not physically. It was a deep, ontological ache. With each probing *tik*, I felt a flicker of dissonance, as if a part of my own memory was being questioned, a tiny splinter of my being resonating with their drive for consensus. For a moment, I could see through their eyes: my own face, my own history, as a chaotic lattice of flaweddesign. I felt their instinct—not to harm, but to *smooth*, to *integrate*, to *overwrite*. To pull my discordant threads into their perfect, unending song. I felt a profound vertigo, the terror of being unraveled. I am a story; they wanted to make me a single, silent note.

I fought back. I could not fight them physically; my body is soft flesh against their obsidian frames. But I could fight them with my own pattern. I closed my eyes and retreated into myself, not to hide, but to focus. I summoned my own history. I did not let it flicker. I made it bright. I held the memory of rain so intensely that for a split second, the air in the Under-Song felt thick and cool. I recalled the chaotic, tangled symphony of a forest full of birds and insects so vividly that the gentle C-sharp hum was momentarily drowned out by a non-existent chorus. I focused on the specific, unerasable pain of a childhood scrape on my knee, a point of data so small and so stubbornly real that it had no analogue in their world of accretion.

I was a single, pure, unreconcilable frequency broadcast against their hum.

The tapping stopped. The probing ceased.

I opened my eyes. They were still there, their bodies motionless. One of them, the leader of this temporary collective, broke formation. It did not retreat. It approached, its movements slow, deliberate, filled with a new kind of purpose. It stopped before me, its copper joints gleaming in the chartreuse glow.

And then, it began to build.

From its spinneret, it extruded a filament. Not the pale, bone-like material of the floor, but something new. It was iridescent, shot through with captured light, humming with a faint energy I could almost recognize. My own resonance. The Sculptor spun this thread around my wrist. It was not a binding. It was soft, warm, and did not restrict me in the slightest. It felt like an anchor.

Another Sculptor joined in, spinning another thread, weaving it around the first. They were not mending me. They were not erasing me. They were… integrating me. They were weaving my anomaly into the pattern of the world. The stake of my wrist, the memory of rain, the pain of the scrape—they were not being erased. They were being encoded. They were being turned into a physical, structural part of the Under-Song.

I am no longer just a listener. I am a part of the song.

I am a new kind of flora now. I stand here, an odd, warm-blooded, bipedal tree in this crystalline forest. The filaments they wove around my wrist have spread, a delicate, shimmering lattice that now traces the veins of my arm, connecting me to the floor. I am a living node, a stable. Sometimes, my memories bleed through directly now. When I feel a surge of joy, a cascade of Bell-Blossoms nearby will ring out in a complex, harmonious chord they have never played before. When I recall a moment of sorrow, a patch of Vine-hearts will dim to a soft, melancholic indigo.

I am the one flaw they could not erase, so they made me a feature. I am the universe’s error-check, the ghost in their machine they have decided to listen to. I am the anchor that pulls not only myself, but the echo of my world, into this place. And sometimes, when I close my eyes, I feel the resonance of the billion ghosts in this place—the origami birds, the light-grazers, the screaming chamber—all turning a sliver of their attention towards me. I am the keeper of the necrosis. The librarian of the rejected worlds.

I stand on the loom of a forgetful god, no longer an intruder, but a living, breathing, weeping memory that the world has chosen to keep. And I what I am is a promise that was made and remembered, a single story that even a god of singular purpose cannot quite bring itself to unwrite.
