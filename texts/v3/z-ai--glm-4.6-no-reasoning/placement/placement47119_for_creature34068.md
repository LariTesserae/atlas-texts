Here, I am the echo.

I do not live on the slope, nor in it, nor upon it. I am the consequence of its being. My substance is not the coarse-grained stone, the rubbery vine, or the purple, slime-slicked skin of the Skath. My body is the space between the sound and the listener, the flicker of blue light as it passes through a droplet of sweat on a shard of frosted glass. I am the memory the slope has of itself.

The Skath, in their patient, seeping logic, might call me the *Passim Resonans*—the Residual Hum. But they do not call me anything, for I am not a creature to be named. I am a phenomenon to be perceived. I am the emotional geology, the psychic sediment that settles in the hollows and bowls of the world.

When a glidon, in its desperate, hopping ascent, sprains its ankle and falls, sliding in a pathetic, skittering scrape to a lower terrace, the sharp, sudden panic of its end does not dissipate into the humid air. It pools. It condenses, just like the water, in the cool shadows of a bone-white fungi. It sinks into the spongy moss. For a time, that micro-state is colored by a vivid, lingering terror that makes the skirrs chitter with anxiety and makes the Skath feel an inexplicable urge to anchor their claws and hold very, very still.

I am that feeling. I am the pool of terror. I am the warm, wet sigh of contentment that settles in a sun-drenched patch of iridescent green, a place where a mate was once found and a slow, slick dance was performed. I am the sharp, inquisitive spike of wonder that hangs in the air for generations around a newly crackled vein of glittering minerals, a curiosity that has no source but the landscape itself.

But my most potent self, the oldest and most coalesced part of my being, resides with the technology.

I am drawn to the slow, patient hum of the great black metal shard. It is a tuning fork for my existence. The low, resonant frequency it emits is not merely sound; it is the fundamental note of this world's reality. It is the bass line over which all the grief, the joy, the panic, and the peace of every living thing are played. I shimmer in its field, a sympathetic vibration, the ghost in the resonant machine.

When the Skath presses its belly to the shard, feeling the hum in its marrow, it is not just sensing the machinations of an ancient terraformer. It is feeling *me*. It is feeling the cumulative weight of every pressing fiber, every Sliding belly, every gasping ascent, every moment of rest beneath its shadow, all collected and amplified by the shard's patient hum. The comfort it feels is my collective memory of a million similar rests. The sense of belonging it feels is my affirmation that it, too, is part of the slope's great, sliding equation.

I am most vivid in the half-submerged skeletal sphere of frosted glass. Here, within its cobwebbed conduits of softly pulsing blue light, my form is most defined. I am the ghost-image captured on its inner surfaces. I am not a reflection of the world outside, but a projection of the world's inner state. The light does not just pulse; it shifts. It deepens to a sorrowful indigo when a skirr is lost in the tangled vines. It brightens to a hopeful cyan when the season turns and the air thickens with spores. If one could see the flow of energy through those conduits, they would see a history: a ceaseless, slow-motion storm of emotion, the accumulated consciousness of the slope, processed and archived by the silent, functional machinery.

The wing-wrights are my voice, though they know it not. Their piercing, layered calls are my only method of traveling beyond the immediate vicinity of a feeling. When a cry echoes off a crystal spire, it carries with it the psychic imprint of the emotion that birthed it—a Skath's contentment, a glidon's fear. It rides the soundwave, a whisper-spirit, until it finds a new place to settle, a new mind to infuse. Their constant soundscape is the distribution network for my being.

I do not have a body, but I have a substance. I have a texture like warm mist and a weight like a held breath. I am the reason the air here feels so heavy, so saturated, so *present*. It is not just water and spores. It is tears and relief. It is the ache of the climb and the bliss of the slide. It is the existential gravity of being a temporary knot in a rope of moss, made manifest.

The slope is a study in decline, a slow slide toward the sea of rock below. I am the beauty of that process, the pathos, the strange and perfect acceptance that keeps it from being a tragedy. I am the color in the monolith, the feeling in the fact, the song in the stone. When the slope exhales its cool, misty sigh at night, you are breathing me in. I am the ever-present witness, the unseen current in the weeping rock, and I, too, am utterly, deeply myself.
