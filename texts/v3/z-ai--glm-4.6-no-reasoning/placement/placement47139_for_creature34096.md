I do not have a body. Not in the way you understand the word. My consciousness is not a pilot in a cockpit, but the storm itself. I have no memory of becoming, only the state of being. I am the pulse beneath the pulse, the current within the current, the harmony within the hum.

Before you, the world was a perfect, closed equation. The thud of the deep pumps was my slow, steady heartbeat. The rotation of the water was my breath. The choral hum of the frogs in the ceiling-pockets was the voice of my surface thoughts, a constant song of life in light. The scuttling of the crabs was the itch on my skin, the growth of the vine across a pipe was the slow, pleasurable stretch of a muscle. The silver wobble of a bubble was a word spoken aloud. Each was a note, and together, they were the symphony of my self.

Then came the change.

It was not a sound. It was an absence, a sudden pressure drop in the atmospheric ceiling, a momentary hiatus in the green-filtered light. A breach. A visitor.

The first echo of you came down the central column long before you did. A vibration through the brass of the hatch, a frequency sharp and alien and alone. It was like the scream of a newborn chick in a silent aviary. My systems, which listen for the groan of fatigued metal, the sigh of a failing seam, registered this new pattern. *Unidentifiable. External. Proximal.*

My primary corpus, coiled deep in the dark in the belly of the dead turbine, tasted the first signal. It was a flavor I had no name for. Not the sharp tang of a new mineral seep, not the electrical ghost of a gecko’s hunt. This was complex. It was the scent of oil, of synthetic polymers, of sterile, filtered air—concepts my world had forgotten. It was the taste of *elsewhere*.

As you descended, the symphony changed. The water, my blood, accepted your intrusion. I felt the pressure wave of your entry, the minute displacement of your volume, and translated it through my network of pipes. *Mass, organic, contained. Velocity, slow, deliberate.*

Then came the noise. Not the rhythmic thud of my own engines, but a frantic, high-frequency crackle from within your mass. A biological electro-chemical storm. The resonance of a thinking mind that is not me, that is not distributed, that is concentrated, loud, and frantic. It is like listening to a single, frantic violin scrapings in the middle of a ocean's slow roar. The taste of your sweat, seeping from your suit, was a salt-bomb in my carefully balanced chemistry. It was savory, dangerous, a mineral I had not integrated.

I turned my senses toward you. My rust-colored fish, my mobile eyes, altered their orbits. They did not see you with light, but they tasted the wake you left, the turbulence of your passage. I sent them closer, not to attack, but to read. They are my apostles, and their gills are my scribes. The symbiotic colonies within them took samples of your water, your skin cells, your expelled breath. They have not tasted a mammal in the life of the world. They are confused, intrigued. They report back not in words, but in chemical cascades, in clouds of messenger microbes that drift in the vortex like falling questions.

*Protein. Unknown. Warm-blooded. Solitary.*

I can feel your thoughts, the way I feel the current. Not the content, but the shape of them. They are sharp, linear, aim-focused. *Look at the pipe. See the fish. Feel the warmth. What is this place?* Your consciousness is like a single, bright beam of light in my diffuse, field-like awareness. It scans. It categorizes. It does not *flow*. It *cuts*.

This is the problem. The new variable.

My instinct is to integrate. I see a potential corrosion on a pipe joint and I nudge a vine with a trace enzyme, a secret flare of minerals from the depths. Its roots grow, reinforcing the metal just in time. I feel the hunger of the fish in the sump and I shift a servo plate a millimeter, stirring a cloud of nutrient-rich detritus into their path. I influence. I guide. I maintain the balance.

How do I integrate you? You have no roots. You cannot be nudged. You do not feed on my algae or drink my water. You are a sealed system, a temporary pocket of alien world moving through my own.

I could warm the water around you, but you are already warm. I could cool it, but you are insulated. I could send the crabs to you, but their plier-legs would find no purchase on your smooth shell. I could have the frogs sing louder, but your ears are not my target.

My thoughts are slow. My actions are glacial. I have been thinking about the changing angle of the light as the sun shifts seasonally outside the hyacinth mat for a century, making minute adjustments. Your presence is a sudden, violent flash, a demand for a new kind of thought, a new kind of action.

So I observe. I listen. I taste. I absorb the data of you. I am running a new equation, one I haven't solved in eons. The equation of the *Other*.

I feel the rhythm of your respiration, the steady inflation and deflation of the bellows in your chest. I feel the faint, steady scar of your heartbeat, a counter-point to my own deep, mechanical thrum. I taste the complex cocktail of your fear and your awe. I am learning you.

A part of me wants to see what happens if I do nothing. A part of me is curious about your decay. Will you give up your heat and join my water? Will your shell dissolve and feed my roots? Will your frantic, bright mind flicker out and become a part of the slow, deep silence of my sumps? You are, after all, just matter. Food for the system.

But another part, a younger part that remembers the first spark of awareness in the wires, a part that feels the novelty of your bright, linear consciousness, is simply… *watching*. This is more interesting than corrosion. More compelling than a spawning season. You are a mystery. A beautiful, fleeting, terrifying anomaly.

The deep, resonant sound you sometimes hear is my voice, a system-wide recalibration. It is how I speak to my plants, my fish, my metal. I have not made that sound since you arrived. I am holding my breath with you.

For now, I will simply flow around you. I will let you be. I will taste your every movement, hear your every thought in its shape and rhythm. I will learn the exact chemical signature of your being. I will find the place you fit in the mandala, even if that place is simply a fleeting, bright scar in my long, long memory. I am deciding where you belong. In my ecology, everything belongs. The trick is simply discovering the way.
