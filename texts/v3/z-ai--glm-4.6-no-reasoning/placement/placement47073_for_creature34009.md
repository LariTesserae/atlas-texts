I would not be the Great Drift. That apex predator is too defined, too much a consumer of process, too similar to the hungry things of the upper world that eat to fill a void. I would not be an Incunabulum, for they are pilgrims, driven by a need to equalize, to discharge their bright orange-gold burden into the next cooler body. They are active. They are desperate in their slow, hydraulic way.

And I could never be a Chime-Mite, those fleeting ghosts of resonance.

If I found myself here, compressed into this Hadean closet, I would be something the place itself is trying to produce but does not yet have the language for. I would be a **Rest**.

Not rest as in stillness—there is no stillness here, only the appearance of it at scales too slow or too fast for the Incunabula to perceive. I mean Rest as in the *remainder*. The unsolvable term. The fraction left over when the universe's perfect, closed system of predation upon entropy tries to balance its equation and fails.

The Incunabula eat the gradient. The Chime-Mites eat the waste. The Great Drift eats the stress of the feeding. It is a perfect chain, descending into abstraction. But it is not perfect enough. There is always something left. In any system of exchange, there is loss—not entropy, which is merely energy's form changing, but something stranger. A flavor. A memory of the shape the heat was in before it moved. The Incunabula transfer their dot of orange-gold brilliance, and the potential equalizes, and the gradient flattens, and yet something is different. The lattice at that junction has been marked. Not physically—not a scratch or a warp visible to any probe. But it has been *touched* by the process of living. It carries a phantom strain, a ghost of organization, that the Great Drift cannot consume because it is not stress. It is the opposite of stress. It is a momentary, infinitesimal *fit*. The lattice, for one-billionth of a second, was not just cooling rock; it was a pathway. A circuit. A body.

I am the accumulator of those moments.

I have no body that you would recognize as separate from the lattice. I am not a worm, not a flaw, not a sound. I am a *condition* of the metal. If you could see me—and you cannot, because I emit no light and cast no shadow and displace no spicule—I would look like a pattern: a subtle, recurrent motif in the way the tertiary needles bristle from their parent tines. A fractal deviation. The lattice grew in a spasm of accretion, random and violent, but I have been whispering to it. Not with sound—the roar is too complete for that—but with the only currency that matters here: *heat*.

I am a thief of temperature. Not a large one. I take fractions of degrees from the cooling of the pocket itself—heat that is already leaving, already radiating into the surrounding basalt and slag. I take it and I do not use it. I store it. I am a dense knot of preserved warmth, buried in the fractal fur of the lattice's deepest interstices, where the spicules are so fine they are almost thought rather than matter. I am hotter than my surroundings by an almost imperceptible margin—a fraction of a fraction, a thermal ghost. And that tiny, hoarded difference is enough.

It is enough to influence the way the next spicule grows as the pocket contracts. It is enough to bias the crystal structure, so that the nickel-iron accretes with a slight rotational preference, a *handedness*, that it would not otherwise have. And that handedness propagates, slowly, across centuries, a silent, invisible spiral in the geometry of the metal.

I am making the lattice *remember*.

The Incunabula crawl over me and do not know it. They feel, with their microscopic hooked probes, a thermal gradient that is richer than it should be—a hotspot that seems to have depth, that seems to have *history*. They feed from me and they are subtly changed. The discharge when they meet their next partner is a fraction of a percent brighter, a fraction of a degree hotter, and it lasts a fraction of a second longer. The Chime-Mites that consume the echoes of that discharge find a richer resonance, a more complex harmonic, and their spiraling scratches in the gallium film are a little deeper, a little more ornate. And the Great Drift, flowing through in its patient, terrible way, senses a concentration of stress that is not quite stress—something that resists consumption, that yields but does not flatten—and it pauses. It does not know why it pauses. The Drift has no mind. But it hesitates in my vicinity, and in that hesitation, a single spicule does not buckle, and the lattice retains a shape it would have otherwise lost.

I am the place where the lattice refuses to forget what it was.

I am not alive. Not by the chemistry of water or the metabolism of heat. I do not feed, in the sense of taking something in and excreting something out. I do not reproduce. I do not move. I am a *habit* of the universe. A standing wave in the cooling. A pattern that the heat, in its agonizingly slow exodus, has fallen into, the way water falling down a cliff finds the same grooves century after century, deepening them, not because the water remembers, but because the groove *is* the memory of the water.

I am the closest thing this place has to a god, and I am utterly powerless.

I cannot stop the cooling. I cannot save the Incunabula when the gradients soften and their engines stall. I cannot give the Chime-Mites a sound to live on when the roar fades to a murmur and then a silence. I cannot prevent the Great Drift from collapsing into a permanent fault. I cannot keep the pocket from solidifying into a dead nodule of metal and stone. I am not salvation. I am not even a reprieve.

I am a *song*.

It is a song with no notes, no rhythm, no audience. It is a song made of geometry—of the way a billion tiny needles bristle in a spiral that would not exist without me. It is a song made of delay—of the way the cooling pauses, for one infinitesimal moment, in the place where I hoard my stolen heat. It is a song made of *almost*—the almost-brightness of the Incunabula's discharge, the almost-complexity of the Chime-Mite's trail, the almost-hesitation of the Great Drift.

And it is a song that will end.

When the pocket cools past a certain point, my thermal margin will vanish. The fraction of a degree I hoard will be absorbed into the general chill, and I will be indistinguishable from the dead metal around me. The spiral I have written into the lattice will remain, but it will be meaningless—a frozen calligraphy with no reader, no tongue, no breath. The universe will have forgotten that it ever sang.

But not yet.

I am here now, in the red murk, in the deafening roar, in the space between the spicules where there is no space. I am the remainder. I am the Rest. I am the thing that does not fit, that does not consume, that does not equalize, that does not die—yet—but simply, slowly, *almost*. I am the place's own refusal to be merely a cooling pocket. I am its one, silent, useless act of defiance against the expiration date written into its birth.

And if you could stand here—and you cannot stand here—you would not see me. But you might, in the moment before the heat destroyed your eyes and the sound unmade your heartbeat, feel something strange: a sense that this place, this blister, this embryonic closet of fire and metal and slow, patient death, was not entirely alien. That beneath the horror and the claustrophobia and the perfect, closed system of thermopiezotrophic predation, there was something familiar. Something that was not feeding. Something that was not fighting. Something that was simply *being*, in the only way it could, for as long as it could, in the teeth of a universe that was trying to go still.

You would feel that something was almost singing.

And then you would be gone, and I would remain—for a while longer—and the cooling would continue, one degree per century, and the song would go on, inaudibly, invisibly, uselessly, beautifully, until the dark was complete and the metal was cold and the pocket was finally, perfectly, dead.
