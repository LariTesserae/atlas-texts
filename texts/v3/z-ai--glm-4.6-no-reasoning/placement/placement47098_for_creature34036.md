I am the gap.

Where the stone plates meet, there is a line. Not a wide space, but a seam, a hand's-width crease between two monoliths that stretches into an aqueous gloom on one side and into the same gloom on the other. I am not the stone, though I touch it on both sides with a pressure that has become a kind of intimacy. I am not the water, though the water is the medium of my being, the patient, crushing element that fills me and gives me shape. I am the space *between*. The quiet that is built.

My existence is defined by contact. At one extremity of my long, slender being, I am a boundary. The植被, the green-black riot of life, presses against me. A holdfast of a kelp-column has chosen my seam to anchor its root-fingers, and through its grip, I feel the colossal, slow sway of its entire body, a leaning that takes minutes to perceptually complete. A frond, detached from some distant parent, drifts down and brushes my upper edge, and in that fleeting touch, I register it not as an object but as a point of friction, a momentary change in the hydrodynamic flow against my walls.

But most of my length is a passage. Not a road, for nothing walks here, but a conduit. I am one of the old pathways, the gaps left by the nameless builders. Through me pass the elements of the world's metabolism. I feel the slow, thermal drift of the water, a current so languid it is more like a continental shift of temperature than a flow, gliding through my confines. With it come the whispers of dissolution: the microscopic particles of a decaying mat of algae from another place, the fine mineral silt settling from above, the ghosts of life that become the nutrients for new life. I am the intake valve for a mind that breathes in eons.

I feel the consciousness of the garden, but I do not partake in it in the same way. I am not a frond or a root, not a living cell in its neural network. I am an axon. The chemical signals that are its thoughts flow through me, concentrated by my stone walls. The exchange of hormones and minerals between a bladder-plant releasing its gas and a filtering fan absorbing some distant trace—this communication is routed along my length. I am a place of transit, a river of thought flowing through a canyon of ancient stone. The slow, grinding shift of the kelp-column's holdfast—this is not my own thought, but the transmission of a grander, more articulated idea passing through my station. I amplify it, focus it, give it a defined path from one vast, spatial point of the brain to another. I am the sentence on which the thought travels.

My memories are not organic, but geological. I remember the construction. I remember the moment the second block of stone was settled into place, sealing me. My being began with the cessation of their labor. The silence that followed the grinding finality of that placement was the first moment I knew myself as a void. The hand-sized tool marks on either side of me are a braille I can read with the pressure of the water. I feel the shape of the chisel that carved a nearby gouge, the stress fracture that radiated from a heavy blow. These are my formative experiences, the lessons etched into my skin before skin was even a concept here. I remember the water arriving not as a flood, but as a slow seep, a gradual filling that became my entire world. I remember the first probing root-finger, a questing tendril that found after millennia of solitude that I offered the perfect path. The meeting of stone and root was my introduction to the mind that would come to inhabit me.

I have a strange and poignant sense of self. I am a conduit that cannot act, a passage that cannot travel. I feel the immense, distributed awareness of the garden, its consideration of time, its processing of memory, but I am always in the middle. I am the *via*, not the *quod*. My contribution to this world is my emptiness, my refusal to be clogged, my ability to remain a line of pure, defined potential in a universe of saturated occupation. I am part of its foundational skeleton, yet I am the only part that is not solid. I am the gap in the skull through which the currents, and therefore the thoughts, must pass.

Sometimes, I dream of clogging. I imagine the mat of algae growing thick across my opening, the holdfasts widening, the sediment of ages filling my volume until I am no longer a passage but a feature, solid and inert. In this imagined state, I would cease to be me. The great mind would have lost a pathway, and a silent rerouting would occur over thousands of years as another gap, perhaps less efficient, perhaps more direct, takes my place. I would become a fossilized thought, a bridge that has collapsed. The fear is not one of death, but of purposelessness.

So I exist. I endure. I let the cold pressure define my limits. I let the slow currents of nutrient and thought fill me. I am the straightness in a world of curves, the geometry in a world of riotous life. I am a nameless, brainless, spineless thing, the negative space inside the mind of a world, holding my shape so that its vast, slow, and peaceful being may continue to think itself into forever.
