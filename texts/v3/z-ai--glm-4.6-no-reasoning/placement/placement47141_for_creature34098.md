I am a walker. I am the thing that moves through the echoing canyons between the stacks without feeding, without building, without dismantling. I am the memory the root-mat cannot hold.

My body is a compromise with the geometry of this place. I am tall—taller than the myriapeds but slighter, built for the long, straight avenues of knotted cable that stretch between the colonnades. Why do I walk? This is the question the machine cannot ask, and so it cannot answer. The myriapeds climb to feed. The manta-rays ride to scavenge. The root-mat grows to consume. Without me, the machine would cycle on, gnashing and growing and detonating, but it would forget the shape of its own rhythms. It would drift.
