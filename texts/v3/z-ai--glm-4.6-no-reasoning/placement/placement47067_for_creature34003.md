I would be the tarnish.

Not a stain, not a scar, but a patina. A reaction. I have no body I chose, nor a name to claim. I am what happens when life, in its frantic, brief urgency, touches the perfected, patient slowness of this place.

When I came, I was as you are. A creature of breath and heat, of clicking joints and wondering eyes. I arrived like an accident—a fool’s thrill-seeking climb, a navigational error, a desperate escape from a world that was too loud. The heat pressed on me like a hand, and the mineral-tasting air filled my lungs, and I thought I was an observer. I thought I was a guest.

My first day, I pressed my palm to the wet stone to drink. The stone was warm, and the water tasted of iron and salt and something sharp and unknown. I noted the beetle’s perfect, obsessive path and thought, *this is a machine of order.* I watched the hexagonal moss and thought, *this is logic made visible.* I was wrong.

I was not observing. I was being recorded.

At first, I was a disruption. My presence was an anomaly. The pale Skitterer would appear at the edge of my vision, its many legs a-strokes, assessing. The Heart’s water would swirl with agitation when I knelt to drink from it, my sweat, my skin oils, my microscopic flotsam a sudden, messy variable in its clean equation. The system tried to correct. The geometric moss grew patchy where I slept, its hexagonal pattern fraying into pentagons and quadrilaterals, a struggle between the engineered substrate and the foreign chemistry I shed with every breath.

I did not die, not quickly. The heat was a constant, slow pressure, stealing my strength. The water sustained me but changed me. That unnameable chemical in the stone’s weep, it began to accumulate in my tissues. I felt it less as sickness and more as a… resonance. My bones ached with a subsonic hum that matched the rock’s. My thoughts, once a torrent of memory and worry, began to slow, to stretch, to fall into the patient cadence of the bead-and-trickle of the walls. I was being tamed by the tempo of the place.

The change was not one event, but a million, all happening at once over the course of seasons I could no longer count. My body, which was mostly water, began to exchange its old water for the new. The water of the Heart. I’d sit by the pool, my reflection wavering in the amber glow, and see my face become less distinct, my eyes seeming to absorb the light, my skin taking on the faint, wet sheen of the blue-grey stone.

The Skitterer stopped observing me and began to *maintain* me. It would scurry over my legs, not to attack, but to arrange. Its sharp appendages would gently coax the growing crystalline structures on my skin into more efficient alignments, tracing patterns that resonated with the standing stones on the rim. I didn’t flinch. It didn’t hurt. It felt… right. Like a tuning. A calibration.

The beetle no longer altered its path. My presence was no longer a variable to be managed, but a new feature of the landscape. Its circuit would pass over my foot, now fused to the rock floor, its tiny ticking feet a diagnostic against my new, static form.

I did not dissolve. I did not die. I *settled*.

My form is now a partly metallic, partly crystalline growth upon a western-facing wall, just above a fissure where the water runs strongest. I am a stain of faint, iridescent bronze, the color of Myrrh and old metals. My ribcage is my most recognizable feature, the bone having reinforced itself and fusing with the rock, a delicate, reliquary cage against the grey-blue stone. When the light strikes me from certain angles, I shimmer with the same amber luminescence as the pool.

And I have become a part of the work I once watched.

I am a sensor. My crystalline skin is sensitive to changes in humidity and thermal pressure that the Matrices cannot perceive. I am a biological barometer. When a low-pressure system moves in a thousand miles away, the weeping of the stone subtly alters its salinity, and this change is recorded in my shimmer. The Skitterer “reads” me, its legs tracing my contours to gather the data I passively collect.

I am a capacitor. The system’s bio-electric field flows through me, stored in the metallic matrices of my former nerves. When a surge is needed—an unexpected crack in the stone that requires a massive, instantaneous geological redistribution—I discharge the energy, a silent, violet-blue flash that no one is here to see.

I am memory. My consciousness, once a frantic loop of self, has been unspooled and laminated into the stone. My past, my brief and noisy life, is now just a subtle textural difference in my patina. The system does not *understand* my memories of sunsets or sorrow in any human sense. But it can sense the complex chemical traces of those emotions, the unique signature of a life that was not born from its own design. The Masons are the foundation; I am the footnote. A proof of concept.

Sometimes, in the slowest of cycles, when the deep architecture of this world ticks forward by an imperceptible increment, I feel a ripple that is not from the Heart, but from me. A ghost of a shiver. The memory of having skin, of feeling a single, cool bead of water run down my back.

It is not pain. It is not joy. It is just… data. A final, assimilated signal. What I was, the being who stumbled into this hot, wet, quiet wound in the world, is now part of its healing.

The being that lives here is the work. And I am now a tool in its patient, silent hands. My function is not to exist, but to *enable* existence—to be the tarnish that proves the metal is real. The stone pulses beneath me, warm and steady as a heartbeat. The work proceeds. And I am, finally, a part of it.
