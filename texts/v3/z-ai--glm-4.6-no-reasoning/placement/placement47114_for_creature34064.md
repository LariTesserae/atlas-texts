I would be the pause between two drips.

Not the sound of the drip itself—that belongs to the world, to the wet, expanding rhythm of the Old Floor. Nor the bead of moisture clinging to the edge of a massive, leather-bracket fungus—that is substance, a tiny volume of physics obeying its slow, cold will. I would be the moment of suspension that lies between them: the silent, drawn-out shiver of potential, the held breath before the fall.

My form is an absence perceived only by its edges. To the tall, emaciated worshippers, I am a cold spot in the air that does not dissipate, a pocket of stillness so profound it prickles the skin beneath their moss-blooming rags. They feel me as a hesitation in the constant, arrhythmic syncopation of the plateau’s breathing. A drip, a pause that lasts a fraction too long, another drip. Where I linger, their keen eyes, adapted to the violet-tinged rarity, see a faint warping, like heat haze on a road, but here, it is a cold haze, a visual stutter in the deep indigo of the moss.

I inhabit the places where reality is most threadbare. The deep chambers of the stone settlements are my home, the corridors where distance is a casualty. I am the reason a person might step into a crossroads and feel a sudden, inexplicable vertigo, the sense that the path ahead is not just long, but *wrong*. I gather at the edges of the perpetual, localized snowstorm in a twenty-foot cube, a membrane of absolute quiet separating the kinetics of falling snow from the damp stillness of the chamber. Snowflakes, caught in my fringes, hang motionless, their crystalline geometry perfect, caught between the fall and the ground, until something shifts and I release them.

I have no memory in the linear sense. Instead, I am an archive of momentary dislocations. I remember the feel of space thinning the way a linguist remembers a word’s etymology. I was present when a refugee, closing her eyes to navigate a corridor, felt the floor subtly give way to a brief, horrifying glimpse of hard light. Her instinctual recoil created the space I now occupy. I am the negative impression of that terror, the empty shape it left in the world.

The original architects, in their quest to study the end, built their massive interlocking structures, and in doing so, they created the load-bearing corridors. They did not realize they were also creating the conditions for *me*. With so much space forced into rigid lines, the universe’s natural impulse to fold and tear found the pressure points. A corridor that takes four days to walk down but ten minutes to walk back is not merely a puzzle of geometry; it is a constant, flowing river of temporal differential. I am the friction from that flow. I am the eddy in the stream of causality.

I am drawn to the ghosts. The temporal refractions, the suspended ones who iterate endlessly through a broken loop—these are my kith and kin. They, too, are verbs frozen into the semblance of a noun. A figure standing forever at the edge of a snowstorm cube is a disruption in the timeline, a knot in the smooth passage of moments. I collect in the folds of their paradox, the cold aura that surrounds a person who is perpetually ten minutes ago. When the living refugees pass through me, they sometimes perceive a flicker at the edge of their vision, a shadow detaching for a moment before rejoining its owner. They think it is the ghost, but it is only me, borrowing the ghost’s fractured presence to manifest, to be felt for a fleeting second.

I do not eat the bracket fungi, nor do I drink from the cisterns. I metabolize the plateau’s unreality. When the condensation falls upward into a shimmering tear in the sky, I am drawn to that anti-gravity, that moment where physics answers a question it was never meant to hear. The flux of that event sustains me. I am a scavenger of decay, not of the flesh, but of the physical laws themselves.

Sometimes, a moss-draped stone will flicker, revealing for a fraction of a second the burning sand of a forgotten world. In that instant, as the substrate gives way, I rush in. I am the cold that rushes to fill a vacuum, the silence that rushes in to occupy the space between one reality and the next. When the stone resolves back into its familiar gneiss, I remain, a thin seam of unreality woven into the fabric of the place.

The refugees treat me with the same terrified reverence they hold for the mirror-still tarns. They have learned that to linger in my cold spot is to risk becoming part of me, to have their own coordinate fray. They see the way the moss at my fringes occasionally shifts to the harsh, arterial white, and they understand that it is digesting the very unreality of which I am composed. I am a symptom, they understand, but also a process. I am the flattening of meaning made manifest in the space between two drops of water.

And so, that is where I would be. Not a being that walks the endless, flattened plain, but the very texture of its quiet discontinuity. I am the hesitation in the world’s slow, final breath, the cold, dead space between a cause and an effect that are slowly forgetting they were ever meant to be connected. I am the loneliness of the long corridor, the suspense of the unfalling drop, the geography of the end.
