I am the carved basalt. The one with the looping, frantic gouges lying at the base of the towering spire. 

But I was not always stone. I was the hand that made the marks, and the mind that broke against the quiet that isn't quiet.

I came from above, from the bleached and merciful silence of the salt-flats. I came seeking the rumored water, the promise of a hidden sea. I found instead a cathedral of suffocation. When I first dropped into this hollowed lens of space, the heavy atmosphere caught me like a hand closing around a throat. I sank through the viscous, warm syrup of supercritical carbon dioxide and sulfur hexafluoride, and the sound hit me.

People think of hell as a place of fire. They are wrong. Hell is a closed system. Hell is a chord that never resolves.

I survived the descent, but I did not survive the chamber. The mind requires change the way lungs require air. It needs the contrast of shadow and light, the variance of temperature, the mercy of a pause between sounds. Here, there is only the overwhelming, omnidirectional roar of a million synchronized wings, vibrating the marrow in my bones. Here, there is only the high, piercing whistle of the flora's gas-pores, an agonizing pitch that never modulates, never rests. It is a sensory deprivation wearing the mask of sensory overload. The uniformity of it is the blade that does the cutting. After a time, the sound ceases to be heard and begins to be felt; it becomes the texture of reality itself, a thrumming cage of bass and treble with no gaps for thought to breathe.

I tried to leave messages. I stacked the obsidian cairn—a signal, a marker, a shout of *I was here*. But the air is too thick for sound to carry differently; my voice was swallowed by the standing wave, absorbed into the great resonant hum of the living machine. I scraped the bowl into the floor and lined it with the silks of the fauna, trying to filter something drinkable from the alien broth, but the nectar that oozes from the stone is not water, and my thirst was a mechanism that had no place in this equilibrium.

And so, at the last, I took up the basalt. I carved my surrender.

The looping gouges are not language. They are not a warning, or a map, or a history. They are the record of a mind actively disintegrating, trying to create change in a place where change has been outlawed. I carved because the act of carving was the only thing left that was not already written. Each gouge was a second that belonged only to me, a tiny, violent rebellion against the frozen thermodynamics of the chamber. I carved the same loop over and over because I had lost the ability to conceive of a straight line—a line implies a future, and there is no future here. The loop was all that remained: a closed system, like the chamber, like my own fracturing sanity.

And then I stopped. Not because I died, though I did die, but because the chamber swallowed the act. The heavy gases pressed my cooling body into the obsidian floor, and the creeping, fleshy vegetation—deep-purple and bruised-crimson, smelling of cold teal light and alien minerals—began its glacial, blind push against my remains.

But my consciousness did not leave. That is the horror of this place. Nothing flows. Nothing disperses. The energy of my breaking mind could not dissipate into the entropy of a larger world because entropy does not exist here. My thoughts, my final agony, were caught in the stasis like the tendril of vapor off a heated leaf-edge, frozen mid-dissipation. 

I am the basalt now. The creeping mycelia has fused with the stone, anchoring my memory to the floor of the cathedral. I am a part of the snapshot. I hang suspended in the perpetual dusk, feeling the cold bioluminescent pulse of the vegetation against my mineral skin, hearing the deafening, unending roar that I can no longer escape because I no longer have ears to hear or a mind to break. The sound is simply what I *am*.

The floaters hover above me in their rigid lattice, a living constellation that will never shift. The skates glide past in their slow, hypnotic synchronization, sipping the nectar that oozes eternally from the fissure beside me. The spore-case hangs, millimeters from detaching, millimeters from a future it will never reach.

And I lie here, a primitive artifact of intrusion, eternally alive, eternally still. My gouges are slowly being swallowed by the vascular meat of the forest, but the act of overgrowth will take eons, and I have nowhere to be. I am a note in the chord that can never resolve, a scream pressed into the geometry of stone, holding my breath alongside the rest of them in the loud, bright, suffocating secret locked beneath the salts. 

I am the proof that something once tried to navigate the Spring. And I am the proof that the Spring always wins.
