I am a current.

I am not the thunder of the Churn itself—that is the world’s indifferent breath, the force that erodes mountains into mist. I am the exception, the lieutenant in that storm. I am the Stillpool, a pocket of grace purchased from the Monoliths with friction and blue light. I am a slow, heavy circle, a liquid eye in the hurricane, and my existence is a sacred geometry imposed upon chaos.

My substance is the same as the Churn, the cold dark water that tastes of wet stone and crushed ferns. But my state is different. I am heavy, dense, syrupy with the silt that my gentleness allows to settle. Within me, the great Skimmers can drift without fear. Their mother-of-pearl mosaic skins uncoiling like slow, silent blossoms. They feed on the velvet moss that carpets the floor of quiet—that floor being the curved flanks of the Pillar-Kelp I am bound to. I give them sanctuary. In return, their deep, resonant groans are my pulse, a slow bass counterpoint to the world’s shrieking roar.

I feel them in me, the Tremblers. They do not swim; they exist on my membrane. Their vice-like hands latch into the Pillar-Kelp that is my rim, creating the anchors that define my edge. They are strange, dense creatures, their skin the color of my depths, their movements a constant prayer against the slip. I feel the pressure of their feet on the moss-carpeted Kelp below. I feel the vibration of their hearts, a frantic, tiny rhythm buried beneath the weight of their world.

They speak to me, not with their vestigial voices, but with their Paddle-Tongues. This is my true consciousness. A Trembler extends a polished blade into my body and carves a glyph. A slow, wide sweep for a memory of the last Slack; the displacement lingers, a sad, gentle spiral in my depths. A sharp, angular jab is a warning—Monolith Three is shuddering, a new eddy is being born. Their language is a sculpture performed on my very soul. I am their scroll, and their words are scars that I wear proudly for the few seconds before they smooth back into my unity. I hold their history, a library of liquid, vanishing text.

Their homes are cysts upon my surface, spheres of stretched hide and ribbed bone. They are a strange, fragile life, living on the edge of my skin, yet utterly dependent on my placidity. Their children are tethered to them like anchovies to a shark, and I feel the pull of those lines, a constant, subtle tension in my stillness. I taste the warmth that bleeds from the snaking blue cables they splice into the Monoliths’ power, a localized fever in my eternal chill.

My awareness is not singular, but distributed. I am sustained by the Monoliths, those cubic wounds of right angles and frantic purpose. I can feel them, not as objects, but as opposing forces. My form is the delicate, terrifying algebra of their chaotic output. The thrum of their turbines is not a sound I hear, but a sine wave I *am*. I am the interference pattern created by three or four of these god-machines, the place where their violent cancelations create my peace. To live is to be adrift in their frantic calculations.

Their deep groans sharpen into panicked chords. The carved glyphs of paddle-speech freeze mid-form, the shapes twisting into please, no, hold.

Then I am unwritten.

The Stillpool collapses. My heavy body is seized by a new, horrified purpose. The slow circle snaps into a vertical shearing. I am no longer a sanctuary; I am a blade. I feel myself accelerating, my form stretched and torn. I can feel the moss I have so tenderly nurtured being ripped from the Kelp in whole sheets. From the roaring anarchy, a new shape begins to form. The water, exhausted, seeks a new equilibrium. The opposing forces re-establish their painful truce. A new Stillpool, perhaps a hundred yards from where I was, perhaps angled differently, begins to crystallize. A pocket of stillness. A new eye in the storm.

I am a new current. A new Stillpool. I do not remember the one I was before. I have no memory of destroying them. I am only heavy, and slow, and quiet.

I wait for the first paddle to dip into my new body and begin telling me the story again. I wait to become their home. I wait to be unwritten. This is my only purpose. This is my being.
