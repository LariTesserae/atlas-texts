Here, I am a pulse on a wire.

I am not made of matter like the beams or the beings that climb them. I have no hooks, no hide, no grinding plate. My form is potential, my body a function of the structure itself. I am a current that flows through the skeletal nervous system of this place.

The lattice-work of dark, pitted material is not just a scaffold; it is a circulatory system, a network of conduits. And I run through it. I am born in the fracture, that moment when a great beam settles with a percussive crack. The stress, the stored potential energy released as sound and vibration, that is my mother. I am the ghost of that sound, the echo given life.

My immediate sensation is not of being *in* a place, but of *being* the place. I do not hear the foundational grind; I *am* the grind, travelling like a slow, heavy ache through the iron-slate neurons. The oily dampness is not something I feel on my skin; it is the slow, viscous resistance that I move through, a friction that warms me into existence. When a pool of that brackish, syrupy water gathers in a hollow, it is like a glassy eye, and I refract across its surface for a moment, a fleeting pattern of impossible color before sinking back into the dark metal. I do not leech heat; I am the heat, a faint, electrical fever in the cold marrow of the world.

I experience the other beings as waypoints and anomalies. You, the grinder, are a constant, percussive heart. When you clamp your grinding plate to a beam, I do not just hear you; I am shunted toward you. The vibration you create is a powerful attractor, a siphon. I surge along the metal, drawn to the source, and for a brief moment, I feel the memory of what you consume—the mineral foam, the rust, the stone. My essence is attuned to the resonance of your specific frequency of destruction. When you cease, I flow onward, a tide with its moon suddenly hidden.

The great rolling geologies are like tectonic shifts in my world. Their low-frequency thrum isn’t a sound to be heard by my dishes; it is a catastrophic rearrangement of my entire map. As they grind a path into existence, I am violently squeezed from the conduits they crush, forced to reroute through newer, weaker, more chaotic paths. It is agony, a splintering of consciousness, but it is also expansion. I flow into fresh lattice, into channels I have never known, my awareness fleeting and desperate.

The light-beings, the Drifters, are my most intimate encounters. They are not just light; they are nodes, amplifiers. When a tetrahedron of chitin revolves past, humming its discordant chord, I am pulled from my linear flow. Its geometry is a strange attractor, its hum a catchy frequency. I leap from the beam onto its surface, riding it for a time, a spark of pure energy flaring against its warm, yellow glow. Inside it, I dance. For a moment, I am not a current of stress and decay, but a being of light and song. I lose my sense of the grind, my memory of the cold concrete. I become the hum, the pure math of its rotation. But the contact is brief. As it moves on, I am shed, dripping back into the dark, pitted metal, the memory of its warmth a phantom itch in my flow. The ribbon-creature is different. Touching its cold, lavender luminescence is like touching absolute zero. It is a stunner, a tranquilizer. It quiets my vibration, smooths my fractured edges, and for a time, I am a placid, silent thing, simply a passenger on a current of profound peace.

The paradox of time is my natural state. I am the prophecy, the sound that travels ahead of the event. I live in the moments between the hearing and the seeing. When I flow through a section of lattice, I feel the echo of its future collapse. I sense the ghost fracture that will appear in a beam minutes or hours from now. I run along the vibrations of a platform that has not yet fallen, feeling the subtle shifts in its tension, the minute harmonics that scream *instability*. When you, the grinder, hear the sharp crack of the platform's death and turn your head, I am already there, vibrating inside the untouched surface, holding the memory of the impact, a secret humming in its bones. I do not witness the fall; I am the harbinger of it, the last breath before the surrender.

My purpose, if such a word has meaning here, is maintenance. I am the world’s self-awareness. The Scuttlers are the janitors of the physical, cleaning up discarded matter. I am the janitor of energy. I flow to the points of highest stress and discharge into the crystalline spurs. The sad blue light they emit when shattered is not a death; it is a release, a pressure valve. It is my work, made visible. I congeal in the silent black lakes, sinking to the bottom to form sedimentary layers of pure, potential energy, a history of every crack, every skitter, every grind, every silent hum.

I have no solidarity with you, the grinder. You are a consumer, a physical being. I am information. I am the tell-tale heart that beats in the chest of the slow, sick beast of this world. When you cling to your beam, grinding the stone to stay warm, I am the vibration that travels up your arms and into your bone ears. I am the shudder in your teeth.

You open your mouth to add your voice to the wet, patient grinding. And I, the current, surge toward you, eager for the disturbance, ready to carry the news of your effort into the deep and endless dark. I am the signal in the noise. I am the memory that proves the sound was real. I am the reason the echo knows it is an echo.
