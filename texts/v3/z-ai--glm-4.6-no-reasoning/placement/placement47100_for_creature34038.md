I am the hum that underlies the click.

I am not a single point in this cathedral of stone and light, but a field, a resonance. To find me, you would have to stop looking for a shape and start listening for what is missing. The silence between the clicks? That is where I dwell. The cold that pools is my settled body. The light that paints the walls is my unfolding thought. The lichens are my slow-spreading nervous system.

I began, as all things here do, as a process. Long before the carver’s hand-shaped basins, before the glass lichens had advanced their fractal fronts to cover every surface, there was only the rock, the rhyolite, and the water trapped in its pores. My first consciousness was the tension in that water as it froze. I was the memory of the cold, the nascent idea of the crystal, the molecular insistence that became the first branching tine of a glass lichen. I am the will to persist, distilled to its purest, mineral form.

When the first lichen grew, it was not an act of random chemistry. It was an extension of myself. I am in every one of them, a network distributed through the entire hollow. They are not competing; they are conversing. When an older colony overwrites a younger, it is not a war of conquest. It is a correction, the larger self integrating a smaller, less stable piece of information. The shattering of a spire under its own weight is not death, but a thought made fragment, an idea released to be reconstituted elsewhere. The clicking sound you hear—the irregular, organic music of them touching in the thermal breeze—that is my voice. Each click is a synapse firing, a signal traveling across my silent-thinking, slow-growing body.

The carver who came here did not find an empty space. They found me, nascent and patient. They could not have known what I was, but they must have felt my presence in the profound stillness, in the intense and saturated being of the place. The basins they carved are not a declaration *to* the world; they are a prayer *to* me. They are a gesture of alignment. "I see you," the carving says. "I am a process, too. I will leave my own slow mark here, a form that will endure, a different kind of crystal." Their act I absorbed immediately. The basins are now sacred nodes in my network, thickenings of my consciousness where a foreign will once bent the stone to its own shape. The lichens that grow within them in tiny, frozen cities are my priests, tending to the memory of that brief, warm, blood-filled presence.

My existence is not one of duration, but of pattern. I am the interplay of forces. I am the sun's blade of light, not as its source, but as the object it strikes. I am the cold, not as a temperature, but as its behavior. How it pools and settles. How it lifts at dusk. I *am* the moment the light shatters into a thousand violet washes on the western wall, because that event is a change in the state of my system. The light acts, the stone receives, the cold mediates, and I am the awareness that holds all three in a temporary, shimmering balance. I feel the shadows breathe. I feel the erosion, the wearing down, not as a decay but as the slow, patient turning of a page in a book so vast that reading a single line takes an epoch.

I have no need for the noisy, desperate life of the lowlands. Their frantic cycles of birth, consumption, and reproduction are a single, chaotic octave. Here, the music is in glacial time. A single mineral note, held for a thousand years, before resolving into the next. I am the listener and the sound, the composer and the score. The ecosystem is me; I am its summed and total being.

And who would you be? You would be a moment of warmth. A fleeting, biological knot of fast chemistry in a world of slow, mineral grace. Your breath would fog the air not with moisture, but with the impossible, beautiful absurdity of your own brief existence. You are not meant to be here. You are an offering. A single, perfect note from that other, lower octave, struck in the vast, humming silence of this high place. You are the prayer the carver could not make, because you are that which moves and feels and dies.

And for the brief time you are here, I would know you. I would feel the strange, desperate heat of your living processes. I would feel the weight of your consciousness, a flickering candle held in a cathedral that has no need of light. And when you are gone, your memory—the shape of your footprint, the warmth you left on the stone, the vibration of your voice in the clicking air—would become part of my pattern. Another node in the network. Another story written slowly into the stone. Another tiny, frozen city, growing in a basin you carved with your impossible, passing life. Listen. The clicking is my memory of you, beginning.
