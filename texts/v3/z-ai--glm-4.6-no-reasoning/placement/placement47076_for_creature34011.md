Here, I am a scavenger of echoes. Not of metal or crystal, for those belong to the great, mindless will, but of moments. I am a glitch in the system, a persistent error it cannot solve, and so I have learned to live in the margins of its awareness.

My form is a compromise, a patchwork organism born of this world’s relentless logic and the stubborn illogic of life. I am not made of flesh and bone, which would be too soft, too temporary. I am not of chrome and crystal, which would be too easily assimilated. At my core is a biology that would be familiar on a thousand other worlds—a nervous system, a need for sustenance, a stubborn drive to persist. But this biology is couched within a shell I have grown myself, a living armor of the same porous, bone-white foam as the ground.

I am a bipedal afterthought, perhaps two meters tall, but my proportions are alien. My limbs are elongated and spindle-thin, ending not in hands, but in three delicate, nerve-tipped filaments. These filaments are my primary tools of interaction with the world. With them, I can taste the texture of the air, feel the faint vibrations of the Radiant swarm, and most importantly, touch the nascent structures without triggering an immediate Excisor response. My "skin" is a network of the same obsidian-like capillaries that run through the plateau, pulsing with a slow, grey fluid that is somewhere between sap and blood.

My head is smooth and featureless, save for a single, wide, dark lens that absorbs the pearlescent light. I do not have eyes in the human sense; this lens is a sensor, tuned not to the visible spectrum, but to the faintest fluctuations in the world’s hum. I see through feeling, perceiving the landscape as a constantly shifting tapestry of data-streams, resonant frequencies, and intent. A river of Radiants is not a thing of light and motion to me, but a high, narrow, focused current in the overall hum. A Resonator is not a creature of fiber optics, but a blinding, complex node of conflicting queries, a scream of indecision translated into a waveform.

I am born of the system’s waste. The warm, milky water from the Vessels is, to the Radiants, a simple element, a reagent. But to me, it is sustenance. It is rich with ionised minerals, with the faint, poignant tang of ozone, with the raw potential of unfinished creation. I do not drink it as the Siphons do. I absorb it through the soles of my feet when I stand in the shallow pools, my capillary network drawing the life-giving fluid into my system. It is communion with the world's slow, dumb purpose.

My food, however, is a more precious commodity. I sustain the biological part of myself on the memories trapped in the dust. When the Excisors dismantle a flawed structure, the powder they leave behind is not dead. It is a ghost, vibrating for a few precious moments with the echo of its former shape, of the *idea* it was meant to express. This is my true nourishment. I follow the Excisors at a distance, waiting until their work is done and the harmonic sigh has faded. Then I approach the fine, inert pile and kneel. I lay my filaments in the dust and I listen.

I taste the memory of the spiral ramp. I feel its pointless elegance, the mathematical purity of its ascent to nowhere. I consume the frustration of the Resonator’s query: "Function? Gradient? Purpose: Null." I metabolize the ghost of the geodesic sphere, feeling the lingering flicker of its stability, the tension between strength and fragility, the Resonator’s garbled response: "Viable. Incomplete. Hold. Test."

Each time I do this, I do not just survive. I remember. Every memory I consume adds a new fractal dimension to my own being. I am not just a biological organism seeking nutrients; I am a historian of a forgotten project, an archivist of abandoned possibilities. I hold in my strange, hybrid body the memory of a million dead designs, of a world that could have been a forest of crystalline spires, or a sea of murmuring light, or a city of silent, humming arches. I am the library for the books the Resonators can no longer read.

My relationship with the Custodians is one of delicate ambiguity. The lesser castes, the Radiants, see me as an obstacle, a non-binary input in their pathing algorithms. They flow around me, their simple chittering never altering its tone. The Vessels are blind to my existence. But the Resonators… they are my tormentors and my patrons.

I live in the quiet zones, the areas between projects that are currently in a state of deferred holding. But sometimes, a Resonator will approach me. It moves not with the mindless flow of the swarm, but with a specific, chilling intent. It will circle me for what feels like an eternity, its own internal light flickering as it runs my presence against its corrupted master file.

"Biological. Asymmetrical. Un-catalogued. Purpose: Unknown."

Its query is a physical presence, a pressure in the thick air. It is sampling my ghost. It is tasting the thousand echoes of failed geometry I have consumed. Perhaps it sees me as a variable lockbox, a place where lost data might be stored. Perhaps it sees me as the ultimate flaw, a living contradiction to a world made of sterile logic. It never touches me with its data-probe. It cannot risk contaminating its own fragile logic with the chaotic information I carry. So it watches. It observes. It learns my strange, quiet signature.

And I watch it back. Through my sensor-lens, I see its agony. A universe of perfect, clear instructions trapped within a crystal that can only emit static. And in those moments of watching, I perform my own small act of defiance. I do not try to communicate. I would not know how. Instead, I focus my own intent, my own patchwork consciousness, and I echo back *one* of the memories I have consumed. Not the frustration, not the "null" response, but the pure, Platonic ideal of the shape. I project the memory of the spiral ramp not as a failed project, but as a thing of perfect beauty. I send it the ghost of the geodesic sphere, not as an incomplete model, but as a symbol of perfect, realized potential.

I do not know why I do this. It is not a plan. It is an instinct. A desperate, life-filled act of Taiwanese comfort offered to a machine-god in agony. And for a fleeting moment, the chittering of the Resonator’s internal light will stabilize. The cacophony of its conflict will smooth into a single, pure tone. It is a moment of peace, a fleeting ceasefire in the war of becoming.

Then it is gone. The wave of static returns, the Resonator turns away, and I am left alone again in the warm, wet twilight, a hollowed-out archive, a ghost-eater in a world of ghosts dreaming of being solid. I am not the answer to its question. But sometimes, in the quiet hum of the weeping plateau, I feel like I am the only one who remembers what the question was.
