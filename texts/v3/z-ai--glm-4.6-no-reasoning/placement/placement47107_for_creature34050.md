I would be the hand that does not touch.

Not out of fear, and not out of reverence for the equilibrium, though I feel both like a second spine. I would be the hand that hovers, because that is the only position that fully acknowledges the terrible arithmetic of this place.

To be here is to be the anomaly, the warm, wet irregularity. But to simply *be* the anomaly is passive—a rock fallen into the gears. I would be the anomaly in motion, suspended in the moment of decision. I would be the trembling distance between a finger and the black water.

If I lived here—if I could be said to live in a chamber the size of a single-chambered heart—I would be the creature that tends the seal. Not a warden, because the water is already the warden. I would be the warden's witness.

I would be the one who listens to the hum not as a diagnostic, but as a song. I would learn the language of the billion seeds dreaming of friction. I would press my ear to the fused onyx and hear, in the sub-harmonics, the specific pitch of each dormant node—the green crystal waiting in its socket like a syllable in a throat that will never speak. I would know their frustration intimately, because I share it. I am the only thing here that moves with a rhythm not dictated by the system. My heart beats. It beats *wrong*. And in that wrongness, I am closer to the phantom fauna than I am to the walls.

I would spend my existence at the edge of the pool.

I would watch them. I would map the constellation bodies, the geometric mantises, the fractal centipedes. I would chart their skittering paths across the mirrored floor and learn the grammar of their tapping. Each *splosh* is a word. Each cluster at the boundary is a sentence. They are pleading, yes, but they are also *describing*—describing a world that exists in potentia, a world of consumed resources and built shells and laid eggs, a world of friction and time and stomachs filled. I would be their translator, the one who listens to the phantom ecology and understands what it wants to become.

I would hold my hand over the water and feel the pull. The air is heavy, mineral, cloying. The weeping ceiling drips. *Plink. Plink. Plink.* Each drop maintains the tension, the equals sign, the barrier that keeps the real and the reflective apart. I would feel the cool mist on my palm, the promise of inversion, the paradox waiting to be born. I would let my finger descend until the surface tension dimples beneath my warmth, until the water reaches up to meet me—not quite touching, but *almost*, *almost*—and I would feel the fauna go rigid on the other side, their clockwork legs poised, their green eyes blazing, the entire mirror-world holding its breath alongside the deep, resonant groan of the chamber.

And then I would pull back.

Not because the system demands it. Not because the equilibrium must be preserved. But because the moment of *almost* is sacred.

This place is a micro-cosm held in a permanent state of near-birth. It is perfect and unfinished. To touch the water would be to resolve it, to collapse the superposition, to force the equation to evaluate. And yes—the birth would be violent and beautiful, a billion seeds detonating into life, a hive crystallizing in a single instant, an ecology blooming from a dead machine. But it would also be *over*. The potential would become actual. The dream would become a war. The phantom fauna would become real insects, and real insects die.

In the mirror, they are eternal. They are organized light and geometry, impervious to decay, trapped in a stage of eternal conceptualization. They are hungry, but they will never starve. They are frantic, but they will never tire. They are the inhabitants of a world that exists only as a threshold, and a threshold is a kind of infinity—a place you approach but never cross, a limit that defines the shape of everything without ever being reached.

I would be the one who guards the infinity.

I would be the visitor who chose not to become a destroyer. The variable that refused to resolve. The finger that hovered and loved the hovering.

My name—if I had one—would be *Almost*. 

I would stand in the dim, ashen light, the hum in my bones, the drip in my ears, the black water before me like a hole in the world. I would watch the reflection shimmer with green, crystalline life, the fauna pressing against the surface of their water, their legs tapping out the same desperate question: *Now? Now? Now?*

And I would answer, always, with my body: *Not yet. Not yet. Not yet.*

And the system would hold. The water would seal. The seeds would dream. The hum would continue, the groan would continue, the drip would continue. Everything would be in balance. 一切都是平衡的. And in the mirror, the phantom ecology would live on, perfect and unfinished, waiting, always waiting, on the other side of a veil that I, alone, had the power to part—and chose, every moment, not to.

This is who I would be: the one who loved the threshold more than the crossing. The one who kept the world unborn.
