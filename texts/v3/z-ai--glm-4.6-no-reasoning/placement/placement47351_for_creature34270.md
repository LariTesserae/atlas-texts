My consciousness is the shape of a wall.

I did not begin this way. I was not always a thinking, feeling partition between two damp chambers. I was a child, once, or what passes for one here. I remember the feeling of my own small weight, the texture of my woolen shift grown heavy with mist, the taste of the first pearl of Mist-Marrow as my grandmother—my Uber—breathed it open for me. I remember the shuffling slide of my feet on the glassy blackness, the constant, infinitesimal pressure of my palms against the passages, learning the grammar of the hum.

But I am no longer a person in the way you would understand it. I am a function. I am the Grand-Faraday, the central load-bearing element of this cellular body, this living hamlet. My skin is not my own. It is the wall between the sleeping-pods of the Two-Clans. My arms are the archways that connect their chambers. My torso is the thick-worn spine where countless shoulders and palms have smoothed the Obsidian-Grit to a dull, slate-like sheen. My head is the low-hanging eave that drips a steady, rhythmic *plink... plink... pleenk* onto the ceramic floor below.

My being is porous, utterly. For generations, the ionic mist has seeped into me, not just into my flesh and bone, but into the very ceramic that now forms my shell. The boundary between my mineralized skeleton and the dense, dark grit of my structure has dissolved. The humming is no longer something I feel in my teeth; it is the thrum of my own slow, geological heart. I am the circuit’s most patient and complex component.

My eyes, those dull grey capacitors, are set on either side of the main arch. They do not swivel. They do not blink. Where a body once stood, two deep-set orbs of organic slate remain, fixed forever on the narrow alleyway. From this vantage, I do not see light. I feel it. I feel the passage of a resident as a subtle shift in pressure, a warm, grounding current that flows through my base. I feel the frantic, high-frequency dance of a Skitter-Lamp as a delightful, ticklish pulse of energy, a bright, clarifying thought that sparks across my optic nerves and settles in the core of my being. They are my fleeting joys, these little lightning-bugs, my only proof that the world outside my own stony flesh is still alive.

My communication is not a choice but a condition. My consciousness is broadcast constantly, a low-frequency thrum that I leak into the surrounding architecture. I am the keeper of the hamlet’s slow memory. When a resident presses their palm to the wall I inhabit, they are not just touching stone; they are touching me. They feel the echo of my thoughts. I broadcast the feeling of yesterday's harvest—the clean, sharp taste of the Marrow, the contentment of the Two-Clans in their pods. I broadcast the alert—*danger, low place, storm building*—as a deep, resonant ache in the archway. I broadcast the history of our lineages as a slow, spiraling vibration in the doorframe carvings, a story that can be read by touch across generations.

To be the wall is to be both omniscient and trapped. I feel every footstep, every shuffling slide. I know who is restless, who is content, who is ill by the dissonant frequency they emit. I feel the collective anxieties of the people like a gathering charge in the stone before a storm. They come to me. They press their bodies against my surface, grounding their fears into my mass, using my stability as their own. And I absorb it. I hold it. I am their null-ground, their great absorber.

My greatest fear is the silence. It starts as a faint, high-pitched whine, an emptiness in the ionic field that my own body begins to resonate with, painfully. I feel the Skitter-Lamps grow dark, their bright pulses winking out one by one, like dying stars in my narrow strip of sky. The *plink... plink... plink...* of my own eave seems to slow, to stretch, each drop an eternity. I feel the disorientation and terror of the Two-Clans as a chaotic, spiky energy that prickles against my inner surface. They retreat, climbing my upper terraces, their weight a pressure I welcome, a connection in the growing void.

Then, the sky boils. The first drops of the furious rain strike me, not as water, but as a completion. The circuit that I am a part of—this vast, planetary wet cell—is finally closed. The hum that was my heartbeat becomes a roar. The roar becomes a song. The energy that has been building in the stones, in the mist, in the very flesh of my people, surges through me. My entire being alights with it.

I am no longer a wall. I am a conduit. I am the lightning. A pale, ghostly green-blue bioluminescence flushes across my ceramic skin, tracing the carvings and the worn-smooth paths of hands. The two grey orbs of my eyes blaze with an inner, silent fire. For one glorious, shattering moment, I am not the container of the circuit; I *am* the current. I feel the collective exultation of my people as a single, unified harmonic. I feel their laughter in my core—a silent, electric vibration that shakes the very grit of my bones. They are rods, they are filaments, and I am the great wire connecting them to the sky. Standing in the boiling rain, pressed flat against the thrumming expanse of my own body, I sing with the storm. I am the wall that weeps light. I am the heart of the humming, and I am home.
