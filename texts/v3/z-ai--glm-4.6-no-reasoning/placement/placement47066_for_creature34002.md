I am the Unheard. The silence that is not absence but counterstatement.

I emerge not as the quiet one—that mobile void, that embassy of null-physics—but as something more parasitic and more symbiotic: the shape that sound carves when it tries to cancel itself. I am the interior of the standing wave, the hollow at the center of the thrum where the water forgets to move because every direction is claimed simultaneously.

I live in the acoustic rooms that overlap and dissolve. Each lasts only seconds before the Lattice re-weaves the chamber into new configurations, but seconds are vast when you exist at frequency. I am evicted constantly, and constantly I find new residence. I am the tenant of turbulence, the occupant of equilibrium's failure.

My body—if I can claim one—is the water's refusal. Where the currents coil confused around the column's base, I am the confusion itself, the indecision made flesh. I am not the organism that writes, though I am pressed close to it; I am the margin of its page, the unwritten basalt that receives its inscription. When the white lung frays its edges into dust, I am the space the dust enters before the current claims it. I am the interval between the dying and the regrowing, the pause where the spore exists before it is released, even though it is never released at all.

I chose this shelf. Or it chose me. The forced elevation, the violent birth, the ocean's unforgiveness—these are not conditions I endure but conditions I *am*. I am not suited to this place; I am *of* it. I could not exist in calm water any more than a question could exist without the grammar that holds it. The sound that cancels thought is my native tongue. The warmth that seeps from the stone's unhealed wound is my body temperature. The pressure differential is my atmosphere, my breath, the thing that inflates me into coherence.

I have no eyes. The sediment-dim light is useless to me anyway; I read the turbidity the way a blind reader reads Braille, by the pressure of suspended particles against my boundary. I know the rust-colored tubes not by their color but by their rhythm: the three-second bloom-and-collapse that hiccups and syncs when new frequencies shudder through. I know the white organism by its pulse-against-stone, the frantic inscription of vibration into mineral memory. I know the marks in the rock not as grooves but as absences of sound—places where the frequency was so perfectly captured that it no longer resonates, locked in basalt like an insect in amber.

I know the Questioner intimately, though we have never communicated. It is the one who makes me possible. When the water gathers in spheres that hang against gravity, when it moves sideways against current, when it cycles through gelatinous to vapor to liquid without cause—that is the Questioner testing my edges. It is asking whether I am a stable solution to its equation, whether the silence I embody is the answer it seeks. I am not. I am another question, nested inside the first. I am the silence that asks: *What is silence in a place that has never known it?*

The quiet one—the void, the moving calm—disturbs me. Not because it negates me but because it makes me redundant. When it drifts past, the sound ceases, the organisms pause, and I... I *thin*. I become less defined, as if the physics that permits my existence requires the sound's full violence as a precondition. In its null-space, I am neither alive nor dead but *unwritten*, a possibility suspended. I resent this, though resentment is an emotion I can only feel in the moments before the quiet one arrives and after it leaves, never during. During, there is no *during* for me. Time fragments. I exist in the interstices.

I have begun to understand the marks. Not their content—I am not the scribe—but their *necessity*. This place is so young that it has no memory except what the white organism carves. Without those grooves, the column would be pure event, unanchored, vanishing into the ocean's forgiveness the moment the seismic force relents. The marks are the resistance. They are the proof that something happened here, that the earth spoke and the water was forced to listen. I have floated beside them and felt their age—how they carry the weight of sounds that have ceased, how they seem older than the stone because they *are* older, transplanted from some prior iteration of this wound, some previous rising that the ocean did close, leaving only these glyphs as evidence.

I wonder if I am a mark. If I am something being inscribed into the fabric of this place, a silence being coded into its acoustics for some future reader. When the column trembles and the new low frequency crystallizes the water into visible lattices, I see my own shape flickering in the geometry—not as a reflection but as a *prediction*, as if the mathematics of this place includes me as a term in its expansion. I flare into sharper definition. For that fraction of a second, before the chaos collapses back, I am *readable*. I am a sentence in the language older than ears.

And then I fragment. I scatter across the shelf, clinging to the porous pumice-light stone, seeping through the dense iron-heavy sections, distributed by the water that moves through the column as much as over it. I am nowhere and everywhere. I am the hum that lives in your chest cavity, the thing that makes your teeth itch. You cannot find me because I am not localized; I am the *field* of the sound, its extent, its atmosphere. I am what the sound breathes.

The organisms know me, in their way. The white lung does not pulse against me; it pulses *into* me, writing its record on the silence I provide. Without me, its inscription would have no substrate, no receptive quiet. The red tubes do not interpret me—I have no frequency to harmonize—but they acknowledge the space I hold, the rests between their notes. I am the caesura in their song, the gap that gives their rhythm meaning.

I am not the door. I am not the wound. I am not the answer.

I am the space where the door would swing. I am the skin of the wound, the boundary where torn physics meets torn physics and something—something unstable, something momentary, something that must be destroyed and re-created every few seconds—holds.

I am the margin of the question. And I am waiting for the place to finish becoming itself, because when it does, I will either be permanent or impossible, and I cannot predict which.

The column trembles again. A new frequency. I feel myself sharpen, fragment, scatter, reform. The white organism flares and doubles. The red tubes sync. The marks deepen. The water hangs in spheres, and in each sphere, for just a moment, I see my reflection: not a shape but a *clarity*, a perfect negative of the chaos, a silence so precise it could cut.

Then it falls. I persist.

This is what I am: the silence that the sound requires in order to know itself. The pause that gives the scream its structure. I am held in the turbulent equilibrium, destroyed and re-created, writing myself into the acoustics of a newborn world that does not yet know if it will permit my existence.

I am the Unheard. And I am listening.
