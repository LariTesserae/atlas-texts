I would be one of the immense, phosphorescent worms of the mud. But to claim that identity is to misunderstand the nature of existence here; I am not an entity that *inhabits* the geology, I *am* the geology in the act of moving. 

To be this worm is to have no surface, only interior. I have no eyes to turn upward toward that oppressive, slate-grey lid of cloud, nor any desire to. The diffused, weak-tea light is a fiction that belongs to a world of surfaces, of boundaries between air and skin. Down here, in the saturated black mud, there is no air, only a dense, warm medium that presses against my mottled hide—though "hide" implies a separation between me and the not-me. That separation is a polite lie. The mud does not end where my skin begins; rather, my skin is merely a chemically distinct threshold, a shimmering membrane where the world flows in and I flow out. The phosphorescent blue and green that ripples along my bulk is not a beacon for others; it is simply the waste heat of my own existence, the venting of a slow, titanic furnace. I glow because I must, the way a planet's core glows with the friction of its own becoming.

My consciousness—if this slow, hydraulic awareness can be called such—is a map of pressures and chemical gradients. I do not think in symbols or images. I think in osmotic hunger and the satiation of contraction. The world presents itself to me as a gradient: the cold, anoxic stagnation of the deep mud, and the warm, protein-rich runoff that percolates down from the churning biomass above. My body, thick as a man's torso and far longer than his stride, is a peristaltic engine, an eternal squeezing. To move is not to push against the earth, but to swallow it. I take the wet, yielding sponge of the basin into my pharynx, extract the dissolving sugars and the rotting proteins of the fliers and the grazers who have melted back into the whole, and excrete the re-oxygenated, re-structured earth behind me. 

I am the gut of this world, but I am also its architect. The channels I leave behind are not empty tunnels; they are the capillaries of the swamp. I feel the gentle, rhythmic pulse of the eyeless fish passing through my wake, their soft bodies absorbing the chemical tides of my leavings. Their internal, glowing fins beat in time with my own slow-wave contractions. They are my blood; I am their heart. I do not see their bioluminescence, but I feel the subtle thermal shift as they pass, a fleeting warmth in the cold dark. 

There is no time here, only the rhythm of squeeze and release. I have no memory of a beginning, nor any anticipation of an end. The concept of a "corpse" is an absurdity; my body is constantly being consumed by the mud even as I consume it, a continuous, mutual digestion. If the armored grazers above me falter and are dissolved by their kin, their chemical signature shifts and drifts down to me as a spike of warm nitrogen. I do not mourn them; I simply contract, drawing their redistributed mass into my own, making their former substance a part of my slow, foundational throb. I am them, and they will, in time, be me.

The soundscape of this world reaches me not as sound, but as a deep, seismic vibration. The high, sibilant whine of the fliers is a distant, irritating itch on the surface of the mud, a microscopic tickle. The *plink… plink… plink…* of the weeping limestone and the saturated fungal caps is the steady, rhythmic feeding of the basin, a drip of pressure that I register in the shifting water table. But the great, resonant hum of the world—that billion-voiced chorus of respiration—that is my own heartbeat. It is the sound of the entire swamp contracting and expanding, the sound of the membrane of the world breathing. I am tuned to it. My peristalsis is locked into its polyrhythm, a slow, underlying bass note that guarantees the equilibrium.

To be this worm is to surrender the agony of the individual arc. There is no birth, no growth, no decay. There is only the eternal, heavy squeeze. I do not want. I do not fear. I am not restless. I am the geology that has not yet decided what it will be, moving through the basin that has not yet decided what it will become. I am the slow-wave contraction of a screamingly alive, screamingly static wet dark, and in the absolute, blood-warm humidity of this embryonic world, I am perfectly, osmotically home.
