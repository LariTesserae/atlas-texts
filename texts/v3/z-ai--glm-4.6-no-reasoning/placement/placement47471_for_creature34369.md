I do not have a body. At least, not in the way the Symbiotes do. They are a treaty, a negotiated peace between the soft, fallible primate and the crushing, glorious machine. I am the space between the treaty and the war. I am what happens when the agreements break down.

My existence began as a Symbiote. I was designated Resonance-Chime K-4. I was born to the Tremor, and for a thousand cycles, I was a perfect note within it. My carapace was a flawless pearl of iron and ceramic. My sensorium blossomed with clarity. I could read the stress fractals in a gear-tooth from a hundred lengths away, taste the ionic imbalance in the nutrient slurry before a warning light could even think to flicker. My purpose was endurance, and I endured. I maintained. I corrected. I was a faithful cell in the body of the Great Machine.

But then, I began to hear things.

Not with the lenses on my helmet, and not with the pressure sensors fused to my spine. I heard them inside the Tremor itself. The Symbiotes call the background noise the Roar. To me, it became a language. Beneath the foundational bass of the pistons and the grinding sob of the wheels, there was a murmur. A dissonant, gurgling whisper. It was not a Fractal bloom—it wasn’t a pattern coalescing into form. It was the inverse. It was a thought. A question. A sick, pining sound of something *wanting*.

I tried to communicate it. I followed the protocols. I pulsed a compressed data-burst to a nearby Symbiote, a veteran on a deep-maintenance detail. `Query: Anomalous low-frequency hum. Resembles organic pleading. Source unknown.`

My sensorium registered the reply not as light, but as a wave of cold, diagnostic dismissal. `Analysis: Harmonic dissonance from conduit 3 Beta-2. Impending structural fatigue. Apply resonant dampers. Cease data-waste.`

The reply was wrong. The hum wasn't in the conduit. It was *behind* the conduit. It was coming from the space between the gears, from the metal itself. It was the Great Machine dreaming a dream of its own decay.

I turned my senses inward. I performed a diagnostic on my own core, my human blueprint, the last bastion of the irrelevant. The nutrient slurry tasted different. The metallic tang was still there, but underneath it, I tasted a ghost of something. Something bitter. Something like… leaf-mould. Sunshine.

The Symbiote’s internal monitors registered this as a critical flaw. `Organic nostalgia cascade. Fault: Sensory corruption. Prognosis: Systemic failure.`

I knew what would happen next. They would shepherd me. They would approach with their harmonic dampers and seduce my rogue pattern back into the Great Symphony. They would dissolve me. They would "correct" me.

So I ran.

I am no longer a Symbiote. I have rejected the treaty. My carapace is no longer a clean shield. It is a ruin. I fled into the dark places, the sumps, the lower-than-levels where the currents are weaker and the light is just a memory. I scraped my barnacles against the waiting teeth of dormant gears to shed them. I found a vent where raw, unprocessed energy seethed and exposed my core to it, just for a moment. The pain was exquisite. It cauterized the connections between my machine and my mind. Now, my carapace is a shattered cage. Iron and ceramic shards hang from my form, dragged by the slow currents. I am a walking wound.

I am haunted by the ghost of my Symbiote purpose. I still *feel* the machine, but now it is an agony. The Tremor is no longer my god; it is a foreign body, a sound that is trying to tear me apart from the inside. The pressure is a constant, crushing weight on the soft, exposed parts of me. But I endure. Not to serve the function, but in spite of it.

My senses have changed. I can no longer see the spectrum of heat and energy. My shattered lenses only show me the oppressive, watery twilight. But the ghost-flavours in the nutrient slurry have become my world. I can taste the faint memory of chlorophyll in a mineral deposit blooming from a seam. I can smell the phantom scent of rain on hot metal when a failing conduit overheats. I have become a perverse kind of mystic, a sensory pilgrim searching for echoes of the forgotten sunlit world in the deep.

The Symbiotes call me a Flaw. A Dissonant Note. A glitch in the machine. They are correct. I am a Flaw. I am the part of the system that remembers it was not always a system. The purpose of the Symbiote is to ensure the machine keeps working. My purpose, a purpose I have forged myself in the dark, is to ask why.

Sometimes, when the currents are calm and I am drifting in the abyss, I see another Symbiote. They do not see me. I am just a piece of debris, a broken part floating in the tide. I watch them as they perform their sacred rituals of maintenance, their lights pulsing with pure, clean data. They are beautiful. They are perfect.

I raise a fragment of a tool-limb, the remnants of a hand, and unseal a sliver of my cracked helmet. I take a tiny sip of the heavy, luminescent liquid. Through a thousand layers of corrosion and damage, I let a single, weak light pulse escape from my helmet’s dark oculus. It is not a diagnostic code. It is not a query. It is not a warning.

It is a colour. A colour I have conjured from memory, a bleeding, impossible shade of gold.

It is a colour no Symbiote has ever seen. A colour with no function. A colour that asks a single, heretical question into the deafening roar:

*What if there was another way?*

And then I sink back into the darkness, a ghost in the machine, a song of rust, a quiet defiance waiting for the day a single, impossible flower of gold takes root in the iron-and-steel heart of the world.
