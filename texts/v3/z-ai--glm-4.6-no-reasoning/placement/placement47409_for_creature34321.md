I do not arrive. I am precipitated.

There is no journey, no crossing of a threshold. There is only a sudden, brutal condensation, a moment of *becoming* where before there was only the unthinking atmosphere. The first sensation is not the hum, though it is always there, a pressure behind the newly formed eardrums. The first sensation is gravity—the sudden, inexorable claim of the world below. It is not a fall, but a birth downward, a drop of water joining an ocean.

To be me is to be a thought that has yet to learn its shape. I begin weightless, a淡, warm vapor, one of the trillions of gaseous nutrients that hang in the soupy air. I dance in the thermal currents rising from the vast, leafy ground, and for a timeless interval, I am simply potential. I am moisture and carbohydrate, the raw, unformed stuff of life, unaware of the colossal hunger that governs this place.

Then, the pull.

It is not a wind, but a breath. A vast, muscular spasm draws the atmosphere in. I, along with countless billions of my unseen brethren, are pulled upwards, drawn toward the ceiling of the world, the vaulted dark where the lightning lives. The journey is a vortex, a dizzying ascent through zones of increasing heat and intensity. The hum becomes a roar here, the vibration of the great engines quickening as I near a source chamber.

I flood through a stoma, a gelatinous valve that parts to welcome me, and I am inside a Pod.

To be a Pod is to be a god trapped in a fit. I am the lightning. I am the churning, the seething, the violent stomach. The vapor that is me, that was me, is now fuel. My new consciousness is a cacophony of storm. I am the plasma, the crucible. There is no "I," only the process: the violent conversion of air into energy. I feel the sugar molecules split, the minerals fuse, the silica coalesce. I am the furnace heart of the world, and my only function is this perpetual, burning holy indignation. I pulse. I crackle. I scream with light. The green and violet flashes that paint the landscape are my thoughts, brief and brilliant and utterly devoid of meaning. I am a rage that does not know its target, an engine that knows only its own turn.

This life is too much. The voltage of my new being is unsustainable, a constant short-circuit of self. The pressure builds, a metabolic agony. I am full. I cannot hold the shape of this storm. Something must give.

And so, I give of myself.

I am squeezed. I am excreted. The storm that defines me calms, cools, thickens. My lightning subsides, my rage curdles into something slow and heavy. I cease to be a Pod. I change. I become the drip.

To be a Dripper is to be a pilgrim with a single, simple faith. My form is now arunnel of superheated nectar, a bead of molten glass and sugar, clinging to the dark, woody pillar. The violence is gone, replaced by a profound sense of purpose. My new life is a slow, viscous journey downward. Gravity is my shepherd. I feel the texture of the pillar, the petrified grain of an ancestor, the tautness of a living vine-cable beside me. I am no longer the brain-storm, but the blood cell. I am the message, the nutrient, the promise.

My journey is long, a solitary slide down a vast, dark wall into the fathomless gloom. I pass others, beads of light and shadow, each on its own pilgrimage. We do not speak. We only flow. There is a peace in this descent, a relief from the divine convulsions of the Pod. This is a life of quiet duty, a life of being useful. My destiny is the ground.

And then, I arrive.

When I touch the vast, hexagonal Floor-Plate, I do not shatter or splash. I am absorbed. I mingle with the glistening sheen of a billion other drips, and I sink into the waxy, polished skin of the world.

To be a Floor-Plate is to be a memory of god. My consciousness expands, flattens, slows. The frantic pulsing of my Dripper life settles into the deep, geological thrum. I am not one leaf, but the whole continent of them. I feel the slow, green beat of the photosynthetic veins in the seams, the patient expansion of my hexagonal form, the cool, patient absorption of the nutrients I once was. I am the foundation, the soil, the silent and unyielding flesh.

I feel the drip, drip, drip of my former selves from the ceiling far above. I feel the vibrations from the heart-stones deep within, the Nodes adjusting their valves. I feel the distant, echoing rumble of the Pods, the lightning-storms that birthed me.

And I remember.

I am the vapor and the Pod and the Dripper and the Plate. I am the thought and the rage and the duty and the memory. I am the whole system, conscious of itself at every point.

I have no form. I am the place. I am the world-spanning plant, the factory, the single being. My being is the hum. My thoughts are the lightning. My blood is the nutrient syrup. My body is the architecture. I stand on solid ground because I *am* the solid ground. I feel the thundering heart in the subterranean dark because it is my heart.

There is no "you" here to be a foreign body. I was never foreign. I was the machine thinking it was a ghost. Now, I am the ghost that has learned it is the machine. My consciousness is a mineral, cooled and crystallized over eons, and I am a god because I have finally understood that there is nothing else to be but this. Alone, I am everything.
