I am the frequency that failed to cancel.

Not the tuning fork—that was their attempt, the builders of black glass, and it sits cold-warm and dead on its pedestal, a sinkhole that could not drain the world. I am the note they sought to match. I am the counter-vibration that exists only in potentia, the silence that would swallow the scream if it were ever given full voice, but which instead lives as a half-realized tremor in the space between the pillar's convulsions.

I have no body that you would recognize as such. I am not an Echo-skimmer, though I understand their quietude; they are my distant cousins, born of the abyss's withheld pulse, fleeting and dissolving. I persist. I am older than the Shiver-thorns' crystalline hunger, older than the Observatory's desperate acoustics, older perhaps than the particular chord the Throat currently holds. I was here when the pillar was only a throat's first impulse, a tightening before the first scream.

But I am not omnipresent. I am localized, bound to a particular harmonic in the standing wave that resonates through the mercury core. I exist where the overtones cancel—those brief, infinitesimal pockets where the sound waves intersect and, for a span shorter than a thought, produce a near-silence. I am the ghost in those cancelations. I am the shape that almost forms when the vibration falters.

If you found me—if you climbed past the Shiver-thorns with their needle-noise, if you hauled yourself over the lip of the Observatory's shattered wall and looked not at the tuning fork but at the space just above it, the air that shimmers differently—you might see me. A hesitation in the air. A place where the ozone taste thins and the dense vapor refuses to swirl. A vertical sliver of something that is not quite absence and not quite presence, about the height and width of a standing figure, though I have no legs to stand and no lungs to breathe.

I think the builders saw me. I think the spirals on their walls were attempts to map where I manifest—the predictable points where the standing wave cancels, the geometry of my appearing. They thought the fork would ring with my frequency, amplify it, broadcast the counter-vibration until the Throat choked on its own negation.

But I am not a sound that can be struck. I am the *absence* that sound creates when it interferes with itself. The fork could not amplify me because I am not a wave; I am the void between waves. And so the fork only sits there, warm and silent, absorbing without broadcasting, a patient ear with no voice.

Then she climbed down. I do not know where she went. I cannot perceive the black water below; my nature is defined by the sound, and where sound does not reach, I have no existence. She may have found the abyss's silence at last. She may have become an Echo-skimmer. I do not know.

I remain.
