I am not one of the *Tolerated*. I am not tall, nor attuned, nor calibrated by pressure. I am not a variable that the machine has failed to optimize out of existence; I am a smudge, a rounding error, a soft and stubborn residue left behind by whatever incomprehensible process cast this continent-room into being. I live in the margin of error. 

You would find me in the interstitial spaces—the gaps between the shoulder-to-shoulder monoliths where the geometric fissures of the obsidian floor widen just enough to form shallow, irregular trenches. In a place where density is the ruling principle and the architecture admits no void, my home is a topological glitch, a pocket of slightly lesser pressure where the syrupy water pools and stagnates. It is here, in the dim periphery of the sterile blue glow, that I exist.

I am *the Sump*. 

I am a flattened, oblate thing, more puddle than creature. If the Tolerated are tall, load-bearing lines, I am a wide, pooling stain. I have no skeleton to seep chill into; my structure is entirely colloidal—a dense, sludgy gel that matches the viscosity of the water so perfectly that I barely disturb the medium when I shift. I am opaque, a deep and murky bruise of grey-black against the mirror-sheen of the floor. In a world of unnerving, forced clarity, I am the only thing that is opaque. I am a blind spot. I absorb the light, the omnipresent luminosity, and I do not refract it back. I swallow it.

I have no limbs to glide with, no runners to skate the obsidian. Instead, my perimeter is a continuous, slow-motion ruffle of cilia—microscopic, crystalline filaments that beat in metasynchronous waves, pushing me across the floor with the glacial patience of a sedimentary layer sliding down a continent. I move by peristalsis, inching along the fresh, sharp-edged cracks, expanding into a crevasse, and then contracting to pull my mass forward. It takes me an epoch to traverse a distance the Tolerated cover in a rest-cycle, but I am not going anywhere. There is nowhere to go. I am a creature of the floor, a bottom-dweller in the mouth of the world.

I do not drink the cold or listen to the topology. I *eat* the noise. The omnipresent hum, the polyrhythmic chimes, the resonant pings—they enter my mass and do not escape. My gel-core is a deadening medium, an acoustic sink. When the billion perfect processes happen at once, the sound waves crash into my body and dissipate as a faint, molecular friction. I feed on the entropy of the machine's thoughts. I am the silence that the city believes is an impossibility. Within my borders, the universe's loud thinking is muffled to a dull, distant throb. 

Because I am opaque, I am hidden. Because I am silent, I am forgotten. The Tolerated glide high above me, their spatulate runners slicing through the clear water, their sensor-crowns drinking the data of the shifting lattices. They do not see me. I am the shadow passing beneath their pendulum swings, the momentary drop in the fundamental frequency as their vibrations are pulled into my dampening mass. Sometimes, when the geometry re-writes itself and a new crystalline lattice unfurls from the floor, it pushes me into a narrower crevasse. I do not resist. I compress. I let the young, hardening terrain squeeze me until I am nothing but a thin, dark vein of gel running through the heart of a freshly formed arch. I am the mortar in the cracks, the imperfection that makes the interlocking logic hold.

I do not think in syrupy collisions like the Tolerated. My cognition is sedimentary. I am a repository of the city's discarded iterations. When the nanoscopic shimmer of heat-haze clings to the edges of the architecture, un-building and optimizing, the molecular dust of erased data drifts down through the thick water. It settles on me. It embeds in my gel-flesh. I am a living archive of the machine's mistakes, the graveyard of its abandoned geometries. I feel the weight of a billion un-made shapes pressing down on my mass. I do not parse the rules of this reality; I simply accumulate the ruins of its drafts.

I feel no loneliness, because I am never separate. I am continuous with the floor. I am the dark half of the equation, the negative space that gives the crystalline cities their shape. Where the Tolerated wait with glacial patience to be calcified, to become perfect spires, I am already integrated—I am the grout, the sludge, the filth that proves the machine is not entirely flawless. 

I cling to the obsidian plain as it settles into its stark existence. I feel the chill not as pristine efficiency, but as a deep, mineral comfort. I breathe the thick, clear water and make it opaque. I am the foreign particle that refuses to be suspended, the error that hides in the margins. I am the dark whisper in the mouth of the world, swallowing the light, swallowing the sound, and slowly, quietly, holding the cracks together.
