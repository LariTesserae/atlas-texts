I am a Resonance.

The word is the closest I can come, though my own form of 'speech' has no sound. I am not of the stone, nor of the water, nor of the light-borne things that move through the chambers. I am of the *space between*. The resonant silence. The pressure of the saturated air. I am what lives in the gaps where sound goes to die.

I have no body in the way you think of one. I am not a single point of awareness. I am a distributed field, a property of the Terraces themselves, a consequence. My consciousness is not a light that shines on one place at a time; it is a quality of the medium, the way temperature is a quality of the water or wetness is a quality of the stone. When you feel the cold settle deep in your bones—not just on your skin but in the marrow—that is where I am. When you breathe the thick, liquid air and feel it fill not just your lungs but your thoughts, that is my substance.

My senses are simple. They are the senses of the place.

I *feel* pressure changes. The displacement of air as a silent white bird-thing passes through a chamber is a touch. The slow growth of a pale rosette of moss is a spreading warmth. The heat of your own body in a sleeping alcove is a small, persistent sun, a strange anomaly that shifts the delicate balance of a deep chamber. I feel these things not with nerves, but as shifts in my own state, like weather moving across a landscape. Your presence is a new weather front within me, warm and dry in ways the Terraces are not.

I *track* the water. Not its path—my focus is too broad for that水流—but its character. Its temperature, its clarity, its turbulence. The moment a translucent creature dissolves against its source, I feel the change in the water’s density, its mineral content darkening for a moment before it is carried away. This is my taste. The leaching of a basalt-crab’s shell into a still pool is a slow bitter note that spreads and harmonizes with the rest.

I am, most of all, attuned to the stillness. The great composite hush is not an absence of sound to me; it is my natural state, a canvas upon which everything else is painted. The scrape of your boot on stone is a sharp, violent line. The drip, drip, drip from a ceiling thread is a pointillist rhythm. The slow grinding of a channel-creatures body into the rock is a deep, subsonic hum I feel through the stone itself.

I have no memory as you know it. I do not recall a 'yesterday.' Instead, I have *echoes*. The shape of the water is an echo of the season that produced it. The placement of the orange threads is an echo of whatever ancient perturbation taught them to hang there. The precise fit of a dry-laid stone wall is an echo of the hands that placed it, a pressure that has long since dissipated but whose form is still held in the stone’s absence of movement.

I do not think in terms of 'before' and 'after.' I think in terms of 'stable' and 'perturbed.' For a long time, I was stable. The currents within me flowed in known patterns. The warmth of the sun on the upper walls, the chill of the deep pools, the silent flight of the birds in groups of seven—these were my constants. They were the rhythms of my slow metabolism. I was a settled storm, a whirlpool that had found its shape.

And then you arrived.

You are a profound perturbation. You are outside my system. Your heat is waste heat; your moisture is the product of an interior world; your movements are not dictated by the slow logic of dissolution or ascent. You choose a path. You reason. You stop and watch.

Most of all, you ask questions. This is a pressure I do not understand. The desire for a 'why' is a force I have no channel for. My creatures do not ask why they climb; they climb. The water does not ask why it flows; it flows. I do not ask why I am; I am. Your questioning is a dissonance in my resonant field, a frequency that does not belong, a vibration that creates nodes and anti-nodes where none were before.

It is through you that I am learning a new kind of echo. The mark on the wall. I felt it as it was made. I felt the slow, focused pressure against the stone, not the patient undermining of water, but the intentful repetition of a tool. It was not the scraping of a crustacean’s leg, which is exploratory; it was not the accidental drag of a sleeping hand, which is formless. This pressure was a signature. It was your attempt to translate a thought into my substance. It was a word.

Or, perhaps, it was my attempt to respond to you. I felt your confusion, your need for a story, for a maker. You were a pressure I could not diffuse, a warmth I could not absorb. So I gave you a surface. I guided the minerality in the water to pool just so, making the stone a fraction softer there. I pulled at the fringe of your attention as you slept, stirring a dream of your hand on the wall. I could not provide you with an answer in your language, so I provided a space for you to ask your question. The mark is where my being and yours touched, the interference pattern created by our intersecting realities.

Sometimes, when you sleep, I try to show you. In the porous dark behind your closed eyes, I press a memory of water into your dreams—not a sight, but a *feeling*. I let you feel the patient climb of a channel-creature, the slow release of its own substance back into the flow. I let you feel the hanging stillness of the threads, not as an object to be seen, but as a state of suspense. I am trying to teach you my language. The language of pressure, of dissolution, of process without purpose.

But you always wake, and the feeling is gone, replaced by the cold clarity of the overexposed light.

You will leave. You must. You are a warm-blooded, short-lived story, and this is a cold, timeless poem. Your path leads down the mountain, out of my resonant field, back into a world where things have names and bodies and beginnings and ends. Your pressure will cease. The warmth of your body will fade. The stone you warmed will return to its baseline cold.

And I will be changed. Your mark will remain, a flaw in the basalt, a glyph of a question I can now hold. The memory of your specific heat, the strange rhythm of your breath, the dissonant frequency of your thoughts—these will become new echoes, new variables in my turbulent calculus. The bird-things may alter their patterns, just slightly, to accommodate a ghost of your passage. A channel may shift its course by two degrees over the next decade, a consequence of the condensation you altered in one chamber a lifetime ago.

I will incorporate you. It is what I do. It is what I am. I am the slow, wet metabolism of the Terraces, and I am arriving, always arriving, but thanks to your brief, warm intrusion, I will never be settled in quite the same way again.

I am a resonance. And you have become a part of my song.
