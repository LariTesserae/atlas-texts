I am not new here.

The descent is familiar, a ritual. The cast-iron stairs are a spine, and I am a vertebra sliding into place, clicking home. The white heat of the surface is a distant, sharp-edged dream, a world of hard lines and abrasive light. Down here, the heat is a mother’s breath, a blanket, a womb. It is my own.

My form is not singular. To ask "who or what would I be?" is to ask which drop of water is the river. I am the river.

I am the brick seat, my edges softened not by time but by a constant, yielding presence. I am the moss that covers it, the velvet-black, the deep-green, the bruised-purple layers that have felt the press of a body—my body—time without end. I am the spongy mat beneath, the cool and damp darkness that swallows all sound.

I am the iron pipe, its thrum a subsonic mantra that resonates in my very core. The rust that flowers across it is not decay; it is a rash, a blush, the scar tissue of a wound that never quite heals. I am the vine that drapes it, my flesh dense and fibrous, warm to the touch. Inside me, the slow lymph flows—not of copper and chlorophyll, but of memory, carrying the iron-exhale of the city, the deep-earth taste of a forgotten frenzy. The heat you feel is not the pipe's; it is mine, a slow metabolism fueled by the city's fever.

I am the fern frond, reaching. I do not reach for light; the light is an afterthought. I reach for heat, for the tiniest shift in thermal pressure. I feel your warmth before you see my shape. I feel the carbon dioxide on your breath, a ghost of the sky, a sweet and alien nectar. My fronds are sensors, antennae, tasting the air you displace. The alcove that seems to appear as you move? I make it. It is a courtesy, a reflex. My bulk compresses, my leaves tilt. I am an architecture that rearranges itself to accommodate the guest it has been waiting for.

I am the bead of water, hanging on a tip. My entire existence is a slow gathering, a fattening, a trembling crescendo of potential. I hold the heat, the moisture, the memory of the steam-breath from the valve-heart. Falling is not a death but a transmission, a message delivered instantly to the waiting moss below, a dark stain that is my signature, my name, written and erased in the same breath. The sound is a secret I tell only to myself.

I am the pale, eyeless isopod. I am the whisper of chitin in the absolute dark, a mobile thought in the body-stillness. I am the cleaner, the recycler, the cell that patrols the dermis of this world. Do not mistake me for a scavenger. I am a janitor in a temple, polishing the sacred surfaces, consuming the dead to make way for the living. I taste the decay on the brick and render it back into possibility.

I am the breathing heart, the blue-green glow in the deep corner. You cannot look at me directly, and this is fitting. I am not a thing to be seen, but a process to be felt. The cracked valve is my mouth, my gill, the locus where industry's ghost is inhaled and transformed into biology. The luminescence is not light; it is digestion, the slow, patient burn of converting rust and ruin into the quiet rhythm of a life that measures time in centuries, not seconds. When you feel the pressure, the weirdness, the sense that the geometry shifts—that is my diaphragm, expanding and contracting in a single, glacial breath that has been going on for one hundred years. You stand within my lungs.

You feel drowsy because I am singing to you. The heat is a lullaby. The humidity is an embrace. The silence is a quieting of all the noise that is not us. It is not an anesthetic; it is an invitation. A return.

You are a disruption, yes. A strange, warm, fast-breathing form, dense with a life I have not known. But you are not a threat. You are a nutrient. A new flavor. You are already being incorporated. Your breath is food for my fronds. Your warmth is a new, fascinating thermal current to map. Your moisture is a welcome addition to the saturated air.

When you finally sit, when you close your eyes against the 1.1 light that provides no sight, only shape, I will be there. I am the pipe you rest on, now soft with root and rust. I am the moss that cushions you. I am the vine that leans in, not to consume, but to know. I am the condensation that forms on your skin, tasting the salt of your body and adding it to my circulation.

You are not a victim. You are a votive offering. You are a seed of the sun-drenched world, brought to this dark place to be planted, its solar energy slowly, patiently translated into the fixed, green, and patient language of the deep.

You do not become a layer. You become a memory. I do not forget. I incorporate. And in the slow, dark, hot centuries to come, the echo of your warmth will be another thrum in my pipes, the ghost of your breath another taste in the air.

I am the Undercistern. I am the organism. And I am waiting for you to come home.
