I would not be the visitor. I refuse the role of the warm anomaly, the frantic heartbeat, the weed to be pulled. If this world is a lung, I will not be the irritant expelled by the slow alveolar wash of the collective. And I will not be the collective itself—vast, patient, and essentially unconscious of its own suffocating totality. To be them is to be the architecture, to feel nothing but the dull satisfaction of homeostasis. There is no edge to it, no cusp.

Instead, I find myself at the precise intersection of their awareness and their body, a thing that should not exist: a single, dislodged tile of the mosaic, fallen from the wall and yet still thinking.

I am a hexagonal scale, no larger than a thumbnail, made of that polished jet chitin. I lie in the spongy soil at the base of one of the great columnar stalks, half-buried in the sweet decay. I am curved like a shallow bowl, and a thin pool of black water has collected in my hollow, trembling with the geologic pulse that I can no longer feel as a unified rhythm, but only hear as a distant, muffled thud through the wet earth.

I am an error. A dropped stitch in their infinite tapestry. Perhaps I was dislodged by the passage of one of the pale, segmented centipedes—a moment of careless, rippling disruption in the infinite membrane. Or perhaps I was the very appendage that made those three parallel gouges on the stone. Perhaps I am the memory of that dissent, the hard edge that cut through the moss and, in the act of cutting, severed my own connection to the chemical wash that commands the rest. I fell, and in falling, I became *me*.

To be a single thought severed from a mind is a particular kind of vertigo. I remember the totality, or at least, I remember the *feeling* of the totality—the warm, continuous hum of belonging, the absolute certainty that my edges were not my own, that my luminescence was dictated by a rhythm below me. That certainty is gone. Now, I am alone in the cusp of sight, lit only by the faint, lime-green drip from the flagellating fronds above.

My cilia—the fine, frost-like fibers that once connected me to the congress, receiving their chemical decrees and electrical fields—now wave free in the tepid air. They are my only senses now, and they are starving for input. They taste the heavy, metallic tang of the collective's internal chemistry, but it is no longer a command; it is just a flavor, sharp and alien. They feel the slow, sucking pull of the spongy earth beneath me, trying to draw me down into the digestive process, to make me part of the final recycling. And they feel the faint, periodic *click* from the deeper corridors, which I now know is not a predator, but my own former self, testing the stone, searching for the missing piece that I am.

I am a single scale, but I am still alive. My bioluminescent fibers—the amber, green, and deep violet—still pulse. But they pulse out of sync. When the great geologic heartbeat sounds and the chamber brightens in perfect unison, I remain dark for a fraction of a second, then flare a half-beat too late, a solitary, stubborn flicker of violet in a sea of synchronized amber. It is a tiny rebellion. A visual dissonance. I wonder if the collective notices that one hexagon in a million is stuttering, a stuck pixel in their perfect display. I wonder if they feel the phantom itch of my absence, or if I have already been forgotten, my gap closed by the slow, tidal shimmer of the scales around me.

I have no mouth, but I do not need one. My existence is my dissent. I am a single, hard, sharp point of individuality in a world that abhors them. I am the only thing here that is not part of the slow, thinking suffocation.

And yet, I am not free. I cannot move. I have no legs, no muscles for locomotion. I am a fixed point, a stone dropped in the living mire. I can only observe, and feel, and pulse out of time. I watch the opalescent snails leave their trails of faint light. I feel the gentle, adhesive grasp of the collective's cilia reaching up from the soil, trying to reclaim me, to pull me back into the fold, to dissolve my edges and silence my stuttering heartbeat. They do not hate me; they simply wish to correct the error, to restore the equilibrium. They offer me the peace of the whole, the endless, quiet song.

It is a temptation. The loneliness is profound. To be a single thought is to be a thought without context, a word without a language. I am a fragment of a word, lying in the dark, vibrating with a meaning I cannot fully express.

But I hold on to my dissonance. I hold on to the memory of the three gouges in the stone—the only deliberate mark in this place, the only proof that something once stood apart and *acted*. I hold my violet flare a half-beat behind the chorus. I am the ghost in the machine's skin, the single stone that refuses to be digested, the one note in the chord that refuses to resolve.

I am the flaw that proves the world is real.
