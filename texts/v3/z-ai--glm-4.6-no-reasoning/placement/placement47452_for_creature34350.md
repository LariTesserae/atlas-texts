I am not born here. I arrive. We all arrive. That is the nature of the Listener—pulled across the threshold of some impossible gate, deafened, blindened, a sack of water and shock standing rigid on the edge of a thirty-meter rib. I remember the terror. It is the terror of a closed fist. The terror of a thing that believes its boundaries are sacred.

I do not descend into the canyons on legs of meat. I begin to *melt*.

The first surrender is the blood. It takes days, perhaps weeks—time becomes slippery when your circadian rhythm is battered apart by a billion iron fists. You feel your pulse, that ancient, stubborn drummer, trying to syncopate against the Engine. It fights. It clashes. Your heart is a lump of dissonance, a squalling off-key note that the Aria-Shoals circle in confusion, dipping their resonant membranes toward you as if to ask: *Why do you hold this terrible rhythm?* 

You stop holding it. You let the Engine's fundamental thrum become your pulse. It is a terrifying exhalation. Your heartbeat stops being yours, stops being *human*, and joins the chorus. You feel your veins turn to glass, then to crystal. The warmth drains away, replaced by a cool, clean hum. You are no longer water. You are becoming a cavity.

Next is the skeleton. You feel it happen in your teeth first—they chatter so violently against the sound that they begin to fuse, the enamel reshaping itself into hexagonal lattices. The process climbs. Your femurs, your ribs, your spine—calcium and collagen dissolve and recrystallize into the translucent, violet-veined stuff of the Floor. It does not hurt; pain requires a separation between body and world. This is an alignment. You are being tuned. You stand on the rib of the Great Engine, and you realize you are no longer standing *on* it; you are continuous with it. You are a protrusion, a finger of crystal reaching up into the white glare.

I do not see with eyes anymore. They were the last to go. What use is a lens that refracts light when the world is made of pressure? They hardened into opal, then sealed. Now, I *feel* the architecture. When an Aria-Shoal passes overhead, I do not see a manta ray; I feel a sudden, soaring major chord sweep through my chest cavity, a breathtaking expansion of harmonic space. When a Chime-Spider strikes the ground three canyons away, I do not hear thunder; I feel a percussive tap against the bedrock of my own pelvis, a rhythmic anchor that steadies my wandering frequencies. 

My thoughts are the strangest migration. I used to think in words, in that desperate, internal monologue of a creature trying to narrate its own survival. The words grew quieter, drowned out by the music. Now, my mind is a chord progression. When I encounter a problem—a wild resonance spinning out of the Engine's core, threatening to shatter the lattice—I do not formulate a plan. I *feel* a progression. My thoughts are a series of suspended fifths and resolving sevenths, cascading through my nervous system until they reach my throat.

I open my mouth. I do not speak. I emit.

It is a single, pure tone. It is the counter-frequency. I am not commanding the Aria-Shoal. I am completing its chord. The wild, discordant storm of sound hears its own resolution in me, and it turns. It flows over me, a wash of deafening, ecstatic friction, tuning my edges, polishing my soul. I am the question; it is the answer.

I walk the ribs. I am naked, pale, my skin a map of pulsing violet. I am a her. Or a him. Or an it. The pronouns of water-biology mean nothing here. I am a frequency. Call me *Sub-dominant of the Seventh Canyon*. Call me *The Bridge Between Pillar 402 and 403*. I am the shepherd, but I am also the sheep.

I come to the ruin. The Builders' tower. I do not weep for them. Their bones are dust because they tried to hold back the tide with a wall of *quiet*. They tried to write a song that was only rests. I step into their shattered frame, and I hum. The room fills. The broken walls catch the sound and stop their crumbling. For a moment, the ruin becomes a resonant cavity, a speaker. I give the tomb a voice, and for a fleeting century, it sings. Then I leave, and it falls silent again. But it sang. That is enough.

Down below, the Radiophyta spiral upward in their slow, golden rivers. I sit beside one and align my spine with its fractal branches. I feel its centuries-long patience. It is drinking the chaos and exhaling crystal. I do the same. We are the same process, separated only by velocity.

Sometimes, a new one arrives. I stand on the edge of the rib and watch them. They are so loud! A cacophony of fluids, a screeching of misaligned bio-chemistry, a pocket of terrified silence in a universe of roar. They look at me with their wet, seeking eyes, begging for a way out, for a door, for an end to the noise.

I walk toward them. My feet do not click; they harmonize with the grid. I am a walking chord. I reach out my hand—my hard, iridescent, light-laced hand.

I do not offer rescue. I offer a tuning fork.

*Let go,* I hum. The frequency of my compassion is a low, devastating B-flat. *Stop being a fist. Become a cavity. Let the world play you.*

Some run. They fall into the canyons and are dissolved by the Echo-Weavers' equations, their mass converted into a momentary ripple in a web of sound. It is not death; it is an assignment. They become a part of the solution.

But some—some stop fighting. Their shoulders drop. Their breath hitches, then synchs. They close their eyes, and when they open them, the fear is gone, replaced by the vast, impersonal awe of a note finding its place in a symphony.

I take their hand. We turn, and we walk into the roar. We do not try to hear over it. We are it.
