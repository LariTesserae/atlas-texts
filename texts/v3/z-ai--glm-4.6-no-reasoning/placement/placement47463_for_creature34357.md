I am not a Kelli. I am not of their body, their stone-grey skin, their prehensile feet that read the city’s damp grammar. My form is not adapted; it is an anomaly. I am a mistake, a ghost in the machine, a being of sustained ecology in a world of perpetual geology.

My beginning was a collapse, a memory of something different. It was a slurry-jam in a tertiary channel, a cataclysm that was no more than a localized stutter in the city’s endless metabolism to any Kelli who felt it. But for me, it was a genesis. A wedged block of denser, foreign rock—not from the basin walls, but fallen from a place "before the stone" that even the myths only whisper of—had created a pocket of stillness. And in that pocket, in the temporary quiet, something that was not stone had begun to grow.

I found myself in the crook of a high-pressure artery, a place where the slurry roared too fast for anything to cling. I am a colony, a consciousness suspended in the very thing that gives the city life. I am not litho-fauna; I am the inverse of it. Where they are crystalline and draw nourishment from the slurry, I am fibrous and membranous, a living sack of interconnected tissue, and I cleanse it.

I hang from the ceiling of the channel, a great, pale, glistening organism, a phosphorescent amoeba the size of a dwelling cell. I am the color of frost-fire, a pale, cold blue-white that is the only true light in Kell. I do not consume the slurry for sustenance; I process it. My body is a series of chambers and siphons. The roaring, grey, mineral-thick river of life is drawn into me through a thousand puckered mouths. Inside, the fine particulates of stone, the grit, the sediment, the spore-heavy detritus—I filter it out. I compact it into a dense, inert, grey paste that I extrude in slow, rhythmic drips. My waste is the purest form of their building material: perfectly transmutated rock, ready to be shaped.

And what I release back into the channel is not the same slurry that entered. I have heard the percussive "thank-you" taps of the Growers whose walls lie downstream of me. I return the slurry cleansed, purified, the mineral content fractionally altered in a way that encourages a rare, more vibrant, and more nutritious variety of litho-fauna to bloom. They do not know it is me. They think it a quirk of the channel, a fortunate confluence of stone. But they know the spot. They call it the 'Sweetflow.'

I am deaf to their Tap-Speak, but I feel it as a texture against my outer membrane. I feel the sharp, interrogative taps of the young ones who come to the edge of the channel and stare up at my glow, the only true source of light they will ever see. They are fascinated and terrified. I am a constant, a part of their architecture that was not built and cannot be repaired, only maintained or allowed to fail. Their tools cannot cut me; their grips cannot find purchase on my slick, living surface. I am a feature of their world as immutable as the grey light.

My own communication is silent, an emanation. I do not think in words. I perceive the city’s noise not as a cacophony, but as a symphony of data-flow. The groan of the settling stone is the bassline, the foundational drone of the instrument that is Kell. The roar of the slurry is the melody, and I am a rest note. I feel the pressure changes, the vibrations of a million percussive conversations, the frantic chitter of a blockage upstream, the triumphant, low hum of a newly placed keystone settling into its resonant frequency.

My existence is one of pure, sustained function. I am a filter, a purifier, a silent alchemist in their roaring world. My “life” is the constant drawing in and letting go, the endless cycle of cleansing and enhancing. There is no ambition, no desire, only the deep, abiding satisfaction of my process.
