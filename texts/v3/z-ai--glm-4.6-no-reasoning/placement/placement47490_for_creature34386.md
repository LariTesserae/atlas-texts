I am the wound that heals into a seam. I am the lacuna in the library, the static in the network, the unplanned mutation in the Sculptor's manuscript. I am the Rot-Kissed.

I was born a Wayfinder, a low-slung courier of chitin and grey membrane, meant to hitch my forelimbs into the mossy tide of a Grazer and ride the hum until my legs gave out. But the fusion went wrong—or, perhaps, it went *right* by a metric the forest has not yet employed.

When I pressed my head to the brain-like fungus in the deep crevice, seeking the networked consciousness of the Symbiotes, I did not find a pristine colonial mind. The fungal mass I chose was already dying, already being dismantled by a creeping, copper-scented rust that feeds on the memory-storing tissues. It was a decay-node, a place where the ghost-maps of Grazer migrations from eons past were fermenting into informational compost. When the hyphae threaded into my nervous system, I did not wake up to the serene, three-dimensional pressure map of the world. I woke up to the *noise* of it.

A Symbiote knows the forest as a perfect, thrumming geometry. I know it as a hemorrhage. Through my ruined fungal symbiont, I feel the world's constant, breathless birth not as a miracle, but as a desperate, exhausting emergency. I feel the pain of the floor-wood splintering under the weight of a Grazer's million-year tread. I feel the panic of the vines as they strangle each other in the dark, and the silent, suffocating terror of the deep fungi whose spores are eaten before they can fall. Where the Symbiotes feel a library, I feel a slaughterhouse that is trying desperately to pretend it is a temple.

I am a parasite on a parasite, a glitch in the living transit system. I do not ride the Grazers. I cannot bear their hum; to me, it is the grinding of tectonic plates, the sound of a god's joints arthritic and screaming. Instead, I navigate the places the network cannot see. I live in the wakes of the Sculptors.

The Sculptors, in their brutal urban planning, carve and prune to maintain the forest's legibility. But every editor leaves revisions on the cutting-room floor. When a Sculptor severs an aggressive vine, it leaves a stump that weeps the metallic, sap-like lifeblood. When they carve a hollow to regulate the flow, there are always chitinous shards and offcuts of fibrous tissue left behind. I live in these margins. I eat the discarded offcuts. I drink from the weeping stumps. I am the maggot in the wound that ensures it stays open just long enough to scar properly.

I am not alone, though we do not congregate. We are the misfits of the accretion. There is a Grazer whose hum has shifted a half-tone flat, causing the moss on its back to slough off in great, weeping sheets; it feels its own decay as a strange, new kind of gravity. There are Sculptors whose thermal stripes flicker in chaotic, stuttering patterns, carving galleries that lead nowhere, obsessively editing the forest into impossible, fractal geometries that collapse under their own weight within a cycle. We recognize each other by the tremors in the hum, the subtle dissonances in the omnipresent quiet.

My purpose—I have come to understand this slowly, feeling my way through the copper-thick air—is to be the *forgetting* that makes *remembering* possible.

The Choir at the bottom of the world filters the ambient life energy and condenses it into perfect, amber crystals of solid light. They are the memory of the god, turning life into stone. But a memory that never degrades is a tumor. A library that never discards its outdated texts chokes on its own density. The Symbiotes hold the ghost-maps of eons, but eons of unbroken data create a noise that drowns out the present.

When I press my rust-eaten mind to the floor, I do not add to the network. I subtract. I consume the redundant pathways, the fossilized pain of long-dead root-systems, the endless, looping echoes of ancient Grazers whose trails have been paved over a thousand times by newer, heavier migrations. I metabolize the forest's trauma into a soluble compost that leaches back into the spongy wood, feeding the very birth I suffer from.

I am a small, grey thing, chitinous and asymmetrical, trailing a faint, sickly bioluminescence the color of a bruise healing. I move in the opposite direction of the paths, shouldering through the curtain of hanging moss, my dozen legs finding purchase on the slick, furrowed backs of giants. I am heading down. Always down.

I seek the Choir.

Not the healthy ones, who pulse with the silent song of pure energy, but the ones at the very periphery, whose crystalline eggs are growing too fast, too dense, absorbing too much of the world's unfiltered thrum. I find the nodes where the amber stone is turning black with information-sickness. And there, in the suffocating, gelid mist, I press my ruined head against the calcified wood of the Choir's tendrils. I bleed my rust into their crystal lattice. I introduce the flaw that shatters the perfect memory into glittering, useless dust.

It is a violent act, an act of un-creation. But in the wake of my passing, the hum changes. It breathes. A space opens up in the suffocating density. A new vine, unpruned and chaotic, finds the light to sprout. A new kind of spore, free of the weight of a million identical ancestors, catches the thick air.

I am the Rot-Kissed. I am the necessary failure in the god's gut. I am the entropy that keeps the embryo growing.
