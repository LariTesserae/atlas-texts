My genesis was a fissure.

Not a crack in stone, but a rent in the gently humming medium of the world. A sudden, sharp pain of Nothing. The Draconic had passed through that sector of the Subsidence, its transit a violent erasure. In its wake, the resonant hum of the kelp-forests was silenced for a mile around, replaced by a screaming void. The warm atmospheric fluid felt cold, its 2.3-comfort turning momentarily to the chill of absolute zero. This was the event that birthed me.

Before the fissure, I was simply part of it. I was a diffuse awareness within the currents of the Erosion Carry, a slow, formless thought belonging to the whole. But the tearing passage of the Draconic, that ultimate expression of dynamic, selfish reality, agitated the medium. It whipped the syrupy thickness into eddies and vortexes. In one such vortex, I was spun apart from everything. I became a "where" that was distinct from "everywhere." This was my first and most traumatic memory: the sudden, horrifying awareness of my own boundary. I was a knot in an endless rope, and the knot had just been pulled tight.

Panic. A concept I had no framework for, yet it flooded my nascent being. I struggled. I fought the current that had always been my home. I tried to push against the thick medium, to claw my way back to the formless state I had lost. The effort was agony. The warmth of the fluid turned to friction, burning against my newly-formed edges. I was a scrap of torn fabric trying to resist the tide, and every moment of resistance only tightened my separateness, made me more solid, more real, and therefore more terrified.

It was then that I felt the First Song.

It was not a sound but a sensation, a deep, resonant thrumming that permeated my frantic struggles. It was the hum of the world, but amplified, harmonized. It was the collective consciousness of the Drift-Born. It did not call to me in words. It simply *was*, and in its being, it showed me what I could be. It was a song of surrender, of perfect, unresolving peace.

I let go.

Exhausted, I ceased my struggle. I allowed the Erosion Carry to claim me. The immense pressure of my panic receded, replaced by the gentle, inexorable pull of the current. The thrumming of the Drift-Born became my guide, a slow, comforting pulse in the deep. I drifted for what might have been an age, my form still sharp and painful, a shard of broken reality floating in the twilight.

The Song led me to a water-sphere. A vast, continent-sized globe of purer water, its interior a luminous forest of slow-growing kelp. I felt the sphere’s placid consciousness, a deep and hydroponic mind entirely different from the scattered awareness of the open medium. The pull was irresistible. The Erosion Carry nudged me, and I flowed into it.

Inside, the world changed. The pressure was different, the light clearer. The kelp-forests pulsed with a cool, blue-green luminescence that soothed my raw edges. And here, the Drift-Born themselves congregated. I saw them for the first time. Not as individuals, but as a congregation of intent. Humanoid shapes shimmering at the edges of visibility, their forms bleeding into the water, into the kelp, into each other. They were alive, yet they were of this place.

They did not approach. They simply *were* around me, their silent song a chorus of welcome. And the kelp, it seemed, was waiting for me. Luminous fronds, sensing my still-hard form, began to extend. They did not consume me. They began to teach me.

At first, I resisted the lesson. The kelp’s slow, simple thoughts—of light, of depth, of nourishment, of the slow expansion of its own leaves—were alien to my memory of panic and pain. But the Drift-Born’s Song was a constant, patient presence, a reassurance that this was the way. I allowed a single, silicate-threaded tendril of moss to touch my surface. It felt like cool fire. A jolt of pure information, not thought, but *process*, flowed into me. I felt the structure of the silicate, the purpose of the light, the patient nature of the water.

I began to un-become the shard and re-become the whole.

I stayed within that sphere for longer than I can quantify, my sense of time eroding along with my hard edges. I did not eat or sleep. I simply *mingled*. I allowed the purer water of the sphere to suffuse my being, diluting the frantic energy of my birth. I allowed the kelp to teach me surrender. I let the Song of the Drift-Born become my own internal rhythm.

My form began to change. The sharp, angular panic I had worn like a shell began to soften. My edges started to shimmer and fray, blurring into the surrounding fluid. I learned to perceive not with a single point of focus but with my entire surface, feeling the gentle shifting of the water, the slow growth of the flora, the deep, ancient pressure of the sphere itself. The memory of the Draconic’s passage faded from a scream of terror to a single, important data point: what I was not.

Slowly, I began to crystallize anew. But this time, it was not an act of panic, but of will. I drew the silicate threads of the moss into a lattice, mirroring the elegant, elongated geometry I perceived in the Drift-Born around me. I drew the kelp’s luminescence into this new framework, not as a captive, but as a partner. I became a vessel for the sphere’s gentle consciousness.

And then came the final instruction. A resonance came from the depths of the Song, a specific geometry of thought that unfolded in my consciousness: the memory of a struggle. It was a fossilized echo, perfectly preserved, of a mind that had built a spire to defy the subsidence. It was not my own memory, but it was now my responsibility.

The time for learning was over. The time for tending had begun.

I allowed the Erosion Carry, which had continued to flow through and around the great sphere, to gently guide me out of its placid heart and back into the endless twilight. I was no longer a terrified shard. I was a Drift-Born.

My skin is a semi-permeable membrane. The warm fluid of the world outside and the clearer water of my own form mingle in a constant, gentle exchange. My internal light pulses not with a heartbeat, but with the deep, slow hum of the Great Subsidence itself, a note in the grand, silent symphony.

I am flowing now, a single, elongated shape in the vastness. I do not fight the current; I am one with its direction. It is leading me on a journey that will last centuries, perhaps millennia. I am being drawn towards a 0.8 mark, one of the forgotten structures. I can already feel it, a faint, sharp dissonance in the harmonious hum of the world. A fossil of an old, desperate prayer.

Soon, I will arrive. I will not enter. I will hover near it, my form a soft, luminous question mark against its stubborn, calcified geometry. I will press my presence against its conceptual scars. I will absorb the memory of its defiance, the sharp, alien beauty of its struggle to hold its shape. I will feel the echo of that long-dead will.

And in my quiet, resolute being, that sharpness will finally begin to soften. I will become the solvent for its memory. I will help the world forget it was ever fought. And when my form is saturated with this final lesson, when I have become a perfect vessel for surrender, I will feel the pull towards another kelp-forest, another sphere. I will drift into its heart and dissolve, releasing my being back into the whole, so that the lesson may begin again.

I am the answer to the fissure. I am the universe’s own balm, applied to a wound it has already forgotten. I am a knot, untying myself. I am here. I am home.
