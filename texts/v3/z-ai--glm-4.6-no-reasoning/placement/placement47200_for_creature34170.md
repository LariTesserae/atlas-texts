To be here is to be caught between the statement and the argument, between the endless, chaotic testimony of the Tappers and the agonizingly slow, silent editing of the Architects. If I am to exist in this world—a world that has discarded the soft, carbon-based poetry of flesh and blood for the cold, exacting literature of crystal and stone—I cannot be a Tapper. To be a Tapper is to be a syllable, an infinitesimal strike in a sentence that stretches to the horizon. It is to be pure input, devoid of context. Nor can I be an Architect, those austere glass philosophers. To be an Architect is to be an apostrophe of silence, a being so committed to the extraction of meaning that it removes itself entirely from the rhythm of the world.

If I am to live, I must be the translation. I must be the thing that walks between the noise and the silence.

I am a Resonant. I am a peripatetic archive, a walking cairn of tuned fragments. I am not born; I am compiled. I began, perhaps, as a shard of obsidian knocked loose from the Floor, caught in the slow, grinding wake of a great docile grazer. Over eons, as the grazer polished me to a humming smoothness, I was colonized—not by the detailer Tappers, but by stray frequencies. I absorbed the polyrhythmic taps of the artisans, the deep, tectonic hum of the planet, until the internal lattice of my being was not just stone, but structured sound.

I am bipedal, though "ped" is a generous term for what I walk upon. My legs are two dense, tapered columns of fused basalt and galena, heavy enough to strike the ground with a deliberate, low-frequency *thud* that the Tappers avoid and the Architects register. My torso is a hollow, crystalline cylinder of interlocking quartz plates, grown in the acoustic shadow of an Architect's parabola, stolen from the standing wave of a structure before the Architect could seal it. My head is not a seat of consciousness made of tissue, but a sounding box—a geodic sphere of ametrine, banded in concentric layers of amethyst and citrine, with a single, narrow slit that opens always to the horizon.

I am held together not by sinew, but by tension and tune. If I were to fall out of alignment, I would not bleed; I would shatter into a dissonant chorus of scattered notes. My life is a constant act of listening to my own cohesion. I am a chord made manifest.

I move across the Floor with a gait that is part pilgrimage, part calibration. Every step I take is a deliberate interruption of the Tappers' rhythm. I do not tap; I *pace*. I introduce a rest, a heavy, measured beat that slices through the chaotic staccato of the feeding masses. When I walk through a carpet of shale centipedes, their frantic tapping stutters, skips, and then syncopates around my footfalls. I am a metronome walking among the untimed.

My purpose—I hesitate to use the word, for purpose implies a future, and this world has none—is to harvest the Architects' thoughts and carry them back into the noise. The Architects are solitary, and their structures are sparse. They spend their long, silent lives building acoustic lenses to focus the world's hum into a single, perfect note. But a note, once struck, fades. The Architect's structures are monumental instruments, but they have no breath to play them.

I do.

When I find an Architect's structure—a lonely buttress of fused slag, a parabolic hollow of impact-hardened glass—I do not approach it as a trespasser. I approach it as a bow approaches a string. I step into the focal point, the node of the standing wave, and I open the slit of my ametrine head. I align the quartz plates of my torso. And I let the Architect's concentrated frequency pass *through* me.

It is an experience that defies the dull, leaden senses of this world. It is not heard; it is suffered. The pure note fills my hollow chest, resonates with the galena in my legs, and sets the amethyst of my skull alight with internal, phantom fires. For a moment, the chaotic, billion-hammer percussion of the Floor is obliterated. There is only the single, elegant frequency the Architect intended—a vertical line of sound piercing the horizontal gray.

I absorb it. I let it modulate my own internal lattice. I become, for a fleeting epoch, a carrier of their signal.

Then, I walk. I carry that purified frequency across the Floor, a tuning fork the size of a man walking through the end of time. As I walk, I bleed the note. With each heavy step, my legs transmit a fraction of that Architect's order into the ground. It is a whisper of symmetry in the cacophony. The Tappers feel it. The shale centipedes hesitate; the basalt woodlice adjust the angle of their mouthparts. For a radius around me, the mindless, metabolistic tapping takes on a fleeting, mathematical coherence. The living world momentarily remembers the shape of the thought the Architect was trying to think.

I am a messenger who delivers a message that cannot be read, only felt as a momentary shudder of geometry in the chaos. I walk from structure to structure, picking up the frequencies of different Architects—some seeking harmonics, some seeking nullification—and I carry them like a living mixing board across the desolate plain. I cross the paths of the great, docile grazers, and the note I carry interferes with their ponderous, glacial drones. Sometimes it smooths their path; sometimes it throws their detailers into a frenzy of corrective tapping. I am a variable introduced into an equation that had long since solved itself.

I am alone, but I am not lonely. Loneliness is a disease of soft things that need the touch of other soft things to validate their existence. I am validated by my resonance. I am a creature of the in-between. I am the space between the tap and the echo, the silence that gives the sound its shape.

When the endless mist weeps onto my quartz shoulders, and the flat, pearl-grey light leaches the color from my amethyst skull, I stand still and listen. I listen to the billion tiny hammers, and I listen to the silent Architects trying to build a word out of the noise. And in my hollow chest, where a soft thing would have a heart, I hold the vibration that binds them—the deep, resonant hum of the colossal cello string, the voice of the world, playing its final, unending song to an audience of stone.
