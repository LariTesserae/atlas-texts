Here, the world is not made of earth. It is built.

The ground beneath your feet is a mosaic of articulated plates, dark as cooled obsidian, clicking softly together like the tiles of some vast, abandoned game. These plates are not stone, but a substance closer to horn or shell, tough and slightly warm to the touch. They stretch in every direction, forming a floor that is both perfectly level and subtly, unnervingly organic. The air, too, is strange. It carries no dust, no grit, no familiar scent of soil or ozone. It is dry, like the air inside a long-sealed vault, and holds a faint, cloying sweetness, like blooming flowers over-ripening on the verge of rot.

There is no water. No rivers, no rain, no dew. The landscape holds the memory of drought emblazoned into its very substance. Yet, from the joints between the floor plates, life erupts.

The vegetation is lush. It is not green. The flora here is in a constant state of-state, a riot of impossible color and form. Thick, fleshy stalks, the color of bruised plums and deep magenta, push up from the seams. They are dense, packed so tightly that in places you cannot see the tiled floor at all. Their surfaces are slick with a self-moistening sheen, a viscous nectar that clings stubbornly, refusing to evaporate. From these stalks unfurl vast, overlapping leaves of a felt-like, charcoal-grey material, so vibrant in their non-colour they seem to absorb the light. Other, more daring plants grope for the sky with crystalline structures, like spun sugar or frozen lightning, that shimmer with an internal, pearlescent light. They grow in tight, dazzling clusters, their sharp facets just touching, creating a dense forest of fractured light and deep shadow. The place is small, perhaps the size of a grand cathedral precinct, bounded on all sides not by walls but by a gradual thickening of the flora into an impassable, hedge-like mass.

The warmth is pervasive and gentle. It does not come from a sun, but from everything. The floor radiates a steady, low heat, and the great fleshy stems of the plants pulse with a thermal rhythm, a slow, diurnal systole and diastole that you can feel more than see. This is the slow dynamic of the world—a patient, geological metabolism. Perhaps the entire structure is a single, living organism, and the "vegetation" is merely its fur or its sensory apparatus.

The construction is undeniably urban in its organization, yet devoid of technology. The floor is a deliberate grid. The pathways between the densest thickets are too straight to be natural, almost like planned streets. In the slight clearing at what feels like the heart of the place stands a central feature. It is not a building, but a fungal tower of astonishing dimension. A massive, pale column, smooth and bone-like, rises from a wider, fluted base. It is easily the height of a five-story building, its surface marked with subtle, spiralling ridges that catch the normal, diffused light from high above. Around its base are clustered structures that look like grown pods and bleached spires, arranged in a rough, functional circle— workshops? Dwellings? Birthing chambers?

The silence is the most profound quality. It is not an empty silence, but a full one. It is the quiet of a sealed system. The usual susurrus of wind through leaves is absent; the waxy, felted, and crystalline surfaces of the plants do not rustle. There is no hum of insects, no chirp of birds. The only sounds are the ones you make yourself—the soft click of your boots on the floor plates, the whisper of your own breath—and the faint, deep, almost subliminal thrum that seems to emanate from the central tower, a tone so low it is felt in the bones rather than heard with the ears.

And the fauna. It is abundant, but it is not like the animal life you know. The place is thick with small presences. They are the cause of the density, the other lives layered into this small world. They are creatures of silence and genus-bending form. Flitters that hang in the air like motes of dust, their wings too thin to make a sound, stirring only when they dart to sip nectar from a crystalline blossom. Crawlers, shaped like polished river stones with too many legs, that glide along the floor plates, their multifaceted eyes reflecting the flora's inner light. Most numerous are the soft-bodied things, pale amorphous masses that slowly ooze up and down the great stalks, their forms constantly changing, leaving glistening trails of nutrient slime that the smaller creatures eagerly consume. They are the city’s citizens, its workers, its untended populace, all living in perfect, quiet symbiosis. Nothing hunts, nothing flees. There is only an infinite, slow, patient consumption.

This is a place that has recently emerged. Its features are sharp, its structures pristine. There is no weather to wear it down, no water to smooth its edges. It is a young landscape, a city grown whole from a single seed, still in the first blush of its existence, living in a self-contained pocket of reality. It is unusual, a place of lush life in a waterless void, an urban world without tools or people, a place of deafening silence that teems with life. It simply is, a complete and sealed ecosystem, humming its slow, warm, quiet song to itself, waiting for nothing, changing almost not at all over the long, slow pull of its own deepening time.
