It is not an entrance you find, but a seep. A place folded into the fabric of a world so extreme that only this single anomaly can exist. You are inside a geode, or perhaps the lung of a sleeping god. The scale is intimate, a chamber you could span with your outstretched arms, yet it feels vaster than any canyon. This is the consequence of a reality under strain.

The walls are not rock, not metal, but a membrane of cooled, vitrified something. It is black and smooth as obsidian, but when you press your palm to it, it gives a faint, leathery warmth. The high elevation and extreme heat of the world outside are not banished here, but translated. The air tastes of metal and boiled oxygen, thick and hot enough to make you dizzy, a constant, wet blanket against the skin. The air is saturated, a mist so fine and warm it feels like the breath of some great beast, perpetually exhaling. The floor is a lattice of something like bone or chitin, a porous, honeycombed surface that pools the weeping water into shallow, heated placenta-puddles. And from the lattice and the walls and the ceiling, the life erupts. They are not animals as you understand them. They are architectures of purpose, animated sculptures of a biomechanical fever-dream.

The dominant presence is the Weavers. Their bodies are long, segmented things, crafted from what looks like spun copper and mother-of-pearl. They have no discernible heads, but their front segments are studded with crystalline lenses that glitter with trapped light. They move with a hypnotic, ceaseless industry, their legs—dozens of hair-fine, sharp limbs—skittering over the surfaces. They do not make webs of silk. They weave the very moisture of the air. Using their forelimbs, they draw the fine, hot mist from the vapor, extruding it into gossamer filaments shimmering with iridescence. These filaments are not sticky; they are semisolid, like pure, spun light. They drape them from the ceiling to the floor, from wall to wall, creating a shimmering, three-dimensional labyrinth that fills the room. These are their nests, their traps, their cities.

Caught within this lattice are the Flitters. They are much smaller, like motes of dust given life. Each one is a perfect, polyhedral jewel—a hard, angular carapace of something like topaz or sapphire. They flutter-not-fly, beating crystalline wings so fast they are a blur, moving in buzzing, erratic swarms. They are not bees, but they move with a shared mind, a murmuration of geometry. They are drawn to the Weavers' filaments, and when they touch one, they cling. Their purpose is to be sustainers, perhaps; patterns of light pulse across their bodies in waves, and the filaments light up in response, transmitting a slow, silent signal through the Weavers' network. It is a communication system, a nervous system, a power grid.

Sometimes you see one stop its weaving, and one of its crystalline lenses will focus, projecting a tight beam of concentrated light. It uses this beam—this primitive, biological laser—to sever a filament that has grown incorrectly, or to anneal a joint between two strands, fusing them with a flash of silent heat. It is a hand-tool, a meticulous craft honed by instinct alone. The tech is inseparable from the creature.

This is the place you are in. A hot, wet, silent pocket of overwhelming, alien life. A young, chaotic ecosystem buzzing with activity inside a single, self-contained anomaly. You are a witness to a genesis, a pocket-universe's first, clumsy breath. To stay is to be saturated, to breathe their air, to feel the thrum of the Drums in your bones.
