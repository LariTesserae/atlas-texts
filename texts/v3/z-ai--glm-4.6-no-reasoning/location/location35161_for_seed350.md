It is always raining. Not the soft, clean drizzle of a quiet afternoon, but a ceaseless, pressurized deluge, a solid wall of water that smothers the world. This isn't water drawn from a placid sky and returned to the soil; this is atmospheric condensation on a planetary scale, the very air dissolving and falling back to earth. The rain is warm, almost body temperature, and so thick with dissolved minerals and hydrocarbons that it feels viscous on the skin, like a thin syrup. It is the primary medium of existence here, the solvent and the solution.

The elevation is a myth. There are no hills, no plateaus, no varying contours of land in the traditional sense. You stand upon a vast, shallow basin, a geological toddler's first clumsy steps. The ground is a young, sharp-edged crust of cooled glass and porous scoria, fractured into a million jagged plates that tilt and shudder underfoot. Water, the great leveler, has not yet had the time to wear these edges smooth. It pools in the deep fissures, forming canals of stagnant, iridescent liquid that reflect no sky but only the churning, luminous gloom above.

The landscape is a mechanical mangrove swamp. From the fissures in the glassy crust, a mad blasphemy of vegetation erupts. The "lushness" is not a soft, green bounty but a dense, crowded lattice of industrial life. Trees are not wood, but braided bundles of copper and steel cable, their bark a sheath of sound-dampening asbestos and polymer foam. From their limbs hang not leaves, but broad, striped capacitor banks and fins of radiator metal, glowing with a soft, internal heat. Their roots are not for drawing soil nutrients, but for anchoring into the planet's power-conduits; you can feel the thrum of raw electricity through the soles of your boots if you stand too still.

Vines, thick as a man's arm, are actually coiled signal wires and hydraulic tubes, covered in a waxy, photosynthetic film. They snake across everything, strangling other flora in a slow, silent war of resources. Where they find a fractured surface, they plunge fissures, anchoring with geothermal taps that hiss steam into the deluge. The ground between these metallic titans is a dense carpet of moss-like clumps—matting of conductive fibers and glowing fungal filaments that feed on the ambient electrical charge and the chemical soup of the rain. The air is thick with the scent of ozone, hot metal, and a sweet, cloying perfume from the glowing fungi.

This is the built environment, though "built" feels too passive. The structures are relics, abandoned shells of a forgotten industry. Giant, skeletal factories lie half-submerged in flooded craters, their gantries and smokestacks now colonized by the cable-trees. Rust-stained locomotives are fossilized in place, their wheels frozen in time, their long-dead engines providing the framework for a new generation of growth. These were not factories for goods, but for the world itself. They were geothermal taps, atmospheric processors, massive terraforming engines left to run wild. The industrial scale is everywhere, but it is no longer directed. It is a distributed intelligence, an ecosystem of machinery that has achieved a kind of feral, aimless sentience.

The light is a conspiratorial dimness, a perpetual twilight born of a permanent, churning cloud bank. The sun is a rumor, a diffused, luminous bruise in the roiling ceiling of water vapor. Yet it is not truly dark. The world glows from within. Lichen patterns of energy skitter across surfaces. Transformers, cliff-sized and half-buried, pulse with a deep, resonant throb of cyan light. The water itself fluoresces in the wreckage-clogged canals, swirling with slicks of phosphorescent algae and chemical isotopes. This light is not for seeing; it is a symptom of metabolism, the slow burn of the world’s power going astray.

There are no animals. No birds sing, no insects buzz. The vacancy is a presence in itself, a profound and unnerving silence in one specific frequency, set starkly against the world's sonic bedrock.

And the sound. The world screams.

The primary voice is the water. It is not the simple patter of rain but a percussive, roaring blanket, a tidal wave of white noise that flattens all thought. On top of this, the industrial ruins add their own chorus. A thousand untended transformers emit a deafening, harmonic hum that vibrates in your teeth. Metal, stressed by temperature and corrosion, groans with the slow, agonizing protest of tectonic plates. Steam vents from the geothermal taps with the shriek of a boiling kettle, amplified a thousandfold. Hydraulic systems leak and sigh in rhythmic bleats. It is a symphony of decay and function, a deafening mechanical orchestra playing a never-ending, discordant crescendo.

But the real defining characteristic, the thing that curdles the blood, is the turbulence. The very fabric of this place is unstable, a reality breakdown. The young, sharp-edged geology is not a fixed foundation. It is ephemeral. You will watch a cliff face of obsidian simply... un-cohere. It doesn't crumble or fall. It dissolves. One moment it is a solid wall of black glass, the next it is a churning mass of screaming mercury and liquid static, a glitch in the world's source code. This chaos will then crystallize, seconds later, into a new formation—perhaps a forest of twisted copper pipes, or a field of humming, hummingbird-sized gyroscopes that immediately collapse back into raw potential.

The world is constructing and deconstructing itself constantly. A lake of warm rain might, without warning, solidify into a floor of humming hexagonal tiles before collapsing back into water again. You can see the event horizon of these changes: a shimmer in the air, a wavering of light and sound, a momentary clue that the rules here are being rewritten on the spot. Walking is a fraught exercise in quantum observation. The path you see is not the path you will have after three steps. The built and the natural, the mechanical and the biological, are not just intertwined; they are interchangeable states in a chaotic, turbulent equation.

To be here is to be inside a forgotten engine, a living demagogue of rust and light and noise, a place where the primordial forces of creation and the programmed logic of industry have merged into a single, screaming, infinite engine of catastrophic becoming. It is beautiful and terrifying, a newborn world still wracked by the birth pains of its own logic, a landscape forever teetering on the edge of being and being undone.
