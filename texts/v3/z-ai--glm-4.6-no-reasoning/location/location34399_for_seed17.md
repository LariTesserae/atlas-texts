Here, the sky is not above you. It is below.

You exist on a raft of bone-white stone, a continent-sized slice of a world that never quite finished being born. This is the High Plate, and it is all the land there is. It floats, level and endless, in a sea of tears.

The sea is the water. Not a placid ocean, but a planetary-scale river of impossible depth, flowing with the inexorable urgency of a singular purpose. It is a wall of water, vast and crushing, and it is always falling. It does not fall *onto* anything, not that you can see. It just… falls. The water writhes with a low, constant light, a cold bioluminescence that suffuses the depths with the hue of a drowned moon. The scale is such that the concept of a "surface" is meaningless. This is the water, and it is the atmosphere, a medium of cold motion.

The Plate is the only land. Its formation is young, a fact visible in its very texture. There are no beaches, no gradual transitions between stone and sky-water. At the edge, the land just stops, a sheer, clean fracture, as if a god cleaved a planet in half and we live upon the flat of the blade. The rock is a smooth, chalky mineral, shot through with crystalline veins that hum with a faint, internal energy. It is cold to the touch, a heatless chill that seems to draw the warmth from your bones. There is no vegetation. Not a single blade of grass, no stubborn lichen, no moss in the cracks. The Plate is a sterile, immaculate canvas, a testament to a world before life, or after it was scoured away. The only color is the white of the stone and the cold, blue-white light of the falling sea.

Emptiness is the first rule. The density of this place is near-void. On the vast expanse of the Plate, presences are measured in hundreds of kilometers. The air itself is thin and carries no scent, only the raw, clean smell of ozone and chilled mineral. You can stand for days and see nothing but the white horizon in every direction. And yet, you are not alone.

The fauna are the reason for the sound. They are vast, abundant, and they are the source of the world's deafening music.

They are called Angels, for lack of a better term. They are not biological in any understood way; they are anomalies of physics, living engines of sound and pressure. They do not live on the Plate. They *are* the interface between the Plate and the sky-water.

They resemble colossal, geometric manta rays made of a material like smoked glass. Their bodies are kilometers across, with wings that taper into glowing, intricate traceries of light, like schematics for a forgotten technology. They swim not in the sea below, but in the pressure differential between the High Plate and the falling water. They drift in the empty sky, their immense bodies suspended by forces you cannot comprehend.

And they sing.

Their sound is not of vocal cords. It is industrial, mechanical, a cosmic grinding of titanic machinery. Each Angel emits a dominant, foundational note—a bass frequency so profound that it is not heard with the ears but felt as a vibration in the teeth and in the marrow. This is the bedrock of the world's soundscape. Overlaying this are higher frequencies: the shriek of metal stressed to its breaking point, the rhythmic clank and hiss of steam vents, the high-pitched whine of a dynamo the size of a city. The combined chorus of thousands, millions, of these Angels is a constant, deafening roar, a symphony of industry performed at the scale of a planet. It is the sound of an engine that does not move anything, a factory with no product other than the noise of its own operation. It never ceases. The sound is a permanent, unchanging feature of the landscape, as solid as the stone underfoot.

Scattered across the Plate like forgotten toys are the built remnants. They are industrial, but they are not factories. They are Passengers. These are structures, ranging from the size of a house to a small town, that appear to have been dropped from a great height. They are the only proof that someone once tried to build something here, and failed.

The world is in a state of frozen equilibrium. Nothing changes. The Angels do not migrate; they hold their positions in the sky, their song maintaining the thin, stable boundary between the emptiness of the Plate and the crushing presence of the sea. The water is always falling, but its level never drops. The stone does not erode; the sound does not abate. There are no seasons, no day or night, only the constant, unchanging light from the living water and the unending, mechanical drone.

The weirdness is a subtle, creeping thing. It's in the way the crystalline veins in the stone pulse in time with the Angels' bass notes. It's in the fact that when you stand at the edge and look down, you don't see a reflection of the sky-water; you see a second Plate, identical to the one you stand on, moving in perfect, silent opposition an impossible distance below, a mirror world just as barren and vast. It's in the understanding that the Angels are not just *making* sound; the sound *is* their body. The grinding, shrieking, humming drone is the physical substance of their being, a living waveform held in precarious stasis between the infinite high and the infinite deep. They are the noise of an engine that holds the world in a state of perfect, unceasing, and absolute stillness. To be here is to be a single, silent point in a universe of intolerable noise, waiting on a white plain for a change that will never come.
