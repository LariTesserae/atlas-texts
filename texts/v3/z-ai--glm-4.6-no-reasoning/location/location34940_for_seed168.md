Here, there is no water. This is not a statement of absence, but a statement of fact, as fundamental and unyielding as the law of gravity. The ground itself is a testament to it.

You stand on a surface that is not rock, not sand, not dust, but a single, continuous sheet of crystalline matrix. It is pale amethyst in color, shot through with threads of gold and milky quartz that catch the light from a sun that feels distant, benign. Stepping onto it produces no sound, no scuff, no impression; your footfall is an event of pure weight, absorbed without protest. It is utterly, perfectly still. Looking down, you can see meters, perhaps kilometers, into its depth. The matrix is transparent, layered like struck flint, revealing a geometry that suggests it has not been eroded but *grown*. The facets are sharp, the angles precise, untouched by the chaotic hand of weather. There are no cracks. There are no seams. It is a featureless, infinite floor locked in a state of pre-formation, a world that has not yet learned the process of breaking apart.

And yet, it is not a desert. It is lush. The vegetation here does not drink. It does not photosynthesize. It *conducts*.

Sprouting from this crystalline plain are colossal, branching structures that are neither tree nor crystal nor fungus. They tower hundreds of meters into the warm, still air, their forms impossibly complex. Imagine a fractal antler of petrified wood, but grown by a lightning strike. Their primary trunks are the color of dark smoke, smooth and cool to the touch, but they branch into finely tremulous filaments of spun glass. Every surface, from the thickest trunk to the most delicate filament, glows with a soft, internal emerald-green luminescence. The light pulses, but almost imperceptibly, with a rhythm akin to the slow, deep beat of a heart. These are the "Lamp-woods," and the sound you hear, should you press your ear to a glowing trunk, is not the rush of sap, but a low, resonant hum—the sound of pure energy flowing.

The light they cast is the only light that truly illuminates the ground. The sun overhead is a bright, white disc in a pale, cloudless sky, but its light is diffuse and disembodied, without heat or glare. It feels like a mere suggestion of day. The Lamp-woods, however, provide substance. Their green glow pools on the amethyst floor, scattering through its facets and creating an illusion of shallow, shimmering water—a phantom memory of a world that might have been. The air is warm, a constant, gentle temperature that neither rises nor falls with a day-night cycle, for there may not be one.

There are no animals. Not a single insect, not a bird, not a sound of chirping or skittering. The silence is profound, a velvet blanket that cushions the senses, absorbing even the circulation of your own blood in your ears. The only sound is the deep thrum of the Lamp-woods, a vibration you feel more in your bones than you hear with your ears, and the occasional, musical *tink* you can generate by tapping the great crystalline spires that grow between them. These spires are the world's other feature: vast, hollow, hexagonal tubes of the same amethyst matrix, rising like silent sentinels, their interiors a perfect, echoing darkness. They are scattered across the immense, open landscape, forming valleys of light and shadow between the glowing forests.

And this is the strangest fact, the one that defines the reality of the place: it is industrial. This is not a primordial world. It is a factory.

The humming of the Lamp-woods is not life; it is the sound of a process. The glow is not biology; it is a state. The entire landscape is a single, self-contained, and perfectly stable engine. It takes the raw, unfocused energy of the distant sun and, through some unknown photovoltaic alchemy inherent in its crystalline structure, converts it. The energy is channeled into the Lamp-woods, which act as vast, flora-like transformers and capacitors, modulating the raw power into a stable, usable form, evidenced by their soft, rhythmic glow. The overall system is in a state of frozen equilibrium, never changing, never depleting, never overheating. The entire open, regional landscape—this town-sized valley of crystal and light—is one component of an inconceivably vast machine, yet it has been built without hands. There are no bolts, no wires, no control panels. It is all simply *grown* into being, a seamless fusion of natural-looking form and mechanical function. The low erosion means it is not yet finished; perhaps it is a prototype, or a garden tending a single, vital process, waiting to expand and replicate across some greater, unseen geometry.

To be here is to be a witness to a mystery of cosmic engineering. It is beautiful in its silent, perpetual operation, a world that has solved its own needs and settled into a perfect, endless loop. There is no life, only function. There is no history, only design. It is a place that is at once lush and empty, ancient and unborn, a landscape of pure, quiet, and unchanging industry, humming softly to itself in the eternal, silent warmth.
