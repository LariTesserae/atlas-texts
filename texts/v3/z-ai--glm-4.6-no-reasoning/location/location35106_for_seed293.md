There is no ground in the Marrow-Bower, not in any solid, geological sense. To "stand" is to be buoyed upon a vast, interlocking matrix of spongy, chromatophore-rich vegetation that functions as a single organism. This is the Plant-Substrate, and it is the world. It absorbs the perpetually high water table, its texture like damp velvet that gives slightly underfoot, whispering with the expulsion of captured air with every step. The water level is just below the surface of this living carpet, a latent squelch that promises to give way to a knee-deep coolness if you press down too hard.

The individual components of the substrate are unrecognizable as plants from any other epoch. There are no leaves, no trunks, no roots in the traditional sense. Instead, there are vast, slow-rolling cushions of cyanotic turquoise and violet flesh, grooved with channels that drip constantly with a clear, viscous fluid. Rising from these cushions are beautiful, skeletal architectures of what might once have been wood or bark, now rendered in a calcified, bone-white material. These are the Groves, forming the walls and buttresses of this place. They curve and spiral in architecturally precise, yet nonsensical, ways, forming partial domes and soaring buttress-arches that frame views of more Groves in the distance, giving the entire landscape a sense of being a single, impossibly vast cathedral. The scale is not grand; it is intimate. The highest of these bone-towers might be equivalent to a ten-story building, creating a ceiling of interlaced white spires that, on overcast days, generates a perpetual, gentle twilight.

But the days are rarely overcast. The light of this world is normal, the star a familiar, placid yellow. Yet the atmosphere is a warm, thick soup, heavy with moisture and the smell of ozone. The light filters through a constant, low-hanging mist that clings to the highest points of the Groves, making them glisten. The air itself seems a shade greener than it should be.

And it is loud. The dominant sound is not the roar of an animal or the crash of a wave, but a persistent, multi-tonal hum. It emanates from the very substrate beneath you, a low-frequency vibration that you feel more in your bones than you hear with your ears. This is the Bower's metabolism, the sound of a planet-scale organism slowly digesting itself. Woven through this foundational drone are a million other noises: the soft, percussive *plock-plock-plock* of condensing droplets falling from the Groves onto the spongy floor; the distant, liquid calls of the fauna; and, always, the sound of the city.

The "urban" parameter is etched into the fabric of the world in a way that blurs the line between construction and decay. The Groves were not built to be buildings, but they have been *inhabited*. The calcified structures are honeycombed with smooth, perfectly cylindrical tunnels, some no wider than an arm, others large enough to house a family. They lead to fluid-filled chambers and hollowed-out chambers, some scraped smooth, others left in their natural, ribbed state. These are the remnants of a civilization, or perhaps a phase of the Bower's own life, when it consciously shaped itself. But now, they are being reclaimed. A phosphorescent moss, bright emerald in the gloom, creeps along the inner walls of these tunnels, pulsing with a soft, internal light that syncopates with the hum of the world. It illuminates the strange art-scrawls left by the inhabitants, patterns that seem to depict fractals and whirlpools, diagrams of fluid dynamics and cellular mitosis.

This is the "ancient" Landscape. Erosion has done its work, not by carving valleys and canyons, but by smoothing all sharp edges into a final, peaceful form. The Bower is old, and it is settling. Its "erosion" is biological, a long process of self-cancelation. The violent, turbulent energy required for its creation is now expended, leaving only the gentle, chaotic dissolution. It is the end-state of a world, warm and quiet and wet and slow.

The fauna is abundant and deeply integrated into this ecology. They are not creatures that *live in* the Bower so much as they are its mobile organs. Skitter-Swarms, fist-sized, semi-transparent amphibians with iridescent scales, gallop across the substrate in chaotic, coordinated patterns, their webbed feet spreading spores from one cushion to another. In the fluid channels swim Needle-Fins, long, bony fish that move with astonishing speed, their function to clear blockages and aerate the nutrient-rich broth. Higher up, clinging to the white Groves, are the Chime-Moths, whose fluttering wings strike the calcified spires, producing a sound like a glass wind chime orchestra playing at random. They are the loudness, the sudden sharp notes that bray against the constant hum.

This integrated chaos is the Bower's "weirdness." The technology that pervades this place is beyond modern understanding because it *is* the Bower itself. The tunnels are not mined but grown, through a process that directs the plant-substrate's digestion along specific vectors. The air is purified by nanoscopic photosynthetic agents distributed throughout the mist. The Chime-Moths are biological radios, transceiving complex data through their percussive songs that the underlying substrate processes. There is no distinction between the city and the organism, between the machine and the lifeform. It is a singularity of biomechanics, an end-point of evolution where technology and biology have become so co-dependent they are indistinguishable.

To walk here is to walk through the final, tired, and beautiful thoughts of a god-like creature that has decided on its own form of death. The warm air and damp flesh are comforting, the light soft, and the constant drone is a lullaby. It is a place of beautiful decay, a world that has become its own museum, its own mausoleum. The turbulence is not a storm to be weathered but the last, shuddering breaths of a being that has found its perfect, final, and ultimately transient form. It is a grove-sized room, partially built, endlessly overgrown, and filled to the brim with the wet, humming, and verdant ghost of itself.
