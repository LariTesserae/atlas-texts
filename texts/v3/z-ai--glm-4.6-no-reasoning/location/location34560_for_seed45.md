The ground is not old. That is the first and most absolute truth of this place.

It is a landscape of birth, not of age. A high plateau, impossibly vast, torn from the crust of a world that has not yet decided on its final name. The material underfoot is dark, a volcanic glass called obsidian, but it is not the smooth, cool stone of a jeweler's shop. It is unformed. The surface is a battlefield of crystalline spines that have just thrust their way up from a molten below, their points sharp enough to draw blood from a careless foot. Between these spikes, the land is a froth of cooling rock, swirling with iridescent shimmer like an oil slick on standing water. In places, the obsidian is still hot to the touch, a steady, concerning warmth that radiates through the soles of your boots. This is the warmth that defines the air: warm, dry, and thin, so thin that each breath feels insufficient, as if you were trying to drink from a cup full of holes.

The landscape is not dark in the way of a moonless night, but in the way of a world without a sun. There is no source of light overhead. Instead, the place is lit from within. The jagged spires and fissures of obsidian glow with a soft, internal luminescence. It is a cold light, the color of deep-sea phosphorescence, a spectral teal and ethereal violet that illuminates the world in ghostly gradients. The light shifts and pulses, a slow heartbeat of geology, causing shadows to writhe and elongen in ways that defy the physics of a single, distant source. It is a world lit from the inside out, by the very act of its own creation.

But it is not silent. The primary sound is the one you feel more than hear: a vast, subterranean humming that vibrates up through the soles of your feet and settles in your teeth. It is the sound of planetary furnace-stacks and the groaning of continents being born. Superimposed over this drone is a much gentler, more insidious sound: a constant, crystalline *tinkling*. Tiny, newly-formed slivers of the glassy ground, made brittle from their rapid cooling, flake off and fall like microscopic hail. They land on the larger formations, creating a sound like a thousand tiny chandeliers shattering in slow motion. It is the sound of the world shedding its baby skin, perpetually.

There is no life, not as any traveler would know it. The lack of water is absolute; the air is a dry-it-your-eyes-out furnace. There is no vegetation to soften the edges of the stone in hues of green or brown. The only motes in the air are mineral dust, sharp black silica that catches the internal light like suspended stars. And yet, something like *ecology* exists. It is an ecology of energy and form. The light itself seems to be a presence. It pools in low places, collecting in the basins between spires like a luminous, weightless fluid. These pools of light have a subtle current, moving with a sluggish grace, drawn and repelled by the unseen magnetic fields within the rock. They are the unseen herd of this world, the silent, glowing fauna that is made of pure geometry and potential energy.

The built environment is a phantom. There are no buildings, no roads, no tools. But the faint, scattered remains of *something* are etched into the freshest surfaces. They are not carvings made by a chisel; they are patterns of *growth*. In a few locations, where the glowing energy pools have rested longest, the obsidian has changed, forming delicate, interlocking lattices. These structures are impossibly complex, finer than any spiderweb, and they resonate with the hum of the world. They are the fossilized imprints of pure information, a single, silent idea applied to the raw material of the plateau by a consciousness that is the planet itself. They are the scattered ruins of a thought, not a civilization. They are technological in a way that bypasses tool and material, a direct write of pattern onto the substrate of reality.

And the turbulence. The ground is not stable. The "emerging" nature of the landscape is not a slow process of eons; it is happening *now*. From a fissure a hundred meters away, a new spine of obsidian begins to shudder. It does not erupt lava; it extrudes itself, a silent, convulsive push. Glass fractures with a sound like a colossal tolling bell, and a new tower, glistening with inner fire, rises to the height of a man in the space of a single breath. The vibration of its birth trembles through the plateau, causing a cascade of tinkling shards to rain down. The energy pools react, swirling away from the new formation like disturbed fish. The light pulses faster, the hum climbs in pitch. This place is an anthill of geology, a solar surface where the creation and destruction of features is a constant, chaotic dance. There is no past or future here, only a perpetual, violent present. To stand here is to stand in the delivery room of a world, witnessing the raw, un-sculpted will of a place becoming itself. The space is open and sparse, but it is not empty. It is simply full of potential.
