There is no firmament here, only a ceiling, a low and heavy lid of slate-grey cloud that presses down, muffling the sky into a perpetual, diffused twilight. The light that filters through is the color of weak tea, a flat and directionless wash that gives no hint of sun or moon, only that there is something luminous, far away and failing. Below this lid, the air is not simply humid; it is a palpable medium, thick with heat and the scent of warm water and wet earth. To breathe is to drink, each inhalation a thick, viscous gulp of the atmosphere itself. The sound is the first thing that truly anchors a consciousness here, a deep, resonant hum that seems to come not from a single source but from the entire world. It is the sound of a billion tiny lives all respiring at once, a chorus of existence, a constant and unwavering drone that has no beginning and no end.

The land is low, a basin cradled by geology that has not yet decided what it will become. Erosion is a concept not yet realized, a principle waiting to be invented. Here, creation is still laying its foundation. The ground is a soft, yielding sponge of black mud, so saturated with water that it quivers with every footstep. It is punctuated not by rock or stone, but by immense, nascent forms that rise like slow bubbles from the planet’s core. These are cataracts of raw, freshly-extruded mineral, their faces slick with seepage. They are not mountains, not yet, but suggestions of them. The material is a soapy, unconsolidated limestone, a sort of planetary scab still wet, its color a milky, bone-white streaked with veins of mineral blue and rust-red. Water does not flow *from* them, so much as they *weep* it, a constant, silent weeping that keeps the ground perpetually swamped and the air forever thick. This is the geology of emergence, a world still in the act of being born.

Vegetation does not grow; it accretes. It is a dense, multi-layered saturation of life, every surface, every cubic inch of air between the mud and the cloud ceiling, choked with it. There are no trees as we would know them, for there is no hard earth to anchor them and no real sun to fuel a woody quest for the sky. Instead, there are vast, low barges of life, spongy mats of symbiotic flora that float on the mud. Host plants, thick as a man's waist and with the texture of wet cork, form the structural base. Upon this skeleton grows everything else. Vines thick as pythons drape from one barge to another, creating a canopy of tangled rope that hangs just above the head. Their surfaces are not smooth but furred with a thick, velvet moss that is in fact an alga, absorbing what little light there is and respiring with a slow, collective sigh. From this moss grow stalks of a different kind, fungal in appearance but with the vascular needs of a plant, their caps iridescent and slick with a condensation that drips, adding to the ceaseless dampness.

But the true movers and shapers of this world are the fauna. They are overwhelming, a presence so dense it becomes the very texture of the environment. There are no large predators, no lumbering beasts. The overwhelming nature of the life here is in its sheer, pestilential abundance. The air is thick with fliers—not birds, but things more like hummingbirds crossed with dragonflies, their bodies gelatinous and translucent, their wings a frantic, invisible blur. They move in gauzy clouds, shifting in the gray light, their bodies thrumming as they feed on the nectar of the fungal stalks. They are the source of the high, piercing whine that threads through the world’s deep hum.

At ground level, the mud itself is alive. It churns with unseen things. Worms as thick as an arm, their skin a mottled pattern of phosphorescent blue and green, move through it with the slow peristalsis of the landscape itself, their passage creating the channels for the standing water. Their rhythmic movements are a slow, underlying throb to the world’s many sounds. Skittering over the mats of vegetation are things like gigantic, armored woodlice, their chitin a mosaic of pebble-browns and damp-greens, their countless legs whispering as they graze on the velvet moss. Below them, in the water-filled ditches, swim translucent fish-like creatures with no eyes but fins that glow with a soft, INTERNAL light, pulsing in time with some unknown rhythm.

The sound is a complex, never-changing symphony of this life. The deep foundational hum of the massed respirations. The high, sibilant whine of the aerial fliers. The constant chorus of chirps and clicks from countless unseen insects in the moss and mud. The soft, *plink… plink… plink…* of drips falling from the weeping geology and the saturated flora. And woven through it all, the whispering hiss of a million invertebrate legs and the slow, digestive gurgle of the worms in the earth. It is loud, yes, but it is a static loudness. It has been this way for longer than memory can reach, and it will be this way for all time to come.

To be here is to be immersed in a state of permanent, high-density equilibrium. The temperature never varies; it is a blood-warm, breathy heat that smoons in the lungs. The humidity is absolute. The soundscape is a constant chord. Life is not a cycle of birth, growth, and death, but a continuous, overflowing presence. When a being here ceases to live, it does not fall and decay into the earth. It is incorporated, consumed and merged back into the living whole within minutes, its biomass redistributed to the ever-hungry, ever-growing life around it. There are no seasons, no day, no night. There is only this one, endless, vibrant present, a world forever on the cusp of becoming, forever held in the same embryonic moment, a screamingly alive, screamingly static wet dark.
