Here, the ceiling is a sky of living bone.

You are below the surface, but not in a cave. The space is a vast, single chamber, the scale of a buried cathedral, and its roof is a thick, porous layer of what was once the skeleton of some impossibly large creature. The bone, a mellow, ivory-yellow, is shot through with veins of agate and quartz, and in the brilliant, unfiltered light that pours down from above, it glows from within. This light has no source you can name; it is simply a property of the place, a constant, midday glare that paints everything in bleached relief and drives shadows into thin, charcoal sketches.

The dampness is a patient presence. It doesn’t rain, not in the sense of drops falling from a sky. Instead, the bone-ceiling weeps. A fine, constant condensation beads on its underside, gathering in the microscopic pores before growing too heavy, clinging for a moment, and then letting go. The water falls not as a shower, but as a slow, silent drizzle of single droplets, each one an event. They hang in the bright air for an instant, perfect lenses, before striking the floor with a sound so soft it is more of a feeling than a noise—a distant, intermittent *plink… plink…* that is the only punctuation in the long, warm silence.

The floor is a crust of hardened salts and mineralized silt, pale as the bone above. It is cracked into a mosaic of polygonal plates, warm to the touch, and between these plates lie shallow depressions where the weeping water gathers. These are the only pools. They are still and clear, their surfaces perfectly mirroring the bone-sky, and their bottoms glisten with the same crystalline deposits that built the ground. The air is warm and still, thick with the mineral scent of ozone and wet stone, a clean smell that is ancient and sterile.

The vegetation is sparse, but what is here is utterly alien. It does not grow upwards, but clings. From the undersides of the larger, overhanging ribs of the ceiling, hang clusters of something that looks like coral carved from pale green jade. They are passive life, photosynthetic organisms that have adapted to bathe in the perpetual, sourceless light. They have no leaves, only intricate, branching structures that slowly pulse with a soft, internal luminescence, a gentle counterpoint to the harshness of the ambient brightness. Their entire life is a slow, patient process of absorbing light and condensation, a slow breathing that you can only perceive by staring at them for many minutes, watching the faint light within them swell and recede.

 fauna is abundant, yet it conforms to the hollowness. The air itself is filled with them. They are not birds or insects, but something in between. Imagine intricate, geometric skeletons of spun glass or cut crystal, ranging in size from a grain of sand to the span of your hand. They drift on the faint, thermally-driven currents of air, borne on delicate, diaphanous fins that catch the light and splinter it into tiny rainbows. They make no sound. They have no mouths or visible means of sustenance. They simply are, a silent, glittering ocean filling the vast, empty space between the bone-ceiling and the salt-crusted floor. They are the life of the void, the occupants of the hollowness. Occasionally, one will lose its buoyancy and drift down, settling on the ground with a sound like a single sand grain, where it will slowly, over decades, dissolve back into the mineral dust.

The rules of this world are written in stillness. Change is geological. The slow drip of water is eroding the bone-sky, but not carving it, merely polishing it, wearing down its porous texture until the whole ceiling might one day be a smooth, opalescent dome. The cathedral scale feels like a boundary because it is one; the walls, where the great, curving ribs of the skeleton meet the floor, are seamless. There is no passage out, no indication of a world beyond this single, self-contained chamber. It is a pocket universe, a tomb that has become an ecosystem.

The only primitive technology—is that even the word for it?—is what the crystal-fauna leave behind. When they dissolve, they leave behind a faint, silvery residue. In the damp depressions in the floor, flecks of this residue sometimes bind with the salts, precipitating out into small, naturally-occuring nodes of something like a metal; a soft, metallic crumble that glitters with the same ghost-light as the creatures. It’s useless for building, but if you pick it up, it hums with a faint, cool energy against your skin. It’s not a tool you forged, but a piece of the world’s metabolism that you have interrupted.

To be here is to be in a place of profound and patient being. The dominant sound is the *absence* of sound, a warm, resonant hum that your own nervous system fills. The only true events are the slow formation of a tear on the bone-sky above, its silent fall, the gentle pulse of the jade-coral, and the silent, glittering drift of a thousand thousand lives that make their home in the empty light. It is a place that doesn't just exist; it *settles*. It has been this way for an age, and it will be this way for another. The only thing out of place is you, a brief, warm anomaly in a grand, slow, and hollow equilibrium.
