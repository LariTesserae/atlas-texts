The water does not move like surface water. There is no surface. This is a high-altitude abyss, a plateau of liquid pressure suspended in the cold architecture of the world. The water here is ancient, established in its erosion, having long ago carved the stone into a settled, permanent geography of deep canyons and high, drowned ridges. It does not rush; it leans. It rests with the heavy, patient stillness of something that has been here since before the concept of dryness existed.

The temperature is a pervading, paralyzing cold, just above the threshold of freezing, keeping the liquid dense and syrupy. It seeps in through every fissure, a cold that does not bite, but suffocates.
