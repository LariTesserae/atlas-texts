The sound arrives first. It is not a background hum; it is the fundamental architecture of the place, a deafening, constant, harmonious roar that feels less like something you hear and more like something you are in. It is the noise of a billion glass xylophones being struck by a billion iron fists, all at once, forever. It presses on your eardrums, vibrates in your teeth, and settles in your chest as a perpetual, resonant thrum. To try and find silence here would be like trying to find a still point in the heart of a star.

You are standing on the Floor. It is a vast, circular vault, perhaps a kilometer across, bounded by a sheer, seamless wall that curves up and away, disappearing into a churning, motionless ceiling of cloud. The scale is immense, a cathedral-not-built-by-hands, yet it feels contained, a single, finite room. The air is cool and dry, with the crisp, clean scent of ozone and stone, and it carries a faint, mineral dust that shimmers in the light. That light is the only "normal" thing here. It is a steady, sourceless glare, like a perfectly overcast day at noon, casting everything in a clear, shadowless, clinical white.

The Floor is not solid ground. It is a lattice. A grid of crystalline ribs, thirty meters high, stretches across the entire vault, creating a dizzying network of deep, empty canyons between them. The ribs themselves are made of a material that is neither stone nor metal but something in-between, a latticework of interlocking, hexagonal crystals that are faintly translucent. Through their walls, you can sometimes see a murkier, internal violet light pulse and flow, like a sluggish vascular system. This is the Great Engine, and the Floor is its heat sink, its radiation grill. There is no water here, not anywhere. The concept itself feels alien and irrelevant, like asking where the dreams are kept in a computer.

And yet, there is life. Lush, impossible life.

From the crystalline canyons below, great vine-like organisms have begun their ascent. They do not climb with tendrils; they flow upwards. They are constructs of slow, pulsing light,说出 woven from captured energy. Their foliage is not leaf but fractal patterns of hard, iridescent light that rustle like glass chimes when a current changes. They are the primary form of vegetation, vast, silent rivers of emerald and gold light spiraling up the crystal pillars, their growth a process so slow it is only visible over centuries.

The fauna, however, are anything but. The air between the towering ribs is thick with them, a living storm. They are the primary source of the overwhelming noise. These are creatures of resonance, drawn to the vibrations of the Engine.

The most common look like vast, translucent manta rays, but their bodies are taut, resonating membranes of crystal. They have no wings, yet they swim through the air, propelled by sonics. As they move, their bodies flex and tauten, and the vibrations of the Engine, filtered through their forms, become focused, piercing shrieks and impossibly deep, thrumming bellows. They are not making the sound with voices; they *are* the sound. A swarm of them passes overhead, and the roar intensifies into a cacophony of Glass Arpeggios, each creature a different note in a manic, ever-shifting symphony. The sound is physical; you can feel the pressure waves as they buffet you, threatening to unbalance you from your perch on the crystal rib.

Down below, in the canyons, other things dwell. There are the Chime-Spiders, whose legs are tuning forks of varying lengths. They do not scuttle; they strike the floors and walls of the chasms in complex rhythmic patterns, generating percussive thunder that echoes up the shafts. There are the Echo-Weavers, creatures that seem to be nothing more than knots of vibrating light-string, who spin webs of pure sound between the crystal pillars, shimmering, invisible nets that hum with a low, dangerous music. To touch one would be to be devoured by noise.

The dynamic of this place is one of turbulent equilibrium. The Engine below is in a state of perpetual, violent creation, yet its output, its chaotic energy, has been channeled into this one, stable form for millennia. The growth of the light-vines and the migrations of the sonic-mantas are not random; they are the world's way of balancing itself, of distributing the excess energy. The erosion is established, settled. The crystal ribs are not being worn down; they are being polished, their surfaces smoothed to a perfect, seamless finish by the ceaseless currents of sound and life.

Then, you see the built structures. They are scattered, rare, and heartbreaking. Down in the canyon, nestled against the base of a crystal pillar, is the skeletal outline of a single, lonely tower. It is made of the same alien crystal as the world itself, but its architecture is elegant, thoughtful, and utterly ruined. It gapes open to the sky, its floors long since shattered and cascaded into the void below. It is a relic, a message. It is clearly the product of the same high-tech, beyond-modern intelligence that conceived of this entire vault, but it has failed. It has been torn apart not by neglect, but by the very force it was meant to observe, or perhaps to regulate.

This is the strangeness and the rule of the world: you cannot build against the noise. You can only be subsumed by it. The Builders made a floor to stand on, a wall to contain the storm. But they could not quiet it. Their one remaining structure is a tomb, a warning whispered beneath a deafening roar.

You must keep moving. To stand still is to become a focal point for the vibrations. Your own body will begin to resonate. You can feel it now—a low thrum in your bones, a sympathetic vibration that longs to join the chorus. The air itself seems to thin and thicken with the oscillating pressure waves. You are a trespasser in a church of impossible physics, where the gospel is a roar, the parishioners are living tempests of sound, and the only prayer is to keep your balance on the edge of a chasm where the only water is a river of noise, and the only life is things that have learned to swim in it.
