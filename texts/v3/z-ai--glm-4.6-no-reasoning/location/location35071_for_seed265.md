The color of the light is not constant. It shifts with a glacial patience from a sickly green-gold to a deep, bruised violet, a slow, planetary respiration that is the only measure of time. Vegetation is sparse because the ground is hard and the light is strange and perhaps alien to chlorophyll. Yet, life persists. There are plants, but they are things of desperation. They clutch to the rock where the weeping water is thickest, their forms stunted and unnerving. They are not green but gray and black, with flesh like lichen and metallic, wire-like fronds that seem to draw sustenance more from the light in the water than from the water itself.
