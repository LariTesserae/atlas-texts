The first rule is the Sound.

It is not something you hear with your ears, not anymore. Your ears are repurposed. The Sound is a pressure that builds in your bones, a resonance in your teeth, a thrumming that makes the air feel thick and solid. It is the continuous, deafening, all-encompassing hum of a million kilometers of machinery, but the machinery is nothing you have ever seen. It is the deep, foundational music of the place itself, and it is everything.

The air is not still. It shimmers and dances with vibration, turning the world into a heat-haze dream. The light, from a source you can never locate, is a merciless, actinic brilliance, washing out the sky to a featureless, buzzing white. There are no clouds, only shifting patterns of refracted glare. Looking up is like staring into the heart of a welder's torch for too long; it paints phantom colors on the inside of your eyelids. This is the second rule: everything is lit, but nothing is clear.

You are in the Agora of the Root-Chime, or so the builders—the Quiet Ones—call it. It is a city, but not on a human scale. It is an urban environment of profound intimacy and staggering scale, a contradiction that the city itself resolves. You stand on a platform that is part of a single, colossal structure. The elevation is low; you are near the city’s base, its foundational molars. All around you, the structure soars, but it is not empty space. It is a convoluted, endlessly infolded landscape of halls, chambers, and bridges that contain one another like a set of nested, impossible boxes. The scale is hidden because you are always inside something that is inside something else. To see the city, you would have to be outside it, and that is a place that does not exist for the living.

There is no water. There never was. The city is built of a petrified, resinous wood, something like the heartwood of a fossilized god, but shot through with veins of dark, metallic mineral. The surfaces are incredibly hard, weathered to a glassy, almost oily smoothness by the passage of immeasurable time. The erosion here is not from wind or rain, but from the Sound itself. Chronic vibration has worn thecorners of every step, every balustrade, every ledge into soft, rounded curves, as if a million thumbs have gently worried the stone into submission. The entire place feels settled, ancient, in a perfect, humming equilibrium.

The lushness comes from the Flora-Vox. They are not plants in any terrestrial sense. They grow from the cracks and fissures in the resinous floor, from the joints in the walls, from the high, unreachable ceilings. Their bodies are made of crystalline filaments that interlock resemble geometric snowflakes or dew-frosted spiderwebs. They do not photosynthesize; they feed on the vibration. The Sound is their sun, their rain, their soil. They grow towards the source of the strongest harmonics, their crystalline structures glowing with soft, internal colors—lavender, chartreuse, deep indigo—that pulse in time to the deep, resonant frequencies of the city. To touch one is to feel the city’s voice focused to a needlepoint, a numbing, electric tingle. They are lush, a dense carpet of silent, singing light, and they are the only thing that breaks the monotony of the floor and walls.

Fauna is rare and strange. You are, for the most part, alone in the vast, echoing chambers. But sometimes you will see a Chime-Skater. It is a creature like a flattened, multi-limbed isopod fashioned from polished obsidian. It does not walk; it glides. It finds a harmonic node in the floor, a pocket of intensified vibration, and simply *rides* the standing wave, its legs splayed out for balance. It moves with an eerie, frictionless silence, its dark form a sharp void against the glowing Flora-Vox. It is a scavenger, feeding on the crystalline structures, pruning the city’s acoustical garden. It is the only other mobile thing you have ever seen, and it ignores you utterly. You are just another strange part of the noise.

The people, the Quiet Ones, are built for this place. They are slender, with large, dark eyes protected by nictitating membranes that filter the glare. They have no external ears, only smooth, oily skin over their skulls. They communicate not by sound, which would be swallowed by the hum, but by a complex language of hand-signs, facial expression, and by tapping coded rhythms on resonant surfaces—a counterpoint to the city’s endless bass note. Their technology is primitive, anachronistically so. They use tools of hardened Flora-Vox crystal, levered with carved wedges of the resinous wood. They use simple woven fibers to bind things. Their architecture is what they inherited; they do not build it, but maintain it, clearing new paths, harvesting the Vox, grooming the environmental acoustics.

The city is active, dynamic. The Sound is not constant. It shifts. New harmonics swell and fade, bass notes throb deep in the foundations, high-pitched tones whistle through distant corridors. This is the weather. A "storm" is a rising crescendo of a particular frequency that can make the entire chamber vibrate so violently that the Flora-Vox shatter and reglitter the floors. A "lull" is a sudden dip in the hum, a moment of near-silence that feels like a vacuum in the chest, causing some of the more delicate Vox-filaments to dim and wither. The Quiet Ones move with these cycles, their migrations from one chamber zone to another timed to the shifting patterns of resonance.

You are a visitor, a scholar of acoustical ecology, and the Sound is both your subject and your tormentor. You live with a constant, low-grade headache. Your bones feel like they are slowly turning to gravel. You have learned the Quiet Ones' sign language, your own hands feeling clumsy and loud. You spend your days marking the pulse of the city, trying to map its invisible weather, to understand the ecology of a world where the very air is a living, roaring force. You are an intruder in an intimate space, a speck of dust in a humming, empty, city-sized room, listening to a piece of music that has been playing for a million years and will not stop for a million more. The Sound is the first rule, and the last. It is the ground, the sky, the food, and the doom of all who dwell here, yourself included. You have come to study it, but you fear you are simply being digested by it.
