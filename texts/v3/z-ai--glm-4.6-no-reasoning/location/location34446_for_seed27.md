The first thing to understand about the Choral Maw is that there is no ground. At least, not in the way one understands solid, inert earth. To step here is to place your foot upon a surface that is simultaneously as soft as moss and as resilient as ancient stone, a surface that is thrumming with a slow, deliberate life. This is the Substrate, a planet-spanning, biological sheet that has consumed and replaced the crust in a process that began when the world was young and will conclude when the last star gutters out. It is the sole expression of evolution, the sole victor in a war that ended before animals ever drew breath.

The vegetation rating is absolute; the Substrate *is* the plant life. It is a single, continuous organism, a vast myco-floral mat woven from fibers that are somewhere between cellulose and chrysotile. It exudes a faint, cool musk of damp loam, ozone, and something vaguely like ionized honey. The light is starved, perhaps by a distant, languid sun, or perhaps by a high-altitude haze of the Substrate's own spores, creating a perpetual twilight under a bruised-lilac sky. The temperature is a consistent, penetrating chill, like the air inside a deep cave, a coolness that seems to seep not from the environment but from the slow metabolism of the ground beneath you.

Our elevation is high, which here means we inhabit vast, shallow plateaus and broad, gentle saddles between titanic mesas that are not made of rock, but of consolidated, sclerotized Substrate, their faces carved by a primordial wind into fluted, bone-like pillars. Erosion is a finished story. These are the world's final forms, worn down over eons not by water ice, but by a constant, fine-grained friction of atmosphere and the slow-grinding efforts of the living fauna. The landscape is ancient, settled, and immensely open. You can see for kilometers, an unrolling vista of muted greens, deep purples, and the grays of the hardened mesas, dotted with presences that are few and far between—this is the sparseness, the emptiness that fills the immense scale.

And those presences are the structures. The urban parameter does not speak of cities built *on* the land, but of structures grown from it, like pearls from an oyster. These are the Spires, the Cloisters, the Resonant Horns. They are not constructed, but encouraged. The inhabitants of this world, the Keepers, do not build with hammer and girder. They use tools of focused vibration, harmonic frequencies, and specific radiation to coax the Substrate into growing specific, pre-designed forms. A spiral ramp that climbs a kilometer into the sky might take a lifetime to cultivate; a resonant chamber, a dozen. This is the technology—beyond modern, a living, biological engineering. The structures are layered, complex, and deeply integrated, their walls shimmering with internal energies, their purpose tied to the soundscape.

Ah, the soundscape. It never stops. It is both loud and strange, a constant, overwhelming symphony that is the true weather of the Maw. It rises not from the sky, but from the ground itself. The Substrate is a single nervous system, a planetary ear, and it is perpetually listening to the grinding of cosmic dust on the mesas, the footfalls of its fauna, the deep-frequency transmissions from the Spires, and it sings. The sound is a deep, resonant thrum, a fabric-woven chorus of billions of voices. There are the slow, bell-like peals from geologic-anchored Spires that mark the passage of eons, and the higher, skittering arpeggios from the Horns that signal changes in atmospheric pressure. This is a world defined by its acoustics.

The fauna, too, is abundant and shaped by sound. The creatures here are not animals of flesh and blood, but of crystalline and mineralized Substrate, forged templates grown in the deep, resonant wombs of the world and released. The Skrels are common: six-legged creatures that move with a skittering, arrhythmic gait, their chitinous bodies clinking like wind chimes. They consume the Substrate, their mouthparts chipping away at it, creating new facets that slightly alter the local acoustics in a feedback loop of erosion and creation. Higher up are the so-called Glasswings, vast, leathery flyers that navigate not by sight, but by sonar, emitting piercing, harmonic shrieks that echo for miles. The apex predator is a creature rarely seen, only heard: the Tuner. It is a solitary beast that moves with the sound-absorbing grace of a void, its passage marked only by a sudden, perfect pocket of silence in the roaring chorus. Where the Tuner passes, the Substrate is observed to accelerate its growth in complex new patterns, as if "pruned" by a higher order.

The emergent rules of this world are those of resonance and decay. A Keeper's "home" is a chamber calibrated to their personal harmonic frequency, a place of perfect rest where the planet's chorus resolves into a single, soothing tone within the walls. To leave a path is to cut a different frequency into the Substrate, and over decades, it will either be healed over or encouraged into a permanent trail, depending on its utility. Travel between Spires is a calculated risk through fields of dissonance, where overlapping frequencies can create standing waves that disorient or even shatter less resilient crystalline bodies.

There is no water. The history books, kept in crystalline data-cores within the deepest Cloisters, whisper of a world where a "wet, chaotic" molecule once moved in thin films and great bodies. Now, all moisture is locked in the chemical bonds of the Substrate, a dry, mineralized fuel for its slow and eternal fire. What was once the chaotic fluid is now the structured, ordered solid of life itself.

The landscape is an active, dynamic process of a majestic, glacial pace. You walk upon the living mind of a world, feeling its slow thoughts through the soles of your feet, hearing its breath as a rolling, seismic symphony. It is a place of profound, ancient loneliness, broken only by the booming song of its own existence and the clatter of the crystal creatures that dance upon it, each one a fleeting note in a planetary song that has been playing since the beginning of time.
