The air is a warm, wet weight. It presses against the skin, not with heat, but with a tangible presence, a saturated suspension that seems to hold the landscape together. There is no sky, not in the way one remembers a sky. Instead, there is a slow, deepening gradient of gloom, a twilight that has no source. Light is not so much absent as it is dissolved, bled into the very medium of the atmosphere, which itself is almost a liquid. It is the color of old jade and moss, a light that has traveled through a million miles of chlorophyll before reaching you, arriving exhausted and green.

Here, there is no ground, not in the sense of soil or stone. The ground *is* plant. You stand upon a continent-spanning, spongy substrate of ancient, interwoven life. It yields slightly underfoot, a slow, patient memory of your weight that is relinquished a moment after you step away. This living ground is a tapestry of matte-finished greens, deep purples, and the bruised blues of rainwater-soaked cellulose. It is punctuated not by rocks, but by vast, concave leaf-scapes the size of villages, their edges thickened into gnarled, woody ridges where they meet. These are the lowlands of this world. In the shallow depressions between them, water does not flow but *sits*, a layer of crystal-clear, still water that perfectly mirrors the dimness above, creating windows into a darker, identical world below. The name for this is the 'Quiefing', the still water that is as much a feature of the plant-body as the veins in a leaf.

There is no beginning or end to this place; its vastness is not a matter of distance, but of state. You are inside the slow, ancient body of a single entity that learned to swallow a continent. The erosion of eons was not an act of wearing away, but of settling. The primordial jaggedness of mountains has long since been subdued, softened, and incorporated. Its final form is this: a near-perfect, albeit rolling, carpet of life. Everything is ancient. You feel it in the springy give of the ground, the placid stillness of the Quiefing pools, a deep geological time expressed biologically. This is not a landscape at the peak of its vitality; it is a landscape that has reached its ultimate, most stable state, a kind of serene, planetary senescence.

The architecture is entirely biological and impossibly dense. Pillars as thick as ancient redwoods are not trees, tightly woven, and self-sealed, disguised as a single, seamless surface that rises into the gloom. Trying to find the beginning or end of a single plant is a fool's errand; they are all conjoined, a ecological superorganism. The density is overwhelming. There are no empty spaces, only layers. One pushes through curtains of feathery, fern-like fronds that vibrate with a low hum, to find beneath them a lattice of woody, knot-like stems, and beneath that, a glowing fungal mycelium that pulses with a faint, cold light, the only true illumination in the place. This phosphorescence paints the undersides of broad leaves and the bellies of water-skimming creatures in transient, ghostly blues.

The fauna is abundant, but it is a strange and quiet abundance. They are not the fauna of a world of predators and prey, but of parasites and symbiotes. The air swarms with dandelion-like spores, each carrying a single, winking mot of light, drifting on imperceptible currents. Gelatinous slug-things the size of a man's arm leave shimmering trails of slime across the broad leaf-plains, grazing on the algae that grows in the Quiefing pools. Above them, manta-like creatures composed of little more than a membrane and a simple nervous system glide through the humid air, their long, trailing tendrils brushing against the vegetation, gathering moisture or spores. The dominant creatures are the Weavers. They are arthropoidal, spindly and multi-limbed, their carapaces made of the same dark, woody material as the plant-structures themselves. They do not scuttle so much as they flow over the surfaces, their movements slow and deliberate. They are the gardeners of this world. With infinitely patient limbs, they prune, guide, and splice the living substrate, clearing blockages from the water channels that run through the plant-body and tending to the fungal gardens. They do not seem to notice an observer, their purpose too ancient and too fundamental to be interrupted.

The sound, or the lack of it, is part of the strangeness. It is mostly quiet, but not a dead silence. There is a deep, pervasive, sub-audible hum, the sound of a trillion fluid-cells moving water through vast, unseen capillaries. Occasionally, a Weave will shed a carapace plate, and it will fall with a soft, hollow *thock* onto the springy ground. The only sharp sound is when a creature breaks the surface of a Quiefing pool, the sound unnaturally loud in the stillness, then swallowed again by the oppressive humidity. The air itself is so thick it seems to dampen noise, holding vibrations and refusing to let them travel far.

The built environment is a ghost, a suggestion. Scarer than any ruin is the hint of what was not made. Here and there, for an acre or more, the density thins. The ground becomes flatter, the broad, leaf-like surfaces are oriented in an almost geometric fashion, and the water pools are perfectly circular. These are Memory-Plains. The Weavers do not enter them. They feel different to the touch—firmer, less yielding. They are the scars of a place where something once stood, something so deeply integrated into the superorganism that its removal has left a permanent, sterile wound in the living body of the world. They are the scattered, faint marks of a civilization that did not build *on* this world, but *within* it, and is now long since reabsorbed.

To be here is to be inside the slow, warm, wet lungs of a sleeping god. It is a place of profound and alien peace, a geometry of life so total it erases the distinction between organism and environment, ground and flesh. The rules are simple: you are a visitor inside a body. You are sustained or tolerated by its systems. To fight them would be like a blood cell fighting a vein. The change is so slow as to be imperceptible; this world will be here, in this state, long after the last sound has faded and the last traveler's footprint has been erased by the patient, yielding, and all-encompassing green.
