The air is warm, heavy, and thick enough to feel like a thing in the lungs. Each breath is a deliberate act, like swallowing soup. There is no liquid water in the traditional sense—no sea to swim in, no rain to feel—but the very atmosphere *is* a water, a hyper-saturated, saline mist that has submerged everything to a depth of perhaps two stories. It presses in on all sides, a uniform, pearlescent gray that blurs the world into misty silhouettes and glinting facets. The light, though originating from some unseen sun above, is diffused and softened to a uniform glow, like being inside a permanent pearl. There are no animals. The builders crafted a world that does not require pollinators or decomposers. There are only processes. Mechanisms. The world is young in its construction, but ancient in its purpose. This is a post-flood refugium, not built to withstand a coming disaster, but made in the aftermath of one, a world that has already drowned and is now slowly, methodically, fabricating a way to drink. You are a visitor in a terrarium built by gods who understood metallurgy better than biology, and you are witnessing the slow, quiet, beautiful, and terrifying process of a world learning how to save itself.
