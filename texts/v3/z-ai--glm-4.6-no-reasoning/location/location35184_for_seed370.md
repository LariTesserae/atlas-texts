The sound is the first and last truth of this place.

It is not a noise that comes from anything you can point to. It has no source; it is the medium, the constant, the defining law. A deep, monolithic thrumming, a resonant chord so low and so powerful that it is felt more in the bones of your teeth and the thick fluid of your eyes than it is heard by the ears. It is the grinding groan of a fault line the size of a world, pressed together forever. It is the fundamental frequency of existence here, a vibration that has, over an eternity, shaped everything else into its current, final, and permanent form.

You stand on The Loom. It is not a place of sun or sky, but of a dense and perpetual twilight. The air is warm and humid, but not with moisture. It is humid with density, a thick, soupy atmosphere heavy with particulate matter—fine, inert dust that never settles, suspended eternally in the thrumming, making the dim light tangible. It swirls in slow, invisible eddies, catching the weak luminescence and creating a sense of being underwater, but without the coolness or wetness.

There is no water. Not a drop. The concept is alien. There is no porous stone, no thirsty dust, no memory of rain. Everything is sealed, non-porous, smooth.

The ground beneath your feet is a floor of interlocking, coppery tiles, each the size of a tombstone. They fit together perfectly, their seams invisible, polished by eons of frictionless vibration to a glassy, seamless sheen that glimmers with a dim, reddish light. The copper is not the metal we know; it is a denser, harder variant, something closer to ceramic, stained and fired by an ancient heat. These tiles, all of them, taper upwards, forming a vast, shallow bowl or a colossal socket, a basin a mile across. This is the entire world you can perceive, a space the size of a grand cathedral, but a cathedral carved into a single, immense, solid object.

The walls of this basin are its defining feature. They are densely packed, impossibly so. They are vertical, ascending into the gloom beyond the reach of the light, and they are made of pillars. Billions of them, perhaps more. Pillars of the same hard coppery material, some slender as needles, others as thick as ancient trees, packed shoulder-to-shoulder, without a single finger's width of space between them. They rise from the floor and stretch into the shadowed ceiling, their surfaces carved, etched, and worn by the never-ending vibration. The carvings are not pictures or words in any known script. They are patterns of stress, geometric whorls, lines of resonance, the physical records of the sound that has been playing here since before time. They are a map of the vibration itself, etched into the substance of reality.

There are no plants. The airless, waterless chemistry forbids it. There are no animals. The environment offers nothing to sustain them, and the relentless, low-frequency drone would pulp soft tissue into jelly in minutes. Ecology here is a different concept. Life, if it ever existed, has been sublimated. The only thing that can be said to "live" here is the vibration itself. It is the sole actor, the sole process. It is an agent of stasis, not change. It has eroded nothing, worn nothing down. Its effect is to polish, to harmonize, to settle every component into its final, perfect, non-reactive state. This is not a ruin in decay; it is a structure in its ultimate, stable equilibrium, so settled that nothing can ever happen again.

Everything is built, not grown. The scale might be cathedral-like, but the hand is that of a titan. The tech is simple—no circuitry, no mechanisms, beyond simple hand-carving that defies the scale. The pillars show the marks of tools, crude pickaxes and chisels that bit into the soft, yielding coppery stuff before it was fired hard by eons of resonance. A primitive, colossal effort, undertaken long ago for a purpose now forgotten.

The entire place is a single, self-contained machine with no moving parts. The sound is the engine, and its function is to maintain its own perfect state. The dim light comes not from a sun, but from the vibrational stress of the material itself. It is a form of triboluminescence, the light of constant, microscopic friction within the crystal lattice of the copper, a glow that will last as long as the thrumming continues.

You are an anomaly, an impossible variable in this solved equation. There are no gradients, no winds, no currents of any kind, save the invisible vibration. Every atom in your body is being battered by that deep, physical pressure, urging you to either vibrate in harmony or be shaken apart. To stand still is to fight the very law of this place. To lie down is to be pressed into the floor, not by weight, but by sound.

There is no horizon, no door, no escape. Just the densely packed, infinity-upon-infinity of the stone forest, rising into a lightless, deafening dark. It is an echo chamber for a chord that has been holding forever, and will continue to hold forever. The rules are simple: endure the thrum, or be erased by it. And never, ever, expect any of it to change.
