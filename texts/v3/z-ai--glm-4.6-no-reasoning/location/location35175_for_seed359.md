There is no ground, not in the way one expects. There is a floor, but it is not earth. It is a lattice-work of pale, bone-like material, a scaffold of woven filaments that extends in every direction as far as the eye can see, with a horizon that is not a line but a gentle, hazy curve upwards. This is the Under-Song, and you are standing on one of its many lofts. The water parameter is effectively zero; there is no liquid here, only the profound stillness of a non-aqueous void, the air itself feeling thin and thirsty, though you never feel the need to drink. The structure seems to drink for you, for everything.

The vegetation parameter reads lush, a contradiction that resolves the moment you look closer. The abundance is not of soft leaves and damp grass, but of crystalline flora. Every available surface—the thick beams of the floor, the walls that rise like organ pipes to a distant, unseen ceiling, the archways that frame impossible vistas—is encrusted with life. It grows in dense, layered saturation, like frost on a frozen window. There are the Glass-Stalks, slender shafts of transparent amber that draw in the normal, calm light from above and channel it downwards in shimmering veins. There are the Vine-hearts, thick ropes of fibrous, spongy material that pulse with a slow, internal bioluminescence, a chartreuse glow that waxes and wanes with the patient rhythm of the environment. Sprouting from these are clusters of Bell-Blossoms, delicate, chime-like cups that ring not with wind—there is no wind—but with minute, thermal expansions, their tones a barely audible, sweet plinking, like glass gently tapped. The air smells of heated quartz, ozone, and a sweet, dusty cumin.

The warmth is everywhere, a gentle, pervasive heat that radiates not from a sun but from the structure itself. The temperature is a constant, humming the low C-sharp that is the ambient frequency of this place. It is the heat of a vast, slow metabolism. The scale is deceptive. Viewed from a single vantage, the area seems the size of a grand cathedral or a baroque chamber, defined by arches and floating platforms. But to walk in any direction is to find the pattern repeating, unending, a fractal landscape that is both room-sized and infinite. It’s a hamlet-scale reality tiled across a conceptual plane. The erosion is young; the edges of the latticework are sharp, the crystalline growths are unblemished, and there are no signs of decay, only accretion. Everything feels freshly emerged, as if it was woven into existence moments ago and has been growing, impossibly, ever since.

The fauna is abundant, but it is life that has adapted to this built, primitive world. They are the Sculptors. Spider-like creatures the size of a human hand, their bodies not of flesh but of articulated, polished obsidian and glinting copper, move with silent purpose. They are the artisans of this realm, their simple, primitive technology their very being. Their front manipulator-arms are fundamentally a loom and a spindle, a pair of fine-tipped tools that glisten with a Naturally-occurring resin. You watch one as it tends to a section of a wall, its legs tapping out a complex rhythm on the bone-like substrate. It spins a new filament of the pale, structural material from a nozzle in its abdomen, feeding the fresh thread into the lattice, mending a flaw too minute for you to perceive. The sound of its work is a soft, percussive *tik-tik-tik-tik*, the only sound that travels far in the quiet air, adding to the overall soundscape of faint, distant chimes and a low, resonant hum.

Everything is built. The total construction parameter is absolute. There is no bedrock, no natural soil. The ground you stand on is but the most recent layer of weaving. The walls are ancient, dense tapestries of generations of growth and mending. It is a fully-fabricated world, a closed system where the environment and its inhabitants are one and the same. The Sculptors do not *build* a place; they *are* the place, its slow thinking, its constant repair, its gradual evolution.

And this is where the weirdness takes hold, the breakdown of reality. As you watch a Sculptor work, you notice something. It is not mending a flaw. It is mending a *memory*. You focus on the spot it tends to, and for a fleeting second, you see a flicker of what was there a moment before—not a crack in the material, but a ghost-image of a different pattern, a previous state of reality. The Sculptor is erasing it, weaving the "now" over the "then." The world is not stable. It is a consensus, constantly overwritten. The slow dynamic is the rate of this reality maintenance. Sometimes, the system misses a spot.

Look there, a curling Vine-heart. For an instant, it uncurls and splayed out into a fan of metallic leaves, a configuration it held a thousand cycles ago. Listen. In a quiet alcove, a Bell-Blossom does not chime with a single, clear note, but with a garbled chord of three notes it played simultaneously a decade ago. These are temporal echoes, moments where erasure has failed and past futures bleed through. The fauna is not just present; it is an abundance of hauntings. You catch glimpses of creatures in your peripheral vision: a flock of things like shimmering, origami birds that fly through the solid beams; a herd of six-legged, silent grazers with coats of pure light that consume the glow from the Vine-hearts and leave behind dimmed patches that will brighten only in a future cycle. They are harmless, because they are no more real than the memory of a dream, artifacts of the slow, gradual change this place is defined by. They are the dynamism of a world whose flux is so weak that its past cannot fully let go.

To be here is to exist on a tapestry being woven and re-woven by a silent, spidery priesthood. You walk on a structure that is both a floor and a history, breathe an air that smells of its own creation, and live in a world so dense with presence that the past is a physical, if flickering, stratum. You are in the loom of a god that is forgetting how to dream, and its forgotten dreams are crystal, and they grow everywhere.
