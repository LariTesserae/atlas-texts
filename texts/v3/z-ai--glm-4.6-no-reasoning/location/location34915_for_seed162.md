It does not have a name. To name it would be to claim some dominion over its logic, and its logic is still being written, still arguing with itself. It is a place of high-altitude consequence, a vastness so grand it generates its own weather, its own tides of unreality. The air is thin here, a mere suggestion of atmosphere, but it is heavy with a warmth that feels more like radiance than heat. It is the warmth of a forge viewed through frosted glass, a constant, intimate pressure against the skin.

Young is not the right word for it. Fledgling is closer. It is a world in the throes of a violent, creative puberty. The ground beneath your feet is not ancient stone but something still settling arguments with itself. Here, the bedrock is a glassy, obsidian-like substance, black and slick with a constant, thin sheen of condensation. But it is not smooth. It is shattered. Jutted. Pinnacles and mesas of this black crystal punch upwards, their edges so atom-sharp they seem to hum with a latent violence. They are the teeth of a newborn god, still too new to have been worn down by wind or time.

But time runs differently here. The erosion is not a patient water or wind. It is a constant, dynamic trauma. The land is in a state of perpetual, chaotic upheaval. You can stand on a ledge of black glass and watch, ten meters away, the ground sublimate—not melt, not explode, but dissolve into a shimmering heat-haze and recondense as a new, impossible geometry. A spire might collapse into a silent avalanche of glittering shards, only for those shards to writhe and knit themselves together inches from the ground, coalescing into a floating, breathing geode that hums a low, infra-note before drifting away on the non-existent breeze. This is the turbulence: the landscape itself as a living, churning engine of creation and destruction.

The vegetation, despite the intermittent moisture, is lush in a way that defies conventional botany. It doesn't grow from the soil so much as it precipitates from the damp warmth of the air. The primary form is a phosphorescent moss, a thick, velvety carpet of blues and greens and deep violets that carpets the less-vertical surfaces. It drinks the ever-present condensation, glowing with a soft, internal light that is the main source of illumination in this dim place. This light is not bright; it is a suffused, ethereal glow, like being inside a cloud during a thunderstorm at twilight. It makes the shifting shadows of the crystal pinnacles dance and writhe.

Interwoven with the moss are more complex structures. There are vines that are not vines, but rather branching conductors of light, their hollow, translucent forms carrying pulses of cool brilliance from one glowing patch of moss to another. And there are the Wells: where the ground stays stable long enough, deep, cup-shaped formations of the black rock pool the condensation. In these Wells, strange flora blossoms: discs of membrane-thin tissue, like luminous lily pads, that pulse rhythmically. They ripple, then contract, releasing a mist of spores into the warm, damp air before refilling with gathered dew. The air itself smells of ozone, wet stone, and something sweetly organic, like honey-dew and bruised orchids.

The fauna is abundant and bizarre, born for this unstable reality. They are creatures of transition. Skitterers that phaseshift through solid rock, their forms glimpsed as distorted ripples in the crystal for a heartbeat before they solidify on the other side. There are vast, airborne beings that look like manta rays made of stitched-together shadows and glinting light, drift-leeching on the energy hum of the forming geodes. They move with a horrifying silence, their passage felt only as a sudden drop in pressure and a brief, localized eclipse of the moss-glow.

It is the engine noise of creation. Following this thrum deeper into the vastness leads to the Foundries. These are not built structures, but zones of hyper-active reality. Here, the land does not simply shift; it is being actively manufactured. Great rifts yawn open, not into darkness, but into a blinding, sourceless white light from which the raw material of this universe extrudes. You can watch streams of molten possibility pour out, cooling instantly into the sharp, black glass.
