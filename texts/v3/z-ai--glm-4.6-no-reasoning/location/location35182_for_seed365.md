This is a place that breathes, though it has no lungs. It is a city built within the ribcage of something vast and newly dead, or perhaps, newly born. The scale is not grand in the way of plains or oceans, but in the way of a single, intricate structure that contains all the knowledge you need. It feels like a room stretched to its breaking point, a cathedral whose every buttress and arch is a living, damp thing.

The air is the first thing you notice. It’s cool enough to raise a fine sheen of condensation on your skin, a constant, gentle dampness that has nothing to do with recent rain. It’s the humidity of breath, of a billion small lungs exhaling into a contained space. The light is a profound, blue-tinted dimness. It’s not the absence of light, but its overwhelming presence in a muted form. The source is the “sky” itself—a vaulted ceiling close enough to feel intimate, so far away to be mythical. It is a membrane, a bioluminescent phosphorescence that pulses with a slow, diurnal rhythm. Waxing, it bathes the city in a soft, ethereal glow, like twilight trapped under glass. Waning, it plunges the world into a deep gloom where only the most urgent life forms generate their own light.

You are standing on a causeway of fused, dark stone. It is not hewn, but grown. The causeways are woven between titanic, pale structures that rise on all sides. These are the buildings, the towers, the homes of this city. They are not built of brick or wood, but of a porous, bone-like material, shot through with veins of vibrant green. It is the fossilized cartilage of some leviathan, young enough that its edges are still scalpel-sharp, resisting the patient caress of time. The erosion parameter here reveals its truth: this entire formation is fresh. Landslides are not events of crumbling age, but sharp, sudden splinterings, as if a colossal creature shifted in its sleep. There are no rounded stones, only shrapnel and shards.

Water is the lifeblood, yet it is nowhere to be seen in a river or lake. It exists as a constant film. It beads on the bone-towers, runs in silent, glittering trickles down the walls, and pools in the cupped hands of giant, fossilized leaves that serve as plazas and courtyards. In the deepest crevasses, the water gathers into clear, cold, perfectly still cisterns, reflecting the pulsing glow of the ceiling like liquid sky. Every surface is slick with this intermittent moisture, making footing a careful art.

The vegetation is lush, but it is a parasite’s lushness. It does not grow *from* the ground so much as *into* it. This is a world in which plants are the primary architect and destructurer. Thick, glossy vines, the colour of polished jade, writhe over the bone-structures. They insinuate themselves into every crack, their pressure widening fissures and prying apart plates of fossil matter. In the dim light, these vines are the brightest green, and they sport colossal, bell-shaped flowers that hang like heavy chandeliers. These blossoms the size of wine barrels are the only consistent sources of sound. At random intervals, often hours apart, one will slowly unfurl its petals with a deep, resonant *thrum*, like a plucked cello string. The sound travels through the humid air without echo, a sacramental note that hangs in the silence before fading. After the sound, the flower's interior glows with a soft, inner light, attracting the strangest of congregations.

The fauna is utterly overwhelming, not in number, but in presence and strangeness. It is a silent, populated chaos. Through the humid air drift creatures that are equal parts insect and manta ray, their membranous wings translucent, catching the dim light and diffusing it into prismatic smears. They navigate not by sight but by a constant, low-frequency hum you feel in your teeth more than you hear with your ears. On the bone-walkways, things skitter. Multi-legged arthropods with obsidian shells mill about, their movements precise and eerily coordinated. They are the city’s maintenance crew; you see them cleaning debris with their delicate forelegs, polishing surfaces with their abdomens, pruning the more aggressive vines with their crystalline mandibles. They are the urban element, the organized infrastructure of this living city, working with a primitive, biological tech.

Higher up, clinging to the sheer, exo-skeletal walls, are larger, more solitary hunters. They resemble elongated salamanders fashioned from obsidian and mother-of-pearl, their bodies blending perfectly with the damp, striated bone. They move with a liquid stillness, and when they strike, it is without preamble—a silent, impossibly fast lunge that captures one of the drifting ray-insects in a gape of needle-thin teeth. The ecosystem is active but quiet, a pantomime of life and death played out in the blue-lit gloom.

The built environment intertwines with the biological. Here and there, you see signs of habitation by another kind of resident—the builders, urbanites of this strange place. They do not construct; they modify. Dwellings are hollowed-out nodules in the bone-towers, and entrances are draped with woven curtains of living, symbiotic moss that responds to touch by contracting. Tools are primitive but effective: sharpened spurs of the local stone used for carving, needles of hardened vine-thorn for sewing hides of fallen creatures. There is no metal here, no fire large enough to smelt it. The preponderance of dampness makes open flame an impossibility, a foreign concept. Light and heat are coaxed biology—phosphorescent fungi cultivated in clay pots, body-warmth conserved in nests of shed shells.

The rules of this world are unspoken but felt. Silence is a survival strategy. Sound is an event, a beacon. The dim light is a lie; the true sight is tactile, a reading of vibrations through the soles of your feet and the damp air. The ground is not solid, but a temporary scaffold, forever being reshaped by the patient, unstoppable force of the green world. To live here is to be a custodian of a moment, a brief organism inhabiting the early, violent, and beautiful youth of a geological formation that is also, unmistakably, alive. It is a city on a cusp, caught between being a corpse and being a cradle, where every breath is shared with the architecture, and every shadow is thick with the silent, overwhelming weight of life, waiting.
