There is no water here. Not in the air, not in the ground, not bound in any cell. And yet, this place is not dry—dryness implies the memory of moisture, the longing for rain. This place has transcended dryness. It is a sealed, aqueous-null ecology, a planetary-scale terrarium where the very concept of hydration is a foreign, ancient absurdity.

You stand on a surface that yields slightly beneath your weight, warm as skin left in the shade. The elevation is virtually nil; the horizon does not dip or rise, but stretches flat and infinite in every direction, lost eventually in the haze of its own vastness. This is a continental shelf of living architecture, a planet-spanning floor of manufactured biomass. Everything here is built. There is no bedrock, no natural substrate, no stone. The ground is a substrate of pure plant-matter, but it is not soil—it is engineered cellulose, a kilometers-deep weave of rhizome and root that has been deliberately grown into the shape of foundations, load-bearing pillars, and vast, flat thoroughfares. 

The vegetation is not merely present; it *is* the space. It is the total environment. Towering structures rise from the flat plain—cathedrals of segmented bark and woven vine, their walls grown rather than assembled, their interiors packed solid with layer upon layer of foliage. The density is staggering, near-suffocating. Everywhere is touching everything else. There is no empty volume. Leaves overlap in sclerotic folds; stems fuse into load-bearing columns; canopies interlock into a continuous, continent-spanning ceiling. It is a pure-plant ecosystem, but perverted by industry—a jungle of gears and green.

Because the tech is industrial, though it lacks the smoke and slag of a metal world. The factories are alive. You walk past a massive silo, three hundred meters tall, its hull comprised of thick, engineered bark. From its apex, a steady pulse of clear, anhydrous sap—industrial coolant—bleeds into a network of ceramic-veined vines. Within the silo, engine-flowers bloom: massive, stamen-like pistons pump slowly up and down, driven by the metabolic heat of the plant itself. The mechanical and the botanical are indistinguishable. Wires are roots; electricity is photosynthetic voltage; scaled production is simply a matter of adjusting the hormonal triggers of the architecture. The whole plain hums with the slow, rhythmic churning of a world-sized engine-flora.

The light is blinding. A severe, high-luminosity star beats down on this world, but the light that reaches the interior is heavily filtered, refracted through millions of translucent, silica-infused leaves. It creates a perpetual, glaring greenhouse noon—sharp, shadowless, and exhausting. The light is so bright it flattens depth perception, making the dense, overlapping layers of the plant-architecture look like a flat, infinite painting. 

And yet, despite the continental scale and the industrial pulse, the world is eerily quiet. Sound is dampened by the sheer density of the biological matter. The soft, fibrous walls absorb the mechanical clatter of the engine-flowers; the immense root-networks swallow the vibrations of the pumping sap. What reaches the ear is a profound, low-frequency silence, punctuated only by the subtlest sounds: the faint, wet *click* of a chloroplast relay switching on, the whisper of a million leaves micro-adjusting their angles to the light. It is the silence of a well-insulated machine running at full capacity.

The temperature sits in a strange, cool equilibrium. Despite the blazing external light and the immense metabolic heat generated by the industrial flora, the engineered environment maintains a steady, cool temperature through hyper-efficient, non-aqueous transpiration. Coolants derived from liquid siloxanes and pressurized gases run through the xylem, bleeding heat away. The air feels like the inside of a server room—sharply cool, moving with a slow, deliberate ventilation.

Fauna is a ghost category here. You might walk for a hundred kilometers and see nothing that moves on its own volition. The ecosystem is a closed loop of plant and machine. The rare animal life that does exist has been entirely subsumed by the architecture; bio-engineered pollinators are permanently fused to the stamens they service, their wings vestigial, their bodies now just another moving part of the flower-engine. 

The erosion parameter tells of a world that has found its balance. This is not a young, aggressive growth, nor a dying one. The forms are weathered, settled into their own long-existence. The bark is burnished smooth by the constant, slow circulation of chemical winds. The interlocking leaves have settled into perfect, friction-minimizing tessellations. It is a landscape in equilibrium, a machine that has been running so long it has worn its own parts into perfect smoothness.

But the equilibrium is deeply wrong. The weirdness here is not a surface detail; it is a breakdown of the physics that should govern a place like this. Space feels soft. Walk in a straight line, and you will find your trajectory bending slightly left, pulled by the localized gravity of a particularly dense thicket of processing-root. The light plays tricks that fibrous leaves cannot explain—you see the shadow of a structure cast against a wall, but the structure is nowhere to be found, as if the architecture is casting shadows of things that used to exist, or things that will be grown next cycle. If you press your hand against the bark of an engine-tree, you feel the wood *breathing*, but the rhythm is backwards—it inhales when the piston descends, exhales when it rises. The boundaries of the self blur here. You realize the root-network is not just transmitting nutrients; it is transmitting topology. The landscape is actively thinking, and as it thinks, the distances between points subtly contract and expand. You take a step toward a distant silo, and the silo takes a step toward you.

This is the Green Engine. A vast, silent, continental factory of total vegetation, operating without water, running on light and strange chemistry, humming in the cool silence of its own reality-breaking computations. It is a world where plant and machine have long since eaten each other, digesting their differences into a singular, infinite, quietly pulsating architecture that is slowly, patiently, solving for something in the quiet, bright air.
