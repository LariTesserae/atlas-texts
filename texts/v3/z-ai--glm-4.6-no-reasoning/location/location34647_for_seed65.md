The first thing is the sound.

Vegetation is sparse, more a memory of life than a presence. The only thing that grows here is the Glass-moss. It is not a moss but a lichen, a crystalline, fibrous growth that hugs the rock where the condensation is thickest. It is the colour of tarnished brass, but where the bright light strikes it directly, it refracts, shattering the glare into a hundred tiny rainbows. It has no discernible leaves, only a dense, felt-like mesh of translucent threads that chime faintly, a microtonal addition to the hum, when a breeze moves over them. Nothing else grows. No trees, no grasses, no flowers. The kingdom of bone-grey rock and silver-thriving Glass-moss is absolute.
