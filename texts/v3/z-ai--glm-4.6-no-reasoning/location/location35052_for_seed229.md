It begins with the sound.

Before the eyes adjust, before the body understands the warmth or the feet register the surface, there is a roaring, chattering, click-clacking cacophony that fills the skull. It is not the sound of wind, though there is wind; nor the sound of water, for there is none. It is the sound of millions of hard bodies snapping against stone, and against each other, in a relentless, turbulent hailstorm of life.

This is the Drum—high altitude, yes, but compressed into a single, cathedral-sized pocket. You stand in a natural amphitheater, perhaps three hundred paces across, suspended in the high reaches of a great, ancient plateau. The walls are sheer, weathered basalt, worn smooth by epochs of abrasive dust, forming a near-perfect bowl. The erosion here is not wearing things away; it is a finished state, a balance attained after incalculable time. The stone is settled, rounded, comfortable in its skin. 

And it is dry. Desperately, absolutely dry. The air tastes of alkali and pulverized shell. There is no moisture here, no dew, no humidity. To breathe is to inhale a faint, warm grit. And yet—this place is obscenely alive.

The floor of the amphitheater is a sparse, scattered garden of the bizarre. Growing from cracks in the bone-dry stone are thick, fleshy stalks of a native flora that has never known a drop of rain. They are chitin-plants—husks of translucent, amber and violet material, as hard as fingernails on the outside, but filled inside with a dense, non-aqueous lymph, thick as syrup, pressurized and hot. They grow in clumps, leaving wide swathes of bare, dusty stone between them. There are no leaves, only fractal tangles of hollow, jointed tubes that hiss faintly in the dimness, releasing puffs of heavier-than-air gas that pools in the low spots like invisible fog. They are in constant, violent motion, snapping and thrashing, driven by the hyperactive, boiling lymph inside them. The plants are twitching. Not gently, not in a summer breeze, but shuddering, whipping, chittering. They knock against the stone and each other with a hard, castanet clatter, contributing to the din. The entire grove feels like an engine, a turbulence of biological and geothermal feedback.

And then there are the keepers of the Drum. The fauna. They are everywhere, abundant and strange. Shells the size of fists, moving on a forest of clacking, crystalline legs. They have no eyes, only vibrating, comb-like mandibles that taste the hot, dry air. They swarm over the thrashing plants, over the dusty stone, over each other. They make a sound like a thousand dried seed-pods rattling in a sack. Some have a single, overdeveloped chitin-hammer on their backs, which they beat against the stone in rhythmic, asynchronous pulses, a syncopated percussion that rolls and echoes off the amphitheater walls. The air is so thick with their clicking that it forms a standing wave of noise, a physical pressure in the warm, amber dimness. The sound is a weight. It vibrates in the teeth.

And in the sparseness, the scattered built forms. A cairn, waist-high, constructed from the shed hammers of the shell-creatures. It is a primitive thing, stacked with an impossible, intuitive genius—no mortar, no water, just dry stone and dry chitin interlocking in a spiral. There is a single shelter, a low, domed hut made of the hollow, amber husks of the dead plants, capped with a single, massive, bowl-shaped shell. Inside, the noise is slightly muted, but the heat is immense, trapped by the chitin walls. The floor is a mosaic of cracked, dry salt, the only flat, horizontal surface in the Drum. Here, a hand-tool lies: a length of basalt lashed with gut to a chitin pincer. The lashing is expert, the gut is from some nameless, non-aqueous beast.

The world here is a closed system, a sealed, dry engine. The heat comes from below. The sound comes from the life, which is abundant, chitinous, and violent. The motion is the norm. Nothing is still. The plants heave, the shell-creatures click and hammer, the very stone seems to vibrate with the aggregate thrum. It is a lush, deafening, dry and primitive pandemonium, a cathedral of chitin and percussion, humming with the turbulent, settled, ancient, eyeless, and absolute.
