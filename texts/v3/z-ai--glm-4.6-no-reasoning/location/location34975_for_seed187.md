It is a place you suspect cannot be mapped from above, not because it is vast, but because it folded in on itself long ago. This is the Grove, though ‘grove’ implies a collection of distinct trees, and here there are no trees. There is only The Mass.

The Mass is the sole substrate. It is an ancient, warm-barked sponge, a continent of interconnected plant life that has settled over eons into its final, stable form. It absorbs all light, all moisture, all sensation. To stand upon it is to stand upon the living skin of a world that has chosen to stop becoming. Its surface is not flat but a complex, shallow topography of soft, sometimes spongy, sometimes firmer mounds and gentle depressions. The colour is a uniform, deep greenish-brown, the hue of wet moss and old wood, uninterrupted by flowers or vibrant leaves. The air is cool, persistently damp, and smells of rich, wet earth, of decay that is not death but a constant, patient redistribution of life from one part of the Mass to another.

Water is scarce but ever-present. It does not fall as rain. Instead, it is breathed into existence. In the deepest of the shallow depressions, where the temperature is lowest, a soft, perpetual condensation weeps from the surface of the Mass. The cool air, saturated with the Mass’s own transpiration, surrenders its moisture in silent, constant beads. These droplets gather, coalesce, and begin a slow, deliberate journey downwards. They are absorbed almost immediately by the porous flesh of the Mass, but they leave a slick, dark trail, a momentary vein of shining obsidian in the otherwise matte landscape. There are no rivers, no pools, only these fleeting, thirsty streams that vanish as soon as they are born.

The space is defined by its hollowness. Though the vegetation parameter is total, the density is almost nothing. The Mass grows into colossal, cathedral-like ribs and buttresses that arch towards a distant, unseen ceiling. These structures are spaced far apart, creating a series of vast, interconnected rooms. There is a profound sense of emptiness between them. You may walk for ten minutes across the spongy floor, the silence so deep you can hear the low thrum of your own blood, before encountering the next wall of living, slowly breathing plant-matter. The scale is immense yet intimate; you are always within sight of a boundary, yet the space between those boundaries feels like a world unto itself.

The sound is what shatters the stillness. It is loud, mechanical, and relentless. Thrumming, heavy, rhythmic. This is the Grove’s single, non-changing function. Embedded deep within the arches and walls of the Mass are the Engines. They are not built structures, but organs. They are the industrial heart of this ecosystem, the final evolutionary destination of this plant life. Each Engine is a vast, woody, fibrous sphere, perhaps thirty feet in diameter, cradled in a lattice of living material. Its surface is ridged with piston-like rods of hardened cellulose, which pump with a slow, inexorable beat. The deep, resonant *WHUMP-whump-whump* of a dozen Engines working in concert is the only true sound in the Grove. They draw water from the atmospheric condensation, they draw sugars from the Mass. They power the circulation of nutrients, the slow osmotic pressures that keep the entire organism in perfect, frozen equilibrium. The sound is the sound of life forever maintaining itself, a factory designed to sustain a single, perfect product: stasis.

This thrumming is the crucible of the Grove’s fauna. The sound is not a byproduct; it is a requirement. The place is abundant with life, but it is life that has adapted to serve the Engines, or to parasites them. The most common creatures are the Chimes. They are insectoid, with crystalline, membranous wings like panes of mica. Their bodies are sleek and black, and they live in swarms, flying through the hollows. But they do not flap their wings; those are for steering and gliding. They propel themselves with a rapid, percussive clicking of the hardened plates on their underbellies, a percussion that vibrates in sympathy with the Engines’ low frequency. The air, when a swarm passes, is filled with a shimmering, metallic chorus of clicks and chimes, a fleeting counter-melody to the deep, foundational bass. They feed on the sugary resins that seep from the casings of the Engines and are its essential pollinators, carried on their legs as they migrate from one Engine-room to the next.

There are other things. Larger, silent grazers methodically scrape algae from the damp surfaces where condensation flows. They are heavy, armoured things that move with the glacial slowness of the landscape itself. And in the shadows where the Engine-light is dimmest, predators wait, their bodies shaped by the hateful acoustic laws of this place. They do not see but listen with immense, sensitive plates on their carapaces, waiting for a tell-tale dissonance in the Chimes’ song, or the softer, tell-tale clicking of a lost grazer’s leg on the creaking floor.

The light is normal, but artificial. It comes from the Engines. A soft, steady, blue-white luminescence diffuses from their surfaces, pulsing gently with each pump. It does not warm, only illuminates. It casts long, clear shadows that make the distances between the arches seem even more profound. The light is constant, just as the temperature is constant, just as the sound is constant. This is a world in a perfect, unchanging stasis. The erosion parameter is ancient because the process is complete; the Mass has grown and eroded and folded its way into a form so stable that change is now a theoretical concept, not a practical reality.

There are no constructed things here, no artifacts of another mind, because there has never been a chance for one to exist. The Grove’s industrial nature is not a thing imposed upon it; it is what it became. It is a place that, in its final act of becoming, chose the machine. It is a factory without a workforce, a power station without a city, a landscape whose only purpose is to power its own, silent, unending existence. To be here is to feel the immense, indifferent weight of a system that has achieved equilibrium and will tolerate no deviation. You are a fleeting ghost in a machine built to run forever.
