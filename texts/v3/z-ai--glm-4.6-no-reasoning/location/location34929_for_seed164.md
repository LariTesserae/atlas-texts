Here, the sky is not empty, but it is hollow. It is a reservoir of cold, thin light that holds no warmth. Below it, a city of incomparable scale sits on a plateau that is the roof of the world.

The ground is young. It has not been smoothed by the patient grinding of ages. Instead, it is a broken, splintered thing, a fresh wound in the planet's crust thrust up into the chill. Great black slabs of basalt, feldspar-rich granite the color of bruised plums, and veins of obsidian so pure they are windows into non-existence, have been shoved up at violent, geometric angles. The edges are knife-sharp, the corners still holding the memory of their cataclysmic birth. There are no rounded hills here, no gentle valleys. Only the stark, jagged planes of a geology still new to the air. This is the Elevation: a platform so high that the atmospheric pressure is permanently low, a thing perched on the brink of the void.

Yet for all this harshness, there is a constant, patient weeping of water. The vegetation is lush, a paradox woven into the fabric of the place. Water does not fall from the sky in rain. It condenses. It squeezes itself from the very minerals of the rock and the thin air itself, gathering along the thermal gradients of the newly emerged stones. This water is not a flood, nor a torrent. It is a constant, silent dew that beads on every surface, a slow-motion weeping that gathers in the crevices and runs in thin, transparent rivulets down the stark faces of the cliffs. The sound is not the roar of a waterfall but the pan-dimensional sigh of vapor becoming liquid, a hundred million whispers blended into a single, omnipresent hiss.

And then there is the city.

A vast, sprawling metropolis forged from the same dark, sharp-edged stone as the plateau itself, as if the engineers simply commanded the crust to rise in the shape of habitation. It is industrial, but not in the way of soot and smoke. This is industrialism on a planetary, almost geological, scale. The city is a machine for living, and its processes are immense and loud.
