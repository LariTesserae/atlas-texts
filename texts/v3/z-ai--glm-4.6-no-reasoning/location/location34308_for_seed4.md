This place is a pocket. A cavity stolen from the cold, pressed into the lowland like a thumb into clay. You enter through a breach in a living wall—a sphagnum throat that contracts behind you, leaving only the dark and the smell of iron and wet cellulose.

**Terrain and Substrate**
There is no stone here. The ground is a churn of peat and root-mass, spongy and unreliable, giving beneath your feet with a cold seep that rises immediately to the ankle. It is a young landscape, freshly erupted from the bog, its edges still sharp where the root-walls shear upward. Water does not flow here; it exudes. It weeps from the surfaces of things. The air is so saturated that condensation runs down the vertical planes in slow, constant tears, pooling in the crotches of branches, trembling, falling. You can hear the microscopic drip of it—a sound so continuous it becomes silence, a textured quiet.

**Architecture and Vegetation**
The construction is vegetable. This is a town grown, not built. Wooden palisades are living roots, still sucking at the wet dark. Walkways are thick mats of reed and living moss, spongy bridges strung between the buttresses of massive, slow-breathing trunks. Dwellings are gourds the size of huts, hollowed by rot and hand, their interior walls lined with shelf-fungus and pale lichen. Doorways are hung with curtains of hanging moss, always dripping. The builders have used no metal; everything is wedged, tied with sinewy vine, or grown into place. The architecture breathes. The walls contract in the cold, squeezing the interior spaces tighter. It is sparse—there are only a few dozen structures, spaced far apart across the root-maze, each one an island of purpose in a sea of wet mulch.

**Darkness and Light**
Light does not reach here. The canopy above is absolute—a lid of interlocking frond and branch that steals even the memory of the sun. The dark is not empty; it is a medium, thick as gelatin, tinted faintly green where bioluminescent fungi spot the ceiling like moldy stars. These glows are sparse and weak, serving only to make the darkness visible, to outline the wet blackness of the stems and the pale bellies of the gourds.

**Fauna—The Overwhelming Presence**
This is the terror and the truth of the place: the fauna. Not large, but inescapable. The dark is *moving*. The air is a fluid of insects—motes of wing and chitin so dense they fill your lungs if you breathe too deep. The surfaces of the dwellings are alive with crusts of pale, blind arthropods, clicking and grooming each other in endless, mechanical cycles. The water in the pools is not water; it is a slurry of larvae, writhing like a single organism, breathing as one. Things with too many legs hunt things with no eyes across the moss-bridges. It is a churning, squirming ecology in absolute overdrive, a predator-prey engine running hot in the freezing dark. You feel them on your skin immediately—the settling, the crawling, the investigation. You are a newcomer, and you are permeable.

**Atmosphere and Dynamic**
It is freezing. The cold is a damp cold that rots fabric and numbs bone, a refrigerator-bleak chill that preserves the dead but keeps the living in a state of slow, wet misery. And yet, the place is violently active. The walls of the gourds pulse faintly as sap slows and surges. Spores launch from puffballs in soft, invisible explosions. The moss grows visibly—if you sit long enough, you can feel the thread-fine push of it against your boot, seeking purchase. The crustaceans molt; the larvae pupate; the hunting things strike with a wet, muffled snap in the dark. The entire pocket-ecosystem is a closed loop of consumption and growth, sealed away from the sun, running on the slow heat of decomposition and the relentless seep of water.

**Rules of the World**
Here, you do not walk; you wade. You do not see; you feel through vibrations and the parting of the insect-cloud. You do not rest; you are assessed, crawled upon, incorporated into the surface area of the place. The builders who grew this town tolerate the fauna as they tolerate the water—as an element, a weather, a medium they breathe and wear. Their skin is thick, calloused, immune to the bite. Yours is not. The rule is simple: everything is eaten, eventually. The slow rot of the cold, the fast gnawing of the jaws. The only sanctuary is inside the gourds, behind the fungal curtains, sitting in the dark, listening to the quiet drip of the weeping walls, and trying not to think about what is covering you.
