It is a throat.

That is the first, primal image the mind settles on. A throat in the earth, a vertical canyon so narrow you could almost brace your shoulders against both sides at once, yet impossibly deep. The walls are not stone, not in any familiar sense. They are petrified dark matter, a material so dense it drinks the light, etched with the scars of a billion years of slow, patient erosion.

The air is the first thing you *know*. It is warm, thick, and heavy, moving ever so sluggishly in the confines of the throat. It smells of wet dust and a sweet, cloying scent like overripe fruit left to rot in the sun. The warmth is constant, a geothermal pulse rising from the unseen depths, pressing in on you from all sides. The humidity is palpable; it beads on your skin, gathers in the corners of your eyes, and makes the ground treacherously slick.

This is the dampness of the place (1.2). It is not the open, falling dampness of a rainstorm, but a closed, weeping dampness. Water does not flow and rush here; it condenses. The cool, dense walls of the throat sweat a viscous, mineral-laden moisture that gathers in the crooks of the rock, slowly tracing downward, a single, patient tear track that may take a year to travel a foot. Here and there, a pocket of this gathered water sags in a hollow, a dark, perfect orb shivering with the resonant hum of the place, before finally, under its own minute weight, overflowing and sending a single drop plummeting into the unseen dark below. The sound it makes is not a *plink*, but a deep, resonant *thoom* that vibrates up the rock, a form of communication.

The vegetation is a testament to this scarcity (0.8). It is not a lush carpet but an occasional, desperate clutch. What does grow here grows on the thinnest of margins. The primary growth is a symbiotic carpet of two lifeforms. One is a matte-black, moss-like organism that clings to the vertical faces, absorbing the constant sheen of condensation. Woven through it, a faintly phosphorescent, scaly lichen provides the only light source (0.3), a weak, sullen green glow just sufficient to outline the shapes of things without revealing their substance. In wider rare crevices, where a pocket of mineral-rich soil has gathered over millennia, a stunted, iron-hard tree grows. Its bark is like flannel, its leaves are stiff, sharp needles, and after a decade of slow growth, it might produce a single, blood-red blossom before withering back to an iron-hard, spine-like skeleton.

For the scale is small (1.1). The area you can perceive is a single chamber, a cathedral no bigger than a village square, defined by the upward limit of the faint phosphorescence and the downward drop into absolute blackness. You are in a room, a prison, a sanctuary. The walls are suffocatingly close, always there in your peripheral vision. The floor is not flat; it is the floor of a throat, a series of slick, rounded "ovoids" smoothed by slow geologic pressure and the constant friction of bodies. Here, the narrowness creates a density (2.3) that is almost psychological. The air is dense; the darkness is dense; the presence of life is crushingly, overwhelmingly dense (2.8).

It is here that the world reveals its true nature as a vertical ecosystem. The darkness is not an absence; it is a medium. The place is loud (2.0), but not with chaotic noise. The sound is a sourceless, deep, polytonal thrumming, like the shifting chords of a colossal pipe organ playing a single, eternal chord. It is the sound of millions of bodies, of their slow, collective existence.

And they are everywhere. The "fauna." They coat the walls in multiple layers. They cling to the inverted ledges. They crawl over each other in a slow, ancient, and constant procession down into the abyss. They are loosely chitinoid, like grotesque, six-legged woodlice the size of a wolf, with bodies that are a mosaic of preserved exoskeletons and living, glistening tissue. They have no discernible eyes. Their primary sense seems to be touch; they groom and caress one another constantly with limbs that end in sucker-pads and fine, hair-like cilia.

They are part of the place, a moving, living geology. They are patient, so patient. They move in a vast, slow queue, a continuous column descending into the absolute darkness below, their individual movements barely perceptible. This is the slow dynamic (1.2): the fall of a single drop, the decade-long bloom of a flower, the glacial migration of the living tide. Their constant, frictional movement generates heat, contributing to the womb-like warmth.

There is no sign of the built world (0.1), no tools, no intelligence beyond the biological imperative. And yet, this place is more "designed" than any city. Its rules are simple and absolute: Down is the direction of life. The purpose is the descent. It is a pilgrimage without a destination, a migration without an end.

We find the weirdness (0.7) in the edges of this system. Some of the creatures, the older ones near the top of the visible chamber, have begun to change. They have ceased their descent. Instead, they anchor themselves to the rock with their sucker-legs. A new growth starts on their backs, a fan-like, leathery structure that trembles and vibrates in harmony with the world's deep note. They are becoming sessile, transforming themselves from pilgrims into infrastructure. Are they dying? Are they metamorphosing into something new? Are they becoming antennae for a signal from the depths, or simply organs for a being so vast this chamber is but a cell in its gut?

The phosphorescent lichen on the walls near one of these transforming forms glows a fraction brighter, its cycle synchronizing with the creature's new vibrations. A single, perfect tear of condensation rolls down the wall, touches the edge of the lichen, and shimmers with a rapid, kaleidoscopic pulse of soft colours before continuing its gravely slow journey down. The air shifts, the chord modulates almost imperceptibly, and somewhere in the deep, a million more creatures shuffle one step closer to the dark.
