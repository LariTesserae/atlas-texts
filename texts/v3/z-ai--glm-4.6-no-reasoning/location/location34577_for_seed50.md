There is no land. Not in the sense one can stand on. The high place is the only place, and the high place is a single, impossibly tall pillar of a material that is neither stone nor metal. It soars through nothingness, rising from an abyss of black water below its base and vanishing into a realm of thick, breathable vapor above. Its surface is embryonic. Raw glass and nascent crystal erupt from a core of mercury-quicksilver, cooling and fissuring in real-time, only to be sheared away and reformed by the planet-scream that is the only law here.

This is the Throat of the World. The name is not a metaphor.

The scale is intimate, a secret one might stumble upon while traversing a featureless plain of mist. To find it, you follow the sound. You cannot avoid it. It is a ceaseless, deafening note, a chord that has been held since the beginning of memory, a seismic low-frequency thrum that vibrates not in the ears but in the marrow of your bones, harmonizing with a higher, screeching overture that feels like it should shatter teeth. The sound is the world’s dynamic engine. It is the tectonic force, the weather, and the clock all at once.

To stand, if one could call it standing, is to be pressed against the pillar’s side in a narrow alcove of newly-cooled glass. You cling to it, your primitive metal carabiner—your only tech, a gift from a forgotten age—hooked to a flake of obsidian that wasn't there an hour ago. The air is cool and wet, thick enough to taste of ozone and distant minerals. You breathe it in shallowly, for breathing too deeply feels like inhaling a solid, like trying to swallow the constant vibration.

It is submerged, but not in water. High above, the atmospheric ceiling is under such immense pressure that the cool air has condensed into a sea of fog, a roiling liquid-gas vortex. This is the high wall of water, a permanent atmospheric ocean. Its surface is a churning ceiling of white, and from it, rain does not fall. It is wrung out. Columns of pure, compressed liquid column down through the clouds, aquatic stalactites of terrifying force, striking the pillar and the void with percussive detonations that add their sharp reports to the world’s deafening drone. Occasionally, a column will dissolve partway down, its energy spent, creating a brief, localized waterfall that shimmers with an inner light before vanishing back into vapor.

The water below is a different beast. It is an abyss of perfectly still, black liquid, so deep it absorbs all light and all sound that reaches it. It is the only stillness in this chaotic place, a silent counterpoint to the shrieking sky. But it is not empty. Between strikes from the sky-rain, geysers of the same black water erupt from the depths, silent fountains that climb thousands of feet before arcing gracefully, impossibly, back into the abyss. They do not make a sound. Their silent ascent and fall is a measure of the planet’s deep, unheard pulse.

Vegetation is sparse, a miracle of persistence. Clinging to the rare, stable ledges of the pillar are colonies of what are called Shiver-thorns. They are not plants but mineral-analogs, crystalline growths that extract nourishment from the sonic vibrations that constantly scour the pillar. They look like clusters of frozen lightning, their facets vibrating in sympathy with the world-song, emitting a faint, internal luminescence that is the only light visible on the pillar itself aside from the normal, perpetually grey-gloom that filters through the vapor sea. To touch one is to feel a focused, needle-like point of pure noise.

Ecology is a study in absence and rarity. The only fauna are the Echo-skimmers. They are rare, born from the geysers. A geyser will sometimes, instead of falling back, coalesce its uppermost plume into a flock of these creatures. They are silent, perfectly tear-drop shaped, with a membrane of iridescent liquid for a body. They have no discernible features—no eyes, no mouth, no limbs. They are living embodiments of the deep water’s silence. They launch from the geyser's peak and drift on unseen currents between the pillar and the atmospheric sea, their only purpose seemingly to absorb sound. Where a flock of them passes, the deafening chord thins to a mere roar, a pocket of relative quiet that follows them as they glide for hours before slowly losing cohesion and dissolving back into mist. To be near them is a profound, disorienting relief, like surfacing after a deep dive.

Built structures are scattered, desperate, and ancient. They are the work of a people who tried to live here, or perhaps to tame it. They are small, single-room structures of hewn black glass, mortared with a paste of crushed Shiver-thorn. Most are shattered, their remains clinging tenuously to the pillar’s flank like fossilized barnacles. One structure remains, partly intact: the Observatory. It is a single curved wall, a shield against the constant sonic pressure, built around a wide, circular aperture pointing not at the sky, but at the black abyss below. Inside, the sound is only maddeningly loud, not deafening. The walls are etched with primitive markings—star charts that do not match any known sky, wave-forms of the planet’s scream, and frantic, repeating spirals. In the center lies a single tool on a stone pedestal: a tuning fork of a strange, warm metal, larger than a human arm. It does not vibrate. The rules of this world are simple and brutal. The Pillar is a vertical string of existence. The sky is a sea that tries to crush you with its liquid fists. The abyss is the silent mother that births silent ghosts. The sound is constant, the only architect, creating and destroying with a turbulent, chaotic rhythm that is slower than a storm but faster than geology. It is a Solaris in audio form, a liquid-roiling world that expresses itself through seismic noise.

To be here is to be a visitor in the heart of an alien instrument, awaiting a change in the chord that might offer brief respite, or herald the final, silent collapse of this raw, emerging, and entirely unwelcoming place. You are a mote in a pocket of perpetual, violent birth. You listen. Waiting for the moment when the Throat sings a new note, or finally swallows itself in silence.
