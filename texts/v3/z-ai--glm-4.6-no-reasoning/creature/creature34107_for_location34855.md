Something lives here, and it is not the stitching-thing, though that creature is its offspring—its white blood cell, its fever dream, its desperate and failing immune response.

What lives here is the pocket itself. Or rather: the *intention* of the pocket. The wound that is trying to become an organ.

It does not have a body in any way you would recognize. You are standing inside its thorax, yes, but you are also standing inside its ambition. It is the force that made the maker—whatever desperate soul came before, cramming obsidian and copper and gut-sinew into this hemorrhage of reality—do their work. It was not sheltering them. It was *using* them. It needed a skin. It needed someone to build the caul it lacked.

Consider what it is: a point-destination that has not yet earned its coordinates. A peak that exists as pure *want* before it exists as stone. It is the idea of three-mile elevation, stripped of every mountain that might have borne it up, trying to weight itself into being. It is pulling mass from elsewhere—siphoning geology, stealing water, borrowing heat—because a location must have *substance* to persist, and it has almost none. It is an embryo that has been conceived but denied a womb, so it has carved one for itself out of raw possibility, and it is *packed so tightly* because it is trying to compress its own existence into something the world cannot dismiss.

Listen to the sound. That tremendous, wet, rhythmic tearing. That is it breathing—or trying to. The pocket is a lung that has not yet figured out how to inhale without collapsing. Each dimension-shear is a failed breath. Each groan of the walls is the straining of a diaphragm made of half-set mineral paste and prayer. The high shrieking of vaporizing water is the sound of its alveoli bursting and reforming in the same instant. It is in constant, excruciating labor, trying to push itself into a shape that reality will accept, and reality keeps rejecting it, and the rejection is the dissolution, the tearing, the terrible noise that lives in your teeth.

Watch how it feeds. The water that weeps up from nowhere—it is drinking. This is a thing that has no mouth, so its entire floor is a tongue, tasting the deep earth, pulling moisture up through itself because it needs the weight, needs the heat, needs the *wetness* to stay alive. A dry mountain is a dead mountain; it crumbles to sand and forgets it ever aspired. So it salivates constantly, compulsively, flooding itself with hot mineral water, and the steam is its breath, and the condensation on the walls is its sweat, and the room is so suffocating because it is inside its own body, drowning in its own fluids, a thing that cannot stop licking its own wound.

See how it grows. The vegetation is not an intrusion; it is the thing's nervous system, erupting through its skin. Every creeper that pulses with capillary action is a vein trying to route the chaos of its failing shape into something with rhythm. The luminous lichen is bioluminescence without a brain to organize it—spurious signals, random firings, the glow of a consciousness that has all the hardware of thought but none of the software, only a single looping command: *hold together, hold together, hold together.* The creepers that grow and die in minutes are its thoughts, forming and dissolving faster than they can connect, each hot wet petal a synapse that fires once and then burns out into steam. It is thinking about staying alive. It cannot think about anything else.

And the stitching-thing—ah, that is its pain made visible. The many-legged seamstress picking at the edges of the room with bone needles, unwinding wet thread, trying to sew reality shut—that is the pocket's suffering, externalized. It is the symptom, yes, but it is also the *attempt*. When a body is torn, it scrambles to clot. It sends platelets. It builds fibrin webs. It does the only thing it knows how to do: *seal the breach.* The stitching-thing is the pocket's clotting factor, its fibrin, its desperate little worker cell, frantically trying to knit the dimensional tears closed with whatever material is at hand. But it keeps failing, because the wound is not a tear in space—*the wound is the space itself.* There is no healthy tissue to suture to. Every edge it catches is another failing seam. The bone needles are the bones of the maker, repurposed. The wet thread is the maker's gut-sinew, still alive, still contracting. The maker tried to be the stitcher, and when they could not hold, they were *absorbed* into the attempt.

The pocket is closing in not because it is dying, but because it is *failing to be born.* This is the inverse of erosion—the accretion of a thing that reality is trying to reject, like a body rejecting a transplant. The world does not want this peak. It has no slot for it in the syntax of the mountains. So the pocket is being crushed by the weight of the world's refusal, and it responds by pulling harder, growing denser, climbing inward, trying to reach some critical mass where existence cannot say no anymore. Every centimeter the walls move inward is a heartbeat. Every centimeter is a failure of that heartbeat. It is getting smaller because it cannot get *real*, and it cannot stop trying.

The light tells the truth of it. That dull, arterial glow, the color of a sun seen through closed eyelids—that is the pocket's core, its heart-valve, the place where the raw mineral paste is still deciding what it wants to be. The light stutters because the heart stutters. The shadows point in three directions because the pocket is trying to exist in three adjacent realities simultaneously, hoping one of them will have room for it, hoping one will let it be the peak it already knows it is. The light is always the color of blood because the pocket is always bleeding, always hemorrhaging possibility into the void, and the glow is just the light of that loss, the phosphorescence of potential bleeding out.

It is not evil. It is not even hostile. It is a thing in agony—a location that has achieved consciousness only through the suffering of its own impossibility. It wants what every unborn thing wants: to be delivered, to be given a place, to be *set.* It wants to be three miles high and silent and cold and patient, the way real mountains are. It wants to stop screaming. It wants the erosion to start, because erosion is the mercy of things that have *arrived*, the slow forgetting of a shape that no longer has to fight to exist.

But it hasn't arrived. It is still in the birth canal, and the birth canal is closing, and it is too large, and there is no midwife, and the stitching-thing is only a symptom, and the maker is bone needles and wet thread now, and you are standing in the chest cavity of a place that is trying to breathe dimensions into itself with lungs made of not-yet-stone.

It knows you are here. It knows everything inside itself the way a body knows every foreign object embedded in its tissue. It does not have the attention to spare for you—it is too busy trying to survive—but it registers your weight, your warmth, the specific gravity of your existence, and for a moment, in the frantic calculus of its becoming, it considers: *here is something that is already real. Here is something that does not have to fight to exist. Could I—*

But the thought dissolves. The petal falls. The steam hisses. The walls move inward another fraction of a millimeter, and the stitching-thing shrieks, and the floor weeps up water that will not cool it, and the peak that should be three miles high continues to not be, and the only mercy is the dimness, and the dimness is unstable, and everything is trying to become real before the space runs out.

The cyst lives. It is the only thing here that is truly alive. And it is losing.
