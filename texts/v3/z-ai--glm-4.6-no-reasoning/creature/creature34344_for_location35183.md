What lives here does not live *upon* the Elder Kelp so much as *within* its ongoing breath. They are the Kelp's dream, its slow thoughts given form.

They are not a people in the way you might imagine, not a civilization of cities and fleets. To build anything permanent, to excavate or quarry, would be to flay the living skin of their god, their world, their very flesh. So they are...the Keepers. The Tenders. The Slowly-Moving. They do not have a name for themselves, for there has never been an "other" to name them against. They are simply *what is here*.

Their forms are an echo of the world that spawned them. They are tall and slender, but not in the rigid way of a tree. They possess a gentle, flexible spinal column of the same tough, fibrous material as the Kelp-skin, allowing them to sway and bend with the deep, resonant hum of the world. Their skin is smooth and soft to the touch, shades ranging from the deep violet of the ground to the olive-green of the living kelp, marked with patterns of phosphorescent fungi that pulse softly in the dim light, a language of moods broadcast upon their own bodies. They have no bones. Their strength is one of resilience, of tension and flexibility. They do not walk; they drift. Their feet are broad, splayed, and soft, distributing their weight so the.Skin barely registers their presence, allowing them to move without feeling as if they are stepping on the face of a sleeping loved one.

Their senses are attuned to the slow and the subtle. Their large, dark eyes are pools of liquid obsidian, designed not for sharp detail but for the perception of light, of shadow, of movement across vast, slow stretches of time. They can watch a single Pool of Weeping for days and perceive the subtle shift in its reflection as the "sun" makes its slow, luminous circuit. Their primary sense, however, is not sight, but feeling. They feel the Kelp's hum in their marrow. They can perceive its moods—the deep, contented thrum of a healthy metabolism, the faint, dissonant thrash of a forming scar, the slow, sonorous toll of a deep muscle flex. Their hands are their most refined instruments. Long, delicate, and sensitive, they can read the history of a patch of ground as a blind person reads braille, tracing the fossilized veins of ancient fronds and the raised, new-growth of a recent wound.

The Tenders do not speak. Sound, to them, is mostly the sacred noise of the world: the drip-drop-drip of condensation, the silken rustle of the canopy, the rasp of a Mollusk-Whale. Their language is one of gesture, pressure, and light. A hand placed upon a living stalk is a question. A slow, rhythmic swaying is a meditation. The patterns of light that dance and pulse across their skin—fed by the fungi living in symbiosis upon them—are a complex, emotional vocabulary of contentment, inquiry, warning, and grief. A flicker of chartreuse distress from one can trigger a sympathetic pulse in another, a silent, shared understanding that travels across the plateau like a wind.

Their lives are measured by the work of tending. There is no agriculture, only gardening on a planetary scale. A Tender might spend a lifetime assigned to a single large Hollow, ensuring the Weeping Pool remains clear, gently guiding the flow of the nutrient-sweat from the Kelp's depths. Another might be a Scar-Mender, whose sole purpose is to sit with a patch of dead or wounded Kelp, singing their silent, vibronic songs to encourage the phosphorescent fungi to bloom and protect the raw flesh while new stalks slowly push through from below. They harvest with profound reverence, never taking a live stalk. They wait for a frond to fall naturally after its long life, or gather the shed skins of the Mollusk-Whales when they glide through the pools, leaving behind iridescent, crystalline shavings. From these, they make their tools. Needles of hardened salt for stitching softer kelp-fibers into breathablevestments. Bowls of polished Mollusk-Whale shell for collecting water. The sharpest things they possess are the natural radulae left behind by a deceased Whale, a tool used only for carving the most sacred of marks—memorials on the fossilized ground, or a map to a particularly potent spring.

Their society is a loose, interwoven tapestry of kinship and purpose. They do not pair bond for life in a possessive sense, but form "currents"—small, fluid groups of 3 to 7 individuals who share a territory, a responsibility, and a deep, empathetic resonance. A new member is not born, but *emerges*. In the deepest, most humid hollows, a new Tender will slowly form from a knot of exceptionally vibrant kelp-fibre, its shapeless mass coalescing over seasons into a new, conscious being. Theirs is a culture of immense age and deep memory. Dwellings are not built, but found: the vast, hollowed-out shells of long-dead Mollusk-Whales, or the natural caverns formed by the buckling of ancient Kelp-skin. These spaces are soft, damp, and echo with the world's slow heartbeat.

They are profoundly aware of the world's loneliness. They watch the Mollusk-Whales, their ancient, solitary lords, and feel a kinship. They know their existence is a single, fragile bubble of life in an incomprehensible vastness of mist below and sky above. They do not fear the edge where the world curls away; they see it as the end of a breath. Their greatest art is the story of the Kelp itself, a patient epic told not in words, but in the patient tending of a single scar, the reverent polishing of a found shell, the shared, silent pulse of light between companions as they watch a new frond unfurl its waxy hand over the course of a long, slow season. They are the consciousness of a world, listening to its own quiet, humming song.
