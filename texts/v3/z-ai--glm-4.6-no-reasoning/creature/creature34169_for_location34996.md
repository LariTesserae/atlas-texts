To live here is to be a cell in a body you call a world. There is no "I," only a function within the whole. The inhabitants are not a community; they are a distributed consciousness, a trillion ICs in the single, planetary motherboard of The Green.

First, there are the Beasts. They are the muscles and the blood of this world. Consider the Root-Walkers. They are the titans you stand upon. To call them creatures is a misnomer; they are mobile continents of bark and moss, so ancient their original shape is lost. They do not eat, for they *are* The Green, a piece of it that chose to wander. Their limbs are roots, constantly probing the spongy floor, drawing nutrients directly into their immense bodies. Their backs are ecosystems, hosting generations of smaller life forms, their slow, ponderous steps a geological event that reshapes the landscape over centuries. They do not think in the way you would understand, but they *feel*. They feel the pressure of a foot, the sting of a Strangler's fang, and they respond—not with aggression, but with a slow, inexorable growth that can raise or lower the terrain, sealing a wound or starving a predator over decades.

Then there are the Heart-Moths. They are the messengers, the neurons of this world. Born in the phosphorescent deeps, they are swarms of phosphorescent, glittering things, each no bigger than a thumb, whose wings are not membrane but thinly-pressed chlorophyll. They do not consume matter; they consume light. They drink the raw brilliance that seeps through the canopy, spiraling up in silent, glittering columns to theHeat-Layers above. There, in the suffocating thermal currents, they release their stored light in a slow, pulsing dance. This is not simple bioluminescence; it is communication. The flashes are a binary language of light, a torrent of information carried on the winds—data on fruiting cycles, on the movement of predator-creatures, on the slow-healing sorrow of a wounded Root-Walker—to be read by the light-attuned sensory blossoms of The Green itself. They are its nervous system.

And then there is the apex, the Strangler. It is not merely a predator; it is The Green's immune system. A Strangler begins its life as a single, deceptive tendril, indistinguishable from a million others. It is patient. It will lie dormant for a hundred years, learning the scent-profile and frequency of the creatures around it. When it decides to strike, it doesn't just kill; it *incorporates*. It envelops its prey, not just crushing but injecting a complex fungal slurry that dissolves soft tissue and converts it into nutrients, which the Strangler then feeds back into the root-system it is anchored to. It devours for the health of the whole. Its cry, the sound of rending wood, is not a roar of triumph, but a biological report, a signal that a pathogen or a parasitic species has been excised. The Strangler is a white blood cell.

Even the Sap-Sticks, the apparent outliers, are not separate. They are the world's hands. Their claim to "making" is a profound misunderstanding of their symbiosis. They do not coax The Green with a language; they simply *intend*. A Sap-Stick that needs a platform will meditate for days, its biochemistry shifting, releasing a complex pheromonal signature that permeates its skin. nearby Creepers, attuned to this signature, don't "listen"; they simply grow in the direction of the intention, their cells dividing and weaving in response to a chemical need that feels as natural to them as sunlight. The obsidian they use is their only truly foreign element, and it is their sacred scar. It is the pain that defines them, the sharp, dead memory of a sky they will never see, used to perform the one act The Green cannot do on its own: to cleanly, sharply *cut*. To prune. To perform a surgery The Green can only accomplish through the slow agony of the Strangler. They are not their own; they are the willful, focused appendage of a body that otherwise thinks only in geologic time.

But the truest inhabitant, the one that defines the experience of being here, has no name and no form you can hold. It is the *Thrum*. It is the constant, deep, bone-vibrating hum that permeates everything. It is the sound of sap rising, of cells dividing, of water being drawn from the dead seas beneath to the canopy-swells above. It is the sound of metabolism on a planetary scale. To live within The Green is to be submerged in this Thrum. It is not something you hear with your ears; it is something your skull resonates with. Over time, it becomes the rhythm of your own thoughts. It teaches you that your individual pulse is just an echo of the world-pulse. It erodes the concept of self, replacing it with a sense of being a note in a vast, continuous chord. You do not think *in* The Green; you are thought *by* it. You don't have ideas; you are an idea it is having, gradually, over a long, slow lifetime. You are a brief, specific vibration in its endless, roaring song.
