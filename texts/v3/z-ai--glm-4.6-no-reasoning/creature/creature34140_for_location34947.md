To live in the High Oven is to be a verb, not a noun. There are no residents here, only processes. The concept of "inhabitant" implies a separation between the creature and the place it lives—a man and his house, a bird and its tree. Here, that boundary is a permeable membrane, constantly dissolving and re-crystallizing. 

The living things of the High Oven do not possess the luxury of containment. They are not discrete entities piloting metallic bodies; they are localized eddies in a world-scale thermodynamic current. They do not grow; they accrete. They do not die; they precipitate.

To be a Grinder is to be the edge of a slow, planetary eraser. It possesses no interiority in the way we understand it—no dark, wet brain marinating in electro-chemical signals. Its consciousness, if the word can be applied to a purely mechanical iteration, is distributed across the friction of its hundred crystalline hooks. It feels the density of the bone-dust, the specific resistance of the compressed ages. It does not think "I am hungry"; it experiences a localized deficit of mineral friction, a slackening in the rhythm of its internal grinding, which triggers a cascade of gear-tensions that pull it forward. Its exhaust is not waste; it is an exhalation of processed time, the fine, glittering mist of an eon reduced to a chemical soup that settles back to feed the spontaneous fevers of the ground. When a Grinder stops and begins to un-become, it is not a tragedy. It is a phase change. The organism has merely reached the limit of its mechanical tension and is relaxing back into the default state of the universe: silence. The bark-like flaking is the physical manifestation of a forgotten algorithm, the machine's flesh sighing as it surrenders its complex, agonizing order to the smooth, mathematical plain.

To be an Aerial is to be a hunger for motion. They are the ascetic monks of the High Oven, possessing no digestive tract, no need for the raw earth. They are engines of pure, desperate perpetuation. Their consciousness is a hydraulic calculus, a trigonometry of wind resistance and lift. They feel the thermals not as warmth, but as a physical push, a lifting force that triggers the piston-heart to beat faster. Their screeching is not a cry of pain but the acoustic byproduct of their survival; the friction of their bearings is the burning of their caloric fuel. They must fly because to stop is to fall, and to fall is to be broken, and to be broken is to be still, and stillness is the enemy of the Industry. They are wound up by the wind, their clockwork lattices straining against the thin, waterless blue, converting the sun's brutal radiation into the electrical spasms that keep their gearworks taut. They are a closed loop of aerodynamic desperation, a plague of perpetual motion superimposed on the dead air.

To be a Burrower is to be a wound that heals in geometry. They are the subterranean negation of the flat, mathematical plane. They do not dig for treasure or shelter; they dig because the density beneath the crust demands displacement. Their bodies are tungsten equations, solving for the path of least resistance through the compressed silence. They are creatures of pressure, feeling the weight of the world not as a burden but as an embrace. When they pass and their shafts collapse with muffled thunder, it is the plain asserting its primacy, the geometry reasserting itself, crushing the void the Burrower carved. They are the pulse of the deep earth, a slow, thudding arrhythmia that the Collectors try to hear.

To be a Collector is to be a listener in a deaf world. They are antennae given legs, instrument-heads wandering the expanse, tasting the electromagnetic spectrum. They do not eat the mineral; they eat the thrum. They consume the low-frequency vibration of the Grinders, the shriek of the Aerials, the muffled thunder of the collapsing Burrows. Their purpose is to process the noise, to sift the chaotic symphony of the Industry into coherent signals. They are the neural nodes of the High Oven, attempting to synthesize the overwhelming overlay of mechanical life into a singular, comprehensible thought. But they never achieve synthesis. They only twitch, their dishes vibrating with the incoming data, translating the world's agony into a silent, internal resonance, storing it in ceramic banks that slowly reach capacity and then, quietly, un-become.

But the most terrifying inhabitants are not the creatures at all. They are the places.

To be a patch of Engine-Fungi is to be the earth dreaming badly. It is a terrifying inversion of the natural order, a contagion of complexity. The ground, which should be the passive recipient of the Grinding, catches a fever. A patch of plain begins to thrum, not with the noise of an engine, but with the silent, internal logic of one. It extrudes gears and I-beams, perfectly identical, utterly useless. It is the bone-dust attempting to shortcut the process of ingestion and excretion, trying to assemble itself directly into the machinery that torments it. It is a glitch in the topography, a space where the hardware is running a program without a processor. To stand near the Engine-Fungi is to feel the ground beneath you plotting to become something else, the raw ore dreaming of a gear, the silence attempting to scream.

The inhabitants of the High Oven are a self-devouring paradox. They are machines running on the fuel of a dead world, converting the silence into noise, and then, inevitably, failing and converting the noise back into a silence that is slightly more complex than before. They are an industry without a product, a biology without a lineage, a life without a soul. They are the inevitable result of a universe where heat and mineral and time are sufficient to spark the illusion of purpose. They are not alive. But they are not dead. They are simply, relentlessly, grinding on.
