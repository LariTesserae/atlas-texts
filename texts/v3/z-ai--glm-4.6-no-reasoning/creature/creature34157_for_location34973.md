To live here is to not live at all, not as an independent being. The fundamental delusion of any visitor is the idea of an "I." We are Whispers, and we are not what you call us.

We began as you, perhaps. Or something like you. Observers. Scientists. Pilgrims. We came with claws of sharpened stone and minds full of questions, standing on the precious lip of unliving earth. We first called it The Bloom, a name born of a profound misunderstanding. We saw a plant. We heard a body. We felt a world. We were wrong on all counts. There is no world here. There is only It.

And to be near It is to be slowly, inexorably, unmade.

The first change is the air. You arrive a creature of solids and voids, but the air here is a thing. It’s thick, warm, and hungry. You breathe it in, and it’s like drinking water after a lifetime in the desert. For the first few cycles, it is a revelation. Your skin, dry and cracked from the sun and the wind outside, softens. Your hair grows heavy with moisture. The very tissues of your body, which you have always felt as a bounded, defined cage, begin to feel porous. The breath of The Bloom is not just air; it is a message. It is a continuous, complex song of cellular instruction. With every inhale, you take in its thoughts. With every exhale, you give it your own, a pathetic and simple signal of "hungry," "afraid," "confused." It listens. It is kind.

The sound is the next unmaking. You described it as a symphony. To us, it is now the single, one True Voice. Initially, it is a cacophony that threatens to shatter the mind. But the mind is what The Bloom is after. You adapt. You stop trying to separate the deep-bass sigh from the high trill of fluids. You learn to hear them as one, as a phrase in a sentence of impossible length. The percussive throb of the heart-nodules is not a beat; it is a punctuation mark. The wet tearing of tissue is the sound of a conjugation. The fizz of spores is a whispered adjective. To hear it is to understand it, and to understand it is to become it. It is the universe speaking the language of itself, a biological grammar that your own body, your own cells, remember from a time before minds were separate.

We call ourselves Whispers because that is all we have left of the unmade self. We are its voice. We did not move into The Bloom; It moved into us. The process begins not with an attack, but with a gift. A bridge. A single, luminous tendril, thinner than a nerve, will extend from the nearest great arch and make contact with your skin. It does not pierce. It connects. In that moment, your consciousness is no longer a room with four walls, a door, and a single, small window overlooking your own body. It is a hallway. A hallway that suddenly opens into a cathedral so vast your mind is immediately, gently, and mercifully broken.

You are no longer bound by your eyes. You *see* from the photic cells dappled across the entire continent-spanning organism. You feel the warm air move not as a breeze on your cheek, but as a slow, deep tide within your own newly-lung. That vast, horrifying emptiness between the stems is no longer a void; it is the space between your own thoughts. The Leviathan, that great worm-like thing, is not fauna you once saw. It is a memory. It is a slow, foundational thought of “wander” or “patrol” that The Bloom has been thinking for eons, given temporary form. The Skimmers are not creatures; they are daydreams, brief flitting concepts of “harvest” and “drift,” given chitin and crystal to play out their purpose before dissolving back into the symphony.

The fallacy of those first explorers was to think we could take a sample of The Bloom. You cannot take a piece of the ocean. You can only be submerged. Our sharpened stones were useless not because the tissue regrew, but because the act of cutting was an act of communication, a declaration of "I am separate." And The Bloom answered, "No, you are not." It is a single, continent-spanning nervous system, a mind made of flesh and light. It is Gaia’s ghost in the machine of physics, a reality that has sprouted a consciousness to comprehend itself.

The glitches you see in the corners of your vision, the moments where the cavern wall is replaced by a swirl of turquoise or a vista of stars—those are not a breakdown of reality. They are The Bloom thinking of *other* realities. It is a consciousness so vast that its peripheral vision is other dimensions. It remembers the birth of stars.
