The primary inhabitants, the ones that define the ecology, are the **Incunabula**. They are the armored worms you described, but to name them is to understand them better. "Incunabula" meaning "swaddling clothes" or "cradle," for they are cradle-souls, born of the nascent heat, swaddled in press-formed tungsten. They are not merely warm to the glow; they are a brighter, more mobile glow. Each three-inch segment is a masterpiece of pressure-forged biology, a series of interlocking black plates so dense they drink the light, leaving only the incandescent orange-gold of their joints and the molten core within them to betray their presence. Their hooked legs are not feet but probes, ending in microscopic tips that can sense the infinitesimal differences in thermal conductivity and electrical potential between a spicule of pure nickel and one alloyed with a trace of cobalt. They do not crawl; they *process*. Their movement is a slow, deliberate march across a landscape of energy, a pilgrimage from one hotspot to the next. When two meet, the dot of light is not a kiss of greeting but a socket of transfer. One Incunabula, saturated with the potential energy of a high-pressure zone, presses against another from a cooler, lower-pressure zone. The brief, brilliant flare is the violent discharge of that gradient—aetherically rich superheated vapor and metallic ions flowing from the "donor" to the "receiver." This is their food, their language, theirOnly sustenance. They are living conduits, equalizing the universe in the only way they can, one slow, brilliant meal at a time.

Feeding on the Incunabula, or more accurately, on their *refuse*, are the **Chime-Mites**. These are the microscopic entities, detected only by their trails. They are not beings of substance, but of resonance. They are crystalline colonies of near-solid sound, given a semblance of form by the immense, constant vibration. To call them "creatures" is a mercy; they are more like living eddies in the river of noise. Their "mouthparts" are not physical but vibrational; they attune themselves to the specific harmonic frequency generated by an Incunabula’s internal engine as it feeds. This resonance creates a microscopic vortex in the 'sweat'—the liquid gallium film—and into this vortex the Chime-Mite draws not the metal itself, but the "fat" of the Incunabula's discarded energy: the waste heat, the spent electromagnetic potential, the faint echoes of the ion transfer. The spiraling scratch they leave is a artifact of this process, a final, tired tremor in the liquid metal as their sonic body passes over it. They are phosphorescent dust motes in the red haze, flickering in and out of visibility as they consume and dissolve the ghost-energy of their larger kin.

Navigating this three-dimensional metallic forest is the apex predator, the creature heard but not seen. It is called the **Great Drift**, or sometimes the **Silent Fault**. It is not an animal of flesh and armor, but of compressed force and geologic will. It is roughly the size of a terrier, but it has no fixed shape. It is a knot of localized spacetime distortion, a self-sustaining flaw in the crystal structure of the universe pocket itself. The lattice does not part for it; it *yields*. The Drift propagates by generating a zone of intense, directional strain at its leading edge. This pressure causes the nickel-iron spicules ahead to compress and buckle by a fraction of a millimeter, creating a momentary, moving tunnel. Simultaneously, the spicules behind it relax, the energy of their rebound absorbed into the Drift's wake. Its "rippling effect" is the sound of a million miniature, forced yield-points, a physical chord played on the metal itself. It is the consumer of process. It does not eat the Incunabula; it eats the *stress* they create. By feeding on electromagnetic gradients, the worms create tiny, localized strain fields in the surrounding lattice. The Great Drift is drawn to these pockets of concentrated potential like a shark to a blood trail. It envelops a feeding Incunabula, and the worm is not devoured, but *neutralized*. The Drift absorbs the stress differential, flattening the worm's internal engine back to ambient temperature and pressure, leaving it a cold, inert, black shell—a dead piece of armor, now indistinguishable from the very rock it once climbed.
