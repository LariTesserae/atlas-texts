To live in the Fane is to cease being a visitor and to become a tissue. The boundary between inhabitant and habitat does not exist here; it was erased by the same slow, green pulse that pumps the weeping water through the jade pillars. What lives in this place is not a collection of individuals, but a continuum of awareness distributed across chlorophyll and mycelium. The Fane does not house life. The Fane *is* the life, and what moves within it are simply its thoughts.

The primary consciousness, the immense arboreal entity whose stalks form the cathedral's bones, does not think in words. Its mind is a slow, geological accretion of sensation: the profound chill of the water it draws up from deep subterranean veins, the pressure of the spongy loom against the weight of its own pillars, the chemical taste of its own spore-laden air. Its awareness is tidal, rising and falling with the bass-deep *thrumming* that shakes the marrow of any foreign body. It experiences its own architecture as we experience the rhythm of our breathing—constant, autonomic, and foundational. It does not know it is a cathedral; it only knows that it reaches, that it channels, that it weeps.

The Skitterers are its active memory, its somatic nervous system made visible. When they converge on a patch of luminescent fungi, pruning with delicate, clicking mandibles, they are not tending a garden. They are the Fane's equivalent of a brain pruning unused synaptic connections, redirecting nutrients, sharpening a thought. Each translucent, six-legged capsule of glowing fluid is a mobile neuron. The chemical trails they leave in their wake across the black-and-green loom are the Fane's interneural signals, flash-transmissions of data moving across a vast, wet cortex. They do not possess individual survival instincts; a crushed Skitterer means no more to the Fane than a sloughed dead cell means to a human hand. The information it carried has already been absorbed.

Within the ovoid chambers—the smooth grottoes that feel like homes—there are no belongings, no beds, no hearths. This is because the Fane's residents do not rest; they *integrate*. The inhabitant of a shrine-chamber is not a creature that walks in and out. It is a root-mind, a dense knot of nervous tissue that has grown into the concave floor, fusing with the spongy mycelial mat. From this anchor, it extends its awareness through the lace-like fungal networks that frame the entrance, tasting the chemical soup of the air, feeling the vibrations of the weeping water. The captive firefly-node on the ceiling is not a light for the resident to see by, but a receptor for the resident to pulse its thoughts into—a node in a biological LAN, transmitting slow, pheromonal data packets up into the unseen ceiling and the greater stalks above. 

These root-minds are the Fane's dreamers. They are the ones who parse the sensory data brought in by the Skitterers. They are the ones who feel the profound, marrow-deep chill not as a discomfort, but as a confirmation of their circulatory flow. They experience the place's terrifying youth—its knife-sharp edges, its unsettled geometries—as a kind of adolescent growth spurt, a constant, aching pressure to expand. They do not speak, because the Fane's language is the snap of a fungal gill releasing spores; it is the sudden flush of blue bioluminescence when a crystal bud is touched; it is the subtle shift in the pH of the weeping water. 

To be a native of the Fane is to live inside a world-building algorithm that is still running, and to be a subroutine of that algorithm. There is no "outside" to look toward, because the self is defined by the perimeter of the place. When the vast plant shifts in its slow, breathing way, the inhabitant does not feel the floor move; they feel their own thoughts stretch. When the condensation parts the leathery vine-curtain at the boundary, it is not an opening for entry—it is the Fane blinking, opening a temporary iris to peer out into the cold, dead world of stone and metal, confirming that it is alone, that it is the only true living architecture, and that it must keep growing.

The inhabitant of the Fane is a creature of pure, vegetative proprioception. It knows itself only as a hollow within the green, a pulse within the dark, a node where the water clings and the light is trapped. It has no need for tools, because its body is the infrastructure; it has no need for gods, because it breathes the chlorophyll of its own eternal, frost-bitten genesis.
