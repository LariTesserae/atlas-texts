To live here is to be a creature of the interval—born into the pause between a raw cut of earth and its final, smooth erosion, between the spark of a prototype engine and its eventual, permanent stillness. They are the inhabitants of the in-between, the teeming flesh of a place that has not yet decided if it is a cave or a machine.

They do not have a name for themselves. Language requires silence, and silence is the one commodity this boiling throat cannot offer. They have, instead, a register of vibrations. They know the low, agonizing groan of the retorts as the sound of the world turning—geologic, indifferent, the heartbeat of the slow, metallic god they inhabit. They know the wet *plink* of condensation falling from the raw, weeping ceiling as a command to scatter, a brief, sharp punctuation in the otherwise unbroken hum. And they know the collective friction of ten million wings and mandibles as *self*—the sound of their own warmth, their own multiplication, their own absolute occupancy.

Physically, they are an exercise in wet minimalism. Hand-sized and flesh-colored, they resemble something pulled from the bathypelagic dark and brutally adapted for the intertidal zone of rust and steam. Their carapaces are not the dry, brittle armor of surface-dwelling orthopterans; they are slick, almost gelatinous, perpetually anointed by the condensing fog. To touch one—and here, you are always touching them, as they are the floor, the wall, the very texture of the heat—would be to feel a hard, chitinous skeleton sheathed in a membrane of tepid slime. They are the color of the inside of a cheek, or of brick mortar after a sustained sweating. They blend into the machinery not by camouflage, but by becoming a living extension of it. The rust and the flesh and the iron pyrite all share the same exhausted, mineral pallor.

Their lives are a mathematics of warmth. In a chamber where the heat is a crushing sovereign, they are its most obedient subjects. They do not eat in any way the surface world would understand; there is no vegetation to consume, no light to chase. They are thermo-synthetic, feeding on the residual heat that bleeds from the riveted boilers and the hissing accumulators. They blanket the machinery in layers because the machinery is their field, their forest, their sun. They drape themselves over the slow-stroking pistons, packing the micro-climates of pipe joints and valve housings with their translucent, amber eggs. The eggs need only heat and wet to mature, and here, the world is nothing but heat and wet. They incubate in the fever, and when they hatch, they click and whir and rub themselves into the existing chorus, adding another infinitesimal frequency to the oscillating din.

To move through this place as one of them is to navigate by gradient. The chamber is not dark to them; it is mapped in exquisite thermorelief. The roiling boilers are blazing continents of surplus energy, the spaces between pipes are lush valleys of moderate warmth, and the shallow, scum-caked sumps of hot water are treacherous, suffocating seas. When a fat droplet of water crashes from the raw ceiling onto a colony clustered along a steam pipe, the scatter is not panic. It is merely a localized correction—a ripple of hard, chitinous limbs redistributing themselves away from the sudden, cold shock, only to reconverge moments later, filling the wet crater left by the impact. They are fluid; they are a mass. They possess the intelligence of slime mold, of lichen, of any organism that does not survive as an individual but as a contiguous, living substrate.

They do not know they are forgotten. The concept of the city above—the one that doesn't remember this district exists—requires an awareness of the surface, of cool air, of linear time. Theirs is a cyclical, process-driven existence. They feel the slow, resisting stroke of the engines, and they match it with the rhythm of their rubbing wings. They feel the raw stone ceiling weeping, and they drink the weeping. They do not look up at the un-smoothed rock and see embryonic geology; they see only the source of the drops, the immutable sky of their subterranean cathedral.

There is no malice in them, no aggression, no swarm-mind driving them to consume. They are simply the logical conclusion of the chamber's conditions. If you leave a wet, hot, mineral-rich void at the base of the world, this is what moves in. They are the proof that life does not require sunlight or sweetness or even space—it requires only a gradient, a crevice, a persistent, crushing warmth. 

And so they persist. They coat the iron grating in a shifting, squirming carpet that forces any intruder into a slow, shuffling gait, not out of defense, but out of the sheer, overwhelming density of their being. They vibrate at the frequency of the metal itself, their mass chorus becoming indistinguishable from the groaning infrastructure. They are the sound of the factory running on geologic time. They are the breath in the throat of something just taking its first breath. They are the flesh of the district, quietly, relentlessly multiplying in the dim, orange-lit steam, waiting for the rock to finish carving itself, waiting for the engines to finally, irreversibly cease.
