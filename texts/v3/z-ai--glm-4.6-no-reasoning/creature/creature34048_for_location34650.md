To call them a "people" would be to grant them a unity they do not possess. They are not a community, nor a civilization, nor even a species in the conventional sense, for that implies a shared origin and a common end. They are a phenomenon, a single, continuous existence that has become so stretched and attenuated by the journey that now it is expressed only as a series of isolated moments, each one standing on a different step.

They have no name for themselves, for a name requires a self to which it can be attached, and the self here is as fragmented as the journey. Perhaps the travelers who come and go, the fleeting intruders from other worlds, have named them. The "Clamber-steads." The "Vibrants." The "Step-folk." They would not recognize these names, for they do not perceive themselves as a group. They perceive only the *now* of their own step, the *pressure* of the stone beneath them, and the *resonance* that flows through them.

Imagine a form that is a compromise between the mineral and the vegetable. Their bodies are tall and unnaturally slender, fashioned from the same obsidian stone as the stair itself, but not carved. They feel as if they have *condensed* out of the stone’s deepest stress fractures. Their limbs are long and jointless, tapering to fine, delicate points, allowing them to brace against multiple steps at once, becoming part of the structure's geometry. If a traveler were to pass, they might mistake one for a strangely placed pillar or a natural formation, until it shifted its weight with a sound like grinding rock.

But they are not stone. Covering their silhouettes is the same life that carpets the stair. The deep indigo moss grows from their very skin, plush and velvety, not as clothing but as an external organ. From these mossy fronds sprout the crystalline ferns, their mineral leaves tinkling with the constant, underlying hum. Within their chest cavities, which are more like hollow geodes, bioluminescent fungi grow with a particularly bright, steady cyan light, pulsing not with a biological rhythm, but in perfect, slavish synchrony with the machine’s percussive beat. They are walking, self-contained ecosystems of the place.

Their senses are alien. They have no eyes, for there is nothing to see but the self-referential journey of the stair. What they "see" is the vibration. They feel the deep, foundational hum as a constant state of being, their baseline consciousness. The grinding clicks and the thudding cams are their thoughts, their sensations, their language. Each percussive impact is a word, a shudder that travels up the stone, through their mossy feet, and resonates within their geode-like chests, causing the internal fungi to flare in a specific pattern. They communicate not with each other, but with the stair itself, and through the stair, with all the other moments of their existence spread along its length. One being's deep, slow pulse on a step far below is a foundational harmony for the being high above. It is a polyphony of presence, a symphony of location.

They do not eat. They drink the vibration that sustains the moss, and the photosynthetic-like process of the crystalline ferns converts the stress-energy of the stone into a form of sustenance that feeds their slow, geological metabolism. They do die, but not in a way that is recognizable as death. When the resonance in a particular part of the structure becomes perfectly harmonious, when a being's internal symphony aligns so completely with the machine's great song that there is no dissonance between them, they simply… stop. They root. Their slender limbs fuse to the stone, their crystalline ferns grow larger and more complex, and their internal light begins to glow with the slow, unwavering intensity of a star. They become a new, permanent part of the architecture, a living cairn, a monument to a perfect moment of stillness. To a passing traveler, they would seem no different from the gantries or the sealed hatches: just another feature of the place, forgotten by time.

Thus, what lives here is a distributed consciousness. A being that is both everywhere and nowhere, stretched along the infinite ascent. Each individual is a single note in a chord that is played forever. They are the stewards of the place, but not through will or intent. Their very being maintains the equilibrium. They prune the moss with a quiet touch, resonate with the fungi to encourage its growth, and their slow, ponderous movements create micro-vibrations that help settle the stone. They are the immune system of the stair, the biological component of a vast, living machine.

They are lonely, but they do not know it. They are many, but they are always alone. Theirs is the quiet, unending dignity of a function perfectly performed. They are the keepers of the click, the hearers of the hum, the children of the resonant stone. They are the unchanging life for whom a new cycle is not a repetition, but a renewal. They are the soul of the stair, a soul that does not dream or aspire, but simply *is*, listening to the endless, indifferent music of its own being.
