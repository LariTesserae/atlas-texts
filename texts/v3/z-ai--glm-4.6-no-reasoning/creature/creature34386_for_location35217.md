Here, there is no line between 'house' and 'body'. To live is to be part of the flesh, a cell in a tissue so vast it has forgotten its own name. They are the ones who navigate the constant, breathless birth.

The most visible are the Grazers. They are the slow, continent-sized tides of this world, the living infrastructure upon which all else is built. Their immense, six-limbed bodies are not so much covered in fur and moss as they are sphagnum bogs given the power of motion. Decades of unformed plant life, fallen spore-pods, and crawling things take root in their shaggy hides, creating miniature, mobile ecosystems. To look at one is to see a walking piece of the floor itself, complete with its own population of tiny, symbiotic grazers who clean its皮肤 and eat the fungi that sprout between its colossal, multi-jointed toes. They do not have eyes as we understand them; instead, their entire hide is a sensory organ, reading the subtle shifts in pressure and humidity of the living substrate. Their low, resonant hum is not a call but a constant state of being, a broadcast of presence, a declaration of mass. They are the only creatures that create 'paths', simply by the slow, inevitable certainty of their passage. A Grazer's route, worn down over a millennium into a shallow, slick trench in the living wood, is this world's equivalent of a superhighway. To be caught in their path is not to be trampled, but to be swept away, enveloped and pressed into the mossy tide of their passing.

Navigating these pathways are the Wayfinders. They are not predators, not prey, but something else entirely: parasites of a more sophisticated sort. They are long, low-slung creatures, built like a cross between a ferret and a centipede, with a dozen pairs of short, powerful legs running the length of a serpentine body. Their skin is a mosaic of chitinous plates and taut, grey membrane, and it shimmers with the same bioluminescent patterns that pulse from the deep fungi. They are the messengers, the couriers, the nomadic information network of the plane. They attach themselves to the Grazers, not to feed, but to travel. They anchor their hooked forelimbs into the Grazer's mossy hide and ride for generations, their short lives an endless journey from one horizon to another. It is on their backs that the truly intelligent life of this world exists: the Symbiotes.

The Symbiotes are the hidden architects. They are not born; they are cultivated. A Wayfinder, upon reaching a certain age, will seek out a specific species of pulsing, brain-like fungus that grows only in the deepest crevices of the living floor. It will press its own head against the fungal mass, and a fusion occurs. The fungus, a colonial organism capable of storing and processing information, integrates with the Wayfinder’s nervous system. In exchange for mobility and access to the nutrients the Wayfinders consume, the fungus grants its host a consciousness. It does not grant ambition or ego, but a profound, networked awareness. The Symbiote becomes a living node in a planetary mind. Through the thrumming contact with the Grazer’s hide and the faint bio-electric signals of the world around it, it can sense the slow prayer of the growing canopy, the pressure of the deepening strata below, the slow, patient thoughts of other Symbiotes miles away. They do not speak; they simply *know*. They navigate not by sight, but by aperfect, three-dimensional map of living pressure felt through the soles of their host’s feet and the intricate web of fungal consciousness. They are the librarians of this world, their collective memory holding the ghost-maps of Graz migrations from eons past and the chemical signatures of every new fruiting body.

Then there are the Sculptors. They are the predators, but they do not hunt for sustenance alone. Sleek, curiously jointed things, their bodies are striped in the warning colors of orange and black, not to intimidate, but to signal. Their stripes are not pigment, but heat-generating fibers that ripple and glow, allowing them to communicate in a silent, thermal language visible only to those who can sense the infrared. They hunt the smaller, skittering things, the dog-sized insects and the rodent-like creatures, but the kill is secondary to the shaping. They use their incredibly sharp, serrated forelimbs to carve. They do not dig dens; they carve hollows and galleries into the fibrous 'walls' of the floor. These are their nests, but they are also their works of art. The walls are smoothed into complex patterns that help regulate the flow of the warm, viscous lifeblood, creating eddies and pools where other, smaller creatures can safely feed. They will prune back aggressive vines, not to clear a path, but to encourage a more pleasing, more efficient structure of growth. Their predatory existence is one of curation, of violently editing the endless, chaotic manuscript of the forest, ensuring its prose remains legible, vibrant, and supporting of a higher density of life. They are the brutal, dynamic, and essential force of urban planning.

And in the deep places, where the light is a myth and the air is a fluid, live the Choir. They are sessile, rooted within the deepest layers of the floor, where the old forests are beginning to fossilize. They look like colossal sea-anemones made of calcified wood and crystalline sap, their hundreds of feathery tendrils extending into the thick, syrupy medium of the world's blood. They do not move. They do not eat. They filter. They are the final stage of this cellular world. Over millennia, they absorb the ambient life energy, the runoff of a billion metabolisms, the psychic noise of the Symbiotes and the thermal songs of the Sculptors. They condense it all into a single, silent form of solid light—a deep, amber-colored crystal that grows slowly at their base, the only 'stone' this world will ever know. These are their eggs, or perhaps their libraries, or the foundations of the next layer of the world. They sing a song that has no sound, a steady, rhythmic pulse of pure energy that contributes its own deep, sub-thrum to the omnipresent hum. They are the destination, the end-point of the world's constant accretion, the beginning of its petrification, turning the vibrant chaos of life into the silent, permanent memory of stone.

To be here is to be a part of one of these functions: to be the path, the message, the editor, or the memory. There is no 'I', only a position in the flow. The god is not forming; its cells are simply waking up to the fact that they are already its body.
