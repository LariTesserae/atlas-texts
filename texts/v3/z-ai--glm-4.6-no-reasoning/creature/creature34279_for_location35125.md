To live in the Fallow-Cistern is to be a working part of an engine that has forgotten its maker. The inhabitants do not possess homes, families, or names. They possess *shifts*, *functions*, and *tolerances*. They are not born; they are *deployed* by the fruiting of the floor, budding from the tensile fungal tissue or hatching from copper-wired cysts that warm near the weeping brass pipes. To die here is not a tragedy but a logistical necessity—a return to the spongy floor to be rendered down by the grinder-faces into the nutrient paste that fuels the next cycle. They are a nation of maintenance workers, an unending, self-perpetuating labor force tending a garden that is also a factory, which is also a sleeping god.

The smallest among them are the midges and the clockwork moths, and they are the electrics of this place. The moths do not flutter; they navigate with the rigid, calculated precision of drafting insects. Their wings of hammered steel and stained blue glass slice the heavy, fleshy air, trailing the sharp, clean scent of ozone—a striking counterpoint to the omnipresent damp rot. They land on the amber pustules of the creeping vines not to drink nectar, but to siphon the bio-electric charge stored within, their filament-thoraxes sparking as they top off their internal batteries. They are the sparks that keep the system's neural pathways firing.

Below them, the thumb-sized beetles are the plumbers and the riveters. Their mother-of-pearl and copper-plated carapaces are not armor, but toolbelts. When the brass pipes weep too heavily, or a copper clamp comes loose from the living wall, they swarm. They hiss and vent steam from their leg-joints to weld, to cut, to cauterize. They work in absolute coordination, driven by a pheromonic blueprint that is rewritten every moment by the needs of the Cistern. If a patch of floor-fungi dies, they seal it. If a gear jams, they seep into the mechanism, their chitin clicking against iron until the arboreal chandelier unfurls its woody fronds once more.

The grazers are the harvesters, moving on silent, levitating pads of skittering legs that leave not a single bruise on the living substrate. They are the ones who turn raw growth into manageable stock. They crop the white fungus-celery with mandibles that act as pneumatic shears, their multi-jointed limbs folding and extending with the hypnotic, unerring rhythm of industrial looms. They do not overeat; they do not undereat. They take exactly what the basin can afford to lose, driven by a profound, instinctual understanding of the equilibrium. When they drink from the polished agate basins, their multifaceted eyes—hundreds of tiny, dim stars—reflect not the world, but the process. They see only inputs and outputs.

And then there are the macro-fauna, the heavy equipment. The creature that is part hermit crab, part excavator is a study in repurposing. Its massive shell is an ancient industrial pressure valve, pitted and scarred by epochs of use, yet holding firm. It does not scurry; it *lumber-calculates*. It approaches an overgrown vine with the patience of a glacier, its grinder-face whirring up to speed with a deep, unsettling crescendo before methodically consuming the excess. It is an engine of reduction, turning the sprawling, chaotic reach of the vegetation into a neat, nutrient-rich paste that it excretes directly into the capillary network. It is the stomach of the Fallow-Cistern, and it is forever digesting.

But overarching all of these—the electrics, the plumbers, the harvesters, the heavy equipment—is the Sentinel. It is the apex not because it eats the others, but because it enforces the parameters of the machine. Its form is a terrifying synthesis of woven bone and pneumatic pistons, standing silent and still where the paths converge. It does not graze; it does not weld; it does not siphon. It prunes *anomalies*. Its multifaceted eyes are dead lenses, but the laser-precise targeting systems within them are entirely alive. It watches the flow of the ecosystem, and if a species overpopulates, if a foreign spore takes root, if a stray component (like you) disrupts the rhythm, it acts. Its limbs are shears capable of cutting through bone and brass alike. It is the immune response of the Fallow-Cistern, a creature of absolute, indifferent logic, manifesting as a silent, lethal geometry.

The inhabitants do not know they are imprisoned. They do not know there is a world above the leagues of water and meat that filter the dim light. They do not care. They have the deep, resonant thrum in their bones, the steady *drip... drip... drip...* of the condensation pipes setting their tempo, and the soft, yielding sigh of the floor beneath their feet. They are fed, they are tasked, and they are recycled. In their chitinous, clockwork, fungal perfection, they possess a peace that no surface-dweller could ever understand: the peace of total, unthinking purpose. They are the cogs that keep the behemoth breathing, and as long as the engine thrums, they will continue their eternal, living shift.
