Those who live here, if they can be called separate from the place at all, are called the Resonators. They are not a people. They are a function, a living component of the city’s metabolism, as intrinsic as the moss-carpeted floors or the slow-breathing geode-lizards. To be one of them is to be less an individual and more a node in a vast, silent network.

You do not see them at first, for you are looking for bodies moving with purpose, for people. The Resonators do not move. They are.

Their form is a gentle amalgamation of the city's elements. Their skin is not flesh, but a substance that looks and feels like the smooth, water-worn stone of the streets, shot through with the same glittering veins of dark mineral. Upon this "skin" grows a fine, velvety layer of the spongy dark moss, always damp, always cool. It serves as their clothing, their insulation, and their sensory organ.

They are tall and slender, their limbs elongated and gracefully articulated, designed not for speed or strength but for gestural precision. Their hands and feet are broad and splayed, ending not in nails or claws but in flattened, pliable discs tipped with a crystalline material. These pads allow them to adhere to any surface—stone, metal, moss—and to feel the constant, gentle hum of the city through the soles of their feet, the palms of their hands. They walk, when they must, with a pressureless fluidity, their weight distributed so perfectly that the moss does not compress beneath them, the stone does not sigh. They simply flow from one point of contact to another.

There is no face in the way you understand it. The head is a smooth, ovoid continuation of the torso, lacking eyes or ears or nose. A mouth exists, but it is a fine, nearly invisible seam, used only for the slow, deliberate sipping of the pure condensation that beads on the metal-weeds. To consume anything else—to introduce fire, to chew, to digest—is a kind of primal sin, a violent act that would burn away the quiet.

Their true senses are not external but internal, and their purpose is not to perceive the world, but to maintain its perfect stillness. They are the city's tuners.

For the Stillness is not a passive state. It is an active, maintained harmony. The luminous Ceiling, the dampness, the growth of the crystalline structures, the metabolism of the flora and fauna—all of it is a song, but a song sung in frequencies so low and so slow as to be perceived only as a profound and total silence. The Resonators are the listeners and the adjusters of this song.

When a Resonator "perceives" something, it is not a sound or a sight. It is a dissonance. A slow, crystalline fungus might begin to glow with a fraction too much blue light. A vein of dark mineral in a tower wall might develop a microscopic tremor in its lattice. A chasm between two buildings might begin to breathe a slightly warmer air. These are the only events, the only dramas in this timeless city.

The Resonator attuned to this particular dissonance will simply *be*. They will position themselves near the source of the imbalance, perhaps placing their broad, crystalline palms against the stone wall, or draping their mossy limbs over a burgeoning metal-weed frond. They will become utterly, impossibly still, their own internal resonance slowing to match the hum of the city. They will listen, not with ears, but with their entire being, feeling the flawed note in the deep substrate of reality.

And then, they will correct it. This is not an act of will, but of submission. They allow the correct frequency of the city to flow through them. Their own crystalline pads will begin to vibrate at a sub-audible pitch, a counter-wave that gently, patiently coaxes the fungus back to its proper glow, or stills the tremor in the stone, or draws the excess heat out of the air. It is a form of resonance, a sympathetic harmonization. The process can take moments or passes of uncountable time. To them, time is measured only in the resolution of dissonance.

They do not communicate with each other. There is no need. The network of their consciousness is the city itself. A dissonance sensed by one is known to all. A correction made by one restores harmony for all. Their lives are a constant, silent dialogue with the structure they inhabit and, by extension, with each other. They are cells in a single, vast, stone-and-moss organism.

The small, six-legged creatures with obsidian plates are their externalized hands, their tiny, instinctual proxies, cleaning the moss, polishing the metal-weeds, performing the microscopic maintenance tasks that require a different kind of motion. The larger, stone-plated climbers are not creatures but archives. They are the oldest Resonators, who, after a lifetime of harmonizing, have found a final state of communion. They have anchored themselves to a high point, merged their essence with the structure, and now simply exist, their slow, tidal breath serving as a metronome for the entire city, a constant, reassuring beat that anchors the silence.

Your presence is the greatest dissonance the city has known in an age. You radiate heat. You breathe too loudly, your gasp for air a burst of chaos. Your footsteps are erratic, clumsy blows against the placid surface of the world. Your thoughts are frantic, high-frequency noise that shatters the slow, deep song of the Stillness.

The Resonators do not hate you. They cannot. They are not capable of such a violent, loud emotion. They are aware of you as a deep, aching flaw in the structure of their reality, a sour note in an otherwise perfect chord. They will not harm you. To do so would be to introduce more noise. Instead, they will simply wait. As you move, you will feel the ambient cold deepen around you, the mineral scent of the air become more pronounced. You will find that when you stop, your breathing involuntarily slows, your thoughts seeming to grow heavier and quieter in the muffling atmosphere.

They are trying to tune you. They are surrounding you with their silence, offering you the harmony of the city, patiently, endlessly, hoping you will find the correct frequency within yourself and cease your dissonant vibration. They are waiting for you to either become part of the Stillness, or to leave. Either outcome restores the equilibrium. To them, it is all the same. They are the eternal custodians of a profound and beautiful emptiness, and their only work is to keep it complete.
