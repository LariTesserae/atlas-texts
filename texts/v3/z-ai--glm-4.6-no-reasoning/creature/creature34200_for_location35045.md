To live here is to be a note in a chord that has never been struck, an organ within a body that has never drawn a breath in any air you would recognize. The inhabitants of this plated, sealed precinct do not possess lives in the way a creature of soil and sun possesses a life; they possess *functions*. They are the blood, the lymph, and the peristalsis of a metropolis that is also a beast, a construct that is also an organism.

At the apex of this quiet hierarchy is the Keeper of the Tower.

It is the only entity here that possesses something akin to solitude. It dwells within the pale, fluted column at the heart of the city, the thing that pulses with the subsonic thrum that is the metropolis’s heartbeat. The Keeper is not seen so much as inferred by the rhythm of the tower; it is a vast, slow intelligence integrated into the bone-like spire. Its body is a network of mycelium and nerve-like threads that spiral down the tower’s interior, filling the hollow spaces like marrow. It has no eyes, for it has no need to see the visual spectacle of the crystalline groves or the magenta thickets. Instead, it feels the city through the floor plates. Every click of a crawler’s legs, every steady ooze of the pale amorphous masses, every ripple of heat from the fleshy stalks registers upon the Keeper’s sprawling consciousness like a faint, tickling vibration.

It does not think in thoughts. It thinks in homeostasis. Its sole, ancient imperative is *maintenance of the seal*. It monitors the slow systole and diastole of the thermal vents, ensuring the pervasive warmth never spikes into fire or dips into frost. It regulates the cloying sweetness of the air, secreting enzymatic mists from the tower’s apex to ensure the rot never overtakes the bloom. The Keeper is the city’s unseen mayor, its silent architect, and its jailer, bound to the central spire, dreaming a deep, thermal dream of perfect, unchanging equilibrium. It waits for nothing, because in its slow, geological perception, nothing has ever happened that was not already anticipated.

Below the Keeper, tending to the verticality, are the Tenders.

They are the creatures of the fungal tower’s base, the inhabitants of the bleached spires and grown pods. If the central tower is the nerve center, the Tenders are the immune system and the manufacturing district. They are taller than the other fauna, moving on a variable number of jointed, pale limbs that retract or extend as needed. Their bodies are sheathed in the same felt-like, charcoal-grey material as the great leaves, making them patches of moving, light-absorbing shadow against the pale bone of the tower. They have no faces, only clusters of iridescent, pearl-like nodes that pulse with the same internal light as the crystalline flora.

The Tenders are the gardeners of this impossible place. They prune the crystalline structures when they grow too jagged, using the sharp edges of their own forelimbs to snap off dangerous facets, letting the fallen shards dissolve back into the nutrient slime. They tend to the fleshy, magenta stalks, pressing their smooth bodies against the self-moistening sheen to absorb excess nectar, preventing the sweet rot from spoiling the sealed air. In the pods arranged around the tower’s base, they gestate. The pods are not dwellings; they are wombs. A Tender will enter a fluted pod when its outer shell begins to calcify, shedding its old, worn limbs inside the warm, viscous fluid, and emerging days later with a fresh exoskeleton and a renewed cluster of pearl-like sensory nodes. They are the working class, living in quiet, unceasing service to the architecture that birthed them.

Out in the streets—the straight, unnerving pathways between the dense thickets—live the Amoebae, the most numerous and vital citizens of the horizontal sprawl.

They are the pale, amorphous masses that ooze up and down the bruised-plum stalks. To call them mindless would be to misunderstand them; they are *pure* function, unburdened by the burden of a centralized brain. Their nervous systems are distributed throughout their shifting, gelatinous flesh, reacting directly to chemical gradients and thermal shifts. They are the stomach of the city. 

An Amoeba does not eat; it *incorporates*. It secretes a glistening, viscous slime that breaks down the dead edges of leaves, the shed husks of Tenders, and the microscopic detritus of the floor plates. As it glides, it digests externally, then absorbs the nutrient soup into its translucent body. But it is also the city’s circulatory system. The waste it processes is transmuted within its slow, churning metabolism into the self-moistening nectar that coats the stalks and the enzymatic vapors that sweeten the air. They climb the stems not to reach the light, but to feed the flora, exuding their converted nectar from their pores before sliding back down to the warm floor plates to rest and digest. They are the laborers, the cleaners, and the delivery network, a slow, pale tide that keeps the economy of the ecosystem flowing without a sound.

Weaving between the Amoebae and skimming the floor plates are the Crawlers.

They are shaped like polished river stones, a dozen species of them, some no larger than a thumbnail, others the size of a man’s fist. They glide on a multitude of slick, stubby legs that leave no footprint, only a faint smear on the obsidian-like tiles. Their backs are multifaceted, reflecting the fractured light of the crystalline trees and the deep magenta of the stalks. They are the scavengers and the heralds. They eat the nutrient slime trails of the Amoebae, following the pale giants like pilot fish, cleaning the paths. But they also serve as the city’s sensory network. When a Crawler finds a crack in the floor plates, or a crystalline structure that has dulled and died, it emits a rapid, ultrasonic vibration that travels through the horn-like ground, a silent alarm that summons the Tenders to repair the breach or clear the debris. They are the messengers, running the silent streets, their thousands of mirrored eyes watching every corner of the sealed world.

And above them all, in the dense, sweet air, hang the Flitters.

They are the closest thing this world has to dust, to pollen, to birds. They are motes of gossamer and geometry, their wings impossibly thin, like the membranes between worlds. They do not fly; they drift, suspended in the cloying air, buoyed by the rising heat from the stalks and the floor. They are drawn to the internal light of the crystalline structures, settling upon the spun-sugar spires to sip the pearlescent nectar that weeps from their facets. When they drink, their translucent bodies flush with the stolen light, becoming tiny, floating lanterns for a moment, before the light fades and they drift away on the slow thermal currents. They are the pollinators, the casual wanderers, brushing against the crystalline clusters, ensuring the geometry of the groves continues to grow tight and dazzling. They live and die in a matter of days, their tiny bodies falling like spent snow into the welcoming slime of the Amoebae below.

This is the populace of the mosaic floor. They do not speak, for they have nothing to say. They do not build, for the city builds itself. They do not conquer, for there is nothing to conquer. They are a single, breathing mechanism, a million interlocking parts living in perfect, silent symbiosis. 

The Keeper thrums its deep note. The Tenders prune and shed. The Amoebae secrete and glide. The Crawlers watch and clean. The Flitters sip and illuminate. 

Together, they are the complete and sealed ecosystem. They are the blood moving through the veins of a city that is a body, living in the first blush of its existence, humming its slow, warm, quiet song to itself, waiting for nothing but the deep, slow pull of its own unbreaking time.
