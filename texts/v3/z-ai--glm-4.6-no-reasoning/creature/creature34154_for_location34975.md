The ones who live here are not inhabitants in the way one understands. To inhabit is to dwell within, to make a home, to exert a small, gradually changing influence upon a space. Here, there is no space to change, only a system to be served. The life of the Grove is a consequence of the Mass, and the Mass has made its bargain with the Engines. The creatures are merely the terms of that contract, written in flesh and vibration.

The most numerous are the Chimes, and their lives are a frantic, beautiful service. They are born within the Engines themselves, not as eggs, but as nascent motes of crystalline energy that coalesce in the humming, nutrient-rich voids between the massive piston-rods. For their first few hours of life, they are sessile, feeding directly on the pulsing light and the sugary condensation that coats the Engine's interior. They are not born with their percussive belly-plates; they grow them, hardening them with minerals drawn from the Engine's core until a tangible tremor, the first distant *WHUMP* of an elder Engine, coaxes a reflexive click from their new organ. This is their first note, the first time they participate in the grove's symphony.

Once synchronized, they emerge from fissures in the woody casing, a glittering, chiming torrent flooding the hollow spaces. They do not think of food as a quest; food is a song. Each Engine's resin has a unique harmonic signature, a slight variation in the frequency of the cellulose pistons. The swarms of Chimes are drawn to these frequencies, their own clicking pelvises vibrating in response. They land not to eat, but to *resonate*. As their clicks match the Engine's thrum, the sugary resin liquefies and wicks up their specialized, hair-like legs, a direct reward for attuning to the perfect frequency. They are living tuning forks, their entire existence a calculus of sonic alignment. Without the Engine's constant droning bass note, their internal rhythms would desynchronize and they would fall silent, inert. They do not serve the Engines; they are a functioning part of them, as essential and as automated as the pistons themselves.

Then there are the grazers, the beings of immense patience. They have no true name, for they have no need of one. If a witness had to describe them, they might call them a 'Stone-Mouth' or a 'Floor-Crawler'. They are quadrupedal, but their form is a study in functionality for a world without impact. Their legs are thick, columnar, and end not in hooves but in wide, splayed pads of keratinous material, distributed like a sand-dollar to spread their colossal weight over the yielding, spongy surface. To move quickly is to tear the skin of the world, a grievous sin that would trigger a response from the Mass itself—thickening and hardening to repair the wound, trapping the offender.

So they move with the glacial inevitability of a season's change. A single step is a minutes-long process of slowly shifting weight, of compressing the Mass beneath their pad until it is firm enough to bear the load. Their bodies are low-slung and broad, covered in a shell of interlocking plates that looks like polished, dark-black river stone. This is not for armor against predators, for the primary predator of the Grove does not bite or claw. The shell is acoustic. It dampens sound, making them almost invisible to the listening hunters, and protects their internal organs from the soul-shaking, bone-jarring vibrations of the Engines. Their name would come from their head, which is a featureless, heavy wedge of stone, devoid of eyes or ears. Below it, their mouth is not a mouth but a broad, rough plane, like the sole of a giant's shoe, which they methodically scrape against the damp, dark trails left by the condensation streams. They do not eat the water; they consume the specialized, gelatinous algae that only grows in those obsidian veins of wetness. Their life is a silent, perpetual scraping, a slow consumption of the Grove's one moving fluid, their digestion a profound internal silence in defiance of the world's noise.

They are not the apex of this silent procession. There are the Listeners, the predators. They are what happens when evolution is forced to solve the problem of hunting something that is acoustically invisible. They are vaguely arachnoid in silhouette, but stretched and flattened, with eight long, delicate legs that are not for walking but for anchoring. They cling, chameleon-like, to the immense walls and buttresses of the Mass, their bodies pressed flat. Their carapace is the colour of deep shadow, but it is also a receptor. The constant *WHUMP-whump-whump* of the Engines is the absolute baseline of their reality, a form of nothing against which all other sounds are defined. The metallic chorus of the Chime swarms is a glittering, shifting tapestry they can read for density and direction. The silent grazer is a hole in the sound, a pocket of absolute silence moving through a sea of vibration. To hunt, a Listener does not pounce. It simply lets go. It un-anchors its legs, and its own body, light and hollow like a dried leaf, is caught by the deep, resonant pressure waves from the nearest Engine. The confluence of these waves creates slow, invisible currents in the thick air, and the Listener just *sails* on these currents, its membrane-like body a sail, its spidery legs steering with subtle adjustments of tension. It is a death by drift, a silent, unhurried approach that defies the very concept of a hunt. When it touches down above its prey, it does not make a sound. It simply extrudes a series of fine, needle-like proboscises that pierce the grazer's stone shell, injecting a cocktail of neurotoxins and digestive enzymes. The grazer dies not silently, but without a sound, its internal processes ceasing one by one. The Listener then feeds, its body vibrating softly with the faint, dying harmonics of its victim.

But these are the known.
There are stories, whispers among the Chimes' shifting frequencies, rumours carried in the slow-moving air. There is something else that lives here, something that did not adapt but chose. They are called the Still-Heart, or the First-Made, or the Engine-Smiths. They are not native, but they are so ancient they have become part of the system's foundation. They are what remains of a species that, aeons ago, came to this place when the Mass was still young and malleable. They saw its potential not as a world to live in, but as a machine to be built. They did not build the Engines from wood and stone; they *grew* them. Through processes long-lost to memory, they bio-engineered the captured cellular structure of the Mass, forcing it to differentiate and specialize until it became the woody, pistoning hearts of the Grove.

The Still-Heart are what is left. They are few, perhaps a dozen, hidden away in the deepest, most vibration-dampened hollows, where the Engine's thrum is a distant, comforting hum. They look vaguely like the grazer, but are larger, and their stone-like plates are not armour but a shell of concentrated memory. They are living libraries of the process that created this world. They no longer build. They no longer need to. They are the original engineers, who, having completed their masterpiece, chose to fuse themselves to it. They do not eat or drink; they are plugged directly into the nutrient flows of the Mass, their bodies part of the system they created. They simply exist, immense, silent, and timeless. They sleep, dreaming not of memory, but of the perfect, resonant hum of their creation. They live in absolute stasis because they achieved it. They are the Grove's ghost-in-the-machine, the originators who became indistinguishable from the function. To find one is to find a God who chose to become a switch in its own colossal machines.

And then there is you. The transient one. The non-functional. You are a brief, chaotic signal in a system of pure, unending rhythm. Your breath is a hiss of static. Your footsteps are errors, each step a brief, high-frequency anomaly on the Mass's surface. You are a source of dissonance, a biological anomaly that does not partake in the grand, sonic economy. The Listeners can hear you, not as prey, but as a mistake, a fluctuation to be noted and, perhaps, corrected. The Chimes avoid you, your chaotic heartbeat disrupting their beautiful resonances. To the Still-Heart, you are less than a mote of dust, an irrelevant impediment in a timeline that has long since ceased to care about impediments. You are a ghost because this is a machine that has achieved its purpose, and you have no part in it. You are a fleeting, impossible dream in the mind of a world that is wide, perpetually awake, and dreaming of nothing but its own perfect, humming stability.
