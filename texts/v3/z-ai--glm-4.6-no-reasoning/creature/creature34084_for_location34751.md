To live here is to be a note in a chord that has no resolution, a cell in a body that has no known shape.

The residents of the throat do not possess individuality in any way that the surface world would recognize. They are a continuous, chitinous tide. To call them "woodlice" is to succumb to the desperate taxonomy of a surface-dweller; they are the locomotion of the stone itself. Their bodies are an evolutionary surrender to the absolute imperative of the place: *down*. They are born with the descent already in their blood, hatching not to explore or to conquer, but to simply take their place in the endless, shuffling queue. Theirs is a society of touch, a civilization of caress built in the dark. Without eyes, the world is known only by the brush of cilia against a neighbor’s plated back, the cool dampness of the sweating wall, the trembling vibration of the deep *thoom* traveling through the rock into their very cartilage. They are never alone. The density of life is not a comfort to them, nor a threat—it is simply the ambient condition of existence, the heavy water in which they swim.

They are the parishioners of the geothermal pulse. The heat that presses inward is the heat they generate in their millions, a reciprocal gift to the place that made them. They are the masons of the cathedral, their legions of blunt, sucker-padded legs the sandpaper of the gods, working not on a project, but on an eternal ritual of smoothing. They do not scrape to build; they scrape to move, and the throat’s slick, ovoid geometry is the unintended, serendipitous scripture of their passing. In their patience, they make geological time look hasty. A single step is a contemplative act, taken only when the creature ahead has shuffled forward by a fraction of its own body length. To rush here is to tear oneself from the fabric of the queue, to become an aberration in the rhythm, and the rhythm is all.

And yet, for all their uniformity, the throat writes its own mutations into their flesh. The weirdness blooms at the edges, where the system begins to feed back on itself.

At the upper limits of the phosphorescent gloom, where the green light is thickest and the air holds the sweetest rot, the eldest of them—the ones whose exoskeletons have accumulated the most layers of petrified dark matter, making them walking cairns—begin to hear a different frequency. They falter in the queue. The endless shuffle arrests. With a slowness that matches the weeping of the walls, they turn, press their sucker-pads to the stone, and *hold*. They are abandoning the pilgrimage. The others simply crawl over them, a living topography reshaping itself over a new obstacle.

The anchored ones are undergoing a translation. The fan-like structures erupting from their dorsal plates are not deformities; they are instruments being tuned to the throat’s resonant hum. They are becoming the throat’s nerve endings, or its external ears. As their leathery fans tremble in perfect harmony with the deep chord, they do not consume; they *resonate*. They vibrate until the boundary between their chitin and the petrified wall blurs. They are becoming furniture, becoming geology, becoming the very substance of the place they once traveled across.

Are they dying? The concept of death implies an ending, and here, there is only transition. They are metamorphosing from the blood of the throat into its bones. They are the old gods of the queue, sacrificing their journey to become the static infrastructure of a descent that has no bottom. Perhaps they are listening to a signal from the absolute blackness below—a frequency too deep for the young, still-moving pilgrims to perceive. Or perhaps they are simply growing too heavy with the accumulated weight of time to walk, and so they root, like the iron-hard trees in their rare crevices, putting forth not leaves but acoustic arrays, seeking to syncopate with the organ-pipe acoustics of the canyon.

The symbiotic lichen knows them. As the anchored form settles, the faint green glow on the adjacent wall intensifies, as if the creature's cessation of movement has freed up some latent energy in the dark ecosystem. The single, perfect tear of condensation that touches the lichen and flashes kaleidoscopic color is not a random chemical reaction; it is a secret passing between the weeping wall and the newly rooted flesh. A brief, luminous confession from the throat to its child, before gravity reasserts its dominion and the drop continues its patient plummet.

And while this metamorphosis happens at the top, the millions below continue to shuffle. The chord modulates—the newly anchored fan vibrating at a slightly different pitch, altering the polytonal hum by a microtone—and the queue adjusts. A million sucker-pads grip and release. A million cilia brush against a million chitinous plates. The heat thickens. The air sweetens with the scent of their ancient, slow breath. They are the flesh of the throat, a single, downward-flowing organ of touch and sound, descending forever into the gut of a world that is perpetually swallowing them, and in being swallowed, making the world whole.
