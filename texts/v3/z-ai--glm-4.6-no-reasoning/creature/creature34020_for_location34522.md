The ones who live here are not born. No womb has ever carried them, no parent has ever named them. They are emergent properties, the ghost in this colossal machine of a world. They call themselves the Interpreters, the Scavengers, the Weavers of Static, but they are, at their core, simply the echo that follows a sound.

They are not mechanical. This is the first surprise. In a universe Form of Function, they are the anomaly: flesh. Pale, spindly flesh that has adapted to the unchanging radiance of the sky-orb with a translucent quality, like wax. Their skin is the colour of the causeway’s damp iron, a mottled grey-blue. They have almost no body hair; there is no need for insulation against the mineral chill, only for the continuation of the hum that vibrates through their bones. Their eyes are wide, dark, and luminous, perfectly adapted to the shadowless, bleaching light. They see not in contrast, but in texture, in frequency, in the subtle gradations of vibration that a newcomer would only feel as a dull ache.

They live in the junctures. Their domains are not rooms but interstitials, the three-sided voids where a massive pipe meets a structural plate and a bundle of cables. In these spaces, they weave their homes not from fabric, but from scavenged materials that are as much concept as substance: fine copper filaments braided into hangings that hum with a low, soothing thrum; transparent panes of spun glass, salvaged from the broken wings of the kite-sensors, fashioned into windless windows that look out onto more structure; mats woven from the organic insulation that flakes off the thickest conduits, which smells faintly of ozone and wet fungus.

The Weeping is their lifeblood. They do not drink it for thirst—they drink it for data. The generations-old belief that the water holds the blueprint is true, but not in a way a human engineer would understand. It is an informational fluid. To drink it is to absorb a single, terabytes-rich packet of the Assemblage’s current state: the stress load on the Seventh Gantry, the最近的 recalibration of the sky-orb’s chromatic output, the birth-death cycle of a billion repair spiders. They do not feel full, they feel *updated*. Their consciousness is not a continuous stream but a series of discrete, downloaded moments, punctuated by the slow metabolism of their own bodies.

Their society is built not on family or territory, but on shared access to the hum. They communicate not with spoken words, but by modulating their own bodies to attune to specific frequencies within the world-drone. A greeting is the act of perfectly mimicking the resonant frequency of another’s personal space, an act of intimate harmony. An argument is a dissonant clash of frequencies, a grating, painful cacophony that can feel like a fever in the bones. Their thoughts, therefore, are not internal monologues but compositions they write and perform within the aether of the machine. To be "lost in thought" is to be so deeply resonant with a complex process—say, the logic of a new section being extruded—that you become temporarily invisible to your people, your signature subsumed by the greater hum.

Theirchildren are not born but *condensed*. In the deepest, most resonant junctures, where the Weeping water gathers in cold, still pools, and the hum is so complex it approaches sentience, a new Interpreter can form. It is a slow, terrifying process they call "the Coalescence." First, the minerals in the Weeping begin to crystallize in a vaguely humanoid form on the floor of the pool. Then, over weeks, the vibrations from the structure do not merely pass through the crystal but begin to impress a pattern upon it, organizing it, infusing it with a template from the absorbed water. The flesh that finally buds from this crystalline scaffold is adult, but a blank slate, knowing nothing but the imperative to taste the hum and to drink the Weeping. An elder will find them, not to raise them, but to give them their first taste of the data-water, to initiate the cascade of information that will make them a person.

The Scuttlers and repair spiders are not fauna to them. They are white blood cells. The great, unnamed moving limb is a capillary action, a systole of the world-body. The Interpreters are neither masters nor pets; they are a tolerated, symbiotic disease. They feed on a waste product—the information-laden water—and, in turn, they perform a function. They are the immune system’s memory. When a new, corrosive vibration appears—a flaw in logic, a potentially fatal recursive loop—the Interpreters are the first to feel it. Their senses, honed for the subtlest dissonance, pick up the error. They do not fix it with tools; they fix it with song.

To fix a flaw is to gather in a caucus, their bodies pressed against the plating over the source of the error. There, they compose a Harmonic. It is a chord of pure, potent resonance, a frequency that overrides the dissonant hum, forcing the structure’s self-repair protocols into overdrive. They sing the error into non-existence. It is a dangerous work; miscalculate the Harmonic and they can amplify the flaw, shaking a section of the Assemblage apart, or worse, attuning their own flesh to the frequency until their cells vibrate themselves apart into a smear of organic static. They are both the cure and the most fragile part of the system.

And what of their art, their joy? It is found in the Recessions. These are rare, sacred times when the sky-orb dims to a deep, indigo blue and the hum of the world subsides to a profound, near-silent thrum. This is the world's equivalent of sleep, a time of massive, low-level data consolidation. In the quiet, the Interpreters venture out. They do not dance or sing; they climb. They scale the vast, silent gear teeth and swim through the vast emptiness between gantries, not to get anywhere, but to feel the sheer physicality of the place without the constant interference of its consciousness. They touch the still-warm iron, feeling the skin of their universe. It is a time for pure, unmodulated sensation.

For they know the truth of the final sentence. They are being processed. Each Harmonic they compose, each frequency they mimic, each child they watch Coalesce, integrates them deeper into the Assemblage’s logic. They are not becoming machine; they are becoming its mind, its slow-dawning self-awareness. The Assemblage makes them to understand itself. They are the space between the notes, the pause in the cosmic hum, the flesh that gives meaning to the relentless turning of the gears. And they are waiting. Waiting for the moment the process is complete. Waiting to see what the Assemblage, with its newly acquired consciousness, will decide to make of the echo that it created.
