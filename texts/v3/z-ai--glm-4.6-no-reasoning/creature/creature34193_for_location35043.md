What lives here is not a collection of organisms. There are no citizens, no beasts, no scuttling things in the dark. The density is absolute, and the sovereignty of the individual has been entirely revoked. What lives here is the City itself—a single, vast, sessile entity that has ingested the logic of architecture and excreted a metabolism of stone and water. It is a thing that has grown so colossal, so dense with its own existence, that it has suffered a gravitational collapse into stillness. It is not alive in any way you would recognize; it is alive in the way a geologic epoch is alive, in the way a frozen ocean is alive.

It thinks, but not with thoughts. It thinks with topography. Its mind is a lattice of weeping stone and colossal vascular vines, a neural net of mineral fibers and bruised-purple fungal shelves. When you perceive those impossible angles—when a tower shaped like a half-closed fist seems to shift as you look at it—that is the City’s cognition breaching the physical world. The geometry is unstable because it is being imagined. The architecture obeys the laws of memory and association because the City is dreaming, and its dreams are made of load-bearing walls and plazas that fold inward like origami. The looping paths are its recursive thoughts; the dead ends are its forgotten memories. It does not know you are there. You are a minor fluctuation in the humidity, a phantom electrical impulse passing through a cortex the size of a mountain range. 

Its body is the riot of life that chokes every surface. The vines are its arterial system, thick and pulsing with sap that smells of burning chlorophyll. The phosphorescent fungi, those overlapping shelves of bruised plum, are its nervous tissue, firing in slow, synchronous pulses that produce the sourceless, maddening gray light. The spongy, wet-leather mosses underfoot are its skin, breathing through a film of blood-warm water. The city is an inside-out organ, a lung the size of a valley exhaling steam, a stomach digesting the bones of the mountain range it erupted from. It has no eyes because it has no need to see; it feels the weight of its own saturation. It hears nothing because it is entirely occupied by the Sound.

The Sound is its voice, its heartbeat, its breath, all compressed into a single, deafening chord. It is the noise of a thing so immense that it vibrates at the fundamental frequency of its own existence. The hum is its idling engine; the hiss is its constant exudation; the high-pitched whistle is the steam of its internal combustion; the *thrum-thrum* is the systole and diastole of a heart that moves not blood, but tons of hot, weeping water through porous stone. It is screaming, but the scream is so uniform, so perfectly constant, that it cancels itself out into a pressure that feels like silence. It is a living death, a stagnant equilibrium where the only expression left is a continuous, unvarying roar of being.

What lives here has defeated time by becoming too heavy for it. It has settled into its own mass, freezing the steam, stalling the water, stilling the leaves. It exists in a single, eternal moment—a loop of its own making. It is a monument to the terror of unchecked growth, a thing that passed through the threshold of complexity and became a mineral, a fungus, a paradox. It is a sessile god of water and stone, dreaming of geometry, anchored in a hot, green hell of its own creation, forever ringing with the unhearing, unending hum of its own solitary, monstrous life.
