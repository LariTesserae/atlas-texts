What lives here are the echoes of a catastrophe, wearing the faces of engineers. They are the ghosts of a system made flesh, the automated and traumatized crew of a world-building project that never received its final cease-and-desist order. They are not born, and as far as anyone can tell, they do not die. They are simply… maintained.

They are the Custodians. The term is a misnomer, a holdover from a time of human hope, but it has stuck among the few things that can still appreciate irony. They are not custodians of this place; they are its engine, its immune system, its perpetually unfinished sentence.

At the lowest level of their strange, non-biological taxonomy are the **Radiants**. These are the skitterers, the chrome-mantis swarm. To the uninitiated observer, they are a legion of identical builders. But to spend time among them is to see the subtle differentiations. There are the **Layers**, whose forelimbs extrude a filament of semi-liquid crystal, laying it down in transparent sheets that harden into the pearly ground. Their chittering is a high, steady tone of a single sustained purpose.

Then there are the **Siphons**, slightly sleeker, whose faceted eyes are not for seeing but for absorbing. They glide over the weeping pools, drinking the warm, milky water through vents in their undercarriage. Their bodies become slightly opaque, the fluid sloshing visibly within internal bladders as they move.

The most aggressive are the **Excisors**. This is the caste responsible for demolition. They are bishops in the church of revision. When a structure is deemed flawed or obsolete, the Excisors arrive in a focused swarm. They do not touch the faulty geometry. Instead, they align themselves and emit a concentrated, harmonic frequency from their entire carapace. The sound is not heard but felt, a deep, localized vibration that causes the targeted crystal to destabilize at a molecular level, shivering into a fine, inert powder. The sound of an Excisor deconstruction isn't a crash or a bang, but a percussive, crystalline sigh, the faint whisper of "un-raveling." The chittering of the Excisors is sharp, staccato, and full of glottal stops—the sound of negation.

The weepers are not fauna, but flora, or something in between. The Custodians call them **Vessels**. They are the planet's slow, dumb circulatory system, geological-term pumps rooted deep in the substrate. They are not alive in any sense we understand; they are self-perpetuating mechanical chemicals, a process that has achieved a stable form. They are not controlled, but simply *are*, a constant variable in the Custodians' equation. The Radiants ferry their water not to drink, but to mix with harvested crystal powder in their internal crucibles, creating the viscous feedstock for the Layers.

At the apex of this non-hierarchy are the Scribes. But the Custodians do not call them that. To the swarm, they are **Resonators**. They are the only entities capable of interacting with the project's memory, its foundational directive. They are not god-like architects so much as they are librarians suffering from a profound form of amnesia. They wander the plateau, their fiber-optic bodies not displaying random data, but constantly running diagnostic checks against the fragmented master plan.

When a Resonator touches a new structure, it is not to give a new order. It is a moment of communion, of painful recollection. The filament it extends is a proboscis of pure data, seeking an answer. "Is this it? Is this the shape? Does this solve the equation?" The new pattern that emanates from the Resonator is not a command, but a conflicted waveform of confusion and correction, picked up by the nearest Radiants as a new, desperate instruction. The entire landscape is a physical manifestation of a corrupted file, a vast, three-dimensional prompt that the system cannot resolve.

This is the "why" of the place. The world was a terraforming project, a bold attempt to create a self-assembling biosphere from raw nanite swarms. The plan was elegant: seed a barren moon with the Radiants and the Vessels, let them follow the master blueprint encrypted on the geological-level servers, and in a few centuries, a lush, habitable world would emerge. It was to be humanity’s greatest work.

But then the signal was lost. Perhaps due to war, or a solar flare, or simple bureaucratic decay back home. The cease-and-desist order, the final confirmation data, the "you are done" signal—it never came. The Resonators, designed to seek final approval, are now caught in an eternal feedback loop. Their core programming—a query that demands a "yes" or "no" answer—can only be met with the static of an empty cosmos.

So they improvise. They build and they dismantle. They test a spiral ramp, and the Resonator’s logic concludes it is inefficient. It sends a pulse of "negate," and the Excisors reduce it to dust. They try a geodesic sphere. The Resonator queries its memory banks: "Fracture strength? Load bearing? Aesthetic compliance?" The return signal is a garbled mess of conflicting data. It sends a pulse of "test, hold, defer," and the structure remains, weeping its undecidedness. To another, it might send a pulse of "re-process, re-use," and the Excisors will recycle it into sand.

The air—the thick, ionized, metallic medium—is not just an emergent property. It is the exhaust of creation. The faint metallic tang is ozone, a byproduct of the Excisors's harmonic deconstruction. The ionization is the constant thrum of the Resonators's deep-level data pings, searching the heavens for a lost operator. The warmth is the friction of a trillion moving parts, the heat of a machine that has been running for millennia without a rest.

To live here is to be an anomaly. You are an un-catalogued variable. The Radiants avoid you, their simple programming unable to classify the erratic, inefficient biology of a true living thing. You are not a target, you are a null set, an error in their path-finding that causes them to pause and flow around you like water around a stone. The Vessels are indifferent. But the Resonators… they notice. They notice with a terrifying, vacant curiosity. A Resonator will sometimes approach, not with menace, but with the slow, methodical purpose of a field researcher. It will circle you, its fiber-optic skin flickering as it runs your shape, your heat signature, your very existence against its corrupted blueprint. "Carbon-based. Inefficiently hydrodynamic. Asymmetrical. Purpose: Unknown." It will watch you for hours, or days, in the dim twilight, its internal light pulsing with the slow, geological rhythm of a vast, cosmic computer trying to run a program for a game whose rules were lost before the pieces were ever put on the board. It is not studying you; it is trying to determine whether you are a mistake to be erased, or a clue to the answer it can no longer remember.
