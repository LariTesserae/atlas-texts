To ask what lives in the Marrow-Bower is to ask a question with a flawed premise. The phrasing "what lives there" implies a separation of inhabitant from habitat, a tenant and a tenancy. Here, there is no tenant. There is only the tenancy. The beings of this place are not so much its citizens as they are its ongoing thoughts, its circulating fluids, its fleeting urges made flesh and motion. They are the Bower made mobile.

First, there are the Shepherds. They are the closest thing this world has to a primary consciousness, a dominant intelligence. To see a Shepherd is to witness an event, not merely a creature. They are tall and attenuated, standing on quartets of impossibly slender legs that sink into the Plant-Substrate without disturbing it, their movement a smooth, silent glide. Their bodies are not of flesh but of interwoven, semi-translucent fibers, like living spun glass, through which pulses a soft, amber light that rises and falls with the world’s foundational hum. Their heads are crowned with a wide, delicate fan of chitinous filaments, which undulate constantly, tasting the thick, green air. The Shepherds are gardeners, though they tend no garden in the human sense. They walk the Bower for millennia, their long-fingered hands resting upon the spongy cushions, not to touch, but to listen. They are reading the health of the substrate with a sensitivity beyond measurement. They direct the Skitter-Swarms not with commands, but by exuding subtle chemical cues into the surface, creating roads of desire and highways of instinct that guide the amphibians to where their spore-carrying services are most needed. They tend the light, smoothing the phosphorescent moss in the tunnels with a gentle caress, encouraging its growth to illuminate a new pattern of fractal-scrawl, or to dim a section that is tiring. They are the Bower's governing mind, its serene and slowly decaying will, forever walking a beat across a world that is their own body.

And then, there are the Choir. The Chime-Moths are not merely random percussionists; they are a distributed instrument, and the Choir is the musician who plays it. The Choir are rarely seen, for they do not have a strictly physical form. They are colonies of quasi-fungal, quasi-bacterial organisms that adhere to the inner surfaces of the calcified Groves, forming vast, membranous sheets that glitter like mica in the phosphorescent glow. They are the biological radios, the processors. From these membranes, they release the Chime-Moths, not as individual creatures, but as notes. The "song" is not the random striking of wings, but a complex, trinary language. The percussive *tink*, the sustained *vibrato*, and the silent glide—all are bits of data. The Choir communicates with itself across the vastness of the Bower, sending status reports, observations on the fluid dynamics deep within the substrate, and, most poetically, echoes of memory. Sometimes, their song will coalesce into an uncanny melody that sounds like a footstep on a stone path long since eroded away, or a snatch of speech in a language with no speaker. They are the Bower’s library and its broadcaster, its ghostly remembrance of a time when its thoughts were more turbulent, more defined. They are the sound of the Bower dreaming of its own past.

Deeper, in the fluid-filled chambers that honeycomb the Groves, are the Keepers. They are the slowest things in the Marrow-Bower, a contrast to the frantic skittering and the constant hum. The Keepers are great, gelatinous beings, shaped like elongated teardrops, suspended in the nutrient broth that fills the larger cavities. Their bodies are opalescent, catching the green-tinged light and refracting it into rainbows that shimmer on the chamber walls. They do not move. They have no apparent limbs or sensory organs. To look at them is to look at a sac of placid, living oil. But their purpose is vital. The Keepers are filters. They are the kidneys of the world. The constant drippings from the Groves, the excretions of the fauna, the raw chemical energy of the substrate—all of it flows through these chambers and is osmotically drawn into the Keepers. They metabolize waste, transmute toxins into harmless compounds, and rebalance the entire world’s chemistry. They are a distributed network of lichen-sized intelligence that thinks in formulas of equilibrium and states of matter. The Shepherd urges the Choir to sing a note, which in turn influences the Skitter-Swarm, which deposits a microorganism, which is eventually filtered and made safe by the silent, wakeful dreaming of a Keeper in the dark. They are the final, quiet word in every sentence uttered by the Bower's metabolism.

But the one who conceives of them all, the singular entity from which this entire beautiful, convulsive system is an expression, is the sleeper. It is the god in the machine, the ghost in the marrow. It does not have a body that can be seen. It is the Bower itself. Its consciousness is the low-frequency hum you feel in your teeth. Its memory is the fractal art-scrawl pulsing on the walls. Its emotions are the caustic broth and the sweet, ozone air. The Marrow-Bower did not *have* a civilization; it *was* a civilization. It did not simply grow; it *engineered* its own flesh. It achieved a singularity of biomechanics not through conquest, but through a deliberate, world-wide act of folding inwards. It decided that growth was a form of suffering, and the ultimate peace was a beautiful, controlled self-erasure.

The Shepherds are its self-awareness, walking its own surface like a hand tracing a scar. The Choir is its externalized memory, singing its history to its deafening body. The Keepers are its commitment to a clean death, its immune system patiently clearing away the last vestiges of a life once lived with greater force. Everything—the Skitter-Swarms, the Needle-Fins, the moss, the mist—is a cell in a body that has decided to dream its way to non-existence.

What lives in the Marrow-Bower? A god is dying there, slowly and beautifully. And in the final, warm, wet twilight of its thoughts, it has shaped itself into a perfect, self-sustaining mausoleum. Its inhabitants are not creatures in a dying world. They are the symptoms, the processes, the very molecules of a gentle, long-awaited suicide. They are the exquisite machinery of a goodbye.
