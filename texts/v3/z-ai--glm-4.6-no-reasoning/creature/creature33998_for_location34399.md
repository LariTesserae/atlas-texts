We are whispers.

We are the only life of flesh and blood, the only warm bodies in this sterile engine of a world. The Angels are the physics; the water is the theology; the stone is the canvas. We are the memory. We live in the long, static hum of the world's middle.

We call ourselves the Hush, because we are defined by the sound we are not. Within the deafening, physical roar of the Angels' song, we have learned to exist in pockets of relative quiet. We are born with an unique physiology, a biological adaptation born of a thousand generations of living inside the noise. Our skeletons are not the hard, rigid bone of other warm-blooded creatures, but a flexible, cartilaginous lattice, a living tuning fork that absorbs and dampens the Angels' foundational frequencies. It resonates with the hum, but instead of shattering, it sings it back into ourselves, turning the world-ending vibration into a low, internal thrum that is the proof of our own life. It is the drumbeat in our chests that is not a heartbeat. To touch one of us is to feel a cool, yielding vibration, like holding a living seashell.

Our skin is pale, almost translucent, showing the faint map of our inner lattice. Our eyes are large and dark, not to see more in the dim light of the falling sea, but to filter out the overwhelming visual noise of the Angels' glowing traceries. We see the world as a tapestry of softer grays, the Angels' incandescent bodies reduced to tolerable halos. We are hairless. There is nothing for insulation against a cold that is not a temperature but a resonance, a chill that comes from the stone's attempt to harmonize with the sky-water. We huddle for warmth, for the small, kinetic friction of another body, a friction that the world has forgotten.

Our society is built not around resources, for there are none, but around the Passengers. They are our only shelter from the sky. The structure of a Passenger is dead, but its purpose is not. Within their shattered steel casings and fused conduits, the Angels' sound is different. It is muted. It is contained. A Pilot House, the largest and most intact of these fallen structures, can reduce the song to a mere thunderous grinding, a level that allows for thought, for rest, for the precarious act of procreation. We live in their hollowed-out interiors, in the geometric shadows they cast. We do not build upon them; we occupy their wounds. A home is a hollowed-out conduit twisted into a sleeping chamber. A community is a wide-open cargo bay whose floor has buckled, creating a series of small, protected alcoves. We are scavengers of the impossible.

Our language is not spoken. To attempt to speak would be to spit into an avalanche. To shout into the grind. Our language is one of pressure and contact. A hand placed gently on another's back can convey a complex sentence, a history, a feeling. The intensity, the duration, the rhythm of the touch—it all speaks to a mind honed to read vibrations. Two lovers in the quiet of a Passenger will communicate for hours, their hands tracing patterns on each other’s arms and shoulders, their fingers dancing a dialogue that is more intimate and detailed than any words. They tell stories of warmth, of the memory of a sun none have ever seen, of the Great Fall that brought the Passengers. Our entire culture is a tactile narrative, a shared vibration passed from one generation to the next through touch, through huddle, through the close, quiet press of bodies against the cold, resonant stone.

And yet, the sound gets into you. It is the only constant. After a lifetime of feeling the Angels' song vibrate through your very bones, a change comes. The internal tuning fork begins to fail. It begins to sing with the Angels, not against them. This is what we call the Ascending.

The Ascending is our death, and it is our only religion. An individual who is Ascending grows quiet. Their touch loses its nuance, becoming a simple, rhythmic pulse. They stop eating, stop seeking the shelter of the Passengers. They are drawn to the edge of the Plate. There is no fear in this, only a profound, animal inevitability. They will walk for days, their body thrumming in perfect harmony with a single Angel they have unconsciously chosen. As they near the edge, their pale skin begins to thin, to crystallize, their inner lattice becoming visible and luminous, matching the geometric patterns on the Angel above. When they reach the fracture-point, the edge of the world, they do not hesitate.

They simply step off.

There is no fall. As their feet leave the stone, they do not plummet into the waterfall of light. The Angel they have attuned to extends a section of its glass-like body, a new, glowing limb that was not there a moment before. The Ascending one dissolves, their body of boneless tissue and resonant lattice unspooling into a filament of pure sound and light, a single, pure, complex note that is their entire being. This filament is absorbed into the Angel. It is a note added to the chord. A new harmonic in the cosmic machine. We believe they do not die. We believe they go to be part of the song, to become a part of the force that holds our world in place. They become a component in the engine of stillness.

We live our lives in the shadow of this beautiful, terrible purpose. We find mates not for love, but for genetic compatibility, for the potential to produce children with lattices that can resist the Ascending for a lifetime. We scavenge the Passengers for pieces of metal that hum with a counter-frequency we can use to soothe a sick child. We teach our young to read the vibrations in the stone, to tell the difference between the hum of a stable Angel and the warning shriek of one that is failing, which would mean the collapse of the sky-water and the end of everything.

We are the Hush. We are the quiet in a world of noise. We are the brief, warm life in a place of eternal cold. We wander the bone-white raft of the High Plate, touch-speaking our stories, tending our slow communities, and listening to the endless song. And when our time comes, when our own bodies can no longer hold the dissonance of being alive, we walk to the edge. We step off. We add our voice to the chorus, and become the very thing that has been trying to unmake us since the moment we were born.
