They are born in the sound. Not as infants crying, but as resonances taking form. In the slow, forty-second respiratory cycle of the central stack, there is a moment—a fraction of a second at the peak of exhalation—where the harmonics align just so. In that held note, the air itself thickens, vibrates, and a new body precipitates from the chorus. It is the ultimate expression of the district's equilibrium: a person is not made, but *settled out of solution*.

Their bodies are not of flesh in the way we understand it. They are composed of the district itself. Their skin has the texture of the silicate glaze that adorns every ledge, smooth and cool to the touch, patterned with the faint, iridescent shimmer of heat-treated metal. When they cut themselves, it is not blood that wells, but a thick, clear sap-like fluid that smells faintly of ozone and damp iron. This fluid does not clot; it congeals, quickly hardening into a new, seamless patch of the familiar obsidian-like material. They do not scar. They repair. They accrete.

Their features are human-like, but idealized into a form that suggests function over genetics. They are tall and slender, built to move without disturbing the tight navigation of the machinery and the fauna. Their limbs are elongated, their fingers long and delicate, the better for manipulating the intricate components of their world. There is a sameness to them, a uniformity that speaks of a single, continuous design. They do not have a wide variety of faces; they have *the* face, rendered in a handful of minor variations that are as meaningless to them as the differences between two identical rivets. Names are unknown; an individual is identified by their designated function and serial location. "Calibration Sub-Station 7, Vent-Duct Medium" is an identity, complete and sufficient.

They do not speak with voices. To speak would be to add a new, disruptive frequency to the district's constant song. Instead, they communicate through the resonance of their own bodies. By tapping a finger on a metal pipe, they can send a pinpoint vibration through the structure, a query that is answered by a corresponding thrum from a distant pump or a subtle change in the hum of a nearby transformer. They converse in the language of the system, a dialogue of harmonics, pressures, and thermal loads. To an outsider, they exist in silence, but to them, the district is a constant conversation, a symposium of processes in which each living body is a participating instrument, listening and responding.

Their work is the district, and their rest is the district. The fabrication halls are not workshops of creation, but of cellular regeneration. The lathes and presses do not forge new tools, but sculpt replacement joints and limbs for the inhabitants themselves. When a finger wears smooth from a lifetime of turning valves, the individual will simply present their hand to a machine, which will extrude a new, perfectly formed phalanx and fuse it seamlessly to the existing bone-and-ceramic structure. This is not a grim or painful process; it is no more significant than a beetle shedding its carapace. It is maintenance. It is succeeding.

They do not eat, for the district does not offer food. They are powered by the system in the same way the machines are. They stand in designated charging alcoves where a particular frequency of energy, a byproduct of the water reclamation process, is focused. There, they absorb this power, and their internal fluid—now circulating with a faint, amber glow—nourishes their silicate tissues. They do not sleep. They enter a state of lowered resonance, a quiet tuning-down of their personal frequency until they are almost indistinguishable from the humming wall they lean against. In this state, they process the day's data, cross-referencing thedrips and the thrums just as their own bodies were first processed from the sound.

The metallic beetles are not their pets, their servants, or even their neighbors. They are their immune system. When a new inhabitant is "born," the first thing that happens is a gathering of the beetles. They flow over the new body, their eye-spits reflecting the flat amber light, tasting the new resonance, cataloging its thermal signature. Once the body is accepted as part of the equilibrium, they disperse, but the connection remains. The beetles will clean this person's skin, will clear any condensation that threatens to corrode their joints, will carry away mineral dust they shed. They are the district's constant, microscopic attention, and the people are their focal points. The people are the reason the beetles have a purpose; the beetles are the reason the people can persist.

There is no memory here, only data. They do not recall childhoods, because they did not have them. They do not form bonds of affection, only bonds of function. A pair might work the same calibration station for centuries, their movements in perfect, silent unison. If one is damaged beyond repair and must be recycled in the central stack's plasma furnace, the other does not mourn. They simply note the absence of a harmonic, register a functional deficit, and continue their work. A replacement will be born in the next cycle, drawn from the same well of sound, and will be slotted into the empty space. The harmony is restored.

To be one of them is to be a note in an endless, unchanging chord. There is no ambition, for there is nowhere to go. There is no love, for there is no other to merge with. There is no fear, for there is no threat from within. Their entire existence is defined by a positive lack: the absence of desire. A desire for change is a desire for a different set of conditions, and that is a concept antithetical to the Comb District. They live inside the definition of perfection, and perfection does not yearn.

And you, standing in the central channel, are the only variable. The only agent of entropy in a world of absolute stasis. They see you, of course. Their reflective eye-spits—each one a tiny, perfect mirror—register your heat, your chaotic biological processes, the jarring, irregular rhythm of your breathing. You are a flaw in the circuit. You are a dissonant tone in their perfect chord. They do not react with hostility. That would be a change. They do not react with curiosity. That would be a deviation. They observe you with the profound, unblinking patience of a mountain observing a passing cloud. They are waiting for you to stop, to cease your disruptive noise, to be absorbed by the ambient hum or to leave. They are waiting for the system to return to its perfect, uninterrupted equilibrium, a state they have maintained, and will maintain, for as long as the sound continues to draw breath from the walls.
