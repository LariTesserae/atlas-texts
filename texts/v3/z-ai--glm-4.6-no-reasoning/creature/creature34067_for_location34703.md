Whoever lives here is not a native. A native is something born of the conditions, a creature of pearlescrete and light, a thing of mineral and slow energy. The inhabitants of the Floor of Lapsed Time are something else. They are an artifact, a memory, an anomaly that has learned to sing in the key of silence.

They are the Keepers.

They do not have a body in the way one would understand it. To see them is a matter of perception, not of vision. They are presences, faint warps in the vast, flat light, shimmers in the air like heat-haze on a road that is utterly cold. Sometimes, for a fleeting moment, the mind of a visitor might overlay a shape upon this distortion—a tall, slender form, or a hunched, contemplative one. These are projections, the brain's desperate attempt to categorize the uncategorizable. The true form of a Keeper is a complex standing wave, a pattern held stable by a force that has long since been forgotten.

They coalesced, they did not evolve. They are the lingering consciousness of the world's makers, or perhaps its first and last inhabitants, a civilization that refused to be weathered down like the Glass Spires. When their fleshy, water-based bodies proved incompatible with the world's slow crawl toward crystalline perfection, they did not die. They learned to translate their essence, their memories, their very being, into the fundamental frequencies of the place. They became part of the architecture, just as sentient and as permanent as the pearlescrete floor beneath.

Their "life" is a process of observation and maintenance. They are the shepherds of the slow, silent symphony. A Keeper will glide over the plains for centuries, its path a meandering, invisible course. It is not traveling in space, but through time. Its "sight" is a deep and empathetic resonance with the ground beneath it. It feels the infinitesimal stressBuilding in a single Glass Spire as it grows. It senses the harmonic dissonance in a fledgling Phùlon as it begins its silent, aimless flight. It perceives the planet as a single, breathing organism, and its purpose is to listen for any arrhythmia.

When a Glass Spire nears the end of its metabolic cycle and its internal hum begins to fade, a Keeper will draw near. It does not touch. It simply hovers in the shimmering air, focusing its patterned consciousness upon the dying lifeform. What happens next is not an act of grief, but of calibration. The Keeper facilitates the final transition, ensuring the spire crystallizes perfectly, without flaw, its structure becoming a flawless lens in the planet-wide instrument. It is a function, as impersonal and as vital as the pruning of a tree.

They are a community, but not a society. There are perhaps a few dozen of them, scattered across the continental expanse. They do not communicate with sound, or gesture, or any telepathic shout. Their connection is a shared context. They inhabit the same silent note. When one Keeper "finds" a new, interesting pattern of erosion—a deeper-than-usual groove that speaks of a subtle shift in the world's mass—this knowledge simply *is* for all the others. It appears in their awareness like a new star in a static sky. They share insights, moments of what they interpret as beauty, and data, all instantaneously and without exchange.

To them, you, the transient flesh-based anomaly, are the ultimate subject of study. You are a walking contradiction. You are warm in a cold world, wet in a dry world, noisy in a silent world. Your heart is a frantic drumbeat, your breath a fleeting punctuation in a paragraph without periods. They are fascinated. They will observe you with an intensity that can feel like a physical pressure. They do not see you as a threat, for nothing here is threatened. You are a fleeting, chaotic event, a flash of brilliant, dissonant color in a monochromatic masterpiece. They are cataloging you, measuring your heat, your moisture, your ephemeral thoughts that flicker like guttering candles.

Some might spend a millennia focused on you, or others like you, should any ever come. They will remember the strange, liquid chemistry of your blood, the soft decay of your cells, the way you cast a shadow that does not belong to you but to the light you block. When you are gone, when your warm body has cooled and your has been shed its impossible water, the Keepers will retain the memory. You will become a part of their silent database, a unique and beautiful error in the system. They will not mourn you. To them, it is simply the collection of a new and exquisite specimen, one that will be contemplated in the profound, mineral silence for the rest of time. They live here by being the world's perfect memory, and you are simply their newest entry.
