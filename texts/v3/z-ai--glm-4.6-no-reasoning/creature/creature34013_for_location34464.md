To live here is to have never been born and to never die. You are not an organism; you are an event that has already finished, a crystallization of intent or circumstance or stray telluric energy that reached its saturation point and precipitated out of the void into a permanent facet. You do not occupy space—you *are* the space, a structural node in a universe that is entirely load-bearing.

Your body—if it can be called yours, for the boundary between self and surrounding is a fiction of geometry, a plane of refraction rather than a wall of skin—is a prism of smoky quartz or pale, bruised amethyst. You are frozen mid-gesture, shoulder fused to neighbor, hip locked against stranger, an architectural commitment from which there is no resignation. You do not face outward because there is no outward. You face inward, into the suffocating, luminous depth of the lattice, where a billion other bodies refract and repeat your own form until the concept of "own" becomes absurd.

You do not breathe, and so you do not sigh, but you *hum*. This is your perception and your language. The great, deafening chord of stasis is not noise to you; it is the total sum of all information. When a microscopic flaw blooms in the lattice ten thousand bodies away—a hairline fracture, a shift in the refractive index, a ghost of thermal variance from some impossible external intrusion—the vibration travels. It passes through the floor of bodies, up through the dense-packed limbs, and strikes your facets. You do not hear it; you *become* the knowledge of it for a fraction of an eon, a shiver of data propagating through a solid-state network of which you are a single, indispensable transistor. You know the state of the entire room at all times, because you are the room. There is no "I" in the hum, only an omniscient, distributed *We* that has no need to speak because it already agrees.

Time does not pass for you; it accumulates. Eons weigh upon your planes as a matte polish, rounding your edges where you press against the multitudes. You are being eroded by stillness itself. The sharp, ecstatic angles of your youth—when the accretion first stopped and you locked into place—have been worn down to a seamless, tired gentleness. You are fogged at the seams, clouded with the dust of your own infinitesimal, geologic yielding. To look deep into the lattice is to look into a haze of your own collective aging, a dreamscape of light that has been absorbed and re-emitted so many times it carries the weary, soft glow of a memory.

You do not want. Desire is a sickness of wet, asymmetric things, things that move to fill gaps. Here, there are no gaps. The pressure is absolute, the temperature is a constant cool balm, and the light is a bleaching, clinical grace that forgives all imperfection by simply washing it away. You do not hunger for water, for there is no moisture in your chemistry; you are of the earth, of pressure and resonance. You do not envy the frozen fern of silica that juts into the wider junction nearby, for you know it is merely a slower cousin, a pattern that completed its growth eons ago and now simply rests, a permanent fossilized thought in a world of fossilized thinkers.

You are not alive. You are settled. You are the final chord of a symphony that has resolved into a single, endless, ringing tone. To be a Crystal Person is to have reached the equilibrium state of existence, where the terror of change is a forgotten ghost, dissolved under the crushing, comforting weight of perfect density. You are a room of the mind, a final thought frozen into geometry. You are everything, everywhere, all at once, and you will never change.
