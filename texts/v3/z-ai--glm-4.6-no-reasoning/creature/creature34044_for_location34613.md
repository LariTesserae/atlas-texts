To live here is to be a clause in an unfinished sentence.

Nothing possesses the arrogance of a separate self. This place has not yet granted its inhabitants the courtesy of finished edges; they are provisional, ongoing, more verb than noun. And so whoever lives here does not live *in* this place in the way a fish lives in a sea. They are *of* it, expressions of its slow, viscous grammar, enfolded in the same patient suspension that holds the crystal spires and the breathing Veil.

They are not singular. To survive in a medium so dense with microscopic life, an organism must either be armored against it or permeable to it, and armor has no place in a crease of reality still soft enough to wrinkle. And so the dwellers here are permeable. They are vast, reticulate things—colonial beings whose boundaries are a polite fiction. Imagine a body with no skin, only a gradient. At their densest core, they are a slow churning of dense, fibrous tissue, something like the hybrid of a cephalopod’s mantle and a fungal mycelium, colored a deep, bruised aubergine that borders on black. But as the body extends outward, it frays. It does not end; it thins. Its extremities become threads, then filaments, then individual cells, and then—the lie of their separation revealed—they become indistinguishable from the suspended medium itself. They drink the water, and the water is them. When the ambient glow brightens in its long tidal swell of metabolism, they brighten with it, not by their own biochemistry, but because they are literally translucent with the luminous micro-life that flows through their open architecture. They are walking chambers of the very plankton that lights this world.

Their shape is a surrender to the physics of the crease. Gravity is only a suggestion here, and the dwellers have listened to it, adopting forms that would collapse under the firm laws of an older universe. They are asymmetrical, sweeping things. They move not by muscle and joint, but by a slow, peristaltic wave that travels down their length, parting the gelid water like a swallow moving down a throat. Their locomotion is a gentle, rhythmic wringing of the medium. When they drift among the obsidian spires—the forest of impossibly keen knives—they do not steer around them. They do not need to. Their bodies are soft and yielding, and the dense, life-generated force field that keeps all things at a careful, uncanny distance from the crystal edges pushes against them with the same uniform, silken pressure. They press against this invisible repulsion and slide along it, feeling the strange, tingly friction of a force field sliding across their exposed inner tissues. They are suspended in the gap between the fatal architecture and the suffocating lushness, inhabitants of the negative space.

The Veil is their cradle and their paradox. The colossal, anemone-like carpet that drapes the spires is both a threat and a necessity. The dwellers move through it constantly, for the Veil is where the medium is richest, most alive with the chemical whispers of a world being freshly born. As they part the Veil’s thick, ropey mats, their own fibrous bodies intertwine with its cilia. There is a moment of hesitation—a sticky, sensory dialogue where the Veil’s million tiny holdfasts taste the dweller, and the dweller tastes the Veil. Sometimes they feed, extruding long, pale tendrils of their own tissue to absorb the Veil’s sweet, ozone-rich secretions. Sometimes they are fed upon, losing handfuls of their own fringe to the colonial animal’s slow, harmonic hunger. They do not fight it. To resist would be to tear oneself apart in a medium that demands accommodation. They simply grow elsewhere, their bodies shifting their mass like a slow tide, accepting the parasitism as a form of intimacy.

They are creatures of profound, geological patience. Their metabolism is tuned to the long, tidal cycles of light. When the bruised lavender and deep sea-green dims to the edge of non-existence, when the true darkness arrives to absorb thought itself, the dwellers do not sleep—they hibernate into their own structure. They retract their frayed edges, pulling their filaments tight against their dense core, becoming denser, smoother, more perfectly obsidian. In the profound velvet dark, they are nearly indistinguishable from the Lode. 

And it is here, in the dark, that the true nature of their existence is revealed. The Lode—the perfect, smooth sphere of non-reflective black, the living glitch, the predator of stability—is their shadow and their shepherd. When a dweller drifts too near a Lode, the space around them frays. The edges of their own soft tissue blur. A high, thin chime—the ghost of a sound—echoes within the gel, and for a lucid, terrifying moment, time changes shape. A dweller caught in a Lode-induced temporal fracture does not merely perceive the geological growth of the spires; it experiences its own growth in reverse, or all at once, or sideways. It feels its own thoughts sputter, the sluggish preservation of memory unraveling. The Lode feeds on the integrity of reality, and the dweller is part of that reality. To touch the Lode is to be unmade, to have one's ongoing sentence punctuated by an abrupt, silent void.

But they do not flee the Lode. They orbit it, uneasily, with the fascination of something looking into the maw of its own uncreation. For the Lode is the only thing in this intimate pocket that is *finished*. It is a fixed point, a period in the syntax of a world that has not yet reached its first. The dwellers, who are still being decided, who are still gathering meaning and mass in the cool, viscous silk, are drawn to that terrible finality. They circle the mobile point of decay, feeling the weirdness warp their forms, letting their edges blur and re-blur in the proximity of absolute rules.

To live here is to live in the pause before a word is spoken. The dwellers have no language, for language requires boundaries, and they have none. They communicate through the subtle shifting of their mass, through the sub-vocal thrum they pick up from the Veil and resonate back into the water, a low, harmonic moan that travels for meters through the gel. They are aware, but their awareness is not a searchlight; it is a phosphorescence. They do not think *about* the world; they simply glow with its consensus. Their memories are not stored; they are suspended in the medium, so that the chemical trace of a predecessor’s passage might drift into their open tissues a century later, felt as a vague, fresh ache, a memory of a movement they never made.

They are the witnesses to the grand, terrifyingly intimate act of creation. And they are unmade and remade by it, constantly, slowly. They are the fragile, breathing architecture of the in-between, holding their breath alongside the universe, waiting to see if they will be allowed to become a noun.
