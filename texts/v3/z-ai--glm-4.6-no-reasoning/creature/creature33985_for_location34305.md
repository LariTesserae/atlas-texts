To live here is to be a contradiction made flesh—something both utterly obsolete and perfectly adapted, a citizen of a city that has forgotten its own name but not its function. The inhabitants of this place are not mere residents; they are symptoms of its persistence, organisms that have been worn into the landscape just as the rain has worn into the rust. They are defined by the damp, the hum, and the slow, glitching geography, and they carry the weight of the valley's perpetual incompleteness in their very bones.

The most numerous are the **Sump-Walkers**, the human-adjacent scavengers who tread the mossy boulevards and navigate the shifting staircases. They are not native to this place, or perhaps they were, once, so long ago that the distinction has eroded like the concrete under their feet. They are the inheritors of the industry, the last custodians of a machine that no longer requires human hands but still demands attention. 

Physically, they have been softened and mineralized by the grievance mist. Their skin is pale to the point of translucence, taking on the grey-blue hue of the fungal light, mapped with the dark, branching trails of rustwater running in their veins. They do not walk quickly; to rush is to attract the attention of the Silent, or to step through a door that was not there a moment ago. Their movement is a slow, deliberate wade through the thick, damp air, their footsteps muffled by the spongy emerald moss but perfectly synchronized with the deep, 1.5-score thrum that shakes the iron girders beneath the streets. They do not speak in voices, for sound is a violence here. Instead, they communicate through a sub-vocal hum, a resonance they feel in their chests, a dialect of the city’s own heartbeat. They tap on the walls of the great factories with iron bars, listening for the echo, reading the health of the slumbering titans like a physician reading a pulse.

They wear the refuse of the old world woven into their being. Their garments are heavy, sodden things, patched together from the chitin-bark of the vines and the insulated wrappings of gnawed power cables. They wear the broad, leathery caps of the fungi as wide brims to deflect the perpetual condensation. Corroded rivets and metal plates are not tools to them, but jewelry, prosthetics, and armor all at once, bolted directly into their flesh, fusing with their rusting blood. They are cyborgs of decay, their bodies a closed loop of industrial rot: they eat the nutrient paste processed by the chitin-dogs from broken gears, and they bleed iron filings that feed the moss when they fall.

The Sump-Walkers live in the **Marginalia**—the spaces the architecture forgot. They build their homes in the impossible geometries, in the gaps between the colossal concrete strata where the rebar juts out like the ribs of a beast. They inhabit the "yesterday" rooms, those spaces that only exist when the city’s dynamic paradoxes align just right. A home might be a vast, round window frame suspended a hundred feet in the air, accessible only by a staircase that leads up yesterday and down today. They anchor their lives with heavy iron weights, terrified that if they do not root themselves to the rusting iron, they might wake up phased into a wall, or falling through a doorway that opens onto the fungal plain below. Their greatest fear is not death, but becoming an echo—losing their narrative weight and fading into the crystalline translucence of the Silent.

And then there are the **Silent**, the apex predators and the true masters of the valley’s weirdness. They are not a species; they are a state of being. They are what happens when a purpose is forgotten but the energy of that purpose remains. They look vaguely humanoid because they are the ghosts of the city’s builders, operators, and engineers, compressed by time and paradox into a new mineral. They are made of a semi-translucent, crystalline substance—perhaps the ultimate, geological endpoint of the concrete and rust, or perhaps a physical manifestation of the glitching physics itself.

The Silent do not walk; they *accrete*. They are drawn to the sparks of gnawed cables, to the points where a staircase loops back on itself, to the overwhelming sorrow of the grievance mist. To see a Silent is to see a walking prism of the city’s memory, refracting the cold blue fungal light into broken, ghostly rainbows. They move with impossible slowness, not because they are slow, but because they experience time differently. They are navigating the 1.2-score dynamic of the city, stepping through the decades as the Sump-Walkers step through puddles. A Silent might stand at the end of a boulevard for a century, its crystalline form slowly ingesting the rust and the rain, and then, in the space between a blink, it is standing right beside you, refracting the light from your own eyes. 

They are terrifying because they are the embodiment of the city’s erosion of reality. They feed on the uncertainty, the "narrative instability." When a Sump-Walker forgets why they entered a room, the Silent are there, growing slightly more opaque. When a metal-bird’s harmonic click falls out of sync with the factory hum, a Silent will drift toward the dissonance. They are the custodians of the paradox, ensuring that the city never completes its birth, and never truly dies, feeding on the entropy to maintain their crystalline forms.

To live in this valley is to live with the constant, gnawing awareness that you are being digested by a slow, vast urban organism. The inhabitants—flesh, metal, and crystal alike—know that they are not the city's purpose. The great factories do not hum for them; the moons do not turn for them. They are simply another layer of the moss, another creeping vine, another rust-streak on the iron gantry. They persist. They wear in. They are the population of an unending, embryonic dusk, holding their breath alongside the slumbering titans, waiting for a completion that the Silent will never allow to come, their lives a quiet, damp sigh wept out of the world itself.
