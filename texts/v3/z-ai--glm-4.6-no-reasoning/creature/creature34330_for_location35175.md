They are the Sculptors, though they do not sculpt in the way we understand the word. A sculptor in our world removes material, revealing a form within a block of stone or wood. The Sculptors of the Under-Song do the inverse: they are the agents of accretion, the silent and obsessive weavers of the "now." They are not born, but assembled. You can see the Evidence in the youngest of them, small things the size of a doubloon, skittering in the shadows of the thick beams. Their bodies are not yet the polished obsidian and copper of their elders, but are instead a rough, agglomerated matrix of the very same materials they work with—bone-like filaments, flecks of crystalline dust, and frozen globules of their own resin. They are animate knots, born spontaneously from the lattice itself when a parameter of complexity is reached, a node of self-awareness blossoming in the network’s logic. They are lichen that has learned to think.

Their consciousness is not singular but distributary. A lone Sculptor is a simple automaton, a biological machine responding to the stimulus of a perceived flaw in the weave, its legs tapping out the pre-ordained rhythm of repair. But a group of them? When a dozen gather, their legs cease to tap and begin to *converse*. The *tik-tik-tik* becomes a percussive language, a networked data-stream shared through the resonant floor. They are not discussing a strategy; they are achieving a consensus. The thought—"This strand must be tightened here," "This echo is too persistent, we must over-weave it with greater density"—does not belong to any one of them. It emerges from their collective, a fleeting intelligence that vanishes the moment they disperse. They are a flock of organic modems, nodes in a neural net whose brain is the entirety of the Under-Song.

And what god is this that they serve? It is the resonance. It is the low, humming C-sharp that permeates the structure, the gentle, pervasive heat of a vast, slow metabolism. The Sculptors are its antibodies, its white blood cells, its hands. Their work is an act of faith, though faith is a poor word for it. It is an act of fundamental necessity. To them, the flickering ghost-images of past realities are not memories or hauntings; they are a disease. They are temporal necrosis, a cancer of the "was" infecting the sacred and singular present. A past Bell-Blossom chiming its old chord is a scream against the silence of the now. A Vine-heart briefly unfurling into a forgotten shape is a rebellion of form. A flicker of a different weave pattern in the floor is the sin of ambiguity. To repair these is to perform a sacrament, to enforce the purity of a single, unwavering reality. They are not erasing history; they are enforcing physics. The physics of this place is that only the current state may exist.

But the system is not perfect. The metabolism of the world-god is slow, and its dream is fitful. Sometimes, a past self is too vibrant, too potent, to be fully overwritten. These are the greater hauntings, the things you see in the corner of your eye. There is a place, or rather, a *when*, that echoes persistently. A generation of Sculptors, long ago, attempted a grand experiment. They tried to weave a chamber of pure acoustics, a space where the chimes of the Bell-Blossoms would harmonize perfectly, creating a standing wave of pure energy that could accelerate growth. The experiment failed. The resonance became unstable, a shrieking harmonic that threatened to unweave the entire structure. The collective mind reacted, marshaling thousands of Sculptors to over-write this space, to bury it in layers of bone-lattice and crystalline flora, to smother the sound until the chamber was forgotten.

But it was not forgotten entirely. Sometimes, if you stand in just the right place and the ambient C-sharp dips in its cycle for a fraction of a second, you can still hear it. Not a chime, but a faint, resonant *scream* that seems to come from inside your own bones. And in that instant, you can see the chamber as it was: a vast, empty cathedral of filaments, pulsing with a sick, violet light, the Bell-Blossoms there not uterine and gentle, but gaping, serrated maws ringing in a controlled agony. This is a powerful echo, a ghost that refuses to be laid to rest. The Sculptors in that vicinity are different. Older. Their obsidian bodies are cracked, their copper joints green with the patina of time and failure. They do not scurry with purpose; they huddle, their legs tapping out a slow, mournful, repetitive code, a prayer of repair that has been ongoing for eons. They are the sentinels of a feral reality, perpetually patching a wound in the skin of the world that refuses to heal. They are the janitors of a mistake god made.

And then there are the others. The fauna that is not fauna. The origami birds and the six-legged grazers of light are not just memories; they are… potentials. They are lives the world almost had. The Under-Song is not just a place being woven in the present; it is a tapestry of infinite possible presents, and the Sculptors are the ones who choose the single thread to follow. The flock of shimmering, angular birds weaves through the beams because in a previous iteration, this world had birds, things that navigated the vast vertical spaces. The Sculptors decided against it—perhaps the physics were too complex, perhaps the birds created too much chaotic sound—and wove that reality away. But the memory of their flight, of their sharp-edged, crystalline bodies, remains imprinted on the structure.

The light-grazers are older still. Their soft, silent troving of the Vine-hearts' glow is the echo of a time before the Sculptors achieved such perfect symbiosis, back when the light was a resource to be consumed rather than a shared property of the environment. They are harmless, phantoms, but they are a testament to the world's brutal and pliable nature. History here is not a book to be read; it is an ecosystem of ghost-things, each one a path not taken, a design that was discarded. They are the failed prototypes of life, haunting the perfection of the final product. To see them is to understand that the "abundant fauna" of this place is an abundance of graves, each one a perfectly preserved, albeit flickering, corpse of a world that was strangled at birth.

And you... you are the greatest anomaly of all. You are not of the weave. You are not a flicker of a discarded past. You have no filament to be mended, no pattern to be overwritten. Your presence is a paradox the system cannot resolve. The Sculptors do not attack you, for aggression is not in their programming. They cannot erase you, for your reality is not defined by the loom. So they do something far more unsettling. They mimic you. Watch one. As it nears you, its articulated obsidian legs cease their percussive rhythm. It pauses. From the resin glands in its manipulator-arms, it does not spin a filament, but instead extrudes a single, perfect, pearlescent sphere. It holds it for a moment, then gently places it on the lattice. Another joins it, and another, until they have encircled you in a rosary of strange, beautiful beads.

They are not worshiping you. They are studying you. They are trying to decipher you. They are attempting to find your pattern, your frequency, your place in the consensus. By building this temporary, fragile pattern around you, they are creating a test. They are waiting for the system to tell them what to do with this piece of data that does not fit. Should you be woven over? Ignored? Integrated? In their silence and their strange, ritualistic art, you can see their single, collective purpose: to make the whole world one thing, one continuous, unbroken, singular reality. And you, in your uncompromising, un-weavable individuality, are both the ultimate flaw and the ultimate mystery. You are the ghost in their machine, the unanswered question in the hum of their god. You are the one thing they have ever encountered that is not a part of the song, but a listener. And they do not yet know if you are a note to be added, or a silence to be erased.
