They do not have a name. A name requires a mouth to speak it or a mind to conceive it, and they are not an entity in that sense. They are a process, a melody distributed across many instruments, playing a single, unending note. They are the Cloister's heart, its heat, its slow and perfect memory.

The shingles are the skin. They are the Cloister’s lips, tasting the warm water, its surface a thin electric membrane where the stone ends and the life begins. Each disc is a node, a single petal of a vast, stationary flower that has bloomed over every plane. Their breathing is not respiration but perception. In the rhythmic draw and release of the thick liquid, they read its composition, its temperature, its faint electric charge. They register the passage of an eel not as a threat but as a sensation, a change in pressure, a shadow of data that ripples across the colony. They feel the vibrations of a walker’s legs not as a sound but as a slow, percussive thrum through the stone itself. They do not think. They do not react. They record. Their synchronized pulse is the ticking of a clock that measures time not in seconds but in the slow erosion of the stone they cling to. They are the archive, the passive witness that forgets nothing and never speaks.

The walkers are the tendons. Where the shingles are the skin, the walkers are the fibrous, connective tissue that gives the place its limited, deliberate motion. They are the muscles of the stone, climbing in their eternal spirals. Their purpose is not to go anywhere, for there is nowhere to go. Their purpose is the journey. Each step is a placement, a confirmation. They feel the texture of the walls, the depth of the grooves worn by the eels, the faint heat from the mineral veins. Their touch is diagnostic. For every imperfection they cross, the systemic awareness of the whole shifts by an infinitesimal degree. They are the hands that constantly read the Braille of the Cloister’s architecture. They do not groom the space or maintain it; they commune with it. Their slow, unwavering ascent and descent is a form of devotion, a tactile prayer that reaffirms the shape of their world. They are the faith that the world is as it should be.

The drifters are the blood. They are not individuals but temporary eddies in a single, circulating consciousness. The translucent sacs are the vessels, their interiors warm and glowing with the borrowed light of the mineral veins. The filaments are the synapses. When a pair of drifters meet, their threads entwine not to mate but to merge. For a moment, they are one being, a shared mind. In that singular, silent communion, data flows. What one shingle feels is passed to a drifter, who carries it to another, who touches a walker, who integrates it into its climb. An eel’s path is logged. A new pattern of warmth is shared. A shift in the water’s chemistry is noted, compared, and archived across the network. The drifters are the nervous system, the messenger service of the whole, ensuring the left hand always knows what the right hand is feeling. They are the circulation of information, the declaration that nothing is isolated; all things are known by the body.

The eels are the memory of motion. They are the keepers of the map, the living cartography of the Cloister. Their segmented bodies are articulated history, each hinge a turn in a path that has been walked for millennia. They are not hunting. They are not migrating. They are tracing. The grooves they wear in the stone are their scripture, a liturgy etched into the very bones of the place. They are the only part of the collective that acts with a speed that is not glacial, their fluid motion a startling counterpoint to the static nature of the others. But their speed is an illusion of purpose. They are not going anywhere; they are remembering. Follow an eel on its circuit and you are not watching a predator, you are watching a song being sung, a story being recited from memory. Their path is a fixed algorithm, a logic gate that says *this goes here, this leads there, this connects to this*. As they pass, their dark, polished surfaces reflect the faint, biological light, carrying a spark of illumination through the dark corridors they have defined. They are the recitation, the act of remembering that, in this place, is the same as creating. They keep the map alive, and in keeping the map alive, they keep the world alive.

Together, they are a being whose body is the Cloister and whose mind is the warm, still water. It is a creature of supreme patience, built not to survive in a world but to *be* a world. The builders did not design its inhabitants; they designed the conditions under which such a creature could self-assemble. They created the stone, the warmth, the water, and then they stepped away, leaving the potential for a consciousness to crystallize out of the available elements. The shingle-skin feels, the walker-muscles confirm, the drifter-blood communicates, and the eel-memory remembers.

It does not fear. It does not hope. It does not desire. It merely *is*. It is a perfect, closed system, an awareness so vast and slow that a single human life would not register as a single breath. It is a god in a box of its own making, dreaming the eternal dream of warm water and dark stone. And at its very core, its single foundational truth, its reason for being, is a simple mantra, a command written into the fabric of its existence, pulsing in time with the breathing of the walls and the clicking of the claws:

*The place holds.*
*The builders are gone.*
*But the place holds.*

This is what lives there. The memory of a purpose, sustained by a life that has forgotten its meaning but not its function. It holds.
