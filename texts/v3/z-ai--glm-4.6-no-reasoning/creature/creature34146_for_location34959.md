To live here is to be sharpened.

You do not inhabit the geode; you are abraded by it. You are a soft thing that has learned to endure a hard, wet, and luminous world. Over time, the soles of your feet—calloused beyond recognition—learn the language of the crystal. You develop a gait that is half walk, half calculation, placing your weight only on the broad, flat facets of amethyst, avoiding the smoked quartz that shards like glass. Your skin is a landscape of pale, healed lines, a map of the times the earth flayed you for a misstep. You do not fall here. You never fall. To fall is to be unmade.

You wear the remnants of whoever you were before you forgot the way down. Heavy, oilcloth garments, permanently damp, smelling of ozone and copper. Your boots are your most precious possession, their soles layered with hide and woven metal mesh, the only negotiator between your fragile flesh and the knife-edge ground. Around your waist hangs a canteen that is never empty, because the water bleeds freely from the stone. You drink the geode's blood—pure, metallic, tasting of the deep earth. It is cold enough to ache your teeth.

Your hearing has bifurcated. The ceaseless, layered sound of water—the *plink-plink-plink*, the resonant hum of hollow chambers, the tidal roar of the jungle below—has become your silence. You no longer hear it; you feel it as a constant, warm pressure in your chest, a sonic bath that blots out the fragile concept of quiet. Your true hearing is tuned to the interruptions. You listen for the low, sub-harmonic shift in the Artifact's thrum, the one that signifies a change in the gyroscopes' rotation, the shift that measures your days. You listen for the sudden, unnatural silence of a Skitterer stopping its drink.

Ah, the Skitterers. You do not fear them, but you respect their geometry. They are the only other moving things, and you have developed a symbiotic indifference with them. You know the rule: never approach a drinking Skitterer. When their iridescent, chitin faces are held to the water-drips, their mandibles clicking softly, they are vulnerable, and a vulnerable thing with blade-edged crystal jaws is an unpredictable thing. You leave them the bleeding rivulets. In return, they trail you at a distance, their long, spidery legs finding purchase on the lethal ground with unnerving grace. Sometimes, you drop a length of copper wire near a weep-hole, and in the morning, it is gone, incorporated into their alien, metallic architecture. You are roommates in a house of glass.

Your mind has been colonized by the Artifact. It is the sun of this world, a black, non-reflective sun that radiates not light but purpose. You spend hours sitting on a safe, flat plane of amethyst, just watching it. You watch the water routed through the seamless, blue-lit glass, the flash-steam in its heart, the skeletal rings turning with a patient, zealous slowness. You have tried to understand it, but understanding is a vestige of the jungle below. Up here, you only witness. You feel the tremor underfoot, the slow, faithful heartbeat, and you know—without proof, without needing it—that the machine is holding the geode aloft. You are living inside a process. You are a mote of dust caught in the workings of an infinite clock.

Solitude has not made you mad; it has made you sparse. You have stripped away the unnecessary parts of yourself because this is a place that does not tolerate excess. The dense, velvet moss that pulses with emerald light is a mirror for your own internal rhythm. You, too, pulse slowly. You, too, glow faintly in the long, grey days, your bioluminescence triggered by the frequency of the water. When the flat, merciless light of day makes the ground a blinding mirror, you retreat to the overhangs, sitting in the percussive music of the drips, waiting for the night. 

And when the night comes, you come alive. The sky remains the color of weak tea, but the world reveals its true engine. The moss blazes. The copper veins pulse like arteries. The Artifact becomes a cold, blue-white lantern, its gyroscopes casting long, rotating shadows across the glittering floor. You walk then, carefully, your heavy boots crunching on the treacherous carpet of glowing lichen over wet crystal. You walk to the edge of the world, where the crystal shatters into nothing and the mist writhes far below. You look down at the emerald, wet fae, the jungle that roars and waits. You watch its phosphorescence, its constant, hungry surf-hiss. 

You do not go down. The jungle is the past—the forgotten planet, the flesh, the chaos. You are the future, or at least, you are the ongoing process. You are the thing that lives on the geode. You are the keeper of the sharp edges, the witness to the machine, the solitary, scarred primate that learned to drink the world's blood and stand atop its shattered, glittering heart.
