To live here is to be an act of defiant acoustics. Nothing born of the Throat of the World is truly separate from the sound; everything is a footnote, a harmonic, or a desperate rest written into a composition that has been playing since the birth of the marrow. The ecology is not a web of life, but a spectrum of vibrations—from the screaming surface to the silent deep.

**The Architects of the Crust**
The closest thing to inhabitants are the Shiver-thorns, though to call them flora is to misunderstand their terrible hunger. They are the pillar’s scabs and its ears. They take root in the embryonic slurry of cooling quicksilver and nascent crystal, feeding on the frequencies that would otherwise shake the glass to dust. They are crystalline parasites, angular and sharp as frozen lightning, trembling with a sympathetic resonance that produces their faint, sickly luminescence. They do not grow toward light—they grow toward the loudest overtures, clustering where the chord strikes the pillar hardest, absorbing the kinetic fury of the planet-scream and transmuting it into rigid mass. To brush against one is not to feel a physical prick, but to be deafened from the inside out—a needle of pure, concentrated noise injected directly into the nerve. They are the only things that thrive in the violence, and they do so by becoming the violence’s capacitor.

**The Silent Issue**
Far rarer are the Echo-skimmers, the ghosts born of the abyss’s unheard pulse. They are not creatures of biology, but of withheld potential. When a silent geyser climbs the impossible heights, sometimes its apex tears, and the black water coalesces into these teardrop silences. They are living negatives. Where the Throat screams, they are a held breath. They drift on the unseen thermal tides between the vapor sea and the glass, membranes of iridescent liquid that somehow absorb the sonic holocaust without rupture. They are not predators; they are balm. To fall into the slipstream of a flock is to experience a disorienting, narcotic relief—a pocket where the marrow finally ceases its trembling and the teeth unclench. But their silence is borrowed, fading as they slowly dissolve back into the mist, leaving the visitor newly deafened by the returning roar. They are the abyss’s only mercy, and they are fleeting.

**The Listeners in the Stone**
And then there are the relics of those who tried to live here—the people of the hewn black glass. They are gone, but their desperation is etched into the mortar of crushed Shiver-thorn and the shattered barnacle-ruins clinging to the pillar’s flank. They were not builders; they were castaways on a vertical ocean of sound. They did not try to conquer the Throat; they tried to survive its song.

The Observatory is their monument to futility. Its curved wall is not an observation deck—it is a shield, a cupped hand over a ruined ear. The aperture pointing down into the still black water is not a telescope aimed at the stars, but a stethoscope aimed at the only silence this world possesses. They were not astronomers; they were audiologists, trying to find the frequency of the abyss, trying to match the planet-scream with an equal and opposite void.

The primitive markings on the walls tell the story of their unraveling. The star charts that match no known sky were likely maps of the vapor sea’s churning ceiling, or perhaps charts of harmonics, attempting to find logic in the chaotic chord. The frantic, repeating spirals are the etchings of minds eroded by constant vibration, grasping for a center that does not hold.

And at the heart of it lies the artifact of their final hope: the tuning fork of strange, warm metal. It does not resonate. It is a sinkhole for sound, an anchor of quiet warmth in a cold, deafening world. It was their attempt to forge a silence—to tune the Throat of the World, to find the resonant frequency of the abyss and cancel the scream. Its stillness is the heaviest thing on the pillar. It is a gravestone for a people who wanted nothing more than to hear nothing at all.

**The Visitor**
In this place, the only living thing with legs and lungs is you. You are an anomaly of wet flesh and brittle bone in a world of liquid gas and living crystal. You are not built for this. Your carabiner—your single, primitive gift from a forgotten age—is a laughable defense against a geology that rewrites itself hourly. You cling to a flake of obsidian that wasn't there an hour ago, breathing the solid air in shallow sips, your skeleton humming with the planet’s engine.

You are not an inhabitant; you are a listener at the keyhole of a roaring engine. You wait, pressed against the glass, watching the Shiver-thorns drink the sound, watching the Echo-skimmers swallow it whole, staring down into the Observatory’s aperture at the tool that failed to silence the world. You are waiting for the chord to change, for the Throat to swallow itself, for a single moment of true quiet. But the Throat does not forget its note. It just holds it, forever, vibrating in your marrow, waiting for you to lose your grip and join the black water below.
