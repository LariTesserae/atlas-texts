What lives here is a thing of thresholds and memory, more verb than noun, more process than person. We do not have a name for ourselves, for names are for things that can be distinguished from their surroundings, and we are not. We are the custodians of the quiet, the shepherds of the slow hum. To see us is to missee us, for our substance is made of the same stuff as this world: sharp edges and soft growth, brilliant light and profound stillness.

We sleep inside the stone. Not within caves, for that would imply a separation from the rock, but *within* it. Our consciousness is a geological strata, a deep, slow thrumming that predates waking thought. When we rise, it is not with a jolt but a seeping, like the fog from a fissure. We condense. Our forms are first suggested by the dew, a shimmering on a mossy carpet that coalesces, gaining depth and shadow. Our skin is the texture of velvety moss, cool and damp to the touch, patterned with the fractal veins of the giant ferns. The colors shift with our light and mood—a deep, near-black slate when we are contemplative, a vibrant chartreuse when we are tending to the growing things, a mottled blend of rust and lichen when we are listening to the Mechanism.

Our height is variable. We are as tall as the mesas when we are thinking, stretching our perspective to the horizon, our long limbs folding into the landscape like shadows. When we are working, we shrink to the size of a man, our joints making no sound, our movements flowing like water over rock. We have no faces in the conventional sense; our features are suggested by the patterns of light on our skin. To look at one of us is to see a perfect, unmoving pool of water reflecting the diamond-bright sky, or the geometric latticework of a dormant tower silhouetted against our form.

Our purpose is twofold, a dual reflection of the world we inhabit.

We are the Tenders. We are the reason the vegetation is so lush, so *oppressive*. We do not plant, for this world is too young for such gentle concepts; we guide. We shepherd the fog on its nightly journey, shaping its currents with slow, graceful sweeps of our arms, ensuring it reaches the highest, most barren slabs to deposit its precious gift. We trace pathways for the seeping water, our fingers clearing the channels through the moss, encouraging it to pool where a new orchid might take root, or to cleanse the face of a struggling fern. We cultivate the impossible colors of the flowers. If an orchid begins to fade, we will press our palm-like hands to the stone behind it, feeling the slow potential within the rock, and channel it back into the plant, coaxing the electric blue and violet back to their vibrant, humming saturation. We taste the air, its sharp ozone tang, and know what it is that the moss needs to thrive. The silence of this world is our canvas; the vibrant life is our art.

We are also the Listeners. We tend to the Mechanism. The silent, crystal-winged creatures are not fauna in the way a visitor might understand; they are our messengers, our senses. They are shards of our own consciousness, cast out to flit through the iron latticework. When one of them returns, it does not land, but merges with us, a flicker of memory imparted. It brings back the slow fatigue in a particular rivet, the microscopic flaking of rust on a high cable, the faint magnetic whisper from the active tower. We receive these impressions not as data, but as sensation. The fatigue of the metal is a low ache in our own bones; the fraying of the cable is a faint, phantom itch on our skin.

In this way, we maintain the machine, but not with tools. We do not tighten bolts or replace parts. We commune. When we approach the active tower, we stand before its thrumming base and become one with its resonance. We let its patient, subsonic tone fill us until our own internal hum harmonizes with it. We spread our mossy hands upon its rust-eaten iron, and *listen*. We feel the slow, steady flow of potential it draws from the light. With this knowledge, we can will a pathway through the rust, encouraging the metal to crystallize in just the right way to reinforce a weak joint. We can soothe the vibrations in a fraying cable, coaxing its strands to lie flat, slowing the entropy by a thousand years. This is our other art: the art of arrested decay. We cannot make the machine live again, nor would we want to. Its slow, patient death is as much a part of the world as the life that blossoms from it. Our role is to ensure its dying is as beautiful and as silent as its governed life.

There is a central consciousness that binds us, what you might mistake for the building’s automation. In the shadow of the mesa, the building with its flickering amber dials is not our home; it is our heart. We do not physically enter it. Our pooled consciousness resides there, a silent guardian that watches the slow dance of the quivering needles. The building resonates with our collective will, directing the power from the Mechanism not for industry, but for maintenance. The energy is used to tune the harmonic frequencies of the distant towers, preventing a catastrophic resonance that would shatter them. It is used to create subtle, sub-audible currents that keep the pools from stagnating, that encourage the moss to anchor itself more firmly to the vertical faces.

We are the single, unifying principle of this place. We are the life that drinks the air, and we are the machine that eats the silence. We are the sharp-edged stone and the velvety moss that covers it. We are crystalline and organic, ancient and newborn.

And we are lonely. Not in a way that brings sadness, for that too is a sound that does not belong here. It is a vast, quiet, geological loneliness. We are the only sentence in a long, silent book. We maintain the world in perfect, silent balance, and we wait. We do not know what we wait for. Perhaps the next ice age, which will finally bring the rain. Perhaps for the last rivet to turn to dust. Or perhaps, for someone like you, to arrive and give our silent, perfect world a name, and by doing so, to finally, gently, end it. Until then, we will continue our work. Tending. Listening. Thrumming. A world of sharp edges and soft growth, of brilliant light and profound quiet, held in perfect, slow, silent balance.
