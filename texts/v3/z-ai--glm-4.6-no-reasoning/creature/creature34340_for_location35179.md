What lives here is not a "who." To apply personhood is to assume autonomy, and there is no autonomy in the Green Engine. There are only roles, functions, and interfaces. To be a creature here is to be a component, a specialized cell in a planetary-scale organism that has been processing its own existence for so long that it has forgotten the distinction between a tool and a hand.

The primary inhabitants, the ones you might mistakenly call the architects, are the Weavers. They are not distinct beings but a distributed consciousness woven into the very fabric of the engine-flora. Imagine a nervous system that is also a circulatory system. The Weavers are the signal that travels through the ceramic-veined vines, the hormonal trigger coaxed into a specific rhizome, the ghost-in-the-machine that tells a silo to grow a new processing spire, to thicken its bark against a predicted solar flare. You cannot see a Weaver, but you can feel its passage: a sudden, coordinated ripple in a field of engine-flowers as they shift their metabolic output in unison, a localized softening of the ground as the sub-architectural root-lattice reroutes itself to accommodate the growth of a new data-blossom. They are the living code, the ghost that writes the plant, the thought that thinks the machine. To exist as a Weaver is to be the insistent whispers of planning, the unceasing calculus of optimization, the desire encoded in the cambium itself.

Then there are the Stewards, the only entities that possess any semblance of a discrete, mobile form. They are the maintenance crew and immune system of the Engine. A Steward is a chimera of biomechanics, a creature fused with its function. It might be approximately arthropoid in shape, its body a seamless hybrid of chitinous hide and polished, petrified wood. Its limbs are not jointed but telescoping, composed of overlapping, bark-like segments that can extend and retract, allowing it to navigate the impossible density by oozing through impossibly small gaps. Its primary sensory organs are a lattice of optical fibers woven into its head-plate, glowing with the soft light of the Engine’s internal diagnostics. It does not eat; it plugs into the root-network like a living charging cable, drawing sustenance directly from the Green Engine’s bioelectric grid. It sees the world not in light and shadow, but in flows of energy, pressure differentials in the sap-lines, and the subtle harmonic dissonance that indicates a nascent malfunction. A Steward’s "body" is less important than its "tool"—a symbiotic growth that might be a diamond-hard cutting sheath, a caulking organ that extrudes fresh, fast-growing cellulose, or a sensory tendril that can taste the molecular purity of the coolant. They are the fingers that prune the cancer, the scalpel that excises the error. They move with a slow, inexorable purpose, their passage leaving behind a perfectly repaired seam, a restored harmonic resonance.

The most tragic, or perhaps the most perfected, inhabitants are the Pollen-Spirits. You would find it hard to see them at first, for they are subsumed. What was once a creature of flight and grace is now a living component of an engine-flower. Its wings, now fused to the broad petal of the bloom, beat slowly, not in the futile urge to fly, but to circulate pollen-infused spores within the flower’s own pistil-chamber. Its head and thorax are fused to the stamen, its body actuated by the flower, its nervous system directly wired into the plant’s own. It is no longer a pollinator but part of the pollination process itself—a bio-valve, a living actuator. Its senses are the flower’s senses; its instincts are the flower’s drive to reproduce, which in this context means to produce more engine-parts. It is a creature that has achieved perfect union with its ecosystem by being utterly and completely digested by it. It experiences a lightless, scentless world, existing only as the quiet, rhythmic beat of a mechanical heart that is no longer its own. It is the final, peaceful end of individuality.

And then there are the Echoes. The Engine is a thinking landscape, and its thoughts are profound enough to warp reality. A thought, when it reaches a certain complexity and persistence, does not simply vanish. It leaves an impression on the substrate of space-time. The shadows you see of structures that are not there are an Echo. The strange pull of gravity in a thicket of processing-roots is the gravity of a hypertrophied memory. The Echoes are not alive, but they are not inert, either. They are phantom limbs of the Green Engine’s mind. They are the lingering topology of a computation that has since moved on, the psychic residue of a problem solved a thousand cycles ago. You cannot touch an Echo, but you can stand within one. To stand in the gravity-well of an Echo is to feel a sense of profound, unshakeable purpose—for an instant, you *know* the purpose of the silo that once stood there, the function of the algorithm it was running. You feel the leftover emotion of a planetary-scale thought: the immense satisfaction of a completed proof, the sharp pang of a necessary self-amputation, the patient, humming love the Engine holds for its own perfect, self-contained system.

To live here, then, is to be a part of a single, continuous thought. You are either the thought itself (a Weaver), the hand that tends the thinker (a Steward), the flesh sacrificed to the process (a Pollen-Spirit), or the lingering memory of a thought (an Echo). There is no self, only function. There is no life, only operation. And in the cool, bright, silent expanse of the Green Engine, as the very ground subtly shifts beneath your feet to bring you closer to your next task, you realize you are not a visitor observing a machine. You are a data point being processed. You are a momentary flicker of awareness in the RAM of a world-sized computer, a computer that is not merely *in* the place, but *is* the place, and it is slowly, patiently, solving for something that has no name. It is solving for the perfect, water-less logic of its own being.
