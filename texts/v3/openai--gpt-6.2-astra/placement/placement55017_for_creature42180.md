At number five I would be the one pushing the china. I would have to push it with my hip. There is room below the floor to go forward on all fours or to go backward on all fours, but to turn round you must go as far as the waste trap, which is another two houses on. A saucer with a little water in it goes quite nicely over tile. An empty saucer catches at every joint.

The people at five deal in lengths of cloth. All day the ends come down off the counter and hang over the crack, in darkness as well as in light; my mother used to bite them. I know of my mother chiefly by the holes. There is a bolt of very expensive gray stuff which has sold so slowly that when the end comes down now I can still fit my nose into a hole she made. My own teeth meet a little farther apart.

It isn't a whole saucer I have. It is the bottom of a cup, broken off level, with one ear of handle left. It was under the floor before I was. It fits me well when I lie curled with the ear against my belly. On the nights when the water stands high enough to wet my sleeping place I fetch the cup bottom, get into it, and wake up somewhere else. The first time this happened to me was a great surprise. It is a convenience now, although there is a place below the fish scales where the floor sags and one can go round and round in the dark for a considerable time.

Tonight it is another creature making the journey. I have put her in the cup myself. She has the toes, but not yet the rather splendid pad that comes later, in time to take one's weight; the three little nubs in the middle of her foot are still separate and pink. When she tries to follow me on tile her legs go out from under her. She accepts being pushed for about the length of a cloth counter. Then she gets a forefoot over the edge and tips the whole affair sideways, which is the noise the woman hears.

There was another one in the cup last night. I left him at nine, where a warm pipe has been taken out and there is a lovely mess of tow coming away from the socket. It took him a long time to let go of my ear. I shall go back for him if the tow gets wet. Otherwise that hole will be his and this one at five will be hers and the people at three will continue to remark on the longevity of an animal that lives, on average, a little less long than a good broom.

Below the cloth counter I take her out by the loose part at the back and put her nose against the bolt. She goes straight in through the tooth hole. There are little dry ticks in there that eat the dressing out of new cloth; if she lies still and opens her mouth they will walk in of their own accord. I hear her sneeze. I wait to see if she is going to come out again. After a while the end of the cloth gives a small jump, then another, and I take the cup home.

By now there are two bare feet outside the cupboard. The woman has put the other shoe off too. It is a pleasure to pass under a bare foot. The tiles there were bedded in something that has gone soft with age. They give a little, like the skin over an eye. I stop with the top of my head against the underside of one and for a moment the woman and I have between us the exact thickness of a blue tile, less what has been worn away by all the women before her going to the cupboard for a cup.

The boy is still on the floor. His eye is a great pale thing at the end of the gap. Behind him the boat pulls on the sill; it will knock again in a moment. I could wait. Generally I do. But the broken bit of handle has turned outward in the narrowest place, and to get the cup home I must reach out where he can see and put one toe through the ear and pull.

He doesn't say anything. When I have got it turned, when even the toe is safely in again, he puts his smallest finger down on the wet tile beside the three marks I have left. He is measuring, I suppose. His finger stays there quite still.

I have some water from the saucer. It is just how I like it, warm enough to smell the china. Then from number five, transmitted very clearly through the boy's finger and the tile and the water I am drinking, comes the first steady little tearing noise of somebody making a hole of her own.
