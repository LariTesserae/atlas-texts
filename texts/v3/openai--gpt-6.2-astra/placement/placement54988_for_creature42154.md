I think I would be on the other side of the plaster, though not in the plaster itself. There is a little room at the foot of the farther survey stair where they used to keep the orange flags for stopping traffic. A flag lacks something as a means of stopping a cart which is already thirty miles away. They have gone over to a system of bells. The room is mine for keeping the stair swept, a good arrangement except on the morning after rain, when everybody goes up for the pleasure of it and comes down with an extra sole of lime on each shoe.

The shell is in the floor. I found it by putting the short leg of my table on it. At noon the sugar spoon began to travel round the rim of the bowl. It took me several days to understand why it never got all the way round.

I used to play second horn in a district band. Second horn suits a person who likes to come in when something is already going on. There was one piece in which I had to start alone. The conductor got into the habit of tapping his stand twice before he lifted his arms, once for the company at large and once, very softly, for me. Nobody who has not had a whole band waiting on the other side of his teeth knows the kindness of that second tap.

My horn went in payment for some work on a tooth. I can still make most of the notes with my lips against the joint of my thumb. That was what the mistress heard. I came in early because the boy was slowing down to take the difficult step, and late on the way back because I slowed down to take it too. I had forgotten how much of playing in company consists of being wrong about what the other fellow is going to do.

I moved the table. At first I tried to give him the note he wanted, clean and square so that he could lay his own alongside it. It was rather high for me. I got down on my hands and knees, which did nothing for my breath, and produced a noise very like a wet finger coming out of a bottle. There was a silence at the school end. Then he did it too. His was an excellent likeness. We spent what remained of that interval taking fingers out of bottles of different sizes, and when the first load went through I had to sit on the floor awhile with my back against the cupboard. It is hard work laughing on one's hands and knees.

I have not been over to see him. It takes less time to get there than it does to get my good coat off the peg, but there would be the business of coming into a yard full of children and asking which one of them had been talking to the floor. Besides, I am not sure he thinks of me as a person. It leaves him free to try things he might not care to try on a person. One noon he spent on a single low note, letting the air out of it by degrees until what came through was the small comfortable draught one feels at the keyhole of a busy kitchen. I put my cheek down to it. Nobody had ever played me anything of that kind in the band.

Yesterday he got the seventh right. He was in such a hurry to tell me that he played it again and got it wrong, but by then of course it was a note on which either thing could happen. I tapped twice with the spoon. After a moment two taps came back.

This morning I have taken a little lime from round the shell with the point of the flagpole. More of the fluting shows now. It is a handsome thing, cream with a narrow brown line which goes in and out of every fold without gaining any ground. I swept out the grooves and brought a cushion down from the bed. The stair overhead is full of people; grit is sifting through onto my clean floor. In a minute the bells will stop. I have a fancy to start us off today, if I can manage it.
