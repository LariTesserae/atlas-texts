I would be the one turning over the crumbs. From where I stand, there is no felt. There are wine-colored uprights, some far enough apart for me to go between without folding my legs, some so close that a crumb caught between them will stay there through the turn of the tide. Those are the ones I try first. I have two little pairs of hands under my chin. If a crumb is too firmly lodged to pull free, I can stand underneath and eat a hollow in it.

I feel the octopus set down seven of her arms, though I would not know there were seven. It amounts to a brief drawing together of the uprights, like the beginning of the ebb, except that the pull comes from all around instead of from the sea. I go on eating. The eighth arm comes down through a space my back just fits. A sucker touches the plate behind my head.

It comes away with the plate. The rest of me comes slipping out almost more easily than I would like. For some time there has been a second suit folded inside the one I walk about in, down to the last joint of the small hands. I had come in among the closer stems on that account. I thought I had longer.

For a little while the octopus has a remarkably good likeness of me. There are even hairs on the feet. She brings it up out of the felt and passes it along under her arms, and the feet, which hang over either side, go walking one after another as the suckers take hold and let go. By then I am down where the weed fastens to a chip of limestone. My new hands are of no use even for holding on. I have to put my whole side against the stone and let a strand press me there.

It is from here that I would come to know the town. Not the extent of it; I would never get an idea of two ways meeting, or know that the water going by at my back had been as far as the square. But for part of each tide the weed lies over inland and the tips comb a bank of shells. For another part it leans out over water too deep for its tips to touch anything. The difference is considerable down at the foot. When the tips catch, the stem that holds me lifts a little off the chip. Things can get in underneath. A grain rolls through, then a length of brown stuff as soft as the inside of a crumb. I have only to wait for my hands to harden.

Above me the octopus lets the suit go. It travels scarcely the length of her arm before it hangs up by one foot. Later something small enough to enter at the split behind the head will find the plates still joined and a little water passing in and out at the wrists. But just now it is empty. The young octopus is coming down again, this time entire, narrowing herself to pass between the uprights that seemed close even to me.

There is a groove in the limestone, packed with the fine white grit from farther in. I can get into it on my side. One new hand takes my weight, then the other. Behind me the tip of an arm goes feeling over the place where I lay soft against the chip. Ahead, under a low fork in the weed, the groove widens by about the breadth of a foot. I could turn around there. I go a little farther in before I do.
