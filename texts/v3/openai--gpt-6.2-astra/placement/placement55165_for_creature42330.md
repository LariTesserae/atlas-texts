I would have to get down on my stomach to find myself. Even then I would be looking a little too high. At the blunt end of the middle ridge, just where it loses the height of its last plate, there is a shelf with nearly enough clearance under it for a sleeper to turn over. A big one has to go out and back in the other way. On the ceiling of this shelf are what look like the prints of very small feet, a left and a right, then another left and right, all the way back into the dark. I think at first I would be the thing that had made the prints. In fact I would be all four dozen feet.

I would have a body like the selvage of a leaf, a narrow tough band with a little give to it. My feet would be pale at their tips where they held to the stone. My head, at the outer edge of the shelf, would be no broader than the band, but it would have two long feelers growing from the lower lip, each with a crook at the end. Hanging there I could put a crook around a single hair on a sleeper's back, in one of the channels between its shields, and feel the hair move loosely inside it as the animal went by. Most of what went on below me I would let go by like that.

There is a particular difficulty a sleeper has in getting out of a tight place. Its shields slide nicely over one another going forward. Backing up, especially if it is young and has put on two flakes when one would have done, it is apt to get a corner caught. It can get free easily enough by going flat and drawing its head in. What it cannot easily do is go flat while it is trying with all six feet to go backward.

I would take the backward part. For this I should want my feet on good stone, not a flake ready to come off, and both feelers down in the channel behind the sleeper's head. For a moment my whole length would shorten. A person watching the shelf from the side might see a little crinkle run along its underside, about as much motion as there is in the edge of a mouth when someone asleep has been offered something sour. Underneath, the sleeper would have ceased to scrabble. The effort it was making would still be going on, with a perfectly satisfactory purchase for every foot, up where I was; down where it was, it could attend to its shoulder. There would be a little gritty slip against my lip. Then I would have to let go in good time or be pulled off my shelf.

I don't think I would do this out of kindness. The sleeper's effort would be the way I drew my rear feet up. By myself I could always reach a little farther with the front ones; there would be no end to the length I could grow to, and very little I could do with the last part of me, far back in the stale crack. A good strong pull from below would bring that part along. In the course of the basin's afternoon I might have advanced the length of the shelf. There would be a bare place behind me on the roof, rosy cream, with little paired dull places in it where my feet had been. So there would be prints after all.

Once well out on the edge I would have a choice of two ways to go. Toward the thicket the stone would be full of minute tremors, some of them the work of sleepers a long way off; toward the level it would be almost still. I think I would put my head out on the still side. There would be a considerable surprise in store for a creature that had always had the whole weight of a ridge on the other side of its soles. The last inch of the shelf would sound different under my feet. I could tap it and feel the tap come back from the free edge. And over that edge, where I had expected the stone simply to give out, there would turn out to be another face of it, and then a sun-warm upper face, quite rough, with room to put a foot down.

I would stop with just the first pair over. My feelers are made to go around a hair; there is nothing up there fine enough to take hold of. But one crook would cast its shadow inside the other, the small dark hook lying very distinctly on the pale living one. When I moved them apart it would slip off onto the stone. I would spend a fair while at that, with most of me still hanging in the cool.
