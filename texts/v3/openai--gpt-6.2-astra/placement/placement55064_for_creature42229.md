I would be on the inside of the watch glass, a little below the worm, at a place the snail has to go round. From the ladder I would look like a fault in the glass. There would be a round spot at which the view of the shoal was quite good, and another spot, a little to one side of it, at which the same piece of shoal could be seen a second time, with all the grains considerably larger.

The first spot is my foot. The other is the end of a soft stalk. I am folded up against it while the delivery lasts. It takes the whole push of the water to flatten me properly; toward the end there is usually a crinkle left standing up, like the corner of a wet label that won't quite stick. A shrimp once took hold of this and pulled. For several days afterward I opened a little crookedly, and got a much better living than usual out of the water that slips down the side of the worm.

When the leaves stand up I stand up too. What looked like a single piece of clear stuff separates into a number of fine hairs, joined by a film for about half their length. I put these out in a shallow cup. The water on its way through brings me things too small for the worm to trouble with. It brings me things too big, as well. A porcelain grain will lodge in the cup and draw all the hairs together under its weight. I tip down on the stalk until it rolls out. There is a little heap below me which for a time was very valuable to one of the digging pairs. They had only to wait with the little one uppermost and presently a stone of about the right size would come down between its feet.

I should have thought it was a very still occupation. But the foot lets go at the back and takes hold at the front. In the course of one blue turn of the worm's wall I have gone the width of the wall itself. The heap is no longer under me. The small animal still gets up on the big one there sometimes and feels about overhead.

What I know best is the difference between a shadow and a touch. There is hardly anything here that makes one without presently giving you the other. The flat fish blots out a great deal of green light and then there is a stroke of water off its tail. Before the snail arrives there is a dark tentacle laid out on the glass, with a pale interval between it and the tentacle itself; the interval gets narrower until the two come together on my foot. But sometimes in the afternoon a shadow comes up from underneath. It covers the foot first. Then it goes right up the stalk to the open ends of the hairs. I have shut for it many times. Nothing follows. Lately I have been able to stay open in it, even when it moves.

Tonight the new shrimp came quite close to me on its way up. It was swimming with such force that its head went backward a little at every stroke. There was a long fine something still attached to one of its legs. This caught across my cup. For a while I had the whole weight of the shrimp, which was scarcely more than that of a grain, but with quick separate pulls in it such as no grain ever gave. The stalk bent. The shrimp went on opening and shutting its fans in the same place, with the light coming and going between their sticks. Then the fine thing slipped off the leg and I tipped it out. It lies on the old heap. In the morning the little digger will have quite a job fitting it in anywhere.

There is a cheek against the rung still. The worm has long since gone down. In the window, just where the blue goes out of sight around the tube, the person on the ladder can see a small clear flower held open against the dark of their own eye. It is a good place to look at me. Almost everywhere else there is too much light on the glass.
