I would be well down the eastern slope, where the blue from the cut is already ground so fine that a person can put a thumb in it and come away with a thumb apparently quite clean. The blue shows up later in the whorls, when the hand is dry. There is enough of it down there to keep me for most of a winter.

I have six legs at the front and a pair of hooks at the back, and in between a good deal of rather tender person to take care of. I am a caddis larva. My house is open at both ends. The narrow end is for water and the wide end is for water and me. When the water is sluggish I have to do some of the work of passing it through, by making a ripple with my belly. From outside this looks like a piece of the floor going quietly about its business with a draught through it.

I started out near the grass, in a house made chiefly of brown vegetable matter. There is still a little of this at the back. At the front I have added a collar of blue grains, laid in silk, and in a week or so the brown end will be too narrow to be much use and I shall bite it off. A fitter who has found several of these little brown sleeves in the strainer has been looking for the place on the new pump where the packing is coming out. She is quite right about their having once fitted something.

There are various ways to get about. One is to put all six feet out and walk, which produces, over a shift, a journey of some inches. Another is to be on a particular panel when the sluice goes up. I have crossed in this fashion a space on which it takes a tram an appreciable time to dwindle. I cannot recommend it. For a long while afterwards I went on walking inside my house without having the courage to put my head out.

The best station is a little way below the bolt. Water remains there after the rest of the sheet has gone. A greenish meal grows on the tile, so thin that my track through it is considerably easier to see than the meal itself. The man who cleans the gauze over the intake noticed these tracks before he noticed us. They were a mystery particularly on account of the corners. A track would proceed along one face of a flake of tile, turn its very sharp edge and continue on the underside, where it was hard to suppose that anybody had had business enough to go. In fact the meal is rather better there.

I feel the plover first through the rim of the house. A number of the neighbours then do a foolish thing and move. I draw in my legs and stay where I am. Once one of the young ones picked me up nevertheless. For a moment I had a view through the narrow end of a lit clock, an object I had not previously seen, nor am I at all likely to see it again. The chick put me down with some force. A chip of tile is a chip of tile even when the evidence for its having walked is fairly good.

At present I am building less and less. I have found a place under the lip of the cut, facing into a small dependable current, and have begun to fasten the house down. Eventually I shall go out of it altogether. There will be a winged thing in the space between the double doors, resting on the underside of a geranium leaf with its feelers laid out ahead of it. If both doors open in the right order it may get as far as the river itself.

But for a while yet, if somebody on the ridge has the glasses and knows which bolt, there is a little blue tube to be seen below it, pointing upstream. Beside it there is another, and beyond that several more. The chipped place on the riverbed has been going downstream for some weeks now, mostly under its own power.
