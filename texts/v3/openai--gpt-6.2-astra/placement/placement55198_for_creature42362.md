I would come in at the mouth, I think. There is the moment when the grass lifts and there ought to be a creature that lives for that view. I stay there through several liftings before I notice that the spider, which has the best view of all, is busy drawing one of its hind feet through its mouth. Farther in, the two lines the cricket made end at a ridge of dust. Something has been along the ridge since then and pushed a little fan of it down into one of the lines. It is easier to go in than to go away.

I find myself under the last shingle before the turn. I am a worker, one of the lighter ones, though my head has begun to darken. There is a nick in the edge of the chaff above me. I can put a feeler through it and touch the feet of somebody going over. This morning a husk caught there and for a while everyone going over put a foot through in the same place. I kept touching it. It was a different foot every time.

I have hold of the awn. The seed is gone. There is still a little bit of pale pith on the thick end, enough to get my jaws into. Pulled one way the awn slides quite readily; pulled the other it fetches up on everything it touches. I get it as far as the lip of the refuse tip, where the thin end goes over and the rest sticks out across the street. A worker coming up from below walks some way along it before her weight tips the whole thing down. We go down together. It is a short fall into husks, very loud, and for a while something smooth is pushing up against me from underneath. I climb off it and it runs away.

Here there is a split seed with a little flour still in it. My head fits in the split. I am busy with the flour when the goat steps on the roof.

It is quite different down here. The husks jump, all separately; one edge of the split nips my head and lets it go. Then there is that stillness, in which I can feel how much of the seed is resting on me. The first thing to move is a beetle. It comes out of the hole the awn made, goes a little way up the slope with flour on its back, and disappears into another hole precisely its own size. By the time I get my head free there are feet going by overhead again.

I take the piece with the flour on it. It is not much bigger than my head and I go forwards, right up to the corner where the stone is so smooth. There I meet the worker with the short leg. She has come out backwards from the store and has a seed held up off the ground, her head bent well under her to keep the end of it clear of the roof. I stand in the little space left by her missing foot. The seed goes by above me. For that much of the way we fit the passage exactly.

It will be warm on the shelf a while yet. I get down there with the flour and work at it beside a large larva whose mouth is very small and set rather far under. At first all I touch is the yielding curve of its side. Then it bends to me. A little of the flour comes away between us. I have not been out on the creeper today, or as far as the stripe of sun. The larva straightens and I go round to find its mouth again.
