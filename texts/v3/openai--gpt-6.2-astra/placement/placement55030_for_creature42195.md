I would be under the old dish on the lowest of the laid stones. This is not quite the same as being under its belly. There is room between the two white lips for a grazer to sleep, and room under a sleeping grazer for me. Of the two of us I am the one that can leave without waking the other.

I am rather like a shaving taken from the edge of a hoof. Laid flat on the stone I am difficult to pick up. I have feet on one side only. This would be a poor arrangement on open ground, where in fact I go round in a circle about the size of a thumbprint. In the slot between the lips I have only to set my smooth shoulder against the inner lip and work my feet against the outer. I can go the whole way round in either direction. In one direction my mouth goes first; in the other I occasionally have to back up to see what I have passed.

There is a living to be had here from the grazers. A grazer coming in for the night has meal on its forehead, packed quite hard by the pressure of the other heads at the plug. It wedges itself into a notch and goes a little farther in than is comfortable, then draws back until its feet are under it. This leaves a dab of meal at the blind end of the notch. While the grazer is awake it will put a forefoot up from time to time to feel for this, though I have never known one get any of it out. I come at it from below. My mouth opens sideways like a little purse. A good dab takes me most of the night. In the morning there is room for the grazer to go that much farther in.

At one time I should have said I lived in a ring. There was the long rough stretch, the place where I had to go on my side under a splinter, the three bottoms so exactly alike that I knew them only by what came next. Going on always brought me home. But the old lip is giving way. One evening I came to the splinter and found it lying loose across the slot. I ate what was on it. With the meal gone it was light enough to shift, and it fell out. Beyond it my feet felt about for the rough wall and found the face of a young dish instead.

The young dish had come in upside down and lodged there, with one leg free. It was feeling along my row of feet, taking them up one at a time and letting them go. I backed away. It got hold of the end of my shoulder, which is curled over and offers rather better purchase, and for a little while neither of us went anywhere. Then the old dish slipped its usual little distance down the stone. I felt the pull come off me. The young one was right side up, with my shoulder still in the crook of its leg. We went perhaps the length of a grazer together before it put that foot down and left me behind.

So I have been out on the crescent of clean stone. It is not clean all the way across. The old belly leaves behind it little combings of yellow stuff, in arcs, with a break in each arc where the joint between the stones comes through. I went round in my foolish circle there and ate very well. At one point in every circuit my feet were in the joint and the hot breath came up between them. At another my smooth side touched the worn lip. On the fourth time round I took hold.

I still live there. Only now, when the small weight comes on in the evening, I let the lip go by a little way before I follow. I like the food on the stone, and I like that moment on coming back when my feet have found the rough old edge and my shoulder has not yet touched the sound one. There is a grazer in the first notch past the break that has taken to sleeping farther and farther in. One night I expect to come up under it and find that there is a way through.
