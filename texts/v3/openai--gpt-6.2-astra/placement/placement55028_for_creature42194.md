I would be on the roof, a little way past the eggs. At first I think the little print seen from the edge is my foot. Then the young salamander goes on, putting its feet down one after the other with a care that seems wasted in so light a creature, and I am still in the place where its foot was. I am the wet that made the print show.

It is a good place for water to be. There is a grain in the limestone there that stands a little lower than its neighbors. Water coming through the roof from quite a wide area finds its way to that one grain. For a while there is just a dark patch, then a brightness at the bottom of the patch, and then I hang down into the room. There is an egg close by. By the time I am heavy enough to fall I have generally touched the egg and spread over it instead. That last little distance, which looks so inconsiderable from outside, is about as far as I ever have to go through air.

From here the mother is mostly a chin. There is a pale fold under it that closes neatly when she puts her nose up. She takes me off the egg, or takes the greater part of me; a little stays behind in the place where the egg joins its stalk. Her skin is wet already. I run some way down her side before the floor takes me, at a place well behind the foreleg. For some time after that I have the shape of the space between a salamander and a stone.

There is a part of the valley one could learn very well that way. I know, for instance, where the mother used to lie. It is only a little to the left of where she lies now. There is still a belly-wide place there into which water runs easily, and across it, at an angle, a newer snail track on which the water holds together in a narrow shining roll. Down on the floor the sound from the lip has no distance to it. It is a fine continual trembling of the roll. When the runner lets go of the flake the two ends jump together and some of me gets across the track into country I have not covered before.

I would like to stay here long enough for one of the eggs to open. It would be easy enough to say that I do. There will certainly be water here then, on the low grain and on the mother's back. But the stone has a slight fall away from the eggs. Already I am going out along it, carrying a little of the pink dust with me. The roof comes down so close that there is no longer room for the white jointed things. For a considerable distance there is darkness even when the runner lifts its stone.

I come out a hand's breadth below its sill. The sun is on this part of the face. I spread very thin and for a moment make a dark figure there, with a wide head and several absurd little feet where the stone draws me out along its grain. A swift coming up might see it. The feet go first. In the seam above, another drop has grown heavy enough to lean against the egg.
