From the valley I would put myself at the notch. I would have a pot with a handle big enough to get my wrist through, so that I could use both hands on the climb. I would have come a long way for warm water, past a stream of perfectly good cold water, but from the valley it is the smooth place for the knee that I keep thinking of. Someone has been expected there often enough to wear the stone away.

Up at the landing I think I would be something with less use for a handle. A long pale thread comes out between two of the courses while the water is flat. It lies across the fine black track that the grazers have made. A foot comes down on it. The thread snatches back into the wall with a little clot of dirt stuck to the end. That's where I would be. The pot could go in and come out again without my knowing what the shadow was.

Unfolded I would be about as long as the nail on a man's little finger. There isn't room to unfold. My tail has grown into the shape of the crook in the joint, and my head lies beside it, with the thread coiled in the space between. What I eat is chiefly what the grazers miss. A little beast feeding sideways pushes a low ridge of stuff ahead of its cheek. When it goes loose in a hurry the ridge stays where it is. I can put my thread out along the whole length of one of those ridges and bring it in green from end to end. It takes some time to get it through my lips. An especially good meal leaves a coil or two outside, with things still walking over it.

For a long time I would know the old one by a mistake. There is the weight of a foot and then the slight combing pull of the fringe as the foot lifts; there are several such pulls in quick succession. With this one a weight comes off clean. I draw the thread in thinking the beast has gone, put it out again and get stepped on by the very same beast. In time I learn to wait. On the morning the leaf goes there are so many feet under the lip that I keep the thread in altogether. A drop works its way along them. Through the stone I can feel the small hard tap each time a drinking mouth shuts on a hair.

There are things I do when there is nothing to eat. Far back in my joint there is a grain of red stone that has come loose, too big to pass my body on either side. I work it round and round with my tail. At first this is on account of a corner that sticks into me. After the corner is gone I still do it. In all that cleft there would be one piece of gravel, then, a rather handsome dark red one, polished with a substance much like the white of an egg. No one coming up with a pot would ever see it.

One afternoon a blow shifts the top course hardly the thickness of the thread. The grain slips past me into the light. It stands on the landing among the six-footed beasts, high as a little beast's shoulder, wet and quite bright. One of the white ones grazes a stripe through the polish. Then the water takes it.

There would be a surprising amount of room at the back after that. For the first time I could get my head round past the crook. There is another opening there. I put the thread through and for a while get nothing on it at all; it hangs a long way down in air. Then something meets it, not the stroke of a fringe but a soft extended touch, moving along it from the tip toward my mouth. From a joint a course below, another thread has been put out. We hold still until the next wash. When we draw apart there is a little green on mine, and a little red on the other.
