I would look for myself a little way behind the blue chip, where its point used to be. There is a nick there in the soft skin. Seen by light from the screw it is dark on one side and pale on the other, as any little hollow ought to be. Seen by light from zero it is dark on the same side. Some of the blue must have rubbed off. There is too little of it to count as a grain, but enough to tell one lip of the nick from the other after the nick itself has closed.

For a long time I think that would be all I was: a place which has had something in it. There would be no sensation of the chip going away. Its point would press a little less hard each winter, until one winter the two lips would touch. The blue on one of them would come off, in part, on the other. It would be rather a surprise to have two sides.

What I would know best would be the difference between having the skin pulled straight and having it let out again. These are nearly the same thing if you are very small and are lying crosswise to the pull. But when the skin is pulled straight, the two blue places fit exactly. When it is let out, one rides a little over the other. There would be a narrow ledge inside me then, with nothing on it. Outside, the ledges on the near slope run for some distance and come to an end in the light. Mine would be quite dark even on the afternoons of the return from zero. I should have a country of my own in that respect.

I don't think I would know about either mountain. I would know something about the little furl in the staple. After everything else had gone slack, the ledge would sometimes grow narrower still. Something at a distance was taking up skin, very sparingly, as one can draw a corner of a sheet from under a sleeping weight without stirring it. That would be my experience of another being, if it was another being. It would take nothing I had any use for. By the next pull the skin would always be back.

In time the chip will come to the turn of the hem. I am interested in what will happen there. Its sharp end, which the folds have kept pointing downhill for so long, will meet skin coming the other way. It may work free and lie in the bay with the other bits of shale. If it stays in, it will pass quite close to its old impression, on the underside this time. There will be two thicknesses of skin between us. The fit will be poor. The point will have lost some blue, and I shall have spent several hundred winters closing.

Still, I should like to be there for that. On the outside there will be very little to see. The hem may lift a little out of its bed and let a few black grains run in. On the inside, for the space between the ticks, my empty ledge will have a weight on it.
