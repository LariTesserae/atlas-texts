I think I would have been in the bedding. When she came back for the first mouthful I would have gone with it, though not on the outside, where there was light and a view of the journey. Somewhere in the middle a fiber bent double would have straightened a little under the pull of her teeth. That would have been the whole business of crossing, as far as I knew.

I would be a beetle about the size of one of the seeds in a fig. There are a number of things that size in the bed. If you shook it out on a leaf, most of them would make for the underside at once. I would stay where I fell for quite a while. I have the trick of drawing in my legs and letting two little plates down over my eyes. With the plates down there is no convenient place for a tooth to take hold. The mother has picked me up more than once in cleaning a youngster and rolled me about against the roof of her mouth. She generally puts me down wet but entire.

What I eat is skin. At the bottom of a bed that has held three generations of gliders there is a considerable quantity of it, especially at the turn in the wall where an animal has to rub its ear going in or out. A flake from the rim of an ear will keep me busy for a night. I stand on the flake to eat it. Toward the end of the meal there is a certain amount of difficulty about getting my feet out of the way.

For the first day in the new tree I would hardly know there had been a change. There is the old smell, and the weight comes on and goes off in much the usual way. Then one of the young ones, heaving up onto its side for a feed, pushes me clean through the bed. Underneath is yellow powder instead of the close smooth wood my feet expect. I go down in it up to the eyes. Something a good deal longer than I am is backing up a passage there, packing the powder behind it with the flat of its tail. Each push brings me a little nearer the top. I come out beside a pink heel and set off along the wall to see what sort of a bottom we have got now.

There would be weeks of going no farther than that. I like the part of the hole that bends upward. Whatever rolls out of the bed lodges there. One day a milk tooth comes down, wonderfully clean except for a string of meat on one end. For two nights I eat better than I ever did in the fig. On the third the tooth is gone. A youngster has found that by lying on its back it can get a hind foot into the bend, and has kicked everything out into the rain.

By then the youngsters would be spending some of the night away. You could tell which had come home without going up. One turns round six or seven times before settling. One drops in from the roof. The smallest comes in so quietly that the first you know of it is the warmth making its way down. It is this one I would leave with. There need not be anything wrong with the bed. At a certain age the soft seams along my back begin to itch; I am forever getting a foot up to scratch at them. Then for a few evenings running I go toward the warmth instead of away from the weight. When the small one gets up, I have hold of a hair.

It is a different matter crossing on the outside. The hair parts. There is cold at the roots of it and for an appreciable time there is nothing pressing up against any part of us. Then a considerable blow drives my head between my forefeet. We have struck a trunk across one of the narrow strips. The youngster hangs there a while. A frog quite close at hand draws its nail across its comb, once, and the ears above me turn separately to bring the sound between them.

I might go a long way like that, or only as far as the fork above the landing place. A glider puts its chin down to drink; two or three hairs catch on a rough lip of bark and draw through it slowly as the head comes up. There is time to step off. Here is a hole with a handful of rather coarse fiber in it, quite new, and at the back the wood is smooth from the passage of an ear. I would try that place. By morning I would be well in under the fold where the bark has grown over an old hurt. From the door you could still see the white fig, diminished to a fork of bone against the green. From where I am there is just the one hair caught in the entrance, going bright along its length as the sun comes up, then dark quite suddenly as somebody comes in.
