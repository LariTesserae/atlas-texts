I would be a chalk at Number 4, though I would spend rather less time at the brass clips than the others. There is a broken place in the binding of the mat. Three or four of the long fibres have come out and lie across the blue foot of the 4, lifting at each stroke and coming down in a slightly different order. A chalk can get a good hold there without having to share.

One of the fibres is caught under the head of the farther clip. It carries the stroke almost as well as the brass itself, and has the convenience that one can take it along. I would have got into the habit of going about the landing with it crooked over my last left foot. This would give me a range of about eight inches. There are quite as many things to eat eight inches from a mat as there are underneath one. At the edge of my range there is a nail hole in the wall, plugged with something soft that grows back overnight.

On the days when the tender comes, the fibre goes slack for a moment. It is not that the mill has stopped. With five pairs of feet on the landing one can feel it going on as before. But the fine shiver that keeps the fibre standing a little way out from the stone is gone, and for that moment the whole wet weight of it comes down on one foot. I think I would know the tender principally as a weight, as the chalk behind the woman’s ear knows it principally as light.

In time the fibre would come out of the clip.

I don’t suppose I would notice at once. I would reach the nail hole, eat, and go a little farther down the wall, the fibre paying out behind me instead of bringing me up short. There is a sill below the landing with a good deal of sand on it. By the time I had gone the length of the sill I would have changed colour twice along my back, which was still grey at one end and already freckled with bits of the shell of a very small blue mussel at the other. The loose end of the fibre would be travelling behind me in a succession of short leaps. Each time it caught on anything I would stop and set all my feet down for the stroke. Once, on a pipe bracket, there would actually be a stroke. I would stay there a long while.

What would take me off the bracket would be the eel. Even the chalks that live well back in the houses let go on one side when it comes; behind the closed doors of Number 6 there must be a little needless showing of white in half a dozen rooms. I would turn, and the fibre would slip, and by the time I had finished with the small leech on my comb I would have nothing in particular to carry home.

I would get down to the turning circle eventually. I like to think I would be the one that lifts the washer, but there is already a chalk there, and the square, for all its appearance of emptiness seen from six storeys up, is a rather exposed place to cross. I would go round by the reddish felt. At its lower edge, where the felt has grown out over the grout, the young are beating their feet. One can stand with one’s threads in the little current they make and pick out wood meal without disturbing anybody. I would do that for several strokes. When the plate had settled and the pink feet were in, I would feel about under the edge for a loose red strand, slip it over my last left foot, and go on eating.
