I should look at first for something on the floor. When the drop lets go of the limpet there is a brown cap for an instant in the hollow it makes below. The cap goes out to the sides, grows two long ends, and breaks up among the springtails. There would be some pleasure in being that quick. But when the water has steadied, the cap is there again. Whatever I took for myself has stayed on the ceiling.

There is a place to look just ahead of the young limpet. The slate there appears blue. From very close up the blue has a nap to it, all combed one way by the water going out. A grain of peat rolls a little distance over it and stops. Presently the head comes out over the grain. The horns pass on either side of it. The next time it moves there is clear stone underneath. I think I would be in that nap, in the part of the meal which, until the mouth goes over it, does not even show as a meal.

A diatom, perhaps. I would have a case of my own, two clear pieces fitting one over the other, with such fine work along the sides that one could spend a good deal of daylight looking at it and still have to turn it over. Inside, a little gold-brown substance. There is light enough for me on the old slate toward the door. A finger put in through the moss and rubbed gently there would come out with a yellowish stain on its pad. In the hollow of one whorl of the fingerprint there might be several hundred cases as good as mine.

I would not be quite fixed. In the wet I could slide the length of my case, pause, and go back again, without anything in the shape of a limb to account for it. The piece of peat which from the floor seemed to have stopped on level ground would be a considerable shelter on one side and a place of troublesome currents on the other. As the film thinned, the water would stay thick against it. There would be time to slip along the sheltered side while the limpet worked its way up the other. Or the grain might turn. A good many things in here depend upon which end of a bit of dirt is the heavier.

Under the lip of the mouth there would be a short rough passage. I don't think I should go on from there as the limpet, and feel the pull of the foot, tempting as that is. There would be an empty case in due course, possibly only one clear half of one, come to rest with its open side up on the pale tip of the stone. It would make a very fair account of where I had been, to anything small enough to find it.

But the grain has not turned yet. The little head is working away on its farther side. Through my two walls comes a greenish light, interrupted at pretty regular intervals as a thread of moss swings across the door. In the interval of light the gold-brown stuff does its work. The sweep left by the large limpet yesterday, so blue and clean when it was made, is just beginning to take on colour again. I would put myself at the edge of that colour. From the floor, even knowing the spot, you would say there was still a little bare slate between the meal and the mouth.
