I would be in the wall behind the fourth hook. There is a pocket there where the drill went through the tile and struck an old nail in the packing. The man putting up the hook turned it a little to the left and tried again. From the room the two holes look like one. Farther in they part, and round the end of the nail is a space about the size of a shirt button. I should have room to turn in it, though not with anything in my mouth.

From outside there would be very little of me to see. A pair of clear blades stand out among the white fur on the curl of the hook. The fur all points one way in a draught; the blades continue to point a little away from one another. They are the two sides of my mouth. I take in the dust the lodger makes. There is food enough in what comes off one tile to last me until the light has been twice round the ward, but most of it goes by at the wrong height. The useful stuff comes along close to the brass. A flake travelling broadside on has to turn on edge to get past the shank, and while it is considering this I can generally have it.

For some time I should seem to be a small and rather unnecessary part of the lodger, like the blue worm. When its fringe is busy my blades are busy. When it puts itself away there is presently nothing doing at my end either. But a thread of something very fine comes out between my blades after a good meal. It goes up the tile instead of down. It is made of all the parts of the meal I cannot use, stuck together with a little oil, and it rises until the draught from the back of the shelf takes it. In time a length of it winds about the curl. This accounts for some of the fur. If the hook were taken out, my work would come away with it and I would be left in the wall, a short fat thing with a remarkably clean front.

I should know the other holes in that wall better than the lodger does. Behind the yellow tiles the work is poor. There is mortar at the corners and a dab in the middle of each, and between the dabs are low passages with the comb marks of the trowel preserved on their ceilings. My undersurface has two ridges the right distance apart to run in those marks. I can go fairly smartly backwards. At a crossing I have to feel for the next pair of grooves with my hind end, which takes time and gives me the appearance, from below, of a person trying to open a door with both hands full.

The passages are too low to turn in. The drill holes are my turning places. In the hole behind the second hook there is a round green grain that I brought there myself when I was narrow enough to get past it. I have to back out of that one now. At the first hook the tile has parted from its dab, and if I press up with my back it gives a little. The water displaced comes out in a dry-looking puff all round the edge of the tile. Sometimes the lodger puts its stamp down on the other side at the same instant. We then remain as we are for a surprisingly long time. Neither of us has any apparatus for finding out what is meant.

On the occasion of the move from the third hook to the first, I would be the last creature in the room to know where the cup had gone. The tremor would come equally through all the holes. I would stand at the fourth with my mouth open for the better part of a tide. A little plaster would arrive, quite fresh but with nothing eaten off it; then a single length of the blue worm’s casting, a very choice article, too long to turn with. By the time I had got it down there would be no dust at all.

I would try the second hole first. Nothing there but the feel of the green grain against my back. At the first, well before I got my blades out, I would feel a steady knocking through the loose tile. It would be the young, still trying their feet after the disturbance. From inside the wall it isn't like keys in a lock. It is more like a shower beginning over one small part of a roof. I would stay there to feed. For several turns of the light no part of me would appear in the room; there would just be a place under the first hook where a surprising amount of powder could go in without the crack filling up.

And at the fourth hook, beyond reach now even at the shank, the two clear blades would still appear to be standing in the fur. Those would be the last skins of my mouth, caught by their hinges on the brass. A week of ordinary tides would bring them together. It would look as though the little thing that lived there had at last got hold of something, and meant to keep it.
