I would have taken the clear place in a collar for the work of the water. There is a good deal of polishing done there. But a clear place made by water widens in the middle, where the set of the water is strongest. This one widens at the closed end. Just beyond it, in the milk of the wall, there are four small interruptions in the grain of the lime. These are my feet. I have grown them in the course of cleaning that window.

The first time I come to the bend with feet I go a little too far. The front pair go round well enough with the lips. For the hind pair to go round I should have had to start turning some way back. I stay folded there, neither going on nor drawing away, and eat a patch of wall which in the ordinary course I should have passed over. It is a good patch. When at last I get straight again there is the shape of a little ear at the end of the window. For a long time afterward the light gets in by this ear, and things going up and down outside the spring pass across it. They do not look like grains from in here. There is a brightening, a pause, a brightening, as if something very large were passing a hand to and fro over the mountain.

Of the great business of coming out I think I would know surprisingly little. There is wall to the right, and then none. The water takes me twice the height of the collar and brings me down with my belly against the very opening through which I came. I have never touched it from this side. It has a bad edge for holding. Nor would I know the moment when I had got clear of the dancing water, except that I have been trying for some time to put a hind foot where a forefoot is, and now I can. Both hold. There is a considerable piece of white afternoon still to run before I have all four feet in the shade.

I would be rather a long time finding my station. There is an excellent crack not far from the collar, with a roof and floor at nearly the right distance and plenty of traffic along the wall. I spend a day there and take nothing. Each time the fine jointed contact comes well within the fringe there is something the matter at my other end. My folds have come quite apart; they take up nearly as much room as I do and still there is no relief. I have to draw back into the wash. Once I draw back with the forward half of a shrimp actually in my mouth. It goes on cleaning the stone a little below me.

Where I end up is scarcely a crack at all. A flake has come part way off the underside of a back and remained attached along one edge. Water from a chamber runs over its free edge in a thin crumpled sheet. Behind it the stone is soft to the lips. I could have walked past many times and felt no more than that softness. But coming at it from below I happen to get the knob into the sheet while my lips are still on the stone. The folds draw comfortably together. I stay to clean a little more of the wall. Something cleans it toward me.

After that there would be days on which I did not shift a foot. Much that one would like to know about the shelf would go past me. Whether the sun had gone behind the western shoulder; whether the water dropping in the chamber was falling the height of a man or the breadth of a hand. I would know the flake quite well. It gives a little on its hinge when the sheet thickens, bringing the roof down against the skin between my shoulders. There is a pit in it where my right forefoot rests. The pit is longer than the foot. In time I grow into it.

It is the place I would look for, coming back as someone able to walk the crowns: not the spring I came from, whose collar by then would have sealed the little ear, but the loose flake with the water going white along one edge. I doubt one could find it. There must be miles of such edges. And it would be easy, on putting a hand down and feeling the flake stir, to think one had frightened away what lived there. In fact I would still have my lips to the wall. The pressure of the hand would have gone through both pairs of feet and out into the mountain. In the shelter of the flake a shrimp has laid its feeler along my fringe. It is feeling its way down the side of my mouth, taking its time, and I have the time to let it.
