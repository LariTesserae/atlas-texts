I would live at the west gate, on the side it opens toward. There is a space there which nobody was at pains to make: the lower rail sags away from the upper one, and the piece of horn meant to face the two is quite straight. Even with the gate shut there is room behind it for me to turn over. I should have the broad foot of the post for a doorstep and the lopsided animal for an upstairs neighbor, though I should know it chiefly by the little siftings of gold that come down when it eats.

I think I would be one of the fifteen scraps that went out through the handles of the boy’s box. The crosswise one, perhaps. Once the curl is out of us there is no telling. For the first few weeks we are pale on the side that was rolled in and can be found at dusk by the little flashes we make in turning a corner. Later we are brown on both sides. We keep the curl in our sleep, however. This is why my house suits me better than a level crack would. I go in flat at the narrow end and by the time I am round I have worked down to the wide end without so much as taking a step.

There is quite good grazing under a gate. A blade that grows up in the sweep of it gets a bruise at the same hour every morning; by evening one can take the bruised part out with the lips, leaving two green threads that will hold the tip upright for another day. I should work my way along the little arc of blades. Now and then the woman who planted them would squat down with her hands on her knees to see how I did it. She has spent some time trying to do it with a thumbnail. The two threads are useful to her for sewing, being already joined at one end.

It would be some time before the boy found me. He is usually looking a good deal higher up, where the white animals are. What would bring us together, I think, is that one evening he would lose the brown mother off his shoulder. She would put her pad out as usual, but against his collar instead of his cheek, and after waiting the proper time she would get down of her own accord. By the time he missed her there would be the gate and half the older quarter to look through. He would come along on his hands and knees calling to her, which is of no particular use with a brown animal, and feel the cool of me with the back of a knuckle.

I would like being carried. I would particularly like the little updraft at his ear when he takes the steps fast. At home he would set me down in the box and sit with his chin on its edge, troubled by the fit of me in it. A smaller animal in the same box looks surprisingly like a larger animal put away farther off. He would know as soon as he laid me across his eyes. There would be brown afternoon on one side and the white plaster of his ceiling on the other.

By then the mother would be home. There is no great knack to coming back to a box that smells of fifteen of your young. She would be waiting underneath it, which is where she used to spend the evenings before the boy took to carrying her out. I imagine him lying there a little while longer than he needs to, one eye open under me and one eye open to the room, not yet having decided that he would rather see alike out of both.

He would take me back to the gate. He might be tempted to put a mark on me. Instead, being his mother’s son, he would put a small fitting on the house: a slip of horn beside the straight one, held at the top by a pin, which he could turn aside to see whether I was in. The first time he opened it after dark I should be round as a shaving in the bottom of the wedge. In time a root would come between the two slips, and there would be a little more shade at my door, and then something fit to eat growing right up against it.

I should still go out to the blades. At their far end, where the gate can only just touch them, the bruise is so slight that it takes a whole evening to have one’s supper. From there I could feel through the paving the boy going out at a walk and coming home at a run. I don't think I would know that he went to visit a hole in the clay, or that the woman with the thumbnail had made the first of all those blades herself. I would know which foot he checked on when he saw me in his way. It would be the left going out and the right coming home.
