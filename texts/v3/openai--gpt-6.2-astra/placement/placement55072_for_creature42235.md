For a long time I would have put myself on the floor, in one of the intervals made audible by the fall of a sole. There must be a last pear at the edge of the covered place whose note, without changing at all, comes out of the general sound. I should have liked to be that one. One could find it with one's eyes shut. But there is a particular loose piece of quartz in the uphill break which I keep coming back to. It is white at either end and quite clear in the middle, and it rocks. I think I would be underneath it.

The daylight discovers the piece about noon. At first it looks like part of the seam on the lower slab. Then a white end goes dark. A little later the other end goes dark and the first is white again. The movement is hardly more than the movement of the shine on it. A jointed animal coming up the seam puts its nose on the quartz and goes partway over before the piece gives under it. The nose goes down, the far end of the quartz comes up, and a short gray fringe is exposed and immediately drawn in. That would be the first good look at me.

I would be a soft animal, folded lengthwise, with one half of the fold fast to the granite. The loose half fits the slight hollow on the underside of the quartz. I can lift it but I cannot pull it down. Nearly everything that passes through the break does a little of the work of closing me. The slab itself does it very thoroughly at intervals of some days. There is a groove in the fixed half of me into which the loose half goes, fringe and all; the quartz can then lie flat against the rock without much damage. A few of the outer hairs get pinched off. They show for a while as fine white strokes radiating from the chip, much longer than one would expect in so small an animal.

What I get here is what comes back. While the water is going into the mountain the fringe lies neatly along the groove and very little sticks to it. When the pressure eases, a little water comes feeling its way out along the lower face of the slab. It is almost clear. The gray backs have taken the milk out of it a good way in. But in shutting, each pair of lips squeezes out a loose roll of skin, pink when it first comes away, afterward the color of the rock. These come tumbling down the seam on the return water. A skin caught crosswise in the hairs makes a perceptible weight. The folded side sags, the quartz settles, and I have the roll inside me. It takes several such mouthfuls to account for one of the changes from white to dark that can be seen from the floor.

There is some floor in my life. When the little stones are being tried out below, the sharp notes arrive through the fixed side of the fold, not through the side on which the stones actually lie. There is no direction to them that would help me find a pear. A sole coming down makes hardly any impression. A single white animal resting its last segment on my bit of quartz outweighs all that business. I would be quite wrong, in the dark, about the size of the things that live with me.

In one of the longer openings of the slab, the quartz turns over. There is no great wash of water. One edge simply goes a little too far up, and the piece slides down beside me on its back. The hollow is full of tiny colorless knobs. In the light across the clear middle each has a gray line doubled up inside it. I have been laying them there, though until the piece turns there is nothing that could be seen to pass between my skin and the stone. The white animal comes over it again. Several knobs stick to the soft place where two of its segments meet. They are carried another segment's length at each shortening, well past the last pink mouths. Most stay where they are.

Without the weight I open to an inconvenient width. The return water combs the hairs out straight and takes the skins through without catching. For much of a day I would be the small pale fan visible in the break, mistaken very reasonably for something that has worked loose and will presently be gone. Toward evening the pressure changes. Red meal goes in across me. After the meal comes a flat black grain, polished on every side but one, where there is still a little roughness like the worn pile on cloth. It lodges in the fold. It is a poor fit; the upper hairs project on one side and admit water even when I am shut. Still, on the first outward trickle a skin catches there. By the time the daylight reaches the lip I have got it nearly all the way in.

I would stay under the black grain. It is the overturned quartz that one would notice next noon, brilliant again and a little farther from its place in the line. One of the knobs on it has come open. The two halves are so nearly alike that for a while both cling to the stone. Then a thread of water gets between them, and there is a minute fan laid out flat on the upper face, with the whole weight of the mountain, as yet, on the wrong side.
