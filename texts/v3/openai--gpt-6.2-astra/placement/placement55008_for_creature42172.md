I would have put myself at the mouth of the blister, where a bird coming home shows the marks on one flank and then, for an instant, the marks on the other. It would be a good place to watch from. But there is room there for very little besides the bird. Even the dead grass at the entrance has been combed flat against the stone by the passage of its sides.

There is more room a yard away, under the roof with the white feather on it. I think I would be there, owing to a small error made some days before, when it was still a good place to be.

I would be one of the flies. In the warmth the egg had been laid in, I ought to have been out by now. The water went down when I was still a little comma of an animal, jointed very distinctly at the bend, with two trumpets at the thick end which I kept up against the surface film. For a time there were a great many of us under the old nest. A shaking of the floor sent us all tumbling down into the water; then, without any particular effort on our part, we came up again. The nestlings must have got quite big. Towards the end there was hardly a minute of the day when we were not going up or coming down.

A last spoonful of water stayed in a pocket at the back. It had a lid of ice towards the daylight and no lid at all farther in. This was where the sleet found me. There had been a long time in which nothing was changed except that the ceiling came nearer. Now the ceiling went up quite rapidly. A piece of sedge which had rested on the bottom at one end got afloat and went round twice, and went out end first through the door the rails had used. I went with it, turning over and over in full daylight with the trumpets of very little use to me.

It was not far to the lower spring. The difficulty was to stop there. The first few blades let me through. At the next there was a duck with its head entirely out of sight and its two feet making little backward scoops to keep it in that position. I was fetched quite smartly back uphill and left in a stand of brown stems so close together that the water among them was almost still. It was warm to the very top.

By evening I should have the use of wings. There would be a little difficulty getting the second one clear and a further wait with both of them sticking out wetly behind, during which I should be better off not noticed by anybody. Then I could go up with the others. From outside, our pillar would look as if it held its place very steadily over the sedge. Inside it there would be a good deal of going too far to one side, finding it bitter there, and coming back.

I doubt that I should ever see the peak. I should see a large round drop on the point of a blade, with a black bird going head downwards through it. Below the drop the actual bird would pass, going the right way up, close enough to set the blade nodding. Long after it had gone indoors we should still be rising a little and sinking a little over the place its foot had left warm water standing in.
