At first I would put myself in the last furrow, on the underside of the bent green filament. It is the only thing in the court that makes anything like a bridge. I should have a white back, I think, and go along it upside down, with the pavement so close to my back that at the lower of the two bends I should have to let go and crawl. But that is a choice made with an eye at the chip. From there the filament and the doors look very near one another. To get from the one to the other with a soft belly on lime would be a considerable undertaking. I don't think I should ever have seen the filament.

What I can come nearer to is the pink dash that turns the wrong way in the fold. There would be no wrong way, of course, from in there. There would be a yielding place all down one flank, and on the other a round hard place, the back of the next disc. By turning I could put my head into the yielding place. Presently it would harden too. The side would be drawing in, the discs sliding one over the other, and for a while there would be just room enough in my own clear skin for me in the position I had chosen. I should learn quite early what it costs to have one's head crosswise.

I should like to stay with that one a little after it is carried out of sight. The room under the runner need not be very large. A crumb pulled out of the bed with the comb would leave room for the whole side to hang loose. In there, with the weight off them, the discs would stand a little apart. Mine, which had been pressed flat on one face, would take a long time coming round; there would be a little click when it did, not loud enough to come out through the chip, but quite a rap to the one next to me. I imagine that is the first thing I should do in the building that could be laid to my own account. The rest of the shaking would belong to the tile.

When the skin gave, I should come out against the runner. Not fall; there would be a film enough on me to stick me where I touched. The loose side would go on gathering and letting out for some time before it went away. Each time it came near I should put my mouth to it. There would be grey stuff in the creases, brought back from the whorl; enough for a creature whose two doors were still only a pair of sore-looking places on its back. It might be a long while before I had to find a line of my own.

There is a place I should try for. It is not under the broad old roof. That must have a good bed, but there is no telling what is still in it. It is just behind one of the little bleached pairs, where one door has slipped inwards and the other is still fast. From the chip the two look shut. From behind, the slipped door would be a slope down to the lime, with a narrow way beside it where the wet could come up. I should be able to reach it without once letting my hind end off the rough underside of the runner. I would go down head first and stay for the first darkening. If it went past below my mouth I would go a little farther down.

It pleases me that I might grow up there and the account of the court need not be altered in any particular. A small old pair of doors, bleached now. The red on mine would be on the backs, turned up against the white ones; even stretched out feeding I should have the slipped door over me. All that would come of my being there, to an eye outside, would be a very short length of line that got clean rather sooner than the rest. And I should have a different use for the great note. Under the runner it would be going all the time, too steadily to take much account of. But whenever I reached down far enough to touch the slipped door with my chin, there it would be again, quite sharp, through the loose lime. I should know how much farther I had to go by how it took me in the jaw.

On a dry afternoon the first news of anyone lying down would be a little ease in that door. The wet would get to the dust in its seat before there was anything for me to drink. It would settle against my back, no more than the weight of a disc settling against another disc. I would turn a little under it. By the time the watcher had lifted a cheek and found the oval on the pillar, I should have my mouth in place.
