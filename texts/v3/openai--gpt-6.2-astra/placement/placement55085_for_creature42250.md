I would be on the upper of the two stems, but when I first touched it I would still be going quite fast. The touch would turn me. For a little way I would go along the lime with one side brushing it, as a boat not quite brought to rest goes along a landing. Then I would be clear again. It would be on the second touch, a little way round the stem, that I would hold.

Until then it would have been possible to tell where I was going. A grain hanging well off the stem would have been first in my way, then beside me, then behind. Now the same grain would keep its place for a long while. The hairs about my middle would cease beating. For a time there would be very little by which to tell me from a bit of the grey that lodges in the scores. The station could do a whole afternoon's work in this light without there being anything like an afternoon on my side of the lime.

The first length of stalk would be a small matter even to me. There would be water, then, between most of my body and the hard place where I had stopped. As the stalk lengthened I would come out past a flake of lime that had sheltered me on one side. My rim would open there. A few particles would begin to describe quite decided curves in the space that, while I was swimming through it, had seemed full of things at rest.

That is where I think I would find myself: a little farther from the stem than the thickness of the flake. Not far enough out to see over anything. There would be no view of the bay from there, though the light that had crossed the bay would pass through my lip. My reach would be rather greater than my size. Something that did not touch me, that was not even headed my way, would turn a little because of what I was doing. It might still get past. Below me there would be a long unoccupied stretch of stem, and below that the bells from among which I had come, drawing water as before.

If I drew up, the flake would be beside me again almost at once. Coming out would take longer. Before I was quite extended the water would be stirring at my rim; a fleck would go round with it once and come loose on the side toward the lake. It would not get as far as the lake. A little beyond the tip of the stem it would slow until it could be seen hanging there, apparently in water in which nothing had happened.
