I would be in the stone with the yellow hollow, on the side away from the bed. You could see where I had been by running a thumb down through the grease. In one place the lime would feel like the underside of a saucer. A little farther down it would catch at the whorls of your thumb. That rough patch would have moved perhaps the width of a hair since the last time the stone had a shadow pointing west.

I have a foot nearly as broad as my back and a row of little black teeth under the leading edge of it. My back is white. The grit has worked the same small pits in it as in the lime, except for two crescents near the rim which I can draw under when there is a blow coming. On a cool morning a bead lodged in one of the crescents will make me lean for hours to that side. I have a remarkably poor purchase on the world for a thing that has never been known to fall off it.

For a long time I would know the beasts principally by the grease. In the high hollows it comes down thick and yellow, good all the way through. Lower down there will be a very promising streak which turns out to be dust with the thinnest wash of fat over it. I eat the lime too. A hair is a considerable nuisance. I have to go its whole length and turn round the free end, and sometimes the free end is bedded in the stone and I come back to the place where I started, having cleaned a long buttonhole with a hair down the middle of it.

One afternoon a shoulder comes against the stone while I am going round a hair. I draw the crescents in. The dark under the shoulder is warm; it smells quite appreciably of the food, yet between me and the hide there is a thickness through which my teeth will not go. Other shoulders come and go in just that place. One alone presses me so close that the part of my foot which was on the far side of the hair comes into touch with the part on the near side. It hurts. I let go of the lime, which I have not done since I was soft enough to fit inside one of its pits.

When the old female gets up I go with her. The hair that gave me so much trouble is still attached to her shoulder.

It is a very bad afternoon. All my arrangements are for having the wind on my back. Here the wind gets underneath, and when the old beast walks there is a separate wrench for every foot. She goes as far as the near lip and feeds. The first knob comes up under me as big as the end of a thumb. I loose my hold ahead to let it by and tighten behind it, and do just the reverse of what is wanted. By the third or fourth knob I can stay where I am. There is grease enough down at the roots of the hair to keep a whole pedestal yellow.

She does not go back to her stone that evening. She folds up on the bed, taking a long time to get the last leg out from under her. A little beast puts its head across her feet. I can feel its jaw bumping occasionally against the joint, though the old one is no longer chewing. Then the shelf cools and the two of them lie so still that I venture a little way down her foreleg. Where the hair is rubbed off the knee the skin has very fine crossing lines. Nothing like lime. I go over it twice.

In the morning the little one gets up first. There is a pull at my foot and daylight in both crescents. For an instant I hang between the two hides on a thread of grease. Then I am going east, quite low, with four jolts close together and two far apart. The old female stays sitting where she is.

It will be some while before this knee is a good place to feed. There is hardly a drop of fat on it. But on the inside, where the leg draws up against the belly, there is a crease exactly deep enough for my back. I get into it before we reach the hot beads. The foot goes down. The two outer toes open, the crease closes over me, and for the length of one step I have as solid a roof as I had in the stone.
