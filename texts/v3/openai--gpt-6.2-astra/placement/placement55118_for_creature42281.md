I would find myself at the back of the crack by the girl’s bed. There is a small piece of weather to be had there. When the wind comes from the sea it pushes right through the wall and lifts the hair off the back of her neck. When it comes from the basin it scarcely gets in, but there is a taste of fish on everything for a day or two afterwards.

I think I would be something that requires very little. The pebble with the soft place in it would do. But I would like to be able to get out from under the pebble, and in fact when she lifts it there I go, making for the dark on seven perfectly serviceable pairs of legs. I am a woodlouse. There is no wood to speak of. I do quite well on the green that grows on the inside edges of the slabs, where daylight comes in narrow enough to put your finger over.

The girl has seen me several times without attaching much importance to the fact that it is me. Once she took me down to the basin in the hinge of the shell. It was an easy journey until she got to the fallen stone, where she put the shell down rather crookedly in order to get her head down too. I climbed out. From my height the finish of an old doorstep is quite rough going. I got about as far as the first knuckle of her thumb before she picked me up again.

For a time after that she thought it was necessary to take me out every morning. She made a wet place and a dry place in the shell so that I could go to whichever suited me. Both places were wet by the time we got anywhere. Her brother said I needed another of my sort for company and brought her one from under a basket. It was considerably bigger than I was. We sat at opposite ends of the shell for the whole of an afternoon, as two people will on a bench which they have gone to some trouble to reach for the sake of being alone.

What put an end to the outings was the discovery of my young. I had been carrying them underneath me for some time. She saw them one evening when I was crossing the pebble and came to the soft place, lost the purchase of my foremost feet and went over on my back. I was up again almost at once, but she had seen the little pale legs all moving against my belly. The notion of taking so many of us anywhere was too much even for her. She put the pebble back and left us in peace. My young ate a green patch quite clean, down to a grain of mica which came as a surprise to everybody when it caught the light.

I would know very little of what goes on out in the basin. I would know when the baskets were brought in, because the far end of the slab on which I live is the place where the basket woman sits to ease the wet straps off her shoulders. One end of the slab goes down and the other comes up, not by the thickness of a hair. It is enough to admit a smell of the dog asleep in the next house.

In the dark I sometimes come out as far as the tooth. The girl has pushed it well in so as not to lose it in her bedding. It is a good thing to go around rather than over. On the bed side it is smooth and dry. On the wall side a drop of water gathers in the hollow where it once fitted over something tender in her mouth. It will be there when all the exposed stone has been dry for hours. I put my head in to drink, and for a little while her brother is nearly right.
