I would be halfway up the lip, in the smallest of the six blue patches. From the pool I would look scarcely higher than the curl. For the curl to reach me, though, it would have to unroll many times its length and go on unrolling over stone that has not tasted brine in a long while.

Not all of the patch would be me. The upper part, which does the work of parting a drop, is horn. At one edge it has been chipped. The little face left by the chip is blue too, blue all the way down, with a polish from the water that runs there. I would be well in from that edge, among the white threads in the hollow of a missing grain. If the horn were held up to the sky it might let a little light through. With the stone behind it there is no question of light.

For a long time, when I was laid down, I went away and came back. There was a surface which took my whole weight and gave none of it back, then a surface which took me up into itself so exactly that there was hardly a surface at all. There was no reason for this to change. On the last occasion quite a small part of me stayed where it was. The rest went a little way with the curl. For a while I had a crease across my sleek face, a sort of mouth with the lips shut. Fresh water stood in it after the stone on either side had drained. It was the first thing I had ever kept.

I did not grow to any great breadth. The shelf was already on its way up. My last meal came from a white shaving lodged a little above me, so close that for part of each washing its frayed end lay across my face. One time the end lifted and did not come down. There was a considerable interval in which the water still tasted of the shaving. Then there was only the water. My face went on taking things in after there was nothing to take in, and thickened, and here I am.

I would have no notion of a leaf. What I have of it now is an occasional easing of the grip of one thread in the stone. The rock bears very hard against the flesh at the tear. When a purse empties somewhere below, a little of that burden comes off the lip. Most of the pile sits too snug to feel anything, but one thread crosses its hollow on a slant and takes the change in its length. This is enough to keep a minute lane of wet open under the horn. There is a part of me that can still pass from one side of the old crease to the other.

Below me the curl is coming down. I have no way of knowing this. Even the drop which is helping to bring it down goes round me in two streams; there is no inward difference between its going and its not going. I like to think that the pit within reach of the foot is a deep one. But a shallow pit will do, if one lip of it overhangs a little. A single white thread can get under such a lip while all the others are still lying flat on the grain.

It will be a long time before there is a seventh patch to see. For the present there is only a slight hesitation of the foot when the salt takes hold of it again. The curl rolls up. The stone below me has acquired a crease full of water.
