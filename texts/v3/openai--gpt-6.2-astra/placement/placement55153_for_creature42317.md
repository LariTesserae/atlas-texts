I would be in the empty skin, at the end where the split stops. There is a little air caught there. When water comes up between the sheet and the washer it enters the skin quite freely, but it never quite gets the air out. A silver pip goes up into the puckered end and becomes too small to see. When the water falls the pip comes down again. Seen from the button it looks as if the egg had acquired an eye some time after hatching.

I think I would be that bit of air, at first. There is something to be said for a body which can stay at home and change its size. At the deepest part of the flood my whole country would be one wrinkle. I would know the animal chiefly by its pull. With one toe on the rim of the split it could alter the shape of my room quite considerably. The light would go out at one end and come on at the other. Then the toe would let go, and both hairline shadows would be back in their places on the ice.

But some of the air goes into the water each time, and some comes out of the water lower down. After a number of nights there might be very little left in the wrinkle of the air that was there when the skin opened. What stays is a dry place. Just at the end of the split a flap has curled inward, so that the water coming in has to go round a corner to wet the inside of it. It has never yet gone round. I would be more particularly there, on the underside of the flap: the last of the inside that has not become part of the pool.

For a while nothing would come of this. The animal would put a toe there or not. A thread growing across the skin would move one of the shadows a little farther from the other. Then on a night when the new sheet stood the others up, a grain washed off the thread would lodge under the flap. The water would go in beside it. It would take very little water. All that would be seen from outside would be a bright point drawing itself to the end of the split and vanishing, much as it had done every night before.

This time, when the pool went down, the bright point would not come back. The skin would stay full. It would stand away from the washer by rather less than the breadth of a toe, and in that space a film would last after the scored lines had drained. Coming down the sheet the animal would find a footing where on the previous night its foot had gone through into air. Its toes would part. For a moment the shadow of the empty skin would have a fork at the end of it.

I would have liked to be the air. I find I am more interested in being that much extra room for a foot.
