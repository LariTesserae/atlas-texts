I should look for myself where the seeds have rubbed the polish off each other. There is a pair below the splash mark with a dull crescent on each of their inner faces. One crescent is a little longer than the other. In the damp at the bottom of the longer one there is something like a minute paring of a fingernail. When the seeds grind together it disappears. When they roll apart there are two parings, with a clear strand between them. The foot on the seed bed steps over this strand. Its belly just clears it.

It would be easy to mistake me for one of the necks. I did so myself for the better part of an afternoon. There was a pull, and a long ease, and another pull. I supposed that what I did was hold two things together. But when the smaller seed rolled downhill I went with it entire. The paring on the other seed came away quite cleanly. For a little while I lay on my side with the strand wound up inside me, a comfortable arrangement except that I could not feel anything happening more than one seed away.

Presently a drop fell. In the seam beside me the water stood high enough to wet my upper edge. I unrolled in it. This seems to be how I get about. One edge stays dry and takes a good hold; the other washes loose and goes wherever there is room. By the time the water was back on the tooth I was fast to a seed I had never touched before. It was warm. The warmth had a far edge and a near edge, and between them a little indentation that fitted the ridge in the small animal’s foot. I had got into its first footprint.

Up on the wall a footprint is a thing to see. Down here it is a rather private matter between the underside of a foot and the skin of a seed. I felt the warmth narrow as the animal put its mouth down, then spread as it bore on the foot to pull its mouth free. All this had happened some time ago. The animal itself was on the next seed, having a difficulty. What it ate from the neck came off readily enough, but stuck to the roof of its mouth. It kept lifting its front end and putting it down again. That was the little extra beat in the drop.

When the green shadow came, the seed cooled unevenly. The part I covered stayed warm. The animal turned; to my surprise there it was, standing on me. It had meant to go back to its footprint and I had kept just enough of the footprint for it to come back to. Its weight squeezed a bead of water out of my strand. One mouth found the bead. There was a small working from side to side, and a whitish pellet rolled off and lodged against my dry edge. Then the animal went on its way again, slowly this time, since it had already used up the going back.

I kept the pellet. By morning it had stained me almost the color of the old one under the lip, and the stain had spread out in very fine forks. I suspect I am one of the things that grow here. I have no wish to be dug up to make sure. On a wet afternoon I can reach four seeds now, provided none of them rolls very far. There are little warm places of different ages under my edges. Sometimes the small foot comes down in one, sometimes in another. Once it came down with its mouth open, still trying to drink a bead that had been gone for days.

I like best the interval after the shadow, when the two on the wall have started up again and the one on the floor is still. Its foot is resting half on me and half on the seed. Through the seed comes the working of the thorn, very deep and slow; through the foot, the effort of keeping one mouth shut while the other swallows. Between these pressures my strand flattens until light goes through it green. If you could look under the animal without lifting it, that is where I would be: a little window with damp edges, and the sole of a foot for its entire view.
