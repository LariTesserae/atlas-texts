I think I would find myself at the break in the back of the middle stone. From the bed it would look like a slant in the ceiling. From a little way inside the joint it would look quite different: there would be a pointed piece of daylight at the bottom of it, with something pale lying across it. The pale thing would be the fold of a grass blade. I would know the way the fold had got there. I would have had hold of it once.

I might be the last of the three, though I needn't always have been last. It would depend on which way the mother turned on the shelf. If she turned one way there would be fur at my mouth almost before I was properly awake. If she turned the other there would be the rather hard back of a head, and by the time I had worked along it and found a place to take hold, the others would be going. On one such occasion I took hold of the grass. It came with me very readily for a little way. Then I put a foot on it and pulled the fold tight against my chin. By then I could hear the others at the joint.

The first few times I went out I would have very little notion of a stone having a top. There would be the underside, which I could go along touching with the hair on my back, and the end of the underside, where all at once I could straighten my legs. There would be the damp with the white stuff round it. One side of the damp would come away under a foot; the other would hold. I would get in the habit of crossing a little higher up than the others. A longer animal would have trouble turning there, but there would be room enough for me.

It might be by that route that I came on the hand. Not the whole hand. A little water would have got under the heel of it and run as far as the crease, and there it would stand, quite a steep wall of water to put a mouth to. I would drink with both hind feet still in the joint. A pulse felt through the stone would not send me away; there are movements in the shelf all night when there are four of us on it. What would send me away would be the person leaning over to see. Even before the shadow came there would be a change in the pressure, a grain turning against another grain under the weight of the hand.

Afterwards I would come back to the same place. There would be no hand and no water up there. I would get one forefoot over the edge, then the other, and lie for a moment with my belly on the level place and my hind feet feeling about against the upright face. So that would be the top. A drop falling into the basin would throw several much smaller drops onto it. One would land on my nose.

From the flats there would still be nothing to see. Even someone standing by the farther tuft of grass might miss me altogether, being inclined to look for something in motion. For once I would be quite still. Under my feet, a little to one side of where I had expected it, I could feel one of the others pulling at the grass.
