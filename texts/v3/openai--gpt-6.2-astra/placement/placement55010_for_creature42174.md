I would be under the scale that came down crooked. At its heavy end it sits so close to the wall that even the gray is rubbed off. At the other end there is room to put the tip of a finger underneath. Halfway along, the edge stands off the wall by about the width of one of its own bright fracture hairs. That is where I would look for myself.

At first I would take myself for a patch of the old lining, dark like the rest when the breath comes. But there is a point in the clearing of the wall when a good eye, watching from the blister floor, could see just what is mine. The thumbprint by then is pale. Its little holes have come out distinctly, like the holes in the face of a dry bone. Below it, almost touching it, a curved stroke is still dark. At one end the stroke dwindles gradually to nothing. The other end is cut off square by a chip in the edge of the scale. I have the shape of the draught that gets in there.

I would have very little use for the distinction between being on the scale and being on the wall. A fold goes out from the wall and catches on a tooth of glass overhead. Along the underside of the tooth there is another pore, upside down with respect to the first and quite as good. What I got in a plentiful breath I could spend for a long while there in the dark, laying down a lining on what used to be the inside of a stone. The double knock would come through both my floors. For an instant, if the scale rocked, I would be stretched between them; afterwards there might be a loose pleat, too slight to fall under its own weight. The next breath would darken it on both sides.

There would be no prospect of the common from here. Of all that business of one bright rim after another going out toward the escarpment, what reached me might be a single grain rolling into the square-cut end of the stroke. For a time the gas would divide at that grain. Two little horns would grow where there had been just the one blunt end. The grain might drop out again without taking the horns with it. That much of a distant heave I could keep.

When the wall began to turn, there would be an interval of extraordinary plenty. A pore at the back of the scale, clean since the day the scale split off, would get a whole breath nearly unspent. I would turn down into it. I think that is the part of me I would most particularly be: the new cuff, scarcely deep enough to have an inside, doing just what the older ones had done when there was all the time in the world for it.

The wall would settle without crushing that pore. A little gas caught in the hollow of it would last longer than the gas along the open edge. While it lasted I would go a little farther in. Long after, if the two stones were parted, the crescent would show on both, a duller gray on the scale than on the wall. It would take a fresh break across the back of the scale to show the rest: in the black of that break, just under the surface, a short gray hook with a rounded end.
