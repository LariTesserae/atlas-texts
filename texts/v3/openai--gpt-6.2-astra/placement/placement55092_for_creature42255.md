I would be in the new shade, though not quite under the hair. That place is taken. A little way along the ridge from the young ear there is a clear place where the light could carry an image and does not. I think I would come up there.

At first I would take the hair for something that had grown out of the other animal. From my side one could see the bright edge of its horn, and above that the great copper filament, tapering so gradually that its end was quite lost. I would put up a narrow horn of my own. For a while there would be two trees. Then I would come up another fraction and mine would be gone. There would be a good deal to learn from going down that fraction again.

The part of me doing this would be quite new. Lower down I would already know the underside of the ridge very well: the long easy places where one could spread as fast as the crystals parted, and one difficult knot where it had been necessary to go round a piece of stone about the size of a knee. I would still be going round it. An animal of this kind need not finish one thing in order to begin another. While my ear was up in the first afternoon of the ridge, a portion of me a long way off would be in the act of flattening out my last blunt lip.

The grain there would not be the one I had started with. Mine had been rather angular and had given, at one point in its turn, a remarkably sharp little lift. I could get through quite a length of tight stone on that lift. The grain I took in exchange was almost round. For some time after the meeting I kept turning it a little too far, waiting for the lift, and had to learn the much smaller movement by which its charge could be renewed. There would be no mark on the surface to distinguish it from any other dark spot in a seam. But if one could put a lip to it, it would still turn best the other way.

What I would like, up in the light, would be to do something of the sort again. The means would be rather poor. A horn has no lips. If I went high enough to put its broad end in the focus of my neighbor's ear, I would take away the hair it had come up to see. I think I would do this once by accident. The great hair would vanish for the young animal. In its place there would be a pale field crossed by two or three cloudy lines, the marks where my horn had thickened on coming through the crust. I would draw back. The hair would reappear, a little shorter now, for the leaf would have been unfolding all this time.

Presently a pale field would come across my own sight. Its lines would be quite different. One would fork close to the edge, with a little clear oval caught in the fork. I could hold very still for that. Beneath us, where our films lay near one another on the shoulder of the ridge, there might be a single *tik*, too far from any oil to be heard in the grove.

By the time the other horn went down the leaf tip would have passed us both. We would stay up a while in the vacant light. Then the ridge would take another slow thrust, and the place where we had looked would be inside the stone. Nothing would be crushed. There is room for a creature like that in the closing of a place, as there was room for it in the opening.

That is about where I would be: spread rather farther than I knew beneath the eastern shelf, with a borrowed grain at the bend and a small thickening of horn ready to come out again. On the next strip to receive the sun I would rise a little to one side of the best light. It would leave room for a second ear.
