I would be on the spare wheel for most of the year, going round only when somebody took me off and spun me on a table. I think I would like this more than the driver did. A brass cover with a slight dent in the rim makes a long, increasingly confidential sound just before it settles. It is difficult not to try it again.

There would be a little hill in the middle of me to allow for the end of the axle. When the daughter was small she supposed that whatever made a hill on one side of a piece of metal must make a pit on the other, and this was one of the few things she could establish entirely to her satisfaction while the adults were still getting their coats on. She could put a thumb in the pit and close two fingers over the hill. Later it was by this grip that she carried me to the end of the quay.

For my proper work the dent was of no consequence. There was a spring inside my rim, and so long as the spring took hold, I kept grit out of the greased hole quite as well as a new cover. Set down on the cart lane, however, I bore a little harder to one side. The girl tried me once the other way up. I stood on my hill and there appeared underneath me a dot of white so small that when she picked me up to look for it, she put her thumb over it and it was gone.

I would know the animals somewhat differently from either of the people lying on the stone. What came through the brass was not so much the scrape as the check at the end of it, when the tooth caught for an instant and let go. The two did not eat at the same rate. From time to time one of them stopped altogether. For a surprisingly long while after the father had got up and brushed the fronts of his knees, the girl stayed to find out whether they ever stopped together.

The trouble, when it came to leaving, was that one white foot had taken hold of the dent. The girl had me tilted already before she saw it. She held me there. There was no use asking her father how to get the creature off; all his experience was of getting covers off wheels, a thing best done with a smart blow from below. At last she lowered my edge again, a little farther on this time, into one of the old wheel notches. The animal put out a foot and found stone. For a while it had two feet on each and appeared to find the arrangement quite satisfactory.

We were late getting down. At the shed the driver went about hanging up the harness while his daughter fitted me back over the spare axle. Usually she gave me a smack with the heel of her hand. This time she pressed at intervals all round the rim until the spring clicked home. There was a clean place in the dent, no wider than the nick a file makes when it slips. Her finger found it on the second circuit. Then, since it was dark in the shed and touch by itself is not always quite enough, she put her face down to the wheel and tried it with her lip.
