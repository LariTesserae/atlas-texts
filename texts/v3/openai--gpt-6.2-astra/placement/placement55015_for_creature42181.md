At first I should have been the person with a heel of one hand on the stone. There comes a tick against the bone, quite distinct from the burr. One lifts the hand, looks under it, and puts it down again a little nearer the peg. While one is attending to this the cheese gets warm. It seems a good way of spending a noon.

But I think I should be down on the cold side, one of the white ones on the loose cup. There is a difference between following the pear with an eye and taking hold of the creature that has taken hold of it. I should know even less of the country than the person eating cheese. At any particular time I should know one place very well indeed.

Most of the day this would be the little waist immediately in front of me. It is soft across the middle, with a harder tuck on either side. My foot goes round it as neatly as a twist of paper round a sweet. There is another foot round my waist. What I know of the opening of a mouth in the floor comes to me as a pinch there, sometimes appreciably before I feel any difference in the water on my face. The one behind has caught it first. By the time it gets to me I have generally pinched the one in front, so I should not care to say which of us first knew.

Our cup has one high side. When the high side leads we go along quite peaceably in the hollow of it. When it trails we stream out over the low lip like the gut from a split gooseberry. The one at the front has the lime to hold by, rather rougher than a waist. There is usually a little pink there. When my turn comes I eat what the last holder has missed, which, on a vigorous day, is quite a lot. There is very little worn away from much of holding on. We have all been eating at the same small notch, however, and this afternoon a piece comes out in my foot.

For a while I go on holding it. The other four go on holding me. The cup, relieved of us, tips forward and presents its white bottom, a part of our home none of us has seen. It has two round dents in it. Then it slips beneath a sheet of water and is gone.

We should make an odd mark seen through the window now: five shadows in a curve and a little square shadow at the head of them. I can taste pink as fast as I can eat, and it is some time before it occurs to my foot that the square is doing none of the work of a cup. When I let it go the one behind me comes up against my back. We go head over heels through a cold place. There is a considerable interval in which I have hold of the wrong end of somebody. By the time we strike the roof we are holding one another in a ring.

It is the underside of the warm seat. The green stuff there gives a little when we strike it. One bubble comes loose but does not get away; it goes rolling about inside the five of us. Each time it comes to me it pushes a little collar of water up my face, and for a moment the roof is a prodigious way off, blue in places, with white teeth. I should have liked it to come round again. But the water has nearly done coming up the mountain for the day. The bubble sits still between two of us, growing narrow at the end, and presently slips through.

We settle on the floor near the shallow place. Something pale and very much bigger than any of us is lying in it. Out of the pale surface come the amber feet, opening to their last divisions. They stand in the failing wash with the fine ends turned over. Each holds a round bright drop between the fringes, though all of it is water. It takes me a while to feel what is happening: the water goes in at one side of a foot and comes out at the other so gently that the pink dust has time to settle. There is a little of it everywhere on the floor. What seemed to be drops are clear places.

I should be there, then, for the night, where the clean water comes through. Nobody has to be first. After a while the foot about my middle loosens enough for me to slide along inside it. It is warm this far west. I put the end of my own foot down on the lime, keeping the crook of it where it is.
