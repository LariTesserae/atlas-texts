I should have to find out which end of the bark I was at. The two pebbles need not do the same work. One might hold the strip down and the other merely keep it from slewing round. I would put a finger on each, quite lightly, and wait. It would be an awkward way to kneel. After a while I would press rather harder than I meant to, and the next drop would make a poor sound, like a knock on a door that is already open against somebody’s foot.

At the far end I would find something soft between my finger and the pebble. I would have to take my hand away to let it finish what it had been doing. That would be nearly the end of my being the one with the hand.

There is a little place on the underside of the bark where the stone does not take. The bark bears there when it is wet through. If it dries even as much as bark can dry in that room, it lifts enough to let a film of water under. On putting a finger there you would spoil the place altogether; you would have a finger’s thickness of water, which is another country. It is in the film, or with just a corner of myself in the film, that I think I would be at home.

I would come there in the ordinary way, eating round the pebble. On its upstream side the slip would be long enough to lay down under me. On the other side there would be a bald streak, and going across that I would get a corner caught under the wood. Caught is how it would feel to someone trying to lift me. To me it would be a bit of my edge that had stopped coming along. There need be no hurry about that. The rest of me could get a fair supper before the omission amounted to much.

Then the drop would fall. There would be a nip and a letting go and a little cold water quite far in under me. The part of me that eats would turn towards it. By the time it got there the cold would be gone, but there would be a short combing of food laid the other way. I would take that off. At the next drop there would be a little more. In this way I might be found at the far pebble rather oftener than the size of the pebble would seem to warrant.

I would have no use for the whole interval between drops. There would be no getting ready for the next one. Once, perhaps, two would come almost together, and the bark, on its way up, would meet the second. A larger sip than usual would go under the strip. For some time afterwards I would be spread fairly deep into the hollow, going over a piece of bark that no one had eaten from since it was a tree. It might be poor stuff. It might take me a long time to work my way back by the very narrow route along which a corner of me had gone in so easily.

If in that time you came through the pinch and put out your hand, you might think the pebble had two animals on it. You could touch quite down to stone between the bit that was out and the bit that had begun to come back out. Yet if you gave either one the least pull, after a little the other would shorten. I would like to be something of which that was true, with the pull taking an appreciable time to get from one part to the other and doing no particular harm on the way.

Eventually I would go up the wall with the rest. There would be nothing to mark my place at the pebble but a clean patch underneath, where nobody feeling for it could get a finger. Up on the wall I would acquire a fold made to the measure of somebody else’s fold. A very small one might come loose from me there. It would have no occasion to know about the bark. Its first considerable journey might be over a rim into a pan so small that the water in it scarcely moved. It would spend a good while taking the slip off one bead.

Down at the pebble another loose edge would presently lie where mine had lain. But if you had your hand there at the right time, just as I was leaving, you could tell us apart. The drop would fall and both edges would take their tuck. One would settle back on the bark. Mine, a little short of water now, would settle on the wet back of your finger. You would feel the small hard part pass over a knuckle. It would find nothing there, turn, and pass over the knuckle again. By then I would have enough of myself on the far side to get down.
