On the school table the old instrument has to be propped up at one corner with a folded attendance slip. Otherwise the brass wheel turns of its own accord. It took the teacher some time to discover this. For nearly a term she had been turning it back a little every Friday and wondering which of the children had such a light hand.

I should have liked to be the keeper of the drum. There would be a drawer for the sheets of smoked paper, with tissue between them, and on the end of each sheet a date in a clear hand and a note of where the apparatus had stood. It is agreeable to imagine opening such a drawer. But the interesting part of that instrument is missing. An inch and a half of it went out of the school before the school ever got it, and I think I would have gone out that way too, backward, with a mouthful of felt, having found a use for the afternoon quite different from the one provided.

I would be one of the brown animals. Not the one whose nest came up into the light. I would live a little farther along, where a waste pipe from the works comes through the outer wall and ends in nothing. On the plans it continues to a settling tank. The tank is there, too, and receives most of what comes out of the pipe, but there is room between the two now for an animal to sit on the lip of the tank with its ears erect. In frosty weather the pipe sheds a sleeve of ice which stands in the tank for days, with a round hole all the way down it. I would know the sound of a loose piece going down that hole. I would not know that it was a pipe.

My particular accomplishment would be getting out of my pocket without waking the other two. There is a way to do it. You leave the tail till last. Its withdrawal allows the one whose cheek was on your stomach to settle by degrees, and by the time its whiskers encounter the lining you are well up the branch. Getting back is more troublesome. As soon as the weight comes on the near end of the pocket both sleepers slide into it. On cold mornings one comes home and finds two warm backs quite effectively shutting one out of one’s own good work.

For a considerable part of my life I would have a bad tooth on the left. Not bad enough to prevent my feeding; I would simply start each stalk a little to the right. This would give my patch of grass a recognizable appearance from below. The bitten heads, relieved unequally of their little weights, would incline all one way, like the nap on a piece of corduroy someone has stroked with a thumb. The woman who oils the hinge on the maintenance stair might notice this. She has to wait after oiling it for somebody to come down from above, since it is only under a person’s weight that the pin turns far enough to carry the oil in. There is time to look up. One day she might see the heads begin to jump at the edge of the stroked place and have the satisfaction of knowing just where an ear would come through. I like to think of affording her this small success without ever becoming aware of her.

There would be one alteration to the country that mattered to me. A white limb below the pipe, formerly too far off to be of any account, would in the course of a winter come within reach of the tank. The first time I put out a hand it would have nothing to do with the limb. There would be a moth scale on the rim, or a bead of water, and beyond it, quite unexpectedly, bark. For several nights I might go no farther than to touch it. Then on a wet night, with the grass letting go underfoot, I would go that way because it was the way there was to go.

On the far side of the limb would be a pocket made almost entirely of blue felt, occupied by an old animal with a bare place behind one shoulder. The blue would mean nothing to either of us. The felt, on the other hand, would be of that particularly close sort which hardly stretches even when two animals hang from the same end. There would be room. In the morning the old one would take my jaw to clean it, and on coming to the tender place would let go, and take it again a little farther forward. After that I would cross quite often.

From above, the survey of that winter would show the settling tank and the tree farther apart. This would be perfectly correct. The tank would have gone down and a little to the west, away from the trunk, bringing its rim level with the long end of a sloping branch. On paper there would be a fresh white space between the two black outlines. Down where I lived it would be the winter the way opened.
