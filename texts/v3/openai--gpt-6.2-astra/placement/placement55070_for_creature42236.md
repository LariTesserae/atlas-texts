I would put my hand where the wall drips aloud. I would be a little late. What I want to feel is the skirt coming out of the snails, and by the time there is anything to hear, it has gone. There would be a snail under my thumb, with a clean streak across its shell where something had kept the iron off. For a while that would have to do.

I think I would come to live in one of those streaks. The shell has a long shallow flute in it, a fault from when its edge was soft. Water runs along the flute and spills over the thick end of the snail, which occasionally puts out a horn into its own spill and takes it in again. There is room at the shallow end for six feet. I would have come aboard from a parent in the last of the flood, without making any very consequential decision. Three of my feet would already be aboard before the other three had occasion to decide anything at all.

The snail would be a good place to grow acquainted with the scale of things. There is a little knock below me, for instance, which persists wherever we go. It takes most of a circuit of a cup for me to discover that this is my snail eating. I discover it by getting big enough to put a foot over the forward edge of the shell. A new foot, still rather given to shutting on itself, gets a brief rasping on the sole. All along I have been hearing from quite far away a thing which it is now possible to interrupt.

We spend some time under the cup. It has begun to tilt with the growth of the root that carries it. There is a wet side and a side on which the mat has gone dry and lifts in a little scroll. The snail eats along the wet side. Two of my feet take the scroll. At first it comes with us, unrolling, and then it doesn't, and the snail goes on out from under me. The last I have of it is the flute, running its length under one shut foot like a thread pulled through a stitch.

That is where you would find me for most of the year: on the underside of a cup, engaged in lowering it. Not on purpose. The parts of me on the wet side do well, and there are presently a great many of them. The rim on that side goes down by the thickness of a root hair. A shrimp combing the surface from below comes to the place where the surface used to end and goes on a little farther. In time there is a continuous damp track down the outside, with good eating along both banks, and no further need for me to reach round under the cup. I do it anyhow. I have a fold left over from when it was the only way to get there.

I would like to know what happens to that fold on the open floor. I think it is the last part of me to come unstuck. I have a foot on the hump, the water has got well under me, and still there is this short doubled thickness, the shape of the underside of a cup that is up on the wall a good twenty paces away. The first few shrimp that strike there are knocked clean away. Then one comes hard enough, or at a fortunate slant. Something gives with a small cold lick of water between skin and skin. For the first time in my life I am my full size. The shrimp is gone.

On the way back there would be no finding that particular cup. I might come up by the warm seam instead, where a throng of shells offers almost more places to get hold than I can use. I would go in a little way. One of the feet still out on the floor would have a grain on the back of it, getting less hard by the hour. There is no hurry about peeling up this foot. Above it I am already deep enough in the colony to have stilled a drip; below, where the water is going, there is just time for something very small to walk down off me onto the stone.

I think I would stay there until it came back up. This need not be kindness. The stone is sticky, and I have plenty to do at the other end. But it would come back, having found in the whole width of the hollow nothing else with an edge it could take. I would feel six little pinches, in two lots of three. Then I could lift.
