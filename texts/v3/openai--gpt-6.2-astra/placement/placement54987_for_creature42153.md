I would not be in the deepest part. I would take a few steps that way at first, expecting the sand to grow cool under my feet. When I looked back, the little cluster of leaves at the end of the woody arm would stand out against the plain. Their edges would move and the stone over them would seem perfectly still. I think I would be up there, where some small part of that movement goes on into the stone.

At first I would take myself for one of the grains. I would have a face almost flush with the scar and a long inner face laid against a grain the color of tea. Light coming up off the sand would enter by the small face. A little would escape along the joint. There would be nothing much to show for this on the outside; a dozen grains quite as clear could be picked out with the point of a pin. What would distinguish me would be a green line very close along one side.

But it is the green side I keep coming back to. The light that leaves the grain there does not go much farther. I would be one of the four cells, the one at the turn, partly behind the brown grain. I would have no view through the clear stone. The saltbush could put out leaves, lose them, put out leaves again, all within a few inches of me, and never once would there be a leaf shape in my light.

In dry weather there would be very little of me even for a cell. The coat would fit down over a slight hollow that had not been there when I was full. The other three would be so close that a section cut through us might show four green flecks in what looked like a single drop of hard gum. No part of me would be kept aside to count the dry mornings. If the stone stayed dry for another year there would not be a year's worth of anything inside me.

The change I would have to do with would begin well out on the flat top of the ledge. Rain would darken a broad patch there. Of all that water a little would go down at a fault in the bedding and spread sideways; of what spread sideways, a little would wet the brown grain on its buried face. The first water to reach us would go into the coat. For a while the stone on either side would still have a dry place to it, but there would be room between the four of us. My hollow would fill out. Then the same poor light that had fallen there yesterday would be enough to do something with.

I might divide on the second morning. Of the two that had been me, one would still lie partly behind the brown grain. The other would press a little farther round the turn, where the clear face was broader. I find I cannot put myself in one rather than the other at that point. Both would have the slight crease left by the division. Over both, the roof would be drying from the outside in.

Below us the rain would have pitted the sand as far as the reach of the overhang and stopped there. Someone coming in afterward could put a foot across that line, from the little pits onto the old smooth crust. The place I had gone a little farther into would have no line of that kind. There would be a rounding at the end of a green fleck, on a face of stone that would have to be broken to be seen.
