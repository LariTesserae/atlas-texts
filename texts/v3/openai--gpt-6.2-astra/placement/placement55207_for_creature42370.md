At the third landing I would be the one who had gone down to whiten the bottom step and come back with white knees and the step still to do. It is quite a trick, getting that mark in the right place. From above, where one has to kneel to put it on, the edge is perfectly plain. From the height of a man sitting in a skin boat, the wet stone shows the sky, and so does the water. You can go on seeing sky until you have stove the boat in.

My aunt did the steps before me. She could keep the line in her head while she got out of a boat, tied up, went round behind the shell baskets and came down on her belly with the little pot. I have to leave a finger where I mean to start. I use the middle finger of my left hand, which has rather a long nail for this purpose, and am consequently very easy to do. I have come into the fish market and seen three people at different counters all trying to pick up a fish without disturbing an imaginary spot on the slab.

There is usually a child to hold the pot. One evening it was the girl from the doorstep. She had a flat sandal under her arm and a brown mark across her forehead where her grandmother had pushed the hair out of her eyes with a wet thumb. She wanted a little white on one end of the sandal. I supposed she was making it into a ferry. I put a good round dab at the toe, where a passenger standing on a landing would see it coming, but it appeared the sandal was still required for walking in. It was a landing she wanted on it. Something that had gone off on the sandal might want to come back.

I showed her how much of the dab to wipe away to leave an edge. She did this with the side of her hand. Then she sat down beside me with the sandal on her lap, blowing on it to dry it, although she could see how much good blowing did me with a whole backwater blowing the other way. Her grandmother sat on the next step up, one foot shod and one bare. The shod foot had a row of little pink dents across the instep. The bare foot did not have them any more.

The ferry came in while we were there. Someone on board had bought a great blue basin, too wide to carry under an arm, and was getting off backwards with it held against his stomach. As he came level with us the girl took her grandmother by the chin and turned her face to see. In the bottom of the basin there was a little water, running from side to side, and in the water, right down in the blue, the man’s nose kept appearing and going away again. They waited for it each time. I missed the first few appearances through looking at the step. My aunt would have had the wit to leave her finger where it was and look up.

After the passengers had gone the master put me off a little way in a skin boat to see my work. From out there the white was too high by nearly the breadth of my hand. What showed beautifully was the pale sole of the grandmother’s bare foot. She had stretched her leg out while the child fitted the sandal on again. For once I could see just where the stone ended. I held up a finger and asked her to stay as she was.

That is where I think I would live, within sight of that landing, in one of the houses that gets somebody else’s rain as well as its own. I would have a pot with a lid to it and a good dry niche for the pot, and for all my care there would be white on the backs of my legs and white in my supper. Some mornings before going down I would look out and find a boat already making for the steps. For those few moments I could stay at the window. It is a pleasure peculiar to my trade to see a stranger put out a foot, still looking over his shoulder at the person he has been talking to, and come down exactly where he thinks the ground will be.
