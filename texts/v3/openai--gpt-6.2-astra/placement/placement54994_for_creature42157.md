I would live a little way inside the lintel. There is a strip there which the young polish with their backs as they are scraped off. Just beyond it the rock is rough again. A drop coming in on a young one's back will run as far as the roughness and stop. By the time the mother has got herself turned round there may be a dozen drops there, all trembling at the knocks from outside. In one of them there would be a small clear animal hanging head down. That would be me.

My hind feet would scarcely ever let go. I could reach a good way by letting out a fold in my middle, so that sometimes my front feet would be walking about in the next drop while my hind feet were still in the first. To eat I would have to get a grain of silt between my front feet and roll it past my mouth. There is quite a lot on a grain that has come in from under a salt cake. A grain from the fan outside can be rolled a long time for very little return.

At first I thought I would be one of the young. I liked the idea of going so far at somebody else's expense, and of the moment when the bottom of the blue tent falls away and there is water instead. I think I would in fact get to go once. It would happen on the way *out*, when there ought to be no wet young coming through the opening. One that had got up behind the lintel in the night would feel the others leaving, turn in too small a space, and take my whole drop on the blunt end of its plow. My feet, which hold very well against a pull, would come unstuck one after the other under that slow sideways wipe.

For a while the ride would be remarkably like being at home. There would be a roof close overhead and a thing to hold on to, and the familiar knocking coming through both. Then the mother would lie down. The young one would let go of her, put both plows into the wet cut under the crust, and I would have more room than I had any use for. My manner of getting from place to place, reaching till I touch something and then bringing up my hind feet, does not work particularly well in open water. A pink comma rowing past would turn me over without touching me.

I would come to rest on the underside of the shelf the young were eating out. Above me, just through the salt, their feet would look like little patches of thaw. Everywhere a mouth took hold, a few grains would fall. I could eat there with scarcely any rolling. I might stay through the call and the tumble and the long draw of the water back into the stones. By then there would be a ceiling again within reach of the floor, and a drop with a skin strong enough to hang from. It would take me a while to find out that what I had taken for the floor was a shed lip of crust, quite loose, which would tip if I went too far along it.

That is where I think you would find me: not in the first hollow after all, but under one of the upturned cakes, doing much as I had done in the hollow. Once in a great while the cake would be lifted a second time. For a moment the wind would get at my drop and flatten it, and through its thin side I would have an excellent view of two enormous ivory ends with a face intent between them. Then the cake would go down a little crooked. I would bring up my hind feet. There would be a fresh edge to work.
