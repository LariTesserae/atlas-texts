The shelf is just wide enough for a shed, a pool, and a walk around the pool if you don't mind getting your ankles wet. Beyond the lip there is no more ground for a long way down. On a clear morning you can look over and see the backs of clouds caught on a lesser range, and through a hole in those clouds a brown river with a fleck of sunlight moving on it. Up here it has been morning for an hour. Down on the river the fleck is the first light.

A piece of the summit came away to make this place. The wall at the back is the dark, clean inside of the mountain, with half a vein of white stone running across it at shoulder height. You can put a fingertip into the little sockets where crystals went away with the other half. Water stands in the print of every crystal. It comes out of the wall warm, even in the shade, and spreads down the face in a skin so thin that from a few steps off the rock merely looks polished. At the foot of the wall it gathers into a ditch. Someone long ago laid a plank across the ditch; both ends have disappeared inside cushions of moss, leaving a hand's breadth of sawn wood, green and very slippery, over the running water.

There is a good deal of garden here for a place above the snow. Ferns grow chest high along the ditch. A low tree has laid its trunk almost horizontal under their fronds and sends up a wood of little vertical branches, each with five or six thick, shining leaves. From the shed door it looks as though you could push through those leaves for quite a distance. In fact the farthest are already hanging in empty air. Now and then a wind comes up under them, showing their pale undersides all at once; you feel the cold of it through a wet trouser leg while the rest of you is pleasantly warm.

The shed houses the equalizing tank for the works below. A riveted pipe comes up the cliff and bends through the wall of the shed, so big that the builders had to cut a bite out of the doorframe to get it in. There is one square window. Through it you can see a float riding on dark water and a chain going up from the float to a wheel. Almost nothing appears to move. Yet the noise in this little patch of greenery is tremendous. Miles of water travelling inside iron send up a deep running note; the sheet-metal roof takes it up, and a loose pane adds a thin, insistent zizz. You can quiet the pane by pressing a thumb against one corner. Take it away and the whole chord is there again. Underfoot, through the soft moss, you feel it in the plank.

The pool has no part in the works. It occupies a shallow hollow left by the break, and drains over a notch into space. In the middle it is clear to the bottom, where angular chips of black rock lie exactly as they fell. Around the edges, fine red roots hang in the water, and among them, if you sit still a long while, you may see a single beetle come up carrying a bead of air like a bright pinhead against its belly. Nothing hunts it here. Very little with legs or wings ever finds this shelf. The leaves grow out to their full shape and fall with scarcely a nick in them.

By afternoon the light reaches under the horizontal tree. There are pale rings on its bark where the pool used to stand, before the overflow wore its notch a little deeper. Each ring has its own minute fringe of dried weed. This is the measure of change here: the notch deepens, the roots follow the water down, the moss takes another saw mark from the plank. All the while the mountain seems, from the sound of it, to be engaged in some enormous and immediate piece of work. A drop swells at the point of a fern. For a surprisingly long time you can see in it the square bright window of the shed.
