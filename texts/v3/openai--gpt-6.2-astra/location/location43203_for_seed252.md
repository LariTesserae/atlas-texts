There is a little room under the lava, hardly bigger than a cupped hand. It opened when a blister of gas lifted the skin of a flow and escaped through one corner. The skin stayed up. The floor sank a little farther, leaving a bright black tidemark on the wall, and froze in the act of draining. It still has the drag marks. One could draw a finger along them and feel, down to the last trickle, which way the rock went out.

Most of the room is taken up by hot clear air. There are three pebbles in it, all splinters fallen from the same crack. There is a shelf barely wide enough for a drop of water. The impression of a much larger country comes from the light. It lies on the floor in a broad, nearly white patch, revealing slopes so slight they would otherwise be invisible. The pebbles cast distinct violet-grey shadows. Nothing up there looks like an opening to daylight.

The roof has cooled into a mineral not yet found anywhere else on the flow. Its crystals run all the way through the thickness of the crust. Where their outer ends are hot and their inner ends are wet, light comes out of their sides. A single loose crystal does nothing of the sort. The whole bent sheet has to be there, down to the cramped and wrinkled rock at its edges. Heat goes in over a wide area and some of it comes out here as a very good approximation of noon. There is no joint or fitting in the roof. Here and there a bubble spoiled it while it was setting, and beneath each such place there is a soft round blemish in the light.

Water comes from the rain of the previous night. There is plenty of it in the loose beds above, but the roof admits it by the bead. It seeps along the edges of the crystals and gathers at their tips until the underside of the crust looks grained like the skin of a lemon. Now and then several beads run together. For a moment there is a little upside-down lens with all three pebbles hanging in it, and then the water is on the floor. The sound is small and rather handsome: a tap with a low note under it. After several taps in succession there is a long interval in which even the water already down seems to move on felt.

At the low end of the floor it is too hot for a pool to last. Water fans out among the drag marks and dwindles from the edges inward. Each time it leaves a little chalk there. The white fans are already wide enough to overlap, though the glass beneath them is so new that the finest points on it have not broken off. At the other end, below the crack, the incoming seep keeps one of the splinters cool. A buff smear on its underside is almost the whole pasture of the place. It feeds on what the water brings down in solution. Once, in the thick of the rains, a white mite came through the crack and spent the better part of a day there. The only sign that anything had visited was a clean crescent no bigger than a nail paring. It is slowly going buff again.

By late afternoon the upper rock begins to run short of water. Light goes out by degrees at one edge of the roof and strengthens at the other, where the last of the seep is going. The shadows of the three stones grow long together. For perhaps a minute the low light catches the water on the shelf, which has been receiving spray all day without letting go a single drop. It is a remarkably large drop by then. Its belly touches the floor before its lip lets go of the shelf; it passes down without a sound.
