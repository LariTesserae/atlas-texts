Between the floor of the cold store and the ceiling of its engine room there is a space about the size of a cutlery drawer. It was left around a sliding joint so that the two floors could contract by different amounts. The rest of the building is bolted down hard. Here, twice a night, a piece of ceiling goes a little way east and comes back.

The country inside is mostly grey enamel. There are two low ranges of weld, a stretch of copper mesh folded almost flat, and, at the western end, a stand of green threads growing on the sawn edge of a fibre washer. Nothing is far from anything else. From the tops of the welds, needles of frost reach nearly to the frost coming down. There are places where the points have met and become little pillars with a waist in the middle. Through these woods of clear ice the joint shows as a broad, perfectly straight street. Nothing can establish itself there.

A glass button in the ceiling admits a little light from an indicator upstairs. The button was installed back to front. Nobody on the working side can see whether the current is on, but down here the green threads have had a pale amber summer for eleven years. They occupy less area than a fingernail. At their outer limit you can see each thread separately, bent over the enamel with a drop caught in the crook of it. There are no flies, no grazing mouths. A thread that dies generally remains in place, and the living ones have begun to grow out along the dead ones, well beyond the edge of the washer.

Most of the time the temperature is just below freezing. When a motor starts downstairs, warmth comes in first along one screw. A round patch clears about its head. Then the melt reaches a buried strand of copper and runs sideways under the white surface, so that for a minute or two there is a dark river with a roof. The motor itself is scarcely audible. What you hear is a small domestic sound like a cup being set down: one of the ice pillars has come loose at the foot, and the roof has settled onto the neighbouring pillar.

The river is making the first valley the place has ever had. In the beginning its route was determined entirely by the lay of the mesh. Now, at the turn by the screw, it has lifted a scale of enamel and found the pressed steel underneath. Each thaw leaves a little more red paste there. Each freeze prises the scale a little higher. Already the water takes this turn even when the strand that first led it there is frozen shut. On the downstream side is a fan of rust so fine that a single large drop can rearrange its whole extent. Lately the green threads have been catching some of it. Their ends are weighted down, and where they touch the floor new green has appeared.

Just before the joint moves, the hanging drops all lean the same way. For a few seconds the western hollow deepens; water seeps out of the mesh and collects there, black in the amber light. Then comes the eastward stroke. It is slower than a blink and faster than the water can get clear. The straight street passes under the edge of the pool and carries off a shining skin of it into a part of the joint where no light goes. On the return stroke that skin comes back frozen, scored with parallel lines from the underside of the upper plate. It slides off against the washer. There is a stack of these delicate ruled sheets there now, leaning one against the next, each with a brown line along its foot marking how deep the pool was on the night it was made.
