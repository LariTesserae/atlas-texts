The lowest part of the plain is usually the greenest. A difference in height of two fingerbreadths will do it. On the high side the ground is the color of an unglazed pot; on the low side there is a ribbon of green so dark it looks wet long after the water has gone. The ribbon wanders for miles. Seen end on, it disappears altogether, and there is just a hot buff plain, very slightly tilted, with a pale strip of sky trembling along its edge.

The plain is the roof of a reef. There used to be a shallow sea here, and on its floor grew sheets of blue-green microbes that caught lime in their skins. A skin would harden while a new skin grew above it. Layer by layer they made banks, joined bank to bank, filled the intervals with shelves. When the sea withdrew it left a country already paved. Even the dust is made of little broken bits of pavement. There is rock underneath somewhere, but nowhere in a summer's walking does it come to the surface.

Very little of the roof has broken in. Here and there a round lid has fallen a foot or so, without tipping, leaving a round, flat-bottomed hollow with a sheer lip. A hollow scarcely wider than a dinner plate may have a hundred fine lines visible around its wall. The upper lines are cream, the lower ones reddish brown. Down in the shade of the lip a film of water persists through the hottest hours. There are whole afternoons when that inch of shade is the only cool place for miles.

The living builders are still here. Most of the year they lie dried into black curls at the bottoms of the hollows, with pale mud showing in the gaps between them. After rain the curls soften and spread until their edges touch. In a few days a pool that was clear to the bottom has a fine green nap. Bubbles formed in sunlight catch under the nap and raise it in blisters; at evening the blisters subside. A little of the lime dissolved by the rain stays behind in the skin. Where water returns often enough, the bright, tender rim of a new shelf is growing out over the old one.

There are long distances between the living places. No grass stirs on the intervening ground. No small thing runs out from under a flake when the flake lifts in the heat. The sun is an ordinary white sun, strong enough by midmorning to make the pale ridges painful to look at. A standing person's shadow would be by far the darkest and tallest feature in sight. The surface gives off a faint dry ticking, especially late in the day when the topmost layers have begun to cool and the thickness below is still hot. A single tick carries surprisingly far.

Farther inland the roof rises into low parallel swells. These were the banks on either side of old passages through the reef. Rainwater follows the passages still, but it seldom runs fast enough to cut. It advances in a sheet no deeper than a fingernail, turns a bend half a mile wide, occupies a basin, hesitates at a sill. Sometimes a whole reach of country goes silver with rain that fell beyond the horizon under a cloud no longer visible. The leading edge makes a soft frying sound as water enters the pores and the air comes out.

By late summer the heat rising from the dry banks draws damp air in from the remaining sea. Clouds build over the same broad reach of pavement three or four afternoons in succession. Under their shade the surface turns a soft pink. A downpour erases a few miles of the horizon, restores them, moves on. For weeks afterward one can tell exactly where the shadow passed and where the rain did: the first has left no trace at all, the second a widely scattered crop of green disks, each fitted to its own hollow.

At the seaward margin there is no cliff and scarcely a shore. The shelves run on under an inch of warm brine, then two inches. In calm weather it is possible to see, side by side, the dry hard layers that will carry a person's weight and the soft submerged folds that cannot yet carry their own. Light moves over both in much the same way. Out there the sea is laying down another floor. In here, in a cup of rain on a hundred miles of heated lime, the floor is laying down a little more of itself.
