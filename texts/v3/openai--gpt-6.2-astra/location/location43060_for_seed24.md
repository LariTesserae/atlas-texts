On the flat desert floor a ledge of brown sandstone overhangs a hollow. Its edge stands just high enough to walk under; a little way back the ceiling comes down, and at the rear there is only a seam. From end to end the hollow is about thirty paces long. Spare as the plain is, it seems crowded beside this place. Outside, every pebble has a shadow. Here a stone can lie quite near the mouth and be visible only along one edge, a thin brown lip in the dark.

The opening faces north. Daylight gets in by way of the pale sand outside, and is so weak by the time it reaches the floor that for a while the floor looks level. Then the shapes of low swells become apparent. There is a long one near the left wall, with a point of bedrock showing at its crest. Wind used to get a purchase here. It wore the soft stone out from under the harder cap, and carried the grit away down the plain. Now the ledge extends far enough to turn most of the wind aside. The swells have kept their shapes so long that a little salt has come up through the sand and set their skins. A curl of this crust, lifted at one end, casts almost the only distinct shadow inside.

At the mouth grow three small clumps of saltbush. Their leaves are the dull gray of the sand, except where a leaf has turned over and shows its paler underside. The branches spread mostly out toward the plain. Only one reaches into the hollow, a bare woody length with a few leaves at its far end, still within reach of the sky. Farther in there are neither roots nor fallen leaves. There are no tracks. A single dry seed husk lies caught against the lip of stone, too light to have come in very far.

The darkness suggests coolness which the place does not have. The low roof holds the warmth of the broad slab overhead; the sand underneath is warm too, though no sun falls on it. There is a faint smell of dry leaf at the entrance. Two steps beyond the bushes even that is gone. Occasionally a grain blows in from the plain and strikes the salt crust. The sound is small and definite, like the tip of a fingernail touching an eggshell. Nothing follows it.

Set back in the lowest part of the hollow is a patch of finer sand, reddish rather than gray. Its color is scarcely visible. It comes from a soft band in the roof directly above it, and the patch has the same long narrow shape as the band. What little stone the roof still sheds falls straight down and stays there. In the deepest shade the two are hardly a hand’s breadth apart: the stone above, the same stone in grains below, and between them a narrow space of warm, unmoving air.
