I’d arrange to be at the baths in the late afternoon, when there is still sun on the stairs, and ask the keeper if I could stay until just after the feed is plugged. I wouldn’t ask for the tread to be lifted. The marble is the roof of the place; take it off and you might as well be visiting a tray of wet tiles.

At ordinary size, visit from the bath. Go down the stairs slowly, both for your footing and because the last footfall sets the light moving. Stand a little to one side until the water quiets. You can feel the outlet with the top of a bare foot without putting your toes into the slot. If you want to see in, bring a little waterproof inspection camera with a rounded tip and a wrist strap. Hold it outside the lip, at tile level, with its lamp off. Let someone else descend the stairs while you watch. What feels to them like a perfectly unremarkable step will send sunlight racing across your view.

If your means of crossing worlds also lets you change size, make rather more preparation. **Being small enough to fit is not the same as being equipped to visit.** Come dressed for complete immersion, with your own air, thermal protection, and a way to return that doesn’t depend on swimming out. Don’t count on finding breathing space under the marble. Have the keeper hold the feed closed for the inside part of the visit, after the last hot flush has passed, and arrange for somebody to keep bathers off the stair for those few minutes. A bucket going into the tank is an interesting sound to hear from the camera’s end of things. Beside the pipe it means hot water is already on its way.

There’s no need to go far in. From the mouth, look along the floor rather than straight down at it. That’s how you’ll make out the slight rise of the mortar and the teeth on the two cut edges. Give both a little clearance. Leave the red chip where it is: the clean crescent beside it tells you something you couldn’t learn by picking it up. Bring clean gear with nothing trailing from it, and take care of the green threads on the sill, especially on the way back out when you’ll be looking toward the bright water rather than at what’s beside you.

Save a little time just to look at the far wall. For most of the afternoon there’s little reason to pick one patch of lime out from another. Then the pipe goes quiet, the low sun gets under the lip, and you can see the ridges of a thumb. Someone could once reach that wall as easily as you can reach the bottom of a bowl. It’s worth seeing from below before you go back up and feel, underfoot, how little of the whole place a bather need ever know about.
