Arrange to arrive on the landward side of the works and to leave from there too. The old street is the thing to come and see, not the way to get in. An hour until the tide covers the cobbles is not an hour in which you can safely walk them. One low stretch near the landward end could disappear while you were still quite dry on the pump house step.

Ask the crew which bund, if any, is open to walkers, where you can stand clear of the lorry, and when you need to be back. Check the local tide times, but take the crew’s advice about the pans: two stretches of water with the same rain falling on them can be at quite different levels, and a gate opening a handspan is enough to start a current. Don’t take a shortcut over the white crust or the pan floors. If there’s no agreed walking route, bring binoculars and stay on high ground. You’ll see more from there than you might expect.

Dress for heat first and rain second. A heavy waterproof will be miserable. Take drinking water, a brimmed hat, a light rain shell, and closed shoes with good wet grip; even on the approved path you’re likely to bring salt mud home. Keep your map and phone in a dry pouch, and have fresh water for rinsing glasses, hands, and gear afterward. A little salt dried on a lens will make an already indistinct afternoon harder to see. If you’re staying for the birds at dusk, carry a torch and settle your return route in daylight. The amber lamps mark valves, not a footpath.

Give yourself time to stand still. At first the place may look almost empty. Pick out the two curb lines, then the rectangles on either side; it takes a moment to realize how many front doors once opened onto what is now water. Look through the binoculars at a pale mark on the far curb and wait for it to move. You needn’t go nearer to make the sandpipers rise. They have little enough exposed ground left as it is.

If the crew can take you to the pump house, ask to be there for the turn. From a safe spot on the steps you may feel the motor start through your soles and then see the fish turn, all facing the same way for a minute. It’s a good place to understand what you’re visiting: the doorstep has outlasted the household that wore it hollow, but the works still has a shift to finish.
