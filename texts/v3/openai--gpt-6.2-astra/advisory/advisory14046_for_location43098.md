At the head of the duck stair, ask someone to point out the way to market and the place to wait if the spring goes brown. Get them to show you both while the water is clear. From above, the turf over twelve kitchens looks much like the bank beside it. A goat can make that distinction at a trot; you should take the lane.

Dress for a cool day of getting wet by inches. A short rain jacket over a warm layer will serve you better than a sweeping cloak. Wear shoes with good wet grip that you can slip off at a door, and keep a pair of dry socks wrapped in the middle of a small pack. Keep the pack close and your hands free. Split bamboo laid crosswise makes quite a good road until your foot comes down lengthwise on one wet piece. At the grey hump by the market, give the bracken and the mule the right of way. Wait till the mule is round the turn before you go on.

The duck stair is easy on the knees, but nobody on it has much choice about speed. If the hurdle begins to heave before you start down, stay at the top. If you're already on the stair, look to the person with the eggs: where she stops, you can stop. Stand still, keep your bag out of her way, and let the noise go by. There's no need to hurry the little drake. He's got something to do there too.

Take the call about brown water seriously even if you're standing in sunshine. Rain over the far ridge can put a stream down the stair without putting a drop on your face. Leave the treads and the landings for the place you were shown. Keep clear of the pegs and screens; what looks like a convenient handhold may be the thing the crab seller needs to reach without looking. Don't judge the strength of the stream by the fact that the shops are still open. The shops can *stay* open because the stream gets the whole stair. Let someone who knows the footing go first when it clears.

Leave yourself time for noon in the market. Order something to eat and listen for the little sounds that come up when the grain stamps stop. If you have a question for a stallholder, ask it then; otherwise you'll keep losing the same syllable to the mortar and they'll keep politely waiting for you to finish. Ask where to fill your drinking bottle rather than dipping it into the nearest runnel. Buy a pear if you want one. Picking one off a house is a remarkably effective way of announcing to the family exactly what you're doing.

If you're staying the night, find your door in daylight and learn it from the lane, not from the shape of its roof. Ask where to hang your wet things. Put the dry socks on then. For a while, lying still, you may think the frogs under the wall will keep you awake all night. Then a few will fall silent, a few more farther off will follow, and you'll realize you can hear somebody nearly home.
