Prepare for a cold ridge crossing, and make the visit to the pool optional. I’d aim to be in the notch when the sun first reaches along it. The western faces will be changing from frost to drops, and for a few minutes you can watch what the maker of the thumb-wide groove accomplished: water that would have gone over the eastern cliff turns back and finds the six stones. That is worth seeing even if you never get close enough to kneel. Have a firm time to start down, with a margin before the fins shade one another. A dry-looking way in can be a glazed way out.

Before ascending, confirm that the air is breathable at ridge height and that you have a known route off the mountain. The clear sky over the eastern cloud deck tells you little about whether you could descend through it. Go with a companion who knows the approach if you can, and leave your route and return time with someone below. Wear boots with good wet-rock grip, close-fitting gloves that leave you able to feel the stone, and layers for a windy stop as well as a sweaty climb. Short gaiters will keep the western mist out of your socks. Bring glacier glasses for the glare, a headlamp for a delayed descent, and enough food and drinking water to get back without using the pool.

Up there, choose your way by looking at the **bases** of the fins. A passage can admit your shoulders quite comfortably and then wedge your boot just as you try to turn. Take the wider way round, even for a view that's only a few feet away. Rounded tops look made for hands, but check the footing before reaching for one; a good grip is little use if the stone itself sheds a layer. Don't step on the upright scales to bridge a slot. Keep well back from either lip, particularly where the mist hides the meeting of floor and air.

Treat the catchment as something still in use. Keep feet out of the little threads, leave the groove and fitted stones alone, and don't wash anything there. Clear meltwater on a lifeless-looking ridge is not necessarily safe for a traveler to drink. If the leaning slab looks sound and the knee place is dry, that low view of reflected sky may be the best view on the ridge. If there are fresh pale flakes underneath, or you hear the sharp slip of stone from above, stay out. You can stand a little way back, be quiet, and listen for the softer, wooden knocks of drops under the roof. Leave while those are still the sounds you hear.
