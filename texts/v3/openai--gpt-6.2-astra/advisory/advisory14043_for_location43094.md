Find out first what your passage does about size. The socket will admit a hand, but a hand in it would be rather like a boat in somebody’s front room. If you arrive at your usual size, arrange to look in from the channel. A small underwater camera held clear of the lip will do. There is no low tide at which you can walk down for a better look. Get the harbourmaster’s advice about traffic, and don’t put yourself between a moored hull and the quay, however quiet the boat seems.

If you can travel small, make the return passage ready before you go. Choose a landing place in open water just outside the weed, and a return that does not depend on finding the same gap in a leaf a second time. You will need your own air, some warmth, and enough control of your buoyancy to stay off both floor and ceiling. Try the equipment in ordinary water first. If holding still involves a continual little jet of water behind you, stay outside. It matters where that water goes.

A dim, shaded lamp is worth bringing. Shine it along the wall rather than straight into the hole. The pale tooth of mortar is a better landmark than the weed, and in side light you may see the slant of the grains under the long blade. Don’t take that slant for a direction out. A harbour tide table is useful for planning your approach; inside, it can’t even tell you which way two things a blade’s thickness apart are being pulled. Give the folds room. If you hear the little tick, wait where you are until you can see the edges again.

Allow much longer for looking than for getting there. One of the best prospects is through a half-moon the white animal has already eaten. If there isn’t one, don’t cut one. There’s also a moment after a boat settles against the wall when the leaves ease apart of themselves. From a safe place you can see the silt come up and, through it, perhaps the green stone among the white ones. That moment is for looking, not for slipping through.

I wouldn’t try to reach the hoof water. Its cold is one of the things you’ve come to visit, and a warm finger put there to feel it would make a poor visit for the next person. Watch the ends of the threads instead. And leave the pointed chip leaning, even if you could set it straight without hurting the pelt. When it does fall, something that has always had a ceiling of weed will have a ceiling of brick. It’s worth coming back for that.
