Early in the morning there appears on the pink shoulder the shadow of a foot. It is rather a wide foot for so little stone. Three toes go over the edge, and the fourth, which is short and set well back on the heel, rests in a nick too small to be seen except when the toe takes the sun out of it. There is no leg to the shadow. At about the time the sun looks into the hollow, the owner of the foot begins to be visible among the old root hairs at the far end of the green curve.

The hairs part. A blunt head comes through, with one of the hairs bent across its forehead; the head goes on a little way, and the hair slips off and stands up straight again. For a while the impression is of a very small animal pushing a very large animal ahead of it. The head is scarcely bigger than a grain of the yellow meal in the leaves, whereas what follows fills up the whole parting, from the floor of the hollow to the tips of the hairs. It is a loose transparent sac, exquisitely pleated where it joins the neck. The pink shoulder seen through its two walls looks as near and large as a knuckle. It takes some looking to make out the little occupied part of the sac, low down, a warm brown twist with a pale end doubled under. The foot belongs to this end.

The animal lives on what remains in a root hair after the root has moved on. It does not bite. It puts its mouth to the cut end and rolls the hair up against its palate, like a man getting the last of the sweetness out of a grass stem. A hair which has been gone over in this way still stands quite stiff, but its color is gone. There is a white patch coming in the green curve, no wider at present than the notch in the leaf shadow. The animal has been feeding there for most of its life.

It has a particular care of the sac. If the skin catches on anything the head at once goes back to it. Two pads under the chin come together on the caught fold and draw it free, with a movement much too practiced to be a struggle. In the folds, near the rear, there are some three dozen little dark bars. They have been taken for markings on the skin. In the direct light one of them can be seen to turn over, put out four toes, and settle down the other way. The sac is full of young.

There is room for them in there because they have very little thickness yet. On the side toward the mouth of the hollow each is quite finished, down to the division between the pads of its chin. On the side toward the mountain there is only a line, very bright in some positions, like the edge of a spoon. They spend a good part of the afternoon with their finished sides laid against the skin. A loose gathering of small faces then looks out into the hollow, all at different heights, one far up in a fold looking straight down on its mother. When the direct sun is cut off they lose interest and drift apart.

One is thicker than the others. The sac sags under it. For several days the mother has been bringing it as far as she can bring it toward the shoulder, without setting foot on the bare part of the floor. She has no more purchase there than she would have on a polished nail. She grips the last root hair with her mouth, lets the brown part of her body fall backward, and unpleats the sac to its full extent. This is a considerable distance. Its farthest fold just grazes the burr of the lowest flake.

Yesterday, at this touch, the young one put its hand out. It was the hand on the mountain side, a new possession, and for a moment it fumbled at the skin as though the skin had been put there to deprive it of the use of its fingers. Then the fold ran out between them. The youngster slipped through after its hand and came to rest on the foil. It is there now. What appeared from below to be a little thickening of the burr is its chin, laid very flat. The toes, overhanging, are spread in precisely the disposition of the shadow on the pink stone.

The mother has gone back among the hairs. In the light which is the color of a butter bean she is very hard to see, but a clean hair springs up now and then, and in the white patch there is just beginning to be a place of shelter for her, an undercut with a roof of pale stone held up by all those fine pillars. Out on the flake the young one is asleep. From time to time the short toe on its heel curls in, and down on the shoulder a pinpoint of sun comes on in the nick and goes out again.
