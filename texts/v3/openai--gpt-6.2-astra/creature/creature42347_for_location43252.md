In the square at the bottom there is one corner that will not fill. A little pink stuff collects there, shivers with the rest, and comes out again in a puff. This happens at considerable intervals. You can be looking straight at it and put it down to the pump. But the pump goes on turning under several minutes of perfectly settled pink, and when the next puff does come it goes sideways first, along the bottom, before it rises.

There is a shrimp in there, small enough to turn round in the impression of the bolt. The hollow under one edge of the impression goes back a little way into the bank. The shrimp lies in it with its tail to the light. What shows, on a good look, is a bent bit of clear shell with three grains of silt on it, the middle grain redder and bigger than the other two. There is a set of little paddles working underneath. When the silt comes too high against its back the shrimp straightens with a kick and the three grains go up separately, the big one hardly going up at all.

It came out with the water. There are others in the wet packing under the road, where the stones touch at their points and leave quite considerable rooms between. It has never come through that narrow place again, though it spends much of the hot part of the day trying. Its feelers will go in there their whole length. So will its first few pairs of legs; then comes the high part of the back, and the shrimp has to ease itself out, taking particular care with the legs. Sometimes it brings out a pale thread of something which it sits and eats, passing it rapidly through its front feet as a woman might run a length of mending wool through her fingers to find the knot.

When the bank shades the pool it comes out backward and tips over on its side. That is how it travels. It goes by short takes over the sand, with a pause to get purchase on each new bit of bottom. At the foot of the steps there is, by this hour, sufficient depth of water for it to get onto the green nap. Seen against the green it is quite a substantial animal. There are little charcoal marks where the eyes are and a brown dash where the last meal is going through; the rest takes the color of whatever is behind it, except at the joints, which catch the remaining light. It works along the lower edge of the nap, drawing one tuft after another down to its mouth and letting it spring up a little shorter.

It does not go as far as the porcelain. A long way short of that shelf the water thins to where its upper side would be in air, and there it turns, quite regularly, at a line which moves a little higher up the stone every evening. If the pump stops while it is out feeding it leaves off at once. For a second or two in the quiet you can see all the separate feet held still against the black. Then one of the feelers begins to sweep again, touches the cropped place, passes over it, and finds a fresh tuft a hair’s breadth farther on.
