In the third sheet from the washer there is a clear place in the brown line. Something lay across the line while it was being laid down. The gap is about as wide as one of the green threads, but instead of the clean edges a thread would have left, it has two little banks of brown pushed up at either side.

The creature that made it is still there, though seldom as far out as the third sheet. When the water goes out of the pool it goes in between the first sheet and the washer. A little water stays there however far the hollow tips. In this water the creature draws itself together until it is nearly round. There is one grain of rust in it which, seen through its folded body, looks like several grains laid out in a curve. It can pass most of a night in that condition.

With the warmth from the screw the sheets loosen against one another. The first sign of the creature stirring is that the grains become one. Then a blunt end comes out from behind the washer and fastens to the ice. The rest of the body comes after it in a rush, like a sleeve pulled right side out. What looked quite smooth when it was folded has rings now, very close at the narrow end and farther apart towards the middle, where there is room enough inside for two little hard parts to work against each other. It has a foot that divides. One toe can stay on the washer while the other feels over the gap for the first sheet. Once both toes have taken hold, the blunt end lets go, opens, and becomes a pair of almost invisible wheels.

There is no room to turn a wheel of any size here. What goes round is a shiver along the edges of two wet lips. A brown speck draws near, shoots away, draws near again; a clear speck beside it takes a shorter course and disappears between the lips. For a while the two hard parts in the middle go busily up and down. The green threads are quite safe. The creature couldn't take in even the fine end of one. What it lives on comes off the dead lengths when they are wetted. Under the amber button a newly wetted thread has a soft outline for a moment, then a sharp one. The creature does well in the interval.

It used to do rather less well. There was a time when it had to cross the enamel to get from the last wet thread to the water that would keep through the freeze. On a flat surface it goes by bringing its foot up almost to its mouth, fixing the toes, and putting its mouth down again a body length ahead. Fast as it did this, the edge of the water sometimes went faster. There are two small husks on the enamel from that time. Neither is quite where the animal that left it stopped. A drop picked one up, carried it the length of the mesh and set it down with the divided foot pointing home.

Now the loaded ends of the threads lie against the ruled ice. The creature can feed with its foot in shelter. There is usually only one to be seen, but in the seam where the first sheet has frozen to the washer are three oval bodies with thick, puckered skins. One has been there long enough for green to have grown over it. One is empty, split neatly down a side; it lets the amber through and casts two hairline shadows instead of one. The third was put there at the last thaw. For a considerable part of that thaw the animal was occupied in getting its foot free of the soft skin, which clung between the toes and stretched when it pulled. It had barely got back into the seam when the sheets knocked together.

At the next movement of the joint a fresh sheet will come up against the others. For a second the whole stack will stand a little straighter. Water squeezed from the seams will rise in the lines scored on the ice, higher than the pool itself has ever stood. It is up one of these lines that the creature occasionally goes, foot over mouth, until it is above the ends of the green threads, above every brown mark on the sheets. There is nothing to eat up there. It puts out its lips all the same. They work for a moment in a bead of water so small that the current they make goes all the way round the bead and stirs the fine hairs on its own back.
