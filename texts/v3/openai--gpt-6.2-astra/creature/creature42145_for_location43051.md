What lives there has got no farther than the sill. There are two green threads, certainly, and what looks like a third when the light comes straight in. The third is the loose end of the longer of the two. It has caught on a roughness in the clay and made a loop so fine that a grain of lime, going through it, opens it appreciably. There is no little head at either end. The green is in the thread itself, all the way down to the small, rather blunt patch by which it holds to the stone.

For much of the day the two lie nearly flat, pointing out into the bath. One might take them for a bit of frayed green stuff off a bather’s hem. But a snag of wool would gather dirt. These have a clean new length at their tips, paler than the rest, which was not there when the keeper first let water under the tread. In those days there were two green stops on the sill, as if someone marking the levels had touched it twice with the point of a brush. Now the longer one can reach out past the shadow of the marble even when the sun is high.

The two patches are scarcely a thumb’s breadth apart, but it makes a difference which one a thread grows from. The short one is in the warm run. When the bucket goes in overhead it is pressed so close to the sill that for a moment there is nothing to see but its root. The other is just where the water turns. Its loop lifts clear of the clay, widens, and hangs out into the blue. As the push eases, the loop comes back through the slot end first, with the pretty, quite unintentional action of something threading a needle. It generally catches again on the same rough place.

Nothing comes up from the back to feed on either of them. The little toothed crevice between the tiles is still as clean as the day the two pieces were fitted. Even the thumbprint, which in the evening looks deep enough to shelter a row of minute sleepers, has only water in it. The green keeps to that narrow strip where there is sun enough and something firm to hold.

When the plug goes in, the threads take a while to come to rest. A little water is still finding its way in at one end of the sill and out at the other. Finally the loop slips from its catch and lies open on the cream tile. In the last level light one can see its shadow separately from it, a hair’s breadth farther in. That is the farthest anything living has reached under the stair, unless one counts the shadow.
