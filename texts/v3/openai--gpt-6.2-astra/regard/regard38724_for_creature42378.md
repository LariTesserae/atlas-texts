For some time the power took the green line around the pin for writing. It had seen writing in less likely places. It waited for the line to be finished, and in this way, which was as good as any other, it came to know the hours of the basin.

There was a danger to the basin that could not be seen from below. On the western face of the tooth a small flake stood out from the glass. In the afternoon its shadow reached almost to the lip. It was coming loose. The hollow behind it was quite dry, and when the flake fell it would leave a shallow cup, admirably placed to catch the first snow. The tooth could rock a long time with air behind it. With ice in that cup it would rock once, in the other direction. From the steps to the exhaust flue the roof would come down by about the height of a drain cover. In most parts of the city this would do no great harm. Under the pivot it was too much.

The power could have taken the tooth in hand. There was even a position, a little to the east of its present one, in which the roof would have rested on sound rock at three points and stood fast for ages. It considered this position through a whole afternoon. The blade of light would not have entered. More to the purpose, the night air would have come in at the thick end of the glass instead of the thin one. The drop would still have formed, but a door in the upper street would have had the benefit of it.

It waited until the flake fell. This happened in hot weather. There was no call for haste; for eleven days the cup held nothing but the shadow of its own rim.

On the twelfth day a little cloud went over the peak. The power let most of it go by. Of the part that touched the glass it kept enough to fill the cup, and with the water it learned where the bottom was. This was not where one would have put it by eye. There was a lip no higher than a salt crescent, below which a drop could have run free over the outside of the tooth. The power took the lip off. The water went down the western face in two fine lines which joined again near the foot. It did not reach the city.

The piece removed was black on one side, gray on the other, and rather handsome. The power laid it well down the mountain among pieces its own size. On its return it found the basin tipped and the keeper sitting out on the knob of water at the last niche. It had gone a little farther over than usual. The blue chip showed distinctly through its middle. One edge of the keeper kept opening toward the rim, missing it, and closing against the other edge with a small, wasteful precision.

It would have been an easy matter to put a corner of something there. The power was looking about for a suitable corner when the knob shrank and the keeper got off. For the rest of that night the power stayed out of the market. It was beginning to have some notion of the scale on which it could be useful.

In the first snow the cup on the summit filled level with its rim. Toward noon a dark thread appeared in the white, and a drop came down the western face, followed at a long interval by another. Under the tooth the daylight crossed the square. The bowl swung level a little late that day, on account of the cold.

There have been heavier snows since. In spring, from a certain distance down the mountain, it is possible to see a narrow polish on the summit glass, like the place on a handrail where a thumb goes. This is all the work the power has done there. It still comes up sometimes in the afternoon. It likes to be at the lip when the light goes in, for the chip is a very good blue and there is hardly time to see it.
