The power had been going to put its hand over the pool to see the husks. It saw the shadow of its fingers fall across the midge on its way home and drew its hand back. There was light under the trees for a little longer. The midge went in by a different way from the one by which it had come out.

For three days the power kept still. On the second day a drop came off the bent twig and the passage under it was open again. On the third the bird came back with a berry stain on its white feather. Somewhere else it had been quite a different size of bird, a bird that could reach round a berry and pull it off its stalk. It stood for a while on the sill wiping first one side of its bill and then the other. A midge went by behind it with its long hind legs trailing in the water. There was no occasion to tell either of them.

On the fourth day, when the cloud had gone through, there was still a sound in one of the streets. It was a dry ticking well down in the seam. The power put its ear to the floor. A little way below, where the crack narrowed and turned, there was a feather laid across it, a long one out of a wing. How long it had been there was hard to say. Grit had come down against it and made a sort of sloping floor. A bird was standing on this, making an effort every so often to open its wings. Its quills struck the walls on either side. Between efforts it was perfectly quiet. From the square you could see the white place under its bill, if you knew where to look.

The power considered lifting it out. There was scarcely room to get hold of it without pressing on the folded wings. Instead it slid one finger down the seam, nail uppermost, until the edge of the nail rested on the grit. For some time nothing happened. A grain rolled down the nail and the bird picked it up and let it go. Then it put one foot on the finger. The claws were cold and took hold much further round than the power would have supposed from the marks they made on water. It waited for the other foot.

The bird walked up as far as the first knuckle and flew the rest of the way. It landed badly on the sill of the willow yard, with one wing out, and had to climb up out of the trees. By the time it had got itself in order there were drops all down its breast. It ate something off a bud. Then it went to the foot of the wall and put its wet feathers in the sun.

There was a temptation to close the seam. Looking down it again the power could see, on the little slope of grit, two clear wings, a willow scale, and something with a great many legs going about its business. It took its finger away. For the rest of that afternoon there was a narrow clean place in the street where the side of the finger had lain. At one end of it, in the damp, you could see the lines of the skin. The next cloud took them.
