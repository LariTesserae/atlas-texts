The power came to the blister while the upper edge of the gray was still pale. It could have raised the wall and looked at the blind ends of all the linings at once. Instead it waited for the breath to finish passing. The last place to darken was not at the upper edge after all. It was a dot quite far down, behind a grain that stood out a little from the wall. A film ran to it from above. The dot took its share on the way back.

There were other blisters on the common. In one, the gas came as often as it did here, but came down the wall. In another there was a very good ledge and nothing under it but a polish left by the lost crown. The power found no gray in any of them. It returned to the heap of scales and attended to the loose grains. On one the lining had turned a little way round onto the underside. There it had the same fine edge as a growing fold on the wall. It had come loose a long time ago, to judge by the scales that had fallen across it since.

When the double knock came, the power let the wall go over. To keep it standing would have meant holding it by more and more of its length as the stone it stood on went elsewhere. Already one end had begun to ride up and the other was in shadow to the height of the lowest ledge. The loose grains slid a short distance. The wall came down on the stout scales almost as gently as a cover put on a dish.

There was still a little warmth to choose between the two ends of the fallen wall. The power took a sliver from the heap and laid it with its point in the join at the warmer end. At the other end it brushed away some chips. There was no perceptible draught at either opening. But when the next sulfurous breath went by, the little gray dot behind the grain darkened, this time before the rest. The breath had come in from the other side.

For a long while that was all the power did. The mountain made a low mound where the blister had been. Under it the gray spread onto two of the scales that carried the weight. When one of these split, there was gray on both pieces. The outer piece, which had scarcely more than a spot on it, came out from under the wall in the course of a heave and lay loose on the pavement.

This the power picked up. It took it to the blister where the gas came down instead of up, and set it under the ledge there, with the spot uppermost. Then it went away across the common. The small weight it had carried had left no impression on the stone.

In the new recess the spot did not widen for a long time. The breaths reached it but did not linger. A little below it, however, there was a notch where the edge of the scale nearly met the blister wall. Gas stayed there somewhat longer. A fold, too thin to alter the shine of the scale, came down as far as the notch. At the next breath a pore in the wall darkened. For some time after that, if there had been light enough to see by, there would have been two gray dots with black between them.
