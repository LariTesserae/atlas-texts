Above the salt there is a power that takes an interest in small differences of level. It can tip a whole reach of country without spilling the water out of a hoofprint. Ordinarily its work is so gradual that a river has time to move its bed as it goes, and an old fish need never leave the bend in which it was young.

It came to notice the town by way of the hare. There had been rain a long way off. Each time the hare took its chin off the rim to crop the grass, it turned one ear toward a part of the flat where there was nothing to hear yet. The power, attending in that direction, felt the weight of a sheet of water coming. It was shallow enough for the hare to stand in. On such level ground this did not mean that there was very little of it. It extended farther on either side than the hare could have run in a morning.

There had been water of this kind before. On the outside of the swelling were several old watermarks, the highest well below the lip. But the salt on one side of the plant had been rising for years. A skin of it, no thicker than the skin on boiled milk, lifted in the heat each day, and loose grains blew under it and would not let it down again. It had risen across the way the water used to go. The first little brown tongue came up against this rise, tried along it in both directions, and stood still. Another came up behind.

The power had only to put a finger under the plant to lift it clear. It put one there. Through the pad of the finger it could feel the three flies at the bottom, each making an impression with five feet, the sixth being otherwise employed. Farther up there was a small hard weight moving on a shelf at every pass of a softer, heavier weight. There were even impressions of weight where nothing now rested. To lift the plant on that finger would have been to turn the square inside out.

It took the finger away and went a little distance off across the flat, to where the old way out ran under the raised salt. Here it let down a strip of ground about as wide as the hare is long. A thin piece of crust settled upon another with a noise like the closing of a biscuit tin. The dirty water went through at a good pace, carrying little rafts of salt on it, quite different from the rafts below. By the time the main weight of the water arrived it had cut its own way wide enough. A little went over the green rim. It took a loose white cuff the length of a street and deposited it on the nose of a dun animal which had been waiting most of the day at an unproductive hole. For some time afterward this animal could be seen attending there with considerable conviction.

The power stayed to find out what the noise of the two pieces of salt would do. It had been a surprisingly sharp noise. The hare had gone off at once, showing the white undersides of its feet. Below, in the yellow street, a moth half opened its wings and shut them again. Nothing much else happened that day.

It was well on into the next afternoon when the noise arrived in the sleeping shelves. It had lost its sharpness on the way. There was a quite small *tuck*, and after an interval another, as the lower piece of salt took the weight. All along the street the stitches came out. Snub noses appeared over the edge, turned first one way and then the other, and lifted expectantly toward the grub holes. One of the animals had come out too fast and was wearing its black youngster across its nose. It stood a little apart from the others, breathing through the child's pink tail.

The power might have stayed forever distinguishing one thing from another in that place. But down the new watercourse there was a hare sitting on a patch of dry salt no bigger than its haunches, considering a jump; and beyond the hare there was more country. It went on.

Water still takes the lowered way in wet years. In dry ones the only sign of anything having been done is a white step out on the flat, not very convenient for a hare, which can clear it without noticing, but of precisely the height to take the weight of a dun animal's hindquarters. Occasionally one comes out there after drinking. It sits on the step like a person having a boot fitted, with one full hind leg stretched out in the last of the sun. Behind it, all the way back to the green lip, are paired wet marks, getting farther apart as it learns to walk at its evening size.
