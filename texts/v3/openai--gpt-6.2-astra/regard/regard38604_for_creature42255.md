The hair is not quite straight. Near the end it has a little elbow, and beyond the elbow the copper gives way to a color so pale that it is principally by the absence of the copper that one sees it. Someone else is looking now. This other can see the hair from the root and from the tip, and can see, through the stone, the delicate change of tension that goes all the way down the young animal when the elbow comes clear. It takes some care on the part of this other to look from the ear alone. From there the elbow comes as a surprise.

It would be possible to keep the hair there. The leaf could go on unfolding, the ridge rising, and the little image stay whole at the focus without so much as a check in the turning of a spindle. But it is the animal that has got itself into position for this particular sight. Even now it is doing something rather difficult with the base of the horn in order to follow the hair a little farther. The other lets it go as far as it can. The elbow goes out, the straight part slips after it, and the last of the copper is gone.

The ear stays up. In the empty place, well to one side of where the hair has been, the other puts the end of a finger.

It is a very small end for so considerable a finger. There are two or three whorls on it. Across one whorl is an oblique nick, quite healed. The young animal brings the whorls into focus with such dispatch that the other, who has taken a great deal of trouble not to startle it, nearly takes the finger away in surprise. Then the horn goes down flush with the plain. The finger moves off. A little farther along the strip of new stone the horn comes up again.

This time the other waits. The horn leans over until the edge of the finger, which is resting some way off on the plain, comes into view. There is a prolonged inspection of the nick. It is a mark such as does not occur on the shelf, where a damaged surface is taken back along with the damage and comes up elsewhere perfectly entire. The horn goes quite round it. Presently the other turns the finger over to show the nail, but the horn has gone in. For the remainder of the afternoon it does not come out.

It is several days before there is anything in the least like a return for this visit. The other is at the corner of the cliff and the lower country, looking up. From here it is possible to see the underside of the oil drop, a view not available on the plain. A little way below the spindle something is working out through the cream-colored wall. It is blunt and has several divisions; it opens against the air and shuts again on a dark grain. The other knows the grain. It has lain at the bend on the eastern shelf since before the ridge began to rise. A considerable length of membrane has had to be gathered in to bring it here.

The blunt lips hold it out again. They are old parts, though they have scarcely been used and the rest of the animal is young. The other puts out the little finger with the nick on it. The grain is laid against the whorl. For a moment nothing happens. Then, remembering what the wandering animals do when they meet, the other rolls it a quarter turn.

There ought to be a second grain. There are any number of things the other could give, some of them very fine, but for this purpose there is wanted a thing that has been carried about. After a little consideration the other puts a thumb against the nick. Out of the old cut comes an exceedingly small chip of blue stone. It was got there a long time ago in a country whose rock does not mend. The lips receive it easily. Deep in the cliff, under the fifth leaf of the grove, there is a *tik*.

They turn the two stones for a while. The blue one has no useful charge at all. When they part the animal takes its own grain back, and the other presses the chip into its old place. There is no occasion to improve either stone.

Up on the high plain the new shade has broadened. A whole rank of copper hairs is passing through the place where the young horn first stood; nothing is there to see them, and they pass quite as finely as the first one did. The other finds a comfortable place on the lower ground. It is pleasant there, in the sun, and from time to time, along what appeared from above to be an absolutely clean inside corner, something small is held out to be looked at.
