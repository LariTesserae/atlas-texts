Before the water had stood on edge, people had come up to the landing with pots. One of them had been in the habit of laying two rushes across the mouth of her pot. They did a little toward keeping the water from slopping out on the way down. On the last morning she came up, she had cut three. The third was still tucked through her belt when she got home. It was by way of this quite unimportant thing that the power found the lake again. For years afterward, whenever the woman opened the chest where she kept her belt, there had been a smell of cut rush.

The power came up the old way from the valley. There were places where a foot went on expecting ground a little after it had left it. At the notch it knelt. It had some thought of putting the water back as it had been. It could remember the taste of it out of a pot, and the two cool stripes the rushes left across an upper lip.

From down by its knee came a small, quite distinct tick. In the general din it would have been impossible to hear it, but it came up through the bone. One of the pink little animals had got hold of a grain too big to graze and was knocking it against the underside of the course. Presently the grain broke. A piece of it fell away and a piece went in, and the little animal backed up against the old one, pinker by that much.

The power remained on its knee through several changes of the water. Once it saw the long-legged thing let itself down its two threads into what was, for the time being, the sky. At the next deep blow the inhabited seam lay farther down than the bottom of the lake had been in the days of the pots. A level lake would have drowned it. The power took its hand off the notch. There was a warm hollow in its palm from having had the stone in it so long.

Late in the day there was a wash that took all four animals out together. Three went by bright underneath. The fourth went by dark, bumped the end of a slab and opened out, although there was no floor there yet. Its five good fringes moved in the water. With the bald foot it felt along the slab, from which it was slowly being drawn away.

The power put the tip of one finger there. It had been out in the wind and there was a little air in the whorls of the skin. The old animal felt the ridge of one of these with its bad foot. It went round the end of the finger to the nail, where there was both air and something to get under. For a while the power could feel a minute, uneven rasping. It was being grazed.

It sat back on the landing with its hand still in the lake. When the hollow came that uncovered the finger, the animal did not get off. There was good feeding along the nail, especially on the side toward the thumb. The power had not known this about itself. It let another hollow pass, and another. Toward dark the old one came out onto the knuckle, reached down into a wash with both front feet, and left by the one that got wet first.

There was still a little light coming up from the valley. In it the power could see on its finger a clean track, finer than the lines of the skin itself, which went half round the nail and stopped and went back a short way, as though the grazing had been particularly good just there. It waited till the track was dry before it got up.
