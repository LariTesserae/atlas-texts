The power that had come down the salt stair had seen the grain under the skin. It was a grain of green glass, quite round by now, though it had gone in with an edge on it. The power could have taken it out without waking the eel. By the time it had attended to the two white claws and the extraordinary purchase the crab was getting with the points of its legs, the thing was done.

It stayed to see the eel go in. There was a difficulty here which did not show from above. The eel got its head and shoulders under the rim readily enough, but at the polished place it had to press so hard with its tail that the dish lifted a little. As soon as the tail went in the dish came down on it. The eel lay for a while with the last inch of itself outside, feeling over the glaze with the tip. Presently there was a small violent movement and the tip was gone.

Three tides ago the dish had stood quite firm. A piece of mortar had come away from under it, on the side toward the door. It was still there, lying broad face down in the fine black stuff that collects wherever the water has not the strength to carry it farther. One end bore the mark of a thumb. There was a very small shrimp in the mark, occupied with something that needed both its forefeet.

The power had time. It went up as far as the green tread and sat there, leaving room for the woman to carry the bucket past. She went up a step at a time, putting both feet on each tread before attempting the next. At the top she poured off a little water for the carter's mare, into the hollow of a stone that had once held the foot of a gate. The mare had to turn her head rather far to get at it. The carter stood by her cheek and held the blinkers out of the way.

By the middle of the flood the shrimp had left the thumbmark. The power went under the dish and lifted it no farther than the eel had lifted it that morning. It turned the piece of mortar on its long edge and tried it under the rim. There was a position in which it took the weight without rocking, and in which the run of water to the crack was much as before. The power let the dish down. A little black stuff came out in two slow curls. On the upper side the strip of gray daylight shook, as it will when a cart goes over with one wheel somewhat out of true. That was the whole of the work.

On the ebb the eel came through facing the stair. It had braced its tail for the accustomed shove and came out rather faster than it meant to. Its nose ran into a heap of empty limpet shells. It lay with a shell over either eye until the movement of its jaw sent one slipping off, and then it backed clear of the other and went on to the crack. The first shrimp had got by. There were others.

Later the power went up the stair. A little water still stood in the gate stone, with three short hairs from the mare's lip afloat in it. Below, the eel had disposed itself in the two hollows. Its tail lay loose in the passage between them. In the space left by the turning of the stone, the small shrimp was at work again.
