At first the power had some thought of putting a stone under the root. There was a hollowness there, on the side away from the silver fish. The old swimmer in going round for a lost one had to go down into it and come up tail first, for there was not room at the bottom to turn. A stone of about the size of a grazer's shell would have brought the two sides of the hollow level.

While the power was considering the size of the stone, one of the silver fish died. It went down on its side, looking very much wider than it had in life, and the other fish moved out of its way and did not close over it. For a little while there was a silver space on the felt with dark water above it. A youngster coming by at the end of a string let go and went down towards the brightness. Before it could get there the current took it. The dead fish too went sliding along, quite smoothly, a little ahead of the young one, until they came to the hollow. The dead fish passed over. The young one, which had put its fins out, went in. Down there it was perfectly quiet. When the old one came round, it had only to open its mouth.

The power left the hollow as it was. It began to learn the lake from below, where quite different things appeared to be wanting than when one looked at it from above.

There came a morning when a long piece of the loose upper weave folded over on itself. This happened without a breath of wind. One of the straps had flowered in the night. Its flowers were on the dry side, an insignificant row of buttons on little crooked stalks, and the weight of moisture on the buttons was enough to turn the strap over. Several others went with it. By the time the old swimmer arrived with her string there was no light in the court.

She went across it just the same. The young did not let go. She went up where the light had been, far enough for the last of the string to clear the bed. Then she came down, and for a time they all lay in a little heap on the swept place, the young still holding the fins in front of them and the old one with nothing in front of her but the dark. Presently she began the journey back.

Over a very great extent of water the straps had fitted together in much the same fashion. Here and there were new openings; at one of them a shaft of light went down beside a pale root, lighting the whole length of it to no purpose that the power could see. It was a fine sight. The little irregularities left in the wood by the wearing away of the bank stood out like writing. On many mornings after that the power saw the root so, and on many mornings the little heap lay in the dark in the court. The young grew. One of them could already lift the ceiling of the seed room by turning over, and had no more color on its back than a drop of water.

What the power did in the end was to take hold of the light where it lay along the root. It left the greater part there. It took about as much as would have lain on the blade of a reed, and laid it across the swept place, low down, so that a swimmer entering the court would put her face into it before she had gone the length of her head.

On the first morning the old one backed out of it. The whole string backed with her, very badly. She went in again at an angle, one tag in the light and one out, and this time, as the young came abreast of it, they let go. They stayed there a long time. The one who was getting too big for the ceiling made a mark like a stem with a leaf coming off it. It was a poor match for the swept floor. But on the way home it left the string of its own accord and came to rest on an old leaf, where even the power, knowing which leaf it was, had some difficulty in making it out.

The light was there as long as the flowers lasted. When the stalks had straightened and shed their buttons the straps drew apart again, though not quite in the old place. The daylight came down near the rim of the court where the felt was still rough. For a day or two the young had the use of both lights. Then the power let the little borrowed breadth of it fall back along the root.

Much later, when the old swimmer had her last room under the leaf towards the west, the power came there also. There was no difficulty about light in that place. It came in sideways under the leaf, reflected from the clouds a long way down, and showed the rubbed place on her chin and the small movements of water in and out under her belly. For the first time the power had a good view of the tips of the two fins. The thin part of each, which felt so soft to the mouth of a young one, was frayed almost to the rays. While the power watched, a little grazer came cropping along the edge of the leaf. Its shell was high on one side. There was no need to bring it any nearer. The old swimmer put out her head.
