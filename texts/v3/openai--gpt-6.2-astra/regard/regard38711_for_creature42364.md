There is a loose tooth at one end of the rim. Iva feels it give a little each time the mouth comes down. When the beast takes its face away there it is in the bottom of the jug, a flat grey tooth with a new white edge where it has broken off. Iva puts it in her cheek, having no pocket. For the rest of the afternoon people keep asking what she is smiling at.

Of the power that has taken an interest in this place, it is difficult to say where it stands. It can see the tooth in Iva’s cheek and the white side of the fallen mountain without having to look away from either. This is not altogether an advantage. It took it some time to understand why the people go up when the stones are uncovered. The picture is there to be seen quite well through the beast, if one happens to see in that fashion. It took longer to understand the stools.

Down in Enne’s room there is a stool on which nobody sits. It has a burn across the seat and a hole in the middle of the burn. When Enne goes over to do a floor, she puts the stool on its side in the door and runs her waist rope through the hole. Two turns round the oven post take the weight. The stool keeps the rope clear of a tongue of stone that sticks out below the threshold. It is the brother’s stool. Among the accomplishments Enne is willing to allow him is that he knew what to do with a thing after he had burnt a hole in it.

On the morning after the rain the power notices something Enne has no means of noticing from the room. A chip has come off the tongue of stone. It lies on a cross timber, a little grey flake with one face much brighter than the other. Where it came from there is now an edge, and the edge reaches the rope. For the present this does not matter. The rope is hanging slack. Once there is a woman on it and the stool has settled on its short leg, it will matter quite a lot.

The power could take the edge off. It is the work of less than a moment to it; indeed it has already considered what to do with the dust. But there are other thresholds up and down the sill, and the water that got into this one has got into several. At two of these houses the people who will next go over on ropes are not yet born. It would rather Enne saw the chip.

This is what it does. It moves the chip from the cross timber to the seat of the stool.

In the morning Iva brushes the chip off to put the stool through the door. It falls down the front of her dress. She takes the dress off, shakes it, puts it on again and goes down on hands and knees to find what has scratched her. By this time Enne has seen the white line below the threshold for herself. It shows up very well with Iva’s dress no longer hanging in the light.

“Put your tooth away,” she says when Iva comes up with the chip.

“It isn’t my tooth.”

They lay the two things on the windowsill. The tooth is rather handsome now that it has dried. On its grinding surface are several grains of the paving stone, pink and black, sunk so deep into it that they will not wash out. The chip fits the white line below the threshold almost as neatly as the mountain fits its place above the town.

Enne does not like to think how long she has been putting her life on a stool. She likes still less to have Iva see her thinking it. She sends her for a piece of applewood and spends the morning making a saddle with a groove for the rope. Underneath, the saddle is hollowed to fit over the stone. She leaves room in the hollow for the end of her finger. This is so that a person fitting the saddle has to feel the edge it goes over, a useful thing to know about in somebody else’s house.

By evening there are three saddles in the street and a difference of opinion about which way the grain should run. The power does not enter into this. The next season there will be an applewood saddle sunk into the paving beside each trap, on a peg that lets it turn out over the brink. Children will sit on them and be told to get off. One will come loose and go all the way down to the slab, where, for a considerable time, it will be the only wooden thing.

For now there is the first one to try. Iva, who has not offered to go over and has not been asked, puts the loop about her waist. She wants a look at the bottom of that patch. Enne gives her the stone to put inside her dress, thinks what she is doing and takes it back. It is her own turn to sit with it in her lap.

From underneath, the patch looks nothing like a coat. There is a little hill in it where Enne is sitting. Iva can see the reason for a thing she has been made to do a dozen times, which is to leave the peeled ends long until the weight comes on. Two that were as long as her little finger have drawn almost out of sight. She pulls them snug. Further along, something pink has caught in a crossing. She is about to pull that too, but it is wool and doing no harm, and she leaves it for the bird.

Inside, Enne feels through the stone a pull, a pause of the right length, and another pull. It is quite distinct. In all her years at floors she has never been at this end of it. She had supposed the second pull would feel exactly like the first. It does not. The first is the rod going home, and the second, much lighter, is a person trying it to make sure.

She moves the stone along a hand’s breadth and waits for it again.
