The trouble at the narrow place was a flake of black glass. It lay across two grains of sand like the top stone of a little tomb. On the far side of it the water was deep enough; on the near side, where the pink stuff gathered, there was room for the shrimp to turn. It was only for the thickness of the flake itself that the shrimp had to be flatter than it was.

The power that had taken an interest in the hollow could feel what lay on the flake. There was a surprising weight on one end of it. Pick it out and a piece of the bank as big as a fist would come down, bringing daylight into several of the rooms under the road. The shrimp in those rooms had no use for daylight. At the other end the flake rested on a round grain, clear except for a fault at its centre, which shone. There was nothing on that grain but the glass. There was nothing under it but more sand.

It was evening before the power did anything. The shrimp had gone up to the green and was busy there, standing almost on its head to get at a tuft in a pit. The power took a grain from well down in the pink silt and set it against the round one. It did this again. From above there was not much difference between the altered place and the place as it had been, but the free end of the flake stood a little higher now, on a packed slope instead of a ball. Water came out under it carrying some very old brown dust. The dust lay for a time in the square of the bolt head, showing the track of the water through the pink in a fine brown scroll. By the time the shrimp came back there was no sign of it.

The shrimp did not try the place that night. It got under the edge as usual and sat there with its paddles going. Once it reached out and took something in out of the water, so quickly that what it took could not be seen. The power, which could have put it through the opening and set it down among the others without bending a hair of its feelers, left it sitting there.

In the heat of the next day it went in. There was a moment when it began to make the careful movements for getting out again, although there was no longer any need. Then one of its hind legs found the round grain. It pushed against it, and the last joint of the tail went out of sight.

For two days nothing disturbed the square. The pink came up nearly flush with the sand, except for a shallow runnel where the water issued. On the third evening there was a shrimp on the green nap. It may have been the same one. The big red grain was down in the silt by then and was no help in the matter. This shrimp worked a little farther up the nap than the other had done, to a place where a tuft had folded over in the water and was green underneath and quite white on top. It turned the tuft over with its feet.

Up on the shelf the porcelain went on chattering. It was tempting, particularly in the close heat, to move it the width of a finger and have that much quiet. But the sound was a very different thing with one's head out of the water. Down in the square it scarcely came through at all.
