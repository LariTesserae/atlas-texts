The first that Et knew of the attention being paid them was that the tassels came up dry. Six people had gone to the hole to haul them in, as usual, and six people sat down rather hard on the ground. A wet tassel weighs about as much as a person. A dry one can be carried off by a child, provided somebody first gets it to stick to him.

There was no noise from the hole. Et got down on her belly. She could see the crooked river and the shadow of the ridge going a long way out across the clouds. A little short of the first cloud there was a broad, rather pink obstruction. It had whorls on it. The wind was going round it on either side.

“Thank you,” said somebody behind her. It was a woman who had not slept through a night since she had been given charge of the tassels.

By noon the moss on the spoon had gone flat. By afternoon there was a skin on the clay in the children’s palms. The fresh air of the place came up the hole with the noise; there was no other way for it to get in among the pleats. The tassel woman came back to the hole.

“I did mean thank you,” she said. “Only not all day.”

The obstruction withdrew at once. There was something considerate about the way the noise came back. It began at the bottom of the note and went up through it, so that there was time to put a hand over a sleeping ear. The moss on the spoon stood up in a ripple from the bowl to the end of the handle.

That evening, rather than stop the hole, the power put the tip of its finger against the fired clay at the rim. It had noticed that a cup makes no great noise under the spoon if somebody has a finger on it. There was wind enough to send the tassels out straight, but the note went out of it. For the first time with the lid on they heard the six drops gathering. It was not the sound anybody had expected. A drop as it fattens moves its edge a very little at a time over the dry clay, with a noise like a bed being pushed across a floor in another house. There were five such noises. In the silence where the sixth should have been Et heard a stalk beginning to split.

There was time to get a binding on it. There was even time to fetch good felt.

The power kept its finger there until morning. When it took the finger away it left a small piece of the whorl behind, a curved pink shaving, soft on one side and hard on the other. The tassel woman spent some days trying it at different points round the lip. Pressed flat it did very little. Set on edge in the seam where the spoon had stuck, it took the note down far enough for sleep. She fitted it there with a hinge of wet thread, so that it could be lifted out on basket days. The old woman could not hear the note but she could feel it through the soles of her feet, and liked to know by it how far she had come up.

As for the power, it had got a dint in its finger. For a being of that size the dint was of no consequence, but on looking into it the power found it held the print of a leaf, the lip of one of the lost cups and the fronts of eight toes. A child had been standing there when the clay was soft. He had had ten toes, as children mostly do; the other two must have been curled up. The power spent a little while trying to curl just those two toes on its own feet. It is harder than it sounds.

By then the basket was going down again. In the hollow the two boys had stretched the hair from the rim of the cup to a pale root. Both ends were wet. Halfway over, the speck stopped and sat for so long that Et’s brother began to wish he had brought a different speck. His host did not mind. There was a very good drop falling just then. They lay with their chins on their hands, attending to their respective interests, until the boy from beyond the tassels felt something prickly on his knuckle.

It had come down the wet hair, gone round the cup and come some distance up him. Against his skin they could see its legs, which were red too, except at the joints.

“Shall I fetch Et?” he whispered.

Et’s brother shook his head. Moving to fetch her would have meant taking away the knuckle. Besides, somebody had seen.

Far out in the blue something of an immeasurable size was also lying on its belly. It was well to one side, so as not to spoil the light.
