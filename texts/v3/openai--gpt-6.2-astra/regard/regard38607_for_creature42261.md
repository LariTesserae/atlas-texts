It was one of these waits at a door that first brought the valley to the power's attention. It had felt the scratching on the stone. To something that can feel a mountain settle on its roots, the difference between a pebble being dragged and a pebble being carried is quite distinct. One of the company at the door had brought the rattle.

The power could have turned the flag on its pivots. It got so far as to take the weight of it, which was very little indeed. Then it felt the creature with the pebble put its cheek against the stone. On the other side, after a time, a cheek was put to the same place. It let the flag alone. A little later the door went round, scraping a white curl off the sill, and there were wet marks all the way down to the lip.

What concerned the power, on looking more closely, was the state of that lower pivot. It was a rounded knob about the size of the end of a person's finger, standing in a cup in the sill. The cup had worn considerably deeper on the valley side. Each time the door swung, the knob climbed a little way out of the cup and dropped back into it. In the neck of the knob a crack had started, exceedingly fine at one end and at the other wide enough to admit the first white grit of the day. Some clear stuff had formed over it. From outside it looked quite sound.

The inhabitants were good hands at a pivot. There were discarded ones in a gallery, worn almost to stalks, and a new one ready in a bed of paste. But there was no getting at this one to examine it without lifting the door. The last shelter had been made when the lip went out another yard. What had been a convenient place to stand and take the weight was now a considerable quantity of pink and white stone distributed over the lower country.

The power, which had no difficulty in standing there, lifted the door. It lifted it while the shelter was empty, and no higher than was needed for the sewing of light under it to show from end to end of the sill.

That afternoon a creature going past on the slant saw the light and came back. First it put its lips to the gap. Then it took hold of the knob with both lips and gave it a turn. The knob came away in two pieces, letting out a small cold gout of water quite pink with the dust of the broken stone. The creature lay with its chin on the sill looking at what it had in its mouth. Presently it went off with the pieces.

Nothing further happened that day. The power held the door overnight. In the morning it learned something of the weight of a flag in a wind, which is a different matter from the weight of a flag. In the afternoon they came with the new knob. There was a great deal of going in and backing out. The one with the new knob stayed well out of it. What the others were doing, as presently appeared, was taking down the upper pivot too. They passed it along from mouth to mouth to the still water, washed it, and passed it back. A little one was allowed to feel the hollow in the lintel while it was empty.

They set the new knob a little farther in, where the stone was unworn. When it was seated to their satisfaction they all backed clear except the one that had brought it. This one lay down across the sill and put its mouth to the bottom edge of the flag. The power felt a very slight downward pull. It gave the creature the weight as gradually as it knew how. Presently the pull ceased. The flag rested in its two good bearings and a breath of air from under the lip swung it halfway round.

There was still the yard of missing ledge to consider. The power had a notion of putting it back. But when the stream stood out that evening it saw, in the space the ledge would have occupied, a row of little eyes below a row of busy mouths. It had not till then seen them feeding from quite so close. The clear skin comes off a grain in one piece sometimes; it hangs for an instant over the lower lip like a bright scale. It left the yard of air as it was.

A long time afterward the power felt a tapping in that part of the mountain and attended to it at once. There was nothing wrong with the bearings. A company at the last shelter had got the flag off them and were swinging it in and out over the drop. They had found a way of doing it with a long thin slab through the doorway of the shelter and six or seven of the heavier sort hanging from its inner end. The tapping was a small one under the flag, knocking out the old cup. It had its eyes shut against the grit and was knocking with great accuracy. Beside it, in the damp, lay a second new knob.

For a little while the power stayed within reach. Then one of the creatures on the inner end gave its place to another, slid down, and went about its afternoon. There was room for that much difference in the load. The power went about its afternoon too.
