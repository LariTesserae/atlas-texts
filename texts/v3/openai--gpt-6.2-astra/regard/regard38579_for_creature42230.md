By the time the moving notch has got halfway round, there is somebody looking in through the hole in the washer. No light is cut off. In this respect it is a better sort of looking than can be done with an eye.

The point of view from the washer is a good one for the egg and a poor one for the keeper. The curve of the drop makes the keeper look very broad, and sometimes like two keepers with one grain between them. The watcher can look from the other side as well. It can look out from the nick in the grass, and from a place between the mill and the wall of clear flesh, without requiring any room in either place. Here it stays some time. There is a small knock each time the mill shuts which cannot be heard even with a cheek against the yard. The keeper has had it going inside it all afternoon.

What the watcher would like to do first is to give the grass another drop. There is plenty on the cold line. One bead has only to be brought a little way along the underside, past a place where the iron is greasy, and it will come of its own accord. The watcher goes as far as the greasy place. From there it can see down the run the water will take. The egg will come out of its nick. It will go very fast over the flat of the glass and stop against a feather of rust, well out in the yard where the sun is still hot. There are ways of attending to this too. By the time all of them have been taken into account the watcher has been away nearly a minute. Coming back it finds the keeper in a place it had not foreseen, standing on the egg.

The shell gives a little under the toes. That is where the other notch comes from. When the keeper steps off, the notch comes out again very slowly. The watcher has seen shells before, of all thicknesses, but it has not seen this. It leaves the bead where it is.

At half past three a man comes across the yard with a square shovel. There is to be an inspection of the works on Monday. He is clearing up the sweepings along the wall, and he does it by running the shovel in on the level of the yard, then giving the handle a little dip to get the stuff that lies below. He has done six yards in this manner. On the seventh there will be a particularly easy place to get the blade in, just under the firm lip of a washer.

The watcher could stop his hand. Instead it lets him hear the grass.

It is the middle blade this time. The smaller beetle has got up it by mistake and has just climbed off onto the glass. The blade hits the brick once, quite hard, and trembles against it twice. It sounds to the man as if somebody had tapped on the other side of the wall. He puts his shovel down and kneels to see if there is a kit shut in behind the brickwork. They had a cat in there last winter. He takes his glove off and puts two fingers into the hole as far as they will go.

He cannot feel a kit. What he can feel is a woodlouse walking the length of his nail. He takes his fingers out with the woodlouse still on them. There is a good deal of trouble getting it back where it came from, since it will go round to the other side of whichever finger he puts down. In the end he bridges the washer with his thumb and it walks off quite pleasantly. He sees the others then, and the well with its green lid, and a drop on a grass blade with something in it too small for him to make out. He sits on his heels a moment longer. Then he gets up and goes on with the eighth yard.

Where he leaves off shovelling and where he begins again there are two clean cuts in the bank. By evening the ants have found both. They bring a good deal of red grit down the first cut, and up the second they take something white and jointed, a piece at a time. The spider puts her strand across the first cut instead of across the washer. Business is a little better there. Nothing knows it has been spared, unless it is the man, who has been glad to get down off his feet.

In the nick in the grass the shell has opened. For a while there is a keeper at either end of it, the old one milling and the new one working to get its toes out. The watcher feels the pull of this quite distinctly. It could scarcely be less. The whole blade of grass does not move by the breadth of one of the wheel hairs. Nevertheless the shell comes away from the nick before the toes come away from the shell, and the new keeper goes over backwards into the thickest part of the dinner.

The watcher has other places to be. It does not have to leave this one to get to them. When the works start up again in the morning it is there for the first cough, and feels, among all the things that stay still and all the things that shift, the small distinct jar of a brown shell lodging in the hole of the washer. For the rest of the week this gives the smaller beetle just the step it wanted.
