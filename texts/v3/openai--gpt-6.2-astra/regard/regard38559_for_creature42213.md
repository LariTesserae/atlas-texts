What the power liked best was the doubling back. From above, the little file seemed to have come to a bad place in the rock and to be feeling along it for another way. One had to be very close to see that the mother was going back by precisely the way she had come. She went past quite good places to turn. When she reached the last one she stood across its path; it ran its nose along her side until it came to the place where the fur lay the way it knew. There was a little difficulty then about which of the others should come next. The order in which they arrived at the pool was not the order in which they had set out.

The power did nothing about the step.

It had occasion to do something that winter. Well out on the plain there had always been a shallow hollow full of rushes. In the short days it held rain almost to the tips of the rushes, and wild duck came down to it from the coast. This year a hole opened in the bottom. One morning the duck were walking about with the mud halfway up their legs. By evening even the prints of their feet were dry at the edges.

The water had found one of the old ways under the grass. It travelled a long distance in the dark before it came out, quite clear, in the flute with the nest in it. There were no young now. There was only the shrew asleep, rolled so tightly that the water wet her chin and the base of her tail at the same time. She came out through the side of the stopper instead of the end. For a while it hung there with a rent in it, letting out down in little grey puffs. Then the whole thing came away.

There were plenty of dry holes. The shrew tried three in the course of the afternoon. In one there was a bird that struck out hard enough to leave a white smear of oil on her forehead. Another was dry at the mouth but had water standing farther in. She settled on the third, a crack under the edge of the block, and took in enough grass to lie on. In the night the water coming down the flute began to carry grit out from under that edge. A soft steady trickle of it ran past the sleeping shrew. The power felt the weight of the block coming onto the little tooth of stone that kept the crack open.

It put its hand there. It could feel the petrels on the upper face, though their feet added nothing appreciable to the weight. Inside, the shrew woke at her ordinary time and washed. There was a seed husk in the fur of her cheek which took some getting out. The power waited. She went off at last in the direction of the pools, stopping once to make brush marks in a patch of grit where the water had passed and gone.

The power took its hand away. The edge sank perhaps the thickness of a finger. There was no report. On the upper face a bird put its head up and after a time put it down again.

For the rest of that winter the shrew slept in several places, none of them very far from the seam. By spring the flute was dry again. She went some way up it once but did not build there. The next nest was under a shelf so low that on coming home with grass she had to lay the stems down, go in ahead of them, and pull them in by their ends. There was no seeing much of this nest from outside. What gave it away in the end was a row of small noses at the level of the grit, all wet along the top from having been thrust out under the same low lip of stone.
