For most of the morning all that the power did was move its shadow. It had fallen across an old court, and a white animal lay out in it considerably longer than the others thought proper. Three times the feet behind it went through the business of letting go and taking hold. At last the power noticed that the lip of the sill was dark, though the sun was well over the basin. It drew aside. In another minute the coat had browned enough for the fingers on the new edge to take a grip without leaving marks.

This was useful to know. It had meant to give the western courts some shade.

Near midday it found a very small rain pocket with a considerable weight bearing on it. A mature stair had turned down into the street. One of its treads rested against a cast coat, and through the coat the power could see a good many small feet spread flat against the windows. There was nowhere for the lid to go when it lifted. The tread overhung it by the length of an arm on either side.

It would have been easy to turn the stair back. But at the far end of the pocket, where the underside of the tread came out into an eastern court, a whole company was feeding. You could see the little ones standing on the pads of the larger ones to get their mouths up to the meal. Besides, there were the white leaves underneath. The power put a finger to the edge of the tread and took just so much of the weight as came off without a sound.

The lid lifted. Nothing came out. After a while one small bunch of fingers appeared, felt the tread overhead, and went in again. The lid came edging along with all its occupants under it. The power had been looking for a score of little creatures to scatter into the street; it had not allowed for the breadth of the thing they meant to take with them. It moved its finger farther under the tread. A second bunch of fingers found it there.

The touch was very thorough. The little fingers went round the end of the great one, crossed the whorls in the skin, and tried the soft place beside the nail. Then they took hold. For the space of a breath the power had the whole weight of the nursery hanging from that one place. Other feet found it and the lid worked its way across the knuckle, rocking considerably. At the first bit of wall it came to, it got off. There was a little hollow knock as the drop gave out, and the stair lay wholly in the west again. The power let it down. Along its edge the white leaves, which had turned quite flat in the sudden light, began to cup again.

There was a mark beside the nail, a row of dimples finer than the pricks in a thimble. By evening it was gone. The power could have kept it, but would have had to hold the skin still; and by then it rather liked to feel what came and went there.

It went back once, late in the dry weather. A good deal of the stair was upright now. Of the little ones it could be sure of nothing. There was a coat caught high up on the wall, with one of its side windows broken, which might have been their coat. A brown animal was at work below it. With the fingers of a single foot it was holding a pink cut open for somebody too small to be seen from the lip. The power had to come quite close to make out the small gray mouth, and took care which side it came from on account of the sun.
