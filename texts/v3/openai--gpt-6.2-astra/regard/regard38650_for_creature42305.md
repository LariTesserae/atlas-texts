At one pool the back did not come up again. It went down on edge into the mouth of a seam. For a while a little water passed it on either side. You could see two places on the skin of the pool where the light would crumple up together and come smooth. Then the back slewed round and lay across the mouth, and the light held still.

One pool out of all those dark coins had stopped beating. You could have stood with a heel on either side of it and felt no difference in the ground.

The power that had taken an interest in the basin could feel the difference. It could put its attention along the length of a seam much as you might put your tongue along a row of teeth and find the one with a chip in it. It felt the cool water checking against the hollow of the back. It felt the hot water taking another way. Under the shelf the hanging drops stood quite round. A young oval in one of them kept putting out its feet and drawing them in without finding the stone.

It would have been easy to take the back away. But the power had felt, a little way in from the mouth, the edges of other backs. They lay one over another in a white mass with a hole through the middle. The water had been passing through that hole for a long time. The power waited to see what a single back would do.

By noon the cool water had found a way around it. The way was through a bedding seam so fine that it had been dark even at midday; now a line of light ran into the stone as the air went out of it. A wet mark appeared on the bed below. It lengthened. Presently the first drop ran along the underside of a shelf on which there was a fine grey meal, ungrazed from one end to the other. The power felt a dozen little pairs of feet come down there together. The drop drew back, leaving the young one fast to its supper.

On the old shelf, meanwhile, the wet was drawing in. Most of the grazers went sideways with it and came by one way or another to the pool. One was left on a patch about the size of a thumbnail. It was an old one, white down to the joints of its legs. Each time it went round its patch it had a little less turning to do. Between it and the damp lip of the shelf there was a dry place considerably shorter than the animal itself. Its long front foot went out over this again and again. The tip of the foot came down dry.

The power took a drop out of the pool on the end of a finger and touched it to the bare place. The drop flattened against the stone. At the next round the long foot went down into it and held. The other feet followed so promptly that the tail end of the animal swung clear of the shelf and hung for a moment in the air. Then it had got its balance and was away along the lip. There was a little oval of dry stone where it had been, with a wet ring round it.

The back remained where it had lodged. In the course of the afternoon its lower edge began to go to pieces in the cool current. Quite late, when the light was nearly level, the pool gave a lift and a single bead came up. The new seam did not shut. At every stroke it let through about as much water as would run off a wet elbow, and that was enough to keep a considerable length of the lower shelf in use.

By then the old animal had settled in the shallows. There were two green places showing on its back. A ruffle that had been tucked so close under the lowest white lip as to be almost invisible was loosening in the wash. The power had still a finger in the water, and after a while one of the ruffle came off on it.

For some time it sat without moving, in the little hollow beside the nail. Then it began the business of getting farther up the finger, taking as good a hold with each foot as if it meant to spend the rest of its life there. The power turned its hand till the back of the finger lay against the underside of the stone. Even so it was a long time before the small thing found anything to its liking. The light had quite gone out of the pool when the last foot let go.
