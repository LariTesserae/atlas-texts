There were usually more young ones in the part of the water that went towards the white fans. As the water thinned, their backs came through it. A foot that had been going round and round inside the skin of the drop could take hold very well now. It took hold of the little grains of chalk, which came away from the glass. By the time the wet edge had drawn back the length of a grazer, there would be two or three of them with their feet quite white. There was nothing wrong with the feet. Given something firm to catch on, one of them could pull the whole animal a surprising distance.

The power that had come to look at the room saw this happen. It picked up the young ones that were out on the fan. This took some care. They had to be picked up with the water round them, down to the last film between the belly and the glass. Set against the cool splinter, each little parcel of water went in among the hairs. The grazer was too broad to go in with it and was left lying crosswise on the nap. It was a much easier position to get up from than the usual one. One of them had begun to eat before the power had finished with the others.

There was no occasion to move the shelf. It was a good place for the young while they were small, and even late in the day, when the drop stood out so far from it, the basalt at the back was cool. What the shelf lacked was a way down its face. The face was undercut. Water could hang from the lip all afternoon within a very little distance of the stone below and never get any nearer to it.

Of the three splinters, the middle one had a long thin point lying flat along the floor. Nothing grew on its sunny side. The power looked under it before lifting it. There were two grazers there in a small damp patch of buff, quite a distance from the main pasture. It let the stone be.

Instead it put the tip of a finger against the lip of the shelf. The finger was of a size to fit the place and a little rough, with the grooves of the skin running lengthways. Down the finger the water came. It was possible in this way to let a little of the drop go at a time, and to put it just where it was wanted, in the fine groove that ran back to the splinter. For several evenings the power did this. There was a tickling when a foot got a hold across two ridges of the skin. Sometimes a young one would go a short way up the finger, against the water. The power waited for it to turn.

All the while a little of what the water carried was coming out on the finger. There was a white line along either side of the wet, very much like the lines on the fans. Where the finger touched the lip these lines joined on to the stone. Farther down they joined on to one another underneath. At the end of the rains there was a narrow flume there, no thicker than the rim of a broken shell. The power eased its finger out from under it. The flume held. Its upper end was a little higher than the hollows in the shelf, so that it took nothing from them. It received only the water that had begun to stand up in a belly above the lip.

It was some time before there was rain enough to try it. When there was, the first young one to come down went the length of the flume on its back. At the foot it turned sideways and lodged. Water rose behind it and came over the white sides in two clear whiskers. The power had its finger close by. But the foot came out from the pleat, took the thin edge of the flume between its curls and drew the grazer upright. It stood there a moment with water running under its belly. Then it let go.

That evening there was still a drop on the shelf to take the last of the light. It was smaller than before. Down on the floor the three stones made their long shadows, and the little white run between two of them made another, so fine it could only be seen where it crossed the light.
