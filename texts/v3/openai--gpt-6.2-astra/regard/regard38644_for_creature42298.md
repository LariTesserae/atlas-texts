The power first noticed the animal by putting a drop back.

It had watched the drop all morning. When the red ring appeared, it lifted the water, gathered the iron into it again, and set it against the sunny face of the cliff. It had taken the ring for the end of something, which it was. But the water did not go in. The opening above it had shut some time before. A little farther up, behind an eighth of an inch of stone, the flesh was drawing away quite neatly from the dry side of the crack. There was no wound there. After a while the drop fell again, on the same stone, a little off centre.

The power went in by the warm water. It was able to do this without taking up any room. For some distance there was only the smooth dark passage, with a grain of metal standing out here and there like a tooth that the water had not yet got round to loosening. Then there was grey growth, then a place kept very clean. Here the animal was still at work. A flap no thicker than the wet skin on a stone rose, folded over a little of the grey, and lay down. It did this again a very long time afterwards. The power, which ordinarily took in a whole season rather as one takes in the colour of a stone, had to make a considerable adjustment in order to see the second movement without missing the interval.

There was quite a lot in the interval. The flap did not merely lie there. A tightening came to it from higher up, and for a while its near edge was drawn fine as a thread; then the tightening went on past it. Another came back. In the course of the afternoon the same small piece of flesh was employed in feeding, in passing water, and in holding still while a blunt head at some distance tried the back of a crevice. The holding still was the longest part. The head had found something.

Two veins of water ran here, one still plentifully supplied from a pocket of the old sea, the other coming up warm. Between them was a flake of stone as long as a hand, bedded on its edges in the crack. Before the mountain rose there had been room to pass it. Now it was pressed flat at one end. The head went along the edge of it, losing the way sometimes in a row of shallow dents, until it came to the place where the two streams could be tasted together. There it stayed. Presently it went back to the beginning and came along the edge again.

The power could have taken the flake out. It could have kept all that mountain wet. It did neither. But when the head next reached the place where the waters mingled, the power took a little of the weight off the flake. It did not lift it. There was now about as much to be done as there would have been to turn over a grain of gravel with the nose.

For a long while nothing happened. The bead of fluid enlarged on the warm side and dwindled on the cold. Then the head turned sideways, an action the power had not seen before. The flake rocked on its bed. At once, from a part of the body so far down the mountain that it was already night there, a squeeze came up. The flake tipped, and a thread of cold water as fine as the head passed underneath it. The power let the weight come back. The flake stayed tipped. What held it was a single grain that had come down in the cold thread and lodged under the lip.

That was the extent of the work. The sea did not come back that year, nor the next. Parts of the animal dried to a yellow dust which remained in the cracks until the first rain capable of reaching them. In the occupied part, however, there was a modest spring. Its cold side would last about nine summers. In the third summer the range heaved, and the flake, along with a considerable thickness of the stone on either side of it, went end over end into green water. Of the portions washed out on that occasion, one was carried well beyond the last summit. The power went some way with it. Against the sky the little brown mark appeared and disappeared with a regularity that was very agreeable to watch. When the current turned north, the power let it go.

It kept the slower way of looking. Later, in quite another country, it would sometimes be supposed that the power was absent, on account of the length of time between its arrival at a place and anything happening there. As often as not it was attending to an interval of the kind it had learned about in the mountain. A thing that lay still need not have finished. There might be a tightening coming from a long way off.
