The power put one finger into the fringe and had it counted. It could have made as many fingers as it liked. It kept to one until the animal had finished with it.

There was a difficulty with being there. A hand in the water held up the water a little; a body of any size parted the wind that came in along the floor. Even standing well back in the passage, the power could hear the difference it made to the snails. It went into the harder bed of stone and attended from there. Stone is a good place to listen to a foot from. There is the pressure, and just before the pressure goes away there is a small increase in it, where the foot that is about to leave gives the rest of the animal a push. In this way it was possible to tell the difference between letting go and losing hold.

It stayed for a flood. One of the skirts from the warm seam was very late coming back. The water had drawn away from both sides of it, leaving it on a patch of wet stone not much larger than itself. A pace off there was a drinking hair hanging down. The power could quite easily have laid the hair across the gap. While it was considering this, the skirt drew its mouth nearly to its forward edge. Then the feet at the back let go all at once. The weight of the mouth took the animal a little way forward, with a noise like a kiss against the floor. It did this perhaps thirty times. When it got to the hair it passed underneath without taking hold. It was going to a split cup a little farther on, which from the stone had felt very much like a sound one.

The power had been prepared to spend one night in the hollow. It stayed a good many years. There were things it got to know quite well, and things, including the use of the split cup, about which it was none the wiser for a long acquaintance.

In the course of those years a tree fell above the entrance. The fall did very little. Down in the hollow a few root fans drew up about the breadth of a foot cleft, and there was a meal to be had from the hairs that died. The hole left by the root ball was another matter. It had a clay bottom. Each year when the clouds came up it filled, and each year it held its water a little longer, until there was a small pond in the forest where there had once been the underside of a tree.

There was a loose stone in the bottom of the pond. Under the stone was the first of the two turns in the passage. The power could feel the roots of the new trees working in around it. In time one of them would put a shoulder under it and the pond would empty into the hollow. An inch of water over sixty paces is a great deal of water; there was enough up there for the inch and for another six feet on top of it. The warm air went out through the same turns the water would come in by.

The power went up to the pond. It was the first time in years it had stood in daylight. There were flies walking on the water and, hanging from the underside of the loose stone, a bag of jelly with a quantity of little black commas in it. On the downhill side of the pond there was a long bank of clay. Behind the bank the ground sloped away among the trees, missing the entrance altogether.

It did the work with one finger. Beginning well down the slope, it drew the finger uphill through the leaves and into the bank, stopping short of the water. Next time the pond filled it would find the cut for itself. The cut was shallow enough to leave most of the pond where it was. There was still the matter of the loose stone. The power sat down on the bank and waited for the commas to come out.

It took nearly a month. By then the pond had gone down in the ordinary way, and it was possible to lift the stone clear, reach underneath, and press the two cheeks of the passage together in a place above the turn, where there was no old way through that anything used. The power set the stone back in its bed of clay. A small black creature with a head too big for its tail went briskly round it twice.

The work did not turn out quite as planned. The first high water scoured the cut deeper than a finger. For several afternoons a thread of water ran down the mountain in the open, making a succession of dark marks on the trunks of fallen trees. The pond was a smaller pond thereafter. But it held through the summer, and far down under the clay the ankle wind still came and went. In the season of crushed leaves it tasted of them.

The power went back into the harder stone. Below, a shell gave way. It felt the feet of the animal that had opened it go still, and then, at intervals about the room, the feet of several others. It had known for years how well the sound came back. It had not known until then how many stopped to hear it.
