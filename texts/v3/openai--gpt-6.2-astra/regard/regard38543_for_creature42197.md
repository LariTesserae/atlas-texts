The power that took an interest in the yard first made itself useful by holding a chair. The woman at eight had set the two back legs on the white lip and the two front ones on what looked like a continuation of it through the leaves. When she leaned over to put her pan down, one of the front legs went into a cut. For a moment she had the quite distinct feeling of somebody’s knee against the back of the chair. It was there long enough for her to get both feet under her. She moved the chair six inches to the left and finished the bush.

It would have been possible to hold everything in the yard as steady as that. But the woman had wanted to get up.

In November a man came with a roll of drawings to measure the opening at the head of the steps. The department had found a use for the levelled ground. There was to be a place for the little buses to turn, so that people coming back from the eye hospital need not be put down on the wrong side of Bell Street. It was a good use. The man had a mother who went to the eye hospital; for the rest of the day after her drops she could see the lights of cars but not the cars themselves.

On his drawing the yard showed up very well indeed. There were figures for its depth in nine places. The backs of the houses were a single heavy line. He was not the person whose job it was to go into the houses. He had a tape with a brass lip on the end, and what he needed was somebody to hold it against the far post while he took a reading.

What the power did then was to carry the sound of his voice. It did not stop the fans. In the kitchen at eight the woman heard, among the ordinary parts of the noise, a perfectly ordinary “Could you give me a hand?” She wiped her hands and came across. He had been calling to somebody on the pavement, who had gone on.

While she held the tape he looked down the steps she had come up. From there you could see the little orange rectangles of the kitchen windows but not the doors under them. He asked her where she had got in.

“I live in the end one,” she said.

“Yes, but how did you get out here?”

She thought he was rather slow. She took him down and showed him. There was her back door, with the pan tilted against the jamb to drain; there were the other doors; there, if he turned his head, was the red mark. On the drawing he had been carrying for three weeks the heavy line had no breaks in it at all.

He did not become a lover of the place. The noise hurt the good ear he turned towards her, and the dust got into the spring of his tape. He did, however, go to the trouble of drawing each door in. He borrowed the side of her fridge to press on. At the other houses he knocked and asked before he measured. At number five he had to wait for a long time with his pencil ready while a little boy did up the chain in his efforts to undo it.

That was as far as the power went with the matter. The drawings took their course. It was the driver of one of the little buses who pointed out that he could turn at the fish depot if somebody would move the empty crates, and a woman from the hospital who went up there in the rain to find out whether it could be done. By March people were being set down on the right side of the street. By June there was a white oblong painted in the depot which even the men on the night delivery knew to leave clear.

There was no paper to say that the cutting yard should remain a cutting yard for ever. There was a new drawing of it, though, and anybody unrolling it now had to account for a row of doors.

The following summer the woman at eight lost sight of the fox’s feet. For a while she blamed her eyes. Then, going across early with a bag for the bins, she saw that the earth behind the cabinet had been scooped out a little deeper. She went home and put a folded towel on her chair to see whether an inch or so of extra height would make a difference. It made things worse. After breakfast she tried the bottom step instead.

From there she could see one ear, lit through by the sun to the colour of the jelly she used to strain from the currants. Every so often it turned towards a sound quite lost to her in the blowing of the fans. She sat very comfortably with her back against the next step up. The power had other places to be, and went.
