It was the feet that settled the question. All afternoon the power had been watching the seed go round, and had had in mind a little alteration to the lip of one of the mouths. It could have been done with less lime than goes into the shell of a snail. There would then have been an easy water under the western seat, into which the creature might come at any hour and be out of the pull.

In the dark the amber fringes opened until they touched the roof. The next water that came up was hardly enough to move a splinter. It entered the fringes, however, and the creature went half its length along the hollow, white side up. At the end it turned. Four times it did this, taking advantage of movements of the water which the power, attending to the rock rather than to the feet, had altogether overlooked. On the fourth passage it found a speck of pink and ate it. The hollow was not a bed, or not only a bed. The power put back the little quantity of lime it had taken up. It put it back in solution, where it had been.

For several seasons it did nothing. This required no particular patience on its part, since it could attend quite as well to the pass in winter as in summer, and had never found a winter much like the one before. It saw the two lines on the pear part and join, and once saw a whole line go off on a cup although the hair was perfectly sound. In the third summer there were two black rings for nine days. One was so much the faster that at intervals they appeared to be a single ring thickening and thinning like the pupil of an eye. What became of the second creature the power knew, but it was a matter belonging to the water a good way down the eastern face, and not to the pass.

Then a man came up with a child. He had sat in the western place as a boy, and had been thinking on the climb how pleasant it would be to show her the white pear. His own father had shown it to him. He remembered distinctly its coldness on the palm and the curious fact that, wet as it was, it left a dry white dust in the creases when the hand was shut on it. He did not remember the little feet. Probably there had been none on it that day.

He had his finger under the loop at the peg when the power did the only thing it ever did on the pass. It let the child feel, through the flat of her hand, what she would have felt there in another twenty circuits or so if the pear had been left alone. She looked at her father.

‘Was that you?’

He took his finger out of the loop. They waited. It was some time before the creature struck the line in the ordinary course of things, and in that time the child found the commas for herself. The second tick was much fainter than the first. She felt it all the same.

The man lay down on his stomach to see. From his height sitting up he had made out the pear perfectly, even the old knot showing brown through its narrow part, and had seen nothing whatever attached to it. Now there were the shadows, six of them; and while he was counting, the shadow at the head of the line went down its whole length like a dropped stitch and took its place at the other end. He got up with a square wet mark on his forehead. They ate on the eastern seat, one behind the other, which is a possible arrangement although it leaves the person behind very little view.

The power did not make the peg fast. Juniper lasts a long time in that air but not for ever, and there was no reason to decree that henceforth all the young in the mountain should be reared on a horsehair. In fact it was the hair that went first, one February night, quite high up where it had rubbed against the chipped edge. The pear travelled for a while with its narrow end foremost and came to rest under the low roof. By the following August there was pink on one side of it. A black creature held it by the knot to eat.

The peg is still there. People who knew the pass in the old days sometimes put a finger to the crack below it and say that something is missing. There is generally lime enough on the stone to leave a little white in the whorls of the finger. If they sit a while the water washes it off.
