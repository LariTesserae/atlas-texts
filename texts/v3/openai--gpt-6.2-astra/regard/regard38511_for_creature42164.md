There is one more sort of meeting, too slight to leave any work in the stone. A power passing far outside the brown air came near the first ridge in this fashion. What it felt at first was the warmth the seat had kept from the day. Then one of the six warm places in the seat grew wider and a little less warm, and after a long interval another did the same. It stayed for the rest of the night on account of this.

It could have stayed quite close. In fact for a little while two or three grains on the lip of the seat stood up against it. The younger animal put out a brush to try them. At this the power drew off. It did not want to be mistaken for a street, particularly by something which had not yet been very far along a real one. There was a distance from which it could feel the feet quite well without picking up any dust.

On the morning of the change the older grazer went round the bend before the younger had come out from under the seat. A wall met it there, sun-warm already, with a seam of black along the bottom; when the wall went away the grazer was on it. Nothing alarming happened at the parting. The last thing to pass across the dwindling space was a little flour from under its chin. It spent the night a long way south, in a court with a very good lee.

The younger one did not take the seat that night. It got as far as the first pair of hollows and then went back under. In coming out the next morning it brought a shaving of horn along with it. The shaving clung for a while to the fringe of a hind foot and came off on the street, where the afternoon grit soon buried it. Under the horn, in a crease which the weight of the animal had kept shut, there were the six pink spots of an occupied one.

At the next thinning a little tongue of the street began to slide over that place. The power felt the horn give. It had a good idea by then of the pressure a case would bear; it had felt the young going about under the sleeper for many nights. It put something of itself between the stones, no thicker than the shaving. A grain moving past would have found a small hard place there, like a knot of the violet mineral in the paving. The tongue rode up on it, shedding flour. When it went by, the horn had opened along one edge.

For the better part of the afternoon nothing came out. The power kept finding that it could have done the next thing too. It could have tipped the case toward the lee; it could have got the flour out of the fringe on the first foot, which appeared at length and for a very long time was the only part to appear. But the foot was doing something with the flour. Presently it found a current in it. The other five came out in such quick succession that the case turned over, and the small thing went down a seam in the paving which from above had looked quite blind. A little later there was the sound inside the stone of one grain being pushed and another grain being pushed after it.

By then the younger grazer was up on the seat. Its feet did not fill the hollows, but this left space along the rough sides for the fringes, which were still soft. It settled rather well. The power stayed to feel the weight come off the first foot. Then it went on, out beyond the weather. There were other places it had been going to when it felt the warmth.

For a few days there was a check in the passage of the grit where the tongue of paving had ridden up. Behind it the flour lay a little deeper than elsewhere. One evening, as the square was coming together, a brush fine enough to be a single hair came out of the blind seam and drew some of it in.
