The power came to the cistern by way of one of the cold drops. There was room in it. The bent rim of the husk showed through the water as a black bow, except at one end where the husk was still soft and the light came through it brown. From here the power could feel the tread above the drop warming in the morning. It could feel, through the tread, the six separate pressures of the crab going by on the inner stair.

It stayed for a circuit. This is not a measure of time much used outside the cistern. The crab spent a long while at the trough doing something to one of its eyes with the tip of a leg. It passed the clean patch at the seep twice, having turned back the first time for no reason the power could discover. At last there was a stir in the mud and then, some way up the stair, the unfolding of the gray coat. One thin animal came off rather late. It had yellow in it still from its last visit. The power had known the colour in the flower, which grew on a tree on the side of the mountain that got the afternoon sun. It had not known what became of it here.

At the next high water the power went out along the underside of the tread. There was a husk that had opened against the stone. All its young ones were moving in the space between the gray fold and a grain of quartz. There was room to move but no way out. When the sheet of water thinned they would dry there.

The power pressed the husk once, near its closed end, as one presses a split pea between finger and thumb to slip the skin. Out came the young in a little billow. Some got into the inward curl of the water under the nosing; some went straight down the mountain. One stayed on the quartz. There was a film there too thin to run and just deep enough for the animal, which began at once to go along it, making its small hem in the direction of a speck of yellow. The power let go of the husk. It did not lay a finger along the crack in the gauge that night to keep the worm in. It had felt that narrow body too, drawn up hungry in the red clay.

In the dry months the husk it had pressed could be told from the others. There was a dent in it, and the cold drop that gathered there was the shape of a pear. A little fly came up through the cloud and stood on the stone beside it to drink. Its long mouth dimpled the water without breaking it. The power, which had been on the point of going elsewhere, stayed until it had seen how this was done.
