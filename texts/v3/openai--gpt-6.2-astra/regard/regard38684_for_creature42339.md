The power attended first to the one in the dent. It was possible to tell it from the dust without wetting it, which was as well. There was water enough in the louvres to float it back to the moss, but the next lowering of the gate would do as much, and in the meantime it was in no want of water. The power let the louvres drip where they usually did.

For a time it listened at the washer. The bead made almost no noise in coming out. It was in letting go, when the narrow neck of it parted and a little of the water ran back up the steel, that it made a sound. There was no need to stop the fall in order to hear it.

In April the joint was made good. It had begun to leak at the next washer too, and a wet line was coming down behind the shelf quite clear of the gutter. The fitter who came to do the work was the one who had lost the spanner. He found it with his fingers under the fold. There was a bright place on the jaw where it had struck, and the screw still turned. He put it in his pocket with considerable satisfaction. Then he took out the four bolts and carried the shelf down the stair to the yard. It was to go on the heap with a length of pipe and some old louvres. There was no longer any use for it up above.

Below the yard there is an outside stair by which the men go down to read the staff gauge. About halfway down, one of the stone treads has settled a little towards the bank. Even in a dry week there is a wet end to this step. Water comes out from under a root, runs along the back of the tread and goes under the next root. There is moss there, of a coarser sort than the moss on the felt, with grains of the stone held well up among its leaves. No one has ever had occasion to put a foot on the last inch of the step.

The power brought the three scraps there. It brought also the crescent from the dent, dust and all. This was less of a disturbance than picking the animal out. It set the felt at the edge of the wet, rather than in the run of it, and did nothing about the root, though the root was certainly going to lift another tread in time.

By evening the water had gone through the felt. One of the grey particles had put out its toes against a grain of stone. It made several reaches over the grain in the direction of the old moss, got the tip of a leaf against its discs and shut up. A little later it tried again on the other side. There was a brown place there, in the angle made by the felt and the stone, to which things came round very nicely.

Up in the tower the bearing stayed dry. There was still the fall to be heard nine storeys down, and the fitter went on kneeling in much the same place to grease the gate. On his way home he went down the outside stair with the recovered spanner knocking against his pencil at every step. At the wet end of one tread a leaf, a crumb and a small animal went sideways together. The crumb did not miss the mouth.
