The power first saw the bay from the height of the ice, and the stonewort was a dark place behind a stone. It came nearer without displacing any water. By the time it could see into a bell, the stone was too far off to be seen at all. This seemed to it a useful distance at which to stay for a while.

There was a bell with a grain of lime lodged in its rim. The grain was thin, with one long edge, and there were hairs beating on both faces of it. When the cup closed, the grain tipped. When it opened, the grain fell back into much the same position as before. The power waited through several of these closures. It could see, on the inside of the cup, a bead of food making its slow way down, and then no bead following it.

It took hold of the grain. At the next closure, when the long edge came clear for an instant, it kept the grain where it was and let the bell draw away from it. That was all the force the business took. The stalk ran out again a little to one side. Water went over the place on the rim where the grain had been, and presently there was another bead.

The grain settled in one of the scores. It lay at an angle across the old direction of the ice. There was no occasion to straighten it.

For a time the power had it in mind to keep the water round the bells quite free of loose lime. There were grains lying ready to come off almost every whorl. But a fleck coming in to a rim and a fleck going past it looked much alike until quite late in the business; and the bells were doing very well at making the distinction, in water which they themselves set moving. The power left the water to them. It did not smooth the stems or occupy the bare length between the companies. When a swimmer came off, it let it go out of sight.

In the afternoon a little snow fell, not enough to cover the ice. Each flake made a dim place underneath, the edges of which spread out so far on the way down that on the granite there was only a slight loss of light. The pink went out of the nearer grains. Down the lake the station continued to run.

The power stayed until it could no longer pick out the bell it had helped. It found that it could have gone on looking at any of the others with quite as much interest. For a while longer, in the poor light, it did.
