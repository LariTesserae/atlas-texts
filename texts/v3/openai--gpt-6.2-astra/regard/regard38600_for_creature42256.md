The first thing the power did was to go round to the other side of the load plate. From there, through the little window, it could see the white line under the smallest leaf. It stayed there when the men came up to close the lift house for the winter. One of them took the clock down. The other opened the supply cupboard. On the inside of the cupboard door was a list of things to be left on, very short now that nobody wintered at the summit. The holding coil was not on it.

The power put out a hand, and for the time it took the man to throw the switch it supplied the coil itself. There was no catch in the hum. The flake between the bowls of the 8 did not so much as nod. The man shut the cupboard with the reasonable satisfaction of a man who has not left a great deal of copper getting hot in an empty house.

It was quite easy for the power to do this. What took some attention was finding out how much of what it was doing was wanted. It could have supplied the four dots direct and let the coil go cold. There would then have been quiet over the whole face of the porcelain. But the power had seen, at the end of one of the sprigs it was hardest to see from the window, a blade so thin that the slight motion of the gas on either side kept it flat. It did not know what that blade would do in an absolutely quiet country. Nor was there anything the matter with the way it lay now.

The power therefore kept the coil, and the weight on the coil, and the brass at its present distance from the porcelain. This last provision turned out to matter. In January the snow on the lift house was deep enough to bring one of the roof timbers down a little. The power took the weight of the timber where it rested on the frame, much as one holds up the sash of a window while somebody frees a cord. In March it was able to let go again.

For a while it had an idea about the long bare stretch above the doubled stair. A fifth dot might do very well there. It looked carefully at that place, then at the round rim of the smallest plant, with the ridge left by the lifting of the stamp. To put down a dot would be easy. What it had in mind, though, when it thought of the fifth plant, was the pleasure of looking through the window and finding five. It left the glaze alone.

In the spring the men came back for the car. By the end of the week one could stand at the bottom of the lift shaft and see a little square of bright sky at the top. The house was left to the swifts. The power kept the swifts out of the supply cupboard, where there was nothing for them, and did nothing about the nest over the place where the clock had been.

It has other things to attend to. It need not always be at the window. What it has undertaken is to be there at the other end of the tin, as the supply once was, without requiring the leaves to do anything in return. From time to time it does look in. The smallest fork is a good place to look, because one can see both tips at once. The long arm spends what comes to it. The short arm, which the power has never once been tempted to mend, spends just as much.
