The power came to the hollow in the afternoon. It would have liked to feel the shrew drink from its hand. It had only to put a hand there, and wait; but the mother had been as far as the second tuft twice since she had last slept, and was asleep now, with her nose turned up into the belly fur of one of her young. The power sat on the bank without a hand or a shadow.

The smallest woke first. It came out backward, hindquarters powdered with the yellow earth, and spent some time trying to turn round in a place where there was no room to turn. A little farther out it managed quite well. It went along the foot of the sill to the pinprick in the lime. Here a mite, red all over, was going about the business of getting its legs clear of a skin it had outgrown. The shrew put its nose to it and drew back smartly. The mite went on coming out of its skin.

From where the power sat it could feel rain falling on the flats a long way upriver. It would be raining in the hollow by night. The first water down the floor would carry off the empty skin, the white rims, the loose blade at the entrance. There would be time for the mother to take the young out. There was a good place in the bank, higher up, under the tamarisk. The power felt along the roots of the tree until it found the way in.

There was a toad there. It had got itself into the earth rump first, and the earth had dried to the shape of it. Its throat moved once while the power was feeling there.

The power left it alone.

When the rain came, the shrew was out in it. She kept close to the ledge, where the drops, striking the floor at a slant, left a narrow way dry. Twice she went in behind the stone and came out without the young. The third time they came with her. She took them neither up toward the tree nor down toward the opening, but across the floor to a flat stone that rang under the rain. There was a space beneath it. She had been bringing grass there too.

The first two went under. The last lost its hold as the mother squeezed in. It ran along the edge of the stone the wrong way and came up against a lip of earth. Water from the upper end was already coming round the lip on both sides. The little shrew climbed to the top of it. For a moment its nose went up and down in the rain with nothing to touch.

The power put a hand there.

It was a warm hand, held edgewise, with the little finger against the earth and the side of the wrist against the stone. The shrew did not climb into it. It did what it had done all its short waking life: went along an edge with its cheek nearly touching. At the wrist it found the underside of the stone. There was a brief pull on the skin, so slight it might have been a hair, and it was in.

The power stayed until the water had gone down. It had not known about the second bed. Under the stone the four of them were shifting the grass to suit themselves; one could feel the small uneven strokes of their feet through the wrist. When they stopped, the power took its hand away.
