The power first saw the shrimp by the shadow it cast in the print. There was just light enough left for that. It followed the two together as far as the middle toe, where the water deepened a little and the shadow went out. The shrimp went on. To follow it farther the power gave up the view from above.

Down here the stone backs were quite high. On the sheltered side of one of them the water still had some of the warmth of noon. The shrimp paused there, its many fine limbs going all the while, and took something off the stone too small to have been seen even as a speck from the height of the bird's knee. There was a great deal of supper on that apparently clean surface.

For a while the power had in mind to keep water over the stone lip where the fish had to turn. It would have taken very little, a runnel no broader than a thumb. But on the lower side, in the damp that was neither quite shore nor quite pan, the first of the rush seeds had lodged. One had split. A white point, bent back by a grain of grit, was growing round the grain. The runnel would have carried it well out into the salt. The power left the lip at its old height. It had not been asked for a river.

Much later, under the shelf, it found one of the winged things lying on its back in the hollow that caught the drip. It was a little brown beetle, quite capable of flight with its wing cases shut and the fine wings folded properly underneath. Now one wing was spread out on the water. Each time the beetle had nearly drawn it clear, a drop fell and the thin bright fan went flat again. The toad was some distance off, looking up at a sound in the bush.

The power put no finger between the beetle and the toad. What it did was to turn a single grain at the edge of the hollow, a long pink one that had lain with its smooth side out. The rough end stood hardly the thickness of the beetle's foot above the water. For two more drops nothing happened. Then one of the feet, sweeping round as before, caught. The water came off the wing all at once. The beetle climbed out and spent a considerable time on the grit attending to itself, drawing first one feeler and then the other through the crook of a leg.

By the time it could fly, the power was listening elsewhere. There was an interval in the knocking from the rushes that it had taken for quiet. Within it, rather far away, another frog was answering.
