The one that came to the eastern rim put a hand into the water, as anyone might. Presently there was a tickling along the shady side of the forefinger. One of the little ones had stepped onto it from the point of a leaf. It went the length of the finger, breathing at the knuckle where the skin came out into the air, and went down again with a bright stroke along its belly. The hand stayed where it was until a foot put out from it had found the leaf again.

What had drawn the visitor from a considerable distance was the fall of the winged ones. From far off it had looked like a misfortune peculiar to this lake: the first flight of an animal brought it almost invariably to a precipice. Near at hand it was possible to follow one down. For the first hundred feet it fell like a seed. Then the wings came out, not to their full extent, but a little way, and the fall took a slant toward the rock. It struck a frond with an audible pat. The frond dipped and shed its water. By the time it had risen again the animal was well in among the leaflets, going along a rib head downward. There was no occasion to put anything across the gap.

The visitor stayed through a night. The white fish came up in the dark and lay just under the floating bed, so close that the red tips made fine lines on its back as it passed. In the morning there was a wind from inland. It turned up the edges of the leaves and made the little knocking sound quite general along the shore.

A grown animal came off the underside of a leaf as it lifted. For a moment it hung by one foot; then it was on the free water, well out from the leaf, which had gone down flat again. The wind took it east. It kept turning as it went, setting its forefeet wide apart and drawing them in together. Each time they came in empty. When it reached the black lip there was the thickness of a finger still to climb, and the wet stone, which would have served it very well a little farther down, overhung the water by something like the breadth of its back.

The visitor laid the same finger alongside it. The two front feet took hold so promptly that the dimples of the hind ones could still be seen drawing out to points on the water as the creature climbed. There was a temptation, now that the whole journey was known, to carry it down to the ferns. Instead the hand was laid on the warm stone. For a while the animal stayed on the finger, though the way off was easy. A gray pad set down on skin leaves a double mark, very faint, which fills with color again almost as soon as the foot is lifted.

In the end it walked off into a shallow nick in the rock and stood there with its back to the wind. The visitor went away before the wings opened.
