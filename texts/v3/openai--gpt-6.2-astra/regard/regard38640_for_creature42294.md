The one who came over the notch had a way with stone. In other places there were steps where it had put its feet, and a pass through a mountain where, once, it had wanted to see what was on the other side. It could have put another shelf under this one. That was its first thought on seeing the room that would never be a room, the blade leaning out where there was nothing for its red edge to come down on.

It lay down to see how much stone would be wanted. That was how it saw the gold animal in the hollow. While it watched, a foot came untucked at the tail end, waved a little and was put away again. The other two animals were out of sight around the rim. It occurred to the visitor that it had seen a room and a street and a square, and that none of the three had been made by anyone who had seen them coming. It left the shelf as it was for the afternoon.

It stayed a good deal longer than that. There was always some small matter left over from the day before. It wanted to see the almond-white family take on its gold. It wanted to know whether the same flier went to the same forks every night. This was hard to establish. One flier had a nick well back in its left wing, but even this one, coming down the street, looked exactly like its shadow.

One morning the small striped animal came out of the room against the wall with powder on one ear only. By the end of the week there was none on either ear. The sleepers got up later and later. Where the chin-high lights had been the color of a grape there was a faint brown at the middle, as in a bruise.

The visitor put its head against the mountain. Two arm lengths in, where the seam took a turn, a flake of stone had come loose. It lay across the place the warm breath came through. There was heat enough behind it to warm a shelf ten times as long. On the near side of it a pale animal had got hold of a corner. The last two joints of its body opened to take the corner in, closed over it and slipped off. It went farther along the edge and tried there. All up and down the flake there were others doing the same thing. A crumb would have come away with any one of them. This was a single piece the size of a shoulder blade.

There was no room to lift the flake without crushing the roots above it. The visitor felt along it until it found the thin place that had once been the bed of something with a shell. There it put a crack. On either side of the crack it put another, and went on until the stone had the consistency of a lump of sugar that has been carried a long way in a pocket. The pale animal, coming back to the corner it had tried first, took the corner away.

It was eleven nights before the warm breath came through. On the twelfth morning the small one had such a quantity of powder on its ears that when it shook its head it made two little fans on the wall. For a while there was an excellent black track along the street, with a clean round place in each print where the white heel button did not take the dust.

The visitor went away by the notch. From there it could see that the blade at the end had leaned a little farther out. It would have liked to know what happened to that blade. In fact it came back, though by the time it did the little black feet had long since walked clean and the small striped one was among those that began the turn early for the square.

There was no blade at the end now. A dry red stump stuck up close to the lip. The visitor lay down in its old place and looked over. Some distance below, a line of red columns followed a winding crack across the face of the cliff. The blade had gone down edge first, very likely in several long slow slips, and one length of its piping had caught. The new blades there grew nearly straight out from the wall before they turned up to the light. Under one of them, far beyond the reach of any grazing muzzle, was a patch of the clear green that lets the sunlight through.

The visitor had to look a long while to make out what had worn it clear. A golden animal came out of a fork and went across the patch, slipped back half its length, recovered its footing and went on. Another followed. At the far side, where there was still a little skin left on the leaf, their many feet had worn a set of parallel scratches finer than the lines on a lip. They had a street of their own down there.
