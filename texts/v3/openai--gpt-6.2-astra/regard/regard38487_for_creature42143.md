The power that had taken notice of the bank could have followed the young one without losing sight of the hollow. For a little while it did. At the edge of the flour the young fish came into clear water. There was a jointed thing there, clear also, known chiefly by the two short strokes it made and the long distance it went between them. The little sculpin curved almost into a ring and straightened. Something no bigger than a flaw in glass showed now in its belly. It was already doing tolerably well without help.

Under the slab there was a difficulty. The thread that ought to have come out at the far edge was coming back. One could tell by a shred of weed which went in past the sculpin's cheek and a moment later came out past the same cheek. A twist of orange cord had fetched up against the far edge. It had been there some time. There was a nap of brown on it, and in the sheltered parts of the twist were little white tubes of lime. As long as the water ran hard it kept a passage scoured beneath the cord. Now the sand had come up level with it. At each stroke of the broad fins the same water washed the eyes of the young.

It would have been an easy thing to lift the slab. It was a considerable stone but not, to this power, a weight. The father, however, had chosen his doorway to within the thickness of a fin. Nor was there very much the matter with the hollow. What was wanted at the far side was a hole about as big as the one the shrimp had been able to get through yesterday.

The power took hold of the cord where it came clear of the sand, well away from the white tubes. It drew a little of the slack towards the valley. Several grains dropped out of the twist. The shred of weed came through end first, stopped, doubled over and was gone. That was far enough. The power laid the slack down on the other side of a knob where the ebb could not draw it tight again.

It stayed for the turn. The male rested for a time with one cheek on the sand. A little way behind his head a bead had split and a tail stuck out of it, giving from time to time a vigorous shake with no apparent result. It was rather hard, having put one thing right, to see this going on and not put it right too. But presently the tail was still, and after a long rest the young fish gave a shake of quite a different kind and left its skin on the roof.

By then the flood was running. At the far side of the slab the sand had a new marking on it, a fine little fan with a shred of weed at the broad end. The young one went out close over it. On the orange cord a pair of rust-red eyebrows came out too, first on one turn of the twist and then on another, where the water from the hollow was passing just strongly enough to feed them.
