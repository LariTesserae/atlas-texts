The power came close enough to see the speck of red on the foot. It had never been obliged to wait for water. It waited now. By and by a dark point appeared on the stone a little to the left of the animal. It lengthened in both directions. The end going uphill had gone some way before the end coming down touched the shell. Out came the foot, with a small sideways sweep as though wiping a sill, and the traveler was off.

The power stayed through the night. Toward morning it saw one of the young come down from an empty bead. At a fork in the grooves it met a latecomer going up, and for a little while the two obstructed one another. The water divided round them and joined again below. It was a very slight obstruction. Yet some distance downhill the power saw a third animal lift its shell at the change in the water. There were ways of knowing what happened on the shelf that it had not considered, even standing so close.

For a long time it did nothing there. It went elsewhere and came back. One of the trees whose bark it had first seen quite smooth developed a split big enough to hold a fallen fern stalk upright. At the foot of the tree the wet stone was crowded every evening. At another bed a fresh root had grown out into an old bead. Animals with a blunt place in their shells ate there beside animals whose shells were perfect.

Then in one of the wet seasons a slab shifted far down inside the ridge. Up on the shelf there was no sound. The sand in three pools lay down, and the separate crescents of water under the lip drew in until there was only a gleam at the hinge of each folded fan.

The power went down into the stone. There was plenty of water. It was backing up against the slab, finding its way sideways through a soft band, and in time it would issue quite freely on the cold side of the ridge. Already there was a stain there like the print of a wet thumb. The power put a shoulder under the slab. It lifted until a few grains in the deepest pool rose and turned over; then it settled the slab on a harder bearing, with room for the water underneath. On the cold side the thumbprint went on spreading, slowly. There was water enough for that too.

It was nearly evening when the first of the wide shells at the lip tipped outward. For a while the fan remained stuck to the back below it. The water worked in from the edges, loosening a hair at a time, until the whole soft pink thing came away and hung in the bright air. Farther down there were two that never opened. The power stayed to see, as it had stayed to see the little foot come out. In the course of the season the shut shells came loose, and the open ones shifted a little, and the water went over them without a sound.

After that, when it came to the ridge, it generally came up the cold side. A tuft of green had taken hold there. It would lay a finger on the wet stone below the tuft and feel for a moment the draw of the water through the hill. Then it would go round to the warm shelf and lie down at the lip, taking care where it put its elbows. This was still the best way to see.
