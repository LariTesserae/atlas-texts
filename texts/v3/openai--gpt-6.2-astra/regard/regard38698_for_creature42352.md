The first thing the power put into the water was the tip of a finger. It put it on the eighth step, a little way from the open hand. There was a dust on the tread, fine as the bloom on a plum, which the shedding of the water had not disturbed. A ring went out through the dust. When it reached the heel of the little hand the fingers closed, one after the other, and opened again.

By then the water had darkened. The power had been looking from the north. It could have gone round to the south and looked through the paving, or taken the weight of the whole square off the stair. It kept its finger where it was. After a considerable time something brushed against it, very lightly on one side and then rather firmly on the other. The little one was trying to turn it palm up. The power let it do so. It had to make a palm, and a wrist to turn on, and in making them it went by what it could feel of the small hand at work in the dark. The result was a hand with two thumbs. This seemed to give no difficulty.

For the rest of that tide the little one slept with the back of its head in the palm and its hands tucked up under its chin. It had a hard place on the back of its head, no bigger than a grain of barley, which had rubbed a corresponding hollow in the edge of the ninth step. Once, in its sleep, it moved its head about as if to find the hollow. The power made one for it. In the morning the little one went down.

There was quite a depth of stair below the part that could be seen. The small soles took hold and let go, took hold and let go, until their going could no longer be distinguished from the settling of the houses on the far side of the square. The power took its hand away. It had thought the hand would go out of existence when it was no longer wanted, but there it was, wet to a little above the wrist, with a grain of chalk in the crease of one thumb.

For several tides the step was empty. There was enough to occupy a hand elsewhere. The piece of eave in the bath quarter still had one sharp edge deep in a fold of the animal below. At the rise, when the fold opened, the power got a thumb between the edge and the skin. It held the eave still while the animal drew its length past it. This took nearly the whole rise. Toward the end there came a part of the flank quite unfamiliar to the touch, tightly puckered, with little shivers running along it in both directions. It went past. The fold closed again on good soft chalk. Only then did the power draw the broken stone out, by the way it had gone in. There was no outcry from under the walk. A hand opened on the eastern bench and lay there with the little sixth finger hanging easy over the edge.

At the next clear water something could be seen on the eighth step. It was a piece of red lintel, about as big as the end of a thumb. On the ninth sat the little one, its back to the north this time. When the power put its hand down the little one took hold of the wrist and set the palm flat on the red stone. The stone rocked. Under it was a shallow cup in the tread, and in the cup, kept from washing away by the bit of lintel, were three of the young. Their soles worked against the palm with surprising strength.

There are things a power can miss by looking at them only when the water is clear. The little one had crossed the square three times in the dark with the young against its belly. Where it had got them was not apparent. Nor was it apparent where it meant to take them. It set off down the stair with the red stone, leaving all three in the hand.

They had to be kept wet. This was easy enough until the ebb, when the ninth step went into the eighth and there was nothing but paving overhead, with the heat of the air coming through. The hand cupped a spoonful of water. To keep the water from seeping out between the fingers the power grew a fold there, and another fold over the first to keep the salt from opening on it in fans. It spent the longer darkness in this attitude. From time to time a pink sole went feeling along the join.

At the first knock of the following tide the little one came back. It had red on the hollows of its nails. A long way below, where the stair turned, there were three shallow beds in a bank of still-damp lintel dust. The hand fitted round the turn badly. The second thumb, however, could reach where the first could not. Between them the two carried the young down.

The power has kept the hand. It knows its way about a fair part of the city now, though it is still apt to come out at the wrong bench when the houses have changed places in the night. There is a patch on the eighth step that has worn smooth under the wrist. Sometimes the little one is there and sometimes it is away for a season. On those occasions the hand rests palm upward on the step for the few minutes of clear water. Then, unless something has taken hold of it, it goes about its business.

Below the turn there are soles at work in the red beds. One of the three has lately got a finger through to the south side of the square. There is no stair on that side. There is only a small, soft fingertip projecting from the level paving, with the shed skin of the water dragging over it. It draws a crooked line in the skin. After a while it learns to lift itself over, and the line breaks off, and a little farther on it begins again.
