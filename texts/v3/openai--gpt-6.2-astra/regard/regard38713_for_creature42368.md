What first caught its attention was the snag. Seen from a great height, a tree going sideways without falling over is a more remarkable thing than a blue cloud. The power came down to have a look. It was still there in the evening when the snag went back. By then it had discovered the moths.

For three springs it did nothing that could have been noticed from the pass. It spent a good deal of the first spring under a lip, where it found that the blue dust, quite dull in daylight, gave back almost all the light there was. A moth coming towards one in that place was visible from below as a travelling patch of belly, with a little dark notch at the front where the forefeet were busy. It spent part of the second spring with the grey bird. The bird had young in a cup of old silk on the sunny side of the tooth of rock. Its way of feeding them was to take a moth by the four wings together and shake it loose into an open mouth. There were wings in the nest long after the birds had gone, folded this way and that by the pressure of their little naked seats.

In the third spring the power learned to tell, by the pull on a single strand in the roof of a moss passage, whether the brown head below it was eating or merely turning over. By this time, if it had been asked which thing in the valley it had come to see, it would have had some difficulty answering.

There was one small business it did attend to. On a night of hard frost a scale came away from the pink seam in the rock. It went a long way over the silver on its flat side and stood up on edge in a hollow. In the morning, as the first root filled, a knob of moss was pressed against it. A slit opened through the silk. There were six moths in that knob, five still shut up and one already out, with its wings wet. The stone had caught it across one wing. It was pulling backwards with all six feet, and each pull brought a little more of the wet wing through the slit and pleated it against the edge.

The power took the weight of the stone. That was all that was wanted, but it was wanted rather precisely. The slit eased; the moth came backwards out of it and hung for a while with one wing long and narrow and the other like a bit of chewed leaf. Presently the creases filled and the two were much alike. The power let the scale down flat in the hollow. Melt ran over it, and in a week it was out of sight under the mould.

It did not follow the moth to see how it came out with the bird. There was a particular pleasure, which the power had not known before, in seeing the six feet get a purchase and then having nothing more to do with them.

It still comes to the pass. Most often it comes on the morning after the ice washers fall. There is then a little while in which the lake is perfectly still and every red tip has a dry place round its base where the ice has kept the water off. If one gets low enough one can see under the raft, along a level passage of brown stems, to a place where daylight comes down green through a full bladder. The power likes to lie there and look up. Sometimes a moth walks over the bladder on its way to a flower. Six small dents travel across the green light. From the pass nothing whatever appears to be going on.
