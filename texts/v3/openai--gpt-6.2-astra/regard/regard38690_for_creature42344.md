The power came to the shelf at the turn, and for the first quarter of an hour it thought the middle staple was too small. A ray with a nick in the edge of its disc lay across the opening. On the other side of the black curve was room enough for a hundred such rays, and here was this one obliged to wait outside in the heat.

It stayed to see what the ray would do. Presently the octopus put an arm out and ran it along the nick. The ray lifted that side of itself a little way off the rock. Cool water went in under it; a morsel of weed came out. For the rest of the opening the two lay quite still. When the water went slack the ray went back under the ledge. There was no mark on its disc where it had borne against the ceramic, of the sort a narrow door will leave on an animal that has been trying to get through.

The power let another tide go by, and another. It had the leisure for this. It saw the rays arrive a little earlier each day as the open rock got warmer. It saw a new octopus go up and one go down, and on one occasion saw the same undecided little animal go both ways, coming to rest at last on its mother's head. It discovered that from inside the bubble, where the old one lay, the one loud thing in the grove was the setting down of the mug. Long before the sound had reached the air there was a fine shiver in every egg stem.

In time it came to know one bunch from another. There was a short bunch far back on the left that always took the old one longer to go over. The hollow there was shallower than elsewhere. She could put the tip of an arm between the eggs and the ceramic, but she could not turn it over. To clean the backs she had to draw it out and put it in again the other way up. By then very often there would be work to do at the mouth of the bubble. The power saw her go out to shift a wad of weed, come back, find the place by taste and begin again.

Of all the alterations it could have made in the shelf, the one it made was to let a little water through the back of that hollow. There was water already on the other side of it. All that was wanted was a passage no wider than the thread a shrimp trails behind it as it goes. It chose a place clear of the egg stems and waited till the old one's arm was out of the way.

At the next opening a single brown grain came out from behind the short bunch. The female caught it. For a while she kept the tip of her arm over the new hole, and the power, which had not thought of this, was pleased to find it had made a thing she could shut. She let it run again. Three more grains came out, then a scrap of empty egg skin so old it had gone the color of tea.

She had the length of a leaf's dripping in which there was nothing in particular to be done. She put an arm down into the silt. The ray with the nick was there. This time it settled on the very end of her arm, leaving her the use of nearly all of it, but she did not draw it away.

Much later, when the last of the short bunch had come loose, a shrimp found the little current and stood head down in it, turning slowly. From the path there was no seeing even the staple. The woman had put her mug a foot or so farther along the lip, in better shade. When she came for it she held it tilted for the usual time, though on this occasion there was nothing in it but water.
