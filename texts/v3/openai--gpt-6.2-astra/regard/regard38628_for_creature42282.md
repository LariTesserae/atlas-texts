What the power first noticed was the egg. It was warm on one side and cooling rather fast on the other, and the cause of this, when looked into, proved to be two people with their arms down a hole. By the time it understood what the two people were doing, the hen was back on the egg. This was very often the way with things it noticed.

It stayed a little, nevertheless. It had never seen distance tied up in a knot. It could follow the two live ends, one to the flowering road and one to a cane underneath the shelf, but when it tried to follow the dead end it found itself in a kitchen where a man was taking a splinter out of his wife's heel. There was a pot on the fire and a child waiting for the splinter, having been promised it for a mast. The kitchen had no present address. Neither, so far as the power could discover, had the little boat. It withdrew with some care. The grandmother had been right to keep her nail out of that knot.

On the eighth night the old male came by with a torn hand. There was a pale flap hanging from the heel of it, and when the hand took hold the flap folded under and hurt. He was going along with that hand shut, which made very little difference to his walking but a good deal to his work. Twice he fetched up a bight of stalk and twice it ran out from under him. At the hole he had only the end of it left. This he put away as usual. Then he went back for the rest.

The power could have held the stalk for him. It was just about to do so when the boy, who had been watching, reached down and held it. The old male came back, took the bight from him and stowed it beyond the nest. In passing he put the shut hand on the back of the boy's open one. The boy felt the little wet flap.

Now there was, growing on the wicker screen, a felt of a kind the animals used for just such hurts. They would press a hand flat on it and peel the hand away with a fine gray skin adhering to it; in a day or so the skin came off, generally with a good deal of dirt and no flap. The nearest patch was on the other side of the screen. The old male knew where it was. So did the power. What the power knew in addition was that the screen was in flower on that side, and that a party of women would be there at dawn to collect the yellow dust which made fritters rise. One of them had a sore heel.

There was no need to arrange a meeting. It was going to happen anyway. What it did was to keep a single drop of water on a crossing of the wicker for about the time it takes to shell a nut.

The woman arrived first. She took off her shoe, put her heel to the felt and sat a little, enjoying the cool of it. Just as she was getting up the old male came round the crossing. At the sight of her he flattened himself against the wood. She could see only his fingers, all down the edge, and the one hand closed up like a bud. Then the drop fell. It struck the end of his nose, which was out of sight on the other side, and he sneezed. The woman, who knew that sneeze quite well from hearing it through the floor of her mother's house, sat down again.

By the time the others called for her she had seen him doctor the hand. She took a patch of the felt home in her shoe, the only thing she had about her with any damp in it. She was the boy's aunt.

It does not grow everywhere. On the wall of the house the little green animals ate it, and on the roof it turned brown and came away in the colander in small disgusting wads. But on the underside of the rim, where the old male put his second hand while his first was in the hole, it did very well. The grandmother uses it for the raw place her awl makes, and the boy has discovered that a piece pressed to the upper lip and peeled very slowly will give one a mustache such as no grown man on the paths has ever been able to produce. For this he has to go round the corner, since his aunt objects to waste.

The power did not wait to see whether the road would hold. That was a matter of some years, and there were people on the spot with their hands in it. It went away rather pleased with the sneeze. It had known the drop would make him take his nose in; that there would be a sound, and that the sound would be familiar to the woman, had been no part of its calculation.
