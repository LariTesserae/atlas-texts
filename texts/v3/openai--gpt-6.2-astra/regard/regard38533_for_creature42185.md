After the fifth knock the old man kept his finger up a little longer. Eda was glad of this, for she had been about to tell him a thing he already knew.

It was in one of these intervals that the power first got a good look at the underside of the street. It had passed the shelf before, and had rather liked the sound of it. From the height at which it ordinarily went about its business there was very little to choose between the city and the town on the hollow ground. Now it came down through a joint in the pavement. It found the caller quite readily. The animal had a nick in its knob which made the last of the three strokes a little untidy. It was eating again.

The power could have tipped the shelf by rather less than the thickness of a saucer. This would have kept the six feet wet for another considerable stretch of time. It followed the proposed alteration as far as the next bluff, where it found a white animal lying with its fringes in a gutter. There was just enough going down the gutter for the animal as things stood. On consideration the power left the mountain at its present angle.

It went home with Eda instead.

There was a difficulty about the dish. More of the dun animals were well enough now to climb the sides than could find room around the foot, and Eda had taken to getting up in the middle of her sleep to run a finger under the rim. It was a big dish to lift when full. She had once had the management of it down to a nicety, but that was when most of the patients could be depended on to stay in bed.

That morning, as she started to put her finger under, she felt a number of very light touches on the other side of the earthenware. She stopped. She moved her finger to the left. There were two together there. Farther on, one, and farther on a space of about a hand’s breadth in which it would be safe to take hold. She got the dish up without pinching anybody.

She set it down again rather hard.

“If you are going to do that,” she said after a bit, “do it before I have hold of the thing.”

The next time it did.

For several days this was the whole extent of the acquaintance. The power took some pleasure in the employment. It had held things apart before, chiefly very hot things at substantial distances from one another, but had never been asked to attend to the instant at which it became convenient to take hold.

The works office meanwhile authorized the removal of the shed. The new site had water under five feet out of six. Eda got down with the man who had left the stud and together they turned his plan round until it had water under all six. This put the door on the wrong side for the covered walk. He pencilled in a bend. It was a satisfactory afternoon for both of them.

There remained the old site. When the shed was lifted there would be some thirty uncovered paving stones which had been in the dark since they were laid. The practice was to let these dry, turn them good side up and lay them close. Eda asked if they might have a look underneath first. The foreman said certainly, provided she could do it in the time it took his men to have their bread.

On the day of the move her father sat at his treadles until the last possible minute. He let out a boy who had spent nearly all his allowance to hear a loose tooth squeak, and who came out entirely satisfied. Then the men at the exchange took the weight. The shed drifted past the bench at the pace of a person trying to remember something. For a little while the old man could see an uninterrupted view across the gap from where he sat. He had no idea his son-in-law’s aunt had painted her house that colour.

Eda was down on the exposed pavement with both hands flat on the stone.

“Now would do,” she said.

At once she felt the little bellies on the farther side. It was a considerably more complicated business than the dish. There were the callers moving about, the dun things hanging still, and a third sort she had never handled, which appeared to have its feet at both ends and was making off in two directions with equal conviction. She marked a stone with chalk. Her father came over and knelt down. As he put his hand out to steady himself he gave a start.

“Here too,” he said.

Between them they marked nine. These the men lifted on edge, and Eda and her father worked along the wet faces with their knives. The old man was quite equal to the callers. One tapped so smartly on his blade that a workman interrupted his bread to listen. By the time the shed came down at its new address they had filled two washing pans and the lid of the foreman’s dinner tin. A little run of brine where the new walk bent proved especially acceptable to the creatures with the feet. They set off along it without settling the question of which end went first.

The power stayed until the pans were empty. When Eda next put a hand to the kitchen dish there was nothing to feel but the cool of the clay. She found on examination that there was nothing under the rim either. She could not say whether it had gone away.

At the old address they put the bench back. For a time it was a good place to hear individual callers. Later the last of the film drew in under the houses, and it was simply a bench with a view, much used by people waiting for somebody to come out of the shed. The old man preferred his new station. There was a slight fall in the floor there which brought the outer leaf snugly home without his having to stamp.

Eda did make one alteration to the new establishment, which was to have the upper saucer in each stack left a little proud of its neighbours. A person coming to wash them could put two fingers under the lip and take the weight quite comfortably, without having to depend on help of an uncertain kind. On her next visit across the gap she tried to describe this help to her son.

“You know when someone puts a hand on the other side of a window,” she said, “and you put yours there too?”

Her son said he did. He had shut his eyes on account of the hair coming down.

“Well,” she said, “it was the window that did it.”

He considered this, keeping very still while she went round his ear. Then he asked who had been in the shed.
