The first thing the power did was to put a finger on the rail. It had no occasion for a whole hand. There was a tremor from the baths, where a plate with a chip out of it had settled a little farther into the plate below. After that came three rather sharp taps from number eight. They came again at the next high water.

The shelf had been fitted to the bend with a pair of iron straps. One was still very good. The other had wasted on the inside, where it was impossible to see it, to something about as thick as the silver paper in a packet of cigarettes. At high water the shelf lifted a little off the bend. The heads of three screws struck the tiles.

It would have been a small job for the power to renew the strap. It could have done it on the inside, leaving the outside as it was, down to a blister of black paint with a shrimp under it. For the time being it did nothing of the sort. There was something else on the rail at high water, too slight to make out while the screws were tapping. It put its finger on the end of the shelf to steady it.

Against the finger there came a very industrious scratching.

The lodger had put one foot through the first hook and one through the second. It was working with its head upside down in the space that opened between the shelf and the bend. Every time the shelf lifted, it got its fringe a little farther in. It was eating a strip of brown felt that had been put there to make the shelf sit true. This was evidently very good felt. Two of the young, still on their stalks, had their feet out through the holes under the rim; a brown crumb that came by one foot was passed to the other and drawn inside. The power took its finger away. The shelf came down, and the lodger got its head out with very little to spare.

In the seventh interval the good strap broke.

It broke at the buckle, with a sound that brought the white fish out of the hollow on the second landing. The shelf swung on the thin strap and came to rest with one corner against the floor. The first hook was now the lowest of the four. The cup hung on it in the ordinary way, but the mouth of it was less than the width of a tile above a level line in the water. As the tide rose the line rose too.

The power took the weight of the shelf. It did not lift it. Already the two narrow feet were out, walking in place on the smooth shaft of the hook. The shorter legs came out in pairs. For a moment the whole animal stood on the little bit of brass with its pleats pressed flat together. Then it set the rim against the edge of the shelf and began to climb.

It went past the second hook. It went past the third, stopping there quite a while, owing to a screw hole which took a foot to the second joint and would not give it back. At the fourth hook it turned off the shelf altogether. The wall had been tiled from the bend upwards, and the top course had a moulding with a flat top to it, quite wide enough for a cup. There was a good supply of things to eat. Unfortunately there was a tile missing.

The lodger worked along the moulding to the break. It put its two long feet out into the water. One found the other side easily enough. The difficulty was that both the long feet were at the same end. For the rim to come across, there would have to be something under it in the middle, if only for the time it takes to draw a short foot out from under a house.

Here the power did a thing for which there was no provision in the plant or in the animal. It laid its finger in the place of the missing tile.

The rim made quite a dent. There was a little scouring at the skin, for even on a journey the fringe did not pass over a new surface without trying it. Then the weight went off the finger, first at one side and then at the other. There was nothing to be felt for a while but a leg feeling about behind for a brown crumb it had dropped. The power pushed the crumb within reach.

By the time the light came round the lodger had settled in the angle over the door. The cup stood level. One of the holes under the rim sent a fine stream out against the ceiling, where it stirred a white patch the size of a shilling. The blue worm had gone round to the side away from the draught. Far below, the line of brine went over the first hook and up the sloping shelf as straight as if it had been ruled there.

The power let the shelf down the rest of the way, taking care of the shrimp. It left the break in the tiles. A young lodger travelling light could get across it, and in any case the exposed bed of mortar was quite a different article from the hard yellow glaze. Something might have a use for it.

For several turns of the distributor the power stayed with its finger on the rail. It was able now to distinguish the setting down of a rim from a plate settling in a rack, and either of these from the minute uneven tapping which came through the wall above the door. It had supposed this last to be the young trying the sides of the cup. On attending more closely it found that the tapping went on when all the young were still. It came from much farther back in the rock.

There was no hurry about finding out. The light went down to the baths. In number eight, in the dark, a length of soft grey pleating paid itself out along the top of the door, where nobody had dusted since the sea came in.
