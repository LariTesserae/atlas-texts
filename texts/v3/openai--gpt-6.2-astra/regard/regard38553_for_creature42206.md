It was the company that went to the left of the roughness that first detained the power. The water went a little way with them and sank in. There were still some hundreds hanging by their thin ends from a surface scarcely farther from the lime than the ends were long. In another minute they would be down.

The power could have tipped the bank. It would have taken very little, and the water on the right, which had depth enough to spare just there, would have come across. But a mile inland that water was coming to a sill. It wanted very nearly all its depth to get over. There were young ones on that side too.

Instead the power put a round weight on the pavement a little ahead of the company and pressed until it heard a tick. There was a pocket underneath. A lid went down, quite level, about the breadth of a thumb, and the last of the sheet went into it. For a moment the young were all at the upper edge on the side the water had come from. Then the surface steadied and they drifted apart. A pair of them went round and round on a little current set up by air coming out of the fresh wall.

There was nothing to eat in the new cup. The broken lines were pale all the way down, even the ones that would be red when they had stood for a time in water. The power had thought of depth and shade and a tight bottom, and had obtained all three. Toward noon it went to an old cup higher on the bank. A flake of green skin had come loose there and was drying on the lip, green side down. The power wet the tip of a finger, took up the flake, and touched it to the new wall just where the sunlight ended. By the third morning there was a little beard on its torn edge. The young took hold there. Seen against the fresh lime they were clear all through except for a green stroke that came and went under the comb.

The power stayed through the drying. Some of the young took seams in the bottom, which it would not have thought of looking in. Some were still on the wall when the water left them. It would have been an easy thing to put water back. But the ones that had gone in were scarcely set; a long soaking would draw them out again, and there was no beard left on the flake. The power left the cup dry. It knew now the look of a foot trying an opening, and the rather different look of one that had ceased to try. It did not find them easy to leave.

In the next rains the cup filled without assistance. Six copper spots came out of the bottom, so small that in the moving light there might have been twice as many. One traveled up the new wall to the place where the flake had been. There was a red line there by then. The power watched until the spot had gone the whole way round.

It made a few more hollows in the course of a long summer, always on ground where a sheet ran out, never in a passage where water was on its way somewhere else. After that there was a long while when it made none. It liked to come in the heat, when there was very little to see. In the first of the cups the fresh break had weathered to the color of the others. Dust lay in the bottom. Only at the high side, on a ledge too narrow to rest a grain of sand on, there was a fine greenish tuck of skin. In time the skin would grow out, harden, and have a skin above it. The crease where the finger had pressed it to the wall was already full of lime.
