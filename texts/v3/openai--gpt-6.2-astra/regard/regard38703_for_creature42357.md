In the cup nearest the eastern break there was a brown parcel that went round with the sun. The water went round considerably faster. Each time the parcel came against the inside of the cup it opened a little. A pale fringe came out, touched the green wall, and rolled back under. By noon it had been past the same yellow bead some forty times. The bead was still there.

What had come to look at the country stayed by this cup for most of a day. It could have taken the animal out. It had seen where to put it, too: there was a fine dark seam on the shady side of the cup, with a branching of white nubs that had scarcely been grazed. But a second parcel came up from the bottom, and presently a third, very small, went by inside the open fringe of the first. The water was brown almost to the lip. There was no telling how many there were.

On the far side of the country, under a stand of old cups, there was one that had begun to come to pieces. A strip of its inner lining had peeled down from the rim and hung into the water. The strip was limp and there was very little to distinguish it from the wall it lay against. Between the two, however, were the prints of several hundred flat animals. Some of the prints were fresh, and where the strip went over the lip there was a continuous dull track down into the leaf bed. A little water went that way too. It had made a black stain hardly wider than the strip. The cords below were all in good colour.

What the power did at the eastern cup was to put a crease in a wet leaf. It did it on the inside, just below the lip, when the water was at the top of its swing and the leaf could be bent without breaking any of the yellow strings in it. At first this made a ledge. The water beat it flat in less than a minute. When it lifted again it was double, with a space between its two faces that ran out at either end. The brown parcel came round, caught in the lower end, and stayed there.

For some time it did nothing. Then a little brown bag filled at one side, and another beside it. By evening there was no bead.

It was not quite so easy as this at the other cups. In one, the water stood too low. In another, a crease made on the shady side took half the spill away from a patch of striped mouths. The power smoothed that one out again before the cords had finished unwinding. There were cups it left alone for a year, and cups already so full of loose lining that a change in the set of the wind sent great soft wafers of animals out over the brim. It learned not to count the husks at the foot of these. They were no measure of what had been lost. Once it watched a single traveller leave three skins in getting down a yellow fibre and arrive below with its whole brown furniture in order.

It did not make the lakes stay where they were. In the long beds under still water the white nubs grew scarce, and when at last the seams parted there was feeding for miles along the tear. Nor did it join the runners that missed each other over the eastern drop. In one such opening a white knob came up out of the old cut, soft as the first tooth of a child. It lay in the light for an afternoon. The flecks moving up into it were green when they went back. No one looking at the smooth unbroken places would have known the tube had any use for daylight there.

After a few seasons the crease in the first cup was no longer to be found. The cup had grown a turn, enclosing it. The new inner wall was smooth, except at the very top where a flat animal had got between two layers while they were still soft. Its back had kept them from taking hold over a patch the size of a thumb. The next animal went in a little farther. In time there was a loose edge all the way down into the water, made not by the power but by the passage of bodies that required very little room and could make good use of a little more.

The power still came by. There were evenings when there was nothing for it to do but feel, against the outside of a cup, the small successive pressures of an animal feeding on the inside. They went round the curve as far as a certain place and stopped. A person in a hurry might have thought the creature was in trouble there. If you waited, you could feel it go the other way.
