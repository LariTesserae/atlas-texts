One of them goes no farther than the flake that stands on edge. The water is coming round both sides of it and meeting in the lee. A chip of pink shell goes round and round there, showing first its bright face and then its dull one. The little shrimp makes six circuits in company with the chip. On the seventh it gives its tail a hitch at just the place where the two streams meet, and is gone.

The power that has come to look at the bank could follow it. It could follow every one of them separately, without the smallest confusion as to which had come out first or which two had struck against each other above the prominence. This is in fact what it does for a while. There is a good deal to see a handbreadth up. The water that seemed quite clear from below is full of things with wheels of hair going round on them. One of the young shrimp has already got hold of something. It tumbles slowly backward with its catch, too busy eating to do much about its attitude.

Far above them, where the light is strong, a boat is making a sounding of the bank. The sound goes down in a succession of knocks so close together that on the boat they amount to a note. On a screen beside a man's elbow the note comes back as a tract of green, rather flat on top, with white streaks up one side. The man moves a cursor along the flat part. There is a weight hanging over the stern that he means to put down there. It is the size of a stove, open underneath, and is made to come back shut with a piece of the bottom inside it.

There is nothing wrong with his wanting a piece. He would be particularly pleased to find the yellow sponge in it. He has seen the little holes in dead shell brought up from much farther east, but never the animal that makes them. At present, though, what he wants to know is whether there is anything on the bank that would foul a net. There is room on his form for a word or two about the bottom. From the look of the green he has put down *level grit*.

It is here, if anywhere, that the power sees a use for itself. The flake does not need holding up. The female, lightened of her load, is already halfway down the seam. As for the young, it would be possible to give each one a run of water without a hungry mouth in it, but the thing tumbling backward with its catch is a hungry mouth too.

On the boat there is a camera in a padded case. It takes some time to fasten it to the weight and rather longer to get a picture from it that is not upside down. The man has not thought it worth the trouble for level grit. What the power does is to let him feel, for a moment, the particular curiosity he last felt as a boy when a flat stone, lifted at one corner, began to stir at all its other corners. He takes his elbow off the lid of the case.

The weight goes down with its jaws made fast. For a long time the picture is of things going up, bright little strokes in the light carried by the weight. Then there is a bottom, so suddenly that the man calls to have the winch stopped. He is looking down into an open court. It is exactly as he supposed. There is even a little run of coarser grit across the middle, the sort that will not stay in a net long enough to do the net any harm.

He has his hand on the switch to bring the picture closer when the whole run gets up and goes away. It was the back of the skate. Beneath it, pressed clean of meal, there are cups.

For the next hour the boat works slowly across the bank with the weight a yard off the ground. The man sees very little of what is in the cups. He sees enough of their arrangement to understand what it is that makes so large a green patch on his screen. At the lee of a rise he asks for the boat to be held still. There are pale fringes here. As the light passes over them they go in, and in the outer part of the picture, where he is not looking until he plays it back, they come out again.

He brings the weight up empty. On the form he crosses out both words. In the space provided he can just fit *living shell bed*. There is a separate sheet for the position and extent of such a bed, and this takes him until nearly dark. He does not claim to have found its farther limit. He puts a small arrow at the edge of the part he has seen.

The power has no further business on the boat. Down in the saddle the first shrimp is out again, going along with its forefeet full. Where the seam turns, it meets the female coming the other way. There is not room in the seam for the two of them abreast. For a little while they stand with their feelers laid along one another, neither giving ground. Then the female, who can fold herself rather closer to the shell than she could this morning, passes underneath.
