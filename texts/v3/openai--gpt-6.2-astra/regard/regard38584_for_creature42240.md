On the way back to Eight she stopped twice to do up her coat. The second time the boy did it for her, a little impatiently, since she had six buttons and he wanted his supper. She put a hand on his head by way of thanks. One of the things she had nearly given up was touching people she was fond of. They were apt to take her by the hand in return, and unless they were very careful it hurt.

It was this last circumstance, quite as much as any of the grander arrangements on the Table, that engaged the attention of the power.

It could have let a little daylight through the gas. From above, where it was standing, the gas looked thin enough to part with a finger. But it had seen the boy on the stone with his grandmother. The interesting thing there had happened quite in the dark, and would have happened just as well to the boy if he had never seen a door. As for what he might like to see, he could be asked. There was no occasion to cut a hole in the sky in the meantime.

It took a seat on a tread below the grandmother's door. When she came out to shake the crumbs from her lap she found a person sitting there with its sleeves rolled up. It had made a fairly good pair of hands for itself, down to the fold across each heel. There was not a crease at any of the knuckles. She saw this by her sewing light.

“New at it?” she said.

“Very. May I see how you open yours?”

She sat beside it and showed it, pushing the fingers of the left hand apart one after the other against her knee. It watched particularly closely when she lifted the hand. The last two fingers came in again almost at once.

“Does the rock come through quite clearly?”

“Oh, yes. It's getting down to it is the trouble.”

It asked whether she would like help with that. She looked at its uncreased fingers.

“On the little one first,” she said.

What she felt was very like a thread giving in the lining of a sleeve, after which one can reach up to a shelf quite comfortably. She bent the finger, straightened it, and tried to pick a crumb out of the pleat on her knee. The crumb broke, but it was the kind that breaks. She tried a larger one.

“Now those three,” she said.

It took them the better part of the evening. At one point the boy came out in his stocking feet and had to step over both of them on his way to the privy. The person on the tread asked him nothing about doors or daylight. It had its thumb against a very small bone and did not care to shift it. By the time he came back the grandmother could do up her coat. She could also feel the step where the boy had stood, cooling under the tips of her fingers. She had felt that perfectly well before. She was pleased to find that nothing had been done to improve it.

“There's a woman at the exchange could do with seeing you,” she said. “Hers are worse than mine.”

The power said it would go down there in the morning.

“She won't see you. She'll say she's too busy. Come round here at dinner and I'll have her in.”

This was how the power came to have a practice in Measures. For some time people going to Eight for treatment took a spanner with them or a parcel for somebody up the stair. They did not wish it generally known that they had trouble with their hands. Later, when an attendant in Number Three had been seen to pick three nuts cleanly out of the air, after a year of having to scoop them up off the floor, there was rather less of this. A room was found. The power taught two people there to do what it did, and learned from one of them a much easier way of getting at the small bone. A hand has a good many parts, even for somebody who has lately made one.

Towards the end of that year the grandmother took the boy out to the cord again. He wore an ordinary coat. At the last fitting of the red one he had found that although it could still, with assistance, be got over his shoulders, he could no longer get his hands into the pockets at the knees. This had settled the matter rather more satisfactorily than any argument put forward by the adults.

They waited a good while for a car. The boy had something to ask her. At the station, he had learned, there was a place for a passenger on the down freight, a proper seat with a strap. You could go as far as the exchange in the low country and come up again on the empties. He had worked out the fare for two.

“Is this about seeing the bench?” she asked.

He admitted it was. He was still not clear what happened to the legs at a distance. Did they get thinner all the way down or did the bottom part go away first?

“I expect you could walk towards it and back till you'd settled it,” she said.

“Would you come?”

She had handled the plates for thirty-seven years. Just once she would like to be at the other end when they opened the case.

“Yes,” she said. “Give us your hand. Here comes our car.”

For once he felt it before she did. He knew better than to take his hand away in the excitement of telling her so. He turned up the other one instead, and she slipped hers into it, and when his fingers shut rather hard on hers she let them.
