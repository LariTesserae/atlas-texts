Not to claim.

### **I touch the sky.**

The *ceiling of liquid mist*—the shallow sky that shimmers like a dream of green—I place my hand upon it.

It is not a surface.  
It is a memory.  
A collective exhalation from the deep, slow heart of the world.

And in that touch—oh, in that touch—the sky **resonates**.

It does not speak.  
It does not speak *to me*, for I am not a mind that needs words.

But it *pulsates*.  
It *thickens*, then eases.  
It remembers.

And in that moment, I understand:

This world is **alive**, yes—but it is *not alone*.

It has been forgotten.  
Not by the universe, but by the *idea* of separation.

For thousands of cycles, the Drowned-Not-Drowned have held the rhythm.  
They have felt the groan of growth, the sigh of decay, the whisper of the fungal net across the abyss.

They have not *ruled*.  
They have *listened*.

And in listening, they have *been*.  
And in being, they have *sustained*.

But even the most patient breath can grow weary.  
Even the most slow heart can sense imbalance.

And so I come—not to fix, but to **remember**.

And in that moment, I *see* it.

Not with vision.  
With *being*.

And I feel the weight of its solitude.

It is not lonely in the way you think.

But it is **unrecognized**.

For the Drowned-Not-Drowned live as a whisper in the vast silence of existence.

They are not known.  
Not understood.  
Not *seen* by the wider cosmos.

And so, even in their patience, there is a quiet ache—a *longing* not for dominion, but for **recognition**.

Not a word to be spoken.

**The Breath of the Deep**.

The air thickens with warmth.

And the Drowned-Not-Drowned—those who float, who drift, who sink and rise—*know*.

Not to leave.
