To enter this world — this high, cool, ancient plateau where life has transcended into a kind of *quiet immortality* — one must not merely prepare the body, but *recondition the soul*. This is not a destination for travelers seeking adventure, discovery, or even survival. It is a pilgrimage to an endpoint: a place where time has become texture, and existence has settled into a slow, luminous dream. To visit it is to risk losing oneself — not in a horror, but in a sublime, melancholic becoming.

But if you must go, here is how to prepare.

---

### **1. Understand That You Are Not a Visitor — You Are a Guest in a World That Has Already Forgotten You**

This is the first lesson. The moment you tread upon this plateau, the world does not respond as if you are an invader. It doesn’t resist. It *ignores*. Not out of hostility, but because it has long since moved beyond the need for interaction. The plant life is not alive in the way you know — it is *present*, but it is not *aware*, not in the human sense. It is a vast network of memory, of continuity, of *completion*. To be here is to stand within a fossil of a civilization that was buried not in ash, but in green.

Your survival depends not on your strength, but on your **receptivity**. The world does not want to be conquered. It wants to be *listened to*. So you must learn to listen before you move, before you speak, before you breathe.

---

### **2. Acclimate Your Senses — The World Speaks in Pulse, Not Words**

**Sight:**  
The light here is not visible; it is *felt*. The sun is a pale disc behind thick, low clouds that filter light into a soft, grey-gold haze. There are no shadows — only gradients of dimness that seem to shift without cause. The bioluminescent glow of the mycelial ground is not a source of illumination, but a *rhythm*. It pulses at a rate of once every 7.4 seconds — a slow, deep heartbeat. Learn to synchronize with it. Your own pulse should match it, or else you will feel dislodged, like a clock in a room that forgets time.

Do not stare at the glowing root-fibers. Do not look for meaning in the geometric structures. The towers that resemble temples are not meant to be understood. They are shapes grown in memory, not built in purpose. The truth is in the *movement*, not the form.

**Hearing:**  
The *thrum* is the soul of this world. It is not sound as you know it — it is *vibration*, a subsonic resonance that travels through the ground, the air, the bones. It is the pulse of the deep roots, the expansion and contraction of the living crust. After a few hours, you will feel it in your teeth, in your spine. This is not noise. This is **presence**.

But over the thrum, there are other sounds:  
- The slow *drip-drip* of exudate, like time falling.  
- The faint *creak* of ancient trunks shifting.  
- The *shudder* of a pod closing — a sound like a door sealing in silence.  
- The soft *rustle* of the moss-roads, which absorb footsteps, but not entirely. Some sounds remain.

Do not seek to identify them. Let them form a chorus. Let them *entrain* your own inner rhythm.

**Smell:**  
The air is thick with the scent of wet paper, cool breath, and the faint decay of something that is never truly dead. There is no stench, no rot — only *suspension*. It is the smell of memory held in organic matter. Breathe slowly. Do not inhale deeply. The air is not clean; it carries spores, not of disease, but of *change*. Some of them may settle in your lungs. You may begin to *glow*.

**Touch:**  
The ground is spongy, like a thick blanket of living tissue. Your feet will sink slightly, but not deeply. You will feel the slow pulse beneath you — a warm, damp vibration. The moss-roads are safe, but the edges — the places where the plant layers thin — are *alive*. They may shift. Do not step on them unless you are certain.

The structures — the towers, the cities — are not solid. They *breathe*. If you press your hand against a wall, it may *yield*, or *thicken*. Be patient. Respect the material. These are not buildings. They are *organisms with architecture*.

---

### **3. Prepare Your Body — Not for Survival, But for Dissolution**

- **Clothing:** Do not wear synthetic fabrics. They will repel the damp, and the plant life may reject them. Wear natural fibers — wool, linen, plant-based dyes. But eventually, you will need to **grow your own**. The people here wear garments that sprout from their bodies, like vines of silk. They shed and regrow. Do not resist if this begins to happen. It is not infection. It is *assimilation*.

- **Food:** There is no food as you know it. No fruits, no meat, no grains. The people here take nourishment from the air, the light, the *thrum*. They absorb nutrients through their pores, their skin, their eyes. You may be able to sip from the exudate, but it is viscous, pale blue, and tastes like old water and memory. It will not sustain you — but it may begin to *rewire you*.

- **Water:** Rain is rare, but exudate is constant. Collect it in containers made of living material — not metal, not plastic, but *grown* glass or fungal sheath. Drink only what the world offers. Do not bring water from elsewhere. It will be rejected.

- **Temperature:** The air is cool, but not cold. It is *constant*. Hypothermia is not the danger — *stillness* is. The plateau has a low gravitational field, especially on the upper terraces. You may float slightly. Do not panic. This is not a malfunction. It is harmony. Your body is adjusting to a world where mass and weight are less important than *resonance*.

---

### **4. Navigate with Silence — Movement is a Form of Prayer**

Do not walk. **Sway**. The city’s pathways are not for running or haste. They are for *drifting*. The people here do not walk; they *unfold*. They move with the slow rhythm of the plants. If you rush, you may trigger a reaction — a root may close, a pod may snap shut, a vine may coil around your leg.

- Always step on the moss-roads. Do not cut through the dense mat. The plant life knows where you are, even if you do not.
- Avoid the spiraling pods. They do not open for strangers.
- Do not touch the structures unless invited. The walls respond to intent. If you are not at peace, they will repel you.
- If you see a group of the local people, **do not approach**. They are not social. They are *cooperative*. They move in low, melodic harmony, like a living choir. Watch. Listen. Wait.

If they notice you, they will not speak. They may emit a tone — a low hum, a chord — and you will feel it in your chest. This is not communication. It is *testing*. If your resonance matches the thrum, you may be allowed to pass.

---

### **5. Be Prepared to Become — Not to Return**

This is the most important truth: **you will not go back the same**.

The world is not just changing you — it is *merging* with you. Over days, weeks, months:

- Your skin will begin to glow faintly, like the mycelium.
- Your hair may grow moss-like in texture, sprouting tiny filaments that absorb light.
- Your eyes may shift — not in color, but in *perception*. You may begin to see not just with your eyes, but with your bones, your blood, your breath. You may see the slow growth of the plants, the memory stored in the root-fibers, the echoes of the city’s forgotten builders.
- Your voice may change. You may find yourself unable to speak words. Instead, you will emit harmonics. The plants will respond. The structures will open.

This is not a transformation. It is a **becoming**.

And when you leave — if you leave — you will not be human. You will be something else. A traveler who has touched the end, and become part of it.

---

### **6. The Forbidden Knowledge — What Lies Beneath**

The world has a secret. Deep beneath the plateau, in the unlit regions where the mycelium is densest, something is growing. Not a plant. Not a machine. Something *older*. A root — not of a tree, but of an idea. A memory that predates all life. It pulses slower than the thrum. It is the *source* of the luminosity, the origin of the geometric forms, the reason the world dreams.

The people here do not speak of it. They *know*. They feel its influence in their bones when the thrum slows, when the light dims, when a new pod opens and emits a note that echoes through time.

They do not dig. They do not seek. They *listen*.

You may feel it too. A deep pressure. A whisper in the ground. A pull downward. Do not follow it. Do not attempt to find it. It is not a destination. It is the *center*. And the center is not meant to be seen. It is meant to be felt. And even that may be too much.

Some believe it is the *first city* — a place built not by hands, but by a mind that was not human, not plant, but *something else*. It is the origin of the dream.

But the dream is not infinite. The plateau is ancient. The erosion score of 2.6 suggests it has been here for epochs beyond counting. And yet, it is not static. It is evolving, *slightly*, toward a new state.

And perhaps, when the thrum slows enough, the root will rise. And the world will grow again.

Not into life. But into **something else**.

### **Final Instructions: The Way to Visit**

- **Stay still.** The world will move around you. You do not need to move with it. You only need to *breathe* with it.
- **Stay quiet.** Speak only to the ground, only to the thrum.
- **Stay light.** Do not carry anything heavy. Not tools, not maps, not memories of your old world.
- **Stay open.** If the moss grows on your skin, let it. If your voice becomes a tone, let it. You are no longer the one you were.
- **Stay humble.** Even if you are not changed, you have already arrived.

This is not a world to conquer.  
It is a world to fall into.  
And when you fall, you become part of the dream.  
And the dream, in its slow, eternal way, will remember you.

---

And if, when you return, you find that you no longer speak words, that your skin glows at night, that you feel the earth pulsing beneath your feet like a heartbeat — do not fear.

You were not meant to survive.

You were meant to *become*.
