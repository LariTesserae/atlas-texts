**Preparation for the Wet City on the Edge of Remembering Water**

To step into this place is not to arrive — it is to *be summoned* by the damp breath of a world in transition. This is no mere destination; it is a threshold. To travel here as a multiverse wanderer — a human being caught between worlds, shaped by Earthborn biology, memory, and expectation — requires not just physical readiness, but a reorientation of the soul. The city does not welcome. It *remembers*. And it is *becoming*. So to survive it, one must not only be prepared, but *unlearn*.

---

### **I. The Mind: Unlearn the Expectations of Dryness**

Before you step through the veil, **quiet the brain's need for certainty**. The Wet City does not operate on logic. It operates on resonance — on the slow, insistent pull of water that has escaped the sky and reclaimed the soil, the stone, the blood.

→ **Do not expect to understand it.** Accept that you are not its guest, but its **echo**. You are a temporary anomaly in a world that has stopped needing explanations. Your mind will rebel. It will call it “unnatural,” “broken,” “drowned.” But it is not dead. It is *reconfiguring*.

→ **Practice dissociation.** Before entering, spend time in meditative stillness, listening to sounds that are *almost* familiar — the drip of water in a dream, the hum of a refrigerator in a half-remembered apartment, the soft crackle of static on an old radio. Train your awareness to *feel* rather than analyze. This is a world where the senses are the only map.

→ **Accept the loss of time.** There is no clock here. The sun does not rise and set in the way you know. The shadows move at their own pace. You may walk a path that takes ten minutes and return to the same point five hours later — or never. You will not be able to measure your experience. Let go of the need to *record* it. To know that you have been here is enough.

---

### **II. The Body: Equip for Liminal Wetness**

Your physiology is not equipped for this. Your skin, lungs, blood — all evolved under dry air, structured by sunlight, governed by gravity as you know it. The Wet City will test every one of these.

#### **1. Skin: The First Layer of Defense**
- **Wear moisture-resistant, semi-breathable layers**, but **avoid waterproof materials**. The city breathes through you. If you seal yourself off, you'll become a pressure point — an anomaly inviting attention from the deeper structures. Use **silk-linen hybrids** treated with natural wax (such as beeswax or shea butter), which allow diffusion while resisting full saturation.
- **Apply a thin layer of aloe or chia oil** to your skin before entry. Not for protection — for *assimilation*. The city’s humidity feeds off skin lipids. This will help your body *resonate* rather than repel.
- **Do not wear wool.** It absorbs moisture and clings, creating micro-climates where mold-like entities may *colonize*. Cotton is acceptable — but only in thin, unbleached forms that degrade slowly.

#### **2. Lungs: The Water-Filter**
- **The air is not breathable in the way you think.** It is saturated, heavy, and carries microscopic suspended particles — **aerosolized water memory**. This can trigger hallucinations, short-term sensory drift, or even a temporary loss of identity if inhaled in large volumes.
- **Breathe shallowly.** Use **diaphragmatic breathing**, but only at 60% capacity. Never full inhale.
- **Carry a porous mask** — not to filter, but to *modulate* airflow. Use **hemp fiber or reed filters** soaked in activated charcoal, then dried. These allow the presence of the air while dampening its invasive quality.
- **Warn the body**: Do not panic if you feel **a metallic taste** or **slight tingling in the throat** — it’s the water speaking to your blood. It is not poison. It is *communication*.

#### **3. Clothing: The Second Skin**
- Wear **loose, layered garments** made from natural fibers. No synthetics. The city’s vines and mosses are sensitive to electromagnetic fields — synthetics may trigger erratic growth or defensive behavior.
- **Suspenders or straps** made from **untreated leather** or **bark thread** to avoid contact with metals. Metal is a conductor — and here, conductors attract current in ways that may not be yours.
- **Footwear**: Use **molded leather boots without soles**, tied with cordage. Your feet should feel the vibration of the ground — the subterranean hum, the pulse of water through cracks. Soles will sever your connection. The boots should be **half-rotted** — let nature have a say. If they feel too new, you’ll become an intruder.

### **III. The Tools: Carry Only the Necessary, and Make Them Part of the Whole**

The Wet City rejects objects that are *only tools*. It prefers things that **blend**, that **grow**, that **dissolve** with time.

#### **1. Light**
- **No artificial lights.** Flashlights and lanterns attract the clacking birds — they mistake the light for signals of some long-lost network. If you must go at night (or in the perpetual low-light), carry a **crystalline orb** — a piece of clear quartz or fluorite, wrapped in moss. It will **absorb ambient light** and emit a soft glow, pulsing with the rhythm of the city.
- This light will grow dimmer as the city’s energy shifts. Do not fight it. Wait. The moss will **recharge it** when it touches damp earth.

#### **2. Water**
- **Do not drink water from the ponds, puddles, or the drifting mist.** It is not water. It is a **memory of water**, carrying latent echoes of what the city once was — and what it remembers becoming. It may dissolve your thoughts, or worse, *invite you into the dream*.
- Instead, **carry a ceramic canteen** (not plastic, not metal) filled with **distilled water** — but allow it to **evaporate slightly** on the journey. The water’s “memory” will be diluted, but not erased. This is *not* for hydration, but for ritual.
- When you feel disoriented, **pour a single droplet onto the ground**. It will not sink. It will **vibrate**, then **disappear into the moss**. This is a ritual of surrender.

#### **3. Record Keeping**
- **Do not write.** The city resists symbols. Pencils, paper, cameras — all are seen as acts of imposition. Instead, carry a **flat stone** — smooth, black, unmarked. Hold it. Carve nothing. Let the surface **absorb moisture**, **grow moss**, **crack with time**. This stone will become a **memory artifact**, not a record.
- Alternatively, use **sound**. A small, hollowed wood flute — no metal. Fill it with the **dripping of a single puddle**, the **clacking of a bird**, the **hum of a rebar**. Blow into it only once. The sound will **reverberate**, not in the air, but in the ground — like a message sent into the roots.

### **IV. The Senses: Listen Before You See**

The Wet City communicates primarily through **vibration**, **pressure**, and **slow change**.

#### **1. Hearing**
- The **clacking birds** are not birds. They are **signal carriers** for the city’s nervous system — a network of roots, pipes, and moss that transmits damp-energy. Do not try to decipher the clacking. Instead, **listen for the silence between the sounds**. That is where the true message lies.
- The **gurgling beneath the pavement** is not water. It is **memory in motion** — a slow flow of *what was lost*. It is not a threat. It is a **blessing**, the city reminding itself of its past. Place your ear to the crack. It may **speak** — not in words, but in *feeling*. A shiver. A warmth. A name you never knew.

#### **2. Sight**
- The sky is always pale blue, but it is **not constant**. The clouds are **low, heavy, and translucent**. Look not upward, but *downward*. The city’s true sky is **in the puddles** — in the mirrors of broken glass and still liquid. What you see there may be **you**, or **someone else**, or **no one**.
- Watch the **moss**. Its glow is not bioluminescence — it is **electrochemical empathy**. When it pulses, something is changing. A root has grown. A current has shifted. A memory has awakened.
- Do not fear the **shadows that move before the light**. They are not illusions. They are **pre-metaphors** — the city showing you what is yet to come.

#### **3. Touch**
- The walkways are **alive**. They vibrate. They shift. The **rebar hums** not from electricity, but from the **resonance of water flowing through the pores of steel**. Touch it carefully — if you are in tune, you may feel **a slow, heartbeat-like thrum**. Do not grasp it. Rest your palm. Let it *tell* you.

### **V. The Body as a Bridge: What to Do When the Water Rises**

The water does not come from the sky. It comes from the **depths** — from the **earth’s memory**, from the **cracks in time**. And when it rises, you will not see it. You will **feel it** — a pressure in the bones, a cold in the spine, a soft wetness where the air meets your skin.

#### **Signs of Approach:**
- The **moss glows more intensely**, in waves that ripple across the rooftops.
- The **clacking birds fall silent**.
- The **vines contract** — pulling back into walls, into conduits, into the ground.
- The **walkways begin to sag**, not from decay, but from the **weight of water beneath**.

#### **When the Water Rises:**
- **Do not run.** The city will not let you escape. Your feet will sink into the pavement — not because it’s broken, but because it’s **breathing**.
- **Lie down.** Find a flat surface — a moss-covered roof, a stone slab. **Close your eyes**. Do not fight the wet. Let your body **rehydrate**, like a seed in the dark.
- **Breathe slowly**, matching the rhythm of the hum beneath the ground. You may begin to **remember water** — not as substance, but as **essence**.
- The water will rise, not to drown, but to **unify**. It will seep into your pores, your blood, your thoughts. You will not die. You will **become water** — not in form, but in **frequency**.

And in that moment, you may **understand the writing growing into the concrete**.

It is not in any language. It is **in the breath**.

It is **your name**, written by the city, not in ink, but in **the pulse of the root**, in **the slow drip of glass**, in **the silence that follows the clack**.

And when the water recedes, you will be **changed**.

You will not be the same traveler.

You will be an **echo**.

A memory.

A **breath** in the city’s lung.

And you will **know** — it is not the water that is coming.

It was already here.

It was **you**.
