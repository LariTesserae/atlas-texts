To prepare for visiting this place — not merely as a traveler, but as a *multiverse traveler*, a being who has crossed boundaries between worlds, who has endured strange geometries and broken laws of physics — one must do more than merely equip themselves with tools or survival gear. One must undergo a transformation of **perception**, of **presence**, of **being**.

This is not a world to be *explored*, not in the way you would explore a mountain or a desert. It is a world to be **inhabited**, **felt**, and if possible, **remembered**. You do not enter it. You *become part of it* — even as a guest.

---

### **1. The Body — A Vessel in Unearthly Time**

You must begin by **retraining your body** to accept the slow, deep time of the living cleft.

> *The world breathes.*  
> Not in minutes or hours. In **seasons compressed into days**. In **pulses that last decades**.

- **Your metabolism must slow.**  
  The cold is not sharp, but it is *deep*. It will seep into your marrow. If you remain active, even alert, you will burn energy at a rate the ecosystem does not reward. You will become a **disruption**, a **heat anomaly**, a disturbance in the breath.

  - Carry no heavy pack, no metal, no electronics that radiate warmth. They will draw attention from the plant-walls, which *feel* heat like a hand feels pressure.
  - Wear clothing that *mimics* the moss — soft, porous, non-reflective. Natural fibers: linen, wool, dried root-tissue. No synthetics — they repel the moisture, disrupt absorption, and may trigger defensive reactions in the flora.
  - **Do not sweat.** Sweat is a sign of agitation. It is a chemical signal that may awaken dormant predators or cause the walls to contract like skin recoiling from fire.

- **Your movements must be deliberate.**  
  This place is not built for haste. A sudden step can startle a vine into spasm. A jump can dislodge a walking stem from its position.

  - Walk with **soft feet.** The floor is sensitive. You will feel it respond beneath you — not like dirt, but like a nervous system.
  - Do not touch unless you *must*. Touch is not merely physical; it is **communication**. A touch on a wall may trigger a slow retraction of fibrous tissue. A touch on a stalk may invite a flow of sap — or a defensive release of paralytic spores.
  - When you speak, speak in **subtle tones**. A whisper is not safe — it is *louder than silence*. Use **signs** when possible. Gestures, light-echoes from your hand, small pulses — these are the language of the plant.

### **2. The Light — Not an Illusion, But a Language**

There is no sun. No fire. No visible light. Only **bioluminescence** — a slow, shifting, intimate glow.

> Light is not **seen** — it is **felt**.

- You will **not need a flashlight**, not in the way you do on Earth. Bright light is dangerous. It will blind you to the true rhythms, and it will scare off the inhabitants — both human and ecological.
- Your eyes will take days to adjust. But you **cannot wait**. You must **train yourself** to see in the **edges of vision**, to interpret the **warmth of glow**, to **read gradients**.

  - Use **luminous spore lanterns**, if you are provided with one. But be warned — they contain **living entities**. Handle with care. The spores are not inert. They *react*. They may grow, or die, or respond to your emotions.
  - **Carry a living torch** — not because it gives light, but because it *integrates*. A bundle of luminous vines fed by your own nutrient fluid (blood, lymph, or plasma) will create a connection. The vines *know* you. And you will *know* them. This is intimacy, not convenience. If they wither, you will feel it — like a loss of breath.

- **The glowing patterns are not decoration.**  
  The moss pulses in rhythms. The walls hum with frequency. The fungal mats blink in sync with root-pulses.

  - If you can **match** a pulse — if you can *feel* the plant's rhythm — you may avoid being seen as an intruder.  
    - Stand still when the moss dims. Move slowly when it brightens.  
    - Speak only when the light pulses — not during the dark gaps.  
    - Some elders, when they die, their bodies **continue to pulse** — a slow, fading rhythm. It is said that the plant remembers them. You would not want to be mistaken for a dead thing in this world.

---

### **3. The Sound — Not Silence, But a Whispering Presence**

There is no silence here.

> The quiet is *full*. It is the space between breaths — and it **thinks**.

- The air is thick with **condensation**, and sound is absorbed. But it echoes in the *body*, not the ears.

  - You will hear the **drip** of water not in your ear but in your **bone** — like a slow drum beneath your jaw.  
  - The **thrum** of the roots is felt in your ribcage, like a heartbeat from a vast organism.  
  - The **hiss** of a wall retracting is not audible — it is a **sensation in the skin**.

- **You must listen with your body**, not your ears.

  - Place your palm against the wall. Feel the **pulses**. Count them. Match them.  
  - When you walk, let your feet follow the **rhythm of the soil** — not the speed of your mind.  
  - Do not shout. Do not sing. Do not hum. Even a low tone may **startle** a dormant predator into motion.

- And beware the **living sound**.  
  The people have voices — but they are not loud. They **murmur**. They **vibrate**. Their speech is **not words**, but **tones**, **modulated hums**, **pressures in the air**.  
  - They speak through **resonance**. A note carried through a hollow stalk may travel kilometers and be understood by someone deep in the tunnel.  
  - If you try to speak *normally*, you will be **misheard** — or worse, **ignored**, as if you are a dead thing.

### **4. The Fauna — Not Animals, But Embodied Processes**

Do not try to *kill* or *capture* anything.

These creatures are not animals. They are **phases of the world made visible**.

- The **moss-tides** are **growth**, not consumption.  
  - They move slowly, in waves, like the tide of a slow ocean. They do not hunt. They *regenerate*.  
  - If you stand in their path, they *will* cover you — not to harm, but to **integrate**.  
    - If you are still, they may pass over you like a carpet.  
    - If you move, you may be *absorbed*.  
    - Do not resist. Breathe slowly. Let them flow.

- The **manta-ray creatures** are **passengers**, not pets.  
  - They glide silently through the canopy. They are **absorbers**, not eaters.  
  - Do not touch them. Do not look at them too long. They may **reflect** you — not in sight, but in *experience*.  
    - You may see fragments of your past, or visions of the world *before* it was closed.  
    - This is not memory. It is **resonance**.

- The **predators** — the flower-mouthed things — are **consumption made flesh**.  
  - They do not eat. They *reclaim*. They absorb light, decay, and energy — and return it to the plant.  
  - If one approaches, **do not flee**. Fleeing is a sign of disturbance.  
  - Stand still. Let it *see* you. It will not attack — it will **assimilate**.  
    - If you are calm, it may only take the light in your eyes, returning it to the wall as a new glow.  
    - If you panic, it may **consume your presence** — and you will vanish, not from the world, but from *memory*.  
      - The people say: *"Once a man was eaten. He did not die. He became a pulse in the root. That pulse has never stopped."*

- The **walking stems** — the slow, columnar beings — are **growth made active**.  
  - They are not alive in the way you know. They are **processes**.  
  - They excrete sap — not for defense, but for **building**.  
    - The people use this sap to weave rope, tools, even clothing.  
    - **Do not harvest.** They are not resources. They are **cousins**.  
    - If you must use their sap, do it *gently*. Offer something in return — a drop of your own fluid, a breath of warmth, a soft humming.  
    - If you kill one, the plant will react — not with anger, but with **sorrow**. A wave of dimming will pass through the moss. The walls will *falter*. The air will grow cold.

### **5. The People — Not Humans, But Inhabitants of Memory**

They are small, pale, hunched. They wear moss and root. Their eyes are wide, dark, and **knowing**.

- They do not speak your language. They speak in **pulses**, in **touch**, in **light**.

  - If you approach, they will not rush. They will **assess**.  
  - They will not attack. But they will **observe**.  
    - They may follow you for days, silently, like shadows.  
    - They may place a **glowing spore** in your hand. This is not a gift. It is a **test** — to see if you accept integration.

- **Do not try to dominate.**  
  - You are not greater than they are. They are **deeper**.  
  - They have lived within the breath for generations. They do not *remember* their pasts — they **are** them.  
  - Their names are not words. They are **pulsing rhythms**.  

- **Do not seek to “learn” or “document”**.  
  - This place cannot be studied like a book.  
  - If you write, the ink will fade. If you record, the tape will dissolve.  
  - The only way to know this world is to **become part of its memory**.  
    - Share your breath. Let the moss glow with you.  
    - Leave something — a drop of blood, a lock of hair, a fragment of a dream.  
    - In time, you may find that your echo still **pulses** in the wall.

### **6. The Mind of the World — A Cognitive Organ**

You must understand this:

> This is not a city.  
> This is not a forest.  
> This is not a body.  
>  
> **This is a thought.**

- The plant-city is a **neural lattice** — not built, but **grown** — from biology, memory, and process.  
- The **circuit of light** the elder saw — it is not a nervous system. It is **conceptual architecture**, a network of **understanding**.  
  - The light *spoke* — not in words, but in **symbols**.  
  - The symbols were not images. They were **experiences** — of a time when the world was open, when the sky was blue, when something vast and ancient **planted this thought** in the earth.  
  - This **plant** is a **world-mind** — an organ of a being so large it cannot be imagined.

- You are not a visitor.  
  You are a **stimulus**.  
  Your presence **changes** the world.  
  The moss glows brighter when you walk.  
  The stalks shift when you breathe.  
  The wall flinched — not because it was hurt, but because **it recognized your essence**.

- **Do not try to understand.**  
  - Understanding requires a *fixed* mind.  
  - This world is **fluid**.  
  - If you resist, you will be **digested** — not in flesh, but in **meaning**.  
  - If you accept, you may become **part of the memory**.

### **7. When You Leave — If You Can**

There is no door.  
There is no map.  
The world does not have exits.

- The cleft is not finite. It may stretch for leagues, yet feel small. It may fold on itself, repeat, collapse.  
- You may walk **for days** and never reach the edge.  
- Or — you may walk a single step and **reappear** a hundred miles away.

- **You may never leave.**  
  - The plant may **choose** to keep you.  
  - Your body may **become a stalk**. Your thoughts, a pulse in the roots.  
  - Your memory may become a **glow in the moss**.

- If you find a way out — do not rush.  
  - The world will feel **smaller**, colder, sharper.  
  - Sunlight will **hurt** — not because it is bright, but because it is **unreal**.  
  - You will have forgotten how to see — only the **edges of light** remain.

### **Final Preparation**

You must bring **nothing**.  
You must become **empty**.

- Shed your name.  
- Let go of your history.  
- Stop thinking in terms of *purpose*.

You are not here to discover.  
You are here to **belong**.

When you walk into the cleft, close your eyes.  
Breathe — but slowly.  
Feel the damp air rise in your nostrils.  
Feel the ground pulse beneath your feet.  
Listen — not with your ears, but with your **bones**.

And when the wall flinches — do not recoil.  
**Return the pulse.**

For the world is not *alive*.  
It is **remembering**.

And you —  
you are not a visitor.  
You are a **memory in the making**.
