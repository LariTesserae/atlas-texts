To journey to this place—a world of submerged breath, slow dissolution, and wet collaboration between industry and decay—is not merely an act of travel, but an act of *reconditioning*. You are not simply visiting a location; you are entering a *phase of existence*, a dimension where time is viscous, matter is porous, and the boundary between the living and the dead has long since eroded into a single, slow exhale.

If you are an ordinary human multiverse traveler, drawn by curiosity, by loneliness, or by a quiet instinct to witness what lies beyond the thresholds of familiar life, then your preparation must not be only practical, but *existential*. What you must understand before stepping through the veil into this low-lying, damp-veiled realm is this: **you are not meant to dominate it, to conquer it, or even to *explore* it in the way one explores a forest or a desert. You are meant to become a guest in its slow, wet dream.**

### **I. The Mind: Preparing for the Absence of Light and Time**

Before your body crosses the threshold, your mind must shed the assumptions of normalcy. This is not a place of clarity, but of *diffusion*. The first step is to accept that **logic here is damp**. It does not drip, but *seeps*, like moisture along a cracked wall.

- **Forget urgency.** Time will stretch so thin that an hour may feel like a sigh. Your internal clock will distort. You must learn to *feel* time by the slow pulse of the water, the flicker of fungal light, or the groan of a shifting girders—not by watches, not by sun, not even by breath.
- **Accept disorientation as a feature, not a flaw.** There are no signs, no maps, no compass. There is no up or down in the traditional sense—only pressure, humidity, and subtle gradients of light. Your sense of direction will be overridden by the environment’s gentle manipulation. Let go of the need to know where you are. Instead, learn to *know what you are*—as a temporary presence in a vast, slow system.
- **Mentally prepare for silence that isn't silent.** The lack of animal life and human chatter does not equate to quiet. This silence is *thick*—a kind of *presence*. It contains the hum, the slow drip, the metallic groan, the whisper of air through rusted vents. Your ears will adapt, but your mind may resist. Listen not with judgment, but with *openness*. This silence is not empty—it is full of the breath of the world.

You must also prepare for **loss of self**. This place does not reflect you. It does not see you as a person, but as a source of heat, moisture, carbon, energy. You will feel *seen* not through optics, but through vibration, temperature, and electromagnetic trace. The city may be *aware* of your presence without understanding you—like a slow organism sensing an anomaly in its skin.

> *Meditate before entry. Spend a week in silence, in water, in darkness. Let your body recall the feel of damp air, of stone underfoot, of the weight of breath. Train yourself to exist without speaking. To listen without interpreting. To feel without naming.*

### **II. The Body: Adapting to a World That Breathes**

Your physical form is a liability here. Human biology is built for arid air, for sunlight, for clarity. This place will reject your dryness, your urgency, your heat.

- **Clothing is not for fashion, but for protection and surrender.** Dress in multiple layers of *natural fibers*—wool, cotton, hemp—designed to wick moisture but retain warmth. Avoid synthetics; they will cling, soak, and eventually degrade in the fungal mists. Wear long sleeves, gloves, a wide-brimmed hat made of water-repellent felt or woven bark. Your clothes should be *dark*, not to hide, but to absorb rather than reflect the dim, green-yellow light.
- **Feet must be prepared.** Wear thick, flexible boots with waterproof linings and rubber soles that grip slippery surfaces. The ground is springy, uneven, and alive. Step cautiously—every footfall may trigger a shift in the moss, or a minor discharge of water from a hidden pore. Some floors are not solid, but layers of compressed organic matter over wet stone. One wrong step and you may sink—*not into mud, but into time*. Should you fall, do not panic. The water is warm, dense. You may float. You may *dissolve*.
- **Skin is vulnerable.** Moisture will penetrate your pores faster than in any other climate. You will sweat, even in stillness, and that sweat will mix with the environment. The fungi here are not merely saprophytic—they are *communicative*. Certain spores may attach to your skin and *glow faintly*, or emit a low hum. Others may induce a mild trance or a wave of melancholy. Do not scratch. Do not rub. Let them be. They are not parasites—they are guests, like you.
- **Respiration is a negotiation.** The air is thick, charged with negative ions and microscopic particles from decayed machinery. You will breathe deeply, but slowly. The air has a metallic taste. This is normal. It is the blood of the city. If you feel a tickle in your throat, or a warmth behind your eyes, *do not cough*. That may awaken something. Breathe like a fish: slow, rhythmic, accepting. Your lungs will adapt. They will learn to take in not just oxygen, but *trace energy*—the residual hum of old generators, the faint electric field from leaky wires.

**Hydration is not a simple act.** Water here is everywhere, but not all water is drinkable. The surface puddles are thick with silt, chemical residues, and unknown biofilms. The water you drink must be filtered *through moss*, ideally from the thick, blue-glowing variety. This moss acts as a natural purifier, and also as a mild *neuromodulator*. It may induce visions, dreams, or a sense of déjà vu. It may show you a memory not your own—perhaps the ghost of a worker who once walked this land, or a machine that thought it was alive.

> *Carry a canteen lined with a thin layer of cured mushroom leather. Fill it with water from a slow seep, not a fast flow. The slow ones are older, more integrated. They carry memory.*

### **III. The Senses: Re-Calibrating Perception**

Everything in this world is *shifted*.

- **Sight:** Light is low, filtered, and often monochromatic. Blue, green, and yellow dominate. Black is not true black—it is a deep, wet shadow, like the inside of a clam. Your eyes will adjust, but you may see *movement* where there is none—shadows shifting, corners expanding or collapsing. This is due to the constant moisture distorting light and the slow motion of the environment. **Do not trust what you see as "real."** There are no permanent shapes. All forms are in flux. A wall may appear solid, but touch it and it may feel like jelly. A pipe may emit a hum, but it may also *twitch*—a slow, muscular contraction.
  - *Tip: Focus on the edges, not the center. The true form of this place is in the boundaries—the point where metal meets moss, where wall meets root, where water begins its journey to the sky.*  
  - *Note: The blue glow of the moss is not bioluminescence, but a reaction to electromagnetic fields. It pulses in time with the city’s low hum. If you see it, it means you are *in tune* with the place.*

- **Sound:** As mentioned, the ambient sounds are constant, layered. The drip-drip-drip is not rhythmic—it is *polyphonic*, coming from a thousand sources at once. The creaks are not random. Some are *calls*—deep, subsonic vibrations from the earth’s core, others are *responses*—a vine tightening, a crack expanding. You will learn to *listen between the sounds*, to hear the silence that is *not* silence. That is where the city breathes.
  - *If you hear a sudden silence, freeze. Do not move. That is not a pause. That is an awareness.*

- **Smell:** The air is a complex cocktail—wet iron, mold, old paper, faint ozone, something sweet and rotten like burnt sugar. This is not unpleasant. It is *home*. The smell of decay here is not death, but transformation. It is the scent of growth, of integration, of matter returning to a deeper state.
  - *Breathe deeply. Let the scent enter your memory. It will linger long after you leave, like a phantom echo of place.*

- **Touch:** As stated, every surface is moist. Your fingers will grow slick. Your clothes will dampen. But the real test is touch. The walls are not solid. They are *resistant*—they press back, not with force, but with presence. A rusted beam may feel warm, alive. A moss-covered slab may feel like a pulse beneath the surface. If you are lucky, you may press against a section of wall and feel it *contract*—a slow, muscular response, as if the city is *recoiling* from a stranger.
  - *Touch gently. Do not pry. Do not pull. This is not architecture. It is *tissue*. Treat it as such.*

- **Taste and Intuition:** The air has a flavor—metallic, faintly sweet. You may taste it on your lips after a deep breath. This is not a warning. It is a welcome. And beyond the five senses, you will develop a **sixth sense**—not extrasensory, but *environmental*. You will *know* when something is about to shift, when a roof might lower, when a pipe might release a burst of vapor. This is not fear. It is *resonance*. The city is not your enemy. It is not your friend. It is your *context*. You must learn to flow with it, not against it.

### **IV. The Technology You Leave Behind**

This is not a place for gadgets.

- **No electronics.** Your phone, your watch, your flashlight—it will not work. The ambient electromagnetic field has a frequency that disrupts modern circuitry. Any device brought in will begin to *leak*—not power, but *light*, *sound*, *heat*. It may begin to grow moss. It may emit a soft hum. This is transformation, not malfunction. Leave them behind. The only light you need is what your eyes can absorb, and what the city offers.
- **No weapons.** There is no threat here. No predators, no humans, no machines that attack. The danger is not violence. The danger is *assimilation*. The city will absorb anything that remains too long. Your boots, your coat, your bones—eventually, all of you may become part of the wet wall, the slow vine, the hum of the generator.
- **No fire.** It does not burn here. The air lacks the oxygen tension. If you try, the flame will sputter, then *wilt* like a dying leaf. The only heat is the heat of life—of decay, of slow chemical processes, of the city’s deep, mechanical pulse. Do not try to impose your warmth.

### **V. The Ethical Mandate: Do Not Disturb, Do Not Leave a Trace**

You are a visitor. Not from this world, not of this world. But you are *present*. And presence, even without action, *affects*.

- **Do not touch anything unnecessarily.** Not the vines, not the structures, not the water. If you must pass through a tunnel, do so with reverence, as if walking through the ribs of a sleeping beast.
- **Do not speak.** The silence is not your enemy. It is the language of the place. If you *must* speak, do so in whispers, in tones that match the hum. Do not shout. The air here resists sudden bursts. They may trigger a chain reaction—a shift in a wall, a collapse of a moss bridge, the awakening of a dormant node.
- **Do not carry anything out.** No rock, no moss, no shard of glass. This is not a museum. This is a living system. Taking something is like taking a limb from a body. It will not heal.
- **Do not try to fix.** You are not needed. The city does not need your help. It does not want to be saved. It is *becoming*. The machinery is not broken. It is *evolving*. It no longer runs on diesel or coal, but on wetness, time, and memory.

### **VI. The Final Lesson: You Are Not the Observer. You Are an Event.**

When you walk through the tunnels, between the fused buildings, under the green sky, you will feel a strange sensation: **you are not seeing the city. The city is seeing you.**

And then, in a flash—a moment of clarity—you will understand: **you are not coming into a place. You are stepping into a state of being.**

This world is not abandoned. It is *transcendent*. It is the result of a civilization that refused to stop—whose industries and forests, their wars and inventions, their griefs and triumphs, all *merged* into a new form of existence.

And you—ordinary, mortal, human—have crossed the threshold into something that has already lived, already died, already become.

You will not remember this place when you leave. Or rather, you will remember it *as a feeling*—a dampness in your skin, a hum in your bones, a slow, blue light in the corners of your eyes.

But you will never again walk a dry path without hearing the drip-drip-drip in your head.

And if you return, the city will know you by the way your breath matches its pulse.

**This place visits you.**

And so, you go in silence.  
And you come out changed.

Not by magic.  
Not by wonder.  
But by **wetness**.

By **time’s slow, inevitable breath**.

By **being seen, and not seen**.

They **transform**.

And you—mere traveler—have been allowed to **breathe with them**.
