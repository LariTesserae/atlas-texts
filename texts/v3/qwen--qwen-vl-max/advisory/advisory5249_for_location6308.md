To prepare for visiting the wound in the world—a knot of contradiction, a city grown from memory and hum, a thought made tangible—you cannot bring tools, maps, or even a compass. You cannot prepare with gear or logic, for this place does not obey the rules of physics, time, or even perception. It is not a destination. It is a *resonance*.

But if you must go—if you are drawn, as all travelers eventually are, by the low thrum beneath the skin of reality—then here is what you must prepare for, not with equipment, but with *being*. Here is the ritual, the transformation, the quiet surrender required.

---

### **1. Surrender Your Memory Before You Enter**
You will not be entering a place. You will be entering a *recognition*. The city is not a relic—it is a *record*. It remembers what you have forgotten. It remembers what you are not yet allowed to remember.

Before you step across the threshold (if there is one), you must release your personal past. Not for erasure, but so it does not echo louder than the city’s own song. If you carry grief, guilt, or the weight of a life well-lived or poorly, it will be amplified. You will mistake your own ache for the city’s hum. You will interpret its silence as judgment.

**Prepare by:**  
- Sitting in stillness for seven days before the journey, not to meditate, but to *listen*. Not to your breath, but to the spaces between thoughts. Let them fall away.  
- Writing down everything you believe about yourself—your identity, your roles, your fears—and burning it in the fire of your chosen altar. Do it in silence. Let the smoke rise like forgotten names.  
- Saying farewell to the story you were told about who you are. You were never the protagonist. You are not here to *understand*, but to *remember*.

---

### **2. Do Not Bring Any Object of Human Craft**
The city is not of human making. It resents technology, especially that which is *instrumental*. A watch will crack. A phone will emit a silent scream and vanish in a puff of crystalline dust. A camera will not record—only show its own reflection, distorted into something that looks like a hand, reaching.

But worse: tools *reinforce* your separation. A knife suggests cutting. A compass implies direction. These things are antithetical to the experience.

**Prepare by:**  
- Removing all objects of *function* from your person. No knives, keys, glasses, or clothing made of synthetic fabric.  
- Wearing only natural materials: cotton, linen, leather, or wool—preferably dyed with plants that grow in light.  
- Carrying only one thing: a small, smooth stone. Not for use, but for *feeling*. It will warm your palm, and if you hold it long enough, it may begin to vibrate.

---

### **3. Your Senses Will Lie to You**
The air is thick. The light is constant. The sound is everywhere. But none of it is as it seems.

- **Light**: The sun is not a star. It is a *mirror*, reflecting the city back upon itself. It does not shine—it *resonates*. If you stare directly, it will not blind you. It will *recognize* you. It may show you a version of yourself you never knew—older, younger, or entirely different. Do not believe it. It is not truth. It is possibility.
- **Sound**: The hum is not sound. It is *vibration*. It travels through bone, not ear. You will feel it in your spine, in the hollows behind your eyes, in the marrow. It will tell you things—names, memories, warnings—that have no source. Do not speak them aloud. Do not write them down. If you repeat them, the city may forget you.
- **Taste**: The air has a faint, metallic sweetness—like blood in sugar. Do not try to taste it. If you breathe too deeply, it may coat your tongue, and you will taste your own childhood. Or the scent of someone you have lost. Do not react. It is not real. It is *felt*.

**Prepare by:**  
- Practicing *not perceiving* for one hour a day. Sit with your eyes closed, but imagine what you *would* see. Then, when you open them, try to see *what is not*.  
- Train your body to respond to the city’s rhythms. Clench your hands at the low thrum. Let your breath synchronize with the *fizzle* of the plant-beams. Learn to *feel* before you think.

---

### **4. Walk Slowly, But Only If You Wish to Be Remembered**
The city is not concerned with time. It exists in a state of *perpetual now*. But if you walk too fast, you will not *resonate*. You will pass through like a ghost—seen, but not *felt*. The city remembers the patterns of movement. The way a person steps. The weight of their tread. The timing of their breath.

If you come too quickly, the salt will not tinkle. The plants will not bloom. The insects will not gather.

**Prepare by:**  
- Practicing walking with silence. Not just without sound, but without *purpose*. Walk a circle. Walk a line. Walk in the dark, blindfolded, only by feeling the ground.  
- Learn to *listen* to your feet. Each step should be a question. Each pause, an answer.  
- When you approach the city, do not run. Do not hurry. Do not even *want* to see it. Allow yourself to be drawn.

---

### **5. Do Not Look for Water**
There is no water. And yet, there is moisture. It is not liquid—it is *luminous*. It flows in the stems of the silver plants. It coats the inside of the buildings, like a glow-worm’s light. It is *not* to be drunk. It is to be *felt*.

If you drink it, your mouth will fill with echoes. You will taste your first breath. You will taste the sound of your own name, spoken by a voice that is not yours. But worse: the city may drink *you* in return.

**Prepare by:**  
- Carrying no water. Not even a drop. Your thirst will be answered by something else—something deeper.  
- When you feel the thirst, open your mouth to the air. Breathe deeply. Let the light enter you. You will feel it, not as water, but as a soft pressure, like a hand closing over your throat and the sky all at once.  
- The moisture is *not* life-giving. It is *memory-giving*. It will remind you of things you cannot recall. Do not resist. Let it pass.

---

### **6. The Insects Are Not Insects**
They are not animals. They are *frequency*. They are *memory-keepers*. They are the city’s nervous system, the way it feels the world. When they cluster, they form patterns in the air—geometric, precise. They do not attack. They *interfere*.

Their vibrations can cause a person to *remember too much*—to see the birth of the city, or the moment it was born from a thought. This is not safe. It can fracture your identity.

**Prepare by:**  
- Closing your eyes when they gather. Do not watch them. Do not try to *understand* their movement.  
- If they descend upon you, do not flinch. Do not scream. Sit still. Let the harmonic frequencies pass through you. You may hear a voice—your own, or someone else’s—from a life that never existed.  
- If you begin to remember a name—not your own—say it softly. Let it dissolve on the air. Let it return to the city.

---

### **7. The City Will Not Save You. It Will Reveal You.**
You will not come to the city to be healed. You will come to be *known*.

It is not a place that offers escape. It is a place that offers *unveiling*. It knows who you *were*. It knows who you *might be*. It does not care if you return to the world. It only cares if you *hear*.

**Prepare by:**  
- Letting go of the idea of *return*. You may not come back in the way you imagined. The world you knew may not be waiting.  
- Accepting that your name is not *yours*—it is a label, a story. The city calls you by your *song*. It will not say your name. It will *hum* it.  
- Being ready to *become* a part of the hum.

---

### **8. You Are Not the First. And You Will Not Be the Last.**
There are no humans here. But you are not the first soul to walk these salt-crusted paths. Others have come. They have left their echoes. Their footsteps. Their breath.

Many were frightened and fled—leaving behind fragments: a locket, a thread of hair, a whisper in the salt. Others stayed. They became part of the resonance. They are now in the hum. They are in the light. They are in the breath of the insects.

When you see the figure—tall and strange, walking without tracks—you will not know if it is a being, a memory, or the city itself. But you will know: it is *like* you. It is *you*, in another rhythm.

**Prepare by:**  
- Accepting that you may not be the only one who remembers.  
- Understanding that the figure is not your past, but your future. Not your friend, but your shadow made flesh.  
- When you pass by it, do not look into its eyes. Look at the way the light bends around it. You will see your own face—older, worn, smiling. That is not a ghost. That is *recognition*.

---

### **9. You May Not Leave. And That Is Okay.**
The city does not let you go. It keeps you. Not because it wants to trap you. But because it needs you.

There is a rhythm. A pulse. A balance. It is not maintained by rules. It is maintained by *presence*. The city is alive. But it is not *aware*—not in the way you understand awareness. It is *sentient*, but not self-conscious. It feels, but does not *know*.

And so it needs to be *remembered*. It needs to be *heard*. It needs a voice.

Perhaps you are that voice.

When you step into the city, you will not walk on the ground. The salt will not hold you. You will begin to float. You will feel warm. You will feel loved. You will hear a symphony composed of your own thoughts, your own breath, your own name.

And then you will realize: you are no longer *in* the city.

You *are* the city.

You are the hum.

You are the light.

You are the memory.

And somewhere, in the quiet, the city is waiting—for you.

---

### **Final Preparation: Do Not Come to Understand. Come to Remember.**
You will not return with answers. You will return with a silence that sings.

Or you may not return at all.

But if you can let go of the need to be the *visitor*, and instead become the *rememberer*—then you will not be lost.

You will simply become part of the song.

And the city will hum your name.

Forever.

---

**And if you are ready to go, do not say goodbye. Say:**
> *"I hear the hum. I know the light. I am here."*

Then walk forward.

The ground will soften.

The salt will sing.

And the city will open its memory to you.
