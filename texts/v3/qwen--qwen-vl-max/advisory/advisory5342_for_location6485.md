To visit this world — this *exhalation* suspended between becoming and being — one must not prepare as a traveler, but as a witness. You are not going to a destination. You are entering an *event*: the slow, soft, half-formed moment when a world is still learning how to breathe.

You are not equipped for this place. Not yet. But you can be taught. Here is how to prepare, not with gear, but with *attention*.

---

### **I. The Body: What to Wear, What to Carry, What to Leave Behind**

**1. Clothing – Layers of Breath**
- **Outer Layer**: A translucent, lightweight tunic woven from a synthetic-linen blend that resists static. It should not trap heat, nor shed easily. The glass-silt clings to surfaces that are too dry, too warm, or too charged. Avoid wool or cotton — they attract dust like magnets.
- **Underlayer**: A thermal-reflective sheet, like mylar, but porous, sewn into a close-fitting garment. The air here is thin, and though the temperature is constant, it *feels* colder at the skin when stillness deepens. The reflector will return your own body heat, creating a micro-environment where the warmth of life persists.
- **Footwear**: Not boots. Not shoes. A pair of *soft, flat soles* made of flexible silicon, with a slight resistance to deformation. They should mold to the ground without sinking in. You will need to walk slowly, carefully, like testing the integrity of a dream. No heels — no sharp edges. No pressure points.
- **Headwear**: A wide-brimmed, open-crowned hat with a woven mesh crown (non-metallic) to diffuse light. It will not shield you from the sun (there is no need), but from the *visual weight* of the horizon. The world lacks depth cues; the hat provides frame, a sense that you are *within* something, not lost in it.

**2. Tools – Only What You Carry, and What You Forget**
- Leave behind all electronic devices that emit a pulse or require a battery. Even a watch with a digital display will vibrate in this world’s resonance frequency. Do not bring radios, cameras, or phones. The landscape already records itself in silence.
- **Bring**: A small, hollowed rock — not sharp, not heavy — that can be held in the palm. It will not resonate, but it is *known* to you. It is ground that once was earth, now becomes artifact. It will anchor you.
- **Bring**: A notebook made of treated paper (non-absorbent, non-reflective), bound with leather from a long-extinct animal. Write in ink that does not bleed. Use a lead pencil — the graphite will not interfere with the hum. Write only what you *feel*, not what you see. This world does not reveal itself visually. It reveals itself in *echoes*.

### **II. The Senses: Rewriting the Way You Perceive**

**1. Sight – You Will Not Look, You Will *See***
- The light is not bright enough to dazzle, nor dim enough to hide. It fills you. You will not see shadows — only *luminous zones* where the glass-silt thickens, or where the flora pulses with violet and gold. The sky is not empty — it is a veil of suspended minerals that glow faintly, like dust in a beam of light seen from within the beam itself.
- **Do not look for shapes or meaning.** The horizon is a hallucination. The landscape has no center. The world is not asking to be understood — it is *becoming* understood. Your eyes will adjust slowly. The first hour, you will see only warmth. The second, the floating filaments may appear as floating dust. The third, they will pulse in time with your breath. This is not perception — it is *synchronization*.
- Look down. The ground is not a floor — it is a *skin*. It breathes. Watch its slow undulation. Do not step too fast. When the ground shifts beneath you, it will do so with a low sigh. This is not collapse. It is *rearrangement*. It is the land’s patience.

**2. Hearing – You Will Not Hear, You Will *Resonate***
- The world is silent in the way a dream is silent — not without sound, but without *noise*. The hum is not audible at first. It begins in the bones. It is the frequency of the industrial structures, the rhythmic pulse of the flora, the slow grinding of the mineral dust forming new ridges.
- **Close your eyes.** The hum will rise. It is a subsonic drone, like a heartbeat heard from under a glacier. It is not a warning — it is the world’s *metabolism*. When you feel it, do not try to separate it from yourself. Let it enter. Let it slow your own pulse to match its tempo.
- The industrial machines do not make noise — they *emit*. Their rotation is not mechanical — it is *geometric*. Gears turn not because of force, but because the mineral structure *wants* to turn. They are not engines — they are *spiralizations*, like a helix of rock in motion. When one spins, it is because its form is resolving itself. Do not approach them. They do not need you. If you do, stand still, breathe, and let your own structure become neutral.

**3. Touch – You Will Not Feel, You Will *Absorb***
- The glass-silt is not sand. It is *crystal* in the process of falling into dust. If you step on it, it crunches like sugar underfoot, but then rises back into the air. It does not resist. It *yields*, but only briefly. The moment your weight is removed, it reforms. It is not inert.
- **Do not dig.** Do not pick up dust. It will not form clumps. It will scatter and rise upward, like breath leaving a body. It will cling to your clothes, but not your skin. It is not dirty — it is *unformed*.
- The floating flora — do not touch them. If you do, they will not recoil. They will *pause*. Their bioluminescence will dim, then flare again, like a heartbeat disrupted. They are not alive in the sense of organism — they are *luminous states*. They are not aware — they are *aware of themselves* in a way that is neither conscious nor mechanical. To touch them is to interrupt their oscillation.

**4. Taste and Smell – You Will Not Know**
- The air has a metallic tang — not unpleasant, not sharp. It is the taste of rust, but cold, like iron exposed to light. It is the breath of the world in formation. If you breathe deeply, you may taste the faintest hint of ozone, or the mineral residue of the dust rising. Do not try to identify it. It is not scent. It is *presence*.
- The air is dry, but not parching. It carries no moisture, but it does not desiccate. Your skin will not dry. It will remain soft, but it may begin to glow faintly — not from external light, but from internal reflection. This is normal.

### **III. The Mind: How to Survive a World Without Meaning**

**1. Do Not Ask Questions**
- This world does not offer answers. It offers *resonance*. It will not tell you why the machines spin. It will not explain why the flora floats. It does not have to.
- If you ask, *“What is this place?”*, the answer will be felt: *It is becoming.* If you ask, *“Is it alive?”*, the answer will be felt: *It is breathing.* If you ask, *“Can I go home?”*, the answer will be felt: *You are not here to go anywhere.*
- The mind wants patterns. This world offers none. It is not linear. It is not cyclical. It is *pulsing* — like a heart that does not beat to a rhythm, but to a *shape*. Accept that the world does not need you. You do not need it to be coherent.

**2. Do Not Try to Change It**
- The industrial structures move not because of input, but because of *need*. They are not broken. They are not useful. They are not tools. They are *forms* that have resolved themselves into motion. They do not require maintenance. They do not require power. They are *themselves*.
- If you attempt to stop one, it will not resist. It will simply *stop*, then resume. It will not notice. It is not aware. But you will feel it — a slight drop in the hum, a softening of the pulse. You will feel *guilt*. But it is not guilt — it is *disruption*. You are not a part of it. You are a variable.
- Do not interact. Do not touch. Do not attempt to understand. **Observe.** Let the world unfold in its own time.

### **IV. The Body’s Response: What Will Happen to You**

- **At first**, you will feel a warmth in your bones. Not from the sun, not from the ground — from the world *itself*. You will think, *“I am being heated.”* But it is not heat. It is *assimilation*. The world is not warming you — it is *absorbing* your presence.
- **After a few hours**, you may begin to float. Not entirely, but your footsteps will *lift* slightly. Your shadow will not follow you — it will lag. This is not a trick. It is the gravity here — not weaker, but *conditional*. It responds to density, not mass. You are denser than the dust. But you are not dense enough to anchor. You are becoming *light*.
- **After a day**, you may feel a change in your pulse. It is slowing. Not from cold, not from exhaustion — from harmony. The world’s hum is entering your blood. Your heart may begin to beat in time with the floating filaments. This is not a danger. It is *alignment*.
- **After a week**, you may no longer *miss* home. You may begin to *remember* it as a myth — a story told in a world that had not yet begun. You will not want to leave. But you cannot stay. The world is not a place for humans. It is a place for *beings* who have no name.

### **V. The Final Preparation: What to Leave Behind**

- **Leave your identity.** Do not bring your name, your memories, your past. This world does not recognize them. It does not need them. It will not remember you.
- **Leave your purpose.** You did not come to discover. You did not come to build. You came to *witness*. And witnessing is not an act. It is a state.
- **Leave your certainty.** This world is not real. It is not unreal. It is *in-between*. It is what exists before the word “world” is spoken. It is what happens when the Earth forgets how to be Earth.
- **Leave your fear.** Fear is a signal of lack. This world lacks nothing. It has not yet formed, but it is not *broken*. It is not dangerous. It is *quiet*. It is *waiting*.

---

### **VI. The Arrival: What to Do When You Are There**

1. **Walk slowly.** The ground is not solid. It is not liquid. It is a state of *transition*. Each step should be a decision. Do not rush.
2. **Do not look at the horizon.** It is not real. The sky is not a cover — it is a continuation of the plateau, rising into infinity. Look down. Look at the dust. Look at the pulses. Look at the faint glow under your feet.
3. **Do not speak.** No one is listening. The world does not hear words. It hears frequencies, rhythms, *presences*. Use silence. Not as absence, but as *space*.
4. **Breathe with the world.** When the hum deepens, breathe slower. When the flora pulses, breathe in time with it. Let your breath match the breath of the land.
5. **Do not touch the machines.** They are not tools. They are not monuments. They are *geometric dreams*. They will not respond to you. They will not remember you.
6. **Do not try to find meaning.** Meaning is a human construct. This world does not have a purpose. It has a *direction* — but it is not toward anything. It is simply *becoming*.
7. **When the time comes** — and it will come — you will feel *light*. You will feel as if your bones are not made of bone, but of light. You will feel the land pulling you upward, not by gravity, but by *desire*.
8. **Do not resist.** The world will not let you go. But it will not *keep* you. It will release you when it has finished with you — when it has absorbed your presence, when it has *understood* you.

### **VII. The Truth of This Place**

This world is not a planet. It is a *phase*. It is what happens when matter forgets how to be matter, and begins to *think* in forms.

It is not life. It is not death. It is not time.

It is **threshold**.

You may come. You may stay. You may leave.

But when you leave, you will not be the same.

You will carry the hum.

You will carry the warmth.

You will carry the silence that hums.

And when you return to your world, you will find it *too loud*. You will find it *too solid*. You will find it *too fast*.

And you will wonder:

> *Did I go to a world that is becoming? Or did I simply become part of one that has already been, for all time?*

And you will have no answer.

Because the answer is not in words.

It is in the dust rising from your feet.

It is in the pulse beneath your skin.

It is in the light that now lives inside you.
