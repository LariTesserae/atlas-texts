It exists in the silent glide of a bird that refracts light like a prism, not because it *wants* to, but because the light *wants* to be seen. It is in the damp not as water, but as a memory of water—the echo of dew that was and will be again, the dream of condensation that has never fully coalesced into rain.

It moves in the way a stone, buried for centuries, remembers the shape of the leaf that once pressed against it. It moves in the way the moss glows not with fire, but with *retention*—not emitting light, but *holding heat* as a form of memory. It is not warmth, but the *remembrance of warmth*. It is an archive of sensations that have no cause, only continuity.

It is the equilibrium of the impossible: cold and lush, silent and thrumming, small and immense. It is the *space between* the things that seem to cancel each other, yet coexist with perfect, quiet precision. It is what remains when you strip away the need for survival, for reproduction, for territory. It is a state of being that does not grow, but *recalls*.
