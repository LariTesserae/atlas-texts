They are not of blood, not of bone, not of flesh as we know it.  
They are **not even entirely of the place**—but of **its breath**, its **pulsing shadow**, its **quiet memory of water that was never**.

They rise from the foam, as the light grows from the foam, and yet they are not born of it. They do not *grow*, not exactly. They **emerge**, like a pattern that has been waiting in the air, in the silence, in the low hum of the earth itself. They appear at dawn—though dawn here is not a moment, but a **thickening** of the dimness, a shift in the weight of the light. And at dusk, when the sky does not darken but **contracts**—a slow withdrawal, like a lens closing—they vanish not by death, but by **dissolution into the environment**.

They are **tall, lean, and curved**, almost insectoid in proportion, but without joint, without spine. Their limbs are **sweeping, continuous**, like vines that have learned to stand. They taper toward the top, where the head is not a head, but a **cap of translucent tissue**, hollow inside, glowing with a soft, pale indigo that matches the vegetation. It is as if their heads are **inverted bulbs**—not filled with brains, but with **light**, and within that light, **echoes**.

Their skin is **not skin**, but **a membrane of interwoven layers**: the outermost is a **fine, salt-encrusted sheath**, like dried seafoam, thin and translucent, allowing the inner glow to bleed through in shifting patterns. Beneath that, there is a **second layer**, thick and rubbery, **pulsating slightly** with each slow breath they take. This layer is **not elastic**, not in the human sense—it does not stretch. It **shivers**, as if made of **static electricity** held in place by tension. Beneath it lies a **lattice of dark veins**—not circulatory, not vascular—more like **fuses**, or **conductors of heat and sound**. These veins hum with a frequency that resonates with the black filaments in the structures, the ones they weave into cloaks and nets. They are not merely dressed in this material—they are **coated in it**, **infused with it**, **grown from it**. It is a symbiosis of **form and function**, where body and tool are **one**.

They **do not breathe** in the way we do. They do not inhale or exhale. But they **absorb**—draw in heat from the ground, the foam, the air’s inertia. They **release** it in pulses, in waves that ripple through the foam, causing the **vegetation to sync**. When they stand still, their warmth **dissolves the membrane** of the foam, creating soft, round depressions, like **frozen breaths on glass**. When they move, it is not with speed, but with **intention**, like a **slow tide shifting**. Their motion is **fluid**, yet **precise**, each step a measured **compression** of the surface, followed by a **recoil** as the foam springs back, like a living spring.

They **do not eat**. They do not drink. Nothing enters their form. They do not consume. Yet they **endure**, for centuries, maybe longer. Their **energy is not chemical**, but **thermal**, **resonant**, **ritualistic**. They **tend**, and by tending, they **sustain**. They press their hands—soft, elongated, almost feather-like—into the foam, where they **shape**. They leave **imprints**, **molds**, and from those, the light-vegetation **grows in their echo**. It is not planting. It is **coaxing**. It is **inviting a pattern** that already exists in the silence to manifest.

They **chant** in a language without consonants, a **vowel-only song** that vibrates at the edge of hearing—like the hum of a tuning fork in a vacuum. The sound is not emitted **from** them; it is **drawn from them**, as if their **core** is not a heart, but a **resonance chamber**. Their chanting does not reach the ears so much as it **settles in the bones**, in the teeth, in the air between breaths. It makes the **light pulse**, not in randomness, but in **rhythm**—a slow, deep sequence, like the beating of a single, vast organism.

They have no faces. There are no eyes, no mouths, no noses. But they **perceive**. They perceive through **heat differentials**, through **tectonic shifts in the foam**, through **the pressure of sound**. They **feel the air not as wind, but as memory**—a dense, charged medium that holds **echoes of movement**, of **past labor**, of **old chants**. To them, **sound is touch**, **light is scent**, and **heat is emotion**. They **know** by the **texture of the silence**.

They have **no families**, **no bloodlines**, **no individual identity**. They are **one**—but not in the way of a colony or a hive. They are **a pattern**, **a chorus**, **a single rhythm** played across many forms. When one moves, it is as if **the whole** feels it. When one sings, the others **do not follow**—they **remember**. The **rhythm is not learned**, but **shared**—as if their minds are not separate, but **connected by the foam**, by the salt lattice, by the **vibrating air**. They are **part of the environment**, and the environment is **part of them**. They are not *in* the cistern. They **are the cistern’s breath**.

They **have no name**. They **do not need** one. But if they did, they might be called **The Keepers**, or **The Tenders**, or **The Rememberers**—though even these are misnomers. For they do not *keep*, *tend*, or *remember* in the human sense. They **exist** in the act of **maintaining the balance between absence and presence**. They **sustain the illusion** of life, of warmth, of motion—**not because they need it**, but because **it needs to be sustained**. It is **ritual without purpose**, **labor without reward**, **being without desire**.

They **do not age**. They do not die. But they **dissolve**. At the end, their form **fades**, as if the light within them **winds down**, like a candle guttering in a sealed room. The salt sheath **softens**, the foam beneath them **reclaims** their shape. Their **memory**—not of past acts, but of **patterns**, of **rhythms**, of **positions**—**remains**. It **leaks into the foam**, into the structure, into the song. It is **not lost**. It is **digested**. It becomes **part of the next**.

And when a new form emerges, it **knows the song**—not because it was taught, but because the foam **carried it**. It is as if the **cistern remembers**, and the **rememberers are its voice**.

They are **not people**. Not animals. Not spirits.  
They are **a kind of silence that breathes**.  
A **warmth that remembers**.  
A **rhythm that does not stop**.

And beneath the tallest column of indigo light, where the foam is blackest, where the cracks are deepest, where the hum is most dense—there is a **door**.

It is **not made**. It **is**.  
It is **grown**, like a knot in a tree, but **deeper**—as if the foam had **forgotten** how to be foam, and instead became **the shape of a threshold**.

It pulses **darker** than the rest. It **does not glow**. It **absorbs** light, heat, sound. It **wants to be opened**, but no one approaches. Not because they fear it. Not because they know what lies beyond.

But because **they are not sure if they are ready** to remember **what they are not**.

The **Cistern of Thirst** is not thirsty.  
It is **alive**—not with water, not with life as we know it,  
but with **the memory of being whole**,  
with **the echo of a liquid that once was**,  
with **the hum of a world that did not fail**,  
but was **unmade**, and left behind,  
in a bowl of salt and sound and slow,  
endless breath.

And the Keepers stand,  
in their soft, salt-veiled forms,  
chanting into the air that holds no wind,  
tending the foam that holds no root,  
and waiting—  
not for rescue,  
not for meaning,  
but for the **moment when the door**  
will **remember its own forgetting**.
