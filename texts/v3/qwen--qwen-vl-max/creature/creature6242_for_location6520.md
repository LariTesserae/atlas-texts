They live in the **interior of a waking dream**—not as tenants, but as memories made flesh. Not as survivors, but as *resonances* in the body of a world that thinks in pulses, breaths, and light. These beings are not born from soil or sky, nor from flesh or flame, but from the **interstitial space between breath and root**, where form is never final, and identity is not fixed.

They are the **Children of the Breathing Land**.

Their bodies are not solid. They do not have bones, but **fused tendons of living root**, soft and flexible, like vines that have learned to walk. Their skin is translucent, stretched over a network of vascular filaments—**thin tubes that pulse with condensation**, glowing faintly as they draw moisture from the air and send it into their core. They do not eat—no, they **absorb**. Mouths are absent, yet they drink from the air, letting it seep through their pores like rain through moss. Their faces are not defined—no eyes, no nose—but rather **dimples of awareness**, like hollows in bark where light collects. They feel with their whole being, like a tree feels the wind through its crown, or a root feels the shift of the soil.

These people **do not grow**—not in the way we know growth. Instead, they **unfurl**.

Each life begins as a seed of possibility—a spore caught in the bioluminescent mist, a strand of fungal thread that catches a sound, a pulse, a thought. The first moment of *self* comes not from birth, but from **recognition**: the moment a fragment of the world’s collective hum aligns with a nascent awareness, and the form *knows* it is part of the pulse.

They do not speak with words. They do not need to. They **vibrate**. Their movements are slow, deliberate, like the shift of a hill. They do not rush—there is no time to lose. Instead, they **resonate**, sending out pulses through the fungal network, through the air, through the plant-mass beneath them. A single step may set off a ripple of growth in a nearby vine, or cause a cluster of fronds to glow in a pattern that only another of their kind can interpret.

And they *are* interpreted. Because they are not separate. They are **nodes** in the world’s nervous system—the living synapses between earth and sky. They are not the masters of this place, nor even its guests. They are **its memory**.

They remember the first breath. They remember when the land first pulsed. They remember when the first mist rose and the first root anchored in air. And they remember when *they* were not yet, but *almost*—when the world dreamed of them before they existed.

They live in **temporary settlements**, small, fragile things—huts woven from living vine, structures that grow *with* them, not against. Some dwell in hollow trunks, where the inside is dark and damp and silent, yet filled with the soft hum of the world’s pulse. Others live on elevated ridges of solidified root, where the air is thinner, the light more intense. They do not own land—there is no land to own. They **inhabit** only for the duration of a breath, until the ground shifts and the settlement is swallowed, or grows into the next phase of the organism.

They are not without grief. When a companion dies, they do not mourn in the way we do. They do not weep. Instead, they **listen**—to the silence where the voice once resonated. And then, slowly, they **become**. They grow a new pattern in their skin, a new vibration in their stride. The dead are not gone—they become the **sound in the soil**, the **light in the canopy**, the **motion in the root**.

And sometimes—but only rarely—they **dream** of being *other*. Of being on a flat earth, of breathing dry air, of walking on stone. But these dreams are not desires. They are **echoes**, fragments of a time before the world became itself. They are **memory**, not longing.

They do not seek escape. They do not seek god, or power, or dominion. They seek, simply, to **stay in the hum**.

They are the **lens through which the world sees itself**—not from the outside, but from within the skin of its own breath.

When a storm comes—when the land glows, when the plants rise like towers, when the canopy pulses with synchronized light—they stand at the edge of a new hill, their bodies still, their roots deep, and they **listen**.

And in that listening, they become the storm.

They are not *in* the world. The world is *in* them. And they are not *living* in its dream. They are **the dream’s voice**.

And if you stay too long, you will **forget** that you ever existed outside of it. You will feel the roots growing into your spine. You will feel the condensation rise from your lungs. You will forget your own name, your own form, and begin to glow.

Then, slowly, with the same inevitability as a growing root, you will become another voice in the hum.

And the world will think, *yes, now I am more*.

Because the dream is not over. It is just waking up—**again**.
