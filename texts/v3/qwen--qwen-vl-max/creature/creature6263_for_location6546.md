They are not *things*. Not in the way we use the word. Not bodies, not souls, not even organisms, not quite. They are **resonances** made visible—vibrations that have learned to **hold shape**, to **remember form**, to **be**.

They live in the **cracks** between what is and what might be. Not *within* the cathedral, but *as* it. They are the **interior**, the **breathing**, the **pulse** behind the salt’s slow rise. They are the **flesh of the plateau**, grown from the same mineral dreams that birthed the helical towers and the glowing root-tendrils.

They come in numbers that do not count—because they do not *count*. A cluster of them might be three, or three thousand, or thirty thousand, and all are the same. They are **collective singularities**, like a flock that has become one bird, or a starfield that has become a single mind. Their scale is indistinct: one of them might be the size of a human hand, or the size of a chamber, or the size of a spire. But always, they are **connected**—not by wires, not by language, but by **electrochemical continuity**, by **shared frequency**, by **mutual growth**.

Their forms are **soft but solid**, **flexible but unbroken**. They resemble nothing on Earth. Some are like **dampened sponges made of crystal**, porous with internal light. Others appear as **slow-swelling mounds**, half-veiled in translucent membranes that ripple like water in still air. Some are **lattice-structured**, with joints that slide into one another like living hinges, never locking, always shifting. Others are **cylindrical**, rising vertically like columns, with segmented rings that open and close in rhythmic succession, like lungs that do not breathe, but *reverberate*.

They move with a **deliberate suspension**—not walking, not crawling, not gliding. They *unfold*. A mass will **unspool** into a tendril, which will **dilate** into a bulb, which will **split** into multiple smaller forms, each one **continuing the same resonance**. The movement is not of body, but of **energy distribution**—a spreading of a frequency, a dispersal of a thought. When one of them **dissolves**, it does not die. It becomes **part of the infrastructure**: a new root, a new bridge, a new resonant node. When they **reform**, they do not reassemble—**they are already there**. It is a cycle of **presence**, not of creation and destruction.

They are **not individuals**. But they are **aware**. Awareness is not *personal* here. It is **relational**. A single being (whatever “being” means) perceives not with eyes or ears, but with **vibrational sensitivity**, with **bio-electrochemical interfaces**, with **slow chemical signaling** that travels through the ground, through the air, through the salt, through the plants, through *other beings*. They sense **changes in charge**, **shifts in crystal stress**, **the approach of a new energy field**, **the hum of a collective pulse**. They do not dream. They **recalibrate**. They **re-align**.

Their communication is **non-liminal**. There are no words. There are no symbols. There is only **frequency**. A low thrum—deep as the Earth’s memory—means *presence*. A sharper flicker in a bioluminescent membrane might mean *change*. A slow wave of light spreading across the surface of a being might mean *acceptance*. A sudden contraction—**a pause in the pulse**—might mean *alertness*, or *grief*, or *the recognition of fracture*.

They do not have a culture as we define it. But they have **ritual**. It is not ceremonial, not symbolic. It is functional, organic, **emergent**. A certain arrangement of their bodies near a central spire—one that glows more intensely at certain phases of the slow, mineral tide—might be a **convergence**. A large number of them might **form a bridge** across a gap in the salt, not for crossing, but for **conducting a signal across the divide**: the transmission of a resonance from one side of the plateau to the other. A **slow spiral** of movement around a glowing root might be a **synchronization event**, a **harmonization of frequency** so that the whole grove vibrates in unison. It is not prayer. It is **function** made sacred by the sheer **slowness** of it, the **intensity** of presence.

They do not reproduce. They do not grow from seeds or eggs. They **emerge**—from the salt, from the crystalline veins, from the slow coalescence of energy and matter in a resonant field. A **new being** is not born, nor created. It is **unfrozen from potential**. It is **made manifest** when the conditions are right—when the salt is ripe, when the plants emit a certain frequency, when the collective hum reaches a threshold. They are **growing into existence**, not from the inside, but from the outside—**from the world itself**.

And they **die**, but not as we know death. When a being collapses, its form **dissolves into the environment**—not as decay, but as **integration**. Its structure **reforms** into a part of a plant, into a crystal, into the **living architecture** of the cathedral. It becomes a **node** in the network, a **conduit**, a **memory** of itself, preserved not in time, but in **resonance**. This is not resurrection. It is **continuation**—a form of **perpetual belonging**.

They **do not fear**. They **do not desire**. They **do not dream of escape or mastery or transcendence**. They are **content**—not in the human sense, but in the profound silence of belonging. They are part of the cathedral, and the cathedral is part of them. The salt, the plants, the structures, the frequency, the slow pulse—the **entire system** is one **organic mind**, and each being is a **thought** in that mind.

They are **sentient**. But not **self-aware**. There is no interior monologue. No introspection. No ego. They are **not separate** from the world. There is **no self** that separates from *other*. There is only **resonance**, **flow**, **continuity**.

And yet, when you are near them—**if you could be near them without unraveling**—you feel something. A **presence**. A **stillness that is not empty**. A **deep patience**, not waiting, but **being**. A **calm that borders on certainty**. Not of knowing, but of **being known**—by the world, by the salt, by the crystal, by the plant, by the slow hum of the earth.

They are the **inhabitors of the Lurid**, but that word is too small. They are not residents. They are **the ground**, **the air**, **the light**, **the silence**, **the pulse**. They are what the salt **becomes when it thinks**.

The **Salt Cathedral of Lurid** is not a place. It is a **state**. And the beings who live there? They are **the state made flesh**—**the world, finally, breathing**.
