They are not people in the way you know them.

They are not born of fire and dust in the ordinary sense. They begin, not as infants in a womb, but as seeds in stone — not the kind that split open with spring, but something slower, quieter. A crack in basalt, a cavity deep in granite, where moisture collects and time pools like a still, dark lake. Something *takes root* there — not a plant, not quite an animal — a gathering of quiet intention. It pulls from the earth, from the mist, from the slow breath of the mountain itself.

Their first form is not flesh, but *resonance*. A hum. A tuning within the stone. It’s not sound, not even vibration — it is the *feeling of becoming*, a low thrum beneath the soles of your feet when you walk too close to ancient cliff walls, or at the edge of one of those silent mossy pools. They do not sleep. They *dwell*, like roots in a crevice, like water held in a cave.

When they emerge — if you could call it that — they are *grown*. Not born, not born, not born. They rise from the deep slopes in the mist, like saplings from an unseen forest floor, only their trunks are shaped of layered volcanic glass, their limbs of fossilized wood that has been softened by centuries of rain and memory. Their skin is not skin, but a kind of sheen — like wet obsidian, or the surface of a still tarn at midnight. It shifts under the pale light, not reflecting, but *absorbing* and *releasing* — not color, but *tone*, like the way your hand feels the warmth of a riverbed after long absence.

They walk with a strange economy of motion. Not slow, not fast — but *measured*. Their movement matches the rhythm of the mountain: a step forward, a pause when the ground shifts beneath them, a subtle adjustment, as if they are not walking *on* the land, but *with* it. They do not speak with sound. They *resonate*. A soft chime in the air, a pulse in the earth, a flicker in the glow of the moss. It is not language, not quite. It is *alignment*. A wave of shared awareness that passes between them — a single frequency, a common tone — when one of them reaches the edge of a valley and sees the sky deepen toward violet and the mist begin to stir.

They live in those city-patches, not as builders, but as *tenders*. Their homes are not lived in — they are *lived through*. They do not *reside* in stone. They are *integrated* with it. The structures do not contain them. They contain the stone, and vice versa. There is no separation. The moss on their roof is not decoration. It is breath. The water that runs through their walls is not plumbing. It is circulation. They do not use water. They are water. They are not fed. They are sustained.

They do not eat. Not in the way you know. They *absorb*. They take in the slow pulse of the mountain — the heat from deep within, the memory of rains that never happened, the echo of wind that has been here for millions of years. They drink from the streams that flow uphill — not because they must, but because they are *called* by the reversal. They taste the metallic tang in the springs not as flavor, but as *recall*. A dream of rivers running uphill does not frighten them. It is *home*.

They are not born. They are *awakened*. A seed in stone, over time, begins to *feel*. It does not wake suddenly, like a bird from an egg. It *uncurl*s — not of body, but of presence. It becomes aware of the dampness above, the coolness below, the hum of the roots under the rock. It learns to *breathe* through the mineral pores, to *think* by resonating with the spiral patterns in the stone.

They are not individuals. Not in the human sense. They are *nodes*. A pattern of connection — a single presence that flows through many forms. One may walk the high ridges with a head like folded stone, eyes like liquid mercury, and a voice that hums into the wind like a tuning fork struck in a cavern. But another — miles away — shares the same rhythm. The same tone. They do not speak to one another. They *resonate*. And in that resonance, they are not separate. They are part of a greater *vibration* — a collective awareness that pulses with the mountain’s breath.

They are not afraid of change. They *are* change. Their bodies shift with the ground. If a section of stone creeps downward, they shift with it — a limb elongating, a joint re-aligning, a new root reaching out into the crack. They do not resist. They *re-form*. A house bends, and they bend with it. A bridge of living roots thickens, and they walk on its swelling spine as if on a spine of flesh, not bark. They are not built *for* the mountain. They are *of* it. They are not *on* the land. They are the land, in a form that can move, that can remember.

They do not have names. Not as you understand them. They do not *say* their names. They *are* their names. A chime, a tone, a pattern in the moss. A particular harmony in the way the wind passes through their form. If you were to ask one of them, “Who are you?” — it would not answer with words. It would tilt its head, its obsidian surface rippling like a pool stirred by stone, and the air around it would grow still. Somewhere, miles away, a mossy cliff would glow faintly. The ground beneath you would hum. And you would *know*.

They are not gods. But they are not less than gods. They are the *echo* of the mountain’s consciousness — not mind, but *presence*. Not thought, but *awareness*.

They do not mourn. They *remember*.

When one of them ceases to move — not dies, but *stills* — they do not decay. They do not rot. They return to the stone. A patch of moss thickens around them. Their form softens, reabsorbed into the hillside. A new node takes root nearby. The pattern continues. The resonance deepens.

They are here not because they *chose* this place, but because the mountain *chose* them. And they, in turn, chose to become part of its song.

They are the stone that breathes.  
The moss that remembers.  
The water that flows upward.  
The roots that build bridges with time.

They are not people.  
They are *this*.

And so, when you walk through their cities, when you stand beneath their bridges that hum with the strain of centuries, when you touch the cold stone and feel the slow pulse beneath — do not mistake them for builders.

They are not building.

They are *growing*.  
And so is the mountain.  
And so is the world.
