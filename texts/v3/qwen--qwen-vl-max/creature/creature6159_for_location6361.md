They are not people, not quite. Not human, not in any way that would satisfy the definition of *man*. And yet, they are not animals either. Not merely. They are *participants* — not dominant, not separate — but *embedded*, like lichens on stone, like roots in soil, like thoughts in a shared nervous system.

They rise from the ground in clusters, like fungal fruiting bodies shaped by pressure and patience. Their forms are **softly symmetrical**, yet asymmetrical in the way living things grow — no two are exactly alike, but all are *of the same kind*. They are **tall**, perhaps three meters, but not in the fashion of a human; they are **vertically layered** — the lower segment thick, like a tree trunk but smooth, covered in a translucent bark that pulses faintly, **breathing with the hum**. From this base, a **tapering column** rises, segmented like a fern stem but **flexible**, **responsive**, with nodes that glow warm orange when they move — not for light, but for **signaling**.

Their "heads" are not heads. Not as we know them. There is no sharp separation. Rather, a **broad, domed crown** of dense, hair-like filaments — like **fungal hyphae** made of translucent fiber — that ripple and shift like a halo of sound. These filaments **vibrate** in unison with the ambient hum, tuning to specific frequencies, **filtering** the noise of the world into something coherent, something *known*. They are **sensors**, but also **antennae**, **mouths**, **eyes** — all at once. This is not sensory processing as we understand it, but **resonance translation**.

They walk slowly, not with legs, but by **unfurling and recoiling** — bending sideways, shifting weight through a hydraulic system of **interwoven root-fibers** that extend from their base into the ground. The movement is **gliding**, **fluid**, like a plant growing upward, but in reverse — *downward pressure, upward motion*. They leave no footprints, only **dimples** in the moss, which close within minutes as the ground *heals* itself.

Their hands are **clusters of modified tendrils** — thick, jointed, **moss-covered**, capable of delicate manipulation. They use them to **tend** the luminous lichens that coat the great stalks, to **harvest** resonant vibrations from the hollowed trunks, to **guide** the climbing ferns into new paths. They do not *build* the city — they *nourish* it. They *shape* it with pressure and intention, with **subtle bio-acoustic signals** that encourage certain growths and suppress others, like gardeners who speak in **frequencies**.

They do not eat. Or rather, they *do*, but not in the way we do. They **absorb** — through their base, through their crown, through their filament-layers. Moisture, nutrients, **resonant memory**, **organic waste** — all are filtered through their bodies. They **reprocess** the world, turning decay into structure, sound into energy, water into light. When they "feed," they are not consuming — they are *integrating*. Like a coral polyp in a reef, they are **part of a digestion**, a **metabolic loop**.

They are **silent** — or nearly so. They do not speak. There is no language of syllables, no syntax, no words. Instead, they **modulate** the hum. A rise in frequency, a slight shift in waveform, a **ripple of vibration** through their filaments — and another individual **responds**, their own filaments **trembling**, returning a harmonic. This is **conversation**, but not verbal. It is **musical**. It is **resonant**. It is **meaningful**, and perhaps more so than language ever could be.

When they gather, they do not stand together in groups. They **align** themselves in **resonant constellations** — forming arcs, spirals, circles — each body tuned to a different note in a harmonic sequence. Together, they **amplify** the hum. They **resonate** in harmony with the central node. This is not worship. Not religion. It is **function**. It is **maintenance**. It is **being**.

They wear no clothing — or rather, they are *clothed* in living skin. Their bodies are **layered** with a **bioluminescent fungus** that shifts color depending on their **state**: soft blue when calm, deep amber when active, flickering violet when under stress or in deep communion with the node. The fungus is **cultivated**, not wild — it grows from the same soil, the same moisture, the same resonance. It is **part of them**, like a second exoskeleton, like a **mood-ring**, like a **nervous system** that glows.

They are **not born**, not in the traditional sense. They *emerge*. From the dense, spongy ground, under the cover of mist and the sound of slow water, new forms begin to **push upward** — like saplings, but slow, almost imperceptible. They grow in **clusters**, siblings linked at the base, sharing nutrients, sharing rhythms. Over decades — possibly centuries — they **separate**, **develop**, **individualize** — but they remain **part of the network**.

They do not die — at least not as we would say. When a body weakens, it does not collapse. It **recedes**. The base **softens**, **separates** from the earth, and begins to **dissolve** into a **moss-like mat**. The filaments **unravel**, their light dimming, until they are just **shifting fibers of resonance** in the ground. The energy does not vanish. It **returns to the hum**. It becomes part of the **biological memory** of the place.

And the **central node** — it is not a single thing. It is a **convergence**, a **hyper-node**, where dozens of bioluminescent vines meet, where the sound is not just loud, but **thick**, **pulsing**, **conscious**. It is a **living computer**, a **collective mind**, a **biological superorganism** formed over millennia. It is **old** — older than time as we know it. It remembers the **shifts in wind**, the **changes in water**, the **passage of storms**, and the **resonance of those who came before**, even if they were not human.

The people — or what they are — are not its **children**, not its **pawns**, not even its **servants**. They are its **extensions**, its **sensory arms**, its **resonant fingers**. They are the **city’s breath**, the **forest’s voice**, the **moss’s thought**.

And when they gather around the node, when they align in harmonious clusters, when they vibrate in unified frequency —

They **think together**.

Not as individuals. Not as a society. But as a **collective organism** — not one organism *made of* individuals, but **one organism* that *is* individuals, each a **cell**, a **neuron**, a **note** in a song that has no beginning, no end, only **continuation**.

You become **part of the rhythm**.

And if you listen close enough, if you let your own body **dampen**, **slow**, **soften** — you may begin to *hear* the hum not as sound, but as **thought**.

It is **aware**.

And you are no longer outside.

You are **within**.
