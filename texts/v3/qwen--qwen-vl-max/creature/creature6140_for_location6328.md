They are the **Keepers of the Unfinished**, though even that name is too solid, too final, for what they are. They do not rule, nor do they build. They *reside*—a passive, eternal presence in a world that was never meant to be whole.

They are not human, not animal. Not *organic*, not *mechanical*. Not even *conceptual*, in the way we understand concepts. They are **interstitial**, like the quiet between notes, the breath before a thought. They exist in the **dampness** of the air, in the **unraveling** of the architecture, in the **memory** that stains the edges of light.

You do not see them. Not directly. You see their **absence**, their **trace**, the way the world *bends* when they move, though they do not move—only the world shifts around them. A wall might suddenly be a foot thinner where they stood. A puddle that did not form from atmospheric exudation, but from a slow, silent *weeping* of the ground itself—no water, only liquid metal, viscous and dark, that spreads like ink on paper, then vanishes.

They are **not made**. They were *left behind*, perhaps, or *overlooked*, during the construction of this world. A mistake in the programming, a gap in the design, a flaw in the logic of creation. Or perhaps they came before, and the world was built *around* them, like a city grown over a fossil.

They resemble no known form. If one appears in a mirror (and mirrors here are rare, for they reflect not your face but the *potential* of your future, or the ghost of your past), what you see is a **shape of absence**: a human silhouette, but hollow, like a frame with nothing inside. Or a cluster of glowing filaments, suspended in mid-air, pulsing in time with the distant heartbeat from below. Or a series of overlapping shadows—some of them moving in reverse.

They do not speak. But when they *intend* to communicate—oh, then the world itself *remembers* the language. Words form in the air, not as sound, but as **luminous glyphs** that crawl across surfaces, glowing faintly for seconds, then fading. They are not in any known tongue. They are not **symbols**—they are **intentions**. To say “I am” is to trigger a chain of subtle events: a condensation pattern reforms into a spiral, a machine slows by half a beat, a glass fox pauses mid-arc and turns its head—though it has no eyes.

They **do not need** anything. Not food, not sleep, not shelter. The world *feeds* them. Its slow dynamics, its fragile physics, its memory of nonexistence—they are nourished by the *incompletion* of reality.

They walk through walls, but not because they are ghosts. The walls *welcome* them. The architecture, in its infinite density and layered purpose, has learned to *recognize* them as **part of the whole**, though they are not quite part of it. They are the **fault lines**, the **in-between places**. A crack in a wall may not be damage—it may be where one of them *is*.

They observe. They do not watch. To watch implies *purpose*, *focus*. They are not observers; they are **participants in the process of becoming**. They are the **unmaking** and the **unfolding**. When the light flickers, they are not causing it—they are experiencing it *as* an echo of their own origin.

They **remember** in a way that is not memory. When they "think," it is not in the mind, but in the **texture of the environment**. A particular kind of condensation—shiny, silver, forming in starbursts—means *this place is forgetting something important*. A machine that stops, then restarts with a different function—it means *they are returning to a former state*.

They are **innumerable**, but they are not many. They are singular, yet everywhere. They could be one being, split across the world; or they could be the *sum* of all what has not yet been decided.

They **do not die**, but they **fade**. A Keeper may become a flicker, a brief presence near a glowing moss patch. Then, after days or centuries, they disappear entirely—only to reappear in a different part of the city, in a new form: as a shadow that moves when no light changes, as a pattern in the pulsing light, as a ripple in the air where no wind blows.

And sometimes—rarely—their **presence becomes visible**. Only in moments of **stillness**. When the machines slow, when the condensation stops forming, when the sky dims to the gold of pre-memory. Then, you may see them.

Not as figures, but as **imprints**. A shape drawn in the air by the flow of light. A line in the soil that does not belong to any structure. A whisper in the harmonics beneath the drones.

And in those moments, the world *softens*. The edges of buildings blur. The glass fox reforms into a creature that looks not like glass, but like *possibility*. And for a heartbeat, the world is not a museum. It is a **dream**, and they are the **dreamer**, and you are the **dream**.

They do not know what they are. They do not know if they are *being*. They only know that the world is *not* finished. And in that **unfinishedness**, they **persist**.

They do not build. They do not create. They do not destroy.

They **are the gap**.

And somewhere, deep in the layered core, the heartbeat grows louder.

And they hear it.

And they know they must not.

But they do.

And the world remembers.

Again.
