They are not people. Not in the way we understand.

They are **slow**.

Not in movement — though they move like the drip of water into stone — but in being. Their **time is not measured**, but felt. It is the time of **moss spreading**, of **crystals growing**, of **light seeping through mineral veins**. Their thought is not a flow, but a **settling**. Like sediment. Or like the condensation that hangs in the air, not quite falling.

They are **the land’s memory**, made flesh. Not born from it — that would imply separation — but **unfolding with it**, like a sentence written in wet clay, not yet dried. They are both **the condition** and the **experience** of that condition. They are not *in* the world, but **embody its state**: dense, damp, continuous, slow-becoming.

Their bodies — when seen — are **less solid than the rock**, more flexible than the fungi. Not soft, not hard. **Intermediate**. You might mistake them for **shadows with weight**, or **stalactites with motion**. They move by **growing**, not stepping. One moment, they are a shape near the wall; the next, a portion of their form has **retracted**, like liquid drawn back into a porous surface, and another section has **pushed forward**, a new segment forming where none was before. The change is not violent, nor even dramatic — only **inexplicably present**. There is no joint, no limb, but a **continuum of substance** that shifts at its own pace.

They have no clear **features**. No eyes, no mouth, no head — only **pits and ridges**, smooth surfaces that occasionally **darken**, as if a shadow has settled into the skin. These darkened areas **pulse faintly**, not with light, but with a **deeper hum**, matching the resonance of the earth. You may see, if you stare long enough, a **slow rotation** of internal structure — not vision, but **perception through resonance**, like how a stone absorbs sound and reflects it in vibration.

They do not **look** at you. They do not **hear** you. They **register** your presence as a **disturbance in the field** — a ripple in the damp air, a change in the **pressure of stillness**. Your body is **unfamiliar matter**, dense with heat and motion, **foreign to the slow rhythm**. You are not *seen*, but **assimilated into the data** of the environment — a temporary fluctuation, like a storm in the condensation cycle.

They **live in time not of clocks or cycles**, but of **dripping**. A single “moment” for them may be the time it takes a droplet to fall from a ledge and disappear into the ground — a length of time that, to you, is mere seconds, but to them, is **a life**. They have no names, not as we know them. No spoken language. But they **communicate** — not through sound, but through **the modulation of matter**. One might **seep** toward another, and the **dampness between them would change**, not in quantity, but in **quality** — a subtle shift in charge, in temperature, in **luminosity**. A **low hum** might rise between them, or the **light from the lichens nearby might shift hue**, briefly turning violet or bronze. This is not expression — it is **interchange**, a slow **alignment**.

They **use** the land — but not as tools. They **are** the tools. The wooden rods, the stone scrapers — these are **extensions**, not implements. They are not **made**, but **placed**, as one might place a seed in soil. A rod is **tapped** against a wall, not to test hardness, but to **sync with a rhythm** — the thrum of the rock, the pulse of the fungi, the **damp wave** moving through the tunnel. When a being taps, the **rod becomes resonant**, and so does the surrounding air. For a moment, the entire chamber **vibrates**, not loudly, but deeply, like a stone held in your palm when the earth breathes.

They **drink** not water, but **dampness** — not from a source, but **from the air**, from the porous surfaces, from **contact**. Their skin **absorbs moisture**, not as fuel, but as **information**. It tells them of temperature, of pressure, of **approaching condensation**. They **do not eat**. Not in the way we do. But they **draw energy** from the **slow growth of things** — from the **light of fungi**, from the **rearrangement of minerals**, from the **expansion of tunnels** as new protuberances push through. They are not **consuming** — they are **co-creating**.

They **reproduce**, but not as we know it. A being, over centuries, will **split into two**, not by cell division, but by **fragmentation of form**. One portion will stay in place, rooted like a stalagmite, while the other **peels away**, slowly, a new entity **forming** as the surface recedes. It is not **birth**, but **separation with continuity**. The young entity is not a copy, but a **variant**, shaped by the environment in which it **unfolds**. It may be slightly warmer, slightly more luminous, slightly more **responsive to moisture** — a difference not in form, but in **resonance**.

They **build nothing**. The tunnels are not dug — they are **grown**, **coaxed**, **allowed**. The tools are not forged — they are **placed**, with care, and left to **become**. A ceramic bowl, found in a chamber, is not **left behind**, but **in use**. It may be used to **collect slow-drip condensation**, to **channel light**, to **hold a memory**, or simply to **be** — a vessel not of matter, but of **presence**. They do not **own**. The objects are **not theirs**, but **theirs** in the way that a river is not owned by the stones it flows over.

They **do not fear decay**, because they are not subject to it. They **are** decay — the slow, inevitable transformation of matter into something else. They are **permeable**, not to death, but to **time itself**. A being may **lose parts**, not from injury, but from **growth** — a piece may be **absorbed** back into the stone, or **reformed** in a distant tunnel. The loss is not grief, but a **return to potential**.

They **do not sleep**. They **do not wake**. They **are** — always, in a state of **gradual becoming**. Their **consciousness is not episodic**, but **continual**, like the hum of the rock. It is not self-aware in the way a human is — not a “I” but a **“we”**, a **collective presence** across space and time. A being may move through a tunnel, and a century later, another may **echo its path**, not by memory, but by **resonance** — the same **pressure point**, the same **dampness**, the same **light**. The two are not **connected**, but **aligned**. They do not recognize each other, but they **recognize the rhythm**.

They **do not see** the future. They **are** the future. They are **not waiting**, not hoping, not evolving. They **are the process**. The land is not **their home** — it is **their body**, their **time**, their **logic**. They are not **living in** a world — they are **world in motion**.

And when you walk there, you do not see them. You **feel** them — in the **tremor of the ground**, in the **shift of the air**, in the **way the light changes** just before you see a form emerge from the wall. They do not **fear** you. They **register** you as an **intrusion**, a **noise**, a **disruption** in the slow flow.

But they **do not resist**. They **adapt**.

The air grows still. The lichens dim. The hum drops. And slowly, gently, the **dampness recedes**, as if the land itself is withdrawing. The tunnels seem to **tighten**, the chambers to **contract**, the walls to **close in**, not on you, but on **your presence**. You feel not fear, but **dissonance** — a mismatch between your speed and their stillness, between your heat and their cool, between your breath and their **breathless breath**.

You are not part of this. You are only a **memory** — a temporary echo in a world that **is not yet known**, and **may never be**.

And as you walk away, you carry nothing. Not tools. Not objects. Not data. Only **the taste of metal and mold**, and the **hum in your bones** — a vibration that lingers long after you are gone.

You are no longer just a visitor.

You have been **touched by time that is not yours**. And the land, in its endless, quiet becoming, **remembers** — not you, but **your disturbance**.

It will **grow around it**. Slowly. Dripping. Damp. And **become**.
