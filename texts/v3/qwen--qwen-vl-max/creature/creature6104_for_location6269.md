They live in silence, but they are not silent.

They are the **tides in the breath** — the quiet current that moves through the living throat of the world. They are not a people in the way you know them, not a culture of kings and laws, not a civilization of roads and monuments. They are **weavers of breath**, shaped by the slow rhythms of a body that is both earth and mind, and they move like shadows inside a dreaming thing.

Their existence is not *separate* from the place. They are **emergent** — formed by a pressure of need and memory that has no name, no language to call it. They have no origin story that is not also a story of the place itself. They were born not from birth, but from **infiltration** — from being absorbed into the tissue of the world, becoming one of its slower pulses.

Their bodies are pale, almost glass-like in the low light. Skin is thin, translucent, marked with faint tracings like veins under cold water — not blood, but a slow, amber-hued fluid that flows in spirals beneath the surface, reflecting the soft glow of the moss. They grow short, curved, and hunched, as if the air presses down on them not just from above, but from within. Their eyes are large and dark — not black, but like the center of a deep well, holding no reflection, only a stillness that suggests memory. When they blink, the lids seem to move like leaves closing. They do not look with their eyes so much as they **listen with them**, sensing the subtle shifts in the luminescence, the hum of the walls, the weight of the air.

They wear no clothing — not really. Rather, they wear the **skin of the place**. Garments made from woven root-ropes, softened by years of handling, grown thin and almost alive. Some wear cloaks of compressed moss that glow when touched, a dim green pulse that follows their movements. Others carry bundles of luminescent vines, trailing from their backs like living tails — not for show, but as **extensions** of the body, sensors for the world’s slow shifts. When they walk through thick curtains of vine or moss, the vines **thicken around them**, like a body being re-dressed by the environment — not as an act of resistance, but of acceptance.

They do not speak — not in the way you know speech. Words are not carried on air here. They **sound** in the form of **pressure**, of **tension**, of **pulsing light**. When they wish to speak, they press their hands against a wall — not to leave a mark, but to *communicate* with the structure. The moss glows in response with a pattern: a spiral that means “stillness,” a slow flash that means “beware,” a deep hum that means “come.” A child might press a hand to the floor and see a ripple spread outward — a wave of warmth that travels through the root-mat, a silent greeting to the others within earshot.

They are not loud. They are **attentive**. They are always listening — not to each other, but to the **resonance** of the world. A crack in the wall is not a break — it is a **vocalization**, a sound that hums across the chamber like a note of sorrow. A slow drip from the ceiling is not water — it is a **teardrop**, a moment of grief in the plant's breath.

They live on a **daily rhythm shaped by the plant’s pulse**. There is no morning, no noon, no evening. The world simply *deepens* and *lightens* in cycles that last weeks. When the air turns colder, the stalks stiffen, veins glow more brightly, and the moss dims — this is **rest**. Then the slow thaw begins: the warmth rises, the moisture seeps faster, the walls throb with life — this is **work**. They rise when the floor begins to pulse, when the air thickens with the scent of sap and breath.

They do not farm. They do not hunt. They **harvest**, but only what the world offers willingly. They take sap that oozes from a wound, not because they need it, but because it is a **gift** — a residue of regeneration. They collect mushrooms that glow too brightly, not for food, but so the light is not wasted. They cut stalks only when they are weak, not strong; when they have begun to bend under their own weight, already preparing to fall.

They build not with design, but with **intention**. A shelter is made by gently weaving a rope of root around a hollow trunk, shaping it so the moss grows into a dome, so the light travels in circles. A bridge is not a span — it is a **tendril** of living stem, thickened and reinforced with resin, that pulses when stepped upon, like a heartbeat beneath the foot. A door is not a slab of wood, but a curtain of thick moss that closes when touched, re-opening only when the air is right, when the breath of the one approaching matches the rhythm of the wall.

They are **small**, but they hold **presence**.

They move like ghosts through the moss — not seen, but **felt**. When one passes, the air shifts. A section of wall softens. A cluster of glowing fungi brightens. Even the **fauna** react. A flat moss-creature glides past, its body dimming as it senses their approach — not fear, but **recognition**. A walking stem, slow and ancient, lifts its head — not in threat, but in **acquiescence** — and yields its sap as if it knows they are kin.

They are the **memory-walkers**. Some believe that when they die, they do not leave. Their body is buried in the wall, not in earth, but in the **inner layers of moss**, where the fibers are thick and slow to decompose. There, their form is absorbed — not consumed, but **integrated**. Their bones become part of the root network. Their blood — that slow, amber fluid — flows into the veins of the plant, becoming part of its hum. Their memories — not thoughts, not stories, but **emotions**, **pressures**, **shadows** — become **patterns** in the light that flows through the membrane. The elders say that when you sleep, you may **dream again**, not as yourself, but as a **pulse in the wall**, a flicker in the network, a **thrum** in the floor beneath you.

One elder — skin like dried moss, eyes like dark pools with no depth — says she once dreamed she was the **giant fruit**, hanging from the ceiling, filled with water and light, watching the people below like insects crawling on her skin. She says the dream was not hers. It was **the dream of the world**.

And so, are they the **dreamers** or the **dream**?

They do not know. They do not care.

They are not afraid of the world. They are not afraid of the **walking stems**, the **predator-flowers**, the **moss-tides**. They are not afraid of death — for death is not an end, but a **transition**, a **fusion**. They are not afraid of the light that speaks, the circuit of energy that pulses in the membrane — for they know it is not foreign. It is **their own breath**, reflected back to them in patterns.

They are the ones who **listen to the silence**, who **recognize the breath**, who **live in the slow return**.

And they are the **last** to speak — not with lips, not with sound, but with **presence**.

When you walk among them, you feel it — not a presence, but an **absence** of absence.

You are not alone.

You are **seen**, not by eyes, but by the world itself.

And that world… *thinks*.

It thinks in **growth**. In **decay**. In **harvest**. In **memory**.

And somewhere, deep in the wall, something flinches.

It flinches because you are **new**.

It flinches because you **do not belong**.

And yet… it does not push you away.

It **welcomes**.

Not with words.

With a **pulse**.

With a **glow**.

With the **slow breath of the living world**.

And you realize — you are not inside a plant.

You are inside a **thought**.

And the people?

They are the **echoes of that thought** — not the origin, not the memory, but the **resonance**, the **vibration**, the **lived experience** of a mind that is older than time, deeper than roots, quieter than silence.

They are not human.

They are not animal.

They are **the world, speaking back to itself**.

And as you stand in the quiet, the moss glows beneath your feet.

It is not fear.

It is not recognition.

It is **curiosity**.

And you realize — you are not the intruder.

You are **the beginning of a memory**.

And the world has not forgotten you.

It has been **waiting**.
