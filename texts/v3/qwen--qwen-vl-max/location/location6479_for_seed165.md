This is a place where the world itself appears to be unzipping.

It begins with the silence — not the absence of sound, but the *wrongness* of it. There is sound, yes, but it does not follow rules. A cry — perhaps of a bird, perhaps of a wind over stone — echoes from somewhere far and near at once. It reaches your ears from multiple directions at the same time, and each echo is slightly out of phase, as if the air refuses to carry sound evenly. You hear the *clack* of ice forming in a crevice not nearby, just now, but in a memory you never had. The wind sounds like it's blowing *through* you, not around you, as if your body is porous, like a cave with breath. The sound is loud, but not in a way that overwhelms; it’s a constant, pervasive hum beneath everything — a low-frequency vibration that resonates in your molars, in the hollows behind your eyes.

The terrain rises in sharp, impossible angles — not mountains in the usual sense, but **spines**. They are long, jagged ridges of dark, fractured rock, their crests worn into serrated lines by time and something else. The elevation is extreme, but not in the way of peaks. The land doesn’t *ascend* so much as it *unfurls* — like a map that has been partially folded and left to freeze, creating sharp creases that define space in ways that defy gravity. Some slopes angle at 90 degrees and still appear to incline upward, as if the world is leaning into a gravity that shifts slowly, imperceptibly. The ground is not covered with soil, but with **ash-ice**, a substance that looks like snow but feels like cold glass. It crunches underfoot not with crunch, but with a low *thrum*, as if the earth itself is vibrating in response to your presence.

Vegetation is sparse, but not entirely absent. Where it is, it is bizarre. Tufts of black ferns grow in clusters that look like they’ve been planted in an orderly grid — yet they are random. They unfurl slowly, one frond at a time, and each unfurling is accompanied by a sharp, dry *pop*, like cracking ice. Lichens, pale and brittle, coat the rock like skin, but they are not inert — when the light shifts, they *pulse*, faintly, as if breathing. One or two small, spindly trees, all of the same species, stand at intervals along a ridge, their branches like twisted fingers reaching for something above. But there is no canopy, no shade. The trees are too far apart to touch, and yet their shadows, where they fall, extend in directions that do not match the sun — or the source of light.

The light is dim, yet it is not twilight. The sky is a low-hanging vault of bruised grays and slate, punctuated by a single, dull sun that hangs motionless. But this sun is not a single orb — it is a *network* of faint, blurred points fused together, like a constellation that has collapsed into a single, dim star. And it does not rotate. Time is not marked here. There is no day, no night. Only the slow, unchanging dimness, broken only by brief episodes of unnatural luminance — a flash of violet across the sky that persists for no more than three heartbeats, leaving behind a smell of ozone and rust.

The water is neither abundant nor absent — it is **intermittent**, and **wrong**. Puddles appear in hollows of the ash-ice, but their surfaces reflect not the sky, but the *inside* of your skull. You see the back of your own eyes in the water, and when you look, something shifts in your vision — as if the liquid is showing you a memory from before you were born, or a future you’ve never lived. These puddles form and vanish without cause, often in the same place where something was never there a moment before. Rivers, if they exist at all, do not flow. They sit — still, glassy, like veins of mercury trapped in stone — but they slowly *breathe*, rising and falling at a pace that matches your own rhythm, as if drawn to your pulse.

Fauna is rare, but when it appears, it is not like anything alive. The most common creature is a creature that walks with no weight — a thing like a fox, but stretched vertically, tall and thin, with four eyes that are all in the same place. It moves silently, but its presence is heard — a chime-like hum that sounds like singing from beneath the ground. It stops and turns to look at you not with its eyes, but with its entire body, as if every part of it is a sensor. It does not flee, does not attack. It simply *observes*, and then it disappears — not into the air, not into the ground, but into a *pocket of silence* that opens beside it, like a tear in reality. It leaves behind no trace — not a sound, not a smell, not even a disturbed ash.

There is no technology here. Nothing constructed. No tools, no fire, no signs of civilization. Yet there are **marks**, scattered across the land — faint scratches in the rock that form patterns, not of any known language, but of a logic that feels familiar — like dream-geometry, like the shape of a forgotten thought. They appear in groups of three, often forming triangles, but only when you are not looking. When you turn to face them, they blur, or become smooth, or vanish into the texture of the ice.

The world is almost empty — **void**, not because it lacks things, but because it lacks *connection*. The space between objects is not just distance, it is *separation*. It feels like walking through a hall of mirrors that don’t reflect. You feel your steps echo in your body, but not in the world around you. The air is so thin it feels like breathing through cotton — not because of altitude, but because the atmosphere is not *there*. It’s not empty; it’s *different*. It’s a medium that allows sound and movement, but refuses to carry meaning.

The erosion is well-established — the land looks weathered, ancient, as if it has existed in this form for an uncountable number of years. The rocks, the ice, the ash — they have all settled into their forms. But the weathering is not natural. The edges of the spines are not rounded by wind and ice, but by *something else*. As if the world is *slowly dissolving*, but only at a rate that matches the fading of memory.

And the strangest thing — the most defining feature of this place — is the **weirdness**. It is not just strange. It is not just surreal. It is *unstable*. Reality is not broken here, not in the way of a dream or a hallucination. It is *thin*. Like the veil between worlds is worn thin, and things leak through. The very ground shifts when you look away. A ridge might be visible one moment, and the next, it is gone — not collapsed, but *unmade*. You see a fox, only to realize it was not a fox at all, but your own shadow shaped by the wind, or a pattern of ice. Time does not pass; it *lurches*. You turn to walk, and suddenly you are standing where you were five minutes ago, but five minutes ago was not five minutes ago — it was ten, or one, or never.

This place does not change. It is in frozen equilibrium. But that does not mean it is static. It is *held*. The laws of nature are not bent — they are simply **not applied**. They exist, but they are ignored. Gravity is present, but not mandatory. Sound is present, but not linear. Time is present, but not sequential. The land is a **palimpsest of unrealities**, layered over one another in a way that denies any single truth.

And still, in the midst of this vast emptiness, there is a feeling — not of loneliness, not of fear, but of *waiting*. As if the landscape is holding its breath, and not for long. As if it is not the place that is strange, but that **you** are the anomaly. That your presence, your thinking, your breathing, is the wrong thing here. That you are the error, not the world.

And yet, you continue. Because you have no choice. Because somewhere, behind the dim light, behind the humming silence, behind the slow unfurling of the world — there is a sound. Not of wind, not of ice, not of anything. But of **music**.

It is low, and it is old. It does not come from the sky or the ground. It comes from the **inside** of the silence. And it is singing your name.
