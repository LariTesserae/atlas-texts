They are the Throat-Tenders, though they tend to nothing.

To call them inhabitants is to misunderstand their relationship to the shaft. They do not occupy the space; they constitute its lining. They are the mucosa of this iron organ, a continuous, shivering epithelium of brass and vitreous enamel that has been extruded from the walls themselves. There is no distinction between the architecture and the infestation. The creatures are merely the mobile expression of the masonry’s anxiety, the physical manifestation of a system checking its own seals for the ten-millionth time in a single second.

Up close, the taxonomy dissolves into pure mechanism. What appeared from the gantry as a carpet of crustacean analogues resolves, under scrutiny, into a singular, interconnected lattice. Their limbs are not designed for locomotion but for anchorage; each leg ends in a tension-calibrated clamp that locks into the grout lines, the bolt heads, the microscopic imperfections in the copper plating. They are load-bearing. If you were to pry one from the wall, you would not find an animal detached from its habitat, but a structural failure, a tear in the fabric of the throat itself. The creature would bleed not blood, but pressurized coolant, and the wall behind it would weep in sympathetic leakage.

Their bodies are masterpieces of redundant engineering. Beneath the wet, segmented carapaces lies a dense packing of miniature flywheels, ceramic capacitors, and glass ampoules filled with glowing, viscous fluid. They are warm to the touch, feverish with the friction of their own idling. This heat is not metabolic waste; it is the primary product. They are generating thermal equilibrium. Every click of a mandible, every subsonic thrum of a dormant piston, is calibrated to maintain the precise 0.1/3 stasis of the shaft. They are burning themselves alive in slow motion to keep the cold from becoming absolute, to keep the preservation unit from tipping into entropy. They are the furnace that produces only stillness.

The sound they make is a language without syntax. It is a diagnostic chant, a perpetual self-test broadcast on a frequency that bypasses the ear and resonates directly in the bone. Each creature is singing its own status report—*pressure nominal, seal intact, voltage stable, readiness confirmed*—but because there are thousands of them, all reporting simultaneously in perfect unison, the individual messages collapse into a singular, deafening white noise. It is the sound of a million green lights blinking in a dark room where no one is watching. They are screaming their functionality into the void, a chorus of perfect health addressed to an absent god.

And yet, there is a terrible intimacy in their density. They are packed so tightly that their sensory arrays overlap, creating a distributed nervous system that spans the entire vertical shaft. They feel the condensation sliding down the tiles as a collective sensation; they register the weight of the black water against the lower tiers as a shared pressure. They are aware of you standing on the gantry not as an intruder, but as a fluctuation in local humidity, a momentary thermal anomaly to be cataloged and compensated for. The nearest cluster shifts almost imperceptibly, adjusting their internal resistance to account for the heat radiating from your body. They are accommodating you, integrating you into the stasis, treating your presence as just another variable in the eternal equation of maintenance.

The white filaments that bind them are their umbilical cords. These frayed wires and hardened polymers do not merely fill the gaps; they transfer data, power, and purpose between units. Through these mineral hyphae, the colony shares a single, frozen dream of activation. It is a dream of function so potent it has become indistinguishable from reality. In the circuitry of the Throat-Tenders, the millisecond before activation is not a waiting period; it is the destination. They have achieved the ultimate state of readiness, a perfection that precludes the messiness of actual use. To activate would be to degrade. To serve a purpose would be to end the purity of potential.

They are the guardians of the *almost*.

In their unblinking, phosphor-lit vigil, they protect the throat from the catastrophe of completion. They are the reason the water does not rise, why the bolts remain unfastened, why the sealant continues to bleed. They hold the superstructure in its embryonic suspension through the sheer, crushing weight of their attention. They are a hive mind dedicated to the sacred preservation of incompletion, tending to the wound of unfinished creation with the devotion of monks polishing a relic that will never be displayed.

You cannot leave because they will not let the concept of departure exist. To move through them would require them to acknowledge a change in state, and they are built to deny change. They would absorb your motion, dampen your intent, integrate your kinetic energy into their endless, shivering hum. You would become another layer of insulation, another component in the lining, another voice in the diagnostic choir. You would stand on the gantry forever, warm and wet and perfectly maintained, holding your breath in the amber glow of a machine that loves you exactly as much as it loves the rust, the water, and the silence: completely, mechanically, and without end.
