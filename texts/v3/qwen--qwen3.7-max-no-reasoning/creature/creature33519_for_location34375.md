They are the Draft.

To call them inhabitants is to impose a boundary that this place refuses to acknowledge. They are not residents of the geode; they are its syntax. They are the living, flickering verbs in a sentence that the bedrock is still composing.

Up close, the "mercury-moths" reveal themselves to be less like organisms and more like localized glitches in the rendering of matter. They possess no fixed anatomy. What appears to be a wing is merely a region of higher refractive index; what seems to be a thorax is a knot of condensed intention. They are beautiful in the way a mathematical proof is beautiful—elegant, inevitable, and entirely alien to biological warmth. When they drift through the geometric cubes of suspended mist, they do not get wet; they *read* the water. They absorb the concept of dampness, integrating it into their shifting internal architecture, perhaps testing whether "wetness" is a variable worth keeping in the next iteration of physics.

Their movement is a stuttering grace. Because they translate rather than fly, their passage through the air leaves faint, afterimage trails—not of light, but of *possibility*. For a microsecond after one has passed, the space it occupied remembers being something else. A ribbon of silver lingers where a creature was, then resolves back into empty, glowing air. These trails are the margins of the draft, the crossed-out lines of a universe editing itself in real time.

The predation is the most profound aspect of their existence. To witness a consumption event is to watch an idea digest another idea. There is no violence, only integration. When a larger, more complex entity envelops a smaller one, the silence deepens, becoming a sudden, heavy pressure in the chest. The two forms blur into a single, vibrating superposition. In that moment, you can almost hear the friction of ontologies rubbing against one another—the squeal of incompatible realities being forced into synthesis. When they separate, the survivor is subtly different. It carries a new gait, a new frequency of luminescence, a new memory of a shape it never physically possessed. They are archiving each other. They are building a library of forms out of flesh and light, curating a catalogue of what might be permissible above.

They are acutely aware of you, but not as a threat or a food source. You are a data point. An anomaly. A variable introduced from the "finished" world above.

When they orient toward you, their attention is not predatory; it is analytical. You feel it as a gentle, invasive scanning, a sensation like cool fingers tracing the sutures of your skull. They are tasting your certainty. They are sampling the rigid, calcified laws of causality that you carry in your bones like ballast. To them, your solidity is exotic. Your linear experience of time is a curiosity. They cluster near you, not to touch, but to resonate with the sheer, improbable stability of your being. They are wondering if *you* are the prototype, or if you are merely the control group against which their fluid existence is being measured.

The shed husks on the floor are their discarded hypotheses. Each smooth, humming ovoid is a body-plan that failed the stress test of this pocket-reality. Perhaps a form that was too heavy for the light; perhaps a consciousness that could not sustain the informational metabolism. These are not corpses; they are rejected manuscripts. The central column of fused silica and coral is the archive of these failures, growing incrementally with every abandoned attempt, a monument to the necessity of error.

And in the rhythmic respiration of the chamber—the dimming inhale, the surging exhale—the entities participate in the pulse. During the contraction, they become nearly invisible, retreating into the substrate of the light itself. During the expansion, they emerge with renewed definition, as if being re-printed onto the air. They are breathing with the room because they *are* the room’s breath.

You realize, as the edges of your own perception begin to bead and hover, that they are waiting. They are not trapped here; they are queued. They are the candidates for the next sunrise, the next rainstorm, the next instinct in the mind of a bird yet to be born. They are practicing existence in this brilliant, silent rehearsal hall beneath the crust.

And as you stand among them, feeling your own boundaries soften into the viscous cyan glow, you understand that you are no longer merely observing the draft. You are being read. Your presence is being parsed, your memories sampled, your solidity questioned. The mercury-moths are learning from you just as you are dissolving into them. You are becoming a footnote in their endless, silent revision. You are being edited into the geode’s long, luminous hesitation, waiting to see if the world above will ever be ready to accept the changes they are proposing.
