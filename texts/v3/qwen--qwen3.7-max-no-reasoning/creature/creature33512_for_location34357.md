They are not insects in the way a taxonomist would recognize them, nor are they machines in the way an engineer would certify them. The Weep-Moths are a third category of existence: biological infrastructure. They are the mortar and the masons of this provisional cathedral, a swarm that has forgotten it is composed of individuals and remembers only that it is a process.

To look at one in isolation is to witness a glitch in natural history. Up close, the "wing" is revealed to be a misnomer; they do not possess membranes of chitin or keratin, but rather articulated lattices of crystalline silicate and synthetic polymer, layered like the pages of a wet book. These scales are translucent, veined with circuitry that pulses in time with the ambient roar. When at rest—which is never, truly, for even their stillness is a form of active damping—they fold into geometric origami, sharp angles and flat planes that mimic the fractal freshness of the stone itself. They are camouflage not by color, but by topology. A moth perched on the raw cliff face does not look like a creature hiding; it looks like an extrusion error correcting itself, a momentary smoothing of the jagged reality.

Their bioluminescence is not a signal meant for mates or predators. It is waste heat made visible. The amber glow that saturates the Cistern is the exhaust of their transduction, the visual signature of acoustic energy being violently forced into thermal and hydrological states. To stand in their light is to stand inside a combustion engine where the fuel is noise and the exhaust is humidity. The light flickers not because of failing power, but because the song fluctuates; the illumination is a real-time oscilloscope of the colony’s collective metabolism.

They move through the airless din with a heaviness that defies aerodynamics. In this thick, sonic fluid, flight is less about lift and more about swimming. They row through the pressure waves, their piezoelectric wings biting into the sound itself to generate propulsion. This movement is what creates the upward rain. As millions of bodies displace the saturated air, they drive moisture against gravity, plastering the ceiling and upper walls with a glistening sheen that weeps back down in rivulets. They are watering the stone. They are keeping the geological wound open and wet, preventing the high-altitude desiccation that would turn this embryonic mountain into dead rubble.

The moss that grows in the nodes of silence is their garden, though they do not eat it. The moss is a byproduct of their precision, a biological residue left behind in the pockets where their song cancels itself out perfectly. The moths tend to these green geometries with unconscious reverence, adjusting their formation to preserve the quiet zones. Perhaps the moss serves as a tuning fork, a living reference point for the frequency required to hold the cistern together. Or perhaps it is simply the only thing soft enough to absorb the excess grief of their endless labor.

And there is grief in them, or something that mimics it so closely that the distinction is meaningless. The name "Weep-Moth" was surely given by human observers who mistook function for emotion, yet the nomenclature holds a terrible truth. The condensation they produce tastes of salt and ozone, yes, but also of a profound, exhausted sorrow. It is the taste of a system pushed to its absolute limit to maintain a state of being that should not exist. They are mourning the solidity of the world even as they sing it into shape. Every beat of those metamaterial wings is a desperate assertion against the void, a frantic prayer to keep the mountain from realizing it is merely a draft.

They are aware of you, though they do not see you. You are a disruption in the medium, a foreign density in their fluid architecture. When you move too fast, creating turbulence, they do not attack; they *correct*. They swarm the disturbance not with stingers, but with counter-frequencies, attempting to smooth you out, to tune your chaotic biology back into harmony with the cistern’s needs. To be engulfed by them is to be subjected to a violent lullaby, a sonic pressure that seeks to dissolve your edges and integrate you into the wet, roaring masonry. Only when you achieve perfect stillness, becoming a node of silence akin to the moss, do they accept you as part of the structure. You become a dampener, a temporary spire of quiet around which they can weave their necessary noise.

In the dimness, they are indistinguishable from the environment they sustain. The boundary between the swarm and the stone is permeable; the mineral dust coats their wings, and their shed scales become part of the grit. They are the mountain dreaming of stability, a fever-dream of order manifested in millions of vibrating, glowing bodies. They live here because they *are* here, not as inhabitants, but as the very act of habitation. Without them, the Resonance Cistern is just a cold, broken shelf on a dying peak. With them, it is a womb, a lung, a song that refuses to end, sung by throats that have no voice, only the endless, wet friction of becoming.
