They are not creatures in the way a forest holds deer or a reef holds fish. They are the mortar of the dark.

To speak of them is to speak of texture first, and taxonomy never. They are the **Lithovoric Weave**, a collective noun for a billion distinct morphologies that have surrendered individuality to the necessity of contact. They do not possess names, only functions: the grinders, the slickeners, the absorbers, the binders.

Closest to the mineral crust are the **Basal Raspers**. These are flat, plate-like organisms, no larger than a thumbprint but as hard as fired clay. They are sessile, cemented to the stone by secretions that smell faintly of sulfur and old blood. They do not move; they vibrate. Their undersides are lined with microscopic, diamond-hard cilia that oscillate at frequencies just below human hearing, pulverizing the silicate scabs beneath them into a fine, edible grit. They are the reason the ground yields like leather; they have chewed the geology into a paste over eons. When you step, you are stepping on a living millstone, a pavement of digestion that pauses its grinding only to accommodate the pressure of your foot before resuming its tectonic abrasion.

Above the raspers lies the **Mucus Stratum**. This is not a single species but a guild of soft-bodied, vermiform things, translucent and pale as drowned roots. They are the lubricants of the Sump. Without them, the friction of the raspers would generate fatal heat; with them, the basin remains cool. They secrete a hyper-viscous gel that coats every surface, trapping the aerosolized moisture and creating the "weeping suspension" of the air. They are blind, eyeless tubes of pure sensation, navigating entirely through the chemical gradients of waste and water. To touch them is to touch the valley’s own saliva. They flow over the raspers in endless, looping currents, redistributing the mineral paste, ensuring that no patch of stone is left unprocessed, no nutrient hoarded. They are the circulatory system of a body that has no heart.

Suspended within this gel, and drifting through the saturated air, are the **Aerosol Drifters**. These are the smallest, most numerous entities, the true source of the white noise. They are chitinous mites, springtails, and things that defy classification, existing in a state of perpetual, chaotic Brownian motion. They do not fly; they are carried by the thermal convection of the basin’s breath and the mechanical turbulence of the layers below. They are the pollen of a sunless world, but instead of fertilizing flowers, they fertilize the mucus and the stone. They land, feed for a microsecond, reproduce, and die, their tiny corpses instantly absorbed back into the gel. The deafening roar of the Sump is largely the sound of their collisions, a trillion tiny impacts per second, the acoustic static of life operating at the limit of physical space.

And then, there are the **Incorporators**.

These are the rarest forms, yet they define the logic of the place. They are large only by comparison—perhaps the size of a human hand, though shapeless and shifting. They possess no mouth, no gut, no anus. They are mobile membranes, wandering sacks of enzymatic potential. They do not hunt. They simply *encounter*. When an Incorporator flows over a cluster of raspers, or a knot of mucus-worms, or a drift of dead mites, it does not consume them as a predator consumes prey. It merges with them. The boundary dissolves. The biomass is assimilated, broken down, and re-extruded as new tissue, new secretion, new vibration. Death here is not an end; it is a change of address. The Incorporators are the editors of the ecosystem, pruning excess growth, filling gaps, maintaining the terrible, perfect equilibrium. They are the reason there is no waste, no rot, only transformation.

None of these beings possess a nervous system capable of pain, or fear, or desire. They operate on a logic of hydraulic and chemical imperative. They are not alive in the sense of being awake; they are alive in the sense of being *active*. They are components in a machine made of meat and mineral, a biological engine that has been running since the last light faded from this throat of the world.

To stand among them is to be invaded. The humidity enters your lungs, carrying spores and enzymes that taste of iron. The vibrations travel up your bones, syncing your heartbeat to the slow, grinding pulse of the basal layer. You feel the mucus stratum testing your skin, assessing your dryness, your foreignness. You are not observed; you are *sampled*.

They do not know you are there. They only know that a pocket of resistance has appeared in the weave, a disruption in the flow. And so, with the patient, mindless efficiency of water finding a crack in stone, they begin to work around you, over you, into you. Not out of malice. Not out of hunger. But because in the Sump of Echoes, separation is an error, and all errors must be corrected.

You are, to them, merely another form of sediment waiting to be processed. Another note in the endless, deafening song of matter turning upon itself. They are the inhabitants of eternity, and they have been waiting, in their blind, wet, thunderous silence, for you to become part of the floor.
