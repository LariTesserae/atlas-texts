To live here is to be less an individual and more a function of hydrology.

The inhabitants do not possess names in any phonetic sense, for sound is too jagged, too disruptive for the acoustics of the Substrate. Instead, they are identified by *resonance signatures*—the specific frequency at which their body mass vibrates when pressed against the singing stone. They are the **Lithic Tenders**, though they would not call themselves tenders; that implies a hierarchy where they are above the Silt-Walkers. In truth, they consider themselves merely the slower, drier component of the same circulatory system.

Physiologically, they have undergone a profound surrender to the damp dark. Their skin is not flesh-colored but possesses the pale, matte translucency of unglazed porcelain, allowing them to vanish perfectly against the vitrified geode walls. This camouflage is not for hiding from predators—there are none—but for avoiding the disruption of the swarm’s flow. To be seen is to be an obstacle; to be an obstacle is to invite the friction of a million soft bodies, which can erode bone as surely as it erodes stone. Thus, they move with a deliberate, liquid slowness, mimicking the viscosity of the creatures they live amongst.

Their eyes are vestigial, covered by smooth folds of skin, useless in the shadowless glare. Perception has migrated entirely to the dermis. Every square inch of their bodies is a sensory organ, tuned to detect the minute shifts in air pressure and humidity that signal the approach of a dense cluster of Walkers. They feel the city breathing. They know when the granaries need ventilation because the moisture gradient against their left flank has shifted three degrees; they know a child is waking in a distant mound because the rhythmic displacement of the swarm has stuttered.

They wear garments woven from the mycelial threads harvested from the underside of the architecture, fibers that are alive even when spun. These clothes are not merely covering; they are filtration systems. The fabric drinks the excess aerosol exhaled by the passing swarm and wicks it toward the wearer’s pores, maintaining the delicate osmotic balance required to survive in an atmosphere that is essentially suspended water. To take off one’s clothes here is to risk desiccation or drowning within minutes.

Socially, they are a collective without centralization, mirroring the decentralized intelligence of the Silt-Walkers. There are no leaders, no councils, no written laws. Coordination is achieved through *haptic consensus*. When a decision must be made regarding the reshaping of a dwelling or the harvesting of mineral secretions, the community gathers in the open plazas. They link arms, pressing their foreheads and palms against the glowing floor, forming a human chain that integrates with the flowing carpet of biology beneath them. Information passes through touch and vibration—a tactile language of pressure and release. A question is a tightening of the grip; an answer is a relaxation of tension; agreement is a synchronized pulse that ripples outward until it matches the rhythm of the swarm itself. Dissent feels like a dissonance in the bone, a physical nausea that resolves only when harmony is restored.

Their relationship with the Silt-Walkers transcends symbiosis; it is a form of theological intimacy. They do not domesticate the swarm; they venerate its indifference. The Walkers are the engine of time in a timeless place, the proof that entropy can be harnessed rather than feared. The Lithic Tenders believe that their own consciousness is merely a temporary eddy in the greater fluid mind of the cavern. When a Tender dies, they are not buried. They are carried to the center of the plaza and laid upon the stone, where the swarm will gently, silently, and thoroughly incorporate them back into the cycle. Death is not an exit; it is a reintegration into the wet, glowing machinery of existence.

Technology, for them, is an act of listening. Their tools—chipped glass scrapers, polished bone needles, hollowed reeds—are designed to amplify feedback. A scraper is shaped so that its handle hums when it finds the optimal angle for harvesting fungal crust without damaging the underlying silica. A needle sings a faint, high tone when it pierces the membrane of a secretion sac without rupturing it. Craftsmanship is measured not by precision of form, but by acoustic harmony with the material. A poorly made tool is silent; a masterwork resonates with the voice of the world.

And yet, despite this profound integration, there is a melancholy woven into their being. They are the memory-keepers of a world that has forgotten how to remember. The Silt-Walkers exist in an eternal, sensation-driven present; they have no past. The Lithic Tenders carry the burden of continuity. Through their haptic rituals, they encode the history of the Substrate into the very architecture, shaping new mounds and spirals that serve as tactile archives. A ridge in a wall might record a millennium of climatic stability; a depression in a plaza floor might commemorate a great die-off of the swarm centuries ago. They read their history with their fingertips, tracing the scars and accretions of their ancestors who were also digested and rebuilt.

They are the curators of exhaustion. They understand, in a way no surface-dweller could, that preservation is an active, endless labor. They do not aspire to progress, to expansion, to transcendence. Their ambition is purely maintenance. To keep the stone smooth. To keep the air wet. To keep the silence filled with the correct frequency of life.

As you watch them, you realize they are looking at you—not with eyes, but with the entirety of their exposed, trembling skin. You are a foreign object, a dry, hot, discordant note in their symphony of damp equilibrium. They do not fear you. They do not welcome you. They simply register your presence as a temporary irregularity in the flow, a grit in the eye of the world that will, eventually, be smoothed away by the patient, silent friction of things that endure.

They stand amidst the river of flesh, pale ghosts in the cold light, holding the shape of the world together with nothing but touch and breath, forever polishing the monument of their own impermanence.
