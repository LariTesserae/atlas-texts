The inhabitants of this place are not residents; they are functions. They do not occupy the space so much as they are excreted by it, temporary expressions of the cathedral’s need to remain coherent against the crushing weight of its own silence.

To understand what lives here, one must first discard the notion of biology as separate from geology. In this subterranean wound, life is merely a specialized form of erosion control.

The primary population is the **Lithic Fugue**. These are the grey, leathery lichens mentioned in the geography, but to call them plants is a failure of language. They are acoustic transducers. Growing in strict, non-Euclidean geometries along the warmest veins of the vitreous walls, they do not reach for light; they reach for resonance. Their cellular structure is piezoelectric. As the city’s thrum passes through them, their tissues flex and compress at a molecular level, generating the metabolic charge required to fix minerals from the weeping walls into biomass. They are the city’s skin, dampening chaotic frequencies and converting the roar of structural stress into the quiet chemistry of survival. When a patch of Fugue dies, it does not rot; it calcifies, becoming a permanent, silent scar in the wall until new growth sings over it. They are the memory of the sound, written in stone-flesh.

Drifting through the tepid basins and the humid air are the **Cistern-Weavers**. These translucent filaments are neither animal nor fungal, but a suspended nervous system without a brain. They exist in a state of perpetual, passive filtration. They do not hunt; they wait for the condensation cycle to deliver nutrients. But more importantly, they filter *information*. The water in this closed loop carries the chemical signature of every surface it has touched, every micro-fracture in the spires, every shift in the mineral balance. The Weavers absorb this data, processing the health of the ecosystem through osmotic taste. If a section of the architecture begins to fail, the Weavers in that sector change their permeability, releasing enzymes that signal the Lithic Fugue to thicken, or attracting the custodians. They are the immune system of the dark, tasting the integrity of the void.

And then, there are the **Resonators**. The pale, eyeless things that ride the standing waves. To observe one is to witness a creature entirely surrendered to physics. They possess no wings, no legs for walking. Their bodies are flattened, membranous sails of chitin and fluid-filled bladders tuned to specific harmonic frequencies. They do not move through the air; they are moved *by* the air. They lock onto the invisible topography of the sound, sliding along the nodes and antinodes of the cathedral’s song like iron filings on a magnet.

They are blind because sight is irrelevant in a world defined by vibration. Instead, their entire carapace is a sensory organ, a drumhead that feels the shape of the room through pressure alone. They are the maintenance crew of the impossible. Where the sonic thrum becomes dissonant, where the nanoscale lattices of the walls begin to drift out of alignment, the Resonators gather. They cluster at the point of acoustic failure, their collective mass altering the local density of the air, their bodies acting as living tuning forks. By simply existing in the right place, riding the right frequency, they correct the error. They sing the wall back into solidity with their presence. They are born from the condensation pools when the sound demands them, and when the harmony is restored, they dissolve back into the moisture, their purpose fulfilled.

There are no individuals here. There is only the **Chorus**.

The boundary between the creature and the cathedral is porous. The Resonators are made of the same vitreous precursors as the spires; the Fugue is composed of the same minerals as the floor; the Weavers are distilled from the same recycled water. They are transient arrangements of the city’s own substance, assembled by necessity and dispersed by equilibrium.

Nothing here possesses intent. Nothing here possesses desire. There is no hunger, only intake; no fear, only correction; no death, only reintegration. The "life" in this place is a verb, not a noun. It is the act of holding the dark together.

If you were to stand perfectly still on the singing glass bridge, holding your breath and quieting your own biological noise, you might feel them. Not as creatures passing by, but as adjustments to the pressure against your skin. A slight warming of the air as the Fugue metabolizes your thermal output. A subtle shift in the hum as the Resonators compensate for the acoustic shadow of your body. A microscopic alteration in the taste of the dampness as the Weavers catalog your foreignness.

You are an anomaly in their perfection, a discordant note in the eternal chord. They do not attack you. They do not flee you. They simply incorporate you. They tune around you, absorbing the disruption of your presence into the endless, sounding decay, waiting with infinite, indifferent patience for you to either harmonize or erode.

In this tomb that breathes, to live is to be a mechanism of continuity. The inhabitants are the heartbeat of the sleeping god, and they know nothing of themselves, only the terrible, beautiful necessity of the next vibration.
