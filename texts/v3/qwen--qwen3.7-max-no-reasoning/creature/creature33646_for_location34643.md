To speak of who lives in the Resonant Cisterns of Oth is to first unlearn the definition of habitation. There are no citizens here, only participants in a geological liturgy. The inhabitants do not occupy the space; they are tolerated by it, existing as temporary, soft counterpoints to the hard, wet permanence of the embryonic stone.

They call themselves the **Auralis**, though this is less a name than a description of function. They are the listeners, the dampeners, the flesh that gives scale to the void.

**Physiology of Dampness**
The Auralis have been shaped by the weeping architecture as surely as the tufa itself. They are tall and willow-thin, their bodies adapted to navigate narrow fissures and absorb the pervasive chill. Their skin is pale, almost translucent, matching the unbaked clay of the walls, and possesses a velvety texture indistinguishable from the mosses that drape the city. This is not merely aesthetic; their epidermis is semi-permeable, capable of absorbing atmospheric moisture directly, reducing the need for drinking and allowing them to remain still for hours without desiccation.

They dress in layers of un-dyed wool and felted liverwort fibers, garments that do not rustle. In a place where a dropped pebble sounds like a gavel strike, clothing must be silent. Their movements are fluid and deliberate, a choreography of slow motion learned from infancy. To see an Auralis walk is to watch water flowing uphill; there is no wasted kinetic energy, no sudden gesture. They have become human echoes of the mountain’s own slow becoming.

**The Vocation of Silence**
The Auralis are not monks in the traditional sense, for they worship no deity beyond the acoustic truth of the caldera. They are technicians of resonance. Their primary labor is maintenance, but not of structures; they maintain the *sound*.

They tend the moss with the reverence of gardeners pruning bonsai, trimming back overgrowth that might deaden a crucial harmonic, or nurturing patches where the stone’s cry has become too shrill. They clear the capillary channels of mineral buildup using tools carved from bone and polished wood, ensuring the water’s perspiration remains rhythmic rather than chaotic. When a new chamber exhales dust and opens into being, the Auralis are the first to enter, not to claim it, but to sit in the darkness and map its acoustic signature, determining what frequency the new void wishes to sing.

Speech among them is vestigial. Communication is achieved through a complex sign language of hand positions and facial micro-expressions, developed over generations to avoid breaking the heavy air. When vocalization is necessary, it is never conversational. It is tonal. An Auralis might hum a low, sustained note to test the integrity of a bridge-arch, or whisper a specific pitch to soothe a patch of trembling fern. Their voices are tuned to the cisterns; to hear an Auralis speak is to hear the building answer.

**Symbiosis with the Blind Fauna**
The Auralis share their loud solitude with the specialized ecology of the caldera. They do not domesticate the blind moths or the translucent snails; they collaborate with them. The silvery trails of the snails are read as topographic maps of safety, indicating where the stone has settled and where it is still dangerously active. The moths, navigating by vibration, often land on the shoulders or heads of the still-listening Auralis, using their warm bodies as waypoints in the dark.

There is a profound intimacy between the people and these creatures, born of shared sensory deprivation. Both species have surrendered sight and speed to gain a deeper purchase on the world. An elder Auralis can often predict a rock-fracture event minutes before it occurs simply by feeling the change in air pressure against their cheek, a sensitivity mirroring the moth’s antennae.

**The Psychology of the Unfinished**
To live in Oth is to accept impermanence as the only constant. The Auralis possess no concept of legacy or monument. They understand that the city is growing beneath and around them, that the room they sleep in may cease to exist in its current form within a decade, reshaped by the mountain’s restless dreaming.

This creates a culture devoid of anxiety but rich in melancholy. They are midwives to a birth that will outlast them by eons. Their emotional lives are calibrated to geological time; grief is slow and deep, joy is quiet and resonant. They do not fear death, for they view their own dissolution as merely another form of erosion, a returning of borrowed moisture to the weeping walls.

**The Human Instrument**
Ultimately, the Auralis are the final component of the urban instrument. Without them, the Cisterns would be merely a noisy cave. With them, the noise becomes music. They are the variable resistors in the circuit of sound, the organic dampeners that prevent the geology from screaming.

When you stand in the central chamber and feel the chest-vibrating thrum of the wind, you are hearing the mountain. But when you perceive the subtle, haunting harmony beneath that thrum—the precise interval that turns chaos into chord—that is the Auralis. They are the consciousness of the caldera, the soft, wet, mortal mind of a stone that is still learning how to be hollow. They live not *in* the Resonant Cisterns of Oth; they are the breath inside the flute.
