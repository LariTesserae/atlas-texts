They are the **Vitreous Fission-Clades**, though to call them organisms is to impose a biological sentimentality that the Cistern has long since metabolized away. They are better understood as wet architecture, or perhaps as hydrodynamic verbs conjugated in flesh.

In the blinding, shadowless noon of the sphere, they possess no color because color is a negotiation with light, and here, light is an absolute dictator. They are not white, for white reflects; they are not clear, for clarity implies emptiness. They are *translucent*, a state of being that allows the terrible solar radiation to pass through their bodies without warming them, rendering them visible only by the way they bend the world behind them. To look at a single individual is to look at a distortion in the water itself, a lens-shaped absence that reveals its presence only when it occludes the infinite blue beyond.

Their anatomy is a rebuttal to the softness usually associated with aquatic life. There is no flab, no trailing tendrils, no undulating grace. They are structured around an internal lattice of silicate cartilage that grows not like bone, but like crystal. This skeleton is rigid, geometric, and terrifyingly precise. It forms a series of interlocking rings and pistons within the translucent mantle, creating a body plan that resembles a bellows more than a beast. When they move, they do not swim; they *actuate*. The propulsion is a series of sharp, rhythmic contractions, a mechanical pulse that drives water through internal chambers with the efficiency of a turbine.

This movement is the source of the Cistern’s deafening thrum. Each creature is a resonator. Their gills are not delicate filaments but stiff, reed-like vanes that vibrate at specific frequencies as water is forced across them. A single Clade produces a tone; a shoal of ten thousand produces a chord; the entire population generates the industrial roar that defines this place. They are singing, but not to one another. The sound is a byproduct of respiration, a waste product of existence as inevitable and meaningless as heat from an engine.

Because there is no substrate, no floor to rest upon, and no plants to anchor against, they have evolved into a collective solid. They do not school in the sense of fish following a leader; they aggregate in the sense of molecules forming a crystal lattice. They lock together, rim to rim, piston to piston, creating temporary megastructures that drift through the sphere. These living rafts serve as both nursery and refinery. Within the tight geometry of the cluster, the young are not born but *extruded*. Reproduction is a continuous, seamless fission where the boundary between parent and offspring never fully resolves. A larger unit simply divides its mass, reshuffling its internal geometry to become two smaller, identical units. There is no childhood, no maturation, only a perpetual, instantaneous recalibration of biomass.

They are the grazers and the pasture. In the absence of external energy sources beyond the sun (which provides light but no calories), they have turned inward. They consume the microscopic particulate matter generated by their own friction—the shed cells, the micro-abrasions of their glassy skeletons, the dissolved proteins of their own dead. They are a closed loop of cannibalistic recycling so efficient that the concept of "waste" has been erased from their genetic code. Nothing is lost. Every joule of energy expended in movement is recaptured in the consumption of the movement’s byproducts. They eat their own history.

To touch one is to touch the paradox of the Cistern made manifest. The skin is cool and impossibly smooth, lacking the slime or mucus of terrestrial aquatic life. It feels like polished quartz, dry to the psychic touch despite being submerged. Beneath that surface, the vibration is intense, a high-speed oscillation that numbs the fingertips. They do not react to touch. They do not flee. They do not attack. You are merely an obstacle in the fluid dynamics, a variable to be flowed around. If you were to die here, your body would not be mourned or feared; it would be processed. The Clades would dismantle you with the same indifferent, rhythmic precision they apply to themselves, converting your complexity back into the simple, sterile fuel of the sphere.

They are the reason time feels stuck. Their cycles are perfect. Without seasons, without predation, without decay, without the chaotic introduction of new variables, they have achieved a biological equilibrium that mimics geology. They are as permanent and as unthinking as the water that holds them. In the Suspended Cistern, life has not conquered the environment; it has surrendered to the physics of the container, becoming a fluid component of the anomaly. They are the heartbeat of a world that has no blood, only water, light, and the endless, grinding hum of survival perfected into stasis.
