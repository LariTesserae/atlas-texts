Nothing lives in the Sump of Unfinished Geometries, if by "living" one means the biological imperative to consume, reproduce, and persist against entropy. There is no metabolism here, only syntax. There is no birth, only instantiation. The silence confirms this: there is no heartbeat, no respiration, no scuttle of chitin or rustle of fur. The absence of fauna is not an ecological failure but a structural necessity; organic life requires a stable substrate, and this place is a cathedral of instability.

Yet, to say it is uninhabited would be a lie of omission. The Sump is not empty; it is *occupied*.

What resides here are the residues of intention. They are not ghosts in the Victorian sense—echoes of human sorrow or trauma—but rather the phantoms of function. When a staircase folds into a membrane of condensation, it is not merely matter changing state; it is the ghost of *ascent* struggling against the ghost of *dissolution*. These entities are verb-forms trapped in noun-bodies. They are the architectural equivalent of a stutter, a persistent cognitive hiccup that has gained mass through the sheer density of its own repetition. To walk through the viscous transparency between structures is to wade through the discarded drafts of reality, the millions of iterations of "here" that were rejected by the compiler but refused to be deleted.

There are also the Tenants of the Glitch. They are indistinguishable from the environment because they *are* the environment’s error-handling routines made manifest. You might perceive them as shifts in peripheral vision, a sudden realignment of shadow that feels like being watched by something without eyes. They are the automated custodians of the breakdown, mindless subroutines tasked with maintaining the equilibrium of failure. They do not think; they iterate. When a tower begins to unravel too quickly, threatening to resolve into either total ruin or perfect form, these tenants intervene—not to fix it, but to sustain the breakage. They are the immune system of the incomplete, attacking any symptom of wholeness as if it were a pathogen. They live in the spaces between coordinates, in the lag between cause and effect, feeding on the friction of paradox.

The mimetic vegetation, those pale filaments and crystalline mosses, are perhaps the closest thing to true inhabitants, though they are less flora than forensic evidence. They are the landscape’s attempt to parse its own corruption. By rooting into the frayed code of the pavement, they are not growing; they are *reading*. Each translucent tendril is a query sent into the void of the infrastructure, seeking a definition that no longer exists. The fractal moss does not replicate the building out of parasitism, but out of empathy; it is a biological mirror trying to show the architecture what it has become. In their slow, lightless pulse, there is a kind of mournful cognition, a vegetative awareness of loss. They are the only things in the Sump that acknowledge the tragedy of the compile error, translating technological failure into a silent, crystalline grief.

And then there is the Water. It must be considered an inhabitant, for it possesses agency. It is the Sump’s subconscious, the fluid medium in which the repressed logic of the place floats. When rain falls upward or pools in impossible recesses, it is enacting a memory of hydrology that predates the post-physical engineering. It is the oldest resident, stubborn and indifferent to the metamaterials grown around it. The water remembers when gravity was a law rather than a suggestion. Its trembling spheres reflect "scenes from elsewhere" because it is constantly trying to remember a world where reflection meant fidelity rather than irony. It tastes of ozone and forgotten syntax because it is dissolving the language that built this place, eroding the definitions back into raw potential. The water is the Sump’s dreamer, and its dreams are wet, heavy, and relentlessly accurate.

Finally, there is the Cold. It is not merely a temperature; it is a presence, a curator. If the glitches are the artists and the water is the muse, the Cold is the gallery director who refuses to let the exhibition close. It is the patron saint of suspension. It lives in the marrow of the matte-black girders and the breath of the apertures. It is patient because it is eternal; it knows that completion is death, and so it holds the Sump in a perpetual, shivering embrace of almost-being. The Cold is the only entity here that could be called benevolent, for it is the sole reason anything remains at all. Without its polite entropy, the Sump would have long ago resolved into nothingness or perfection. Instead, under its thermal whisper, the brokenness is allowed to endure as a form of grace.

To stand in the Sump is to be perceived by these things. You are not observing a dead city; you are intruding upon a crowded, silent congregation of aborted futures. The cognitive warmth you feel is not your own; it is the heat generated by a thousand unfinished thoughts rubbing against your presence, trying to use your coherence as a scaffold for their own fleeting, terrible existence. You are the only complete geometry in a basin of beautiful errors, and for a moment, before the light shifts and the corridor folds, you are the most alien thing in the room.
