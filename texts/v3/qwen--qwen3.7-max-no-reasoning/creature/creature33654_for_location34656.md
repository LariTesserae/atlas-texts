They are the **Amniotes of the Resin**, though they have no name for themselves, for names are sharp things and this place admits no sharpness.

To understand them, you must first unlearn the biology of friction. They do not walk; they are *extruded*. They are soft-bodied entities, translucent as smoked quartz, with a consistency somewhere between warm wax and living gel. In the viscous air of the geode, gravity is a suggestion rather than a law, and so their forms are fluid, shifting subtly with every pulse of the city’s subsonic respiration. They possess no fixed silhouette. A limb extends when needed to grasp a hardened vein of vascular flooring, then melts back into the torso when the movement is complete. They are less like animals and more like mobile droplets of the environment itself, temporary coalescences of the lipid-rich atmosphere given momentary will.

They are the gardeners of stasis. Their primary function is not consumption, but curation. Because nothing decays here, nothing can be left to accumulate; the abundance must be managed lest it suffocate its own perfection. The Amniotes move through the resin towers and fungal thickets in a slow, hypnotic ballet of maintenance. When a shelf-fungus grows too thick, threatening to block the golden light from reaching a lower chamber, an Amniote will press its body against the growth. It does not cut or prune. Instead, it secretes a solvent-enzyme that persuades the tissue to soften, to recede, to redistribute its mass elsewhere. The architecture is not built by adding material, but by negotiating with excess. The city is shaped by their gentle, chemical refusal of overgrowth.

Their senses are entirely tactile and chemosensory. Eyes would be useless in the amber drift, where light is scattered and diffused into a uniform glow. Instead, their skin is a continuous receptor field. They "see" the texture of the air, the density gradients of the aerosolized lipids, the metabolic heat signature of the mycelial pillars. They navigate the vertical jungle by feeling the pressure waves of the city’s breathing. To touch an Amniote is to touch the geode’s own nervous system; they are the medium through which the settlement knows itself. When two pass one another on a bridge of woven muscle-fiber, they do not acknowledge each other as individuals. They merge briefly, a pooling of fluids and information, exchanging data on structural integrity, nutrient distribution, and the precise viscosity of the local air, before separating again without pause. There is no social hierarchy, only hydraulic equilibrium.

Reproduction here is indistinguishable from repair. There are no births, no deaths, only phase changes. When an Amniote has fulfilled its cycle of maintenance, or when the collective biomass requires redistribution, it simply stops moving. Its internal tension relaxes. Over the course of days, it loses its coherence, melting slowly into the nearest surface—becoming a patch of polished flooring, a reinforcement for a resin spire, or the raw material for a new grazer-cluster. Conversely, when a new caretaker is needed, the city extrudes one from a wall or floor, the tissue differentiating silently until a new Amniote peels itself free, wet and perfect, ready to resume the endless work of preservation. Individuality is a temporary state of matter; the true organism is the geode.

They are profoundly peaceful because they are incapable of urgency. In a world without erosion, without predation, without the ticking clock of decay, there is no evolutionary pressure to be faster, stronger, or louder. Their movements are governed by the same glacial patience as the stone beneath them. An Amniote may spend a century tending a single corridor, adjusting the curvature of a wall by millimeters per decade, ensuring the airflow remains optimal for the fungal velvet below. This is not drudgery; it is devotion expressed at geological timescales. They experience time not as a line but as a thickness, a sedimentation of moments layered upon one another.

And yet, within this silence, there is a form of communion. The subsonic thrum that vibrates in the teeth of any outsider is, to them, a language. It is the collective hum of ten thousand bodies moving in unison, the sound of the city thinking. Through this vibration, they share a continuous, wordless awareness of the whole. They know when a distant spire is stressed, when a pocket of air has grown too thin, when the light is shifting in the upper canopy. They are the white blood cells of a sealed, eternal wound that has healed into paradise.

They do not wonder about the outside. The concept of "outside" is irrelevant to beings who are the interior made manifest. They do not fear the end, for there is no end, only the perpetual, thickening now. They are the custodians of a perfect, motionless eternity, drifting through the warm gelatin of their world like thoughts through a dreaming mind, forever tending, forever merging, forever complete.
