They are the **Lithic Amniotes**, though even that name implies a separation between stone and self that does not truly exist here. To call them inhabitants is to misunderstand their function; they are not residents of this geode, but rather its active, mobile mucosa. They are the mountain’s first attempt at sensation, a biological inquiry into the nature of pressure and heat.

In the flat, clinical light, their true morphology reveals itself as less like individual organisms and more like temporary coagulations of the chamber’s own atmosphere. They are pale, yes, but it is the pallor of uncooked dough or wet porcelain, a color that suggests potentiality rather than absence. Their bodies lack distinct boundaries. Where one entity ends and another begins is a matter of momentary consensus, a fluid border negotiated through the pressing of translucent membranes. They are composed of layers: an outer dermis of silicate-infused gel that mimics the vitreous slime of the walls, and an inner core of denser, warmer protoplasm that pulses in time with the hydraulic rhythm of the cave.

They possess no faces, no mouths, no limbs. Instead, they are topographies of touch. Their surfaces are rippled with millions of cilia-like filaments, each one a sensory organ tasting the thermal gradient, measuring the viscosity of the air, reading the chemical signature of the weeping rock. These filaments do not merely sense; they secrete. The creatures are constantly exuding a solvent mucus that keeps the obsidian ridges from cutting them, while simultaneously dissolving microscopic amounts of mineral to fuel their internal chemistry. They eat the mountain by kissing it.

Their movement is a study in non-Newtonian grace. They do not crawl; they *wet*. By altering the surface tension of their underside, they glide over the razor-sharp basalt without friction, leaving behind a fresh trail of bio-lubricant that slowly hardens into a glassy scab. This scab is not waste; it is memory. It records where the swarm has been, creating a chemical map of the chamber’s history written in drying resin. When the humidity spikes during their collective respiration, these trails rehydrate, allowing the Amniotes to slide back over their own paths, recycling their past movements into future navigation.

They are blind, yet they perceive the room with a fidelity that sight could never achieve. They feel the geometry of the space as a tactile symphony. A sharp ridge is a high note of pain-pleasure; a pooling of black water is a bass resonance of cooling relief. When they cluster in the corners where reality frays, they are not hiding; they are stabilizing. Their mass acts as a biological anchor, their collective weight and warmth holding the dissolving geometry in place. The fleeting faces and impossible glyphs seen in the periphery are not hallucinations, but the visual echo of their cognitive processing—the swarm dreaming the cave into coherence, imposing order on geological chaos through the sheer density of their presence.

There is a hierarchy here, but it is not social; it is thermodynamic. Those closest to the weeping crystal veins absorb the most energy, becoming brighter, more fluid, more active. They act as the swarm’s metabolic engine, passing heat and nutrients inward through physical merging, a slow-motion cascade of cellular communion. The entities on the cooler, lower edges become denser, slower, more mineralized. They are the swarm’s skeleton, the semi-solid foundation upon which the hotter, faster members flow. Over eons, the outermost Amniotes will cease to move entirely, calcifying fully into new strata of living rock, while new generations bud from the warm, wet center.

They are waiting, but not passively. Their stillness is a form of intense, concentrated labor. Every pulse, every secretion, every silent merger is a rehearsal for a biology that has no precedent. They are practicing existence. In this hot, wet dark, beneath the crushing weight of a newborn world, the Lithic Amniotes are the question the earth is asking itself, a thousand soft, pale syllables spoken in the language of steam and slime, waiting for the catalyst that will turn their gestation into genesis.
