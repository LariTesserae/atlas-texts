They are the Silt-Walkers, though they have no name for themselves that can be articulated without the wet click of a tongue against a palate made of porous stone.

To call them inhabitants is to misunderstand their relationship to the bruise. They do not occupy this space; they are its temporary viscosity. They are tall, impossibly thin, their limbs elongated by generations of moving through air that resists like syrup. They possess no fixed silhouette. Their edges are soft, blurring into the ambient gloom as if they are perpetually being erased by the dampness, only to be redrawn by an act of collective will. Their skin is not flesh but a living membrane, a translucent dermis that mirrors the bioluminescence of the moss beneath their feet. When they stand still, they are indistinguishable from the fungal stalks; when they move, they are merely a darker current in the flow of the landscape.

They are blind, or rather, sight is a sense they have discarded as too aggressive for a world that refuses to be observed. Instead, they perceive through *resonance*. They feel the tectonic groan in the hollows of their collarbones; they navigate by the varying density of the silence. To walk through one of their shifting cities is to be perceived by them not as a shape, but as a dissonance, a jarring note in the low-frequency hum of their existence. They turn their heads toward you not to see, but to taste the disturbance your mass makes in the heavy air.

Their lives are an exercise in extreme gentleness. In a world where the ground itself is a gelatinous suspension of memory and rot, violence is physically impossible. You cannot strike what yields; you cannot break what has already forgotten its own integrity. Thus, their culture is one of guidance. They are the gardeners of entropy. With fingers that branch like fine rootlets, they stroke the walls of their breathing homes, soothing the architecture back into dormancy when it grows too feverish. They sing to the suspended spheres of water, low, vibrating thrums that encourage the liquid to remain aloft, maintaining the delicate hydrology that keeps the city from drowning in its own tears.

They do not speak to one another. Communication is osmotic. When two Silt-Walkers meet, they press their foreheads together and share a momentary fusion of fluids, a transfer of sensation and intent that leaves no residue of ego. Grief, joy, hunger, and exhaustion are communal properties, circulating through the population like nutrients through a mycelial network. There is no privacy here, because there is no individual boundary distinct enough to house a secret. To be alone is to be dead; to be alive is to be dissolved into the whole.

They eat the decay. Not out of necessity, but out of reverence. They consume the slurry of the eroded peaks and the spent spores of the false-light forests, incorporating the geological exhaustion into their own biology. In doing so, they become the archive of the bruise. A walker’s memories are not their own; they carry the sedimentary record of the mountain range that was ground down before they were born. To touch one is to touch the history of erosion, to feel the weight of eons pressing against the inside of your own skull.

They are dying, always. Birth and death are not events here but gradients. A child is not born; it precipitates from the humidity of a nursery-grotto, coalescing over decades until it achieves sufficient density to stand. An elder does not perish; they simply lose coherence, their form relaxing back into the moss, their consciousness diffusing into the architectural rhythm of the city. The plaza that shifted three meters last week did so because an ancestor finally let go, their body becoming the mortar for a new alignment.

They are the custodians of the unfinished sentence. They understand, with a patience that spans geological epochs, that the world is not broken, but merely resting in its ruin. They do not seek to heal the bruise, nor to accelerate the fade. They exist solely to witness the stasis, to be the conscious point within the amber, ensuring that even as the continent forgets its shape, the forgetting itself is felt, is honored, and is held in the terrible, beautiful embrace of those who have become the silence they walk upon.
