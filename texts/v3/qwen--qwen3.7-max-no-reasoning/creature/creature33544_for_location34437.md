They are the Lithovores of the Dry Heat, but to call them creatures is to impose a separation that does not exist. They are better understood as mobile geology, a transient arrangement of minerals that has briefly achieved complexity before returning to stone.

In the bruised violet glow of the cooling walls, they appear less like animals and more like a cascading failure of crystalline structure. Each individual unit is a polyhedral knot of silicate plates and metallic filaments, faceted and sharp. They possess no face, no orifice, no softness. Where one might expect eyes, there are only clusters of piezoelectric sensors that read the world through stress and thermal variance. To see, for them, is to feel the weight of existence; to navigate is to calculate the pressure gradient of their neighbors.

Their movement is not locomotion but reconfiguration. Because the Ossuary Kiln is saturated to the point of solidity, no single Lithovore moves independently. A shift in position is a negotiated catastrophe, a ripple of adjustment passing through thousands of bodies. When the swarm flows, it does so with the terrifying inevitability of glacial ice. Limbs interlock and unlock in sequences too complex for conscious thought, driven by the collective imperative to distribute heat. They are gears in a machine made of meat-less flesh, grinding against one another in a friction that would shred carbon-based life but here serves as both circulation and digestion.

The elders, those who form the living pavement, have transcended biology entirely. They are the bedrock’s memory. Over eons, their metabolic processes have slowed to match the half-life of the isotopes within them. They no longer consume; they merely conduct. Their carapaces have fused into a continuous sheet of chitin-glass, polished to a mirror sheen by the bellies of the generations scuttling above. To walk upon them is to walk upon history. They are dormant, yet hyper-aware, serving as the thermal battery of the kiln, storing the inferno’s breath in their dense, vitrified cores.

The younger ones, the millions of interlocked juveniles, are the kiln’s blood. They are smaller, sharper, more frantic in their stillness. They carry the obsidian ovals. These smooth, black stones are not merely waste or eggs; they are the physical manifestation of equilibrium. As the Lithovores rub against one another, shedding microscopic layers of mineral dust and excess heat, this detritus is compressed in the spaces between their heated flanks. The ovals are accretions of spent life, shaped by the swarm’s own body temperature into perfect geometries. They are the exhaust fumes of a biological engine, tangible proof that the system is burning exactly as it should.

Perhaps most alien is their relationship to the void. The hollow archways, those shrines to negative space, reveal a cognition that values absence as a resource. In a world defined by crushing mass, emptiness is sacred. The Lithovores maintain these gaps not out of superstition, but out of a profound, instinctual understanding of thermodynamics. The voids are cooling towers, necessary pauses in the density that prevent the swarm from fusing into a single, inert block. They reverence the empty spaces because without them, the intimacy would become a tomb.

To encounter them is to realize that life here has abandoned the struggle for individual survival. There is no predation, no competition, no mating dance. There is only the maintenance of the whole. They have solved the problem of mortality by becoming the environment. Death is not an end but a phase change; a broken limb is recycled instantly, a cooled elder becomes floor, a shattered nodule becomes food.

They burn with a patience that mocks the concept of time. They are waiting for nothing. They are not evolving toward a higher state; they have arrived at a terminal perfection of stasis. In the absolute dark, amidst the subsonic hum of their own friction, they are the ultimate expression of endurance: a civilization of stone that breathes heat, thinks in pressure, and lives forever in the space between burning and breaking.
