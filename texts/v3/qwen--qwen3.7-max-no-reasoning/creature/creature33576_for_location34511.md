The inhabitants of the Sump do not perceive themselves as residents of a ruin, nor as survivors of an apocalypse. To them, the concept of "ruin" implies a loss of function, but nothing here has lost its function; it has merely changed its master. They are the biota of a machine that has forgotten it was ever built, organisms for whom the industrial is not artificial, but elemental.

Foremost among them are the **Resonance Crabs**. They are not true crustaceans, though they occupy that ecological niche. Their exoskeletons are a mosaic of scavenged ceramics and ball-bearings, fused by biological secretions into carapaces that mimic the texture of the rust-coral walls. They do not see; the amber fungal glow is irrelevant to them. Instead, they navigate entirely through the tower’s vibration. The failing pistons provide a topographic map of sound and tremor; a seized gear feels like a mountain range, a burst pipe like a sudden valley. They are the gardeners of this iron reef, grazing on the bacterial films that coat the machinery, their clicking mandibles keeping the intake valves clear of necrotic sludge. In doing so, they maintain the very heartbeat that sustains them. They are maintenance crews evolved from bottom-feeders, performing their duties without mind or mandate, driven only by the hunger that aligns perfectly with the needs of the engine.

In the mid-depths, where the currents are strongest, drift the **Galvanic Eels**. Blind and pale as milk, they are living sensors. Their bodies generate weak electrical fields, not for hunting prey in the traditional sense, but for reading the conductivity of the water. They taste the chemical gradients of leaking coolant and oxidizing metal. When a piston slows or a seal fractures, the eels are the first to arrive, drawn to the electrochemical distress signal. They wrap around damaged components, their mucus secreting enzymes that accelerate corrosion in some places and precipitate mineral deposits in others. They are the immune system of the Sump, identifying mechanical trauma and responding with biological triage. Their movements are sinuous and deliberate, weaving through turbine blades with a grace that suggests the metal lattice was grown specifically to accommodate their passage.

Higher up, in the humid twilight band above the waterline, cling the **Velvet Polyps**. These are not animals, nor are they plants; they are a hybridized filter-feeder that exists at the interface of air and fluid. They carpet the underside of catwalks and the ceilings of service tunnels, pulsing in sync with the respiration of the tower. They feed on the aerosolized nutrients carried by the perpetual steam-rain, absorbing moisture and particulate matter directly from the atmosphere. But they also serve as acoustic dampeners. Their dense, fibrous mats absorb the harsher echoes of grinding metal, softening the cacophony into the womb-like hum that defines the Sump’s atmosphere. Without them, the noise would be lethal; with them, it becomes a lullaby. They are the lungs and the insulation of this world, breathing the mist and quieting the pain of the structure.

Scattered throughout the open water are the **Membrane Drifters**. Gelatinous and nearly transparent, they are the closest thing this ecosystem has to plankton, yet they possess a terrible patience. They do not swarm; they wait. Trailing filaments dozens of meters long, they catch the "nutrient snow"—the constant fallout of organic detritus and flaking rust—that descends from the upper levels. They are silent accumulators, storing energy in internal bladders that glow with a faint, rhythmic luminescence, mimicking the flickering of the deep generators. Other creatures mistake this light for power sources or mating signals, drawing near only to be enveloped. The Drifters are the recyclers of last resort, ensuring that no calorie, no molecule of dissolved iron, is lost to the abyss. They turn death back into potential, floating in the dark tea of the coolant like suspended thoughts.

And then there are the **Custodians**, though they have no name for themselves. They are bipedal, vaguely humanoid in silhouette, but their anatomy has been reshaped by three miles of pressure and centuries of adaptation. Their skin is thick, leathery, and covered in sensory bristles that detect pressure changes and thermal gradients. Their eyes are vestigial, sealed shut by evolution, but their inner ears are hypertrophied, capable of hearing the subsonic groans of the foundation. They do not build; they do not repair in any conscious sense. They *tend*. Moving along the submerged gantries with slow, fluid motions, they scrape away excessive growth, nudge debris into flow channels, and nestle against warm pipes to regulate their body temperature. They are indistinguishable from the fauna, yet they carry a ghost-memory of purpose. Perhaps they were once engineers, or perhaps they are simply another expression of the tower’s drive toward equilibrium. They sleep when the pumps rest and wake when the rhythm stutters. They are the consciousness of the Sump made flesh, unaware of their own history, knowing only that the machine must breathe, and that they are part of its breath.

None of these beings fear the dark, or the noise, or the crushing weight of the manufactured ocean. They do not long for sunlight or silence. They are perfectly adapted to a world that is broken, because in the Sump of the Last Gear, brokenness is the only stable state. They are the children of obsolescence, thriving in the warm, loud intimacy of a dying god’s body, complete in their dependence on the very decay that surrounds them. They are not surviving the end of the world; they are living, fully and richly, inside it.
