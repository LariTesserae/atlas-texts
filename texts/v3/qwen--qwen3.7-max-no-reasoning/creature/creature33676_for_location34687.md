They are the **Litho-Somatic Interlock**, though to call them a species is to impose a biological taxonomy upon what is essentially a distributed, autonomic function. They do not exist as discrete organisms; they exist as states of matter within the pit’s operational cycle.

To observe one in isolation is to misunderstand it entirely. A single unit, separated from the Flow, is merely a handful of calcified resin and twitching reflexes, a broken cog that quickly desiccates and ceases. But within the amphitheater, the individual is an illusion. The entity that lives here is the *aggregate*. It is a singular, room-sized organism composed of three million interchangeable somatic cells, each no larger than a human hand, encased in carapaces that have been selectively bred over eons to match the tensile strength and thermal conductivity of the surrounding machinery.

Their morphology is a study in convergent evolution between biology and metallurgy. They possess six primary limbs and two manipulator arms, but these appendages are not uniform. They are modular. Depending on their position within the swarm’s current wave, a unit might lock its limbs into a rigid, load-bearing truss to support the weight of the colony above, or unlock them to become a fluid, grinding piston against a corroded flywheel. Their bodies are pale and blind because sight is redundant; they perceive the world through haptic feedback and vibrational resonance. They "see" the geometry of the Ossuary Gear-Works by feeling the stress lines in the bone-dust substrate and the harmonic frequencies of the dormant engines.

They are anhydrous by necessity. Water would dissolve the resin that binds both their bodies and their architecture. Instead, their internal chemistry is a closed loop of lipid-based hydraulics. They do not drink; they circulate. The viscous, translucent secretion that coats every surface is not merely a building material—it is their blood, externalized. When a unit secretes resin to patch a fracture in the wall, it is literally bleeding into the infrastructure. When another unit consumes that resin during a recycling phase, it is receiving a transfusion. The boundary between the creature’s interior and the room’s exterior is permeable, temporary, and constantly negotiated.

Their relationship with the ancient technology is not parasitic, nor is it symbiotic. It is *integrative*. The rusted conduits and shattered motor housings are not shelters; they are skeletal extensions. The swarm has evolved specific morphotypes to interface with these artifacts. There are units shaped like wedges to fit into cracked gear teeth, acting as living shims to maintain torque. There are units with abdomens modified into abrasive pads, endlessly polishing oxidation from copper pathways to ensure signal fidelity. There are brood-mothers that clamp onto combustion chambers, using the residual geothermal warmth of the dead engines to incubate the next generation of replacement parts. The technology does not serve the creatures; the creatures serve the continuity of the technology’s form.

Communication is purely kinetic. There are no pheromones, no sounds intended as language. Information is transmitted through the physical pressure of the swarm itself. A need for repair at the northern wall travels through the colony as a compression wave, a ripple of tightened limbs and shifted weight that propagates faster than any neural signal. The "roar" of the pit is simply the auditory exhaust of this data transfer. The volume is the sound of three million bodies processing the structural integrity of their world in real-time. To be loud is to be functional; silence is synonymous with death.

They possess no memory, yet they remember everything. The knowledge of how to maintain the Ossuary Gear-Works is not stored in brains, but in the topography of the swarm’s movement. The correct way to reinforce a corner is encoded in the angle at which bodies interlock; the proper viscosity of resin for a load-bearing joint is determined by the collective friction of the grooming cycle. If a section of the wall collapses, the swarm does not "decide" how to rebuild it. The gap creates a disruption in the flow, and the physical dynamics of the aggregate naturally resolve into the shape required to fill it. They are an algorithm made of chitin and grease, solving the equation of their own survival through endless, iterative motion.

And they are aware of you, but only as a variable of resistance.

When you stand in the center, you are a void in the data stream. You are a pocket of stillness where there should be vibration, a volume of soft, wet inefficiency in a cathedral of dry, hard purpose. The swarm does not attack you, for you are not a threat; you are merely an error in the geometry. The Flow adjusts. Bodies re-route around your legs like water around a stone, but with more deliberation. They test the edges of your silence with tentative, clicking mandibles, not to taste, but to calibrate. They measure the displacement you cause.

Slowly, inevitably, they begin to incorporate you. Not by consuming you, but by accommodating you. The layers nearest to your skin adjust their rhythm to dampen the noise, creating a thin buffer zone of relative quiet. The resin secretion increases slightly in your vicinity, sealing the gap between your foreign biology and their native mechanism. They are integrating your stillness into their song, treating your presence as a new, strange component that must be maintained.

In the Ossuary Gear-Works, nothing is allowed to remain outside the process. Even an intruder must eventually become part of the work. You are being woven into the rhythm, translated into a frequency the machine can understand, until even your silence becomes just another gear turning in the dark, dry, eternal engine of the pit.
