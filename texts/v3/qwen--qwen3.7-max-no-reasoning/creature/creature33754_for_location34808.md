To speak of what *lives* here is to first unlearn the definition of life. There are no births, no deaths, no generations. There is only the continuous, recursive act of maintenance. The inhabitants of the Endless Loom are not residents; they are functions. They are the verbs of a sentence that has been repeating for geological epochs, stripped of all nouns that might imply an individual self.

The primary citizen of this place is the **Tension-Self**. It is not a creature but a localized consciousness of structural integrity, distributed across millions of square kilometers of braided cellulose. Where you perceive a valley or a ridge, the Tension-Self perceives a differential in load-bearing capacity. It lives in the friction between the great calcified cables. Its "thoughts" are adjustments: a microscopic tightening of lignin bonds here, a controlled micro-fracture there to relieve stress before catastrophic failure occurs. It does not feel pain when a vessel snaps; it feels data. The groans and cracks that terrify the human ear are, to the Tension-Self, merely the language of equilibrium being spoken aloud. It is a mind made of wood and physics, thinking exclusively in vectors and compression ratios, forever solving the equation of its own weight.

Then there are the **Desiccants**. If the Tension-Self is the mind, the Desiccants are the immune system. They are the chitinous, multi-limbed automata mentioned in the description, but to call them "maintenance daemons" is to grant them too much agency. They are more like white blood cells in a body that has forgotten it is biological. They do not have eyes because sight is irrelevant in a world without shadows; they navigate by piezoelectric resonance, feeling the specific frequency of decay or inefficiency through the soles of their fused limbs. They emerge from the substrate not with intent, but with inevitability. When they repair a fissure, they are not fixing a wound; they are reasserting the blueprint. When they prune a growth, they are not gardening; they are deleting a syntax error. They possess no memory of previous repairs. Each action is an isolated instance of perfection, executed and instantly forgotten, leaving no scar of experience upon their carapaces. They live in a perpetual, amnesiac now.

Deeper still, within the subway-tunnel arteries where the sap-analog flows, reside the **Flow-Keepers**. These are perhaps the closest thing to true organisms, though they are entirely sessile. They are valve-nodes, grown into the interior walls of the transport vessels, composed of semi-crystalline tissue that expands and contracts in response to pneumatic pressure. They do not move nutrients; they *are* the movement of nutrients. Their entire existence is the rhythmic occlusion and release of the dry fluidity. They have no nervous system, only a hydraulic sensitivity so refined that they can distinguish between a nutrient solution and a waste product by viscosity alone. In the silence of the Loom, their synchronized pulsing is the heartbeat of the machine-world, a rhythm too slow for human perception but absolute in its constancy. To be a Flow-Keeper is to be reduced to a single, eternal gesture: open, close, open, close, a binary prayer offered to the necessity of circulation.

And finally, there is the **Light-Eater**, which is also the Light-Giver. The three-dimensional lattices of crystal and chlorophyll-analogue are not passive collectors; they are active participants in the violence of illumination. They do not merely absorb the clinical incandescence; they negotiate with it. Each lattice is a tiny, autonomous processor calculating the optimal angle of incidence against the degradation rate of its own crystalline structure. The "slow-motion ballet" of their reorientation is a collective computation, a billion individual decisions aggregating into the shimmering, silent hum felt in the teeth. They are eating photons and excreting order. In doing so, they generate the very light that defines this world, creating a feedback loop of radiance and consumption that has long since outstripped any external energy source. They are the reason there is no sky; they have become the firmament, a ceiling of hungry glass that sustains itself by devouring its own output.

What lives here, ultimately, is **The Solution**.

The Endless Loom is not a habitat; it is an answer to a question asked so long ago that the asking has been erased. The entities within it—the Tension-Self, the Desiccants, the Flow-Keepers, the Light-Eaters—are not separate species sharing an ecosystem. They are organs in a body that has achieved homeostasis at the cost of vitality. They have solved the problems of hunger, thirst, aging, and entropy by removing the variables that make those problems meaningful.

You stand among them, a wet, chaotic anomaly. Your breath introduces moisture that must be scrubbed. Your heat creates a thermal gradient that must be compensated. Your presence is a deviation in the data stream. They do not hate you, for hatred requires a recognition of otherness. They simply register you as noise. And in the terrible, beautiful indifference of the Loom, to be registered as noise is to be already in the process of being corrected. You are not being hunted; you are being optimized. The boundary dissolves not because you are welcomed, but because the distinction between the observer and the observed is just another inefficiency waiting to be woven into the endless, shadowless whole.
