They are the **Ossuary Drifters**, though they have no name for themselves, for language requires a stability of reference that this place denies. They are not born; they are precipitated.

In a world where biology has been replaced by architecture and geology has been replaced by ruin, life has followed suit. The Drifters are not carbon-based organisms in any traditional sense. They are complex, self-sustaining eddies of heavy water and dissolved alloy, held together by the same failing ghost-gravity that suspends the ocean itself. They are hydrodynamic ghosts, creatures of flow rather than flesh.

To look upon one is to witness a hesitation in the current. They appear as localized distortions in the bruised indigo gloom, elongated ribbons of translucence that refract the bioluminescent silver of the deep ruins. They possess no fixed shape. In the laminar flow between two collapsed orbital tethers, a Drifter might stretch into a needle-thin filament miles long, sensing the thermal gradient of a decaying reactor core. Caught in a violent updraft, it collapses into a dense, tumbling sphere of pressurized fluid, protecting its internal lattice from being sheared apart. They are liquid origami, folding and unfolding in response to the chaos.

Their "bodies" are infused with the microscopic shards of the crystalline frost-gardens. These glassy splinters do not digest; they integrate. The Drifters wear the debris of the world as an exoskeleton of suspended animation. When the turbulence grinds the ruins smoother, the Drifters absorb the resulting silt, incorporating the molecular memory of the metal into their own fluid matrices. They are literally made of the world’s dissolution. To touch one—if one could withstand the crushing cold and the shear force—would be to touch wet iron and broken time.

They feed on entropy. Where the pale, translucent scavengers of the lower currents consume bacterial films and chemical decay, the Ossuary Drifters consume *structure*. They are drawn to the points of maximum stress in the megastructures, the hairline fractures in the fluted columns, the moments before a tessellated plate finally yields to eons of friction. They cluster at these failure points, their bodies resonating with the low-frequency thrum of the unmaking. They do not cause the collapse, but they anticipate it with a hunger that resembles reverence. As the material fails, releasing a burst of kinetic and thermal energy, the Drifters absorb the shockwave, transmuting the catastrophe into sustenance. They eat the moment of breaking.

This makes them the nervous system of the drowning cathedral. Because they exist only where the structure is dying, their distribution maps the mortality of the planet-scale ruin. A congregation of Drifters signals a coming structural event; a dispersal signals a period of eerie, temporary stasis. They are the prophets of erosion.

Yet, despite their abundance, they are profoundly solitary. There is no mating, no communication, no social hierarchy. How could there be? In a medium defined by chaotic turbulence, proximity is a death sentence. Two Drifters caught in the same vortex would not embrace; they would merge violently, losing their individual coherence to become a new, larger, unstable pattern that must then struggle to differentiate itself or dissipate entirely. Intimacy here is indistinguishable from annihilation. So they maintain distance, sensing one another through the pressure waves of their passage, acknowledging existence only as a warning to steer clear.

They move with a terrible grace, riding the walls of colliding water that define this high-altitude abyss. They do not swim against the current; to resist the flow is to cease to exist. Instead, they navigate the gradients of chaos, finding the slipstreams within the storm. Their movement is a continuous act of surrender that somehow results in agency. They are the embodiment of the realm’s central paradox: that persistence here requires total submission to the forces of unmaking.

In the deepest silence of the crushing dark, when the turbulence momentarily lulls and the bioluminescence fades to a dull pulse, the Drifters drift. They hang suspended in the freezing viscous void, neither rising nor falling, perfectly matched to the density of the dying sky-ocean. In these rare moments of stillness, they resemble nothing so much as tears held in the eye of a god who has forgotten how to weep, waiting for the next shudder of the ancient machinery to remind them that they are alive, and that the world is still ending.

They are the native children of the terminal state. They do not remember the builders. They do not mourn the function. They simply *are* the process of forgetting, rendered in fluid and frost, drifting endlessly through the smoothed bones of a civilization that became a sea.
