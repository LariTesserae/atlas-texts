The place has no name yet, because it has not finished becoming.

It is a landscape of violent genesis, suspended in a warm, suffocating twilight. There is no water here, nor any memory of it. The air is a thick, dry suspension of silicate dust and ionized gas, tasting of copper and ozone on the tongue. It is an atmosphere that does not circulate so much as *heave*, driven by thermal differentials so extreme they mimic the tides of an ocean that never existed.

The elevation is punishing. You stand upon a shelf of raw, uncooled geology, thousands of meters above a floor that cannot be seen, only sensed through the barometric pressure that presses against the eardrums like a heavy hand. This is not a mountain range carved by time; it is a mountain range being extruded by it. The ground beneath you is not solid in the way stone should be. It is viscous, warm to the touch, trembling with a subsonic frequency that travels up through the soles of boots and settles in the marrow.

Because the erosion parameter is near zero, there are no smooth lines. Nothing has been worn down. Every edge is a fresh fracture; every ridge is a jagged tear in the planetary crust. The terrain is a chaotic topography of cooling lava tubes, obsidian shards the size of houses, and fountains of mineral glass that erupt silently into the gloom, freezing in mid-arc before shattering under their own impossible weight. It is a geography of sharp angles and sudden drops, a world without the softening grace of wind or rain.

The light is failing, or perhaps it was never truly present. A bruised, amber luminescence bleeds from the fissures below, casting long, erratic shadows that seem to detach from their objects. In this semi-darkness, scale becomes treacherous. What looks like a boulder ten meters away might be a tectonic plate shifting three kilometers distant. The openness of the space is deceptive; the darkness acts as a visual viscosity, making the vastness feel claustrophobic, as if the void itself is crowding in.

There is no vegetation, but the land is not barren. It is infected with structure. Scattered across this turbulent expanse are geometric anomalies—monoliths of matte-black material that absorb the dim light rather than reflecting it. They are not built; they are *grown* from the chaos, or perhaps imposed upon it. They appear at irregular intervals, lonely and purposeless, resembling the ribs of some immense, buried machine or the seeds of a mathematics that hasn't learned to bloom. They hum with a resonance just below hearing, vibrating in sympathy with the tectonic agony around them. These are the only stable things in a world defined by instability.

The silence is profound, yet total quiet is an illusion. The soundscape is composed entirely of geological stress: the deep, grinding moan of lithosphere against lithosphere, the crystalline ping of cooling glass contracting, the dry hiss of particulate matter sliding over new rock. There is no biological sound. No breath, no footfall, no rustle. The turbulence here is purely physical, a hyperactive geology that creates and destroys formations in the span of hours. A spire rises, glowing cherry-red, dominates the horizon for a moment, and then collapses into a cascade of slag, only for the ground to buckle upward elsewhere in a spasmodic correction.

This is a world in its embryonic fury. It is warm not with the comfort of a sun, but with the friction of creation. It is sparse, yes, but the emptiness is pregnant with potential energy. To walk here is to trespass in a womb of stone, where the rules of permanence have not yet been written, and where the only law is the relentless, silent, turbulent imperative to *become*.

And as you watch, the darkness shifts. The amber glow from below pulses, synchronized with the humming monoliths. The ground ripples like a disturbed pond, though it is solid rock. You realize, with a slow-dawning unease, that the turbulence is not random. The chaos has a rhythm. The emerging landscape is not merely forming; it is *searching*. The scattered black structures are not artifacts. They are questions, posed in geometry, waiting for the molten earth to provide an answer.

The warmth increases. The air grows heavier. Somewhere in the unseen valley below, something vast and dry begins to unfold.
