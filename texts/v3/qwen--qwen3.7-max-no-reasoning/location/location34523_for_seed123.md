The terrain is young, violent, and unfinished, composed of pale, fractured limestone and dark, wet basalt that still smells of mineral heat and crushed silica.

Despite the arid appearance of the surrounding lowlands, this pocket holds water with a desperate, capillary intimacy. The moisture does not flow in rivers but sweats from the walls. Condensation beads on the cool underside of overhangs, gathering into heavy, trembling drops that fall in irregular rhythms onto the saturated floor below. There is intermittent rain, but it arrives as localized micro-bursts, warm mists that materialize within the depression itself rather than falling from the open sky above. The air is thick, not with humidity alone, but with a suspended density of matter. This is a packed environment. There is no empty space, no visual rest. The air itself seems colonized.

The vegetation is sparse in biomass but aggressive in presence. It does not carpet the ground; it erupts from the fissures in tight, knotted clusters of grey-green lichen and translucent, vein-like fungal threads that bridge gaps between rocks. These growths are not merely biological; they are structural, binding the loose scree together, acting as a living mortar for this new, unstable geology. They thrive in the damp warmth, their metabolism synchronized with the condensation cycles, expanding visibly when the mist rolls through and contracting into brittle dormancy during the brief, dry intervals.

These are not ruins of a factory, but artifacts of a process that was interrupted before it could begin, or perhaps after it had already consumed its purpose. The technology feels less like an imposition and more like an organ transplant rejected by the host landscape, now being slowly digested by the dense ecology. Small, boxy sensor-housings cling to the cliff faces like barnacles, their glass lenses clouded, their internal mechanisms clicking softly in the quiet.

To stand within it is to be inside a lung, breathing in time with a system that is neither entirely alive nor entirely dead, but something else: a mechanism of becoming, lubricated by sweat and silence, grinding forward in the dark.
