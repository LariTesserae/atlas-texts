The place is known as the Cistern-Groves of Aethelgard, though "grove" is a misnomer for what is essentially a suspended, liquid architecture. It exists at a high elevation, nestled within a colossal geological caldera that has long since ceased its volcanic violence and settled into a permanent, cool equilibrium. Here, the air itself is heavy with moisture, not merely humid but structurally saturated, creating an environment where the distinction between atmosphere and ocean has blurred into a singular, breathable medium.

**Terrain and Hydrology**
The terrain is vertical rather than horizontal. Because water saturation is absolute (3.0) and vegetation is near-total (2.9), there is no exposed soil, no bare rock, no dry ground. The "floor" of this world is a dense, woven mat of living roots and petrified wood, suspended hundreds of feet above the true basin floor by the tension of massive, cable-like vines. This root-mat acts as both land and filter. Above it, the space is filled with a perpetual, slow-motion deluge—a wall of rain that never ceases, yet falls with such viscosity and gentleness that it feels less like weather and more like a shifting, liquid wall. One does not walk through this landscape so much as wade through a warm-cool gel, the air resisting movement like shallow water. Pockets of true liquid collect in the hollows of giant branches, forming aerial lakes that spill over in silent, glassy sheets when they reach capacity, only to be caught by lower canopies and cycled upward again through capillary action and transpiration.

**Vegetation as Architecture**
The flora here has abandoned the struggle for sunlight in favor of structural integrity. The plants are pale, ranging from bleached bone-white to translucent jade, lacking chlorophyll in the traditional sense; they derive energy from the chemical richness of the endless rain and the geothermal warmth trapped in the caldera’s stones. They are vast, cathedral-scale organisms (Scale 1.1), their trunks wide as towers, fluted and buttressed to withstand the weight of the water they hold. But despite their immense size, they are sparse (Density 0.9). There is great distance between these titan-trees, vast corridors of open, wet air that allow for movement and sightlines. This sparseness is deliberate; if they grew closer, the weight of the accumulated water would collapse the canopy. The emptiness between the trees is as important as the trees themselves, a necessary void that gives the grove its breath.

This vegetation *is* the city. The built environment (Built 2.3) is not imposed upon the forest but cultivated from it. Dwellings are not constructed but coaxed: chambers hollowed out of living boughs over generations, sealed with secretions of resin that harden into amber-glass windows. Walkways are braided from living tendrils, guided by hand into railings and steps that tighten their grip when stepped upon. Bridges span the gaps between titan-trees, woven from fiber that heals itself when frayed. There is no metal, no mortar, no nail. Every surface is grown, shaped by simple tools of obsidian and bone, polished by the constant friction of water and skin. The urban layout follows the organic logic of the grove, clustering around nodes of fresh water collection and dispersing along the natural grain of the wood.

**Atmosphere and Sensation**
The temperature is a persistent, enveloping cool (1.3)—never cold enough to shiver, never warm enough to sweat. It is the temperature of deep well water, of stone cellars, of blood cooling on skin. This coolness slows everything. Metabolisms run low; decay is patient; growth is measured in decades. The air tastes mineral and green, carrying the scent of wet bark, ozone, and something faintly sweet like fermenting nectar. Visibility is limited not by darkness but by the density of the medium; light (1.7) diffuses through the watery air in soft, pearlescent gradients, eliminating harsh shadows and making distances difficult to judge. Everything glows with a borrowed luminescence, the bioluminescent fungi embedded in the wood casting a gentle radiance that pulses in time with the water cycle.

**Sound and Fauna**
Despite the slow dynamic (0.8), the soundscape is overwhelmingly loud (2.3). It is not the chaotic noise of a storm, but a layered, harmonic roar. The primary voice is the water: the deep thrum of millions of gallons moving through root systems, the crystalline chiming of droplets striking resonant seed-pods, the subsonic groan of wood flexing under hydraulic pressure. Beneath this is the biological chorus. Fauna is abundant (2.3) and adapted to the medium. Creatures here do not fly or walk; they drift and glide. Translucent, ray-like beings with trailing filaments navigate the air-currents, feeding on airborne plankton. Arboreal cephalopods with six limbs move silently through the canopy, their skin matching the wet wood perfectly. Vocalizations are low-frequency and carry for miles through the dense air—booming calls that vibrate in the chest cavity, used for navigation and mating across the vast gaps between trees. The volume is constant, a white noise so total it becomes silence to those born within it. Outsiders find it deafening; inhabitants find its absence terrifying.

**Rules and Rhythm**
Time here is hydrological, not solar. Days are marked not by light but by the tidal pulse of the cistern system: the High Flow when the upper reservoirs release, filling the air with mist and sound; the Low Ebb when the water retreats into the deep roots, leaving the grove momentarily still and relatively clear. Social rhythms follow this tide. Work is done during the Ebb; rest, ritual, and communal gathering occur during the Flow. Technology remains primitive (Tech 1.0) not from lack of intelligence but from philosophical alignment. Complex machines would corrode, jam, or disrupt the delicate hydraulic balance. Instead, knowledge is encoded in song and knot-work, passed down through oral tradition and tactile memory. Tools are extensions of the body, crafted for specific tasks and returned to the grove when worn out, composted back into the system.

**Weirdness and Balance**
The weirdness (0.9) is subtle, pervasive, accepted. It is strange to breathe liquid air, to live inside a tree that drinks you back, to measure your lifespan in the growth rings of your home. But strangeness implies deviation from norm, and here, this *is* the norm. The ecosystem is ancient, established (Erosion 1.9), having found its balance millennia ago. Nothing is new; nothing is decaying faster than it regenerates. The city will eventually be consumed by the very trees that house it, and this is understood, even welcomed. Buildings are designed to fail gracefully, their collapse seeding new growth. Death is not an end but a redistribution.

To stand in the Cistern-Groves is to be held. The cool, wet air presses against you like a second skin. The roar of water fills your bones. The light is soft, the space between presences vast and sacred. You are small, temporary, and entirely necessary—a brief pattern in the endless, patient cycling of water and wood. The place does not welcome you; it simply includes you, as it includes the rain, the root, the drifting ray-creature, the amber window glowing in the gloom. It is complete without you, and because of that completeness, you are free to belong.
