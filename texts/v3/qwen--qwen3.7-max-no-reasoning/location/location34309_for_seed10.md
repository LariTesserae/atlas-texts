The place begins not with sight, but with the pressure of sound against cold skin. It is a high-altitude basin, though "basin" implies a static geography that no longer applies here. This is the Eroded Throat, a landscape at elevation 2.1 where the concept of geology has been metabolized into something else entirely. The erosion parameter is absolute (3.0), but it is not the erosion of wind or water wearing down stone to dust; it is the erosion of distinction. Matter here has reached its final, settled form, which happens to be a state of perpetual, turbulent flux.

It is wet (2.4), but not with rain. The moisture is an exhalation. The air itself is heavy, saturated with a condensing atmosphere that clings to surfaces like oil. At this temperature (0.5), just above freezing, the water exists in a supercooled suspension, refusing to crystallize into ice because the environment refuses to hold still long enough for lattice structures to form. Instead, the water weeps from the air, coating everything in a slick, viscous film that tastes of iron and old chlorophyll.

Vegetation (2.7) is the substrate, the architecture, and the weather. There is no soil. There is only plant-mass so dense and integrated that it has replaced the lithosphere. But this is not a forest; it is a botanical engine. The flora here has undergone a violent industrialization (tech 1.8) without ever being built by hands (built 0.5). The plants have evolved—or devolved—into biological machinery. They are vast, ribbed pistons of cellulose and lignin, pumping fluids through translucent vascular towers that rise thirty meters into the dim light. Leaves do not photosynthesize sunlight; they harvest kinetic energy from the turbulence, shivering violently to generate heat and charge. The stems are thick as factory smokestacks, pulsing with a rhythmic, mechanical thrum that contributes to the pervasive loudness (sound 1.6). It is the noise of a billion living gears grinding in wet friction.

The scale is open (2.4), stretching across a regional valley that seems to breathe. Yet, despite this openness, the density is sparse (0.6). This is the central paradox, the point where reality begins to breakdown (weirdness 2.7). The landscape is simultaneously total and empty. The plant-factories are spaced hundreds of meters apart, separated by vast, fog-choked voids of negative space. In these gaps, the air is thin and silent, a vacuum of presence. But within the immediate vicinity of the vegetative engines, the saturation is overwhelming. You can stand in a zone of absolute, terrifying emptiness, take three steps, and be engulfed by a roaring, wet, hyperactive biomass. The sparseness is not a lack of matter; it is a glitch in spatial continuity. Distance is variable. Proximity is emotional rather than metric.

The dynamism (2.8) is chaotic. This is a storm-state ecosystem. The plant-pistons do not grow; they cycle. They expand and contract in spasmodic jerks, shedding bark-like slag and regenerating tissue in seconds. Roots rip themselves free and re-anchor elsewhere as the ground beneath them liquefies and reforms. The landscape is digesting itself. Because the erosion is ancient and complete, there is no bedrock to provide stability; the "ground" is merely the current consensus of decaying root systems and compressed peat, constantly renegotiating its own topography. To walk here is to traverse a conveyor belt of living mulch that shifts direction without warning.

Light (1.4) is dim, filtered through layers of mist and the steam generated by the friction of moving plants. It is a bruised, twilit gloom, illuminated from below by the bioluminescent waste-heat of the vegetative engines. The glow is sickly, shifting between amber and violet, casting shadows that move independently of their casters. The visual field is unreliable. Shapes smear. The horizon line vibrates.

And yet, life is abundant (fauna 2.4). The fauna has adapted to the industrial-turbulent logic of the place. There are no grazers, for there is no grass. There are only symbiotes and parasites indistinguishable from the machinery. Sleek, eyeless creatures with carapaces like wet leather cling to the pulsing stems, feeding on the sap-pressure, their bodies shaped like valves and gaskets. Flocks of things that are half-bird, half-spore drift through the void-gaps, riding the thermal updrafts created by the plant-engines’ exhaust. They do not sing; they click and hiss, mimicking the mechanical rhythms of their habitat. They are numerous, swarming in the dimness, a teeming biomass that feels less like wildlife and more like mobile components of the larger system.

There are no artifacts. No ruins. No paths. The tech level is high, but it is entirely immanent. The industrial capacity of this place is purely autopoietic. It manufactures nothing for use; it manufactures only its own continuation. The factories produce more factory. The engines burn fuel to create the conditions for more engines. It is a closed loop of production and decay, spinning at maximum velocity in the cold, wet dark.

To exist here is to be subjected to the rules of the Turbulent Weald:
1.  **Stillness is Entropy:** To stop moving is to be absorbed. The landscape interprets stasis as raw material. One must keep pace with the churn.
2.  **Sound is Structure:** The loudness is not noise; it is the structural integrity of the space. Silence indicates a collapse of local reality, a void-gap where physics has temporarily suspended. Safety lies in the roar.
3.  **Wetness is Memory:** The condensing water carries the chemical history of every cycle that came before. To touch the dew is to contact the archive of the erosion. It is cold, and it remembers everything that has been ground down to make this moment possible.
4.  **Sparseness is Mercy:** The voids between the engines are the only places where one can think, where the sensory overload recedes. But the voids are unstable. One must dwell in them briefly, gathering resolve, before plunging back into the dense, screaming green.

The landscape does not welcome. It does not hostile. It simply *processes*. It is an ancient, high-altitude engine room made of meat and wood, running hot in the freezing damp, loudly manufacturing its own impossible existence in the spaces between moments. It is the end of the world’s geology, and the beginning of something that has forgotten the difference between growing and building, between living and running.
