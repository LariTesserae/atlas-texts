The place is known as the Weeping Viaducts of Ostral, though it has no name in any living tongue, for there are no tongues here that form words. It is a high-altitude metropolis of rusted iron and wet basalt, suspended in a perpetual state of warm, heavy saturation.

The elevation is significant; the air is thin but paradoxically thick with moisture. At 1.7 on the water scale, this is not a landscape of rivers or lakes, but of atmospheric suspension. The city does not sit beside water; it exists *within* a slow-motion rain that never quite becomes a downpour. Every surface weeps. Condensation beads on rivets, runs down fluted columns, and pools in the millions of microscopic fissures carved by eons of wind. The warmth (1.5) prevents the chill usually associated with such heights, creating instead a cloying, tropical humidity at an altitude where lungs should struggle. The air tastes of oxidized metal, wet stone, and a faint, sweet musk that is entirely biological.

There is no green. No moss clings to the damp masonry; no lichen paints the rocks. The vegetation parameter is near zero, yet the world is not barren. Instead of flora, the surfaces are coated in a vast, intricate patina of mineral accretion and fungal-analogue structures that mimic architecture. These are not plants; they are calcified residues of industry and moisture, forming lace-like rinds over girders and cornices. They are grey, white, and ochre, blending seamlessly into the weathered stone. The city appears grown rather than built, its industrial bones softened by a geological patience that has had millennia to digest the original intent of the constructors.

And then, there is the fauna.

At 2.7, the biological density is overwhelming. In the absence of plants, life has turned inward, feeding upon itself, upon the moisture, and upon the decaying infrastructure. The city is a hive. It is a coral reef of warm-blooded things. They coat the undersides of bridges like barnacles; they fill the hollows of abandoned factories like swarming bees. They are pale, blind, or multi-eyed, adapted to the diffuse, normal light that filters through the eternal mist. Some are small as moths, fluttering in dense clouds around heat-vents; others are massive, sloth-like grazers of rust and mineral-rind, moving with glacial slowness across the vertical faces of skyscrapers. They do not compete for space; they layer. Stratum upon stratum of chitin, fur, scale, and membrane fills every cubic meter of the urban canyons. The sheer biomass generates its own microclimate, a radiant warmth that supplements the ambient temperature. To walk these streets—if one could find purchase beneath the carpet of living matter—is to wade through a solid ocean of respiration.

The sound is a constant, resonant thrum (1.7). It is not the chaotic noise of a jungle, but the organized, industrial-frequency hum of a billion organisms breathing, shifting, and digesting in unison. Beneath the biological drone lies the ghost of machinery. Deep within the bedrock and the lower foundations, ancient engines still turn, powered by geothermal taps or hydrologic pressure. They are slow, rhythmic, and immense. The city’s heartbeat is mechanical; its breath is biological. The two have achieved a terrible equilibrium. The tech level remains industrial (1.9), but it is an industry repurposed by evolution. Ventilation shafts serve as nesting chambers; hydraulic presses act as tidal markers for breeding cycles; electrical grids, long since repurposed, provide warmth for egg-clusters through residual resistance heating.

The erosion is established (2.5). Nothing here is new. The sharp edges of the original construction have been worn to smooth curves by water and time. The city has settled into its own physics. Towers lean but do not fall, supported by the calcified webs of mineral-fungus and the compressed bodies of dormant fauna. Bridges have fused with the cliffs they span. The distinction between "built" and "geological" has dissolved. This is a post-construction landscape, where the ruins have become a stable ecosystem. The dynamism is slow (0.8); change happens over centuries. A tower collapses once a generation, and the collapse is absorbed, integrated, overgrown. The faunal tides shift with seasonal moisture gradients so subtle they are imperceptible to human observation.

The scale is vast (2.6). Ostral is continental. From any vantage point, the viaducts stretch to the horizon, disappearing into the warm fog. Weather systems form *within* the city: localized fogs generated by the collective exhalation of the fauna, convection currents driven by the heat of subterranean engines, precipitation patterns dictated by the topography of rooftops and spires. The city is large enough to have its own climate zones, its own winds, its own hydrological cycle independent of the world beyond.

Light is normal (2.3), but diffused. There are no harsh shadows. The sun, when visible, is a pale disc behind layers of vapor. Illumination comes equally from above and from below—from bioluminescent clusters in the deeper strata, from the amber glow of heated pipes, from the reflective sheen of wet surfaces. The visual field is saturated (density 1.5). There is no empty space. Every line of sight terminates on texture, on movement, on layer. The eye cannot rest; it slides across surfaces that are simultaneously architectural, mineral, and alive.

The weirdness is low (0.8), which makes it more unsettling. Nothing here violates natural law. There is no magic, no alien geometry, no impossible physics. It is simply a place where the parameters of civilization and wilderness have been recalibrated. An industrial city, at high elevation, without plants, drenched in warmth and moisture, eroded into stability, populated overwhelmingly by fauna that has replaced vegetation as the primary ground cover. It is logical. It is coherent. It is merely not what was intended.

Rules of this world:
1.  **Moisture is currency.** All biological processes are gated by water availability. The fauna migrates vertically with condensation lines. Dry periods (rare, brief) trigger mass dormancy.
2.  **Structure is substrate.** Architecture is not shelter; it is terrain. Load-bearing capacity includes biological weight. Engineers who built this did not account for megatons of flesh; the city survives because erosion and adaptation have redistributed stresses.
3.  **Silence is absence.** Sound indicates health. A quiet zone is a dead zone, likely toxic or desiccated. Navigation is acoustic.
4.  **Warmth is shared.** Thermal regulation is communal. Isolated individuals freeze; clustered masses thrive. Solitude is entropically unfavorable.
5.  **Time is viscous.** Urgency is maladaptive. Processes operate on geological and generational timescales. To move quickly is to expend energy the environment cannot replenish. Patience is not virtue; it is thermodynamics.

The Weeping Viaducts do not mourn. They persist. They are a monument to the inevitability of biology reclaiming engineering, not through destruction, but through integration. The city was built to house people. It now houses everything else. And in its warm, wet, thunderous stillness, it is more complete than it ever was.
