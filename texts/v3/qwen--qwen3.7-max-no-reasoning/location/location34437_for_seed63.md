The place is known, in the few records that mention it, as the Ossuary Kiln. It is a landscape defined not by space, but by mass; a cathedral-sized geode buried deep within the upper crust of a tectonically active world, where the air is too thin to breathe and the heat is sufficient to soften lead.

There is no water here. There has never been water here. The chemistry is anhydrous silicate and metallic vapor. The darkness is absolute, save for the dull, sullen incandescence of the walls themselves, which glow with the color of cooling iron. This is the only light: a thermal luminescence that reveals texture rather than form, painting the world in gradients of infrared and bruised violet.

The scale is intimate, almost claustrophobic. You could walk from one end to the other in three minutes, yet the density of existence makes the traverse impossible. The chamber is packed solid, not with soil or stone, but with *life*.

This is a fauna without biology as we recognize it. They are the Lithovores of the Dry Heat—creatures of articulated mineral plates and piezoelectric musculature, evolved to thrive at temperatures that would sterilize carbon. They do not grow; they accrete. They are born as small, dense nodules shed from the cavern ceiling, and they mature by fusing with the debris of their ancestors. There is no vegetation, no fungal mat, no primary producer. The ecosystem is entirely closed-loop, fueled by the geothermal gradient and the slow decay of radioactive isotopes embedded in the bedrock. Energy passes from stone to creature to creature in a silent, grinding chain.

Because the space is saturated, there is no ground. To enter the Ossuary Kiln is to step onto a shifting, living pavement. The floor is a mosaic of dormant elders, creatures so old and heavy they have ceased movement, their carapaces fused into a continuous tectonic plate of chitin-glass. Upon this living crust scuttle the younger generations: millions of them, interlocked like gears in a watch mechanism. They are packed so tightly that individual boundaries are suggestions rather than facts. A limb belongs to three bodies; a sensory cluster serves a dozen minds. They move in a viscous, collective fluidity, a dry ocean of clicking joints and sliding plates.

The sound is deceptive. Parameter 0.9 suggests quiet, and to the human ear, it would be. There are no roars, no calls, no wind. But the silence is heavy with micro-vibration. It is the sound of friction on a geological scale, the subsonic hum of a million crystalline limbs adjusting to thermal expansion. It is felt in the teeth and the marrow before it is heard. The dynamic activity is relentless despite the stillness; the swarm is constantly reconfiguring, cycling nutrients through physical contact, transferring heat from the hotter lower strata to the cooler vents above. It is a biological convection current.

Scattered through this crushing density of life are the artifacts. Tech level 0.4 means no machines, but the built parameter (1.0) manifests as singular, enigmatic interruptions. Embedded in the wall of living creatures, or sometimes cradled gently between their heated flanks, are smooth, ovoid stones of polished obsidian. They are not tools. They appear to be waste products, or perhaps excreted memories, shaped by the swarm’s collective body heat over millennia into perfect, useless geometries. Occasionally, a fissure in the living wall reveals a structure: a hollow archway formed not by masonry, but by the deliberate absence of creatures, a negative space maintained by instinctual taboo. These archways lead nowhere; they are shrines to the void, pockets of cool emptiness revered by the heat-dwellers.

Erosion here is biological. The landscape does not weather from wind or rain; it wears down from the inside. The constant friction of the swarm polishes the cavern walls into mirrors. The sharp edges of the volcanic glass are smoothed by eons of abdominal rubbing. The environment is in a state of established equilibrium, but it is an equilibrium of exhaustion. The heat is high (2.6), but stable. The creatures have reached the maximum carrying capacity of their thermal niche. Nothing can be added; nothing can be removed.

The weirdness lies in the inversion of expectation. We associate desolation with emptiness, and heat with barrenness. Here, the aridity and the inferno have birthed a saturation so complete it resembles a solid. Life has not conquered this hostile pocket; it has become indistinguishable from it. The fauna *is* the terrain. The darkness is not an absence of light, but a presence of insulation.

If you were to stand in the center of the Ossuary Kiln, you would be crushed not by pressure, but by intimacy. You would be engulfed by a dry, hot tide of clicking geometry, a blindness of touch and vibration. You would realize that the "landscape" is merely the skin of a single, vast, distributed organism, and that the scattered obsidian ovals are not ruins, but eggs waiting for a temperature drop that may never come. The world is active, packed, and terribly, silently alive, holding its breath in the dark, burning with a patience that outlasts stars.
