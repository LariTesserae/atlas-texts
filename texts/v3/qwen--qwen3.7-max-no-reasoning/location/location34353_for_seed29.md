The place is a throat of geology, a singular, vertical fissure buried miles beneath a planetary crust that has long since forgotten the sun. It is not a cave in the traditional sense; it is a wound that never healed, now packed so tightly with matter and motion that the air itself feels solid.

Here, at this crushing elevation deep within the lithosphere, the heat is a physical weight. The temperature hovers near the threshold where rock begins to sweat its volatile minerals, a dry, suffocating warmth that tastes of copper and ozone. There is no water. The desiccation is absolute. Instead of moisture, the atmosphere is saturated with superheated silicate dust and ionized gas, a haze so dense it behaves like a viscous fluid. You do not breathe this air; you wade through it.

The scale is intimate, almost claustrophobic. This is a pocket-world, a single geological feature no wider than a cathedral nave but stretching down into an abyssal dark. Yet, within this confined volume, the density is absolute. There is no empty space. Every cubic meter is occupied by the machinery of the earth’s own metabolism.

This is the domain of the Lithovoric Engines.

They are not built; they are *grown* from the planet’s industrial adolescence, or perhaps they are the fossilized remains of a technology that predates biology, now indistinguishable from the bedrock. Massive, articulated pistons of basalt and iron-nickel alloy fill the fissure from wall to wall. They are packed shoulder-to-shoulder, a forest of blind, grinding columns. There are no factories here, no assembly lines, only the raw, unrefined architecture of tectonic processing. These machines do not manufacture goods; they manufacture *pressure*. They are the planet’s peristalsis, slowly digesting stone, transmuting gravity into heat.

The darkness is total, save for the dull, angry cherry-red glow of friction points where metal shears against mineral. It is a lightless luminescence, visible only as thermal radiation. In this gloom, the fauna thrives.

They are not animals of flesh and blood, for there is no water to sustain such chemistry. They are creatures of piezoelectricity and silicate lattice, abundant and restless. They resemble crabs forged from slag and wire, or perhaps beetles made of cooling lava. They swarm over the hot machinery in the millions, their carapaces clicking against the iron pistons. They feed on the kinetic energy of the grinding stones, absorbing the vibrations directly through their legs. They are the lubricants of this system, their bodies breaking down into conductive grease when they die, instantly replaced by new hatchlings emerging from the porous rock walls. The ecosystem is a closed loop of motion and decay, entirely devoid of photosynthesis or respiration.

The sound is the defining characteristic of the Throat. It is a deafening, continuous roar, a subsonic thrum that bypasses the ears and rattles the marrow. It is the noise of a world being compressed. Overlaid upon the bass is the high-frequency shriek of the swarm, a billion ceramic limbs skittering across hot metal, sounding like rain on a tin roof amplified to the volume of a jet engine. There is no silence here. Silence would mean death; silence would mean the engines have stopped and the fissure has collapsed.

Despite this violence, the change is agonizingly slow. The landscape is embryonic, still forming. The pistons move with glacial patience, completing a single cycle over decades. The swarm lives and dies in rapid bursts, but the geometry of the Throat shifts only across geological epochs. It is a place of immense energy trapped in a state of near-stasis, a high-tension spring winding tighter over millions of years.

There is no erosion here, for nothing is worn away; everything is merely rearranged. The walls are raw, jagged, untouched by wind or rain. They bear the scars of compression, not abrasion. The ground is a compacted layer of metallic droppings and shattered gear-teeth, fused together by heat into a floor that rings like a bell underfoot.

It is a normal place, in its own terrible logic. There is no magic, no eldritch horror, no defiance of physics. It is simply a machine that has been running too long, in the dark, without oversight. It is the hidden, beating heart of a dead world’s industry, a pocket of infinite density where the earth continues to work, loud and hot and dry, long after anyone was left to listen.
