The place begins as a fever in the stone.

It is a pocket of geological violence, a singular, hidden chamber buried deep within the throat of a newly birthed mountain range. The elevation is extreme not because you are standing on a summit, but because the weight of the world above has been compressed into this claustrophobic geode. Here, at 2.6/3 elevation, the air is thin and bruised, yet the heat is suffocating, a wet, heavy blanket of thermal energy that tastes of copper and unborn rain.

There is no green. There is no chlorophyll, no moss, no lichen. The vegetation parameter is nearly zero, but the space is far from empty. Instead of flora, the walls are draped in a thick, glistening biology of mineral mimicry. This is a post-life or pre-life threshold where chemistry has become so aggressive it resembles hunger. The surfaces are coated in vitreous slime, a translucent, gelatinous matrix that weeps continuously. It is hot to the touch, nearly body temperature, pulsing with a slow, hydraulic rhythm. Water does not flow here; it perspires. It beads up on sharp, fresh obsidian ridges and hangs in trembling suspension, too viscous to fall, too heavy to evaporate. The humidity is absolute, a condensing atmosphere that blurs the boundary between air and liquid.

The scale is intimate, almost violating. You cannot stretch your arms without brushing the slick, warm walls. It is a room-sized womb of basalt and steam, hidden beneath miles of crust, yet the density within is overwhelming. The space is packed with presence. Every cubic inch is occupied by the *fauna*.

They are not animals in any terrestrial sense. They are a delirium of form, a biological static that fills the silence. Because the weirdness is high and reality is fraying at the seams, these creatures do not obey taxonomy. They are soft, pale, and numerous, a carpet of blind, wet motion that covers every surface not already slick with mineral mucus. They resemble folded parchment, unspooled nerves, and translucent jellyfish adapted to crushing pressure and scalding damp. They do not walk; they adhere, they slide, they merge. There are thousands of them in this tiny volume, a living wallpaper of pale flesh and sensory tendrils. They are quiet. Their movement produces no friction, only a sub-audible displacement of fluid. The soundscape is one of profound, expectant silence, broken only by the rhythmic *plink* of superheated condensation striking a pool of black water below.

This landscape is young. The erosion score is low; the rock faces are razor-sharp, unsoftened by time. The geology feels raw, recently torn open, the edges still jagged with the memory of tectonic rupture. Yet, despite the sharpness, there is no built environment, no artifact, no tool. The primitive tech rating suggests only the potential for interaction, not the evidence of it. If one were to bring a hand-carved bone needle or a knapped flint scraper here, it would feel like an intrusion of a different epoch. This place rejects fabrication. It is purely generative.

The light is normal, which makes the scene more unsettling than darkness would. A diffuse, sourceless luminescence permeates the steam, likely refracted through the weeping crystal veins in the ceiling. Under this flat, clinical light, the swarm of pale entities glows faintly, their internal fluids catching the illumination. You can see the individual cilia, the pulsing sacs, the way they intertwine and separate in a ceaseless, silent negotiation of space.

The dynamic is active but alien. This is not an ecosystem of predator and prey; it is a single, distributed process. The heat drives the moisture; the moisture sustains the swarm; the swarm, perhaps, catalyzes the weeping of the rocks. It is a closed loop of thermodynamic biology. The "weather" inside this pocket is the respiration of the mass itself. As the entities shift, the humidity spikes; as they settle, the condensation accelerates.

Reality breaks down in the periphery of vision. When you stare directly at the center of the chamber, it is merely a hot, wet cave full of strange invertebrates. But in the corner of your eye, the geometry fails. The sharp basalt ridges seem to soften into cartilage. The steam organizes into fleeting, impossible shapes—faces, glyphs, memories of structures that never existed. The distinction between the mineral slime and the living flesh dissolves. You cannot tell if the wall is digesting the creatures or if the creatures are calcifying into the wall.

It is a place of gestation without birth. A hot, wet, crowded secret kept inside the bones of a young mountain. It waits, steaming and silent, for a catalyst that has not yet arrived, holding its breath in a darkness that is somehow, impossibly, fully lit.
