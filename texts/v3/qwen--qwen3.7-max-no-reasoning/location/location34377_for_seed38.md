The place begins not as a vista, but as a texture against the skin: a warm, suspended breath.

You are standing in the **Silt-Canopy of the Drowned Peneplain**. It is a landscape that has forgotten the concept of "ground." Here, at this immense altitude, the world has been eroded over eons into a near-flat, continental expanse, but it has not become barren. Instead, the erosion was so absolute, so patient, that the distinction between soil and root dissolved millions of years ago. The earth is gone; there is only the biomass, a planetary-scale accumulation of living matter that has become its own geology.

Because the elevation is high but the terrain is ancient and flattened, there are no peaks to pierce the clouds. Instead, the cloud layer *is* the ceiling of the forest, and the forest *is* the floor of the sky. The light is perpetually dim, filtered through miles of translucent, gelatinous foliage above. It is a bioluminescent twilight, where the only illumination comes from the slow metabolic glow of the plants themselves—a shifting, bruised purple and sickly amber luminescence that pulses with the respiration of the continent.

The vegetation parameter of 2.7/3 manifests here as total saturation. There is no empty space. The air itself feels thickened by spores, pollen, and drifting vegetative detritus. You do not walk on dirt; you walk on a sponge-mat of mosses, liverworts, and fungal networks hundreds of meters deep, resting atop the bedrock like a wet quilt. Trees here are not distinct entities rising from soil; they are merely vertical extrusions of the substrate, their bark indistinguishable from the epiphytes that sheathe them, their roots fused into the communal mat below. They are architectural pillars holding up the weight of the water cycle.

Water (2.1/3) is everywhere, yet nowhere still. It does not flow in rivers because the land lacks the gradient for speed. Instead, water exists as a pervasive, weeping presence. Every surface glistens. Condensation forms instantly on your skin, on the leaves, on the air itself. The atmosphere is so saturated that breathing feels like drinking. Water moves vertically, wicking upward through capillary action in the plant-mass, transpiring into the fog bank above, and raining back down in a silent, endless loop. There are marsh-pockets where the silt-mat dips, creating dark, tepid pools that reflect nothing but the glowing canopy, but even these are choked with life.

The warmth (2.0/3) is not solar; it is biological. This vast ecosystem generates its own heat through decomposition and respiration. It is a humid, womb-like temperature, constant and cloying. There is no wind, for the density of the vegetation breaks all airflow before it can begin. The weather of this place is internal: micro-climates shifting based on which sector of the forest is currently blooming or rotting.

Sound (1.1/3) is an exercise in absorption. The sheer density of the damp vegetation acts as an infinite acoustic baffle. There is no echo, no distance. Sounds are born and die within arm's reach. You hear the wet tear of a fiber splitting, the viscous slide of sap, the soft exhalation of a pore opening. The abundant fauna (1.7/3) is largely unseen and unheard, adapted to this muffled realm. They are soft-bodied things—slugs the size of forearms, pale moths with wings like wet tissue, amphibians that have lost their legs to become serpentine swimmers through the moss. They do not call out; they signal through vibration and chemical trails. To be loud here would be to be alien.

The built environment (1.3/3) appears only as ghostly interruptions in the green. These are not ruins of a civilization, but calcified memories of one. Occasional shapes emerge from the living wall: smooth, geometric curves of white stone or ceramic, completely enveloped by centuries of growth. A perfect archway leading nowhere; a basin now serving as a nursery for ferns; a fluted column that has been digested halfway by lichen. They are scattered, singular, and utterly silent. They possess no function anymore, serving only as hard anchors in a soft world, strange minerals in a biological ocean. Technology (0.1/3) is nonexistent; even these remnants are pre-tool, perhaps shaped by biology itself or by beings who did not distinguish between making and growing.

The weirdness (2.0/3) lies in the scale of patience. This landscape is in its final form. It has reached equilibrium. Nothing is rushing toward a climax; nothing is recovering from a disaster. It simply *is*, in a state of eternal, slow-motion maintenance. The dynamic (1.2/3) is glacial despite the warmth. A tree falling takes decades to reach the mat; its decay takes centuries. Your movement through it feels violently fast, a disruption of a stillness so profound it feels like pressure.

To exist here is to be submerged in a living archive. The air tastes of old rain and sweet rot. The dim light makes time irrelevant. You are walking across the top of a world that has composted its own history into a warm, wet, endless present, suspended high above a planet that no longer matters, cradled in the arms of a vegetation that has learned to be stone, water, and sky all at once.
