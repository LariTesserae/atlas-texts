The landscape is a paradox of total fabrication and embryonic genesis, a place where the concept of "construction" has been decoupled from the concept of "completion." It is the **Ossuary of the Unfinished Loom**.

**Terrain and Materials: The Wet Scaffolding**
There is no soil here, nor stone, nor bedrock. The ground is an endless, undulating topography of woven cellulose and calcified resin, a bio-architectural substrate that stretches across a high-altitude valley. Because the erosion parameter is so low (0.4) but the built parameter is absolute (2.8), the terrain does not feel ancient or weathered; it feels *raw*. It is geology in the moment of being born, but a geology made entirely of manufactured plant matter.

The surface is a lattice of thick, pale cables—some as wide as rivers, others as thin as thread—interlocked in patterns that suggest both structural engineering and nervous systems. This lattice is perpetually wet. Water does not flow in channels carved by time; rather, it weeps directly from the material itself. The constructed fibers are hygroscopic to an impossible degree, pulling moisture from the warm, heavy air and sweating it out in a continuous, glistening perspiration. To walk here is to walk on a living sponge that has been engineered into the shape of a mountain range. The water is not separate from the land; it is the land’s blood, circulating through capillary-tunnels within the woven floor, emerging at junctions and knots to pool in shallow, translucent basins before evaporating back into the condensing atmosphere.

**Vegetation as Architecture: The Total Mass**
With vegetation at 2.8 and built at 2.8, there is no distinction between forest and city, between garden and machine. The plants *are* the buildings; the buildings *are* the plants. But because density is sparse (0.6), this total mass does not manifest as a jungle choke. Instead, it manifests as colossal, isolated megastructures separated by vast, humid voids.

These structures are not trees. They are vertical extrusions of the woven terrain, rising hundreds of feet into the normal light like stalled explosions of timber and vine. They look less like grown things and more like architectural blueprints realized in biological matter but abandoned at ninety percent completion. Beams of braided wood terminate in mid-air; arches of fused leaves hang open without keystones; spirals of bark-cladding unspool into nothingness. The vegetation is total, yet it is defined by its gaps. It is a cathedral system where the walls exist, but the roof was never conceived.

Because the tech level is near zero, none of this is assembled by tools. There are no joints, no nails, no grafts. The construction is ontological. The environment simply *is* this shape, having emerged from a biological imperative that mimics masonry. It is pre-tool creation carried out on a civic scale.

**Atmosphere and Light: The Warm Suspension**
The temperature is a steady, enveloping warmth (1.8), lacking the bite of wind or the chill of altitude despite the elevation. The air is thick, saturated with the smell of wet starch, ozone, and new-cut lumber. It is a womb-like climate, perfectly regulated by the respiration of the unfinished structures.

Light falls normally (2.1), but it behaves strangely against the sparse, wet architecture. Because the structures are widely spaced, shadows do not blanket the landscape; they fall in sharp, distinct geometries across the glowing, damp floor. The light catches the millions of suspended water droplets clinging to the incomplete beams, turning each megastructure into a prism. The world looks rendered in high-definition clarity, every fiber and bead of moisture distinct, illuminated by a sun that feels indifferent to the strangeness below.

**Sound: The Loud Equilibrium**
Despite the stability of the landscape (dynamic 0.5), it is profoundly loud (sound 2.0). This is not the noise of activity, but the noise of tension. The woven terrain is under immense hydraulic pressure; the weeping rocks and sweating beams emit a constant, resonant thrum, a subsonic cello-note generated by water moving through tight biological conduits.

Overlaying this bass is a higher frequency: the sound of surface tension breaking. As condensation gathers on the raw, unworn surfaces of the plant-architecture, it reaches a critical mass and releases in rhythmic, percussive drips that echo like hammers striking anvils in the open spaces. The loudness is uniform, unchanging, and omnidirectional. It is the acoustic signature of a system holding itself together in perfect, eternal stasis. Nothing is collapsing; nothing is growing. It is simply *being*, loudly.

**Ecology and Weirdness: The Absent Fauna**
Fauna is rare (1.3), and this absence is what makes the landscape truly strange (weirdness 2.5). In a natural ecosystem, such abundant water and plant mass would teem with life. Here, the biology is sterile. There are no insects in the weeping pools, no birds nesting in the open arches, no moss on the damp cables. The flora is so thoroughly "built," so perfectly optimized for its own structural existence, that it leaves no ecological niche for anything else.

The only movement is the water. The only consumers are the evaporation and the capillary action. This creates a profound sense of loneliness and alien purpose. The landscape feels like a stage set prepared for a play that will never begin, or a body grown without a soul. It is a masterpiece of biological engineering that forgot to include the messy chaos of life.

**Rules of the World**
1.  **Stasis is Active:** Nothing changes, yet everything requires energy to maintain. The lack of erosion and dynamic shift is not passive; it is a fierce, energetic resistance to entropy.
2.  **Water is Structural:** To remove the water would be to demolish the buildings. Dryness is collapse.
3.  **Incompleteness is Function:** The gaps, the open arches, and the terminated beams are not errors; they are necessary pressure valves for the hydrological system. A "finished" structure would burst.
4.  **Silence is Impossible:** To be present here is to be vibrated. Quiet is a foreign concept; peace is found only in the acceptance of the thrum.

This is a place of wet, warm, loud permanence—a valley-sized artifact of pure form, forever emerging, forever halted, forever weeping its own existence into the still air.
