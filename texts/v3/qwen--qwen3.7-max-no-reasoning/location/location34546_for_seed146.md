The place is known, in the few tongue-shapes capable of naming it, as the **Silt-Womb of the Lower Geode**. It is a world without water, yet it flows.

### Terrain and Materials
You are beneath the surface, suspended in a planetary crust that has not yet settled into geological maturity. The terrain is defined by a paradox: it is vast enough to possess its own barometric pressure systems, yet it is enclosed, subterranean, and eternally warm. There is no sky, only a ceiling of compressed basalt and vitrified clay so distant it vanishes into the gloom.

The ground is not soil. It is a dry, particulate ocean of silicate dust and metallic oxides, ground to the consistency of flour over eons of tectonic friction. Because the erosion parameter is young, the landscape is sharp, jagged, and unfinished. Massive shards of obsidian and raw quartz protrude from the silt like broken teeth, their edges unweathered, fresh from the planetary cooling that birthed this cavity. These formations do not crumble; they snap. The land is brittle.

Despite the aridity (water: 0.5), the air is thick with suspension. This is a dense atmosphere (density: 1.6) composed of heavy noble gases and mineral aerosols. It does not behave like air; it behaves like a translucent fluid. You do not walk through it; you wade. The warmth (temperature: 2.1) is uniform, a body-heat ambient temperature that permeates the rock and the dust alike. There are no thermal gradients, no convection currents, no wind. The heat is a static property of the space, locked in equilibrium.

### Light and Darkness
Light is scarce (0.4/3), but the darkness is not absolute. Illumination comes from two sources: the faint, amber bioluminescence of the fauna, and the piezoelectric glow of the stressed quartz formations. When the dense silt shifts underfoot or when the creatures move, the crystals sing with cold light, illuminating the immediate vicinity in stroboscopic flashes. Between these events, the dark is heavy, pressing against the eyes like velvet.

### Ecology: The Lithovoric Abundance
Life here is abundant (fauna: 1.6) but entirely alien to surface biology. There is no vegetation (0.2). Instead, the ecosystem is based on *litho-resonance*.

The dominant organisms are the **Resonators**. They resemble nothing so much as articulated geodes—crab-like, multi-legged things encased in shells of grown crystal. They do not eat matter; they eat vibration. They cluster in high densities around the sharp, fresh obsidian spires, tapping them with specialized limbs to harvest the stored kinetic energy of the planet’s settling crust. Their abundance creates a living carpet of clicking, crystalline bodies that coats the valley floors.

Above them drift the **Sift-Moths**, though "moth" is a misnomer. They are gas-bladder organisms, buoyant in the dense atmosphere, trailing filaments of electrostatic silk that catch mineral particles. They are silent, blind, and numerous, forming vast, slow-moving clouds in the upper reaches of the cavern. They are the pollinators of this dead world, transferring charged dust between the crystal gardens.

There are also the **Deep-Root Fungi**, though they are closer to slime molds than mushrooms. They exist only in the microscopic fissures of the sharp rocks, secreting acids that etch patterns into the stone. These etchings are not random; they are acoustic channels, carved to direct the vibrations that the Resonators feed upon. The ecology is a closed loop of sound, stone, and static charge.

### The Built and The Primitive
Scattered through this alien wilderness are the marks of the **Knappers** (built: 1.0, tech: 1.4). They are not ruins, for they were never meant to last, nor are they grand. They are functional, ephemeral modifications to the landscape.

The Knappers are a nomadic, subterranean species, perhaps distant cousins of the Resonators or perhaps something else entirely. They possess simple metal tools—copper chisels, bone hammers, leather bellows—but their technology is acoustic rather than mechanical. They build *sound traps*.

You find them occasionally: carefully arranged piles of resonant stones, tuned to specific frequencies, placed near migration routes of the Resonators. Some are small cairns that hum when the dense air shifts; others are shallow grooves carved into the bedrock that amplify the footfalls of prey. There are no walls, no roofs, no permanent shelters. The Knappers live in the interstices of the sharp terrain, using the natural geometry of the young geode as their architecture. Their artifacts are indistinguishable from the geology to the untrained eye; a tool looks like a shard, a shelter looks like a crevice. Only the deliberate arrangement betrays intent.

### Sound and Stasis
The landscape is mostly quiet (sound: 0.9), but the silence is textured. In the absence of wind or water, every sound is discrete, isolated, significant. The click of a Resonator’s limb travels for miles in the dense air. The low-frequency thrum of the planet’s cooling core is felt in the marrow before it is heard.

And yet, despite the abundance of life and the presence of tool-users, the place feels frozen (dynamic: 0.3). This is because the system is in perfect, timeless equilibrium. Nothing decays, for there is no moisture to rot and no wind to erode. Nothing grows, for the energy input is constant and finite. The Knappers’ sound traps have been in place for millennia, unchanged, because they work perfectly and the environment does not degrade them. The Resonators reproduce at exactly the rate the crystals recharge. The silt neither accumulates nor disperses.

Time here is not a river; it is a held breath.

### Rules of the World
1.  **Sound is Currency:** Energy transfer occurs through vibration. To make noise is to spend; to listen is to earn. Silence is conservation.
2.  **Sharpness is Safety:** The young, uneroded terrain provides anchor points, resonance chambers, and shelter. Smooth ground is barren, exposed, and dead.
3.  **Density Masks Distance:** In the heavy atmosphere, things appear closer than they are. A mountain range might be a mile away or ten; the haze flattens perspective. Navigation relies on echo, not sight.
4.  **Stasis is Survival:** Change is entropy. The ecosystem persists because it does not evolve, does not fluctuate, does not surprise. To introduce novelty is to invite collapse.
5.  **Warmth is Structural:** The temperature is not weather; it is the binding agent of the atmosphere. If the warmth were to fade, the heavy gases would liquefy, the silt would cement, and the world would become a tomb.

### Atmosphere and Emergent Feeling
To stand in the Silt-Womb is to feel immersed in amber. The air tastes of copper and ozone. Your skin feels the constant, gentle pressure of the dense gas, like being swaddled in warm silk. Every movement you make sends ripples through the medium, disturbing the equilibrium, announcing your presence to every Resonator within a kilometer.

It is a place of profound intimacy and profound indifference. The abundance of life does not welcome you; it merely includes you, briefly, as another source of vibration, another variable in an equation that has already solved itself. The scattered Knapper artifacts suggest intelligence, but not curiosity. They are the marks of beings who have achieved perfect adaptation, who no longer need to strive, to wonder, to change.

The weirdness (1.9/3) lies not in the strangeness of the creatures or the physics, but in the *completeness* of the system. This is a world that has finished becoming. It is a masterpiece of equilibrium, sealed beneath the earth, warm and dark and full of life, waiting for nothing, needing nothing, forever.

And as you stand there, breathing the mineral air, feeling the pulse of the lithovores through the soles of your boots, watching the piezoelectric stars flicker in the endless ceiling, you realize: you are the only thing in this place that is still becoming. You are the anomaly. The dissonance. The unresolved chord.

The landscape tolerates you. For now.
