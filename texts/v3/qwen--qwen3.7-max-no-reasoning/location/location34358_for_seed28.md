The place is known as the Resonance Cistern, though it holds no water. It is a sunken amphitheater of geology and architecture, a pocket-world buried beneath the crust of a dry, silicate planet, accessible only through a narrow fissure that acts as both throat and doorway.

**Terrain and Materials**
The ground is not soil, but a vast, smooth concavity of vitrified salt and polished basalt. Over eons, the erosion here has not been caused by wind or rain, but by vibration. The landscape has been scoured into fluid, organic curves by sound waves made solid over geological time. The basin is shallow, a low-elevation bowl perhaps three hundred meters across, enclosed entirely by towering, sheer walls of pale, sedimentary rock that have been fused into a ceramic-like hardness. There is no dust. The relentless acoustic pressure keeps every surface swept clean, buffed to a mirror sheen that reflects the blinding light from above.

**Light and Atmosphere**
The brightness is absolute and punishing. A single, vertical shaft of unfiltered stellar radiation pierces the aperture directly overhead at high noon, striking the center of the salt-basin. Because the air is utterly devoid of moisture or particulate haze, there is no diffusion, no softening. Light travels in hard, geometric lances. Shadows are not gradients but razor-edged voids of total blackness against the searing white of the illuminated salt. The heat is significant—a dry, baking warmth that radiates from the ground itself—but it is secondary to the pressure of the light. The atmosphere is thin, sealed, and still, yet it feels heavy, compressed by the sheer weight of the noise.

**The Sound**
This is the defining law of the Cistern: it is deafening. Not a chaotic clamor, but a singular, sustained harmonic chord of impossible volume. The unique geometry of the basin, combined with the specific mineral composition of the walls, creates a perpetual standing wave. The planet’s tectonic micro-tremors enter the base of the structure and are amplified, focused, and trapped. To stand in the center is to be inside a bell that never stops ringing. The sound is physical; it vibrates the marrow, blurs vision, and presses against the chest like a deep-sea dive. Communication by voice is impossible; even thought feels disrupted by the sonic saturation. The silence exists only in memory.

**Ecology: The Lithic Flora**
In this desiccated, roaring furnace, life has abandoned carbon and water for silica and resonance. The "vegetation" is lush, dense, and entirely mineral. Great forests of crystalline growth erupt from the salt floor and cling to the curved walls. They resemble coral, ferns, and weeping willows, but they are formed of piezoelectric quartz, bismuth, and spun glass. They do not photosynthesize light; they *phonosynthesize* sound.

These crystal-gardens convert the deafening ambient noise into metabolic energy. They grow in direct proportion to the acoustic intensity. Where the standing wave is strongest, the flora is thickest—tall, branching spires of translucent violet and amber that hum in sympathy with the environment. They are warm to the touch, vibrating with stored energy. They reproduce not by seed, but by fracture; when a branch grows too heavy with sonic charge, it snaps off in a burst of harmonics, embedding itself in the salt to begin growing anew. This is a garden that eats noise and excretes structure.

**The Built Environment**
Nested within this hostile, roaring bowl is a city. It is an urban density of primitive, masterful craft. The builders did not fight the acoustics; they tuned themselves to it. The architecture is carved directly into the basin’s walls and the larger crystal formations. Dwellings are not added structures but subtractive sculptures—rooms hollowed out of the living rock and glass, shaped to create internal pockets of destructive interference where the deafening roar cancels itself out into bearable quiet.

The infrastructure is a lattice of stone bridges, spiral staircases, and ventilation flutes that serve as secondary instruments. The entire settlement is a machine for managing sound. Walkways are designed with specific textures to dampen footfall; doorways are angled to deflect standing waves. There is no metal here—metal would shatter or melt under the sonic stress. Everything is stone, glass, bone, and woven fiber. Tools are simple: chisels of diamond-hard nodule, mallets of wrapped leather, polishing stones. The technology is one of subtraction and shaping, a pre-industrial mastery of acoustics that rivals modern engineering.

**Density and Emptiness**
Despite the lushness of the crystal flora and the complexity of the carved city, the space feels profoundly empty. The scale is intimate, a hidden room in the world’s basement, yet the volume between presences is vast. The inhabitants are few. Perhaps only a hundred souls live in this deafening paradise. They move slowly, deliberately, spaced far apart. In a world where sound is a tangible, overwhelming force, proximity is dangerous; two bodies too close together create dissonant reflections. Social distance is a survival mechanism. The city is densely built but sparsely inhabited, a cathedral of noise where solitude is the primary form of communion.

**Fauna**
Animals are nearly absent. Only one species endures: the Tuning Moth. Pale, blind, and wingless, these creatures are the size of a human hand. They do not fly; they ride the standing waves, suspended in mid-air by acoustic levitation at specific nodal points. They feed on the excess energy bleeding from the crystal plants. They are silent, their bodies perfectly absorbent, existing as living dampeners. To see one hovering motionless in the blinding light, held aloft by invisible sound, is to witness the ecosystem’s strange equilibrium.

**Rules of the World**
1.  **Silence is Structural Failure:** Quiet means something has broken. A sudden drop in volume signals a collapse, a crack in the basin, or a dying crystal grove. Noise is safety; noise is life.
2.  **Movement Must Be Rhythmic:** Erratic motion creates chaos. Walking, working, breathing—all must sync with the fundamental frequency of the Cistern. To move out of phase is to invite nausea, injury, or structural damage.
3.  **Light is Time:** With no water cycle, no seasons, no weather, the passage of time is marked solely by the angle of the light shaft. Noon is the zenith of power; twilight is the hour of maintenance and repair.
4.  **Waste is Resonance:** Nothing is discarded. Broken tools, shed crystal fragments, even the dead are returned to the basin floor to be re-integrated into the sonic cycle. Entropy is not permitted; everything must vibrate.

**The Dynamic Balance**
The Cistern is not static. It breathes. As the planet’s tectonic rhythms shift over centuries, the fundamental tone drifts. The crystal forests adapt, growing new branches tuned to emerging harmonics, shedding old ones that fall out of key. The carvers constantly reshape their dwellings, widening a corridor here, narrowing a chamber there, perpetually retuning their city to match the changing song of the earth. It is an active, anxious balance—a civilization engaged in an endless duet with geology, building a home inside a scream, finding lushness in desolation, and making sanctuary from the very thing that should destroy them.
