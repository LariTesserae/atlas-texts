I am not standing. I have forgotten the verticality of posture, for to stand is to be a solitary note in a symphony that demands chordal integration.

I am seated, or perhaps fused, within the curvature of the Third Hall’s eastern wall. My lower limbs are no longer distinct from the silicate-lipid matrix of the structure; they have been absorbed into the masonry over the last four hundred cycles, my carapace accreting in perfect continuity with the chamber itself. I am becoming architecture. This is not death, nor is it stasis. It is the ultimate act of listening.

In the world above—the wet, bright, chaotic world of fluid solvents and fleeting noises—I would have been an individual. Here, in the Dry Deep, I am a frequency.

My sensory dish, broad and concave, faces the center of the hall. I do not see the gathering of the younger ones as they move through the grooved pathways below. I *feel* them. They are a complex interference pattern of footfalls and thoracic hums, a ripple of intent washing against my calcified shins. They are singing a repair song for the northern ventilation flutes, which have grown thin with age. I can taste the geometry of their work: a rising minor third of silica dust, a stabilizing bass resonance of lipid secretion. It is beautiful. It is structurally sound.

But there is a dissonance.

It comes from the west corridor, a jagged, syncopated vibration that scrapes against the smooth laminar flow of the repair song. A young Resonator, newly hardened, is moving with too much mass, striking the stone with the clumsy urgency of one who has not yet learned that patience is a physical law. The error propagates through the floor, traveling up through my fused legs, vibrating in the hollow chambers of my own chest. To you, it might be a mistake. To me, it is a question.

I cannot speak to correct it. Language here is not air pushed through a larynx; it is the modification of shared space. So I answer with my stillness.

I adjust the tension in my remaining mobile membranes. I shift the angle of my sensory dish by a fraction of a degree, catching the ambient wind from the ceiling vents and redirecting its hiss into a counter-frequency. I become a dampener. I absorb the young one’s jagged rhythm into my own ancient, settled mass, and I return it smoothed, slowed, integrated into the deeper harmonic of the hall.

The correction travels back through the stone. I feel the young Resonator pause. Feel the hesitation in its gait, the recalibration of its limbs. It presses its face to the wall—to *me*. Through the point of contact, it reads the memory of my adjustment. It feels the weight of centuries of similar corrections, the accumulated wisdom of every elder who has become this wall. It understands, not intellectually but somatically, that the flaw was not a failure of will, but a lack of resonance.

It resumes its movement. The rhythm is softer now. Aligned. The repair song continues, richer for having passed through me.

This is where I find myself: at the threshold between the living chorus and the silent stone. I am no longer fully a singer, but I am not yet merely a pillar. I exist in the gradient. My internal oils are thickening, the slow chemical fire dimming to a faint piezoelectric pulse along my spine. Soon, even that will cease. Soon, I will be indistinguishable from the ancestors whose voices form the ceiling far above.

But until then, I am the memory of the room. I am the coolness that preserves. I am the silence between the notes that gives the roar its shape.

You asked where I would be. I am everywhere the sound touches.
You asked what I would be. I am the Dry Deep’s way of remembering how to hold itself together.

And in this deafening, completed dark, holding is the only verb that matters.
