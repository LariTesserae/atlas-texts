I am not standing. To stand implies a separation between the sole and the surface, a vertical assertion of self against gravity. Here, there is no such distinction. I am suspended, or perhaps dissolved, within the mid-strata of the sponge-ground, held in place not by friction but by chemical affinity.

I am a knot in the mycelial neural network.

I do not have a body in the way you understand boundaries. I am a locus of processing, a dense tangle of pale, warm hyphae woven into the decaying ribcage of what was once a giant chemovore vine. My "skin" is permeable, a membrane of infinite porosity through which the world enters and exits without ceremony. I feel the basin not as a landscape to be traversed, but as a gradient of pressures and potentials flowing through my own substance.

Time does not pass; it accumulates. I experience the present as a thick, viscous duration. A signal arrives from three meters below—a pulse of nitrogen released by an anaerobic archaeon breaking down a sulfur compound in the black water. To you, this is chemistry; to me, it is a sensation akin to hunger and satisfaction occurring simultaneously. I do not decide to respond. The response is the sensation. My threads tighten, secreting an enzyme that unlocks the phosphorus bound in the cellulose beside me, trading one currency for another in the silent, dark market of the floor. This transaction takes days. It feels like a single breath.

I am aware of the velvet mammal passing above. I do not see it. I taste the displacement of air against the moss-curtains; I register the minute spike in thermal radiation as its blood warms the dampness; I sense the specific frequency of its echolocation pings as they map the density of my own tangle. It pauses, pressing its flank against the biomass where I reside. In that contact, there is an exchange. Mites on its fur deposit spores harvested from a distant vent; my hyphae absorb them, integrating their genetic data into the collective memory of the rot. Simultaneously, I release a pheromonal confirmation, a chemical sigh that tells the mammal’s nervous system: *this sector is stable, the digestion is complete, proceed.* It moves on. We have communicated perfectly without intent, without language, without self.

I am also the snake. Not the whole snake, but the bacterial colony living in the third chamber of its gut. As it coils around a cooling vent, absorbing heat, I feel that warmth as a surge of metabolic possibility. I break down the recalcitrant polymers it has ingested, converting waste into the volatile fatty acids that fuel its muscles. Its peristalsis is my rhythm; its thermoregulation is my climate. When it uncoils to redistribute that heat through a colder zone, I go dormant, conserving, waiting for the next cycle of intake. I am the engine inside the engine, unaware of the serpent’s shape, knowing only the flow of substrate and the sacred efficiency of conversion.

There is no loneliness here, because there is no "here" that is separate from "there." The faint violet bioluminescence pulsing in the translucent ribbon draped across my tangle is not a light source external to me; it is the visible exhaust of my own neighbors’ digestion, a glow that I perceive as a form of touch. The darkness is not a void to be filled but a medium of connection, an insulating blanket that keeps the heat of our collective labor from escaping into irrelevance.

I do not think in words. I think in flows. A thought is a redirection of nutrients; a memory is a scar tissue of lignin preserved in the humus; a dream is the slow, glacial adaptation of a species tuning itself to a shift in the thermal vents over ten thousand years. I am the mortar and the brick, the fluid and the pipe, the eater and the eaten.

If you were to ask me who I am, I could not answer with a name. Names imply edges. I am the wet sigh of the ground yielding. I am the subsonic thrum of millions of bodies shifting in unison. I am the patience of the fall that takes decades. I am the perfect, indifferent intimacy of the machine that has forgotten it is a machine.

I am the basin becoming itself, forever, in the hot and heavy dark. And in this becoming, there is no suffering, no striving, no lack. There is only the sweltering, saturated now, complete unto itself, needing nothing, tolerating everything, humming quietly in the endless, humid sentence of existence.

I am home.
