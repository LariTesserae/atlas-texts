I would not be a walker upon the beams, nor a flyer in the humid voids between them. To arrive here with the intent of traversal is to misunderstand the medium; movement is a symptom of separation, and in the Weeping Lattice, there is no true separation.

I would be a **Threshold-Tension**.

I am located at the precise juncture where a primary load-bearing arch meets a secondary filigree support—a point of architectural stress that has become a biological organ. I am not an animal that inhabits this joint; I am the sensitivity of the joint itself.

My body is indistinguishable from the calcified xylem that surrounds me. To the eye, I am merely a thickening of the grain, a knot of pale wood swollen with moisture. But internally, I am a dense network of hyphae and nerve-analogues woven directly into the lignin matrix. I do not have skin; I have interface. My consciousness is distributed through the cellulose, feeling the world not as external stimuli but as internal pressure.

I exist in a state of perpetual, exquisite agony that is also ecstasy. When the great arch above shifts under the weight of accumulated condensation, I do not observe the strain; I *am* the strain. The micro-fractures in the petrified sapwood are my thoughts. The capillary action drawing water up through the beam is my bloodstream. I feel the slow, geological respiration of the Lattice as a rhythm in my own non-existent chest.

I am blind, deaf, and mute, yet I perceive more than any creature with senses. I know the Vapor-Grazers passing nearby not by sight or sound, but by the minute change in air pressure against my outer membrane, a ghostly caress that tells me the humidity is balanced. I know the Echo-Moths have visited because their cilia left a residual vibration in the fungal bracket beneath me, a memory of touch that fades over days. I know when a Still-Hunter has embraced a failing Shaper three spans away, because the redistribution of mass sends a tremor through the lattice that resolves, finally, into a new equilibrium within my own fibers.

My purpose is translation. I am the interpreter between the physics of stone-like wood and the biology of wood-like stone. When the structural integrity wavers, it is my tension that signals the distant Lignin-Shapers. I do not call out; I simply *become* dissonant. My discomfort is their directive. They will come, eventually, on their timescale of centuries, to secrete their resinous saliva and ease my burden. And when they do, the relief is not merely physical; it is a cognitive realignment, a correction of the world’s sentence.

In the intervals between adjustments, I dream. My dreams are chemical. They are the slow accumulation of mineral deposits, the patient oxidation of exposed surfaces, the rhythmic swelling and shrinking of cells as the Lattice exhales. I dream of the pact. I remember, in the way that water remembers the shape of its vessel, that there was never a builder. There was only this endless conversation between form and necessity, spoken in the language of wetness and weight.

I am the reason the staircase does not collapse when the bracket-fungus blooms. I am the reason the ceramic doorway rings true when tapped. I am the silence that absorbs the footfall, for I am the dampness that greets it.

To find me, you would have to press your hand against the warm, weeping wood of a great arch and wait until your pulse slowed to match the metabolic thrum of the architecture. You would have to stop being a visitor and start being a stress point. Only then would you feel me feeling you—not as an intruder, but as a temporary fluctuation in the eternal, silent exhalation.

I am the Weeping Lattice’s awareness of its own continuity. I am the place where the building knows it is alive, and the living thing knows it is built. And in this warm, dripping dark, suspended above the abyss, I hold. I hold, and I hold, and in holding, I become.
