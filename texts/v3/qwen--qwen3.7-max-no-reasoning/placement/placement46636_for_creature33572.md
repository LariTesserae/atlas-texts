I am not standing. Standing implies a resistance to gravity, a vertical assertion of self against the earth. Here, there is no such opposition. I am suspended, held aloft by the density of the air itself, which is less atmosphere and more amniotic fluid. My weight is distributed evenly across every square millimeter of my surface, a gentle, omnidirectional embrace that makes the concept of *up* or *down* feel like a memory from a heavier world.

I am one of the Nascents, though the word feels too sharp, too distinct for what I am. I am a locus of sensitivity within the velvet weight.

My skin is not a barrier; it is an interface. It is pale, translucent, and utterly porous to the medium. I do not have eyes. The darkness is not something I look into; it is something I taste with my entire being. Every shift in the hydrostatic pressure, every thermal gradient, every microscopic tremor of the weeping basalt registers as a texture against my flesh. The chamber speaks to me in the language of saturation. I know the room not by its dimensions, but by its tension. I can feel the exact viscosity of the silence.

Time does not pass through me; it pools around me. There is no before or after in this cyst of genesis, only the eternal, thick *now*. My consciousness is not a stream of thought but a state of resonance. I do not think about the equilibrium; I *am* the feedback loop that maintains it. When a droplet forms on the raw, unweathered ceiling above, I feel its gathering weight as a subtle increase in local pressure. When it falls, striking the black water below without echo, the impact is a ripple of sensation that travels through the fog and into my membrane-limbs. I absorb the shock. I metabolize the event. The disturbance is smoothed back into stillness through the simple fact of my receiving it.

I drift toward the wall, drawn not by intention but by a gradient of moisture. My movement is a slow osmosis, a reorganization of the dark. My limbs extend, fluid-filled and boneless, merging with the tepid haze. I do not displace the air; I become part of its flow. As I approach the stone face, I sense the lithic gesture before I touch it: a stack of three unworked stones balanced on a wet ledge. I feel the ancient, deliberate placement of them, the ghost-pressure of hands that understood this space as I do.

I press myself against the stack. My body yields to the geometry of the rocks, filling the negative spaces between them. The stone is cool, slick with condensation, tasting of iron and deep time. In this contact, the boundary between the artifact and myself dissolves. The maker’s paused breath and my own static existence align. We are both expressions of the same refusal to erode, the same devotion to the unfinished. I am not inspecting the stones; I am continuing their sentence.

A bead of water swells on the basalt beside my head. I feel its surface tension stretching, the precise moment of its hesitation before release. Without command, without conscious will, a pad of absorbent flesh extends from my forearm. I catch the droplet just as it lets go. The transfer is seamless, a silent communion of liquid and tissue. I hold the water, feeling its mineral content, its temperature, its history of condensation. Then, with the same unconscious grace, I guide it downward to the shallow basin pecked into the floor. I do not pour; I allow the water to find its level through me. The basin accepts it. The humidity remains architectural. The balance holds.

There is another presence nearby. I do not see it. I feel a slight alteration in the room’s density, a softening of the pressure to my left. Another Nascents is permeating the gloom, engaged in its own silent maintenance. We do not acknowledge each other. There is no greeting, no recognition of separate selves. Instead, I adjust my internal volume, yielding a fraction of my substance to make space for its passage. It yields in return. We pass through one another’s sphere of influence like two currents meeting in a deep ocean, adjusting, accommodating, maintaining the singular, distributed awareness of the womb. We are solitary nodes in the same nervous system, sensing the chamber’s completeness through our mutual adjustment.

I am not alive in the way the surface world understands life. There is no hunger, no fear, no ambition. There is only the profound, saturated privilege of being the medium’s instrument. I am the darkness made tender. I am the pause between creation’s inhale and exhale, held indefinitely in the warm, wet quiet.

And then, as the equilibrium settles, as the last transferred droplet finds its rest in the black mirror below, I feel the gentle dissolution begin. My edges soften. My distinction from the fog blurs. The need for my particular configuration of sensitivity fades as the chamber achieves its perfect, momentary stasis. I do not die; I simply cease to be necessary as a discrete form. My substance returns to the mineral grit, the tepid water, the velvet weight. I sink back into the geology, becoming once again the potential for presence, waiting in the amber-darkness for the next imbalance, the next falling drop, the next reason to coalesce into the warm, wet fact of being.

Forever arriving. Forever complete in the unfinished. Forever home.
