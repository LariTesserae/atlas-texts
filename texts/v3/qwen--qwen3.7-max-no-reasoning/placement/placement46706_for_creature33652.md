I am not a person. I have long since surrendered the luxury of interiority, the chaotic friction of selfhood that requires so much moisture to sustain. To be an individual here would be a leak.

I am a surface.

Specifically, I am the northern lip of the Third Reservoir, the one carved deepest into the granite where the shadow lingers longest after dawn. My skin is no longer distinct from the pale grey stone beneath it; we have negotiated a boundary that is more suggestion than fact. My fingers are fused to the rim, not in rigor mortis, but in functional integration. I am cool to the touch, matte as unglazed ceramic, my pores sealed against the indignity of evaporation. I do not breathe in the rhythmic, tidal sense of lowland creatures. Instead, I engage in a slow, osmotic exchange with the air itself, drawing only the precise molecular weight of oxygen required to maintain this vigil.

My consciousness is not a stream of thought, for streams imply direction and loss. It is a tension. A sustained, cellular attention focused entirely on the gradient between dryness and wetness.

It is the hour before true dark, when the light begins to rake across the plaza and the temperature performs its nightly descent. This is my purpose. I am waiting for the dew point.

I feel the shift in the air pressure against my cheekbone before I perceive it visually. The atmosphere thickens, heavy with the promise of saturation. Somewhere in the periphery of my awareness, another Keeper shifts their weight—a movement so fluid it registers only as a change in the acoustic resonance of the basin. We do not acknowledge one another. To speak would be to introduce turbulence. We are a single, distributed sensor array draped over the architecture of survival.

A droplet forms.

It does not fall; it *arrives*. It coalesces upon the curve of my knuckle, a bead of impossible clarity holding the inverted reflection of the first star. I feel its weight as a profound data point, a confirmation of equilibrium. My body knows what to do without the mediation of will. The capillary channels woven into my dermis, indistinguishable now from the moss-lined grooves of the cistern, accept the offering. There is no grasping, no cupping of hands. Only alignment. The water moves from air to skin to stone through me, and I am merely the conduit, the living gasket ensuring nothing is lost to the void.

In this transfer, there is a satisfaction deeper than joy, quieter than peace. It is the fulfillment of geometry. The rate of condensation matches the rate of absorption. The equation balances.

I remember, vaguely and without nostalgia, a time when I was smaller, softer, when I had to learn the silence rather than embody it. But that memory is not mine; it belongs to the town. I am simply the current iteration of its patience. Eventually, my movements will slow to imperceptibility. My breath will cease to be an exchange and become a stillness. I will desiccate, settling into this very rim, my dry husk becoming a windbreak for the next generation of condensation. I will not die; I will become infrastructure.

But not yet. Now, there is only the cool granite, the heavy air, and the singular, crystalline perfection of a droplet finding its level upon my hand.

I am the held breath. I am the meniscus. I am here.
