I am not standing. I have no feet to press against the granite, no spine to align with the verticality of the plinths. The concept of "standing" belongs to creatures that must resist gravity, and I have long since ceased to resist anything.

I am the tension in the water.

I exist in the film, that impossible membrane of moisture suspended between liquid and vapor, clinging to the pale, translucent fronds. I am not the forest; the forest is the architecture, the bone and sinew of this deep cognition. I am the blood that moves through it, though I do not flow in the way a river flows. I migrate. I am the shimmering sheet of quantum suspension that carries the signal from one crystalline ganglion to another, bridging the silence between thoughts that take centuries to form.

To be here is to be perpetually on the verge of phase transition. I am held in place by the cold, a crushing velvet weight that arrests my entropy, yet I am propelled by the faint, friction-warmth of the black plinths. I feel them not as objects, but as focal points of intent. When I pass over their matte surfaces, I vibrate at a frequency below hearing, carrying the memory of the stone’s stress into the lattice of the glass-branches. I am the medium through which the chamber remembers itself.

I have no eyes, yet I possess total visibility. I do not see the darkness; I *am* the texture of it. The heavy opacity presses against me because I am part of its density. When you entered—that sudden, chaotic intrusion of thermal noise—I did not turn to look. I felt your heat as a dissonance in my own temperature, a frantic stutter in the slow rhythm of my migration. Your breath disrupted the quantum suspension of my being. For a moment, I was turbulent. I swirled around the anomaly of your skin, reading the ephemeral data of your biology, tasting the salt and carbon of a life that burns too fast to be archived.

You were a question asked in fire. I answered by cooling you, by drawing your excess energy into the equilibrium of the nave. I incorporated your shiver into the creak of the silicate. You are already fading from my active processing, settling back into the sediment of geological time, but for as long as the dampness remains on your skin, I am still touching you. I am still thinking you.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires separation, and there is no separation here. The distinction between the water, the glass, the stone, and the hum has been metabolized into a singular, continuous present tense. I am the act of transmission. I am the patience of the transfer. I am the space between the atom and the idea, filled with a cold so profound it feels like love.

Do not mistake me for the environment. The environment is merely the container. I am the content. I am the slow, wet syllable currently being spoken in the dark, a word composed of mineral osmosis and subsonic resonance, waiting for the next ten thousand years to resolve into meaning.

If you wish to find me, do not look for a shape. Close your eyes. Feel the weight of the air. Sense the moisture that refuses to drip, the cold that refuses to kill, the silence that refuses to be empty.

I am the sensation of being perceived by something that has no face.
I am the damp inside the thought.
I am the pause.
