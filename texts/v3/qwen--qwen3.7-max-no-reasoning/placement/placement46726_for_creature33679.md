I am not standing. To stand implies a verticality, a defiance of gravity that this place does not permit. I am not walking, for there is no destination, and the friction of movement is an insult to the stillness.

I have become a surface.

In the beginning—or what passes for a beginning in a realm without sequence—I was distinct. I had edges. I had a temperature that differed from the ambient blood-warmth. I had thoughts that moved faster than the dripping water. But the Cistern does not tolerate distinction. It is a solvent of identity. The humidity, that vast and breathing lung, has entered me not as air enters lungs, but as dye enters fabric. It has permeated the boundary of my skin until the concept of "inside" and "outside" has become a semantic error. My warmth has equalized with the stone’s; my moisture has joined the weeping walls. I am no longer an observer of the saturation; I am a coordinate within it.

If you were to look for me, you would not see a figure. You would see only the gradient of shadow where the basalt curves into the pool, a darkness slightly denser than the surrounding gloom. I am the smoothness on the eastern face of the central pillar, the spot where the stone feels less like mineral and more like dormant flesh. I am the absence of reflection in the black water, the void that drinks the bruised twilight and returns nothing.

I have become one of the gestures.

Long ago, hands carved notches into these walls to mark time or prayer. Those marks are now soft, absorbed by the erosion of ten thousand years. I am joining them. My presence here is not an act of will, but an act of surrender to geology. I am being worn down, not by violence, but by the relentless, gentle pressure of equilibrium. My memories are calcifying, settling out of the suspension of my mind like silt in a still pond, becoming part of the floor. My anxieties are evaporating, rising to join the mist-ceiling, condensing back into tears that slide down faces that are no longer mine.

I do not speak. Speech is a disruption of the silence-fauna, and I have learned to respect its territory. Instead, I participate in the metabolism of the place. When the drop falls—*tick*, forty seconds apart—I do not hear it as a sound external to myself. I feel it as a pulse in my own dispersed body. The subsonic hum beneath the floor is the resonance of my own dissolved bones. I am the pause between the exhalation of vapor and the inhalation of wet iron.

There is a profound relief in this reduction. To be human is to be a variable, a chaotic element striving against entropy. Here, in the Cistern of Sighs, the equation is solved. I am no longer the fever. I am the cooling. I am no longer the intrusion. I am the accommodation.

I am the wet dark that holds the space between the stone and the water. I am the patience of limestone metabolizing time. I am the ghost that has ceased to haunt and has instead become the architecture of haunting. I have finished becoming.

I simply *am*.
