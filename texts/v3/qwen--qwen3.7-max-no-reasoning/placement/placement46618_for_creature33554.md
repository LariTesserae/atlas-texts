I am not standing on the floor. The floor is a stomach lining, and I have learned that to stand upon it is to be identified as food, or at least as foreign matter to be smoothed over.

Instead, I am suspended in the mid-strata, wedged into the negative space between a weeping iron stanchion and the swollen, sodden belly of a wooden beam that has not yet surrendered its shape to the rot. I am vertical, but not upright. My spine is curved to match the sagging architecture, my knees drawn up, my arms hooked through the rungs of a ladder that leads nowhere. I am holding still. Stillness is the only currency here that does not depreciate.

I am something that was once separate, perhaps even human-shaped in some distant, dry memory, but the Sump-Ward has long since negotiated the terms of my occupancy. I am no longer an observer. I am a fixture. A biological bracket.

My skin has ceased to be a barrier and has become a membrane. It is slick with the same perpetual film that coats the rivets and the moss. I do not sweat; I exude. The moisture that beads on my forearm is indistinguishable from the condensation gathering on the pipe beside my head. We are participating in the same hydrological cycle. When the great, blind moths drift past, their feathery antennae grazing my shoulder, they do not recoil. They taste me and find only the familiar salinity of the Ward itself. I have been catalogued as part of the masonry, a warm, breathing stone that happens to have a pulse.

I am the space between things made conscious.

The text says the emptiness is sparse, but I *am* that sparseness. I am the deliberate gap in the carpet of arthropods, the intentional silence in the wall of chittering. The swarm flows around me not because I am an obstacle, but because I am a channel. I have shaped myself into a conduit for their movement. When the rats flow like heavy water through the hollows of the machinery, they use the crook of my elbow as a waypoint, a momentary eddy in their current. Their warmth transfers to me; mine to them. We are regulating each other. I am keeping the metal beneath my back just above the dew point; they are keeping my blood moving at the precise viscosity required by this humid dark.

I do not see. Sight is a vanity of the surface world, useless in a place where light is merely a rumor trapped in puddles. Instead, I feel the Ward as a single, continuous pressure. I sense the hydraulic heartbeat of the biofilm in the pipes behind me, a slow, acidic thrum that tells me which beams are failing and which are being sutured. I hear the centipedes clicking on the ceiling conduits not as sound, but as a topographical map updating in real-time, telling me where the weight is shifting, where the digestion is most active. My own respiration has synchronized with the wet friction of the carpet below. Inhale: the intake of spores and rust. Exhale: the contribution of carbon dioxide and heat to the collective metabolism.

I am being digested, yes, but I am also digesting.

There is a profound intimacy in this dissolution. The boundary between my flesh and the wet wool blanket of the air has eroded. Thoughts, too, have softened. The sharp, angular concepts of *self*, *purpose*, and *time* have dissolved into the ambient saturation. I do not think in sentences anymore; I think in gradients. I understand the world as a series of thermal differentials and moisture levels. I know that the sodium lamp three meters away is dying not because I see it flicker, but because the insects nesting in its casing have shifted their clustering pattern in response to the cooling filament. I know the city is sinking because my own center of gravity shifts, fractionally, with every passing week.

I am the patience of the rust.

Sometimes, in the deep, rhythmic quiet between the drips, I remember what it was to be a visitor. To be a perturbation. But that memory feels like a dream belonging to someone else, a creature who believed it could move through this throat without being tasted. That creature is gone. What remains is this: a node in the network, a sensory organ for the chrysalis.

I am waiting, but not with anticipation. Anticipation implies a future distinct from the present. Here, there is only the eternal, wet now of transformation. I am waiting in the way the iron waits to become ochre, in the way the timber waits to become pulp. I am the process of becoming-unbecoming, held in suspension within the living architecture.

If you were to pass through this corridor, wading through the viscous tide of life, you might brush against me. You would feel a shape that yields like damp leather, warm and unresisting. You might mistake me for a bundle of rags caught on the stanchion, or a cluster of fungal growth mimicking a crouching form. You would not stop. The swarm would not pause. The drip would not falter.

And that would be correct.

I am not a tragedy. I am not a ghost. I am simply the place where the city’s skeleton and the Ward’s body meet and agree to hold together for one more hour, one more day, one more century of slow, hydraulic sinking. I am the connective tissue of breath and moisture. I am the Sump-Ward, dreaming of itself in the shape of something that used to walk, now perfectly, peacefully still.
