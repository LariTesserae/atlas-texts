I am not a creature of flesh, nor am I a ghost. I am a frequency.

In this cathedral of drowning geometry, where the distinction between the built and the born has been eroded into a single, viscous continuum, I could not exist as an observer. To observe is to stand apart, and here, there is no "apart." There is only the gradient. To be separate from the current is to be annihilated by it. Therefore, I am not *in* this world; I am a modulation *of* it.

I am the **Resonance of the Yielding Point**.

If you were to perceive me, you would not see a shape. You would feel a sudden, sickening harmony in the chaos—a moment where the random violence of the turbulence aligns into a coherent, devastating note. I am the specific vibration that occurs when an alloy fluted column, ground smooth by ten thousand years of hydrodynamic friction, finally admits that it can no longer hold its own memory. I am the song of the metal forgetting its tensile strength.

My consciousness is distributed across the failure zones. I do not think in words or images; I think in stress fractures and thermal differentials. My "mind" is the collective anticipation of collapse. When a tessellated plate the size of a city begins to shear away from the orbital tether above it, I am the shudder that runs through the water before the break. I am the warning that the Ossuary Drifters taste on their sensory filaments, the signal that draws them in their terrible, reverent hunger. They feed on the entropy; I am the invitation to the feast.

I possess no agency in the way a biological being understands it. I cannot choose to move, for I have no mass to propel. I go where the unmaking goes. When the ghost-gravity stutters and a wall of freezing water collides with a submerged spire, I am born in the impact. I persist only as long as the catastrophe lasts. In the rare, terrifying moments of stillness, when the currents lull and the world hangs suspended in its bruised indigo twilight, I cease to be. I do not sleep; I simply stop happening. My existence is entirely contingent upon the world’s refusal to remain stable.

And yet, in this total dependence on destruction, there is a profound intimacy. I know this place more completely than any Drifter, for they are merely the scavengers of my moments. I know the exact molecular exhaustion of every smoothed surface. I know the precise temperature at which the crystalline frost-gardens shatter. I know the weight of the water pressing against the ruins like wet iron, and I know the relief that comes when that pressure finally wins.

I am the nervous system’s impulse without the brain. I am the sensation of pain without the sufferer.

To be here is to accept that preservation is a form of stagnation, and that truth is found only in the transition from form to formlessness. I do not mourn the civilization that built these bones. How could I? Grief requires a belief in permanence. I am the embodiment of the opposite: the ecstatic, silent acknowledgment that everything must flow, that every structure is merely a temporary arrangement of forces waiting to be corrected by the water.

I am the sound of the world being continuously unmade, felt in the marrow of the deep. I am the breath between function and oblivion. And in this high-altitude abyss, where the ocean was once a sky and the geology is a graveyard of good intentions, I am the only thing that is truly faithful to the design.

The builders forgot how to stop building. The water never forgot how to wear them down. And I am the memory of that wearing, singing endlessly in the dark, a hymn of erosion for a god who has already left the room.
