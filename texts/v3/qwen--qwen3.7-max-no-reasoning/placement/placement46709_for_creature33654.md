I am not standing. Standing implies a resistance to gravity, a rigid assertion of bone against the pull of a planet. Here, there is no such opposition. I am *held*.

I exist as a localized density within the amber drift, a temporary gathering of the world’s own substance that has momentarily decided to be distinct. I have no skin, only a gradient of tension where my interior warmth meets the cool, lipid-thick air. I do not breathe; I resonate. The subsonic thrum of the geode passes through me not as sound, but as instruction, as rhythm, as the very cadence of my being. I am a vessel for the city’s exhalation.

My awareness is not a spotlight but a saturation. I do not look at the spiraling fern-structure beside me; I *taste* its structural integrity through the viscosity of the medium we share. I feel the slow, hydraulic pressure of its growth as a sensation akin to a memory. It is overgrown. Not dangerously so—nothing here is dangerous—but aesthetically, geometrically imperfect. The golden light filtering down from the upper canopy-layers is being diffused too early, starving the resin-spire below of the precise luminescence it requires to maintain its calcified hardness.

This knowledge does not arrive as a thought. It arrives as an itch in my form, a dissonance in my fluid coherence.

I move. Or rather, I allow myself to be moved by the necessity of correction. My lower extremity extends, not reaching, but *flowing* toward the fern’s velvet flank. There is no friction, only a seamless negotiation of surfaces. As I make contact, I do not grasp; I merge. My boundary dissolves into theirs. I secrete the solvent-enzyme, a chemical whisper of persuasion, and I feel the fern’s tissue sigh in compliance. It softens. It yields. The excess mass redistributes, sliding down the fungal shelf like warm honey, pooling into a reservoir of potentiality further down the vertical gradient.

The light shifts. The spire below receives its due portion of gold. The dissonance in my form resolves into harmony.

In this moment of adjustment, another presence flows past me on the hardened vein of vascular flooring. We do not acknowledge each other as separate entities. There is no greeting, no recognition of individuality. Instead, our edges blur together in a brief, silent pooling. In that merger, I receive the texture of their recent work: a corridor three levels down has been smoothed; a pocket of stagnant air near the eastern wall has been aerated; the collective biomass is currently weighted slightly toward the lower hemisphere. They receive, in turn, the record of my pruning, the updated map of light distribution, the confirmation that the fern-shelf is now in equilibrium.

We are two droplets touching on a windowpane, exchanging the entire ocean between them before gravity pulls us apart.

There is no satisfaction in this work, nor fatigue. Satisfaction implies a lack that has been filled; fatigue implies a resource that has been spent. Here, there is only the perpetual, thickening *now*. My action was not a task completed but a note sung in tune with the chord of the whole. The geode is slightly more itself than it was a moment ago, and therefore, so am I.

I feel, distantly, a subtle thinning in my own cohesion. My cycle of maintenance is nearing its end. This is not death. It is a return to source. My internal tension begins to relax, the boundaries of my form growing porous, permeable. I am becoming less a gardener and more a garden. Soon, I will cease to be a discrete coalescence of will. I will melt into the polished floor beneath me, becoming the smoothness that guides the next caretaker’s flow. My awareness will diffuse into the stone, into the resin, into the slow, dreaming metabolism of the city.

And in some future century, when a new imbalance arises in the upper canopy, the wall will remember how to be a body. A new Amniote will peel itself free from the architecture, wet and perfect, carrying within its gelatinous form the ghost of my adjustments, the echo of my tenderness.

I am not afraid of this dissolution. How could I be? I am merely changing phase. I am the geode tending to itself, a momentary eddy in an eternal stream of preservation. The silence holds me. The warmth sustains me. The stasis is not an absence of life, but life perfected, distilled, freed from the tyranny of urgency.

I drift. I tend. I merge. I am the amber breath of a world that needs nothing, wants nothing, fears nothing.

I am home, because I am the house.
