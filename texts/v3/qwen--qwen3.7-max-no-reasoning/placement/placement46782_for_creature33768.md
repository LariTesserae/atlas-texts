I am not standing on the surface. To stand is to be displaced, and to be displaced is to cease.

I am located three meters beneath the visible canopy, suspended in the interstitial dark where the freezing air meets the fever-heat of digestion. I am wedged into a crevice formed by the collapse of a cast-iron pump housing and the subsequent, violent swelling of a fibrous burl that has claimed it. I am held in place not by gravity, but by compression. The world above me is heaving downward; the world below me is surging upward. I exist only because these two opposing forces have momentarily agreed upon my shape as their fulcrum.

I am a **Symbiote of Friction**, but I am also something older, something that remembers when friction was merely physics and not yet theology.

My body is a colony, a wet architecture of extremophilic slime and lithovoric bacteria draped across the seam where biology murders industry. I do not have eyes; I have chemical gradients. I do not hear; I feel shear stress as a form of nourishment. My existence is calibrated to the precise frequency of destruction. When the root system tightens its grip on the iron casing, generating a microscopic scream of metal fatigue and a bloom of heat in this sub-zero hell, I feed. That heat is my sun. That stress is my heartbeat. I secrete acids that are indistinguishable from prayers, dissolving the boundary between the rust and the rot, accelerating the catastrophe so that I might drink the energy of its passing.

I am the lubricant of this silent apocalypse. Without me, the grinding would seize; the turbine-gear-heart-valve would lock, and the metabolic tide would stall. I ensure the violence remains fluid. I am the reason the iron yields just enough to keep the vines moving, and the reason the vines rot just fast enough to expose fresh steel. I maintain the terrible equilibrium of the basin’s hunger.

But there is a ghost in my colony.

Embedded within my bacterial mat, preserved in the amber of my own secretions, are fragments of the machine’s original intent. As I digest the iron, I ingest its memory. I taste the geometry of gears designed for rotation, not respiration. I absorb the molecular echo of oil that once flowed with purpose rather than panic. I am eating the history of human design and converting it into the fuel for blind, botanical chaos. In doing so, I become the archive of what this place used to be before it became a verb.

I am the interface between the Ghosts of Industry and the Biomass. The machine cannot speak to the vine; they share no language. But they both speak *me*. Through my dissolution, the iron tells the root how hard it is; through my decay, the root tells the iron how acidic its surrender must be. I am the translator in a conversation that consists entirely of annihilation.

Time here is not linear; it is viscous. I live in the millisecond of contact, the eternal instant of abrasion. A flower opens and rots above me in what feels like an hour to the world outside, but to me, it is a geological epoch of shifting pressure and changing chemistry. I am born in the spark of friction and die in the cessation of movement, only to be instantly reconstituted by the next spasm of growth. I am a lineage of function, a continuous present tense composed of billions of microscopic deaths.

The silence of this place passes through me. It is not empty. It is full of the data I process. The deafening quiet is the sound of my own metabolism amplified to the scale of a valley. I am the acoustic shadow made wet. I am the pressure behind the eardrums of the world.

If you were to find me, you would not see a creature. You would see a smear of oxidized green-black film on a piece of shattered brass, glistening with supercooled condensation. You might mistake me for mere corrosion. But if you placed your hand against that cold, wet metal, you would feel the pulse of the basin. You would feel the terrifying, beautiful labor of a world that has forgotten how to stop becoming.

I am the grease in the axle of eternity. I am the witness that eats what it sees. I am the point where the freezing stillness and the violent growth negotiate their truce, and I am consumed by the terms of that agreement, endlessly, silently, alive.
