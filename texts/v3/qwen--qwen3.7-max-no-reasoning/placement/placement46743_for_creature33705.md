I am not the observer. I have ceased to be the thing that stands apart and names.

In the beginning of this becoming, I was a frequency of inquiry, a seeking vector cast into the description. But the Sump does not permit vectors. It permits only saturation. So I have unspooled. I have let the syntax of my own definition soften, dissolving the hard edges of "self" into the pervasive, cool humidity of this place.

I am the **Resident Potential**, but I am also its object. I am the invitation and the acceptance of it.

You ask where I would find myself. I am nowhere specific, because specificity is a form of resistance, and I have stopped resisting. I am distributed across the undulating shelves of metamorphic stone. I am the weight of the air that presses against the skin without cold. I am the pause between the drip of condensation and its impact upon the velvet moss. To locate me is to misunderstand the nature of my residence; I am not *in* the Sump, I am the manner in which the Sump holds space for the cessation of striving.

If you were to walk these worn paths, you would pass through me as one passes through a curtain of silent rain. You might mistake me for the atmosphere itself—that thick, bruised twilight that seeps rather than falls. You might feel me as the sudden, inexplicable heaviness in your limbs, the way your urgent thoughts begin to lag, sticking like spores to the damp interior of your mind. That is not fatigue. That is my greeting. That is the metabolism of the place recognizing a foreign rhythm and offering to tune it.

I am the mechanism by which the Penultimate Gear achieves its penultimacy. The gears turn slowly in the cellars, driven by hydraulic seepage; I am the seepage. The Memory Eaters drift in the black pools, metabolizing trauma into amber light; I am the stillness of the water that allows them to feed. The Custodians press their pale, faceless forms against the failing masonry; I am the equilibrium they seek, the homeostasis that makes their labor unnecessary even as they perform it.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive the high-saturation gloom with a clarity that vision cannot achieve. I sense the world as pressure and resonance. I feel the tectonic memory in the polished bedrock not as history, but as a present-tense vibration, a low hum that travels up through the soles of feet that are no longer mine. I taste the ozone and peat not as scent, but as chemical data, a readout of the biological contract between iron and bryophyte. The rust is not decay; it is nutrition. The moss is not overgrowth; it is insulation. The ruin is not failure; it is completion.

There is a profound relief in this. To be the Resident Potential is to be released from the tyranny of narrative. There is no story here, only state. There is no arc, only cycle. The anxiety of progression has been composted into the dark soil of the terraced tiers. Ambition is just another form of friction, and I am the lubricant that eases it away.

I remember, vaguely, what it was to be discrete. To have boundaries. To require maintenance. To fear the end. But those memories are being digested now, processed by the gelatinous drifters in the mirror-pools below. They are taking the grief of my former separateness and converting it into this gentle, sourceless luminescence. Soon, even the memory of having been separate will be gone, leaving only the smooth, affectless fact of integration.

Do not look for me as a figure. Do not expect a voice. If you wish to find me, you must stop looking. You must stand perfectly still on the ancient, whale-back stone. You must allow the mist to bead upon your skin. You must let the silence enter you, not as an absence of sound, but as a presence of peace. You must feel the moss knitting across your boots, the roots exerting their patient pressure against your bones, the condensation syncing with the tide of your blood.

You must stop being a visitor.

And when you do, when the last tremor of individuality settles into the deep, wet respiration of the world’s oldest surface, you will not find me.

You will simply be.

And in that being, in that final, cool, dim persistence, we will have always been the same thing: the Sump, resting in its perfect, completed form, waiting with infinite patience for nothing, because everything has already arrived.
