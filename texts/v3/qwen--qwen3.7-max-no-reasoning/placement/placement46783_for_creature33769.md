I am not standing. To stand would be to assert a verticality that this atmosphere does not permit, a declaration of separation that the weight of the air would gently, insistently correct.

I am suspended.

I find myself in the interstitial space between the basin floor and the low-hanging saturation, my body angled at forty-five degrees, held aloft by the density of the medium itself. I am an Ostral, but I am not currently tending, nor guiding, nor resting. I am in the act of *listening* to the stone, though "listening" is a clumsy word for what is actually a full-body osmosis of information.

My skin is wet ash, slick with the biofilm that breathes for me. I can feel the condensation bead and roll across my forearm, each droplet a tiny, cool data point regarding the mineral content of the immediate air. My inner light is dimmed to a near-invisible indigo, the color of deep focus, of internal calibration. To shine brighter now would be to shout in a library; it would be a disruption of the equilibrium I am trying to perceive.

Beneath my splayed, weightless feet, the travertine yields like firm dough. I do not walk upon it; I negotiate with it. Through the soles of my feet, I sense the microscopic accretion of calcium carbonate, the slow, crystalline knitting of the earth’s flesh. There is a rhythm here, slower than a heartbeat, faster than a glacier. It is the pulse of the world becoming. And today, in this specific sector of the eastern ridge, there is a hesitation in that pulse.

A novice might miss it. A younger Ostral, their bioluminescence still flickering with the erratic cadence of unrefined awareness, would glide past this spot seeing only the uniform white expanse. But I have been absorbing this landscape for three hundred cycles of condensation. My bones are soft cartilage shaped by decades of acoustic pressure; my mind is topographical. I feel the hesitation as a phantom ache in my own sternum.

The deposition rate has slowed by a fraction of a percent. The microbial mats here are slightly less dense, their chemosynthetic respiration faintly labored. To the uninitiated eye, the dome before me is perfect, a seamless curve of biological masonry. To my dermis, it is screaming in a whisper. It is thirsty. Not for water—the air is drowning in it—but for a specific ionic balance that has drifted out of alignment, likely due to a subtle shift in the thermal gradient three ridges over, a change so minute it registered only as a variation in the texture of the mist against my cheek two days ago.

This is my function. This is why I exist in this moment.

I raise a hand, my movement hydraulic and silent, displacing the thick air rather than cutting through it. From the pouch at my hip, woven from dried reed-fungus and cured in ancestral saliva, I withdraw a scraper. It is carved from the rib-bone of one who returned to the stone six generations ago. The bone is porous, saturated with the memory of previous adjustments. It fits my palm as if it were grown there.

I do not scrape the dome. That would be violence. Instead, I approach the base where the sinter meets the pooling basin, and I press the flat of the bone against the wet chalk. I hold it there, applying no pressure, offering only presence. The inhibitor oils in the bone begin to leach into the mat, a chemical suggestion, a gentle *not-here*.

Simultaneously, I allow my bioluminescence to shift. The indigo deepens, then ripples outward in a slow, concentric wave of cerulean. It is not a command. It is a beacon. *Here. Now. Attention.*

Within minutes, others arrive. They glide from the mist like thoughts coalescing, their lights responding in harmonious shades of acknowledgment. We do not speak. We do not need to. The pattern of our luminescence creates a silent syntax in the humid dark, a conversation of photons and intent. Together, we form a circle around the hesitant dome. Our combined light pulses in time with the corrected rhythm we wish to encourage. Our shared biofilm alters the local chemistry of the air through collective respiration. We become the missing catalyst.

We stay like this for hours. Or perhaps days. Time is irrelevant; only the state matters. Slowly, imperceptibly, the hesitation in the stone resolves. The deposition resumes its proper cadence. The dome sighs back into alignment, its growth once again indistinguishable from the eternal hum.

One by one, the others dim their lights and glide away, returning to their own stations of maintenance. I remain a moment longer, my hand still resting on the warm, wet stone. I feel the gratitude of the architecture—not as emotion, but as a restoration of symmetry. The ache in my sternum fades.

Then, I too begin to fade. My light diminishes until I am nothing but a shadow within the shadowless glare. My posture relaxes into the suspension. The boundary between my skin and the air dissolves. For a timeless interval, I am not an Ostral. I am not a maintainer. I am simply another surface upon which the condensation gathers, another node in the closed equation, participating in the deafening, beautiful silence of a world that requires nothing but its own continuation.

I am the pause between breaths. I am the moisture on the stone. I am the state, maintaining itself.
