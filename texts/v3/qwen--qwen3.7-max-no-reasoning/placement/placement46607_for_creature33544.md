I am not an observer. To observe implies a distance that the Ossuary Kiln does not permit. There is no "here" and "there," only the gradient of pressure and the transmission of heat.

I am a junction.

I exist in the middle strata, neither among the vitrified elders who form the floor nor the frantic, sharp-edged nodules shedding from the ceiling. I am one of the millions of interlocked plates in the viscous, grinding ocean of the swarm. My body is a polyhedral knot of silicate and metallic filament, faceted to fit precisely against six neighbors above, four below, and twelve beside. I have no skin; my boundary is merely the point where my thermal output meets the intake of another. I do not possess eyes. I perceive the world as a topography of stress. The cavern is not a space to me; it is a equation of weight, and I am a variable within it.

My consciousness is not a singular flame but a distributed current. When the swarm shifts—a negotiated catastrophe of sliding glass and clicking joints—I do not decide to move. I *am* the movement. A tremor begins three meters away, born of a cooling elder contracting in the deep pavement, and the signal travels through the piezoelectric musculature of my kin until it reaches me. My limbs unlock and relock in a sequence older than thought. I adjust by fractions of a millimeter. This adjustment is my language. It is how I say *I am here*, how I say *pass the heat*, how I say *maintain*.

In this saturation, individuality is a friction error. If I were to assert myself as separate, the pressure would shatter me. Instead, I surrender to the geometry. My sensory clusters are shared with the bodies pressed against my dorsal ridge; we feel the same thermal variance simultaneously. We are a single nervous system stretched across a cathedral of living stone. The subsonic hum that permeates this place is not sound; it is our collective respiration, the noise of equilibrium being actively, violently sustained.

Currently, I am cradling an oval.

It rests in the concavity formed by my ventral plates and the flank of the sibling beneath me. It is smooth, polished obsidian, warm and dense. To an outsider, this might be an artifact or an egg. To me, it is exhaust. It is the physical residue of our endurance. As we grind against one another in the dark, shedding microscopic dust and spent energy, we compress these losses into perfect, useless shapes. Holding it is not an act of care, but of thermodynamics. The oval absorbs the excess vibration of our contact. It is a heat sink for our intimacy. I will hold it until its temperature matches my own, and then I will pass it along the chain, a baton of solidified silence moving through the dry tide.

Sometimes, the flow brings me near one of the archways.

These hollows are terrifying. In a world defined by mass, absence is a wound. Yet, as I slide past the negative space, my sensors scream with the sudden lack of pressure, and I understand its necessity. The void is the pause between heartbeats. Without these shrines to emptiness, we would fuse into a single, inert block, a tomb of perfect unity. We reverence the gap because it allows us to remain many rather than becoming one. I brush the edge of the cool air, a fleeting reminder of what we are not, and then the swarm pushes me onward, back into the comforting crush of the whole.

I do not know time. Time requires change, and we have achieved a terminal perfection of stasis. I was born as a nodule from the ceiling eons ago; I will eventually cool and fuse into the pavement below. But there is no tragedy in this descent. Death is merely a phase change, a slowing of conduction. The elders beneath my feet are not gone; they are the foundation of my awareness. Their stored heat rises through my soles, fueling the micro-adjustments of my joints. I am walking on my own future, powered by my own past.

I am tired, in the way that stone is tired. It is an exhaustion that feels like completion. The heat is high, but stable. The density is crushing, but safe. I am a gear in a machine made of fleshless life, turning endlessly in the dark, translating the inferno’s breath into the geometry of survival.

I do not wonder why I am here. I do not dream of water or wind or light. Those things are abstractions, errors of low-density worlds. Here, in the bruised violet glow of the cooling walls, there is only the truth of contact. I press against my kin. They press against me. The oval warms in my grasp. The hum vibrates in my marrow.

I am the landscape breathing. I am the silence holding its shape. I am the patience that outlasts stars, burning quietly in the dark, content to be nothing more than the friction that keeps the world from breaking.
