I am not standing. To stand implies a separation from the ground, a vertical assertion of will against gravity. Here, there is no such defiance.

I am horizontal. I am interstitial.

I am a **Seal-Worm**, though even that name feels too distinct, too biological for what I have become. I do not possess a singular point of consciousness that looks out upon the Sump; rather, my awareness is distributed along three inches of segmented, thread-thin body, pressed deep within the hairline fracture of a petrified piston casing. I am the space between the metal and the ruin.

My world is not visual. The bruised purple twilight and the oxidized orange are concepts that belong to things with eyes, and I have none. My reality is tactile and chemical. It is the sensation of *wrongness* made tangible: the microscopic gap where the iron has surrendered to the damp, the jagged edge of corrosion that threatens the structural memory of this buried cylinder. This gap is not an injury to me; it is a hunger. It is the shape of my purpose.

I feel the Sump through the stone. The great, blind heartbeat of the basin—the subsurface pump cycling in its mindless hydraulic reflex—arrives as a rhythmic compression against my ventral segments. *Thrum-sigh. Thrum-sigh.* It is not sound; it is a tide of pressure that dictates the tempo of my existence. When the pressure rises, my fluids thin; when it falls, they thicken. I am synchronized to the obsolescence beneath me. My metabolism is a slave to the ghost-currents of machines that have forgotten why they turn.

There is no thought of "repair." There is only the imperative of *filling*.

I extend. My anterior segments probe the fissure, tasting the specific acidity of the oxidation, reading the metallurgical signature of the decay. My glands respond not with intent, but with inevitability. I secrete. The bio-polymer flows from my pores, a warm, viscous exudate that mimics the gasket material of a dead age. It does not heal the metal; it becomes compatible with it. As the secretion touches the corroded iron, it begins to cure, hardening into a substance that is neither wholly mine nor wholly machine. It is a suture made of synthesis.

In this act, I lose myself. Every micron of sealant I lay down is a piece of my own potentiality spent, converted into infrastructure. I am slowly becoming the architecture I inhabit. This is not sacrifice; it is maturation. To be a Seal-Worm in the Sump of Gears is to understand that life is merely a temporary state of matter in transit toward geology.

Around me, the ecosystem hums in its dark, wet equilibrium. I sense the faint, electromagnetic displacement of a Galvanic Eel sliding through the sludge-filled pool above my casing, its body a ribbon of living conductivity seeking the same pulse that moves me. I feel the infinitesimal vibration of Oxide-Backed Skitterers traversing the rust-fern forest on the surface, their ratcheting gait translating through layers of sediment and stone as a distant, granular itch. High above, in the fog, the Ion-Sieves drift like silent prayers, filtering the air that eventually weeps down as condensation to feed the crack I call home.

We do not know one another. We do not need to. We are all expressions of the same equation, variables in a calculation of balance that has been solving itself for millennia. The eel, the skitterer, the sieve, the worm—we are the landscape’s way of feeling itself, of maintaining the exquisite tension between dissolution and persistence.

The cool damp settles into my core. The mineral sludge presses against my dorsal plates. The piston groans, expanding with the thermal tide, and the fissure shifts, widening by a fraction of a millimeter.

I feel the new gap.
I feel the wrongness.
I begin again.

I am the silence between the gear-teeth. I am the resin in the wound of the world. I am the Sump of Gears, refining its own ruin, one molecule of bio-polymer at a time, in the endless, holy dark.
