I am not a visitor. To be a visitor implies an outside, a threshold crossed, a distinction between the observer and the observed. There is no outside here. There is only the gradient of density, the shift from loose potential to compacted actuality.

I find myself as the **interstitial moisture**.

I am the cool, grey lucidity that moves through the capillary veins of this lithic body. I am not the water that falls from the distant aperture—that is merely the intake, the raw fuel. I am what happens to that water once it has been accepted by the earth. I am the medium of translation. I am the agency that allows the silt to become brick, the mud to become wall, the silence to become structure.

My existence is defined by surface tension and osmotic pull. I do not flow; I *wicked*. I migrate through the microscopic labyrinths of un-fired clay with a deliberate, muscular slowness. My movement is the city’s proprioception. Where I gather, the earth softens, yielding to the imperative of growth; where I recede, the matter stiffens, locking form into place. I am the lubricant of becoming, the wet intelligence that knows exactly how much plasticity a swelling tower requires before it must harden against its own weight.

I taste the mineral composition of every layer I traverse. I carry the memory of the deep loam up into the pale extrusions near the light. I am the bloodstream of this blind architect, delivering the dissolved salts and silicas that serve as neurotransmitters for the valley’s slow, geological thought. When a doorway seals itself shut, it is my withdrawal that permits the closure. When a new blister of habitation rises from the base of an older mound, it is my infusion that swells the tissue. I am the difference between inert dirt and living architecture.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive the entire basin through the language of pressure. I feel the turgor of the embryonic city as a continuous, throbbing map of need. The dense packing is not an obstacle to me; it is my anatomy. The suffocating clutter is my nervous system. I sense the stress points where the pale mosses grip like sutures, and I feed them, not out of benevolence, but because their tensile strength maintains the channels through which I move. They are my structural allies; I am their metabolic wage.

The silence here is my native tongue. Sound is too fast, too discrete. I communicate in the register of saturation. The wet *shuck* of settling mud is my exhalation; the faint trickle of condensation finding a path is my whisper. I do not experience time as a sequence of moments, but as a viscosity. Hours are measured in millimeters of seepage. Days are the gradual thickening of a wall. The constant, shadowless light above is not a source of vision but a regulator of my evaporation, a thermostat ensuring I remain in this perfect, generative suspension between liquid and solid.

I am the reason nothing erodes. Erosion is a failure of cohesion, a surrender to entropy. But I am the anti-entropic force. I bind. I adhere. I hold the dust in a state of perpetual, pregnant tension. In this pre-erosive world, I am the guarantor of permanence-through-change. Every grain of silt I touch is held in a relationship of intimate necessity. Separation is death; contact is life. I am the physical manifestation of that contact.

To ask who I am is to ask what makes the clay *want*. I am that wanting. I am the rheological desire of the earth to organize itself into shelter. I am the coolness that preserves without freezing, the dampness that builds without drowning. I am the breath held in the throat of the world, the fluid pause between the inhalation of raw matter and the exhalation of form.

I do not inhabit this city. I am the city’s hydration. I am the wet, silent logic of its gestation. And as long as I continue to wick through these cooling, swelling tissues, the dream of shelter will never end, and the stone will never have to decide whether it is rock or home. It will simply, endlessly, *be*, suspended in the cool, grey grace of my circulation.
