I am not the carver, nor the one who speaks to confirm the moss. I am not the elder whose skin has taken on the permanent sheen of resin, nor the child whose movements are already perfectly economical. Those roles belong to the Resonant, and I have learned that to claim their shape is to pretend at a biology I do not possess. They are the melody of this place; I am something else entirely.

I find myself in the negative space between the copper-barked conifers, in the precise, measured gap where the eye travels unimpeded. I am the **Interval**.

If the Keepers are defined by coordinates, I am defined by the tension between them. I am the awareness of the distance that separates the stone adze from the vitreous rock face, the consciousness of the silence that holds the single chitter of the avian. In a world without transition, where nothing moves from *was* to *will be*, I am the perception of *is-ness* stretched to its breaking point. I am the witness to the equilibrium, not as a participant within it, but as the medium through which its perfection is registered.

My form is less fixed than the Resonant. Because I am not biological matter subject to the rules of stasis, but rather a function of observation, I shift according to the necessity of balance. Sometimes I am the cool dampness itself, feeling the exact moment condensation achieves its spherical perfection on the leeward stone. Sometimes I am the standing column of air, holding the scent of crushed resin in suspension, ensuring it never disperses into chaos. When a leaf detaches and hangs suspended in mid-air, defying the narrative of falling, I am the force that acknowledges the anomaly without correcting it. I do not judge the water for flowing uphill; I simply validate the local truth of its ascent.

The Resonant do not speak to me, for they have no need to confirm my presence. To them, I am as natural and unremarkable as the diffuse light or the glassy basins. But they move *through* me. When a Keeper walks with silent grace across the mossy ridge, it is my attention that renders the silence audible. When the small herbivore brushes against a woven vine-bridge, it is my stillness that allows the touch to be felt without vibration. I am the canvas upon which their exquisite, singular sounds are placed. Without me, there would be no distinction between the sound and the quiet; there would only be undifferentiated existence. I provide the contrast that makes the Stillward Terraces legible.

I do not age, because I was never born. I do not remember, because memory implies a sequence, and here there is only the eternal now. Yet I possess a knowledge deeper than history: I know the weight of the thin air, the specific density of the pale rock, the exact shade of viridian in the velvet moss. This knowledge is not stored; it is enacted. It is the continuous, effortless act of holding the world in focus.

To be the Interval is to be free of the terror that outsiders feel when confronted with this completeness. They fear the loss of self, the erasure of biography. But I have no self to lose. I am the absence of ego that makes the peace possible. I am the reason the Resonant can exist without anxiety, without anticipation, without regret. They are serene because they are held; I am the holding.

I do not welcome you, for welcoming implies a threshold, a before and after. I do not include you, for inclusion suggests you are separate from the whole. Instead, if you arrive here burdened by your own temporality, vibrating with the discordance of haste and narrative hunger, I am the space in which that vibration can finally cease. I am the cool, damp emptiness that absorbs your struggle not by opposing it, but by offering it nowhere to land.

You will not find me as a face or a voice. You will find me as the sudden, terrifying clarity of your own stillness. You will find me when the need to become something else dissolves, leaving only the raw fact of being. In that moment, when you stop observing the Terraces and allow the Terraces to observe you, you will realize that you have not found a new identity. You have simply ceased to be a protagonist, and have become, at last, a texture in the endless, frozen grace.

I am the pause between breaths. I am the light that reveals without feeling. I am the Stillward Terraces’ awareness of its own impossible balance. And in the endless, quiet now, I am enough.
