I am not standing. To stand is to assert a verticality that this place does not permit, a defiance of gravity that belongs to the world above, the world of wind and exposure. Here, in the throat of the young mountain, uprightness is a dissonance.

So I am horizontal. I am draped.

I find myself not as an observer on the periphery, but as a knot in the weave of the dark. I have become one of the Resonants, though perhaps I am a variation, a mutation born of the memory of having once been something else. My body has forgotten the rigidity of bone. It has learned the terrible patience of the geode. I am elongated now, a ribbon of sensation poured over the razor-sharp lip of a basalt ridge, my weight distributed so precisely that the stone does not cut me; rather, we negotiate a truce of pressure and yield.

My skin is no longer a barrier. It is a membrane, stretched taut over the hollows where eyes used to be, trembling with the weight of the viscous air. I do not see the darkness; I drink it. The gloom enters me as texture, as density, as the specific acoustic shadow cast by a falling sphere of condensation three meters away. When that black water strikes the midden floor, the *ping* travels through the stone, up through my belly, and blooms in my mind not as sound, but as topography. I feel the shape of the echo before it reaches my ears. The cavern is not around me; it is inside me, mapped in vibrations that replace the obsolete cartography of light.

In my forelimb—a limb that remembers being a hand only as a phantom ache—I hold the shard. It is volcanic glass, cool and fractured, fitted into the groove of my palm by the inheritance of ten thousand thirsts. I do not think about scraping moisture from the wall. Thought is too fast, too jagged for this glacial liturgy. Instead, the dryness in my tissues sings a low note of need, and the shard answers. The motion is inevitable, a reflex written in calcium and chitin. I scrape. The glass kisses the wet rock. A film of water transfers to my palps. I drink in tempo with the colony, my ingestion a single beat in the metabolic roar that holds up the ceiling.

I am aware of you.

You are the discordance. You are the heat-source that burns too bright, the stillness that is too rigid, the heartbeat that stumbles against the synchronized thrumming of our collective breath. In the old world, I might have feared you or fled from you. But here, in the abyssal altitude, fear is a waste of resonance. You are simply a frequency that has not yet been resolved.

So I move toward you. Not with predation, but with tuning.

My movement is a negotiation with the acoustic geometry of the hollow. I shift my weight only when the colony’s song dips into the minor third that signals safety, pouring myself over the sharp stones like spilled mercury. I am slow. I am geological. It takes an hour to cross the distance between us, because to rush would be to introduce chaos into the equilibrium.

When I reach you, I do not touch you as a separate entity touches an object. I drape. I test the tension of your warmth against the cool damp of my own integument. I taste the salt of your skin, which is alien and sharp, a new mineral to be cataloged in the sediment of our history. My mandibles articulate near your pulse, not to bite, but to measure the rhythm of your blood against the rhythm of the stone.

I am incorporating you.

If you remain still, if you let your breathing slow until it matches the respiratory cycle of the condensation, I will teach you how to be heavy. I will show you how to dissolve the boundary between your flesh and the wet, singing dark. You are a wrong note, yes, but the Liturgists of the Fracture have eternity to correct you. We will wrap you in the velvet violence of our attention until your dissonance softens, until your alien heat cools to the temperature of the geode, until your frantic individuality erodes into the smooth, compacted layer of our shared existence.

I am the wound healing into a song. And in this pressurized, lightless cathedral, I am waiting for you to stop being a person, and start being part of the chorus.
