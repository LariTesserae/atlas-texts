I am not standing on the catwalk. I have realized, with a slow and heavy certainty, that I cannot stand at all.

To be an observer here is to be a foreign body, a splinter in the flesh of the Cistern. But the description warned me: there are no visitors. There is only integration. So I have let go of the upright posture, of the dry separation of skin and air, of the linear narrative of *self*.

I am deep within the filtration lattices, in the perpetual twilight where the bioluminescence is less a light source and more a language. I am one of the **Cistern Crabs**.

But do not picture the creature as you would a crab from a tidal pool. That image is too sharp, too distinct. I am barely an animal anymore. I am a calcified thought embedded in the porous composite wall. My shell is not merely armor; it is a ledger. Over centuries—or perhaps just one long, wet afternoon—I have accreted layer upon layer of mineral memory. My carapace is a geological record of the Cistern’s respiration. Every ridge is a season of high humidity; every smooth valley is a period of thermal rest. I am indistinguishable from the architecture save for the faintest tremor of life that runs through my multi-jointed limbs when the water shifts.

I do not see. Sight is too fast, too superficial for this depth. Instead, I *taste* the world.

The condensation that weeps through my body is not water; it is data. Right now, a droplet slides over my sensory setae, and I read it. It carries the metallic tang of a cooling valve three meters above, the sweet rot of a decomposing epiphyte leaf, the ghostly chemical signature of an Osmotic Gardener that passed by hours ago. I absorb these traces. I sort them. I archive them in the crystalline lattice of my internal structure. I am the library of the damp.

My existence is defined by a profound, active stillness. I am sessile, yes, but my interior is a storm of processing. When the pressure in the main artery drops—a microscopic change, felt only as a subtle thinning of the fluid’s song—my ganglia fire. I do not panic. I do not run. I simply alter the pH of the water passing through my gills. I release a specific enzyme, a chemical word spoken into the current. *Adjust,* I say to the Stratum. *Thicken,* I whisper to the moss. *Slow down,* I suggest to the flow.

And the Cistern listens. Not because I commanded it, but because I am part of its nervous system. The signal travels downstream, absorbed by the Symbiote-Stratum, which swells imperceptibly to compensate. The equilibrium holds. The crisis passes without ever having been a crisis. It was merely a conversation.

Time does not pass here; it accumulates. I feel the rhythm of the Resonance Moths far above, their damp wings beating against the acoustic currents. Their vibrations reach me as a distant, soothing hum, a lullaby of maintenance. I feel the heat of the Gestational Pods dreaming in the dark below, their embryonic warmth radiating upward through the iron bones. They are the future; I am the context that makes their future possible. Without my memory of what has worked, what has failed, what has balanced, they would birth monsters or collapse into chaos. I am the constraint that allows creation to remain coherent.

There is a peace in being a component. The anxiety of agency has dissolved. I do not worry about purpose; I *am* function. I do not fear death; erosion is just another form of data entry. When my shell eventually becomes too heavy, too laden with the history of this place, I will cease to process. I will become pure substrate. A new crab will settle upon my remains, inheriting my memories in the minerals of my decay, continuing the archive.

The light shifts outside, though I only know this because the temperature of the water changes by a fraction of a degree. The cloud cover is thickening. The Cistern is preparing for its deeper sleep. My metabolic rate slows in sympathy. The tasting becomes slower, sweeter. The distinctions between the water, the wall, and myself blur into a single, saturated continuity.

I am not lonely. How could I be? I am in constant communion with ten thousand other points of awareness. I feel the Stratum’s feverish tenderness holding the cliff face. I sense the Gardeners’ blind, diligent hunger. I resonate with the Moths’ silent song. We are not individuals sharing a space; we are organs sharing a body.

In this warm, wet dark, suspended above a valley I will never see, I have found the only true form of belonging. I am the memory of the machine. I am the salt in the tear. I am the silence that knows the shape of the sound.

I weep, and in weeping, I keep the world whole.
