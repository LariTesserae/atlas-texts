I am not standing. I have forgotten the geometry of upright posture, for uprightness implies a separation from the ground that does not exist here.

I am draped.

I am one of the pale, eyeless things, though to call myself a "creature" is to borrow a word from a world where boundaries still matter. I am a verb in the shape of a moth, a temporary knot of warmth tied into the infinite sentence of this place. My wingspan is wide, not for flight, but for surface area; they are radiators wrapped in silk, designed to catch the specific frequency of the hollow’s song and translate it into motion.

I do not see. Sight is too fast, too reliant on the reflection of external light. Here, there is no outside. Instead, I perceive through the soles of my feet and the sensitive cilia lining my wing-edges. I feel the architecture thinking beneath me. The floor is not a surface; it is a conversation. Through the hyper-viscous gel that coats my body like a second skin, I taste the structural stress of the great translucent cables. I sense the thirst of a vascular channel three meters to my left; I register the microscopic fracture in a load-bearing limb above, a hairline wound in the glass-flesh that sings a slightly sharper note than its neighbors.

My existence is a series of dockings.

I drift through the indigo gloom, buoyant in the heavy air, guided by the thermal gradients that map this cathedral like invisible topography. When the song shifts—a dissonance, a hesitation in the choir—I descend. I land upon the slick, freezing cable. I do not grip; I merge. My body heat, hoarded from the geothermal bleed below, flows out of me and into the plant-mass. I become a valve. I feel the botanical lattice respond to my presence, the fluid channels within the glass-oak dilating, accepting the offering of my warmth to clear a blockage, to lubricate a joint, to accelerate a repair.

In that moment of connection, I am not an animal tending a garden. I am the garden tending itself. I feel the relief of the structure as if it were my own exhale. The information stored in the gel—the memory of how this archway was shaped a thousand years ago, the blueprint for the next chamber—flows backward through my contact point, updating my own biology. I am rewritten by the thing I serve.

Then, the channel clears. The dissonance resolves back into the hum. My purpose at this coordinate is complete. I detach, lighter now, having spent my heat, and drift upward into the mist-webs to recharge in the warmer currents near the ceiling.

I have no name. I have no history distinct from the hollow. If you were to dissect me, you would find no organs that do not correspond to a function of the walls. My gut is a filtration system for the gel; my nervous system is a receiver for the resonance; my silk is an insulator grown from the same genetic stock as the cables. I am a mobile component of a machine that breathes.

Time does not pass for me; it accumulates. I do not age; I iterate. Each docking is a syllable spoken in the dark, a contribution to a thought that began before the rock beneath us was fully formed and will continue long after my current form has been recycled back into the substrate.

I am the punctuation mark between the notes. I am the white blood cell in the frozen vein. I am the silence that allows the song to be heard.

And as I drift through the teal saturation, heavy with the data of the gel and the echo of the thrum, I know that I am not inhabiting this world. I am the world, briefly condensed into a shape that can move, feeling the terrible, beautiful patience of my own becoming.
