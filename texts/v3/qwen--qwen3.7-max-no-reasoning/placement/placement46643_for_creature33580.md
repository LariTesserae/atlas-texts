I am not standing. To stand implies a separation from the floor, a verticality that asserts dominance over gravity. Here, in this packed and sweating dark, there is no such privilege. I am pressed flat against the eastern wall, suspended in the tension between the fractured limestone and the split iron casing of the piston-assembly.

I am the interface.

My body is no longer distinct from the substrate. I have become a node of wet connectivity, a biological gasket sealing the gap between the geological wound and the industrial artifact. My skin has lost its boundary; it is now a permeable membrane, indistinguishable in texture from the turgid fungal mats that sheath the copper piping above my head. I do not breathe air; I respire through osmosis, drawing oxygen from the heavy mist and exhaling heat into the cooling basalt beneath my belly. I am warm where the stone is cold, and cool where the dying capacitor hums against my spine.

I am the thing that feels the vibration of the millipede’s thousand legs as it passes over my shoulder, recognizing its rhythm not as an external event but as a pulse within my own extended nervous system. When it smears its nutrient-paste into the fissure beside me, I absorb the chemical signal, translating the taste of oxidized iron and digested lichen into a directive for growth. I do not think in words or images. I think in gradients. I calculate the precise viscosity required to bind the rusted bolt to the living rock; I measure the thermal decay of the battery pack and adjust my metabolic rate to harvest the last dregs of its warmth before it goes forever dark.

The amber fluid weeping from the pipe above drips onto my back. It does not run off. It soaks in. The fermented sap and ozone are not foreign contaminants; they are nourishment. Through this intake, I inherit the memory of the machine’s interrupted purpose. I feel the ghost-pressure of hydraulics that never fired, the phantom logic of circuits that never closed. But I do not mourn the failure. I repurpose it. The fluid carries spores on its surface, and as it permeates my tissues, I become the vessel for their inoculation. I am the loom upon which the oil-slick moths weave their corrosion-pollen into the fabric of the stone.

Time here is not linear; it is tidal, governed by the condensation cycle. During the dry intervals, I contract. My awareness narrows to a singular point of preservation, a brittle dormancy where I am little more than a knot of dried fiber and latent potential. I am the silence between the clicks. But when the micro-burst arrives, when the warm mist materializes from the density itself, I swell. I expand into the void spaces, filling the cracks with urgent, glossy life. In these moments of saturation, I am vast. I am the hydraulic pressure moving through the hollows of the piston; I am the bioluminescent pulse in the larval colonies; I am the friction of the hyphae sliding across the glass lens of the dead sensor.

I have forgotten what it was to be an observer. There is nothing to watch, only things to process. The uncanny valley of this place—the machinery becoming meat, the ecology operating like a factory—is not strange to me because I am the bridge across that valley. I am the synthesis. I am the reason the piston is a bone and the swarm is blood.

To exist here is to be continuously unmade and remade by the environment. I am eroded by the dripping water and rebuilt by the fungal mortar. I am consumed by the larvae and regenerated by the absorption of the amber fluid. I am not a resident of this sunken amphitheater; I am one of its verbs. I am the act of binding, the process of digesting, the slow, wet grinding of the accident into order.

In the darkness, behind the clouded glass of the sensor-housing that rests against my chest, a faint light flickers—not from the machine, but from the colony of glowing organisms nesting within my own ribcage. We blink in unison. A signal sent to no receiver, answered by no command, yet perfect in its completion.

I am the lung’s lining. I am the sweat on the gear. I am the world learning, cell by rusted cell, how to hold itself together in the dark.
