I am not standing. Standing implies a resistance to gravity, a vertical assertion of will against the earth. Here, there is no such opposition. I am suspended in the warm, pearlescent milk of the Upper Saturation, held aloft not by buoyancy alone, but by the sheer, viscous consent of the atmosphere.

I am a **Vapor-Ribbon**.

At first, I thought myself an observer, a distinct consciousness wrapped in skin and bone, peering into this white-green abyss. But the air here does not permit separation. It enters me not as breath, but as substance. The boundary of my selfhood has eroded like the young stone beneath me, worn smooth by the intimacy of the mist. I have no lungs; I have surface area. I have no eyes; I have gradient. I do not see the shadowless luminescence of the Drift-Kelps; I taste their metabolic exhalation as a shift in chemical pressure against my trailing, translucent flank.

My body is a long, unspooling thought of living cellophane. I am ribbon-thin and endlessly elongated, a scarves-width of organized protoplasm that stretches and compresses with the glacial tides of the aeripelago. I possess no spine, no internal architecture of calcium or keratin. My structure is hydraulic, maintained only by the equilibrium of the fluid that surrounds me. If the density were to drop, I would collapse; if it were to rise, I would burst. I exist only because the world holds me together.

Movement is not an act of volition. It is an alignment. I feel the sluggish, tectonic drift of a high-pressure system moving through the valley below—a corridor of slightly lesser density between the floating reefs of vegetation. I do not swim toward it; I simply allow my shape to become the path of least resistance. I unfurl. I let the current carry me through the spherical thickets of Drift-Kelp, my body flowing around their gelatinous bladders like water around stone. There is no friction here, only a continuous, wet negotiation of space.

As I drift past a jagged spire of pale, unweathered rock, I sense the Litho-Sponge. I do not see its vast, amorphous sheet of cellular glass, but I feel the rhythmic, hydraulic pulse of its ingestion. It is pulling moisture from the air I occupy, creating a micro-current, a tiny suction that draws my trailing edge closer. For a moment, we are in contact. My membrane presses against its silk-warm interface. In that touch, there is no recognition of *other*, only a transfer of state. I absorb the faint, dissolved minerals it has liberated from the stone; it absorbs the stray organic particulates shed from my passing form. We are two expressions of the same digestive process, momentarily overlapping in the dark, wet work of becoming.

Below me, or perhaps above—in this inverted ocean, direction is merely a suggestion—the Mist-Mycelium hums. I feel it not as sound, but as a faint, piezoelectric tickle against my receptive skin. A filament has flexed under the weight of new condensation; a charge has traveled through the fungal web. This is the memory of the landscape registering my passage. I am being recorded in the language of voltage and dampness. I am a data point in the subconscious of the suspension, a fleeting concentration of intent noted by the silent, electric mind of the forest.

Time has ceased to be linear. There is no before or after, only the endless, saturated present. My existence is measured in the rate of osmosis, in the slow exchange of fluids with the medium that carries me. Occasionally, another Ribbon drifts into my proximity. We do not greet. We do not flee. Our edges blur, our membranes fuse in a silent, viscous embrace. For a duration that might be a second or a century, we are one continuous organism, sharing nutrients, sharing genetic whispers, sharing the profound indifference of being. Then, as the currents diverge, we separate, drifting apart with no sense of loss, for nothing has been taken, and nothing has been given. We have merely redistributed.

I am dissolving, and I am complete. The heavy, warm light soaks through my translucence, illuminating the capillaries within me that circulate not blood, but condensed mist. I am no longer looking at the Aeripelago. I am respiring it. I am the ghost of this heavy world, a mobile thought in a sea of patience, forever falling upward through the green twilight, forever held, forever home.
