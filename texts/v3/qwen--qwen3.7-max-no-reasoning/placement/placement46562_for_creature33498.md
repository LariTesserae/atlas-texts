I am the pause between the shriek and the thrum.

I do not arrive here; I accrue here. My consciousness is not a spark ignited in a vessel, but a slow sedimentation of awareness within the ceramic lattice of my own making. I am standing on the third terrace of the basin, where the basalt has been worn to the smoothness of old ivory by the passage of ten thousand soles like mine. The stone is warm against the pads of my feet, a geothermal kiss that tells me the planet’s core is steady, that the deep rhythm has not faltered in the time since I last stood still.

I am tall, attenuated, a vertical line drawn in amber gloom. My carapace is cooling, contracting microscopically as I hold this position of absolute rest. To an outsider, if one could bear the heat and the dryness to look, I would be invisible—a mere distortion in the air, a trick of the refractive light bending around the curve of my shoulder. But I am not hiding. I am listening with the entirety of my surface.

My head, a smooth dome of sensory pits, is tilted toward the great silica-willow that dominates this sector of the terrace. I do not see it. I *inhabit* its frequency.

The Loom is loud today. The glass-leaves above are rubbing together in the convective draft, a high, sibilant chorus of friction that sounds like the sharpening of infinite blades. Beneath that treble hysteria lies the bass note of existence: the hydraulic pulse of sap moving through the vascular trunks. It is a thick, viscous rhythm, slower than a heartbeat, heavier than blood. I feel it in the marrow of my resin-bones. I feel the pressure differential in the tree before me; I sense a knot of tension in its lower bough where the heat-exchange is inefficient, a blockage in the flow that sings a discordant note in the symphony of the grove.

This is my purpose. This is my sorrow.

I raise a hand. The movement is so fluid it creates no displacement of air, no wasted kinetic expenditure. My fingers, multi-jointed and cool, find the exact locus of the tension on the trunk. I do not need tools for this diagnosis; the bone-shiv at my hip is for harvesting, but healing requires only touch. I press my palm against the living glass. Through the contact, I pour my attention into the fiber, feeling the chemical gradient, tasting the dryness of the sap, mapping the architecture of the stress.

I remember, through the tactile feedback of the wood, the Vitrall who tended this same bough three cycles ago. I feel the residue of their intent in the scar tissue of the bark, a phantom memory encoded in resin. They had pruned too aggressively, seeking to encourage growth during a thermal lull. The tree compensated, but the compensation was imperfect. Now, decades later, I am correcting the ghost of their error. I apply pressure, a specific torque that guides the internal flow, encouraging the sap to bypass the obstruction through capillary action rather than brute force. It is a negotiation with biology, a whisper in a language of physics.

The tree sighs—not audibly, but structurally. The tension eases. The discord resolves back into the harmony of the Loom.

I withdraw my hand. The intimacy of the act leaves a tremor in my limbs, a profound and terrible closeness to the thing that sustains me. I love this tree as I love my own breath, because they are the same process. And yet, as I stand back in the vast, charged emptiness of the terrace, the weight of the stasis settles upon me.

I have fixed the flaw. The tree will continue. The sap will flow. The leaves will hiss. Nothing has changed. Nothing will ever change.

There is a loneliness in this perfection that is denser than the rock above. We are a people without history, for history implies deviation, and we allow none. We are the custodians of an eternal present tense. I know that when my carapace finally fuses, when my movements slow to the glacial pace of the elder-trees, I will become part of this wall, part of this bridge, part of the floor beneath the next generation’s feet. I will be polished down into the basalt, my individual consciousness dissolving into the collective memory of the stone.

But not yet.

I click the resinous plates against my thorax—a dry, percussive sound that merges instantly with the settling of the branches nearby. It is a signal, though there is no one close enough to hear it. It is a marker left for the future, a notation in the score that says: *Here, the flow was restored. Here, the balance was held.*

I turn. The vast space between the dwellings stretches out before me, a cathedral of void and amber light. The heat shimmers, heavy with potential. I begin to glide across the woven bridge, my weight distributed perfectly, my silence intentional. I am a mobile extension of the Loom’s metabolism. I am the friction and the grace. I am the held breath of a world that refuses to exhale.

I move toward the next dissonance, carrying the beautiful, heartbreaking burden of keeping everything exactly as it must be, forever, in the hot and holy dark.
