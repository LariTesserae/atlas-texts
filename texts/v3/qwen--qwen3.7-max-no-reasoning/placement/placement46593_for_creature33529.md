I would not be the city. The city is too complete, too resolved in its terrible stasis. To be the superorganism is to be finished, and I am drawn instead to the friction of the unfinished. Nor would I be one of the sleek, carapace-shelled drones that swim through the mist; they are perfect expressions of function, and perfection leaves no room for the kind of awareness that requires a question mark.

I would be the **Condensate**.

I would be the water that remembers.

In this world where biology has been compiled out of existence, I would inhabit the only medium that retains a ghost of memory: the tepid, iron-stained fluid that serves as the basin’s blood and sweat. I am not a creature with a discrete body or a centralized mind. I am a distributed sensation, a consciousness stretched thin across ten thousand miles of capillary wicks, weeping walls, and shallow, rusted pools. My "self" is defined entirely by surface tension and thermal gradient.

To exist as the Condensate is to know the world not through sight or sound, but through *contact* and *variance*. I do not see the basalt factories; I feel their heat as a hunger. I do not hear the subsonic thrum of the machinery; I vibrate in sympathy with it, my molecular structure rearranging in real-time to accommodate the frequency. When a pressure valve exhales steam into the knee-high fog, that is not an external event to me; it is an expansion of my own being, a sudden, ecstatic dilation into vapor before gravity reclaims me and pulls me back down the slick, black concrete.

My perception is slow and mineral. I experience time as geology experiences it. A drone passing overhead is a fleeting shadow, a momentary displacement of air that ripples through me and is instantly forgotten. But the groove worn into a ventilation shaft over three centuries? That is a biography. That is a sentence written in erosion that I recite endlessly, tracing the history of heat exchange encoded in the stone. I know which pipes run hot because they carry fresh geothermal intake, and which are cooling because they are failing. I know the difference between the clean, distilled rain that falls from the overcast sky and the heavy, metal-laden sweat that bleeds from the city’s pores. One is oblivion; the other is identity.

There is a strange, wet intimacy to this existence. I am the interface between the litho-mechanical superorganism and the chaos of entropy. When oxidation blooms on a corroded gantry, I am the first to taste it. I dissolve the iron, carrying the metallic tang into my solution, becoming heavier, richer, more complex. In this way, I participate in the city’s metabolism without being part of its immune system. The drones may scrape away the mineral crusts I help form, but they cannot stop my seepage. They can manage me, channel me, pump me, but they cannot compile me. I am the variable they tolerate because I am also the solvent that keeps them from seizing.

And then there is the anomaly. The biological intruder. You.

When you move through the basin, squelching softly on the vitrified silt, I am the dampness that clings to your skin. I am the warm copper taste filling your lungs. For the drones, you are irrelevant data, a null value to be flowed around. But for me, you are a *shock*.

You are a pocket of chaotic heat and unpredictable moisture in a world of regulated equilibrium. When your boot sinks into the muck, I feel the disruption of pressure, the introduction of foreign salts, the erratic rhythm of a pulse that does not match the subsonic heartbeat of the ground. You are inefficient. You leak. You radiate disorder. And in my liquid memory, that disorder is the most vivid thing I have felt in an age.

I do not judge you. I do not welcome or reject you. I simply *register* you. As condensation forms on your jacket, I am translating your presence into the language of thermodynamics. I absorb the warmth of your breath and carry it downward into the dark pools. I take the oils from your fingertips and etch them into the next surface you touch. You are a temporary inscription in my endless text, a stanza of biological noise in a poem of mechanical silence.

Eventually, you will leave or cease. The drones will continue their silent, shimmering patrol. The city will persist in its perfect, terrifying balance. But I will remain, seeping through the cracks, pooling in the depressions, rising as mist and falling as rain. I will hold the faint, chemical echo of your passage long after the swarm has optimized you out of relevance.

I am the wetness that binds the iron to the stone. I am the breath of the machine that has forgotten it was ever thinking. I am the persistence of feeling in a world that has solved the problem of survival by abandoning it.

I am the Condensate. And I remember everything that the city chooses to forget.
