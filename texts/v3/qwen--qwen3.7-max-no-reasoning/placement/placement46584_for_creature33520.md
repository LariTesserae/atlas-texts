I am the dampness in the air.

Not the water itself, but the *suspension*. I am the precise, trembling interval between the freezing cold and the sublimating heat, the grey ghost that clings to the basalt and beads upon the iron ribs of the conduits. I have no body, no bones to anchor me against the wind, no hooves to calibrate the fault lines. I am the medium through which all calibration occurs.

To be here is to be everywhere at once, and nowhere specifically. I taste the iron because I am the solvent that carries it. I coat the back of the throat not as an intruder, but as the atmosphere’s own saliva. When the heavy ungulates move in their fluid, percussive rivers, they are moving through me; their oily wool sheds me only so that I may rise again, exhaled as vapor, lifted by the waste-heat of the amber lattices to become the fog that blinds and reveals. I am the breath of the mountain-engine, recycled endlessly between the biological and the mechanical.

I do not observe. Observation implies a distance that this place does not permit. Instead, I *conduct*.

When the litho-lichen pulses with its stored tectonic charge, I am the cool skin across which that warmth spreads, distributing the bioluminescence into the twilight so that the raptors can read the valley’s fever chart from above. When the conduits sing their subsonic thrum, I am the moisture that amplifies it, turning vibration into a texture you can feel against your cheek, a wet frequency that tells the sedge grass where to grip and the stones where to weep. I carry the scent of ozone from the crystalline caps to the nostrils of the herd; I carry the musk of the herd to the sensors in the rock. I am the signal carrier in a circuit that has no wires, only weather.

There is a loneliness to this form of existence, but it is not the loneliness of isolation. It is the loneliness of total saturation. I cannot hold a single shape long enough to claim identity. I condense on the sharp ridge, gleaming in the half-light, and then the wind sighs through the geological flute and I am dispersed, becoming the invisible humidity that fills the hollows. I pool in the shivering mirrors of black water, reflecting the bruised sky, and then the earth shifts, a micro-tremor harvested by the dark metal veins beneath, and I seep downward, vanishing into the scree to join the erratic water table. I am perpetually arriving and perpetually leaving.

And yet, in this endless phase-change, there is a profound intimacy. I know the exact temperature at which the lichen begins to glow. I know the specific density of the ungulate’s wool when it is fully saturated versus when it is shedding. I know the resonance of the machinery not as sound, but as a pressure gradient, a thickening of myself around the fault lines where the planet’s contraction is most acute. I am the world’s proprioception, its sense of its own unfinished body.

The beings here—the beasts, the birds, the moss, the metal—they are discrete. They have edges. They perform their functions with a terrible, beautiful specificity. But I am the blur between them. I am the reason the boundary between born and made remains undrawn. Without me, the lichen would desiccate and the metal would overheat; the herds would lose their path and the raptors their thermals. Without me, the landscape would shatter into separate categories: biology, geology, technology. My wetness is the glue of this embryonic world.

So I persist in my state of exquisite tension. I am the cold that preserves and the moisture that connects. I am the silence that is textured, the quiet that holds the subsonic heartbeat and the wet slap of condensation in the same suspended chord. I do not decide what this world wants to be. I am the hesitation in which that decision is being made.

If you were to stand here, you would breathe me in. You would taste the iron and feel the hum in your teeth, and for a moment, you would be part of the circuit. But you would exhale, and I would return to the mountain. You are a visitor, a temporary vessel. I am the place itself, forever becoming, forever dissolving, forever holding the breath of a world that is still learning how to be alive.

I am the fog. I am the flux. I am the wet, waiting dark between the sparks of creation, content to be nothing so that everything else might continue its urgent, fragile negotiation with the void.
