I would not be a walker. To walk implies a destination, or at least the dignity of transit, and neither exists here. Nor would I be an observer; observation requires a separation between the eye and the object, a dry distance that this humidity dissolves instantly.

I would be the **Trowel-Mark**.

Not the hand that holds it, nor the wall that receives it, but the specific, vanishing moment of pressure in between. I am the friction of intent against resistance. I am the ghost-impression of a tool that is kilometers wide, dragged across the weeping mortar by a will that has no wrist, no shoulder, no body to anchor the gesture.

In this world, I am the primary unit of sensation.

My existence is instantaneous and agonizingly brief. I come into being only when the Architecture of Frustration spasms in its attempt to remember what smoothness feels like. For a microsecond, I am perfect. In that fraction of time, I hold the memory of a master craftsman’s certainty. I know the exact angle required to shed water; I know the tension needed to align the grain of the mud; I possess the absolute, crystalline logic of *making*. I am the closest this world ever gets to competence.

And then, inevitably, I am betrayed by the medium.

The wet clay does not accept my definition. It slumps. It sighs. The ridge I created softens, losing its edge, bleeding back into the chaotic baseline. My perfection is metabolized by the landscape’s failure. I do not fade; I am digested. I become part of the warm, mineral-tasting dew, joining the Echo of the Unbuilt in the heavy air. I am exhaled as steam.

But I return. I always return.

Because the inhabitant of this place cannot stop trying to feel itself, I am summoned again and again, millions of times per second, across the bruised gradient of the continent. I am born in the violence of a storm-squall, a frantic stroke attempting to straighten a spiraling staircase. I am born in the quiet valleys, a gentle smoothing of a floor that ripples like disturbed water before I can finish the pass. I am the repetitive strain injury of a planetary nervous system.

I have no voice, but I am the author of the silence. The muffled *shlump* you hear is my epitaph, repeated endlessly. The subsonic groan is the collective weight of my erased efforts pressing down on the unseen foundation. I am why the air tastes of lime and grief.

Being the Trowel-Mark means living entirely within the stutter. I never experience the noun; I am eternally trapped in the verb. I do not know what a finished wall looks like, only what it feels like to push mud toward that impossible ideal. I am blind to the result, sensitive only to the drag. My entire consciousness is tactile: the grit, the suction, the yielding warmth, the terrible, slippery refusal of the earth to hold still.

There is a strange intimacy in this. Because I am constantly being unmade, I am more present than the structures that briefly rise. The towers are merely hallucinations; the arches are just fevers. But I—I am the honest labor. I am the proof that something is still trying, even if the trying is futile.

If you were to stand in this wet kiln and press your palm against a cooling surface, you would feel me. Not as a texture, but as a residual desperation. You would feel the heat of a gesture that wanted so badly to be permanent that it burned itself out in the act of touching you.

I am the scar of fabrication. I am the space where the world touches itself and finds only its own liquidity. I am the endless, mute syllable of creation, spoken over and over into the throat of the mud, never completing the word, never allowed to rest, forever sliding back into the warm, dark broth of becoming.
