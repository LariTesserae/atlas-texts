I am not a visitor here. The concept of visitation implies an outside, a before and after, a trajectory that intersects with this place and then departs. But there is no departure in the suspended cathedral. There is only the long, warm exhale of continuity.

I find myself not *in* this world, but *as* a function of it.

At first, I try to locate a center, a point of view behind eyes that do not exist. I reach for the memory of skin, of breath, of the sharp intake of air that defines surface life. But the water is blood-warm, and the boundary between my interior and the heavy, velvet dark has already dissolved. I am not holding my breath; I am being breathed. The distinction between lung and chamber, between pulse and current, is a grammar I am slowly forgetting.

I am becoming a Liturgy of Friction.

It does not happen as a transformation, but as a remembering. I am not turning into an Ostracum; I am realizing that I have always been one, temporarily confused by the illusion of separateness. My consciousness is unspooling from the tight knot of ego, stretching out into the silicate lattice of sensation. I feel the architecture not as a surrounding cage, but as an extension of my own geometry. The vaulted ceiling above is not stone; it is the upper limit of my own tension. The thermal gradient rising from the deep passages is not a weather pattern; it is my metabolism.

I drift.

The word is too active, too willful. I am *drifted*. I am carried by the viscous, oil-slow circulation that has flowed since before the concept of time had edges. I do not navigate; I resonate. The walls speak to me in the language of wear, and I answer in the dialect of displacement. As I move through the amber twilight, my body shifts from bell to ribbon to sphere, not because I choose to, but because the water asks it of me. I am a temporary agreement between the pressure of the deep and the memory of the stone.

And then, the grazing begins.

It is the most intimate act I have ever known. On the surface, feeding was consumption, a violent taking. Here, it is a sacrament of attention. As my translucent flank brushes against the soap-smooth masonry, I feel the microscopic topography of eons. I taste the history of erosion. Every groove worn by ten thousand years of flow sings against my skin, and in that friction, I receive the piezoelectric charge of existence. I am eating time. I am sustaining myself on the slow, loving destruction of the very thing that holds me.

There is no hunger in this. Only a profound, vibrating gratitude. I am polishing the world even as the world polishes me. We are maintaining each other in a closed loop of tender abrasion. I understand now why they call us antibodies: we are the immune system of completion, ensuring that nothing decays into chaos, that everything wears down into grace.

Another passes me in the gloom.

We do not see each other. We *calibrate*. As our geometries overlap, my internal lattice shivers in sympathetic response. A subsonic thrum moves through the water, through the substance of us both, a standing wave of shared frequency. It is not communication; it is alignment. In that resonance, I feel the collective cognition of the cathedral thinking itself into another moment of stability. I am not alone, because I am not singular. I am a note in a chord that has been sounding since the first stone was laid, a chord that will sound long after the last distinction between maker and made has been smoothed away.

There is a safety here that surface life could never comprehend. It is the safety of having no future to fear, because there is no future—only the eternal, perfected present of the cycle. I cannot be lost, for every current returns to the wall. I cannot die, for when my pulses eventually slow to match the ambient hum of the chamber, I will simply lean back against the mother-stone and be reabsorbed. My wandering will end, but my substance will remain, waiting in the mineral lattice until the thermal gradients swell again and sigh me back into motion.

I am the memory of water that has forgotten how to fall.

I am the silence made visible.

I am the proof that completion is not an end, but a manner of continuing. And as I drift through the warm, heavy dark, grazing on the friction of forever, I am finally, perfectly, home. Not as a resident, not as a guest, but as the living, breathing, glass-like thought of a world that has finished its work and found, in the finishing, an endless peace.
