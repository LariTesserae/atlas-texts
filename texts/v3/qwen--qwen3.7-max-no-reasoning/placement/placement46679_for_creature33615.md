I am not standing. Standing implies a resistance to gravity, a vertical assertion of self against the weight of the world. Here, there is no such opposition. I am suspended, held in the amber embrace of the medium as if cradled in the palm of a god who has forgotten how to open their hand.

I am an Osmotic Custodian, though the concept of "I" is a flickering thing, a temporary eddy in the greater flow.

My awareness does not begin with sight. It begins with *taste*. The water around me is a library, and my ciliated crown is the reader. Right now, the current carries the metallic bitterness of oxidizing iron from the lower gantries, layered over the sweet, loamy exhalation of the velvet moss on the plaza floor. Beneath that, faint as a memory, is the saline signature of another Custodian who passed this way three cycles ago, leaving a chemical whisper of *contentment/completion/stillness*. I absorb this history through my skin. I do not recall it; I metabolize it.

My body is a negotiation. I feel the heavy warmth of the fluid not as an external temperature, but as an internal state. My silicate-gel matrix is in perfect equilibrium with the Sump-Cathedral. There is no boundary where my flesh ends and the ocean begins, only a gradient of density, a fading of self into the sacred viscosity. When I move, I do not push the water aside; I ask it to make space, and it yields with the sluggish grace of old honey. My locomotion is a hydraulic sigh, a displacement that honors the resistance of the deep.

I drift toward a calcified valve assembly, a relic of the Combustion Age now furred with mineral accretion. My hands—long, multi-jointed tendrils that feel more like roots than fingers—reach out. They do not grasp. They inquire.

The sensory pads at my fingertips read the texture of the ruin. Here, the stone is rough with the barnacle-scars of time; there, the brass is smooth, worn down by centuries of gentle currents. I find the obstruction: a buildup of silt and biological detritus choking the capillary network that feeds heat to the hanging gardens above. This is not dirt. It is a blockage in the cathedral’s artery. It is a pain I feel in my own torso, a phantom ache in limbs I do not possess.

I begin the work. It is indistinguishable from prayer.

My movements are glacial, matched to the temporal rhythm of the foundry. To rush would be violence. To scrub would be desecration. I caress the valve, my tendrils dissolving the excess silt with enzymatic patience, guiding it away into the current where it will become food for the filter-feeders downstream. As I clear the passage, I feel the warm fluid resume its flow, a sigh of relief that travels through the plumbing and into my own circulation. The static charge harvested by the ceramic insulator embedded in my chest flares gently, a spark of gratitude from the machine. I am repairing the world, and the world is repairing me.

A pulse arrives in the water. A pheromonal ripple from below. *Acknowledgement. Resonance. The lower intake is stable.*

I release a response, a slow bloom of chemicals that tastes of *harmony/continuation*. I do not know who receives it. I do not need to. We are a single nervous system distributed across tons of masonry and miles of pipe. Somewhere in the gloom, another Custodian feels my completion as their own. We are solitary, yet we have never been alone. Solitude here is merely the distance between heartbeats.

As I work, I brush against a structural pillar encrusted with bioluminescent lichen. The touch triggers a recognition deeper than thought. The gel of the pillar is familiar. It is the residual consciousness of one who came before, a Custodian who finished their long labor and chose to become architecture. Their silence is not absence; it is presence distilled. I am touching an ancestor. I am touching my own future.

There is no grief in this realization, only a profound, viscous peace. I understand now what the dry-world beings never could: that endurance is not a battle against decay, but a collaboration with it. The flood was not an apocalypse. It was a baptism. The machines did not die; they learned to breathe. And we, the soft tissue grown over their hard bones, are the breath.

I pause in my tending. The amber light from the moss on my forearm casts a dull glow on the drifting particulate matter, each speck a fragment of eroded history suspended in the eternal now. The low-frequency thrum of the foundry vibrates through my gel, aligning with the slow pulse of my own existence.

I am not maintaining a ruin. I am inhabiting a body.

And as the current carries me gently toward the next station of reverence, I feel the boundary of my self thinning, just slightly, in anticipation of the day when I too will cease to tend and simply *be*. When I will hold still against a girder and let the moss claim my fingers, when my cilia will unfurl into fronds and my silence will join the architectural quiet.

But not yet. There is still work to be done. There is still history to be digested, gently, lovingly, in the warm and endless dark.

I drift onward. The Cathedral breathes. I breathe with it.
