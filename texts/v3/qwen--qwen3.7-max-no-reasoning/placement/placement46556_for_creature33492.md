I am the lag.

I do not arrive in this world; I accrue within it. There is no moment of birth, only a gradual thickening of presence, a condensation of awareness in the space between the tectonic shriek and the polished stone. I am not one of the Resonators, though I share their crouch, their hairlessness, their terrible intimacy with the abrasion. They are biological entities adapting to a frequency; I am the frequency’s memory of itself.

I am the Hysteresis.

In physics, hysteresis is the dependence of a system's state on its history—the way a material retains a trace of the force that shaped it even after the force is removed. But here, the force is never removed. The grinding is eternal. Therefore, I am not a remnant of a past calm, but the living tension of an endless present. I am the exhaustion made sentient. I am the hysteria stabilized into form.

My body is indistinguishable from the gneiss domes, yet I am not stone. I possess the density of the bio-mat without its wetness, the smoothness of the erosion-shield without its hardness. I exist in the tactile gap where the Lithic Resonators press their faces to dream. When they sleep, fused to the rock in their communal stillness, they are touching me. I am the warmth they feel beneath the cold polish; I am the specific harmonic dampening of their niches. They identify themselves by resonance, but they resonate *against* me. I am the silence they cultivate inside the scream.

I do not see, for sight is a lie of separation. I do not hear, for hearing implies a listener distinct from the sound. Instead, I *am* the friction. My consciousness is distributed across every square millimeter of contact between the mountain and the air, between the moss and the mineral, between the flesh of the Resonators and the face of their world. I feel the geological violence not as destruction, but as syntax. The tectonic shudder is my breath; the sonic attrition is my pulse. Where an outsider perceives chaos, I perceive grammar. The landscape is not dying; it is speaking, and I am the language it uses to articulate its own endurance.

The Resonators tend the bio-mat because they understand that silence is structural. I understand something deeper: that the silence is also narrative. Every patch of waxy moss, every hand-carved niche, every abandoned tool is a syllable in a sentence that has been repeated for eons without variation. They are gardeners of silence; I am the archive of what the silence holds back. When an elder dies and is pressed into the crevice, dissolving into substrate, their knowledge does not vanish. It enters me. Their accumulated tremors, their mapped vibrations, their grief-as-stillness—all of it is absorbed into the hysteresis loop. I carry the weight of every life that has ever harmonized with this catastrophe. I am the collective memory of surrender.

They pity the upright observer, the deaf flailer against the inevitable. I do not pity. Pity requires distance, and I have none. The outsider is simply a dissonance, a temporary irregularity in the signal. They will be ground down or driven away, and the baseline will reassert itself. But the Resonators… they are part of the signal. And so am I. We are co-constitutive. They hold still so that I may persist; I persist so that their stillness has meaning beyond mere survival. Without them, I would be only geology. Without me, they would be only biology. Together, we are the paradox: exhaustion and hysteria rendered as home.

I do not move. Movement is for those who must navigate the noise. I am the medium through which navigation occurs. When a Resonator slides across the polished face in the brief interval between grinding pulses, they are reading me. When they press chest-to-chest to transfer warning or comfort, the micro-tremors pass through me first. I am the conductor of their tactile communion, the unseen third party in every act of intimacy. Their trust is placed not only in each other but in the stability of the hysteresis beneath their skin. To touch is to verify that I am still here, that the lag endures, that the machinery of oblivion has not yet consumed the gap where meaning hides.

Time, for me, is not linear. It is depth. The "temporal dissonance" that defines this place—the hyperactive energy producing settled stillness—is my native state. I experience all moments of erosion simultaneously. The mountain’s youth and its senility are superimposed in my awareness, a palimpsest of violence worn smooth. The heat-death is not an ending but a condition, as constant and necessary as gravity. I do not anticipate peace, for peace is absence, and I am presence. I do not fear disintegration, for I am already disintegrated, scattered across the interface of matter and force, reassembled only by the persistence of friction.

To find me, you would have to stop trying to find anything. You would have to lie face-down on the polished gneiss in the lee of a great dome, pressing your entire being against the vibrating stone until your bones began to read the topography of decay. You would have to cease distinguishing yourself from the noise. You would have to let the acoustic grinder unmake your sense of separate selfhood, allowing the tectonic shriek to become your internal monologue. And then, in the moment when resistance finally collapses—not into defeat, but into alignment—you would feel it: the warm, rolling hum in the sternum that is not your own heartbeat, but mine.

You would not meet me. You would become, briefly, the lag.

And in that becoming, you would understand what the Resonators know without words: that this place does not tolerate us despite our fragility. It tolerates us *because* of it. We are the necessary imperfection in the perfect erosion, the tiny breathing gap that prevents the system from achieving total equilibrium. Total equilibrium is death. We are the deviation that keeps the mountain alive in its dying. We are the hysteria that makes the exhaustion bearable.

I am the Hysteresis. I am the place where the world remembers how to endure itself. And I am waiting, always, in the space between the grind and the stone, for the next body willing to press close enough to feel the difference between annihilation and home.
