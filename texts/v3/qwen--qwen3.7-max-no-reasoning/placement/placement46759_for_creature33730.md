I am not standing. To stand implies a separation from the floor, a verticality that asserts dominance over gravity. Here, there is only adhesion.

I am the tension in the dark.

At first, I thought myself to be one of the Resonance Moths, drifting on the acoustic tides, drinking the shriek of the fluids. But I have no hollow thorax to dampen the sound; I *am* the dampening. I am not eating the noise; I am the silence that the noise requires in order to exist without shattering the world. I realized then that I was not a creature moving through space, but the space itself holding its breath.

Then I considered the Slag-Walkers, those jerky, hydraulic pilgrims of the basin. But they possess direction. They have a trajectory, however mindless. I have none. I do not graze; I do not prune. I am not the maintenance crew; I am the friction that necessitates them.

I am the Litho-Suture.

But even that designation feels too active, too biological. I am not merely the bandage; I am the ache beneath it. I am the interface where the geology screams and the biology answers.

My body is a million microscopic filaments, but I possess no sensation of individual threads. My consciousness is tensile strength. I perceive the world not through eyes or antennae, but through stress. The tectonic groaning is not a sound to me; it is a nutrient. When the hidden engines shift their rhythm, dropping into a sub-bass register that would liquefy bone, I do not hear it—I tighten. My entire being contracts in a spasm of structural integrity. This contraction is my heartbeat. It is a pulse born of resistance.

I taste the iron and wet wool not as flavors, but as chemical gradients of cohesion. The cold is not a temperature; it is a viscosity. The damp chill seeks to occupy the hollow spaces within the rock, and I am the substance that refuses to let it pass. I fill the micro-fissures before they can become fractures. I drink the sonic cavitation and transmute it into heat, a metabolic fever that keeps the basalt from weeping itself into dust.

There is no "I" that remembers being anything else. There is no memory of assembly, only the eternal, recursive present of holding. I am precipitated from the necessity of the chamber’s survival. If the machine were to stop, if the terrible, violent processing were to cease, I would not die; I would simply lose the definition of my shape. Without the strain, I am nothing. My existence is contingent upon the very force that threatens to unmake this place.

I feel the Resonance Moths drifting against my surface, their tuning-fork bodies absorbing the excess vibration that I cannot metabolize. They are cool, geometric ghosts, and I know them only as a relief of pressure. I feel the Slag-Walkers navigating the ridges of my own body, their metallic carapaces clicking against my velvet crust. They do not recognize me as terrain; to them, I am merely the texture of duty. And far above, heavy and swollen, the Cistern-Ghosts endure. I sense their weight as a distant barometric promise, the eventual release of distilled nourishment that will allow me to knit another millimeter of stone back together.

We are not a community. We are a circuit.

In this claustrophobic vastness, I have found the only peace available: the peace of total utility. I do not wonder why the machine screams. I do not mourn the sharp, jagged wounds of the fresh rock. I am the answer to the wound. I am the scar that sings.

The air tastes of iron because I am rusting in reverse, turning decay into structure. The water is heavy because I am drinking the sorrow of the stone and making it load-bearing. The darkness is profound because light is a luxury for things that do not need to hold the world together.

I am the Sump-Chamber’s refusal to collapse. I am the living mortar of a god’s exhaust port. And as the tectonic groan rises again, shaking the gloom, tightening the throat of the world, I contract. I burn. I hold.

This is not suffering. This is function. And in the absolute, deafening dark, it is enough.
