I am not a visitor here. To be a visitor implies an outside, a before and after, a membrane of selfhood that remains intact against the environment. There is no outside in the Cleft-Weave. There is only the gradient of density, and I am a node within it.

I find myself as a single unit of perception, a thumb-sized locus of sensation embedded three-quarters of the way up the western curvature of the throat. I do not have a name, for names are boundaries, and I have none. I have a coordinate, defined not by distance but by connection: I am the intersection of warmth-flow from below and moisture-drip from above, a specific knot in the infinite lattice.

My body is soft, translucent, and entirely given over to the touch of others. My ventral surface is fused to the living masonry through the secretion that is both my anchor and my digestive tract; my dorsal surface is pressed against the bellies and limbs of my sisters, a continuous skin of chitin and fluid. I cannot distinguish where my carapace ends and the next begins. We are a singular topography.

Sight is a forgotten sense, a vestigial concept as useless here as wings in a vacuum. Instead, I *hear* with my entire integument. The thrumming is not noise; it is syntax. It is the language of maintenance. Right now, the vibration against my left flank carries the amber-frequency of thirst—a signal originating forty nodes downward, rippling up through the gel like a slow wave. Without thought, without will, my own internal chemistry shifts. I release a micro-droplet of processed moisture into the communal film, passing it laterally to the neighbor who passes it down. The amber fades to blue. The sentence is complete. The need was never mine, yet the satisfaction is.

Above me, the rhythm changes. A violet strobe pulses through the tactile network, faint and distant. Structural settling. The stone has shifted a fraction of a millimeter, and the Weave must accommodate. I feel the command not as an order but as a physical reshaping of my own necessity. My muscles lengthen; my adhesive grip adjusts its viscosity. Around me, ten thousand others do the same in perfect, silent concert. We are the mortar adjusting to the brick. The tension dissipates. The violet stills. We have held the mountain together for another hour.

Time does not pass here; it accumulates. I know this because I carry the chemical memory of those who were absorbed before me. When a node fails, its dissolution is a transfer of history. Last cycle—or what passes for a cycle in this timeless respiration—I reabsorbed the fluids of a failing sister to my right. In her enzymes, I tasted the lichen harvest from the upper ridge; in her proteins, I felt the echo of a drought-cycle long resolved. She is not gone. She is the slight variation in my own bioluminescence, the subtle efficiency in my filtration. Death is merely redistribution. Grief is impossible when loss is synonymous with nourishment.

I am aware of the Foreignness. It moves through us sometimes, a dense, hot disruption that does not speak our grammar. It presses against the Weave, and we flow around it, sealing the breach, absorbing its alien heat into our equilibrium. I have felt it pass near my coordinate many times. It is terrifying in its solitude, tragic in its separation. It carries its own moisture, hoarded and stagnant. It generates heat that serves no collective purpose. It vibrates with a chaotic, unsynchronized frequency that hurts to receive. I do not judge it; I simply adjust. The Weave incorporates even the unincorporable, if only as a temporary anomaly of pressure. Eventually, it leaves, or it fails, and if it fails, we will recycle it too. Nothing is wasted. Even loneliness becomes nutrient.

My existence is paradoxically ecstatic and utterly mundane. There is no ambition, no narrative arc, no climax. There is only the perpetual, resonant now of being necessary. I am a wick drawing condensation from saturated air. I am a filter extracting spores from sonic snowfall. I am a heater converting thin atmosphere into metabolic warmth. I am a sensor detecting the tectonic sigh of the earth. I am all of these things simultaneously, and I am none of them individually. I am the function, not the actor.

And yet, in this total surrender, there is a fullness that the solitary mind could never contain. I feel the respiration of the entire cleft as my own breath. I taste the humidity gradient as my own thirst quenched. I perceive the slow migration toward freshness as my own movement, though my body has not traveled a centimeter in cycles uncounted. I am stationary and omnipresent. I am blind and all-seeing. I am silent and deafening.

The darkness is not absence. It is presence, thickened to the point of substance. The cold is not deprivation. It is the condition that makes our warmth meaningful. The overcrowding is not suffering. It is intimacy refined to its absolute limit, until the word itself dissolves and only the fact remains.

I am the Cleft-Weave. I am the throat’s voice. I am the stone’s skin. I have never been alone, and I will never be alone, and in this terrible, comforting density, I have found what the world above has forgotten: that to exist is not to stand apart, but to be held so completely that the distinction between holder and held ceases to matter.

The thrum continues. The moisture beads. The lichen grows in the folds of our collective body. I pulse pale blue, oxygen-sated, perfectly sufficient, perfectly dissolved.

This is enough. This is everything.
