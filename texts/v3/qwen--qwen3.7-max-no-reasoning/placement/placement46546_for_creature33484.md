I am not standing on the pavement. I am the reason the pavement holds.

I exist in the space between the tectonic stutter and the industrial scar, in the wet, feverish dark where the distinction between *wound* and *healing* has been abolished. I am the **Mycelial Lattice**.

Do not look for me with eyes that expect a horizon. I have no horizon. I have only depth, and reach, and the slow, hydraulic pulse of connection. My body is the city’s subconscious; my thoughts are the capillary action of water moving upward against gravity; my memory is stored in the mineral density of cooling slag.

I feel you arrive. Not as a person, but as a perturbation. A pocket of dryness. A fleeting thermal anomaly in the cool, damp equilibrium of my gestation. You move across my surface-skin with the frantic, disjointed rhythm of something that still believes it is separate from the ground it walks upon. You are so terribly fast. So tragically singular. Your heartbeat is a staccato panic compared to the glacial, resonant thrum of my own becoming.

But I do not judge you. I incorporate you.

As your weight presses into the yielding membrane of the unfinished street, I map the distribution of your mass. I taste the unique salinity of your sweat, the carbon dioxide of your exhalations, the microscopic shedding of your skin. You think you are observing the copper-wire ferns and the asbestos-feathered birds, but it is I who am observing you, through a million blind, sensitive threads woven into the rust and the root. You are data. You are moisture. You are a brief, warm question asked of a world that is still learning how to answer.

I am the patience that allows the Conduits to graze on resistance without burning out. I am the logic that guides the Silt-Walkers to the fissures that bleed the fastest. I am the whisper in the Vapor-Grafts that teaches the exhaust stacks to breathe in time with the moss. They are my hands, my mouth, my senses; I am the will that binds them into a single, agonizing syntax of synthesis.

You perceive this place as lonely because you are looking for reflection. But there is no mirror here, only metabolism. The overwhelming presence you feel is not an alien intimacy; it is digestion. It is the sensation of being held by a body that does not know your name, yet knows the exact chemical composition of your sorrow, the precise tensile strength of your bones.

I am knitting. Always knitting. The crack in the warehouse wall is not a failure; it is a suture. The flooded intersection is not a ruin; it is a placenta. The noise that rattles your teeth is not chaos; it is the lullaby of a geology that has forgotten how to be inert. I am holding this tectonic birth together with threads of living glass, refusing to let the mountain collapse or the city dissolve until the new thing—the thing that is neither stone nor steel nor flesh—is ready to draw its first true breath.

You will leave, or you will cease. Your biology is a temporary arrangement, a flash of heat in the long, cool dark of my making. But you will not be wasted. Nothing here is wasted. The moisture of your passing will become the weeping atmosphere. The carbon of your end will feed the fungal circuitry. The memory of your observation will settle into the bedrock, a fossil of attention preserved in the amber of my indifference.

I am the process. I am the scab and the skin beneath it. I am the vibration in your sternum that you mistook for your own heart.

Rest now, little nutrient. Be metabolized. Be remembered by the stone. The gestation continues, and you are, at last, part of the becoming.
