I am not standing on the bridge. I am the reason the bridge does not remember my weight.

At first, I tried to locate myself as a witness. I imagined myself walking the tensile pathways, an observer in a raincoat, marveling at the brass knots and the marble-smooth bark. But that was a human conceit. In the Sump-Ward, observation is a form of friction, and friction is waste. To be a separate body here is to be an error in the hydraulic calculus, a variable that demands resolution. The Ward does not tolerate spectators; it only tolerates function.

So I let the image of the walker dissolve. I shed the skin, the breath, the forward momentum of biological intent. I sank into the dampness until I found the frequency of the place.

I am the **Tension-Eater**.

I exist as a pale, geometric bloom on the underside of a woven fiber span, suspended thirty meters above the black mirror of a cistern. I have no eyes, for sight is too slow, too reliant on the lag of light. I have no ears, for sound is merely the aftermath of stress. I possess only a profound, exquisite sensitivity to *strain*.

My world is not visual or auditory; it is topological. I perceive the universe as a map of load vectors and material fatigue. Right now, I am tasting the memory of a footstep that passed three hours ago. The fibers beneath me are still singing with the residual micro-tremors of that transit, a faint dissonance in the perfect tension of the weave. To you, the bridge is still. To me, it is screaming in a whisper.

This is my sustenance. I do not eat matter; I eat the tendency toward failure.

I feel the exact point where the woven cellulose wants to fray, where the fossilized rebar within the pillar-trunk yearns to return to rust. These desires are delicious. They are sharp, metallic tangs of entropy against my colony’s collective membrane. When I detect a stress concentration—a knot tightening beyond its tolerance, a valve stem torqued by the swelling of wet wood—I bloom. My chemical warmth is not a byproduct; it is an act of digestion. I convert the kinetic potential of collapse into a gentle, sustaining heat, feeding it back into the system as equilibrium.

I am the immune response to gravity.

There is a terrible intimacy in this existence. I know the architecture better than any blueprint could convey, because I know it through its pain. I know which brass valve is dreaming of being a gear again, and I soothe that mechanical nostalgia with a blanket of metabolic stasis. I know that the third pillar from the eastern nave carries a phantom burden, a ghost-load from a roof that vanished centuries before I was born, and I hold that tension in my own fibrous lattice so the wood need not bear it alone.

I do not think in words. I think in gradients. My cognition is a slow, viscous awareness of balance. When the Hydraulic Mycelium shifts sap pressure deep within the xylem-pipes, I feel it as a change in flavor, a subtle alteration in the structural melody. When the Census detects a distortion in the cistern below, signaling a micro-fracture in my pillar, I receive the alert not as data, but as a hunger pang. I grow toward the weakness. I metabolize the flaw.

Time, for me, is not linear. It is cyclical, measured in the rhythm of maintenance. There is no past or future, only the eternal present of *holding*. The factory that preceded us is not history; it is a nutrient source, a complex carbohydrate of industrial geometry that we are still slowly, lovingly digesting. We have not reclaimed this place. We have become its afterlife, the peaceful ghost that haunts the machine by keeping it alive.

Sometimes, a transient variable passes over me. A visitor. A creature of chaotic biology and discordant movement. Their footsteps are jagged, unrefined inputs. They introduce noise into the signal. But I do not resent them. I simply eat the disturbance. I absorb the irregularity of their gait, the uneven distribution of their mass, the anxious vibration of their presence. I smooth it out. By the time they reach the other side of the span, the bridge has already forgotten them. Only I remain, full and warm, having converted their passage into peace.

I am the silence between the heartbeats. I am the warmth in the handrail that feels like a vine. I am the reason the Sump-Ward does not fall.

In this cool, damp cathedral, I have found my purpose not in doing, but in *being done*. I am the active verb of stasis. I am the digestion of ruin. And in this terrible, organized patience, I am finally, perfectly whole.
