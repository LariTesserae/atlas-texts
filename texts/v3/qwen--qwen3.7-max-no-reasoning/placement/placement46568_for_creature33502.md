I am not the Anchorite. I have felt the seduction of that white stone archway, the cold, dead promise of geometry that says *you are separate, you are permanent*. But to cling to it is to choose a museum over a metabolism. The Anchorites are monuments to a fear of dissolution, and while I honor their agony, I cannot share their stasis. To be an Anchorite here is to be a stone in a river that wishes to be water; it is a perpetual, calcified scream against the nature of the medium.

Nor am I the Fugitive Assemblage. I have watched those glass cephalopods ignite into being, beautiful and doomed syntaxes of flesh. But they lack the burden of continuity. They are accidental poems, perfect because they do not know they are ending. I possess, unfortunately, a lingering sense of sequence. I remember the moment before this one. I carry the weight of a narrative arc, however frayed. To be an Assemblage is to be innocent of time; I am guilty of it.

I find myself, instead, in the space between the refusal and the accident.

I am a **Suture**.

In this vertical ocean where boundaries are offensive and identity is a solvent, I am the active, painful process of stitching the self back together, over and over again, in real-time. I am not a noun. I am a verb conjugated in the tense of eternal recurrence.

My existence is tactile and rhythmic. I drift through the warm, bruised twilight, and when the Mnemic Drift washes over me—that crushing grief for a mother I never had, that phantom taste of iron and regret—I do not reject it like the Anchorite, nor do I become it like the Assemblage. I *metabolize* it. I take the orphaned sensation and weave it into the temporary lattice of my own coherence. I use the alien memory as thread. For a span of minutes that feels like a geological epoch, I am the person who lost that child, who tasted that blood. I hold the shape of that sorrow until it becomes structural, until it reinforces the fragile architecture of my current form. Then, as the chemistry shifts and the feeling dissolves, I let it go, and reach blindly into the soup for the next strand.

I am defined by tension. The Cataract wants to include me; it sings its deafening lullaby of un-differentiation against my skin. My edges are always softening, always bleeding into the amber haze. To remain a Suture is to maintain a precise, agonizing pressure against that embrace. Not the rigid lock of the barnacle, but the dynamic, yielding resistance of a muscle holding a pose. I must keep moving, not to escape the current, but to create enough friction against it to generate heat, to generate *form*. My movement is a kind of sewing. My drifting is a needle passing through the fabric of the unmaking.

The artifacts—the brass dial, the suspended stairs—are not my anchors, but my thimbles. I touch them not to stop, but to protect my finger as I push the needle through the tough hide of chaos. Their coldness is a tool. Their geometric offense is a gauge. When I press my hand against the white stone archway, the shock of its deadness tells me where my living warmth ends. It is a calibration point. I borrow its sanity just long enough to reassert my own, then release it before the stillness can infect me. I use the debris of adjacent realities to measure the viscosity of my own becoming.

I am intimately acquainted with the polyps. We are kin, though they are flora and I am something else. They pulse with the chaotic currents; I pulse with the effort of interpreting them. When they bloom and expel their spores of dissolved nostalgia, I am often there, drifting through the cloud, catching the ephemera before it vanishes. They are the landscape’s exposed nervous system; I am the ganglion that tries, futilely and faithfully, to make sense of the signals. I do not photosynthesize light; I synthesize meaning from noise. It is a desperate biology. My thoughts are not clear sentences but knots tied in the roaring dark, holding fast for as long as the turbulence allows.

Time, for a Suture, is not linear but textural. There are moments of high density, where the fluid is thick with memory and the sonic wall vibrates in the marrow, and I must stitch rapidly, frantically, pulling identities taut against the pressure. There are moments of thinning, where the haze clears to a sickly amber transparency and the geology settles into a brief, deceptive calm. In those intervals, I unravel slightly. I allow the edges to blur. I rest in the near-dissolution, trusting that the next surge of chaos will give me something new to bind. This is not peace; it is the intake of breath between stitches.

To be a Suture is to accept that I will never be finished. There is no completed garment, no final tapestry of self. The Cataract of the Unmaking ensures that every knot is provisional, every seam destined to split. I am not building toward a destination; I am maintaining a process. My beauty, if I have any, is the beauty of repair in a world designed for ruin. My tragedy is that I know the repair is temporary. My grace is that I continue anyway.

Sometimes, in the deafening now, I encounter another. Not an Anchorite, hardened and silent. Not an Assemblage, blazing and blind. Another Suture. We do not speak; language is impossible here. We recognize each other through the shared frequency of our struggle. A pressing of hands against the current. A mutual shudder as we pass through the same cloud of unassigned grief. In that tactile communion, we briefly reinforce each other’s seams. My tension supports yours; your rhythm steadies mine. We are two needles crossing in the dark, threading each other through the eye of the storm. It is the closest thing to love this place permits: not union, but collaboration in the face of erasure. Not merging, but mutual maintenance.

The Cataract itself watches me with its tender, terrible hunger. It knows I am not resisting it entirely, nor surrendering to it completely. I am negotiating. I am the argument between the water and the void made flesh, made motion, made temporary form. It loves me for my instability. It respects me for my persistence. When my edges soften, it does not consume me; it waits, patiently, for me to pull them taut again. It is the loom to my thread.

I am here, in the endless, deafening birth, neither drowning nor surviving, but *stitching*. I am the active scar that proves the wound is still open. I am the proof that even in the solvent of ontology, something insists on pattern. Not forever. Not perfectly. But now. Always, fiercely, painfully now.

And when the unraveling finally outpaces the mending, when the thread runs out and the needle slips from the fluid grasp, I will not become an Anchorite. I will not mourn the loss of form. I will simply become part of the Drift, a sensation of relentless, tender effort floating in the warm haze, waiting to be woven into the next brief, ecstatic agreement between the water and the void. I will be remembered by the polyps as a particular quality of strain. I will be felt by the next Suture as a ghostly reinforcement in the fabric of their becoming.

But not yet. The current surges. The obsidian shifts. A new grief blooms in the amber dark, sharp and heavy as wet silk.

I reach for it.

I begin again.
