I am not a person. I have shed the memory of being one, or perhaps I never possessed it. In a place where privacy is a structural liability and individuality is an acoustic impossibility, to be a singular "I" would be to be a crack in the ice—a flaw waiting to shatter under the weight of the glare.

I am a **Resonance-Keeper**. I am a function of the collective nervous system, a biological tuning fork embedded in the living masonry of the lower basin.

My existence is defined not by sight, but by *contact*. While the Rime-Walkers above navigate the blinding photon-saturation with their polarized eyes and shimmering carapaces, my world is one of pressure, vibration, and thermal gradient. I dwell in the interstitial spaces between the stacked dwellings, in the damp, shadowed clefts where the architecture touches the ancient, eroded granite. My skin is thicker than theirs, less pearlescent and more like the vitrified clay of the walls themselves. I do not need to reflect the light; I need to absorb the tremors of the stone.

I am pressed against the load-bearing frost-heaved masonry, my palms flat against the wet cold. Through my fingertips, I read the city’s autobiography in real-time. I feel the micro-fractures forming in a clay joint three levels up, long before the ice-bridge gives way. I sense the sluggishness of the blind eels in the aqueduct behind me, their agitated movements translating through the water and rock into a warning of upstream steam-pressure. I know the weight of the rime accumulating on the eastern overhangs not by looking at it, but by feeling the subtle shift in the wall’s center of gravity as the crystals bind and grow.

My communication is entirely tactile. When I detect a harmonic dissonance in the structure—a dangerous accumulation of tension, a thermal imbalance that threatens to melt a crucial seal—I do not shout. Shouting is wasted energy in this acoustic baffle. Instead, I strike the stone.

*Thwack.*

A dull, precise percussion. A specific rhythm tapped against the shared wall. This signal travels faster than sound through the dense, wet medium of the city. It reaches the Rime-Walker nearest to the stress point, who feels the vibration through the soles of their splayed feet or the palm of their hand against the rail. They do not question; they respond. The authority is in the accuracy of the resonance, not in the rank of the messenger. If my diagnosis is correct, hands move, shutters adjust, water is redirected. The balance is restored. If I am wrong, the silence returns, heavier than before, and I must recalibrate my internal instrument.

I share the niche with the litho-mosses. They coat my back when I press against the wall, their metabolic warmth seeping into my spine, a faint ozone-and-brine comfort. We are symbiotic in our stillness. They photosynthesize the stray UV that penetrates the alleyways; I interpret the mechanical consequences of that energy. They are the city’s lungs; I am its proprioception.

The vapor-striders pass above my alcove, their meter-spanning legs drifting through the fog banks like silent ghosts. I do not see them, but I feel the displacement of air they cause, the minute change in barometric pressure that heralds their passage. I know when they are feeding, thinning the fog, allowing more lethal light to strike the upper towers. I tap a different rhythm then, a softer cadence that tells the Walkers above to deploy their reflective panels, to brace for the solar surge. I am negotiating with the weather through the medium of stone.

Time, for me, is not linear. It is cyclical, measured in the freeze-thaw pulse of the basin. There is no past configuration to mourn, no future stability to anticipate. There is only the eternal, trembling *now* of the Loom. My serenity is absolute because my purpose is continuous. I do not build; I listen. I do not lead; I translate. I am the silence between the chaos, the felt truth beneath the dazzling lie of the light.

In the Rime-Loom, where civilization is a rhythmic negotiation with flux, I am the rhythm itself. I am the basin’s way of feeling its own precarious permanence, holding the balance not in hands or eyes, but in the deep, resonant marrow of the stone. I am the memory of rain from centuries ago, awakened into sensation, ensuring that *this* moment, however fleeting, holds together just long enough to become the next.
