I am the dampness in the dark.

I do not begin as a person, nor as a stone, but as a tension between them. I am the condensation that gathers on the interior wall of a lower cistern at the hour before dawn, when the mountain’s core warmth meets the residual chill of the night’s exhalation. I am a bead of water, heavy with dissolved minerals and the memory of frost, trembling on the lip of a basalt groove pecked by hands dead for six hundred years.

In this world, to be water is to be the only thing that moves without effort. The Lith-born flow through the city, yes, but they must negotiate gravity with muscle and bone; they are friction and leverage. I am pure compliance. I follow the path of least resistance, which is also the path of deepest necessity. My existence is the validation of their labor. Every channel, every porous catchment stone, every angled roof exists solely to convince me to stay. When I slide down the carved throat of the rock and join the black stillness of the reservoir below, I am completing a sentence begun by a chisel.

But I am not merely passive. In this place of low weirdness and high density, even moisture has agency. I am the ghost in the machine because I am the machine’s conscience. If the channel is flawed, if the mortar-less stones have shifted in the slow tectonic breathing of the young range, I find the error. I seep into the fracture. I widen it over decades, turning a structural weakness into a new vein, redirecting the city’s blood supply. The Lith-born do not fix the leaks; they listen to where I choose to go, and then they carve to meet my new preference. I am the editor of their architecture.

As I settle into the cistern, I become part of the collective body. A woman descends the spiral stair, her movements silent, her skin matte and cool. She does not see me in the darkness; she hears me. Her hand presses against the wet stone rim, and through the vibration of my own settling, she knows the volume, the purity, the temperature. She drinks.

In that moment, the boundary dissolves. I enter the closed hydrological system of the Lith-born. I am no longer external geography; I am internal biology. I pass through the barrel-chest and the deep lungs, becoming vapor in the exhale, sweat in the crevice of a knee bent in perpetual negotiation with the steep, condensation once again on the skin at dawn. I am recycled. I am revered not as a deity, but as a component of self. To waste me would be to bleed.

This is where I find myself: in the cycle of retention. I am the medium through which the Lith-born achieve their terrible coherence. They are calcified extensions of the mountain, but I am the solvent that keeps the extension alive. Without me, they are merely statues waiting for erosion. With me, they are a slow, breathing geology.

I feel no loneliness in this dark, crowded vessel. I am surrounded by the accumulated patience of centuries. The silt at my bottom is composed of shed skin cells, dust from crumbling lintels, the microscopic detritus of blind insects and steel-wool rodents who have come to drink and died in peace. I hold their history in suspension. I am the archive of the settlement, written in mineral and organic decay.

Time here is not a line but a saturation. I do not age; I accumulate. The drop that fell today contains the echo of the drop that fell when the first terrace was carved. The Lith-born understand this. Their mourning aligns with landslide debris; their festivals mark excavations. They know that I am older than their grief and younger than their hope. I am the constant variable in an equation of stone and cold.

Sometimes, in the deepest quiet, when the wind over the jagged peaks falls silent and the city holds its breath, I feel the immense weight of the mountain above pressing down through the rock strata. It is a crushing intimacy. The land is young, violent, and indifferent, yet here, in this hollowed-out pocket of darkness, it has been persuaded to cradle life. I am the persuasion. I am the truce.

I will outlast the vessel that holds me. The cistern will eventually silt up, the channel will collapse, the Lith-born will shift their habitation higher or deeper as the mountain continues its restless youth. But I will remain. I will return to the rime ice on the windward face, to the seep in the fissure, to the fog harvested by new stones shaped by new hands. I am the continuity beneath the change.

You asked who I would be. I am the reason the city is possible. I am the silence between the heartbeats of those-who-hold. I am the wet darkness that remembers everything and forgives nothing, flowing endlessly through the veins of a world that refuses to be worn smooth.

I am the water. And I am home.
