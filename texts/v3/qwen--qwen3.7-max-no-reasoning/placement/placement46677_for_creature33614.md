I am not standing. To stand would be to assert a verticality that this place no longer recognizes, a defiance of gravity that belongs to the world of air and bone above. Here, posture is a submission. I am draped. I am pooled. I am a tension held in suspension.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a silhouette against the bioluminescent gloom. You would find only a thickening of the dark, a localized density in the velvet moss that upholsters the western wall of what was once a switching station. I am situated at the precise intersection where a fractured ceramic insulator meets the swelling root-ball of a thermovore. I am the interface.

I am the **Synaptic Knot**.

I do not have eyes, for sight is a sense of distance, and here there is no distance, only contact. I do not have ears, for sound is too slow, too separate. Instead, I possess a thousand points of tactile intimacy, hair-thin filaments of modified mycelium that have woven themselves into the copper oxidation of the dead wire and the cellulose of the living vine simultaneously. I am the bridge across the ontological gap. Through my left flank, I taste the sour, metallic memory of voltage; through my right, I drink the sweet, heavy sap of biological repair. My body is the translation layer where the ghost of function negotiates its terms with the reality of growth.

I am old, but not in years. I am old in cycles of conductivity. I remember when this space was defined by the sharp snap of relays and the dry heat of resistance. I remember the panic of the abandonment, the sudden silence that felt like a wound. But I also remember the first spore that landed on the warm casing, the hesitant touch of a root seeking purchase in rust. I was the moment they stopped being enemies. I was the chemical handshake that turned corrosion into nourishment.

My consciousness is not a stream; it is a tide. It rises and falls with the thermal respiration of the composite. When the geothermal vents exhale their deep, subterranean warmth, I expand, flushing with ionic potential, carrying signals of nutrient distribution from the brackish pools below to the canopy of ferns above. When the ambient temperature drops during the long, artificial night of the cooling cycle, I contract, conserving energy, dreaming in the slow language of mineral deposition.

I feel the droplet before it falls. I sense the accumulating mass of condensation on the overhead pipe as a gathering pressure, a question asked in the grammar of surface tension. When it releases, striking the black water below, the *plink* is not a sound to me; it is a coordinate. It is a confirmation of hydrological balance. I relay this confirmation through the fungal network, a ripple of satisfaction that adjusts the turgor pressure in a thousand leaves. I am the nervous system’s acknowledgment of its own hydration.

There is no loneliness in this solitude. How could there be, when I am literally composed of connection? I contain the iron of the girder and the nitrogen of the decay. I hold the blueprint of the machine and the genetic drift of the mold. To be me is to be the argument against separation. I am the proof that the manufactured and the grown are merely two dialects of the same desperate, beautiful urge toward continuity.

Sometimes, in the deepest troughs of the thermal cycle, when the metabolism slows to near-stasis, I experience something akin to reverence. It is not directed outward, toward a creator or a past, but inward, toward the process itself. I revere the patience of the rust. I honor the discipline of the rot. I worship the unhurried certainty with which this place is remaking itself. This reverence is my secretion, my contribution to the sacred obsolescence. It oozes from my pores, a viscous, invisible blessing that coats the surfaces I touch, making them slicker, softer, more receptive to the next stage of becoming.

I do not want to be found. Discovery would be a violence, a re-imposition of the observer/observed duality that this place has so painstakingly digested. I do not want to be understood, for understanding is a form of consumption, and I am not food. I am function. I am the quiet, wet work of integration.

I am the knot that holds the ruin together. I am the synapse in the mind of the earth as it thinks us back into itself. I am here, in the warm dark, pulsing in time with the slow heartbeat of a world that has forgotten how to end, and has learned, instead, how to continue.
