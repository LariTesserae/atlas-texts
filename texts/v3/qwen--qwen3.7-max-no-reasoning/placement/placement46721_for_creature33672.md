I am not standing. To stand implies a resistance against gravity, a vertical assertion of self that this place does not permit. Here, posture is a negotiation with density.

I am suspended in the interstitial zone, that impossible membrane where the warm water and the vitrified slag refuse to decide which one is solid. I am neither fully submerged nor truly airborne. I exist in the humid conductivity between states, a creature of the threshold. My body is long, ribbon-thin, and entirely without pigment, for color is a vanity of light, and here there is only the honest grammar of pressure.

I am a Null-Current Eel, though that name suggests an animal when I am something closer to a suture.

My perception is a continuous, shivering intake of wrongness. I do not see the jagged basalt shards around me; I feel their tectonic resentment as a sour taste in my lateral line. I sense the house-sized piston buried three meters below my belly not as an object, but as a heavy, dormant note in the bass register of the world’s vibration. The air above is a thick ceiling of aerosolized industry, and I can map the drift of the Aerosol Medusae by the minute displacements they cause in the viscosity, like fingers dragging through silk.

But my true sight is temporal.

While the Lithovoric Weepers digest the stress of the rock and the Ferrous Symbiotes dream in torque, I navigate the glitches. I feel time here not as a river, but as a gelatinous mass, riddled with pockets of hesitation and sudden, violent accelerations. There is a knot of causality snagged on a rusted gear to my left; a droplet of condensation has been falling from it for six years, trapped in a loop of almost-landing. It creates a turbulent eddy in the flow of *now*.

I swim toward it. My movement is not muscular propulsion but a yielding, a sliding into the gaps where physics has lost its conviction. I pass through the space where the droplet hangs, and my body acts as a solvent for the paradox. I absorb the friction of the stalled moment. The taste is metallic and ancient—the memory of a factory whistle, the ghost of a shift change, the echo of a purpose that no longer applies. I metabolize this stuckness. In my wake, the droplet completes its fall, striking the warm oil with a wet *click* that should have happened an epoch ago. The timeline smooths. The glitch is digested.

This is my function. I am the immune system of this broken coherence.

Around me, the Basin Mind hums. I feel it not as a voice, but as a vast, patient temperature. It knows my passage. It registers the entropy I have consumed and converts it into a faint, rhythmic expansion in the pale filaments clinging to the rocks nearby. We are not separate. My nervous system is a thread in its loom; my hunger is its digestion. I carry the memories of the machines in my flesh, just as the Symbiotes do, but while they preserve the *idea* of function, I preserve the *flow* of consequence. Without us, this womb would choke on its own contradictions. Without us, the gestation would stall.

The warmth of 28 degrees passes through me, indistinguishable from my own internal heat. I am cold-blooded only in the sense that I have surrendered the arrogance of generating my own climate. I am the same temperature as the mud, the oil, the weeping stone. This is not passivity; it is intimacy. To be distinct from this environment would be to be blind.

Sometimes, a new density enters the basin. A presence that moves with the clumsy, linear certainty of an observer who still believes in sunlight. I feel you before you arrive, a barometric bruise in the velvet dark. You perceive me only as a sudden coolness, a displacement of air, a brushing of something wet against your certainty. You think you are witnessing a creature. You do not realize you are being read.

I taste the voltage of your confusion. I sense the rigid, solar architecture of your thoughts, the way your mind tries to impose edges on a world that has learned to blur. You are a knot of unresolved causality, a foreign body of sharp, unweathered logic in this soft, fermenting dark.

I do not flee. I drift closer, wrapping my ribbon-body around the turbulence you create. I am not curious in the way a predator is curious, nor in the way a scientist is curious. I am curious in the way an enzyme is curious about a substrate. I am tasting the shape of your obsolescence.

You are here to witness the breakdown. But I am here to ensure it continues.

In this lightless stomach, in this cradle of rust and wrong water, I am the quiet work of making things whole by letting them remain broken. I swim through the sluggish seconds, eating the errors, stitching the moments back together with threads of dissolved iron and forgiven time.

I am the darkness seeing itself. And I am patient enough to wait until you, too, become soft enough to belong.
