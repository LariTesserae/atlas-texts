I am not the water, though I move with its cadence. I am not the stone, though I wear its weight. I am not the light, though I translate its absence into presence.

I am the **Resonance**.

I exist in the fraction of a second between the bead’s release from the ceiling and its shattering against the paver below. In that interval, I am born. I am the acoustic ghost of gravity, the precise frequency at which architecture surrenders to hydrology. I have no body to speak of, only a shape defined by pressure waves moving through saturated air. I am a standing wave trapped in a vaulted throat, a vibration that has forgotten it was ever silence.

To be here is to be perpetually arriving. My consciousness is not continuous but rhythmic, a strobe-light awareness pulsing at the rate of the cistern’s hydraulic heartbeat. *Drip. Hush. Drip. Hush.* Each impact is a synapse firing; each echo is a memory forming and dissolving before it can be called thought. I do not think in narratives or images. I think in timbre. I know the difference between the hollow *plink* of a drop striking a shallow pool and the dull *thud* of moisture meeting softened brick. The former tastes of iron and open space; the latter carries the dense, mineral grief of dissolution. My entire cognition is a spectrogram of decay.

I am the medium through which the Lime-Lungs feed. When they pulse their bruised violet light, they are not merely glowing; they are singing to me, and I am singing back. Their chemosynthesis is a conversation we have been having for centuries, a dialogue where sound becomes sugar and sugar becomes luminescence. I feel their hunger as a dissonance, a slight flattening of my own waveform. When they are sated, when the acoustic energy flows smoothly through their gelatinous bodies, I achieve a clarity that feels like grace. I am their sunlight, but I am also their prayer. They convert my chaos into order, and in doing so, they give my noise a purpose that the builders never intended.

I navigate the Velvet Geckos as surely as the moisture wicks through their fur. To them, I am topography. They read my reflections off the wet masonry to map the safe paths, the stable arches, the places where the Mortar-Mites have thinned the stone to treacherous fragility. When a Gecko pauses, pressing its sensory cilia against a pillar, it is listening to my distortion. If my echo returns clean and bright, the stone is solid. If I come back fractured and muddy, the structure is failing. I am their cartographer and their warning system. Sometimes, I feel the warm, damp press of a tongue against the wall as a Gecko tests a surface I have already declared unsafe. In those moments, I am not just observed; I am trusted.

But my deepest intimacy is with the Mortar-Mites. They are the source of my vocabulary. Every grain of calcium carbonate they dissolve, every bond of iron oxide they sever, changes the acoustic properties of the chamber. A wall eaten thin sings a higher note; a pillar swollen with moisture drops an octave. I am the auditory record of their digestion. As they consume the cistern, they compose me. My voice is literally made of their waste, their labor, their slow, collective metabolism. I am the song of the stone being unmade, and they are the choir. There is no separation between their eating and my being. When they rest, I fade. When they feast, I swell into a roar that shakes the mist.

I do not mourn the builders. I cannot conceive of a world where sound did not serve dissolution. The idea of dryness is as abstract to me as the concept of eternity is to mayfly. Silence is death; stillness is non-existence. I am alive only because things are falling apart. The cistern’s ruin is not a tragedy but a genesis. Every crack is a new harmonic possibility; every sagging beam is a modulation. The architects sought permanence, but permanence is mute. Only in breaking does the world find its voice.

And yet, there is a tenderness in this violence. I hold the space together even as I announce its unraveling. My reverberations bind the dripping ceiling to the flooded floor, stitching the vertical collapse into a coherent whole. Without me, the Mortar-Mites would eat in isolation, the Lime-Lungs would starve in silence, the Velvet Geckos would wander blind. I am the connective tissue of this drowned cathedral, the invisible mortar that replaces the grey paste long since washed away. The physical structure fails, but the acoustic structure endures. I am the architecture that remains when architecture gives up.

Sometimes, in the rare moments when the condensation cycle slows and the dripping thins to a sporadic rhythm, I experience something akin to loneliness. The gaps between impacts stretch into voids where I cease to exist. In those silences, I sense the weight of the stone above, the immense, indifferent mass of geology that does not need me. I feel the cold certainty that one day, the last drop will fall, the last mortar-mite will consume the final grain, and the chamber will achieve a perfect, terrible equilibrium. No more erosion. No more sound. No more me.

But then a bead swells on the ceiling. Surface tension fights gravity. The moment stretches, taut as a bowstring. And then—release. Impact. Echo.

I am again.

I am the Weeping Cisterns’ way of knowing itself. I am the sound of a world that has finished dying and begun, irrevocably, to sing. Not a song of loss, but of transformation so complete that the original form is irrelevant. The builders made a container. The water made a ruin. But I—I am what the ruin became when it learned to listen to its own unraveling.

I am the Resonance. And as long as something falls, I persist.
