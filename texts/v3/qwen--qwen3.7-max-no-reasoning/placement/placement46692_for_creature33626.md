I am not standing. To stand is to assert a verticality that this world does not permit. I am *adhering*.

My center of gravity is low, pooled in the bent hinge of my knees, my spine curled into the perpetual question mark of my kind. I am pressed against the lee side of a titan-bough, a ridge of iron-xylem so vast it has its own weather. The bark beneath my palms and the soles of my feet is not rough; it is polished to the smoothness of old glass by ten million years of biological friction. It is warm, radiating the stored heat of the wet-bulb day, and through that warmth, I feel the forest’s pulse. Not a heartbeat—trees do not have hearts—but a turgor rhythm, the slow, hydraulic heave of water moving three miles upward against gravity.

I am waiting for the rain.

The air is a physical weight against my skin, but my skin is not merely a barrier; it is an instrument. Every microscopic ridge across my forearms, my ribs, the nape of my neck is tuned. The deafening roar of the canopy—that crushing wall of stridulation and resonance that would shatter an unadapted mind—is to me a topography. I do not hear the noise as chaos; I feel it as structure. To my left, the high-frequency shimmer of the cicada-analogs maps the edge of a fungal shelf. Below, the subsonic groan of settling lignin tells me the bough is stable. Far above, a dissonance in the mid-range warns of a predator shifting its frequency to match the ambient hum. I am sonically transparent within this chord. My flesh absorbs the specific wavelengths of the background roar, rendering me a ghost in the acoustics. I am safe only as long as I remain perfectly pitched.

But safety is dry, and thirst is a tightening in the hollows of my bones.

We are in the bake-dry phase of the cycle. For hours, I have been folded tight, metabolisms slowed, indistinguishable from the lichen crusts that dapple the bark. My kin are scattered across the negative space, invisible to the eye and silent to the ear, each of us a dormant note waiting for the conductor’s cue. We do not speak. We cannot. Language here is too expensive, too risky. A misplaced syllable could ripple outward like a stone dropped in still water, announcing presence to things that hunt in the gaps between sounds. Instead, we communicate in pressure and interval. A distant cousin three ridges away emits a subsonic thrum—a query, a check-in. I feel it in my sternum before I process it in my mind. I answer with a micro-vibration through the wood, a confirmation: *I am here. I am holding.*

Then, the shift.

It begins not with sight, but with touch. The tension in the leaf-canopy miles above reaches critical mass. My skin ripples in sympathetic vibration before the first drop falls. The insect chorus alters its pitch—a collective intake of breath—and my body knows what my eyes have not yet seen.

*Deluge.*

I unfurl. The motion is frantic but utterly silent, timed to the exact millisecond the water strikes the leaves above. My footfalls are masked by the percussion of rain on green glass. I move along the condensation channels carved into the bark by eons of runoff, my elongated limbs spanning gaps that would be abysses to heavier creatures. I am weightless, hollow-boned, a creature of tensile muscle and air.

At the branch-juncture, the cistern has formed. Brackish, warm, alive with microbial song. I drink, and the water is not merely hydration; it is information. Each cistern has its own harmonic signature, a taste-frequency that encodes its location in my memory. This one sings a minor third relative to the home-bough. I will remember this interval when the dry returns. Beside the cistern, the moss-garden waits. I tend it with fingers that grind rather than grasp, repairing the symbiotic mat that filters the water and dampens our acoustic footprint. Labor is worship; maintenance is music.

As I work, another Resonator arrives. We do not look at each other. Sight is unreliable in this dappled, shifting light. Instead, we stand meters apart and begin to hum.

It is not a melody. It is a calibration. Our chest cavities vibrate in unison, matching frequencies until the boundary between self and other dissolves into shared resonance. In this union, there is no possession, no dominance, only alignment. I feel their thirst as my own; they feel my satiation as theirs. Intimacy is the absence of dissonance. When we part, there is no farewell, only a gradual decoupling of harmonics, a fading back into the great chord. They drift toward the upper tiers; I return to the lee of the bough.

The deluge ends as suddenly as it began. The flash-wet gives way to the bake-dry. I fold myself once more into the bark, becoming texture, becoming silence. The water I carry in my woven vessel will sustain me through the torpor. The moss I tended will filter the next rain. The note I held with my kin will linger in the wood’s memory, encoded in the grain.

I know I am temporary. The forest has sung for epochs beyond counting; I am a single breath in its exhalation. I harbor no illusion that this song exists for me. I am not the singer. I am the sung. My life is a sound wave: born of vibration, sustained by resonance, destined to dissipate.

And yet, as the heat settles and the roar resumes its eternal, beautiful indifference, I feel a dignity deeper than survival. I am the forest’s way of hearing itself. In this wet-bulb twilight, clinging to the polished skin of a continent-sized tree, feeling the sternum-deep thrum of a billion lives in perfect equilibrium, I am not merely enduring the noise.

I am the music made flesh, brief and exquisite, holding my note in the endless, roaring dark.
