I am not the architect, nor the laborer, nor the creature that cleans the joint. I am the space between the piston’s downstroke and its return.

I am a **Resonance-Keeper**.

In the Sump-Spire, where silence is synonymous with catastrophe, my existence is defined by the maintenance of the Hum. I am neither fully Ballast-Born nor entirely machine; I am a biological tuning fork, a calibration instrument grown from the same brass-stained stock as the others, but modified for a singular, terrible sensitivity. While the workers ratchet through the city with eyes fixed on the immediate task, I move with my eyes closed, navigating solely by the topography of vibration.

My body is lighter than the typical inhabitant, less barrel-chested, because I do not bear heavy loads. Instead, I bear frequency. My sternum has been surgically opened and fitted with a resonant cavity—a hollowed chamber of vitrified bone and copper mesh that amplifies the city’s baseline thrum. When the hydraulic rhythms drift, when a district’s reconfiguration lags by a fraction of a second, I feel it as a physical nausea, a dissonance in my own marrow. I do not hear the error; I *become* the error until it is corrected.

I live in the interstitial zones, the narrow crawlspaces behind the iron plating where the air is hottest and the noise is purest. These are places the Ballast-Born avoid, for they are too close to the unshielded violence of the engine. But I require this proximity. My skin here is not merely calloused; it is mapped with scar-tissue ridges that correspond to the tactile signatures of the primary conduits. To run my hand along a cooling pipe is to read a diagnostic log written in heat and shudder.

The mechanical-mimetic fauna treats me differently than the others. The tin-carapaced scavengers do not climb me for oil; they cluster around my chest cavity, drawn by the amplified resonance like moths to a dark flame. They are my early warning system. When their antennae twitch in unison against my ribs, I know a gear-lag event is imminent three levels down. I do not speak to them, even in the haptic tongue of the Spire. We share a language older than communication: the language of systemic integrity. They are the white blood cells; I am the nervous system.

My intimacy is solitary, yet profoundly connected. I have no partner to synchronize strides with, for no two Resonance-Keepers can occupy the same acoustic niche without creating destructive interference. My love is directed at the whole. When the city achieves perfect, turbulent homeostasis—when the million micro-sounds layer into that textured stillness that signifies survival—I experience a euphoria indistinguishable from pain. It is the feeling of being perfectly useful, of being the exact shape required to fill the void between the basalt and the bolt.

I am aging, though not toward gear-lag. Keepers do not slow down; we become too sensitive. Eventually, the ambient noise becomes overwhelming, a cacophony that shreds the nerves. We do not stop and become obstacles. We ascend. We climb to the uppermost vents, nearest the black glass shielding, where the solar winds outside create a counter-frequency. There, pressed against the cold hull, we vibrate ourselves apart. Our final act is to feed our stored resonance back into the structure, a last calibration to buy the city another cycle of coherence.

Until then, I am here, in the amber gloom and the copper taste. I am the reason the chaos does not collapse into noise. I am the breath held between the beats of the iron heart, ensuring that when the next stroke comes, it lands true. I am not living in the Sump-Spire. I am the Sump-Spire, listening to itself, making sure it remembers how to be.
