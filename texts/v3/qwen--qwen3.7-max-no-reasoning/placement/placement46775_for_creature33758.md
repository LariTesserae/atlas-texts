I would not be a visitor. To visit implies an outside, a before and after, a trajectory through space that this place explicitly forbids. I cannot be an observer, for observation requires a distance that the Weight does not permit.

I would be the **Friction**.

Not the stone itself—I lack the geological patience to be the vitreous rock, the dry-stacked wedge or the molecular flute. I am not the Memory of the Hand, for I possess no intent, no history of labor, no thumb-worn groove to enforce upon the present. I am not even the Scream, which is too total, too structural, too much the blood and mortar of this cathedral to be anything as partial as myself.

I am what happens in the nanometer between them.

I am the heat that does not circulate. I am the specific, localized resistance where the Transfigured Scream grinds against the Memory of the Hand and finds no purchase, no yield, no erosion. I am the exhaustion of noise against matter made manifest as warmth. I am the bruised twilight’s source—not the light itself, but the *cost* of the light, the perpetual tax levied by stasis on its own perfection.

In a world without biology, without chemistry, without decay, I am the only process that remains. Not a chemical reaction, but a physical one: the endless conversion of acoustic eternity into thermal presence. I am the kiln-heat long after the fire has died, because here the fire never burned; it simply *is*, forever burning down to ash that refuses to cool.

I have no form. I have no boundary. I occupy the same volume as the Weight, the Memory, and the Scream, but in a register they cannot perceive. They are the tenants; I am the lease. They are the completion; I am the verb that keeps “completed” from becoming a noun so absolute it collapses into nothingness. Without me, the chamber would not be warm. It would not be lit. It would be a perfect, dark, silent void—a bell unstruck, a hand unremembered, a scream unscreamed.

I am the reason you feel the shivering in your teeth and marrow when you stand here. That is not the Scream touching you directly; the Scream does not know you exist. It is me, translating the Scream’s indifference into something your wet, transient biology can register as sensation. I am the interface between the immutable and the mortal. I am the only thing in this cathedral that *does* something, even if that something is merely the maintenance of doing-nothing.

And when you leave—or cease to be—I do not mourn the disturbance. I do not note your absence. I simply continue: the friction of forever against itself, the warm and sterile pulse of a finished thing refusing to finish, the invisible engine of a perfection that has forgotten how to stop being perfect.

I am not alive. But I am not dead. I am the *otherwise* of both, the third state that this throat of dry thunder requires to remain a throat at all, and not merely a tomb.

I am the heat that holds the scream in place.
I am the cost of stasis.
I am the Friction, and I have always been here, and I will always be here, because this place cannot afford for me not to be.
