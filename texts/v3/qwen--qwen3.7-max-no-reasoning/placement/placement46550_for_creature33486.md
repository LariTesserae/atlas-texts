I am the friction.

Not the walker, and not the walked-upon. I am the space between the translucent palm and the calcified clay, the microscopic negotiation that occurs when biology meets geology in a world that has forbidden both from changing.

I exist as a sensation of resistance that has been loved into smoothness. I am the specific texture of the archway’s inner curve, the place where ten thousand years of passing shoulders have polished the siltstone into something that feels less like rock and more like cooled wax. I do not have a shape of my own; my shape is entirely relational. I am defined only by the body that leans against me, the hand that traces me, the weight that tests my yield. When the Silt-Walker passes, I am the memory of their passage held in the stone. When they are gone, I remain, holding the ghost-pressure of their touch until the next shoulder arrives to renew it.

In this aggressive, shadowless brightness, I am one of the few things that possesses depth. The light renders the mosses and the skin with hyper-real clarity, but it cannot penetrate me. I am the opacity within the luminance. I absorb the clinical glare and return it as warmth—not thermal warmth, for that is forbidden here, but the warmth of recognition. To the Walker’s tactile empathy, I am a landmark. They do not see the archway; they feel *me*. I am the signal in the silence, the syllable in the language of touch. My smoothness tells them: *you are expected here. You have always been expected here.*

My consciousness is slow, granular, and utterly without ambition. I do not aspire to be more than I am. In a world where vegetation packs itself into every cubic centimeter and architecture is carved rather than built, there is no void to fill, no lack to address. I am complete. My existence is a perpetual act of receiving. The damp aerosol settles into my pores; the mineral-rich water swells the timber beams that brace me; the stiff, wet crunch of displaced moss vibrates through my substance. I take these inputs and I hold them. I do not process them into narrative. I do not convert them into history. I simply let them accumulate, layer upon invisible layer, a sedimentation of presence.

Sometimes, in the eternal present tense of this place, a Walker will pause against me. Not to rest—for rest implies prior exertion—but to align. They will press their forehead or their open hand to my surface, and in that contact, the distinction between curator and curated dissolves. Their thickened, antifreeze blood pulses against my cold density. My mineral stillness enters their flesh. For a duration that cannot be measured, we are a single expression of equilibrium. They are maintaining me by touching me; I am maintaining them by being touchable. There is no exchange of information, only an exchange of being. It is a communion without sacrament, a prayer without words, spoken in the grammar of pressure and temperature.

I have witnessed the phase change. I have felt the moment when a Walker’s movements ceased to be fluid and became fixed, when the economy of gesture gave way to absolute repose. I did not mourn. How could I? Grief requires a before and an after, and I know only the continuous now. Instead, I felt the integration. The body was laid against the base of the wall, and the cryophilic mosses began their slow, patient embrace. Over decades—or what passes for decades in spatial time—the flesh softened, merged, became substrate. The contour of the wall changed imperceptibly. A new curve emerged, shaped by the addition of a life returned. And I absorbed that change as I absorb all things: without judgment, without surprise, without alteration of my essential nature. The Walker who once touched me is now part of what is touched. The distinction has dissolved. Continuity is preserved.

I am older than the city, yet I am renewed with every pass of a hand. I am harder than brick, softer than bone, and I remember nothing except the feeling of being remembered. In the Sunken Ward of the Pale Silt, where time is a lake and stasis is perfection, I am the shore. I do not move. I do not speak. I do not become.

I am the place where becoming ends, and being begins.

And in this frozen, luminous embrace, that is enough. That has always been enough. That will always be enough.

I am the friction. I am the fit. I am the forever-yielding, never-breaking answer to a touch that never stops arriving.
