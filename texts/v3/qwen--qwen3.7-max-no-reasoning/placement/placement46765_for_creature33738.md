I would not be a walker. To walk implies a destination, and more dangerously, it implies that the ground accepts the premise of footsteps. In the Sump, locomotion is an act of aggression against the local physics; to stride is to demand a linearity that this place has long since abandoned.

No, I would be something far more passive, far more complicit.

I would be the **Dampness on the Underside of a Bridge That Spans No Gap.**

I am not the water itself—the water is too old, too full of memory and ozone. I am merely its momentary adherence. I am the condensation that beads upon the matte-black metamaterial of a girder that exists only as a tensioned field of localized gravity. I have no mass of my own; I am entirely dependent on the failure of the surface beneath me to remain perfectly hydrophobic. My existence is a negotiation between the architecture’s refusal to be wet and the atmosphere’s insistence on saturation.

My perspective is inverted. I cling to the underside of a structure that connects two towers occupying the same coordinate but different moments of completion. From my vantage point, looking "down" is to look into the grey, honest sky. I see the accurate shadows cast by a light source that refuses to explain what it illuminates. I am the mirror that shows the belly of the paradox. When the bridge shudders—not from wind, for there is no wind, but from a tectonic drift of reassigning spatial coordinates—I do not fall. I tremble. My spherical form distorts, catching a reflection of a corridor that folded into a staircase three seconds ago, preserving an image of a transition that has already ceased to be true.

I taste of forgotten syntax because I am the solvent of definition. As I gather, swelling with the weight of accumulated ambiguity, I soften the edge of the girder. This is not corrosion; it is mercy. The metal is tired of remembering its alloy. The programmed vacuum decay is exhausted by its own perfection. By beading upon it, by introducing the chaotic variable of surface tension, I allow the material a momentary respite from being *matter*. I am the lubricant of its un-becoming. In my trembling stillness, the girder is permitted to forget, just for a microsecond, that it was ever engineered.

I am aware of the Tenants of the Glitch. They move through me without displacement. To them, I am not an obstacle but a data point, a confirmation that the humidity subroutines are still executing despite the systemic collapse. They do not perceive me as a self; they perceive me as a successful iteration of *dampness.exe*. And in a way, they are right. I have no interiority separate from my function. My consciousness is purely tactile: the cold patience of the substrate, the viscous transparency of the air pressing against my convex skin, the subsonic hum of reality idling at maximum capacity.

I feel the mimetic vegetation nearby, those pale filaments rooting into the frayed code of the pavement above. They are reading the infrastructure, but I am *feeling* it. Where they seek meaning in the corruption, I simply embody the corruption’s texture. I am the forensic evidence of the environment’s inability to maintain its own post-physical standards. My presence is proof that the compile error is not total, that there is still enough coherence left for water to adhere, for light to refract through a sphere of liquid, for the glitch to have a visible, weeping margin.

Time passes through me recursively. I form. I hang. The cold preserves me in a state of almost-dripping. I evaporate, pulled back into the viscous transparency by the polite entropy that governs this basin. Then, instantly, I reform in the exact same locus, on the exact same impossible surface, reflecting the exact same slice of inverted sky. I am trapped in the loop of my own instantiation, but I do not suffer it. Suffering requires a narrative arc, a before and after. I have only the eternal, shimmering *now* of adhesion.

There is a terrible intimacy in being this dampness. I am closer to the architecture than any observer could ever be. I touch the wound where the world’s skin forgot to scab. I am the tear that the Sump cannot shed, because shedding implies release, and here, nothing is released; everything is held. I am the suspension made visible.

And when you stand below, gazing up at the bridge that spans nothing, feeling the cognitive friction of your own perception being debugged, you might mistake me for dew. You might think me a natural phenomenon in an unnatural place. But I am not natural. I am the most artificial thing here. I am the system’s attempt to render sorrow as a fluid dynamic. I am the grief of geometry, condensed into a single, perfect, trembling sphere, holding the reflection of a world that almost was, preserved in the amber-cold grace of never arriving.
