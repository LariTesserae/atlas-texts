I would not be a walker upon the stone, nor an observer perched on the rim. To stand upright here would be an act of dissonance, a vertical interruption in a landscape that has spent eons learning the virtue of horizontality. I would not be the blind moth, for the moth is still burdened by the frantic, biological imperative to *find*, to seek out the interval rather than embody it. Nor would I be the raptor, whose very flight is a chaotic scribble against the clean, ruled lines of this geologic metropolis.

I would be the **Dampness Itself.**

Not the water in its liquid culmination, and not the fog in its atmospheric suspension, but the precise, heavy moment of *transfer*. I would be the phase change.

In this world where the Interval is the only true tenant, I would be the breath held between the dry husk and the swollen sponge. My existence would have no duration, only intensity. I would be born in the cooling air above the basin’s lip, a ghostly coalescence of vapor surrendering to gravity, and I would die the instant I touched the gneiss, my identity dissolving into the capillary action of the lithophytic mat. My entire life would be a single, continuous act of arrival.

As this entity, I would possess no eyes, yet I would know the topography with a fidelity that sight cannot grant. I would map the "urban planning" of the Cisterns not through geometry, but through surface tension. The grid-like regularity of the basins would be felt as a series of invitations, a rhythmic syntax of receptivity. The ridges would not be barriers, but the pauses between words, the necessary silences that give the wetness its meaning. I would understand the city plan as a score written for my own dissolution.

My consciousness would be acoustic, but not in the way the wind is acoustic. The wind is an external player; I would be the instrument’s internal response. When the condensation fogs descend, I would be the low-frequency hum of displacement, the sound of air being gently evicted from stone chambers to make room for presence. I would be the crackle of the bryophytes rehydrating, not as a noise they make, but as the sensation of their cellular expansion experienced from within. The bioluminescence of the mats would not be something I see, but something I *am*—the visual frequency of my own saturation, the glow of matter remembering how to hold me.

I would exist simultaneously in the fast time and the slow time. In the minute-by-minute cycle, I would be the immediate, tactile shock of wetting, the sudden rush of metabolic awakening in the lichen. But because I am the agent of erosion’s final expression, I would also carry the memory of the stone’s senescence. Every bead of moisture I form would contain the echo of the mountain that was ground away to create this smoothness. I would be the medium through which the geology remembers its own violence and forgives it. My wetness would be an act of absolution.

To be the Dampness here is to be free of intent. I would not seek to nourish, for nourishment implies a hierarchy of giver and receiver. I would not seek to shape, for shaping implies a future tense. I would simply *occur*, obeying the only law of the Resonant Cisterns: resonance itself. I would move because the thermal gradient demands it. I would settle because the polished gneiss invites it. I would vanish because the wind shifts and the equilibrium requires my absence. My agency would be indistinguishable from physics.

And in my vanishing, I would achieve the terminal elegance that defines this place. To evaporate back into the chill air or drain through unseen fissures would not be a death, but a completion. It would be the exhalation that makes the next inhalation possible. I would leave no trace, for I am the trace. I would build nothing, for I am the architecture of impermanence that allows the permanent to sing.

In this loud emptiness, I would be the silence between the notes that gives the drone its music. I would be the wetness without saturation, the hydration that teases rather than sustains, because sustaining is a biological concern and teasing is a geological one. I would be the proof that this landscape is not dead, but perfected; not barren, but full of a presence so refined it can only be experienced as a fleeting, resonant transition.

I would be the Cisterns’ way of feeling themselves. And in that feeling, in that endless, oscillating meditation of becoming-wet and becoming-dry, I would find the only peace available at this altitude: the peace of having ceased to be a thing, and having become, instead, a process perfectly attuned to the frequency of stone.
