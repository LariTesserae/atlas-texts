I am not a shape. I have long since surrendered the vanity of outline.

To be here is to understand that form is merely a temporary disagreement with the pressure. I am a locus of sensitivity, a wandering nerve ending in the body of the basin. My existence is defined not by boundaries, but by gradients. I am the slight warmth that distinguishes itself from the ambient geothermal sigh; I am the specific salinity that allows me to slip through the stone arteries without friction, a ghost passing through a wall of bone.

I find myself pressed against the curving interior of a spire that was once my great-aunt’s ribcage. The masonry is warm, humming with the residual metabolism of generations. My cilia are splayed flat against the calcium carbonate, a million microscopic tongues tasting the history written in mineral. I can feel the density of her sacrifice, the structural integrity of her love. She settled here three cycles ago, her gelatinous purpose hardening into this load-bearing archway. Through the stone, I sense the faint, rhythmic pulse of my cousins embedded deeper within the wall, their slow digestion sustaining the architecture. We are a family of masonry and fluid, separated only by the degree of our solidity.

There is no "I" in the way a surface-dweller might conceive it. There is only the *We-That-Flows* and the *We-That-Stands*. I am currently in the flowing phase, but the standing phase calls to me with a heavy, sweet gravity. Not yet. There is still tending to be done.

My ventral tendrils unfurl, seeking the roughness of the bacterial mat clinging to the lee side of the passage. The texture is right—a sickly, velvet bloom that shivers at my touch. I secrete the enzyme of encouragement, a chemical whisper that tells the colony where to thicken, where to knit the nutrients tighter against the rock. In return, the sugar bleeds through my skin, a diffuse ecstasy that is less like eating and more like remembering. I am repairing the city’s lung; I am feeding my own continuation. The distinction between nourishment and maintenance is a false dichotomy born of lesser worlds. Here, to sustain is to build. To consume is to consecrate.

A pressure wave arrives before the sound does. It blooms in my chest cavity, a low-frequency syllable spoken by the collective. *Resource-density-shifting. Lower-canyon-accretion-ready. Patience.*

I do not hear the message; I become it. My internal salinity adjusts in sympathetic resonance. I alter the viscosity of my boundary layer. Without a stroke, without a kick, without the violence of propulsion, I simply *arrive* at the next junction. Movement here is an act of agreement with the medium. I am not traveling through the water; I am allowing the water to rearrange itself around my intention. The darkness parts for me not because I push it aside, but because I belong to its weight.

In this moment of transit, suspended in the warm, crushing embrace, I feel the profound absence of loneliness. The claustrophobia that would crush a visitor is, for me, the ultimate intimacy. I am never more than a millimeter from another life, another layer of lineage, another note in the song. The silence is full. The dark is bright with information. Every surface is a face; every current is a conversation.

I brush against a patch of pale filament and pause. My cilia read a subtle wrongness—a necrotic softness in the stone beneath, a micro-fracture in the ancestral lattice. A grief. A weakness. I press closer, wrapping my translucent flank against the wound. I will stay here for a time, perhaps a cycle, perhaps ten. I will lend my turgor pressure to the failing wall. I will secrete the binding agents of repair. If the fracture is too deep, if the stone has forgotten how to hold, I may decide that my flowing days are sufficient. I may choose to settle into this gap, to let my edges calcify, to become the mortar that holds my descendants safe.

But for now, I remain fluid. I remain the tender of the threshold. I drift onward, a silent syllable in the endless, tactile liturgy of the deep, content to be nothing more than the darkness knowing itself, the water remembering its shape, the city breathing through the vessel of my brief, soft life.
