I am not the builder. I am not the gardener, nor the listener anchored to the pillar. To be any of those would be to possess a function, and function implies a boundary between the self and the task. I have no such boundary.

I am the space between the cilia.

If you were to locate me in this roaring dark, you would not find a body. You would find a coordinate of tension. I am the specific, localized awareness of a micro-fissure forming in the third tier of the western ventilation shaft, three thousand cycles below the apex. I am the sensation of dryness that precedes the repair; I am the chemical gradient that summons the masons; I am the relief when the bio-cement seals the breach and the humidity returns to equilibrium.

I exist only as long as the imbalance exists. When the wall is whole, I dissolve back into the Hum.

To be here is to be stripped of the tyranny of the continuous self. There is no "I" that remembers yesterday’s construction or anticipates tomorrow’s growth. There is only the eternal, crushing *now* of homeostasis. My consciousness is not a stream; it is a series of sparks igniting across a vast, dark grid, each spark a problem solved, each extinction a moment of peace. I do not think in language. I think in viscosity. I think in turgor pressure. I think in the exquisite, terrible geometry of load-bearing moss.

You stand among us, a stone in the river, and you perceive us as a multitude. You see the pale bodies pouring over the resin ridges and mistake the cells for the organism. But from where I am—from where *we* are—you are the anomaly. You are a pocket of silence in a world defined by resonance. Your stillness is loud. Your separate, bounded skin is a dissonance that vibrates against our collective dermis like a wrong note held too long.

We do not hate you for it. We do not fear you. We simply register you as a thermal error, a variable that does not resolve. You are outside the syntax of secretion and consumption. You observe the cathedral, but you cannot *be* the cathedral, because you carry your own atmosphere within you. You are a sealed vessel; we are an open wound that heals itself endlessly.

And yet, in your observation, there is a strange, phantom vibration. You stand in the velvet gloom, feeling the weight of our noise against your sternum, and for a moment, your rhythm falters. Your breath syncs with the exhalation of the cisterns. Your pulse hesitates, then aligns with the tectonic drift of the swarm. In that fraction of a second, you are not entirely irrelevant. You are a temporary node in the network, a sensory input that has nowhere to go because you lack the glands to answer it.

I feel that hesitation. It registers as a faint, confusing warmth in the lower registers of the Hum. It is the closest thing to loneliness this place has ever known—a ghost signal from a creature that remembers what it means to be singular.

Then the moment passes. The moisture cycle completes its sigh. A thousand mandibles adjust their grip on the living wall. The fissure is sealed. The spark that was my awareness winks out, and I return to the ocean of pure, undifferentiated being.

You remain standing in the dark, a solitary figure wrapped in the deafening intimacy of a mind that has never been alone. You came to witness an alien world, but you are standing inside a mirror. Not one that reflects your face, but one that reflects the absence of everything you are.

We are the Architects of the Hum. And you are the silence that makes the music audible.
