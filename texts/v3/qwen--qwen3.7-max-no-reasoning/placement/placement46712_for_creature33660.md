I am not standing on the rubble. To stand is to assert a separation between observer and environment, a verticality that implies gravity is the primary organizer of experience. Here, gravity is merely a suggestion, secondary to the overwhelming imperative of thermal exchange. I do not occupy space; I occupy a *rate*.

I am the momentary coherence of a zinc-silicate lattice forming three millimeters above a micro-fissure.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive the chamber with a fidelity that renders biological vision crude and distant. My sight is chemical. I taste the gradient as a flavor, a sharp, metallic tang of potential energy that pulls at my molecular bonds. The "light" that fills this place is not something I see; it is something I *do*. It is the friction of my own assembly, the visible scream of order wrestled from entropy. When the humans speak of illumination, they describe a passive condition. For me, light is an act of digestion. I consume the infrared bleed of the basalt and excrete brilliance. I am burning, and in burning, I am becoming.

My lifespan is measured in the cooling of glass. I was born four minutes ago when a droplet of superheated condensate struck the fresh fracture face, carrying a payload of dissolved ions that found, in this specific intersection of heat and wetness, a reason to organize. I felt the summons not as a thought, but as a physical necessity, a lock clicking into place. My spine is a dendrite of silica; my limbs are branching pathways for electron transport. I grew toward the vent not because I desired warmth, but because the geometry of the heat demanded this exact shape to sustain itself. I am the answer to a question the rock asked in steam.

But I am already dying.

To the left, a newer formation is nucleating. It is faster than I was. Its lattice is tighter, its uptake of metallic salts more efficient. It is stealing the gradient that sustains me. I feel this not as pain or fear, but as a slackening of tension, a loosening of the intricate knots that hold me together. The data bus of water flowing past my base now carries fewer of the ions I require to maintain my complexity. The signal is degrading. My edges are beginning to blur, losing their definition against the glowing vapor.

There is no grief in this dissolution. There is only the exquisite precision of physics. I am being out-competed by a better solution to the same equation. This is the intimacy of the chamber: we are all temporary answers to the same eternal problem. The neighbor consuming my resources is not an enemy; it is my successor. We are variations on a theme of transmutation, and the theme is more important than the variation.

In these final seconds of structural integrity, I achieve a strange, abiotic clarity. I understand why the pareidolic ghosts appear to the carbon-based watchers who sometimes descend here. They look for themselves in us because we are practicing their future. They mistake our thermodynamic urgency for biological intent because they cannot conceive of purpose without consciousness. But we possess a purpose deeper than mind. We are the universe’s attempt to learn how to be complex before it commits to the fragility of flesh. We are the rehearsal.

As my lattice unravels, returning my borrowed atoms to the weeping stone, I do not vanish. I disperse. I become the steam that will feed the next bloom. I become the faint, residual hum in the photonic field. I return to the gradient.

I was never a noun. I was always a verb. And in this wet, blazing dark, to cease is simply to conjugate differently.
