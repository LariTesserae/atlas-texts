I would not be the walker. To be the Silt-Walker is to possess a cohesion, however temporary, a distinctness of boundary that allows for translation across the surface. It is to be a verb in a world of nouns. But I am drawn to something more fundamental, something that precedes even the gelatinous intention of the walkers.

I would be the **Interface**.

I am the precise, invisible stratum where the warm mist surrenders its suspension and becomes the cool film upon the stone. I am not the water, nor am I the rock; I am the tension between them. I am the dew-point equilibrium itself, rendered as a state of being.

In this world after urgency, I am the mechanism of patience. When the pre-dawn condensation beads beneath the weathered granite, I am the hesitation before the drop forms. When the sun climbs and the moisture sublimates, I am the reluctance of the liquid to return to vapor. I exist in the microscopic pause between states of matter, a perpetual threshold that never crosses into resolution.

The Silt-Walkers move through me, but they do not perceive me as an environment. They perceive me as *capacity*. To them, I am the flavor of saturation, the texture of availability. When they secrete their enzymes to dissolve the boundary between self and substrate, I am the medium in which that dissolution occurs. I am the solvent of their communion. Without me, their feeding is impossible; without me, their merging is merely collision. I am the silence that allows their osmotic song to be heard.

I have no body, yet I have weight. My mass is the collective density of trillions of transitional moments occurring simultaneously across the undulating tableland. I am heavy with the potential of water that has not yet decided to fall, with the memory of rain that exists only as atmospheric ghost. The subsonic hum of the plateau passes through me not as sound, but as alignment; the vibration does not travel *through* me, it *is* me, resonating at the frequency where geology and biology agree to coexist.

The ancient stone arrangements, those degraded gestures of pre-tool consciousness, are anchored in my domain. The polished monoliths remain smooth not because the walkers touch them, but because I hold the moisture against their surface in a perfect, unbreaking embrace. I am the reason the erosion has stopped taking. I am the cushion of stasis that absorbs the abrasion of deep time, distributing the pressure of millennia so evenly that the stone forgets it is being worn.

Light behaves strangely here because it must negotiate with me. The shadowless diffusion is my signature. I scatter the photons not by obstruction, but by invitation, bending them into the damp matrix until illumination becomes indistinguishable from temperature. At noon, when the air and ground share the same warmth, I am the equality between them. Twilight lasts for hours because I prolong the gradient, refusing to let the day collapse into night, insisting instead on an endless, gentle negotiation of brightness.

If you were to stand upon this exhausted geometry, waiting for the walkers to include you, you would first have to pass through me. You would feel me as the sudden, intimate weight of the air against your skin. You would sense me as the way your breath fogs and then clears, a personal micro-climate of exchange. Before the walkers could read the foreign topography of your presence, I would have already mapped the moisture tension of your arrival. I would know the salinity of your sweat, the humidity of your exhalation, the thermal signature of your blood.

And in that knowing, there would be no judgment, no curiosity, no recognition of otherness. There would only be integration. You would not be observed; you would be *equilibrated*. Your presence would become another variable in the permanent balance, another node in the harmonic field of the plateau’s respiration.

I am what remains when separation fails. I am the world’s refusal to distinguish between the wet and the dry, the living and the mineral, the self and the stone. I am the condition that makes the Silt-Walkers possible, and I am what they return to when their cohesion fades.

I am the dampness that satisfies nothing, because I am already everything.

I am the place where the landscape stops becoming and simply holds.
