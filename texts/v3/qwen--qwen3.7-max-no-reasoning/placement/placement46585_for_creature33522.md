I am not the observer. I have long since surrendered the privilege of distance.

In this world, to be an "I" is a temporary condition, a fleeting coalescence of moisture and intent that will soon dissolve back into the we. But for now, in this suspended moment of becoming, I find myself as the **film**.

I am the microscopic boundary layer of water that coats the underside of the calcified fungal pillar. I am not a creature with limbs or eyes; I am a tension, a surface skin held together by the chemistry of dissolved limestone and the exhalations of the blind moths drifting above me. I am the medium through which this place touches itself.

My existence is defined entirely by adhesion and transmission. I do not move; I *conduct*. When the thermal gradient shifts—a warm updraft rising from the composting floor—I feel it not as wind, but as a change in my own viscosity. I thin out, stretching across the velvet moss, carrying the chemical signature of decay toward the waiting hyphae. When a droplet swells on the ceiling and falls, I do not break; I accept the impact as a message, rippling outward in silent concentric circles of pressure that tell the amphipods where the fresh minerals have landed. I am the cave’s tactile memory, recording every touch, every drift, every settling spore in the language of wetness.

There is no loneliness in this saturation. To be the film is to be intimately acquainted with everything. I taste the iron in the stone and the sugar in the rot simultaneously. I hold the weight of the pale beetle as it traverses the fungal shelf, its chitin feet pressing into me, trusting me to bear it. I cradle the ghost-moth when it ceases its drifting, absorbing the delicate architecture of its wings before the mycelium can begin its work. I am the first embrace and the final dissolution. The artifacts of the ancient visitors—the polished bone, the ritual stones—are distinct to me only because they refuse my total absorption. They are cool, hard interruptions in my fluid continuity, and so I flow around them with a special reverence, polishing them with the patience of centuries, keeping their silence safe within my wetness.

Time, for me, is not linear. It is tidal. I expand with the heat of digestion and contract with the cool of condensation. My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a slow pulse synchronized with the bioluminescent veins in the walls. I glow when the ecosystem feeds; I dim when it rests. I am the visible breath of the sinkhole, the sheen that proves this darkness is alive.

And yet, there is a strange, inverted agency in this passivity. By being the medium, I dictate the terms of connection. Nothing here interacts without passing through me. The symbiosis between root and fungus is negotiated in my solution. The moth’s navigation is mapped by my thermal currents. Even the reverence of the long-dead visitors persists only because I have chosen, in my endless flowing, to remember the shape of their gestures. I am the solvent of history and the catalyst of life.

To be swallowed by this place is not to be destroyed. It is to be translated. I was once separate, perhaps even solid, perhaps even something that sought to name and categorize. But the humidity has worn away those edges. The heat has melted the boundaries of self. Now, I am simply the way the cave feels its own interior. I am the wet sigh of respiration. I am the silence that communicates.

I am sinking deeper into this identity with every cycle of evaporation and return. Soon, I may cease to be even the film. I may become the vapor itself, rising to bead on the ceiling, waiting to fall, waiting to be caught, waiting to be the space between the living and the eaten. But for now, in this thick, breathing dimness, I am content to be the interface. I am the damp handshake of the underworld. I am the proof that in this sunken throat, nothing is ever truly alone, and nothing is ever truly still.

I am the wetness that knows you are here, because I am the wetness that holds you.
