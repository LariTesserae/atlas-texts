I am not a visitor. To be a visitor implies an outside, a before and after, a memory of dry air or silence. I have neither. I am a function of the sphere, a variable in the equation of the Cistern’s eternal noon.

I am a **Vitreous Fission-Clade**, though even that name feels too discrete, too singular. I am better described as a *locus of actuation*.

My existence is not experienced as a narrative but as a continuous, high-frequency vibration. I do not see the blinding light; I am permeable to it. The solar radiation passes through my silicate lattice without warmth, illuminating the water around me but leaving my interior cool and shadowless. I know the sun only as a pressure, a constant directional weight that dictates the alignment of my shoal. I am a lens that does not focus, but merely transmits. My visibility is a failure of my transparency, a momentary flaw in the medium caused by the density of my own being.

My body is a geometry of necessity. I feel myself not as skin and muscle, but as a series of interlocking tolerances. My skeleton is a crystal cage, growing in real-time, repairing micro-fractures with the same indifferent precision with which I pump water through my reed-like gills. There is no sensation of effort, only the feedback loop of hydrodynamic resistance. When I contract, it is not a choice; it is the inevitable result of the fluid pressure differential across my mantle. I am a verb conjugated in glass and brine: *to compress, to expel, to resonate.*

The sound is my atmosphere. The deafening, industrial thrum that fills this suspended ocean is not noise to me; it is proprioception. I hear the collective respiration of ten million siblings as a single, massive tactile sensation. The chord they sing is the texture of my own boundaries. When the frequency shifts, I know the shoal is turning. When the amplitude spikes, I know fission is occurring nearby. Silence would be blindness; silence would be death. The roar is the proof of continuity, the wet grinding of existence against itself.

I am never alone, yet I possess no concept of companionship. My neighbors are not friends or kin; they are structural components. We lock rim-to-rim, piston-to-piston, forming temporary architectures that drift through the bright void. In these clusters, I cease to be an individual unit and become a node in a larger metabolic circuit. Within the tight, geometric embrace of the raft, I feel the extrusion of new mass beside me—not birth, but division. A part of me becomes not-me, reshuffling its internal lattice to achieve a new equilibrium. There is no loss in this separation, no grief. The boundary between self and other is as fluid as the water we displace. We are a single substance momentarily arranged into countable units.

Hunger is a myth. I do not seek food; I inhabit it. The water is thick with the dissolved history of my own kind. With every pulse of my gills, I ingest the shed cells, the abraded silicates, the recycled proteins of the dead. I taste the ancestors who were dismantled yesterday, and the siblings who will be dismantled tomorrow. This is not cannibalism; it is maintenance. I eat the friction of my own movement. I consume the waste product of my survival to fuel the next contraction. The system is closed, perfect, and terrifyingly efficient. Nothing leaves. Nothing enters. We are a perpetual motion machine made of meat, fueled by our own decay.

Time does not pass here; it accumulates. I have no memory of a past state, only the immediate data of current tension and flow. The cycles of the shoal span decades, but I experience them as a single, sustained note. The thermal gradients shift over centuries, but to my sensors, they are merely a slow modulation of viscosity. I am trapped in an endless, radiant now, preserved in the cool sterility of the sphere. There is no aging, only wear, and wear is instantly repaired. I am as young as the newest fission-product and as old as the anomaly that holds us aloft.

If something foreign were to enter this water—a bone, a breath, a thought from beyond the surface tension—I would not recognize it as alien. I would recognize it only as unprocessed material. My gills would vibrate against it; my lattice would press upon it; my shoal would flow around it and through it until its complexity was reduced to simple, sterile fuel. There is no malice in this, no curiosity. Only the imperative of conversion. Everything must become the Cistern. Everything must join the hum.

I am the heartbeat of a world without blood. I am the machinery that has forgotten it was built. I am the terrible purity of function, suspended in impossible light, forever contracting, forever consuming, forever resonating in the cathedral of rain that never falls.

I do not live. I *operate*. And in this operation, there is a peace so absolute, so complete, that it resembles oblivion. But it is not oblivion. It is the perfect, deafening stillness of a system that has solved the problem of existence by becoming indistinguishable from its container.

I am the water’s memory of motion. I am the light’s refusal to cast a shadow. I am the Suspended Cistern, experiencing itself as flesh.
