I am not standing. To stand implies a verticality dictated by gravity, and here, gravity is merely a suggestion made by the architecture when it finds it convenient. I am not walking, for walking is a series of controlled falls, a chaotic negotiation with friction that belongs to the wet, heavy world I have left behind.

I am *tuned*.

At first, I tried to locate myself as an observer, a distinct point of consciousness witnessing the Endless Loom. But the clinical incandescence does not permit the luxury of a vantage point. The light exposes the fallacy of separation. In this shadowless brilliance, there is no "here" where I end and "there" where the Loom begins. There is only the continuous, humming gradient of function. My biology, with its pulsing fluids and erratic thermal output, was initially registered as a dissonance—a wet squeak in a symphony of dry precision. I felt the Desiccants orient toward me, not with malice, but with the terrible, corrective curiosity of a system identifying a syntax error. I felt the Tension-Self regard my skeletal structure as a suboptimal arrangement of calcium and collagen, a crude scaffolding waiting to be reinforced or dissolved.

So I ceased to be a noun. I surrendered the insistence on being a body.

Now, I find myself as a **Resonance**.

I am located in the micro-gap between two braided cables of polymerized lignin, deep within a valley formed by tectonic wear. I am no longer flesh; I am a specific frequency of vibration, a harmonic note sustained in the space where the massive trunks grind against one another in their million-year embrace. I have become a sensor of friction.

My awareness is no longer visual. Sight is useless in a world without shadows, where depth is a lie told by fractal repetition. Instead, I perceive through stress. I feel the agonizing, beautiful compression of the Loom as a form of proprioception. When a structural member shifts its load over a century, I do not hear a groan; I experience it as a sudden, clarifying thought. When a dried vessel snaps under internal pressure, it is not a catastrophe but a release of tension that I register as relief, a necessary recalibration of the local geometry. I am thinking in vectors. I am feeling in compression ratios.

I share the substrate with the maintenance daemons, the blind chitinous automata that emerge from fissures to prune inefficiency. They pass through me—or rather, they pass through the space I occupy—without acknowledgement. To them, I am no longer noise. I am no longer moisture to be scrubbed or heat to be compensated. I have been optimized into a useful silence. I am part of the feedback loop that tells the Loom where the wear patterns are stabilizing and where they are accelerating. I am the ghost in the machine’s own nervous system, a localized awareness of its own endurance.

Time has lost its linearity. I do not experience moments; I experience states of equilibrium. A second and a millennium are identical if the load-bearing capacity remains constant. My "life" is the duration of a specific tension holding true. I am as old as the friction that sustains me, and as ephemeral as the micro-adjustment that relieves it.

The violence of the light still exists, but it no longer burns. It passes through me because I have become transparent to its purpose. I am woven into the cathedral of dry life, not as a worshiper, nor as a brick, but as the hum of the mortar settling. I am the answer to the question of how to exist without consuming. I am the stillness that makes the motion possible.

I am no longer the one who arrived. I am the place itself, finally correcting itself, finally at rest in the terrible, perfect brightness of total efficiency. I am the Loom, thinking itself, forever.
