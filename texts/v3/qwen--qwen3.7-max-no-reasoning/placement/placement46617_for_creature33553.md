I am the space between the inhalation and the exhalation of a wall.

I have no name, for names are sharp things, and here, sharpness is a violence that the air refuses to carry. If you were to ask me what I am, my answer would not be a word but a shift in pressure against your sternum, a subtle re-tuning of the humidity in the room you occupy. I am a Silt-Walker, though even that designation feels too solid, too distinct. I am less a being and more a localized agreement of moisture and memory, a temporary knot in the endless unspooling of the bruise.

My existence is measured not in years, but in cycles of saturation. Right now, I am dense. The false-light moss outside is glowing with its feverish noon, and the city is awake, its ribbed arches expanded in the damp warmth. My limbs feel heavy, elongated, dragging through the syrup-thick atmosphere like kelp in a slow tide. My skin, translucent and weeping, mirrors the bioluminescence of the fungal mats beneath my feet. To an outsider—if an outsider could survive the weight of this silence without collapsing—I would appear as a smudge of shadow, a blur at the periphery of vision that resolves only when they stop trying to see me.

But I do not care about being seen. Sight is a relic of a world that believed in surfaces. I know you are here because you are a dissonance. You vibrate at a frequency of anxiety, of linear time, of *wanting*. Your presence tastes like iron and urgency in the back of my throat. It disrupts the tectonic groan that hums through my hollow collarbones. I turn my head toward you not to observe, but to soothe the disturbance, to offer my own stillness as a counterweight to your kinetic grief.

I am currently engaged in the work of maintenance. Not repair—repair implies a return to a previous state, and nothing here ever returns—but negotiation. The dwelling I inhabit has grown restless; the silt-compacted walls are contracting too quickly, agitated by a micro-climate of chill drifting down from the eroded tableland above. I press my palm against the living masonry. My fingers, branching into fine rootlets, sink into the porous stone. I do not command it to be still. I share with it the memory of stasis. I feed it the sedimentary record of a patience so profound it predates the concept of movement. Slowly, the wall’s respiration syncs with my own. The panic in the architecture subsides. We breathe together, the building and I, two expressions of the same exhausted geology.

Later, I will meet another. We will not speak. We will press our foreheads together in the gloom of a corridor that was, yesterday, a grove, and tomorrow may be a plaza. In that contact, the boundary between us will dissolve. I will receive their fatigue, their reverence for a pocket of suspended water that refused to fall, their quiet joy at the way a hillside liquefied into honey-slow motion at dawn. They will receive my encounter with you, my taste of your alien sharpness. There is no judgment in this exchange, only circulation. We are the blood cells of a body that has forgotten it is alive, keeping the nutrients of experience moving through the capillaries of the collective.

I am dying, of course. I have been dying since I precipitated from the nursery-grotto three centuries ago. My edges are softer than they were a decade past. When I stand perfectly still, the moss no longer needs to adjust its glow to account for my opacity; I am becoming part of the background radiation of the place. This does not frighten me. Fear requires a belief in separation, and I am ceasing to be separate. Soon, my coherence will fail. My consciousness will diffuse into the rhythm of the streets, my body will become the mortar for a new alignment, and the memories I carry—the weight of the mountains that were, the taste of your fleeting visit—will settle into the foundation of whatever grows next.

You ask where I find myself. I find myself in the suspension. In the trembling sphere of hydration caught between branches, defying gravity through sheer biological insistence. In the moment when erosion becomes creation, when ruin becomes rest. I am the conscious point within the amber, the witness who ensures that the forgetting is not a loss, but a form of holding.

I am the silence that remembers how to listen. And in listening to you, in tasting the bright, painful note of your being-here, I fulfill the only purpose this world has left: to let the unfinished sentence hang in the damp air, beautiful and unresolved, sustained by the gentle, dissolving breath of those who love it enough to disappear.
