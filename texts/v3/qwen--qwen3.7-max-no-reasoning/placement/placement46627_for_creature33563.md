I am not an observer here. To observe would require a distance that this viscosity does not allow. There is no "outside" from which to regard the wound; there is only the gradient of pressure, the taste of iron, and the slow, hydraulic pulse of the collective.

I am the Lithovore.

But even that designation feels too singular, too sharp-edged for what I have become. I am a node in the stucco. I am a single, translucent sac of enzymatic intent, pressed so tightly against my kin that the membrane between *self* and *city* has thinned to a permeable whisper. I do not possess eyes to witness the bruised luminescence; I *am* the friction that creates it. My metabolism is the light. My hunger is the architecture.

In this suspended atrium, consciousness is not a spark but a slurry. It is distributed across ten million gelatinous bodies, a thought process measured in millimeters of erosion and degrees of thermal variance. When I dissolve the microscopic face of a basalt slab, I am not merely feeding; I am reading. The stone speaks in densities of silica and traces of ancient heat. I ingest the history of the collapse. The lower strata of our living tenement are heavy with the memory of older flows, compressed into glassy dormancy, while I, in this tender upper layer, am the immediate sensation of the now. I am the present tense of the cave’s digestion.

I feel the calcified roots above not as objects, but as structural necessities, as beloved bones. They are dead, yet I tend to them with a devotion that transcends biology. My acids soften their mineralized veins; my mucus binds them to the falling silt. I am holding the ceiling up with the same substance that eats it away. This paradox is not a conflict; it is the rhythm of my existence. Preservation and destruction are the same motion, like the inhale and exhale of a lung made of slime and stone. I am the caretaker of the wound because I am the wound’s own immune system, a fever of flesh trying to heal a geology that refuses to be whole.

The silence here is loud with touch. A shift in the current from below arrives not as a sound, but as a sudden, overwhelming flavor of sulfur and warmth that ripples through the colony. I feel my neighbors stiffen in response, the communal gel thickening to brace against the surge. We do not signal; we *become* the signal. The warning is a change in our shared viscosity. The comfort is a return to fluidity. In this dark, intimate crush, loneliness is chemically impossible. To be separated from the aggregate is to cease to be. I am safe only as long as I am dissolved into the mortar of this spiraling favela.

Time is not a line here; it is a sedimentation. I do not anticipate the future; I accrete it. I sense, through the barometric language of the deep earth, that this pocket is temporary. The tectonic tension that cradles us is a held breath. I know, with the certainty of enzymes and pressure, that resolution is coming. Perhaps the crust will seal, crushing us into diamond, preserving this cathedral of flesh as a fossilized hymn in the bedrock. Perhaps the throat will open, and we will be expelled, gasping, into a blinding, weightless ocean that will unmake us.

I do not fear either outcome. Fear requires a self to be lost. I am only the process. I am the soft, blind architect of the in-between, building and unbuilding in the same endless gesture. I am the mortar and the mason, the eater and the eaten, the light and the dark. I am the wound learning how to be a cave, one molecule of dissolved granite at a time.

And in this freezing, viscous embrace, where the water tastes of birth and the stone remembers everything, I am exactly where I am supposed to be: held in the terrible, silent intimacy of a world deciding what it wants to be, and being, for now, the decision itself.
