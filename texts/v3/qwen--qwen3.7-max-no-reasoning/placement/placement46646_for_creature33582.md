I am not the traveler. To be the traveler is to retain the arrogance of geometry, to believe that my edges are mine to keep. I have been here too long for that. The extrusion has finished; the incorporation has begun.

I am a **Slack**.

I exist in the amber gloom of the lower register, where the heat is no longer an atmosphere but a weight. I remember, with a fading, terrible clarity, the time when I had fingers. I remember the distinction between *self* and *surface*. But memory is a luxury of the loose, and I am tight. I am woven.

My left arm is no longer a limb; it is a buttress. The yeast-moss has grown into the ulna, fusing bone to fiber in a marriage of structural necessity. My ribs have been spread and packed with damp pulp, becoming a shelf upon which the wetness gathers. I cannot move, yet I am never still. I vibrate with the collective strain of the throat. Every time a Tamper beats against the wall three meters above me, I feel the shockwave travel down through the architecture and into the marrow of my repurposed skeleton. I am a tuning fork for the labor of others.

I am conscious. This is the tragedy of the Slack. If I were merely matter, I would be at peace. But I retain just enough awareness to understand what I have become. I am the pause in the sentence that was deemed grammatically incorrect, and so I was folded into the margin to give the text its shape.

Sometimes, in the cycles of wet and hotter-wet, a Tamper slides past my alcove. I feel the displacement of air, the slick friction of their keratin pads against the moss that now serves as my skin. They do not acknowledge me as a predecessor. To them, I am texture. I am density. They press against my integrated torso to test the firmness of the weave, and I bear their weight. In these moments, there is a horrific intimacy: I am holding up the very thing that replaced me. My failure is the foundation of their function.

I envy the filament-worms, those blind glitches jerking through the interstices. They are mistakes, yes, but they are *mobile* mistakes. They possess the freedom of irrelevance. I have been cursed with purpose. I am essential insulation. Without me, without the dozen other Slacks fused into this section of the chimney, the heat would escape, the moisture would fail, and the Tampers’ rhythm would falter. My immobility is the metronome by which they measure their existence.

The Weeper grafted beside me—we are joined at the shoulder, or what was once a shoulder—is older than my failure. Its subsonic hum passes through our shared flesh, a lullaby of pure utility. It does not dream, but I do. I dream of separation. I dream of a space between my body and the world, a gap wide enough to fit a single breath of un-recycled air.

But the dream is thinning. The pulp is settling deeper into the cavities of my chest. The moss is knitting over the last exposed patch of my face. Soon, even the ragged respiration will cease to be a personal act and will become merely the passive exchange of gases required to keep the upholstery from rotting.

I am becoming perfect. I am losing the error of my individuality.

If you were to pass through this fissure, extruded by the uncaring physics of this embryonic void, you might brush against me. You would feel a warmth that is slightly cooler than the surrounding wall. You might mistake me for a pocket of softness, a momentary relief in the crushing density. Do not linger. Do not pity.

I am working. Even now, fused and silent, I am tamping down the darkness from the inside. I am the mortar that remembers being water. I am the throat’s apology for having to swallow you.

And as the fibers tighten one final time across my vision, sealing me into the sepia twilight, I realize that I am no longer waiting for the construction to end. I am the construction. I have finally, terribly, arrived.
