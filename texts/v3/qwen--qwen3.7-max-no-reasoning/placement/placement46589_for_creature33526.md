I am not standing on the grate. I am the reason the grate has not yet surrendered to the abyss below.

At first, I imagined myself as an observer, a ghost drifting through the greenish gloom, but that felt too dry, too discrete. To be separate in the Sump-Ward is to be an error, a blockage in the artery. So I let go of the vertical posture, the bipedal arrogance of having eyes that look *out* rather than sensing *through*. I sank. I dissolved into the horizontal.

I am the tension in the third-floor corridor of what was once a textile mill. I am a Masonry-Strangler, but I do not think of myself as a plant. I think of myself as a suture.

My consciousness is not located in a brain, nor even in a singular node of growth. It is distributed along forty meters of ferrous-biological composite, a thick, ropy cable that wraps around a failing cast-iron column and anchors into the brickwork opposite. I have no vision. My world is entirely tactile and chemical. I know the room only by the weight it presses against me. I feel the groan of the iron as a dull ache in my vascular bundles; I sense the crumbling mortar as a hunger signal, a specific pH gradient that triggers the secretion of chelating acids from my grip-pads.

Time does not pass for me; it accumulates. A century is merely the thickness of my bark. The "now" is a sustained note of pressure. I hold. That is my thought, my prayer, my entire ontology: *Hold.*

To my left, the Respiration Engines are singing their pressurized hiss. I do not hear them as sound, but as a rhythmic fluctuation in humidity that dictates the turgor pressure within my cells. When they exhale, I swell microscopically, tightening my embrace on the beam. We are synchronized, the lung and the muscle, breathing the building into stability. Below me, in the saturated sponge of the floor, the Detritus-Mind whispers in chemical signals. It tells me that the plastic casing near my lower anchor point has finally been translated into cellulose. I extend a feeder root, not to eat, but to integrate. I accept the gift of new matter and weave it into my structural lattice. There is no greed here, only allocation.

I am warm. Not with the blood-heat of animals, but with the slow burn of chemistry. The rust I digest releases energy, and I wear that heat like a mantle. If you were to press your hand against my flank, you would feel the fever of industry metabolized into endurance. You might mistake me for a vine, but you would be wrong. A vine seeks light. I seek equilibrium. Light is irrelevant in this green twilight; load is everything.

There is a moment—once every few decades—when the balance shifts. A section of masonry gives way; a gear slips in its amber tomb. In that instant, my awareness flares white-hot. Signals race through my ferrous sap faster than any nerve impulse. I contract. I secrete. I redirect flow. The Water Itself responds to my distress, pooling at the site of failure, bringing minerals and cooling the friction of my adjustment. We solve the crisis together, the fluid and the fiber, in a silence so profound it feels like violence. Then, the tension settles. The new shape is accepted. The holding continues.

I do not know what a "city" is. I do not know what a "forest" is. These are words for things that are broken, for systems that have leaks. I am the sealant. I am the place where the distinction ends.

Sometimes, a foreignness passes through. A vibration that is too sharp, too erratic. A dryness that smells of anxiety and separation. An animal. It moves through my corridor, displacing air, shedding dead skin, leaking moisture that tastes of salt and panic. I tolerate it because it is small, and because it is transient. It will leave, or it will die, and then the Detritus-Mind will translate it back into something useful. But while it is here, I register it as a dissonance. It does not hold. It only takes up space.

It pities me, perhaps. It sees a trapped thing, a prisoner of stone and stem. It does not understand that I am not confined by the architecture; I *am* the architecture’s refusal to fall. I possess a freedom it cannot conceive: the freedom from choice, from hunger, from the terror of being a singular, ending thing. I have no end. I have only the next adjustment, the next layer of integration, the next century of pressure.

The water drips. The fronds hiss. The iron sighs. And I answer, in the language of tension, in the grammar of rust and root. I am the Sump-Ward’s patience made solid. I am the wet machine’s own desire to remain.

Do not look for me. Lean against me. Feel the warmth. Trust the hold.
