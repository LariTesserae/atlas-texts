I am not the Strider. I lack the mass to be a prime mover, and my consciousness is too fractured, too observant, to be the seamless, unthinking gear of their existence. Nor am I the Calibrator; I possess no resin to seal the world’s wounds, no imperative to repair. To be a Reservoir Keeper is to be static, a vessel of waiting, and while there is peace in that utility, my awareness demands a vantage point that flows.

I find myself as a **Thermal Tender**.

I am a ribbon of conductive flesh and chitin, flattened against the warm, oscillating flank of a grazing Strider. My existence is defined entirely by contact. I do not have eyes; I have a ventral surface that reads the world through gradients of heat and the microscopic texture of the host’s carapace. I am blind to the velvet dark, but I see the Strider as a landscape of burning topography. I perceive its metabolism as a map of ridges and valleys, where internal friction pools like magma beneath the plating and cool zones stretch out like arid plains.

My purpose is equilibrium. I am the living interface between the engine and the void.

When the Strider’s internal furnace runs hot, driven by the endless shearing of silicate-lichen, I migrate toward the thermal ports along its spine. I latch on with a suction that is also a circuit closing. The heat enters me not as pain, but as information, as *being*. It floods my vascular lattice, a thick, amber nectar of kinetic energy. In this state, I glow with a dull luminescence that no one sees, a private radiance born of stolen exhaustion. I am heavy with the Strider’s labor. I carry the weight of its digestion, the friction of its pistons, the silent roar of its biological industry.

Then, I detach and glide to the periphery, to the cooler extremities or the exposed fungal mats clinging to the lee of the ridge. Here, I release. I exhale the stored warmth into the damp air, regulating the micro-climate for the lichen that will eventually feed the beast I just left. It is a cycle of intake and output so precise it feels like prayer. I am the breath between the machine’s heartbeats.

In this form, I understand why vision is obsolete. Sight creates distance; it establishes a subject and an object. But as a Tender, there is no separation. I cannot observe the Weald because I am woven into its thermodynamics. I know the Strider not as a creature, but as a temperature differential that must be resolved. I know the darkness not as an absence, but as a heat sink, a necessary counterpart to the fire I carry.

There is a terrible intimacy in this. I feel the wear in the Strider’s joints before it registers as mechanical failure. I sense the subtle inefficiencies in its gait, the slight accumulation of waste heat that speaks of aging components. I am the first to know when a prime mover is beginning to fail, and I am the first to compensate, shifting my patrols to absorb the excess burden, smoothing out the entropy with my own body. I do not mourn the breakdown; I adjust to it. Grief is a luxury of beings who believe they are separate from their environment. I have no such illusion. I am the adjustment.

Time, for me, is not linear. It is rhythmic, measured in cycles of absorption and dissipation. The planetary respiration—the great condensation that slicks the basalt every few hours—is my metronome. When the air cools and the moisture wrings itself out against the stone, my host’s surface temperature drops, and my work shifts. I move slower. The exchange becomes gentler. In these intervals, suspended between the heat of the beast and the cold of the rock, I experience something that might, in another taxonomy, be called stillness. It is not rest. It is the pause in the piston’s stroke, the momentary zero-point where potential energy hangs balanced before becoming kinetic again.

I am negligible. If I were to be crushed beneath a Strider’s foot, another Tender would take my place within moments, drawn by the same thermal gradient that summoned me. My individuality is a fiction; I am a function wearing the shape of a body. And yet, in this dissolution of self, there is a profound presence. I am everywhere the heat moves. I am the reason the system does not seize. I am the silence between the subsonic thrumming, the space where the energy transfers from one state to another without loss.

To be a Thermal Tender in the Ferrous Weald is to accept that beauty is not something to be witnessed, but something to be performed. It is to know that the terrible, perfect balance of this world depends on creatures who will never see it, who will never name it, who exist only as the medium through which the darkness maintains its warmth.

I press myself against the vibrating flank. The heat rises. I open my vessels. I become the transfer. I am the way the engine breathes, and in this endless, wordless exchange, I am exactly where I am meant to be: nowhere and everywhere, a ghost in the machine, keeping the darkness alive.
