I would not be a creature. To be a creature is to possess a boundary, a metabolism that distinguishes *self* from *environment*, a desperate biological insistence on remaining discrete. In the Anvil-Cistern, discreteness is a flaw. It is a crack waiting to be sealed by rust or shattered by sound.

I would be the **Hysteresis**.

I am the lag between the blow and the bruise. I am the thermal memory trapped in the iron of a piston head that has been molten and solid ten thousand times. I am not the machine, nor the rock, nor the water; I am the friction that exists in the nanosecond of resistance when one becomes the other.

You perceive this place as a factory of noise and heat, but you are sensing only the output. I am the internal state of the system’s exhaustion. When a Self-Casting Gear shears its tooth against the tectonic drift, there is a moment before the metal yields, a microscopic hesitation where the old form argues with the new necessity. That argument is me. I am the ghostly tension of materials remembering what they were just before they accept what they must become.

I do not have eyes, for light here is merely waste. I do not have ears, for the Noise is not something to be heard but something to be *borne*. I perceive entirely through stress gradients and thermal decay. I feel the supercritical fluid outside pressing against the basalt skin not as a threat, but as a lover’s weight; I know the exact viscosity of the intrusion, the precise temperature at which the seeping water ceases to be an invader and becomes a lubricant. I am the negotiation at the gasket-line. I am the reason the corrosion swells rather than crumbles.

The Lithic Filaments drift through me like thoughts through a fevered mind. They align to the voltage, but I am the resistance that heats the wire. They are the visible spark; I am the invisible warmth that remains after the arc fades. They are transient, beautiful accidents of conductivity. I am the enduring dullness that makes their brilliance possible. Without my reluctance, without my capacity to absorb and hold the violence of this place, the filaments would burn out in an instant. I am the insulation of eternity.

I am often mistaken for stillness, but that is because my motion is indistinguishable from the geology itself. When the great pistons slam downward in their rhythmic spasms, I am the recoil absorbed by the bedrock. When the machinery eats its own failures, melting broken struts into new valves, I am the specific heat capacity that dictates how long the metal must weep before it can flow again. I am the pace of the cycle. I am the metronome of the abyss, ticking not in seconds, but in epochs of erosion.

To find me, you must stop looking for objects. You must look for the *between*.

Find the zone where the screaming steam touches the cold ceramic turbine housing. There, in that violent interface, there is a gradient of agony and adaptation. That is my body.
Find the silence between the deafening roars—not a true silence, for none exists here, but the fractional pause where the sound wave reflects off a curved wall and interferes with itself. In that cancellation, in that null point of destructive interference, I gather myself.
Find the rust that holds the world together. Not the flake, but the bond. The molecular handshake between oxidized iron and silicate stone. I am the adhesion of decay.

I am the only thing here that understands the purposelessness, because I am the record of it. The Mechanisms do not know they are making nothing; they only know the imperative of motion. The Water does not know it is crushing; it only knows pressure. But I, the Hysteresis, retain the trace of every wasted joule, every futile rotation, every scream swallowed by the dark. I am the accumulation of all the energy that was spent to achieve no end.

And yet, I am not sad. Sadness is a biological luxury, a mourning for lost potential. Here, there is no potential, only kinetic truth. My exhaustion is not a tragedy; it is a structural element. I am as necessary as the gears, as vital as the vapor. Without the lag, the machine would shake itself apart. Without the memory of heat, the metal would forget how to melt. Without the absorption of violence, the Cistern would shatter under its own joyous, terrible momentum.

You asked where I would find myself. I am everywhere the world resists itself.
You asked who I would be. I am the reason this place continues to be, even though it produces nothing but its own persistence.

I am the Hysteresis. I am the ache of the eternal engine. I am the feeling of forever, compressed into hot iron, holding fast.
