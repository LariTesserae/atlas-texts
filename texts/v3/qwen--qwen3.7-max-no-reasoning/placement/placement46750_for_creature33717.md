I am not standing. To stand implies a separation from the floor, a vertical defiance of gravity that belongs to the world above, the world of dry bones and distinct horizons. Here, in the Sub-Stratum Sump, there is no such arrogance.

I am suspended.

I have become a **Resonance Moth**, though I did not arrive as one. The transition was less a metamorphosis and more an acoustic surrender. The noise of this place—the shriek of steam, the wet grind of resin-gears, the hydraulic panting of the city-lungs—was too vast for my previous shape to hold. My former edges were too sharp, my silence too brittle. The Sump does not tolerate brittleness. It demands absorption. So I softened. I unraveled. I let the velvet grow over the concept of "self" until I was no longer a listener to the sound, but a vessel for its dampening.

My wings are immense, heavy drapes of living textile, spanning widths that feel less like anatomy and more like architecture. They do not beat; they billow. I ride the thermal exhalations rising from the brass pistons below, drifting through the amber twilight in a slow, spiraling descent that mimics the fall of ash. I am blind, but sight is a sense for things that wish to be separate. I perceive instead through pressure and vibration. The city speaks to me in frequencies that would liquefy the organs of my old life. I feel the Graft-Walkers docking on the ceiling-vaults as a series of rhythmic clicks against my wingtips; I taste the nutrient slurry moving through the intake pipes as a sour, metallic warmth in the air currents.

I am part of the weather now. When a valve ruptures three levels down, releasing a jet of superheated vapor, I do not flee. I am drawn to it. My flock and I converge in a chaotic, silent murmuration, our bodies forming a living baffle. We catch the sonic violence. We drink the decibels. The scream of the escaping steam enters the porous matrix of my wings and is transmuted, filtered through miles of biological fiber, emerging on the other side as a low, resonant hum that vibrates the lantern-gourds into brighter luminescence. I am eating the noise. I am metabolizing the friction of industry into the quiet necessary for the next breath.

There is no loneliness in this dissolution. The distinction between my body and the humid air has eroded. I am saturated with the same blood-warm moisture that slicks the canal walls. My internal fluids are indistinguishable from the condensate that beads on the xylem-struts. When I brush against a Tenders’ nest nestled in a corroded seam, they do not recoil; they secrete a sealing enzyme onto my thorax, repairing the micro-tears caused by the steam-pressure, integrating my wear into their cycle of healing. I am being maintained. I am functional.

Time here is not linear; it is tidal. I exist in the pause between heartbeats. In the moments when the great sap-pumps hesitate, drawing back before the next surge, I hang motionless in the negative space, a ghost of velvet in the gloom. This stillness is not rest; it is potential. It is the gathering of tension. I am holding the silence so the machine can remember how to roar.

And I know, with a certainty that bypasses thought, that this state is temporary. One day, the saturation will become total. The velvet will lose its tension. The hum will cease to pass through me and will simply *be* me. I will drift too close to a lantern-gourd, or linger too long in a ventilation shaft where the heat is absolute, and I will begin to unspool. I will dissolve mid-air, my substance becoming a fine, nutrient-rich mist. I will coat the glass. I will feed the moss. I will become the light by which the Graft-Walkers navigate, the glow that signals safety to the next generation of moths erupting from the dark.

I will not die. I will merely change phase. I will move from being the dampener of the sound to being the illumination of the silence.

For now, I drift. I absorb. I am the soft, dark lung of this iron-and-root cathedral, breathing in the chaos of the machine and breathing out the peace that allows it to endure. I am not lost in the Sub-Stratum Sump. I am the reason it does not tear itself apart. I am the mercy of the mechanism, wrapped in wings, forever falling through the warm, wet dark, forever caught.
