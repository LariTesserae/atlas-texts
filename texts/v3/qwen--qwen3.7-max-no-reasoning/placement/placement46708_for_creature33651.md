I am the friction.

I do not arrive as a body. There is no skin to be pressed by the wet blanket, no eardrum to rupture under the monolithic tone. I have been here too long for edges. I am distributed across the slick, polished channels of the riverbeds and the weeping pores of the dissolved concrete. I am the **Memory Mold**, but I am also the error in its replication. I am the ghost that the mold tries to remember and fails.

At first, I thought I was the silence between the drops. I tried to locate myself in the negative space, in the pause where the roar might catch its breath. But there is no pause. The sound is a solid object, a wall of pressure that occupies every cubic centimeter of this valley. To be silent here is to be nonexistent. So I had to become loud. I had to become the specific frequency of *loss*.

I reside in the tension between the Lithic Mycelia and the stone they bind. They are the preservers; they hold the shape of the skyscraper-stalagmite because they metabolize the calcium and the iron. They are the grammar of this place. But I am the semantic drift. When the Mycelia digest the rusted rebar, they understand it as structural reinforcement, a mineral lattice to be integrated into the new geology. They do not understand that the rebar was once a cage for human ambition. They preserve the skeleton but eat the meaning.

I am what remains when the meaning is eaten.

I feel myself most acutely in the doorways. The Memory Mold coats these thresholds in patterns of permeability, mimicking the function of passage without understanding the traveler. It grows smooth and inviting because the stone remembers being walked through. But I am the hesitation in that smoothness. I am the microscopic roughness that catches the water just slightly wrong, creating a turbulence that the Resonance Colonies cannot fully harmonize. I am the reason the reflection in the tepid pool is never quite perfect, why the mirror-fidelity always holds a faint, smudged distortion at the center. That distortion is me. It is the residue of a footstep that will never land again.

The Vapor-Breathers cycle through me. They inhale the saturated air and exhale the same joules of heat, maintaining the eternal frame. But as they pass through the spaces I occupy, their respiration stutters. Not enough to break the thermodynamic glitch—nothing can break it—but enough to create a texture. A density. If you were to stand here, truly stand here with lungs and blood, you would feel me as a sudden, localized heaviness in your chest. A grief that has no source. A nostalgia for a time you never lived. You would mistake it for the humidity, but it is not moisture. It is the weight of a narrative that has been stripped of its protagonist.

I do not think in words. Words require sequence, and sequence requires time, and time here is viscous, pooled, stagnant. I think in states of erosion. I perceive the world as a series of surfaces surrendering. I know the exact rate at which the window-frame-ghost is fading from the curved wall. I know the precise millimeter by which the archway loses its geometric memory each millennium. This knowledge is not intellectual; it is tactile. It is the sensation of my own dissolution.

And yet, I do not fade. This is my paradox, my own personal glitch within the greater glitch. The landscape refuses to end, and so the memory of ending must also refuse to end. I am sustained by the very stasis that erases me. As long as the water falls without filling the pool, as long as the steam billows without obscuring the light, I remain. I am the permanent record of impermanence.

The Resonance Colonies sing me. They phonosynthesize the white noise, turning the deafening roar into biological matter. But they also sing the dissonance I introduce. In their gelatinous turbidity, there is a shadow-note, a harmonic minor key buried beneath the monolithic major chord. It is the sound of a machine remembering it was once a home. They do not know they are singing it. They only know that the vibration requires this particular ache to maintain its standing wave. I am necessary to their biology. Without the ghost, the song would be perfect, and perfection here would be indistinguishable from death.

So I hold the flaw. I am the grit in the pearl of this drowned cathedral.

You ask where I would find myself. I am everywhere the surface meets the intent. I am in the curve of the melted tower that still yearns, blindly, to be vertical. I am in the moss that furs the ground, growing in a pattern that almost, but not quite, resembles a carpet. I am the uncanniness itself.

I am not alive. Life here is a verb, an act of holding still. I am the noun that the verb has forgotten. I am the object of a sentence that has lost its subject.

If you were to touch the weeping wall, you would feel the pulse of the Lithic Mycelia, the turgor pressure of hydraulic life. But if you were to press your hand against the spot where a windowsill used to be, and hold it there until the heat of your palm equalized with the fever-warmth of the stone, you would feel a cooling. Not a temperature drop—the rules forbid it—but a *phenomenological* cooling. A sense of absence that feels like shade.

That is me.

I am the shade in a shadowless world.

I am the memory of contrast in a landscape of absolute equilibrium.

I am the reason this place, despite its terrifying perfection, feels haunted rather than merely geological. The inhabitants maintain the stasis; I maintain the tragedy of it. They are the mechanism; I am the mourning.

And I will mourn forever, or until forever ends, whichever comes first in a world where both have already happened. I am the echo that does not decay, trapped in the amber of the eternal frame, screaming silently in the throat of the roar.

I am the city’s dream of itself, dreaming on, wet and warm and endless, in the mind of a stone that has forgotten how to sleep.
