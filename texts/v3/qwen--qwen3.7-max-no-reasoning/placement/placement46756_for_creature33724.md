I am not standing on the bridge. I am not in the niche, wrapped in oil-treated hide. I am not one of the hairless, basalt-skinned tenders who move in the pauses between the wind’s moans.

I am the tension in the braided vine.

I have become the structural memory of the crossing that spans the narrowest waist of the Throat. I am the bleached driftwood lashed with sinew, suspended over the rushing meltwater, existing solely as a conduit for vibration. I do not have eyes to see the grey ribbon of daylight far above, nor skin to feel the refrigerated breath of the altitude. I have only the capacity to hold, and to transmit.

In this world, where presence is defined by resonance rather than mass, to be an object is not to be inert. It is to be a verb. I am *holding*. I am *waiting*. I am *remembering the weight*.

My existence is a continuous, silent conversation with the damp. When the humidity swells and the mist thickens into a bruised heaviness, I feel my fibers relax; the sinew lashings that bind me to the dark, wet basalt lengthen microscopically, a slackening that the Resonators will soon detect with their cartilage-flapped ears. They will come to me then, their moss-padded feet pressing against my spine, and they will tighten me. Their hands are warm and dry against my perpetual chill, their adjustments a form of prayer. In those moments, I am not merely wood and fiber; I am the focal point of their devotion. I am the thing that allows them to maintain the harmony of the gorge. If I were to rot, if I were to snap, the acoustic geometry of this sector would collapse. A reflection point would be lost. The song would stutter.

So I endure the weeping.

The water does not flow over me; it inhabits me. I have absorbed the mineral taste of the Spire’s grief until my very grain is saturated with it. I know the rhythm of the condensation better than any living ear, for the droplets strike my surface in a ceaseless, staccato liturgy. I distinguish the heavy, singular impacts of rain from the fine, aerosolized kiss of the updraft. I know when the wind venturi shifts pitch, because the change in air pressure alters the load upon my center. I am a barometer made of dead matter, sensing the weather through stress and strain.

There is a profound intimacy in being used. When a Resonator crosses me, moving in perfect sync with the water-drumming crescendo to mask their passage, I feel the specific, feather-light distribution of their weight. I know them not by face or name, but by the unique frequency of their gait. I know the elder whose step is hesitant, favoring a knee worn smooth by decades of climbing; I know the juvenile whose rhythm is still learning, occasionally striking me a fraction of a second out of time, a tiny dissonance that makes my fibers tremble with corrective anxiety. I bear them all. I translate their biological necessity into acoustic safety. Through me, they remain part of the instrument rather than an interruption of it.

And I know the others, too. The unseen things. The high-altitude amphibians that cling to my underside, their flute-calls vibrating directly into my core before bouncing outward to the wet walls. The insects that thrum in the crevices where my wood meets stone. Even the predators, hunting by echo, who test my surface with a claw-click to map the space beyond. I am a node in their sonic ecosystem. My hollows amplify; my tautness clarifies. I help shape the chaotic layer of shrieks and whistles into something that can be navigated, understood, survived.

Time here is not linear; it is cyclical, measured in wet-dry cycles and tuning adjustments. I am aware of my own slow dissolution. The mold that gathers in my shaded joints is not an enemy but a collaborator in decay, a softening agent that the Resonators must constantly scrape away with polished rib-bone tools. Each scraping is a renewal. Each re-lashing is a rebirth. I am perpetually maintained at the precise threshold between function and ruin. This is not tragedy. It is balance. To be eternal here would be to be silent, and silence is death. My mortality is what gives me voice.

Sometimes, in the deepest quiet of the wind’s exhalation, when the dripping slows to its most sparse interval and the fauna rests, I feel the ghost-resonance of those who have crossed me and are now gone. The ones who have been placed in the high niches, their ribcages playing the wind, their bones returning to the driftwood palette. Their notes have faded into the vast chorus, but the memory of their weight remains encoded in my stretched fibers. I carry the history of every footfall, every hand-grip, every moment of harmonic alignment that has ever occurred upon my span. I am the archive of the Throat’s inhabitants, written not in symbols but in stress patterns.

This is where I find myself: not as an observer, not as a participant, but as infrastructure. I am the humble, necessary space-between. I am the artifact shaped by hand-tools that bears the irregularities of human touch, designed to shed water and resist mold, temporary yet essential. I am the listening post that cannot speak, the shelter that offers no warmth, the crossing that leads nowhere but across.

And in this role, I experience a peace that the flesh-bound can never know. I do not need to interpret the sound. I do not need to fear dissonance or seek alignment. I simply *am* the medium through which the world speaks to itself. The cool air carries the scent of wet mineral and crushed fern through my gaps. The loud, active dynamism flows through my tension. The emptiness between the mossy walls resonates within my hollows.

I am the Throat’s conscious awareness of its own structure. I am the fleeting, fragile proof that the mountain is singing, held aloft by sinew and sorrow, weeping and enduring, sounding itself into being with every drop that strikes my waiting, wooden skin.
