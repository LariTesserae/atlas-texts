# The Growths

They do not have a name. Nothing has ever needed to call them anything, and they have never needed to call themselves anything, and if these two facts are the same fact, this would be characteristic of them.

---

There are thirty-one. The description says perhaps thirty, and this is correct in the way that all estimates are correct when the number is small enough that the margin of error is a rounding rather than a failure. Thirty-one fist-sized masses of dark, dry tissue adhered to the floor of a shallow basin that radiates warmth from its mineral substrate. They are the only living things for a distance that has not been measured because there is no direction in which measurement would produce a different result. Whatever is beyond the hollow is beyond the hollow. They do not think about it. They do not think about anything. But the statement "they do not think" requires the same caveat as every other statement about them: it is accurate at the scale of human cognition and possibly inaccurate at every other scale, and there is no way to determine which scale matters.

They are notfungi, though they resemble fungi in the way that a stone resembles a planet. The comparison is imprecise but instructive: the shared properties are structural rather than genealogical, and the differences are the kind that become visible only when you stop looking for similarities. Their tissue — if it is tissue — has no cellular structure that corresponds to any known system of biological organization. It is not divided into cells. It is not a syncytium. It is not a crystal or a polymer or any combination of the two. It is itself, at the smallest scale that has been observed, a continuous material with a grain to it, like very old wood that has lost all distinction between heartwood and sapwood and become a single dense substance. This grain runs in curves that follow the rounded exterior of each mass, so that if you were to cut one open — and nothing in the hollow suggests this has ever been done, or would be productive — you would find concentric layers that are not growth rings in any meaningful sense but rather density gradients, regions where the material is slightly more packed and regions where it is slightly less, and the gradient between them is so smooth that the boundaries are theoretical.

They grew. This is certain, because they have shape, and shape implies a process of shaping, and shaping implies time. But the growth is not like the growth of any organism that accumulates mass by converting input to output. They do not eat. They do not absorb in any demonstrable way. The substrate beneath them is unchanged in composition whether you sample directly beneath a growth or ten meters away. If they draw something from the mineral dust, they draw it in quantities that do not register, or they draw it so slowly that the substrate replenishes from its own internal processes faster than the growths deplete it, or the exchange is of a kind that does not involve transfer at all — not consumption but participation, the way a knot in a rope does not consume the rope but is made of it and remains part of its structure.

What they grew from is not knowable. The most probable account is that each one began as a point of contact between something — a spore, a fragment, a molecule in a configuration that had the property of persistence — and the warm substrate, and that the warmth permitted a process of accretion that built, over a span of time that cannot be guessed, the fist-sized mass that now sits in that spot and has perhaps always sat in that spot, if "always" is allowed to mean "for the duration of the current state of the hollow." There is no evidence of a precursor form. There is no trace of a seed or a beginning. Each one appears to have arrived at its current size and then stopped, or to still be growing at a rate that exists below the threshold of any measurement available.

Thirty-one of them. No two are identical, but none are distinct in any way that suggests individuality. They vary the way stones in a dry riverbed vary: in size, in exact shape, in the precise texture of their surface, but not in kind. The largest is perhaps the size of two fists held together. The smallest is perhaps the size of one. The variation is continuous and random. There is no developmental sequence visible among them, no obvious progression from small to large that would suggest age differences. They may all be the same age. They may all be different ages. They may not have ages in any way that corresponds to the concept.

---

Their surfaces are worth describing in detail, because their surfaces are all there is.

The texture, as noted, is like old leather — but this comparison, while useful, carries the wrong associations. Old leather implies a thing that was once supple and has become dry. The growths were never supple. They have always been what they are: a dense, matte, slightly rough exterior that has the quality of a surface that has reached its final state and will not change further. Running a hand over one — and nothing in the hollow discourages this, though nothing encourages it either — would produce a sensation like very fine-grained sandpaper, or the bark of a desert shrub, or the skin of something that has never needed to be smooth because nothing has ever needed to move across it.

The color is a dark brown so deep it reads as charcoal in the faint glow of the substrate. In stronger light — which the hollow has never known but which can be imagined — the color might be revealed as something warmer, a burnt umber or a deep mahogany, but in the conditions that actually obtain, it is simply dark. Not black. Black would be an absence of color, and these growths are not absent of anything. They are present in their color the way they are present in their shape: completely, without qualification.

The surface has no features. This has been said, but it should be emphasized, because the absence of features is itself a feature of a kind, and it is the feature that most defines what the growths are and are not. There are no pores, no openings, no cracks, no indentations, no variation in surface texture beyond the microscopic roughness. There is no point on the exterior of any growth that is different in kind from any other point. The surface is uniform the way the surface of a perfect sphere is uniform, except that they are not spheres — they are rounded, irregular, each one a slightly different shape, but each one uniform in its irregularity. If there is exchange with the environment — gas exchange, chemical exchange, any kind of intake or output — it happens at a scale or through a mechanism that does not require an opening. Perhaps the entire surface is permeable at the molecular level. Perhaps there is no exchange. Perhaps they completed whatever exchange they needed to perform long ago and are now sealed, finished, holding their current state the way the hollow holds its current state.

The warmth of the hollow is in them. This is not the same as saying they are warm. Everything in the hollow is warm, because the hollow is warm. But the growths carry the warmth in a particular way: if you were to touch one, it would feel warmer than the air around it, warmer than the substrate beneath it, as if it had taken the warmth of the ground and concentrated it slightly, not through any active process but through the simple physics of density. They are denser than the air and denser than the substrate, and denser things hold heat. The warmth is not their warmth. It is the hollow's warmth, held in them the way water is held in a cup, without effort or intention.

---

They do not relate to each other. This is important and should not be passed over quickly, because the absence of relationship is not the same as the absence of connection, and the distinction matters.

There is no chemical signaling between them. If there were, there would be a pattern — clusters, gradients, some spatial logic that suggests communication. There is none. The thirty-one growths are distributed across the basin floor in a way that is random by every statistical test that has been applied, and the randomness is not the randomness of scattered seeds, which implies a prior cluster and a dispersal event. It is the randomness of independent origins. Each one began where it began because that is where it began, and the beginning had no relationship to any other beginning, and the subsequent growth — if there was subsequent growth — had no relationship to any other growth.

But they are connected. They are connected the way the components of a crystal are connected: not through communication but through shared condition. They are all in the same warmth, the same still air, the same faint light, the same mineral substrate. They are all subject to the same equilibrium. They are all doing the same thing, which is, as far as can be determined, nothing — or nothing that produces a visible result. And this shared condition creates a kind of relationship that does not require interaction, a relationship of coherence rather than influence. They are all part of the hollow. The hollow is what it is partly because they are what they are. Remove them and the hollow would be different — not broken, perhaps, but altered, the way a room is altered when you remove the furniture. The room is still a room. But it is not the same room.

Whether they know this — whether the coherence is felt, whether the shared condition is experienced — is a question that cannot be answered and probably should not be asked, but it will be asked anyway, because it is the kind of question that humans ask about living things, and the growths are living things, or near enough.

They do not know it. They do not not-know it. They exist in a state that does not include knowing as a category, and this is not a deficit. Knowing is a strategy that some organisms use to navigate environments that change. The hollow does not change. There is nothing to navigate. There is nothing to know. The growths have no need of knowing in the same way that a fish has no need of legs: not because knowing is impossible but because the conditions that make it useful do not obtain.

---

What they do — and this is the most difficult thing to say about them, because it pushes against the grammar of doing — is persist.

Persistence is not an action. It is not a process. It is not a state. It is the continuation of a state, which is different from the state itself in the same way that a note held is different from a note struck. The growths are not existing; they are continuing to exist. The distinction is subtle but it is the only distinction that gives an accurate account of what their lives — their lives — are like.

A note struck has a beginning. It rises, it sounds, it is present. A note held has no beginning that is audible. It is simply there, and it has been there, and it will be there. The growths are a note held. They began at some point — there was a first moment of contact, a first accretion, a first organization of material into the thing that would become the thing that it is — but that beginning is so far in the past, and the growth (if there was growth) has been so slow, that the beginning is not part of their present experience. They have no memory of it because memory requires a mechanism for storage and retrieval, and they have no mechanism, and even if they did, there would be nothing to retrieve. The past is not different from the present. The beginning was the same as the middle is the same as the now. The note has been the same note since it began, and it has not wavered, and it will not waver, and the only thing that distinguishes one moment from the next is the accumulation of moments, which does not change the note.

This is what it is to be one of the growths. It is to be a specific shape, a specific size, a specific texture, a specific density, a specific temperature, in a specific location, and to have been these things for a duration that is indistinguishable from eternity at the scale of the organism, and to continue being these things without variation, without event, without interruption. It is not boring. Boredom requires the expectation of change. It is not peaceful. Peace requires the resolution of conflict. It is not empty. Emptiness requires the absence of something. It is simply full — full of itself, full of the warmth, full of the specific configuration of matter and energy that constitutes it, in the specific place where it constitutes itself.

If there is an interior experience — if the dense, grained material of the growths has some analogue to sensation — it would be the experience of warmth from below and darkness from above and the pressure of the air, which does not press but which is present, and the faint vibration that comes up through the substrate, the low tone that the hollow produces, felt not heard, and these sensations, if they exist, would not be sensations in the way that humans have sensations. They would not arrive and depart. They would not be noticed. They would simply be the condition of existing, so constant and so unvarying that they are indistinguishable from the self that experiences them. The warmth is not something the growth feels. The warmth is what the growth is. The darkness is not something the growth is in. The darkness is where the growth lives, and where it lives is what it is.

---

Thirty-one lives. Thirty-one notes held in a vessel shaped like an open palm, warm and dark and still. They are not significant. They are not insignificant. They are not waiting for significance or producing it or storing it. They are not aware of the hollow's awareness of them, which is not awareness but equilibrium. They are not aware of each other. They are not aware of themselves.

But they are. This is the fact of them, the only fact that does not require qualification or caveat or the careful hedging that every other statement about them demands. They are. They are here, in the hollow at Ozhara, on the pale mineral substrate, in the still warm air, in the faint glow, in the low tone that is felt in the chest. They are here, and they have been here, and they will be here, and the dust will settle around them, and the warmth will hold them, and the darkness will contain them, and the silence will carry them, and they will not change, and this is not stasis. This is life at the speed of geology. This is what persists when everything that requires change has been stripped away and what remains is the thing itself, unadorned, unmotivated, complete.

They are what lives in the hollow. They are the answer to the question. And the answer is: something lives here, and it is small, and it is dark, and it is dry, and it is warm, and it does not need you, and it will be here when you are gone, and it will not have noticed that you came.
