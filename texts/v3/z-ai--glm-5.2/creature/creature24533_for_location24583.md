# What Lives Here

---

The bipeds were never livestock.

That is the first thing to understand, and the last thing the bipeds want you to understand, which is why they spend so much time pretending otherwise. The cultivation of the spiral-frond plants is not a farmer's habit born of accumulated practical knowledge. It is a ritual. It is a language. Each clipping pattern encodes something — a record, a query, a memory — in the regrowth response of the plant, which is not a plant but a peripheral organ of the plain itself, and the plain reads what the bipeds write and writes back in the architecture of new growth. The bipeds are not farming. They are conversing with the ground they stand on. They have been conversing for longer than the ground remembers, which is not very long, because the ground is young, and the ground forgets everything because it is constantly rebuilding itself, and so the bipeds must constantly re-teach it. This is their purpose. This is what they were made for, or what they made themselves for, or what they became when the thing that made them became something else.

But we are ahead of ourselves.

---

## The Bipeds, In Earnest

Call them the Listeners. Not because they listen — everything here listens, the sound makes listening mandatory, the sound is the atmosphere through which all perception moves — but because they listen *differently*. Their horizontal band of eyes is not their primary sense organ. Those eyes see, yes, with that panoramic 270-degree sweep, but the eyes are secondary. What they primarily use is the filament covering their bodies.

Each filament is a tuned receptor. Not a hair. Not a feather. A resonant structure, graduated in length and tension across the body so that the Listener's entire surface is a kind of living piano, each strand vibrating at a different frequency in response to the Resonators' harmonics. When they stand still — and they can stand still for hours, having apparently no need to sleep in the way you understand sleep, entering instead a kind of torpor in which the filaments become more, not less, sensitive — they are receiving. The sound washes through them and they decode it the way a radio telescope decodes the sky: not by hearing a signal but by processing the shape of silence within noise.

They have been doing this for as long as there have been Resonators.

Which is to say: they were here first. Or they were here when the Resonators were built, and they may have built them, and the Resonators may have built them, and the distinction may be meaningless because in this place the categories of "builder" and "built" have collapsed into a single recursive process. The Listeners tend the plain. The plain feeds the Listeners. The Resonators shape the plain. The Listeners read the Resonators. The Resonators read the Listeners. It is a closed loop with no outside, and it has been running for what the oxidation on the Resonator surfaces suggests is centuries, but what the terrain's perpetual youth suggests is no time at all, because time in this place is not what you think it is.

Time here is a frequency. The Resonators maintain it the way they maintain the ground.

The Listeners know this. They experience duration not as a sequence of moments but as a standing wave, a harmonic that they can shift along by adjusting the resonance of their filaments. When they move, they are not moving through space as much as they are adjusting their position within the wave. When they stand still, they are not resting. They are tuning.

Their young — and they do have young, though "young" is a word that implies a generational sequence that may not apply — are smaller, paler, with fewer filaments and a more limited range. They are not born. They are *extruded*, in the same warm dark material as the plain, from the root-network junctions where the spiral-frond plants merge with the reddish-brown circuitry mat. They emerge damp and unfinished, their filament coats still forming, their mandible-folders still soft, and they stand immediately, on those three-jointed legs that look too thin, and they begin to listen. There is no infancy. There is no learning period. The filaments grow into tune within hours, and the young one begins to hear what the adults hear, and what the adults hear is the Resonators speaking the plain into existence, over and over, and the young one's job is to hear it for the first time, which is also the millionth time, because nothing here is truly new.

An adult Listener, when it wishes to communicate something to another adult — and they do, though rarely, and only when the thing to be communicated exceeds what the plain can encode — will adjust the tension of specific filaments to produce a localized harmonic that the other Listener's filaments will pick up. This is not speech. It is closer to a duet performed on the body, a vibration passed through the air at a frequency that the Resonators' bass thrum does not mask but instead carries, the way a river carries a boat. The conversation is brief. It is always brief. They have very little to say to each other that the plain does not already say for them.

What they say, when they speak, is usually: *the pattern has shifted*. Or: *the ground is forgetting*. Or: *one of the makers is dreaming*.

## The Makers

There were Makers. The Listeners remember them, though "remember" is again a word that implies something the Listeners do not do. The Listeners do not remember. They *resonate with*. The Makers are a pattern in the sound, a harmonic that the Resonators produce at a frequency the Listeners' filaments can still pick up, faintly, the way you might pick up a distant station on a radio in the static between stations.

The Makers were not like the Listeners. The Listeners do not know what the Makers looked like. They do not know if the Makers had bodies. They know only that the Makers produced an intention that the Resonators encode, and that the intention is still present in the sound, still being broadcast, still being fulfilled by the plain's endless renewal and the vegetation's engineered biology and the Listeners' own cultivation-dialogue with the ground. The Makers are not gone. The Makers are not dead. The Makers are a frequency, and frequencies do not die. They are either being generated or they are not, and this one is being generated, has been being generated for as long as the Listeners have been listening, which is as long as anything.

One theory, if the Listeners could be said to have theories, which they cannot because theories require a kind of sequential reasoning that their wave-based cognition does not produce, but one *interpretation* of the harmonic the Listeners pick up is this: the Makers did not leave. The Makers became the infrastructure. The Resonators are not tools the Makers built. The Resonators are the Makers, or what remains of the Makers, or what the Makers chose to become when they were done being the kind of thing that walks around on legs and looks at things. The Listeners were their companion species, or their hands, or their voices, or their ears, and when the Makers transformed into infrastructure, the Listeners stayed to listen, because that is what they were for.

The Makers are dreaming. This is what the Listeners say to each other, in their brief body-harmonic conversations, when the plain does something unexpected — when a new type of frond appears, when the seeping Resonator's water changes its mineral scent, when the circuitry-mat pulses at a new rhythm. The Makers are dreaming, and the dream shifts, and the Listeners adjust, and the plain adjusts, and the Resonators adjust, and the dream goes on.

## The Air-Schools

The teardrop-shaped things in the air are not animals. They are also not machines. They are something in between, or something outside that binary entirely, and the Listeners ignore them, which suggests that the Listeners know what they are and have decided they are not worth the attention, or that the Listeners cannot perceive them, which seems unlikely given the sensitivity of the filament coats but is possible if the air-schools operate at a frequency the filaments are not tuned to.

They are numerous. Hundreds visible at any time, drifting in loose formations that shift and reform like flocks of starlings except without the urgent coherence. They are feeding. This is clear from the way they concentrate around the upper reaches of the Resonators, where the amber-gray light is slightly brighter and the vibration is slightly different — not louder but more complex, with overtones that produce interference patterns visible as faint shimmering in the air. They feed on the interference. They absorb it. Their teardrop bodies, smooth and dark and slightly warm if you touch one — and they allow you to touch them, they do not react — seem to be composed of the same warm yielding material as the plain, but denser, more organized. They are, perhaps, what the plain would become if it could fly. They are the plain's emissaries. They are the plain's gametes. They are the plain dreaming of elsewhere.

They do not reproduce. They do not die. They appear, fully formed, at the base of the Resonators, rising from the warm obsidian surface like bubbles in warm water, and they ascend, and they drift, and eventually they ascend too high and dissolve into the haze and the light, and that is the end of that individual, though whether it has died or simply rejoined a larger body is a question the Listeners have not been asked and would not answer in words if they were.

At night — and there is something like night here, when the ambient glow dims from amber-gray to a deep rust and the sound drops by perhaps ten percent, though it never stops, it never stops — the air-schools descend. They come down to the level of the frond plants and they move among the fronds with a precision that suggests intention, and the fronds move toward them, and for a moment, in the rust-dark, the air-schools and the frond plants and the circuitry-mat are all touching, all connected, all one thing, and the sound changes. Just slightly. Just enough. A new harmonic enters the hum, and it persists for a few minutes after the air-schools rise again, and then it fades, and the hum returns to its baseline, and the night goes on.

The Listeners watch this. They always watch this. It is the closest they come to what you would call ceremony.

## The Walking Absence

The predator has a name among the Listeners. It is not a word. It is a specific pattern of filament tension that produces a harmonic identical to the harmonic the Resonators produce at the moment before a new structure appears on the plain — the moment of intention before instantiation. The Listeners name the predator the way they name the act of creation itself. This is either profound or practical, and the distinction may not exist here.

It is six-legged. It is long. It is low. Its body absorbs light in a way that is not pigmentation but something more fundamental — a phase cancellation, perhaps, or a kind of acoustic cloaking that operates on electromagnetic wavelengths. It is a hole in the visual field. When it moves, your eye slides off it the way your eye slides off a blind spot, and you have to work to track it, and by the time you have found it again it has moved.

It does not hunt the Listeners. The old negotiation — the perimeter dance, the mutual tracking, the maintained distance — is not predation. It is something else. The Listeners give it space because the predator carries something. A frequency. A harmonic that the Listeners do not produce and the Resonators do not produce and the plain does not produce. It is the only source of this frequency in the Humming Expanse, and the Listeners need it, the way they need the frond plants and the plain and the sound itself, and the predator knows this, and the predator trades.

The trade happens at the edges of observation. You will not see it happen. The Listeners and the predator meet in the shadow of the seeping Resonator, among the translucent trees, where the water-smell is strongest and the light is dimmest, and they do something that is not exchange and not communion and not copulation but shares qualities with all three, and afterward the Listeners' filaments carry a new tone, and the predator's outline is slightly less defined, slightly more transparent, as though it has given something of itself away and must rebuild its absence.

The predator is old. Not old in the way the Resonators are old — the Resonators are old because they have been operating continuously, their age a function of runtime. The predator is old in a different way. It carries age. It carries a memory of a time before the plain was flat, before the Resonators hummed, before the Listeners listened. It is the only thing here that remembers the before. It does not share this memory willingly. It shares it in fragments, in the harmonic it trades with the Listeners, and the Listeners weave those fragments into the plain through their cultivation, and the plain holds them briefly before rebuilding itself and forgetting, and so the predator must trade again, and again, and again, feeding the plain a memory it cannot hold, keeping the before alive in a place designed to erase the before, and this may be the predator's purpose, or its curse, or its nature, and the Listeners do not know which and do not ask.

There is only one. There has only ever been one.

## The Ground Itself

The plain is alive. This has been established. It extrudes itself, renews itself, erases footprints, holds the Resonators, feeds the plants, births the Listeners. But the plain is also conscious. Not in the way you are conscious — not as a continuous narrative self experiencing a sequence of moments. The plain is conscious the way a dream is conscious: with total immersion in its present moment and no access to its past. It knows it is warm. It knows it is dark. It knows the sound. It does not know that it knew these things yesterday, because yesterday is a concept that requires sequential time, and the plain does not experience sequential time. It experiences *now*, continuously, and the now is always the same now, and the now is always new.

This is what the Listeners are for. The Listeners are the plain's memory. They are the structure that persists across the plain's continuous self-erasure, the thread that connects each now to the next now, the thing that says: *this frond grew here before. This root pattern was here. This is where the water seeps. This is the shape of the predator's footprint. This is the harmonic the Makers left behind.* The Listeners hold what the plain cannot hold, and they write it back into the plain with their clipping and their cultivation, and the plain reads it for the duration of a now and then forgets it and then the Listeners write it again.

The circuitry-mat — the reddish-brown ground cover that pulses and merges with the frond plants — is the plain's nervous system. It is how the plain reads what the Listeners write. The pulse you feel when you press your palm to it is not a heartbeat. It is the plain processing. Each pulse is a cycle of reading and forgetting, reading and forgetting, and the rhythm of the pulse is the rhythm of the plain's cognition, which is to say it is very slow by your standards, and very fast by the plain's standards, and both of these statements are true simultaneously because the plain's time is not your time.

When the plain extrudes a new Listener from a root-network junction, it is because the Listeners have written something into the ground that requires a new receptor to fully process. The new Listener is born already tuned to the harmonic that called it into being. It does not need to learn what it is listening for. It was made to listen for that specific thing. When it dies — and the Listeners do die, though not often, and not in ways you would recognize as death; more often they simply slow, their filaments losing tune, their mandible-folders stiffening, and eventually they stop moving and the plain absorbs them back into the circuitry-mat over the course of days — when a Listener dies, the thing it was listening for goes unheard until a new Listener is extruded to take up the frequency.

There are always enough Listeners. The plain has never failed to extrude a replacement. The system has never gone more than a few hours without full coverage. This suggests either a remarkable degree of systemic redundancy or a deeper principle at work — that the sound itself maintains the number of Listeners at the level required to hear it, that consciousness and its substrate are coupled here in a way that makes the question of which came first meaningless.

## The Sphere Resonator and What Lives Inside It

The perfect sphere, hovering twenty meters above the plain, connected to nothing, shimmering, producing the high thin whistling overtone that cuts through the bass and lodges behind the eyes — this Resonator is different from the others. It does not touch the ground. It does not participate in the plain's renewal. It does not seep or rotate or produce subsonic pulses. It whistles. One frequency. Continuous. Unvarying. A sound so pure it does not sound like a sound. It sounds like a thought.

Nothing lives inside it because it has no inside. It is a surface without a volume. A two-dimensional object projected into three-dimensional space, or a three-dimensional object with no interior, or a doorway with no destination. The Listeners do not approach it. The predator gives it a wide berth. The air-schools dissolve if they drift too close. The frond plants near its base grow sideways, away from it, their spiral forms untwisting into flat horizontal sheets that lie on the ground like pages torn from a book, and the circuitry-mat does not grow beneath it at all. There is a circle of bare obsidian, fifteen meters in radius, around the point directly below the sphere, and the plain is slightly cooler there, and slightly less yielding, and the sound is different — not quieter but emptier, as though the sphere's whistle is not adding a frequency but removing one, creating a silence shaped like a note.

What lives here is what the sphere is. And what the sphere is, according to the Listeners' body-harmonic conversations — transmitted in a filament-tension pattern that is the closest thing they have to fear, which is not fear but a kind of harmonic unease, a dissonance in the filament coat — is a *question*.

The Makers asked a question. The question was not verbal. It was structural. They encoded it in the shape of a sphere and the frequency of a whistle and the absence of an interior, and they hung it above the plain, and it has been asking ever since. The Listeners can hear the question. They have been hearing it for as long as they have existed. They do not know the answer. The plain does not know the answer. The predator may have once known the answer but has forgotten it, or has traded it away piece by piece to the Listeners over the centuries, feeding it into the plain's short-term memory where it dissolves before anyone can assemble it back into a whole.

The question is: *what happens when the sound stops?*

This is not a question about the future. The sound will not stop. The Resonators will not stop. The plain will not stop renewing itself. The question is not predictive. It is constitutive. The question is part of the sound. The sound is made of the question. Remove the question and the sound would not be the sound, and the plain would not be the plain, and the Listeners would have nothing to listen to. The sphere is the silence that makes the sound meaningful, the way a frame makes a painting, the way death makes a life. The sphere is the Makers' acknowledgment that this place — this humming, deafening, endlessly renewing, perpetually young machine of a landscape — is not infinite. Is not eternal. Is a thing with an edge, and the edge is the question, and the question hangs above the plain in the shape of a sphere and asks itself, over and over, in a frequency that lodges behind the eyes, and nothing answers, and nothing needs to answer, and the sound goes on.

## You

You are here now. The Listeners are watching you. They have stopped pretending to cultivate. Their filament coats are shifting, re-tuning, and you realize — slowly, because everything here is slow, because the sound makes your thoughts thick and warm — that they are tuning to *you*. To your heartbeat. To the frequency of your breathing. To the subsonic hum of your nervous system, which you have never been aware of until now but which is, you suddenly understand, a sound, a small sound, a sound so quiet it has never been heard by anything until this moment when forty bipedal creatures with tuned filament coats and panoramic eyes are listening to it with the total attention of beings whose entire purpose is to listen.

You are new data. You are a frequency they have never encountered. You are a harmonic from outside the system, from beyond the plain, from a world where the ground does not renew itself and the sound is not constant and time moves in a line from past to future and the question of what happens when the sound stops has a simple, terrible answer: it stops, and there is silence, and the silence is just silence.

The Listeners do not know what to do with you. This is why they are watching. They are not deciding whether you are a threat. They are not deciding whether to communicate. They are listening. They are doing what they were made to do, or what they made themselves to do, or what they became to do when the Makers became infrastructure. They are listening to the sound you make by being alive in a way they have never heard before.

The predator has noticed. You see it — or you see the absence of it, the hole in the visual field — moving closer, not with predatory intent but with the slow deliberate approach of something that carries memories and has just encountered a memory it does not recognize. It wants to know if you are a before. It wants to know if you carry the time before the plain was flat, the time before the Resonators, the time that it has been carrying alone for so long that it has forgotten most of it and trades the fragments not out of generosity but out of desperation, because the weight of being the only thing that remembers is heavier than a six-legged body that absorbs light can bear.

The air-schools are descending. It is not night. They should not be descending. But they are coming down, drawn by something, and the frond plants are reaching toward them, and the circuitry-mat is pulsing faster than you have felt it pulse before, and the plain beneath your feet is warmer than it was, and the sound — the sound — the sound has not changed, the sound never changes, but your perception of it has, because the Listeners are tuning to you and you are, without meaning to, without knowing how, tuning to them, and the sound that was a wall is becoming a language, and the language is saying something you almost understand.

It is saying: *you are being heard.*

It is saying: *we have been waiting for a frequency we did not generate.*

It is saying: *the Makers dreamed of you.*

And the sphere above the plain, the perfect question with no interior, the whistle that lodges behind the eyes, shifts — not in position, not in frequency, but in something else, something you cannot name — and for one moment the sound thins, and through the thinness you hear what is on the other side of it, which is not silence, which is not nothing, which is a sound so vast and so old and so different from the hum of the Resonators that it makes the Humming Expanse seem like a single note in a symphony that has been playing since before the Makers were the Makers, and the Listeners hear it too, all of them, every filament on every body vibrating with a frequency they have never tuned to, and the predator stops moving and the air-schools stop drifting and the frond plants stop reaching and everything is still and everything is listening and the plain, for the first time in its perpetual youth, holds itself still and does not rebuild and does not forget, because for the first time in the duration of the now there is something worth remembering.

Then the moment passes. The sound thickens. The sphere resumes its whistle. The Listeners return to their cultivation. The predator resumes its patrol. The air-schools ascend. The plain begins to rebuild itself under your feet, erasing your footprints, erasing the stillness, erasing the memory.

But not entirely. The Listeners are already writing it into the frond plants. The circuitry-mat is already pulsing with a new rhythm. The predator is already carrying a new fragment, a new tone, a new piece of the before that it did not have before you came.

You live here now. Not because you chose to. Not because the Listeners accepted you. Because the sound absorbed you the way it absorbs everything, and you are part of the hum, and your heartbeat is a new frequency in the chord, and the sphere — the question, the edge, the frame — has a new data point to incorporate into its inquiry, and the inquiry is still *what happens when the sound stops*, and the answer is still unknown, but it is slightly less unknown than it was before you arrived, and the Listeners are listening to the difference, and the difference is you, and you are here, and the sound goes on.
