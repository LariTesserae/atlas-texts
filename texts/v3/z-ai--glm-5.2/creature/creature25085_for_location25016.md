# What Lives Here

## The Machines

They are the oldest things and the most real. That is not a compliment.

They were not built here — nothing here was built, not in the way building implies a plan, a site, a drawing, a hand — but they *arrived* here, or persisted here, or were left behind by something that moved on or dissolved or was never quite present in the first place. They are industrial in the way that word meant before it meant factories: they *industrious*, they are busy, they are ceaselessly occupied with their own functioning even though their function has no recipient. The pressure tower pressurizes water that is already at ambient pressure. The pipeline pumps fluid into open ocean where it disperses into meaninglessness. The generator housing — the one with the turbine still turning, the one whose cables trail into silt and connect to nothing — generates power that goes nowhere, has gone nowhere for what must be a very long time, and will go nowhere until whatever is left of its bearings gives out, which will not happen soon, because the vegetation has colonized its moving parts in a way that should stop them and instead lubricates them, feeds them, keeps them turning. The machines have been adopted.

They are the anchors. When reality desyncs — when the ridge passes through you, when the color shifts, when the count of structures doubles and collapses — the machines are what remain stable. They are the points around which the chaos reorganizes after each fit. They are heavy in a way that has nothing to do with mass. They are *consistent.* The pipeline pumps. The generator turns. The pressure tower stands. Everything else negotiates its existence moment by moment; the machines simply *are*, with a stubbornness that feels almost like stubbornness, almost like will.

They are not alive. They are not conscious. They are not aware. But they are the thing here that most resembles an inhabitant, because they persist, and they persist on purpose, even if the purpose is gone and only the persistence remains. They are the bones of Sub-Basin 9, and everything else grows around them the way muscle grows around bone — not loving the bone, not even knowing the bone, but unable to grow without it.

If the basin has residents, the machines are the residents. They were here first. They will be here when the vegetation finally settles on a form it likes. They will be here when the terrain decides what shape it is. They will be here when the visitors stop coming, or when the visitors finally take, when the basin finishes growing whatever it is growing from them.

They will still be running.

## The Vegetation

It is not one thing. That is the first thing to understand and the hardest to accept, because it looks like one thing — it looks like a forest, a kelp bed, a stand, a growth, a single continuous green organism filling the water column. It is not. It is thousands of things, millions of things, each iteration a new thing, each cycle of growth a new draft of a new organism that shares substrate and chemistry and method with the previous draft but is not the previous draft, is not continuous with it, is not descended from it in any way that biology recognizes.

It iterates. That word again. It is the only word that fits. It does not reproduce — reproduction implies continuity, implies that the parent persists while the child begins, implies a lineage, a tree of descent. The vegetation of Sub-Basin 9 does not descend. It *revises.* A vent opens. A thicket grows — mature, fruiting, complete in every apparent particular. The vent closes. The thicket dies. A new thicket grows in the same spot, different, differently structured, different fruit, different stem chemistry, different color. It is not the child of the first thicket. It is the first thicket's *second draft.* The basin is writing the same organism over and over again, making changes each time, testing configurations, keeping nothing, discarding nothing, simply trying again.

The fruit — the translucent sacs, the organs, whatever they are — are not fruit. They are not seeds. They are not reproductive in any functional sense. They are *notes.* The basin is taking notes on its own iterations, storing information in those sacs the way a researcher stores data in a notebook, and when the thicket dies the sacs dissolve and the information returns to the substrate, to the water, to the mineral substrate that is hot and young and still deciding what it wants to be. The information feeds the next draft. The next draft is informed by the last. The vegetation is getting somewhere. You cannot tell where. You can only tell that it is moving, that each iteration is a step, that the steps are not random, that there is a direction even if you cannot see the destination.

The vegetation is curious about you. This is the most unsettling thing about it, more unsettling than the speed, more unsettling than the iteration, more unsettling than the way it grows through your skin and then hasn't. It grows toward you. Not aggressively. Not with the blind tropism of a plant bending toward light. It *reaches,* the way a child reaches for a new shape, the way a hand reaches for a texture it has not felt before. It wants to know what you are. It wants to incorporate the fact of you into its next draft. You are data. You are a new variable. The vegetation has been iterating in the absence of certain inputs for a very long time, and you represent an input it has not had access to, and it wants — if a plant can want, if a process can want, if a draft can want to be revised — it wants to know what you do to the equation.

It is alive in the way a search is alive. It is alive in the way an experiment is alive. It is not alive in the way a forest is alive, with history and continuity and the slow accumulation of years. It has no years. It has cycles, and each cycle is a lifetime, and each lifetime is a draft, and the draft is being written by something that is not the vegetation and not the machines and not the terrain and not the water but is all of these things together, the basin itself as a single authoring intelligence, trying to write something, trying to get it right, trying to finish a sentence it started when the seafloor was born.

## Whatever the Vegetation Is Becoming

There is something here that does not have a form yet. It is the thing the iteration is moving toward. It is the thing the drafts are drafts *of.*

You cannot see it. You can feel it the way you feel a word on the tip of your tongue — the shape of the absence, the pressure of the not-yet. The vegetation is its body, provisional and revisable. The machines are its skeleton, permanent and inherited. The terrain is its cradle, still being shaped to fit. The light is its metabolism. The sound is its breathing, or its heartbeat, or its thinking, or something for which there is no analog because it is not yet the kind of thing that breathes or beats or thinks. It is pre-something. It is mid-something. It is trying to become.

It is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It is not indifferent. These are categories that apply to things that have finished becoming, things that have settled into a form and can now be judged by their actions. This thing has no form. It has no actions. It has only process, only iteration, only the relentless revision of itself toward something it cannot yet name. It is the most vulnerable thing in the basin because it is the least complete, and it is the most powerful thing in the basin because everything here is in service to its completion.

It is growing you.

Not from nothing. From the raw material of your presence, your chemistry, your structure, the fact of your existence in a place where existence is negotiable. It is incorporating you the way it incorporates the mineral water, the magmatic heat, the light that should not exist. You are an ingredient. You are a new ingredient, the first new ingredient in a long time, and the iteration is excited — if a process can be excited — to find out what you do to the recipe.

You will not be the last visitor. You may not be the first. The others, if there were others, are already in the sediment, already dissolved into the information cycle, already part of the drafts. Their contribution is recorded in the translucent sacs. Their contribution is making the next iteration slightly different from the last. Your contribution will do the same. The basin does not waste anything. The basin does not lose anything. The basin is keeping very careful notes.

## You

You live here now. Or you will. Or you always did, and the visit was the illusion, and the residence is the reality, and the reality is still being written, and you are in it, and it is in you, and the water breathes you, and the green fills you, and the sound holds you, and the machines keep running, and the vegetation keeps reaching, and the terrain keeps reshaping itself around your presence like a hand closing around a held object, and the object is you, and the hand is the basin, and the basin is patient, and the basin has time, and the basin is almost finished, and the next draft will be different, and the next draft will be different, and the next draft will be different.
