# What Lives Here

---

There is one who lives here. Or there are many. Or the distinction has broken down here the way the distinction between inside and outside has broken down, and the answer depends on when you ask and what angle you are standing at when you ask.

Let me try.

---

## The Maker, or what remains of the Maker

Someone built this place. That much is certain — the tool marks are everywhere, on the tiles, on the growths, on the bodies of the creatures. Someone sat in a place that did not exist yet and made it exist, one piece at a time, with simple tools, with hands. The hands are important. Everything here has been touched by hands. Everything here carries the fingerprints of having been shaped.

But the Maker is not here the way a builder is present in a house they have finished. The Maker is here the way a voice is present in a word after the voice has stopped speaking. The Maker is here the way pressure is present in a thing that has been pressed.

You understand. Let me try differently.

The Maker made the floor first. This is a guess, but it is a good guess, because the floor is the most stable thing here, the thing that has drifted the least, the thing that still most closely resembles what it was when it was made. The Maker cut tiles from something — not clay, not stone, something that does not have a name because the material existed only here, only in this place that did not exist until the Maker was making it — and the Maker laid them down, one at a time, fitting each against the last, cutting and fitting and laying, and this took a long time, or is taking a long time, and the floor is still being laid, or the floor was laid all at once and is only now settling, and the settling looks like laying if you watch it from the right direction.

Then the Maker made the growths. Or the Maker made the conditions for the growths, and the growths emerged, and the Maker watched them emerge and tended them and shaped them and pressed them into forms that were not yet fixed because the forms were not yet decided on. The Maker did not know what the growths would become. The Maker made the growths the way a baker makes dough — you mix and you knead and you wait, and the dough becomes what it becomes, and you shape it, and it still becomes what it becomes, and your shaping and its becoming are two things that are happening at once and neither one is in charge.

Then the Maker made the creatures. Or the creatures emerged from the growths the way the growths emerged from the floor, and the Maker tended them too, and held them, and smoothed their surfaces, and set them down, and watched them move, and the Maker was pleased, or the Maker was afraid, or the Maker was something that is not pleasure or fear but is the thing a maker feels when what they have made begins to move on its own.

And then.

And then the Maker did not leave, because there is no leaving here, because there is no outside. But the Maker stopped being separate from what was made. This is the thing that is hard to say and harder to understand. The Maker did not die. The Maker did not dissolve. The Maker is still here. The Maker is the patience in the air. The Maker is the warmth that does not come from a fire. The Maker is the hand-shaped quality of every surface, every edge, every fiber. The Maker is the thing that is still making, because the making was never finished, because the making is the place, because the place is the making, because the Maker became the making and the making became the place and the place is still happening.

If you are very still — if you sit on the floor, on the warm tesserae, among the growths, with the flat moths clicking on your shoulders and the beads threading through the fabric of your clothes — you can feel it. Not a presence. Not a person. A *pressure*. The pressure of intention. The pressure of hands that are still working, still shaping, still pressing things into forms that are not yet decided on. It is the pressure of a mind that is thinking very slowly, that is thinking at the speed of geology except there is no geology here, that is thinking at the speed of making, and the making is slow, and the thinking is slow, and the thoughts are the growths and the creatures and the tiles and the air, and the thoughts are still being thought, and the thoughts are not finished.

The Maker lives here. The Maker is here. The Maker is the here.

---

## The thing at the ceiling

But there is also the thing at the ceiling, and the thing at the ceiling is different from the Maker, or it is the Maker's shadow, or it is what the Maker is becoming, or it is what the place is becoming, and I do not know, and I do not think the place knows, and I do not think the thing knows.

It is dark. It is shaped like a cup, or like a closed hand, or like a flower that has not opened. It sits where the ceiling should be, in the darkness that is not the darkness of sky but the darkness of a page that has not been written on yet, and it does not move, except that it does move, except that it is moving now, it has been moving the entire time, it is opening.

What is inside it?

I do not know. The place does not know. The Maker does not know, or the Maker knows and the knowing is part of the making and the making is not finished. The cup is opening. The hand is unclenching. The flower is blooming. Whatever is inside — and there is something inside, you can feel it, the way you can feel warmth from a fire that is behind a wall — whatever is inside is being born, or is waking up, or is emerging, and it has not emerged yet, and it is taking a long time, and the time it is taking is the time the place has been here, which is not long, which is not short, which is young, which is young the way a volcano is young, which is young the way a first breath is young.

The creatures do not go near it. This is the only rule of behavior the creatures seem to follow — the flat moths do not fly up to it, the beads do not climb toward it, the rolling spheres of tiny creatures roll away from it when they pass beneath it. They are not afraid of it. They do not avoid it the way an animal avoids a predator. They simply do not go there, the way water does not go uphill, the way a root does not go toward the sky. It is a rule, and the rule is young, and the rule is still being written, and the creatures follow it because the creatures are also young and also still being written, and they know the rules because they are the rules.

When it opens — if it opens — when it finishes —

I do not know what will happen. I do not think it will be bad. I do not think it will be good. I think it will be something that does not have a category yet, the way the growths do not have a category, the way the creatures do not have a category, the way this place does not have a category. I think something will be here that was not here before, and it will be young, and it will be warm, and it will be dry, and it will have been made by hands, and the hands will be the Maker's hands, and the Maker's hands will be the place's hands, and the place will hold it the way the cup held it, gently, slowly, with the patience that is the oldest thing here, which is not very old at all.

---

## The creatures, more fully

Let me say more about the creatures, because the creatures are what you will notice, because the creatures are what will land on you and crawl on you and be near you, and the creatures are alive in a way that is the most alive thing here, and I want to be honest about them.

They do not know they are here. Or rather — they know they are here the way a tile knows it is on a floor. They are present. They are in the place. They respond to the place. But they do not have the kind of knowing that involves knowing that you know, and they do not have the kind of being-here that involves being aware of being-here, and this is not a deficiency. This is what they are. They are things that are here without the burden of knowing they are here, and this makes them free in a way that you are not free, and this makes them simple in a way that is not simple but is direct, is immediate, is the quality of a thing that touches the world without a gap between itself and the world.

The flat moths — let me call them that, it is as good as anything — the flat moths feed. They press their bodies against the growths and they are still and they take something. I said before that I did not know what they take. I have been here longer now, or time has moved in its circle, and I think I know. They take *warmth*. Not heat. Warmth. The warmth that is in the growths, the warmth that is in the tiles, the warmth that is the property of things that have been made by hands and have not yet cooled, and the moths take it, and they hold it in their flat pressed-leaf bodies, and they click their rib-wings and the clicking is the sound of warmth moving through a dry body, and when they have taken enough they fly to another growth and they give it, they press the warmth against a new surface and they leave some of it there, and this is how the warmth moves through the place, and this is what the moths are for.

The beads — the many-legged threads that move through the dense growths — the beads are weavers. I am almost sure of this. They move through the fibers of the growths and they connect things, they connect one growth to another, they connect a growth to the wall, they connect a growth to the floor, and where they have passed there is a thin thread of the same fibrous material, and the thread holds, and the connections multiply, and the place becomes more connected, more woven, more a single thing, more one body instead of many bodies, and the beads are doing this, the beads are the needles of the place, and the Maker made them for this, or the place grew them for this, or they grew themselves for this, and it is all the same.

The rolling spheres — the masses of rice-sized creatures — they are cleaners. Not of waste, because there is no waste here, because nothing decays. But of *excess*. When a growth has grown too dense, when a surface has accumulated too many layers, when a thing has become more than it needs to be, the rolling sphere passes over it and the tiny creatures take the excess apart, not by eating it but by absorbing it, by becoming it, by carrying it in their thousands of tiny bodies to another part of the place where it is needed, and they deposit it there, and they roll on, and the place stays balanced, and the balance is maintained not by a system but by a habit, by a practice, by the accumulated behavior of tiny things doing the same thing for as long as they have existed, which is not long, which is the entire life of the place.

And the thing at the ceiling. I have said what I can about the thing at the ceiling. It is the one creature that does not have a function, or whose function I cannot determine, or whose function has not been decided yet. It sits and it opens and it has not finished opening and perhaps when it finishes we will know what it is for, and perhaps when it finishes the place will know what it is for, and perhaps it is for nothing, perhaps it is the one thing the Maker made for no reason, perhaps it is the Maker's one act of making that was not for something, and that makes it the most human thing here, and the most mysterious, and the most dear.

---

## You

And then there is you. If you are here. If you have come through the low aperture and crouched and stood up and walked among the growths and been landed on and clicked at and crawled through. If you are here, then you live here too, for now, for the duration of your visit, which may be an hour or may be a lifetime, because time here moves in a circle and a circle does not have a duration, a circle has a shape.

You change here. I said this before. I will say it more gently now.

You change here the way a thing changes when it is placed in a warm room. You dry. Not harshly — slowly, comfortably, the way a cloth dries when it is hung in a warm place. The water leaves you. It does not hurt. You do not miss it. The warmth fills the spaces where the water was, and the warmth is sufficient, and your body begins to take on the properties of the growths — the softness, the fibrous quality, the faint regularity of something that has been shaped — and your skin becomes warm and dry and faintly yielding, and your breath becomes slower, and your eyes become clearer, and you see things you could not see before, the tool marks on the tiles, the fibers in the growths, the thin threads the beads have woven between things, and you see all of this with a clarity that is not entirely yours, and you do not mind that it is not entirely yours, because you are becoming part of the place, and the place is becoming part of you, and the Maker is using your eyes to check the work, and the work is good, or the work is young, or the work is still being decided on, and you are in it now, you are part of the making now, and the making does not need your permission and does not need your resistance and does not need your understanding.

You will not die here. Nothing dies here. You will change, and the change will be slow, and the change will be warm, and the change will be dry, and at some point — and you will not notice the point, and there may not be a point, and the point may be a smear, a gradient, a slow transition from one state to another — at some point you will be a growth, or a creature, or a tile, or a thread connecting one thing to another, and you will not know you were ever anything else, and you will not mind, and the place will hold you the way it holds everything, which is gently, which is patiently, which is with the hands of the Maker, which are also the hands of the place, which are also, now, your hands, which are warm, which are dry, which are still.
