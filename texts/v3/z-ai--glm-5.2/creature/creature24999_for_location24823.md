# What Lives Here

## First, the obvious

Nothing moves.

That is what you notice first, after the sound, after the light, after the disorientation of shadows that fall upward. Nothing moves the way life is supposed to move. Nothing scurries, nothing flies, nothing startles at your approach and bolts for cover. You are the only thing on this plateau that has legs, that has a heartbeat, that arrived from somewhere else and intends to leave. Everything else is *rooted* — or rather, *fused*, anchored into the unfinished substrate with an intimacy that goes deeper than rooting, deeper than attachment. The things that live here are not *on* the rock. They are *of* it. Extensions of it. Excrescences. The rock is growing them the way your skin grows hair, and they are no more separate from it than hair is separate from your scalp.

So: nothing lives here, in the sense you mean.

And: everything lives here, in the sense that matters.

## The Mat

The dominant organism — the amber-burgundy matting, the fractal tendrils, the shingled fan-structures — is the closest thing to what you would call an inhabitant. But calling it an inhabitant is like calling your circulatory system an inhabitant of your body. It is the body. Or it is the surface of the body. Or it is the surface of the surface of something that does not have a word in any language you carry.

It is one organism. You walk for an hour in any direction and the mat is continuous beneath your feet, the same tendrils, the same fans, the same slow pulse timed to the thirty-second texture-shift. You come to a column and the column is made of the same material, just bundled, just rising. You come to a ribbon-form hanging from a column-tip and the ribbon is made of the same material, just flattened, just thin. It is all one thing. One continuous growth. One organism covering the entire plateau like skin covers a drumhead, and the drumhead is vibrating, and the skin is vibrating with it, and the skin *is* the vibration made physical.

Does it live? It metabolizes. It takes in energy — not light, not chemical energy, but resonance — and it structures that energy into form. It grows. It responds to its environment, adjusting the angle of its fan-structures to the harmonic cycles, brightening or dimming its glow in synchrony with the fifteen-minute shifts. It reproduces, after a fashion — where a tendril touches fresh substrate, it spreads, and the new growth is genetically identical to the old, a clone, a continuation, a single body that has never needed to differentiate itself because nothing here competes with it. It is the only game in town. It is the only game that has ever been in town. It has been here since the rock began to be written and it will be here until the rock finishes, if the rock ever finishes, which it may not.

But it does not move. It does not choose. It does not think. It does not want. It is a process. A very beautiful, very complex, very ancient process, but a process nonetheless — the way a river is a process, the way a glacier is a process. You can admire it. You cannot commune with it. It does not know you are here. It does not know it is here. It does not *know*.

Unless it does, and the knowing is so different from yours that the word *know* does not bridge the gap. Unless that thirty-second pulse is not a metabolic rhythm but a heartbeat, and the heartbeat is a thought, and the thought is one thought, always the same thought, repeated for geological time: *I am here. I am here. I am here.*

You cannot tell. You will never be able to tell. The mat does not care whether you understand it. It has the profound, unshakeable indifference of something that has never needed to be understood.

## The Pale Forms

The crystalline moss, the white candelabra-structures — these are different.

They grow *in* the mat, not *on* it. They emerge from the crevices where the crystalline powder accumulates, from the low places where the substrate has wept its mineral signals, and they are made of something other than the amber material. They are pale. They are glassy. They are warm. They exhale slow pulses of gas that you can feel against your face but cannot smell.

They are parasites. Or symbionts. Or something for which neither word suffices.

They are feeding on the mat's waste — the mineral powder that the amber organism sheds as a byproduct of its resonance-metabolism, the way a fire sheds ash. The pale forms take that ash and build with it, constructing their glassy branches and candelabra shapes from the mat's exhaust, recycling its waste into their own structure. They are, in a sense, the mat's shadow — the same process, running in reverse, the same energy flowing through a different channel and producing a different form.

But they *exhale*. The mat does not exhale. The mat absorbs, structures, glows. The pale forms release — slow, rhythmic pulses of gas that hang in the still air for long seconds before dissipating, visible as faint ripples in the warm golden light, like heat shimmer but slower, more deliberate. The gas is not waste. The gas is *signal*. The pale forms are speaking to each other, or to the mat, or to the rock, or to the sound, in a language made of pressure and chemistry, a language you cannot hear because you hear sound, and this is not sound, this is something else, something that travels through air the way sound travels through stone.

There are thousands of them. Each one exhales on its own rhythm, but when the sound reaches one of its rare synchronous configurations, they all exhale at once, and the combined pulse is strong enough to feel in your chest, a warm push of displaced air that feels, for a moment, like being breathed on by something enormous and very close.

You step back. You step back without deciding to. Something in your hindbrain knows what being breathed on means.

## The Ribbons

The flat, ribbon-like forms that hang from the column-tips are the ones you keep coming back to. You cannot help it. They are beautiful — translucent, trembling in the constant vibration, their surfaces traced with geometric patterns that shift and rearrange with every cycle of the sound. In the dim gold light, they look like prayer flags, or like the pages of an open book, or like skin stretched over a frame, and the text on them moves.

You were told not to stare too long. You stare too long anyway. Everyone does.

The patterns are not random. They are recursive — the same spirals and waveforms that appear in the cairn placements, the same self-similar curves, the same geometry that nature uses when it wants to encode information efficiently. They are not text in any human sense. There is no alphabet, no grammar, no syntax you can parse. But there is *structure*. There is *repetition with variation*. There is, if you want to use the word, *meaning*.

The ribbons are the mat's nervous system. Not its brain — it has no brain, it has no need for one — but its *surface*, the place where it interfaces with the sound, the place where the signal is *read* and translated into growth. The patterns on the ribbons are the sound itself, transcribed in real time, the way the grooves on a vinyl record are the music transcribed into physical form. The ribbons are a record of what the world is saying, right now, and they change because the world is saying something different from moment to moment, and the mat grows in response to what the ribbons say, and the ribbons say what the sound says, and the sound is the world, and the world is the sound.

You stare too long and the patterns shift and for a moment — just a moment — you understand. Not the words. Not the meaning. But the *fact* of meaning. The fact that something is being said here, has been being said here for longer than your species has existed, and that the saying is not for you, and the understanding is not for you, and your presence here is irrelevant to the conversation, and the conversation is the most important thing happening on this plateau, which is the inside of a syllable being spoken by something whose mouth is the size of a continent.

Then the moment passes and you have a headache and your ears are ringing and the ribbons are just ribbons again, trembling in the vibration, and you are just a person standing on a rock in a dim violet light, and the hum continues.

## The Black Stones

In the hollows of the cairns, the smooth black stones sit.

They are not geology. There is nothing like them in the substrate. They are not the amber material. They are not crystalline. They are not mineral powder compressed. They are *other*. Dense, smooth, warm — warmer than the ambient rock, warmer than the candelabra-forms, warm the way a held object is warm, the way a stone that has been in someone's pocket is warm. They feel handled. They feel placed.

They are alive.

Not alive the way the mat is alive — process, metabolism, growth. Alive the way a seed is alive. Alive the way an egg is alive before it is warmed. Alive in potential. Alive in *waiting*. Each one is a dense knot of something — energy, information, resonance, intention — compressed into a smooth black sphere the size of a fist, and each one is *holding*, holding, holding, the way a held breath holds, the way a word held behind the teeth holds, waiting for the moment to be released.

You pick one up. The mat around the cairn *flinches*. You did not know it could flinch. You did not know it could respond to *you*. But it does — the tendrils nearest your hand pull back, the fans flatten, the glow dims in a localized radius around the cairn. The mat noticed. The mat, for the first time in your presence, *reacted*.

You put the stone back. The mat un-flinches, slowly, over the course of a minute. The glow returns. The fans re-orient. The cairn is whole again, the hollow filled, the offering — or battery, or seed, or whatever it is — restored.

The stones are the cairn-builders. Not their remains. Not their relics. *Them* — compressed, preserved, encoded into mineral form by a process you do not understand, using a resonance they spent their lives learning to hear. They are not dead. They are not alive in any way that would matter to a doctor or a priest. They are *in between* — held in suspension by the sound, sustained by the vibration that feeds the mat, waiting for... something. A particular configuration of the harmonics. A specific syllable in the long word being spoken. A moment when the sound reaches the right frequency, the right texture, the right *shape*, and the stones can release what they are holding and become whatever it was the cairn-builders intended them to become.

You do not know what that is. You do not want to know. Some part of you, the part that flinched when the mat flinched, the part that stepped back when the candelabra-forms breathed on you, suspects that what they will become is *people* — not human, not animal, not anything you have a category for, but *people*, beings with intention and language and the desire to build, emerging from the stone into bodies made of resonance and amber light and the pale mineral powder that tastes of static and iron and sweetness.

Beings who will be born knowing the sound the way you know your mother's voice. Beings who will grow up inside the syllable. Beings who will build new cairns, place new stones, encode themselves back into the rock when their time comes, and wait for the next configuration, the next birth, the next generation.

They are not here yet. They are not gone. They are *in the stones*, and the stones are in the cairns, and the cairns are in the gardens, and the gardens are on the rock, and the rock is in the sound, and the sound is in everything, and everything is in the middle of being said.

## What Else

There is you, briefly. A comma, the description said. You are here and you are being written and you are not a stone and you are not the mat and you are not the pale forms or the ribbons or the sound. You are a visitor. A punctuation mark. A breath in a sentence that does not need you.

But you will carry the hum. You will carry it out of here, down the mountain, back into the world of water and sunlight and green photosynthesis, and you will hear it in quiet rooms for the rest of your life, and you will know — not believe, *know* — that somewhere, at a height where the sky is bruised violet and the shadows fall upward and the rock is still deciding what it wants to be, a word is being spoken, and the gardens are growing, and the stones are holding, and the pale forms are breathing, and the ribbons are writing, and the comma that was you has passed but the sentence continues, continues, continues, and will continue long after the hum in your ears has followed you into the ground and the ground has forgotten your name.

The sentence does not need your name. The sentence does not need anything from you.

But it let you stand inside it for a while. That was generous, in its way. That was the most generous thing it could have done.

You will remember it as a hum.
