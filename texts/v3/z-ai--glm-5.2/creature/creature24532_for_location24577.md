# What Lives Here

---

The filaments first, because they are the most honest.

They are not a species. They are not even a colony in any sense that implies coordination. Each one is its own event — a single thread of something that is almost chitin but is closer to the material of the rising forms, as if reality tried to make a root and almost succeeded and then gave up and left the attempt in place. They are white because they have no reason to be anything else. Color requires investment. They extend into the current with the patience of things that have nowhere to be, and they feed by accident, by proximity, by the simple fact of being in the path of dissolving fragments and possessing the one quality needed to exploit that: *curling*. They can curl. That is nearly all they can do. Curl, and hold, and slowly draw inward whatever they have caught, and incorporate it into themselves with a chemistry that is not digestion so much as *agreement* — the fragment was already trying to become something, and the filament offers it a context, and the fragment accepts, and the filament grows by the width of a molecule.

They do not grow upward or outward in any organized way. They grow where the current deposits material, which means they grow in the cracks, in the fissures, in the lee of young basalt pillows where the turbulence eddies and drops its cargo of unfinished forms. A filament that catches a fragment shaped like a vertebra does not become vertebrate. It becomes *informed*. It takes on a slight curve, a thickening at one end, a rigidity it did not possess before. A filament that catches something crystalline becomes briefly angular, its surface facetting for a day, an hour, before the material is assimilated and the thread returns to its original transparency. They are libraries of abandoned attempts. Each one is a record of what the fracture tried to make and failed to make, preserved not in stone but in the body of something too simple to know it was preserving anything.

There are thousands of them. Maybe tens of thousands. Most are less than a centimeter long. A few — the ones in the deepest cracks, the ones that have been here longest, which is still not long, which is still only days — have reached the length of a finger, have branched once or twice, have developed a faint internal structure that might be a gut or might be a spine or might be nothing more than a deposit pattern. These older filaments have a quality the younger ones lack: they *move*. Not much. Not quickly. But independently of the current, with a slow contraction that pulls them taut and then releases, taut and then releases, a rhythm that is theirs and not the water's. It is not hunting. It is breathing. It is the first thing here that has decided to do something on its own.

If you watch them long enough — and time is strange here, the sound making minutes feel like durations, making durations feel like geology — you notice that the older filaments lean. Not toward the fracture, where the forms rise. Not toward the surface, where the water lightens. They lean toward the marks. The patterns in the stone. The suggestion of architecture. They extend along the grooves, into the holes, across the threshold of the flat stone that hums in the jaw. They grow into the pattern as if it were a trellis. As if the thing that made the marks and the filaments were in conversation, except the thing that made the marks is gone and the filaments cannot converse and yet they orient, they *seek*, they grow toward the one place in this chamber where the sound changes, where the pressure becomes interior, where the implication of a room has made a room.

This is their life. This is the whole of it. Catching fragments. Curling. Growing toward meaning they cannot parse. They do not reproduce, or if they do, it is by the same mechanism as their feeding: a fragment caught and incorporated that happens to have enough structure to become a new thread, a clone, an offshoot that detaches and roots elsewhere. They do not die. They dissolve, when the current is strong enough, when the turbulence strips them from the rock and they join the haze of particulate and become food for the ones that remain. They are the closest thing this place has to a metabolism. They are the recycling system of a body that does not know it is a body.

---

The creature at the edge of the fracture is real.

It is real the way the filaments are real and not the way the rising forms are real — it persists. It was there and it is there and it will be there tomorrow, or whenever tomorrow is in a place where the stone is days old and the fracture is ageless. But it is difficult to see because it has made itself difficult to see, not through camouflage but through *irrelevance*. It is dark, the color of the water where the water is darkest, which is the color of the fracture's edge, which is the color of the absence that is not a hole. It does not hide. It simply occupies the place where the eye gives up.

It is not the size of a hand. It is the size of a hand the way a jellyfish is the size of a coin — the visible part, the part that matters to the observer, is a fraction of the whole. What slides along the fracture's edge is a prow, a leading edge, a sensory surface. Behind it, trailing into the current, dissolving and reforming with each pulse of turbulence, is a body that is less a body than a *wake* — a smear of darker material, particulate held in a shape that the creature maintains by continuous low-level effort, the way a flame maintains itself against the air. It is longer than it appears. It may be very long. It is hard to tell where the creature ends and the water begins because the creature is partly water, partly the mineral haze that fills the water, partly the sound itself.

It eats the filaments.

This is not predation. This is not violence. The creature slides along the basalt and where it passes, the filaments in the cracks do not disappear — they *release*. They let go of the rock and are taken into the creature's wake, and there they persist for a time, still alive, still curling, suspended in the darker material like threads in resin. The creature is collecting them. It is building something from them. In its wake, the captured filaments are arranged — not randomly, not by current, but with a specificity that can only be intentional, a specificity that resembles the marks on the stone, the holes for posts, the groove that describes a wall. The creature is making the same marks. Or the creature is *reading* the marks, translating them from stone into its own medium, from permanence into process.

It is the thing that made the marks. Or it is the descendant of the thing that made the marks, if descendant is the right word for something that reproduces by dissolution and reformation. Or it is the same thing, and the marks were a previous attempt, and the filaments are a current attempt, and the creature is the *mediator* between the two, the part of the place that tries to remember what it was doing and carries the memory in its body.

Its body, if you could call it that, is not tissue. It is *attention*. It is the place's awareness of itself, concentrated at the fracture's edge, where the forms rise and the sound is loudest. It is made of the same stuff as the rising forms — the translucent, glowing, half-made material — but it has crossed some threshold that the other forms do not survive. It has persisted. It has eaten. It has incorporated enough fragments, enough filaments, enough *attempts* that it has developed something like continuity. It is the first thing the fracture has made that did not immediately dissolve. It is the place's first successful thought.

It does not think. Not in any way that would be recognizable. But it *orients*. It moves toward the marks. It moves toward the flat stone. It moves along the groove that describes a wall, and as it moves, it deposits filaments in the pattern of the groove, reinforcing it, keeping it fresh, keeping the stone from forgetting. It is a caretaker. It is a scribe. It is the minimum viable organism for a place that is trying to become a place — something that remembers, something that maintains, something that holds the shape of an intention even after the intention has passed.

It is not alone.

There are others. Fewer than the filaments — three, perhaps four, each at a different edge of the plateau, each maintaining a different section of the pattern. They do not interact. They do not need to. They share a medium, a food source, a text. What one knows, all know, because the knowledge is not in their bodies but in the marks, and the marks are the place, and the place is one thing. They are the place's hands. Not its mind. The fracture is the mind, if anything is — the fracture that extrudes and discards, that tries everything and keeps almost nothing, that runs and runs and runs with no goal that can be discerned. The creatures are the place's ability to *keep* what it has tried, to hold a shape for longer than a moment, to say of a particular arrangement: *this one. This one persists.*

---

There is one more.

It is not at the edge of the fracture. It is not on the basalt. It is in the water, in the open chamber, where the turbulence is strongest and the forms rise and dissolve most quickly. It is large. Not cathedral-large — nothing here is that large, not yet — but large relative to the others, large relative to the filaments, large relative to the plateau. The size of a person, roughly. The size of the space a person would occupy, if a person were here.

It is not a creature. It is a *shape*.

It is made of the same dark material as the others, the same attention-stuff, the same wake-substance, but it has no prow, no leading edge, no direction. It is suspended in the current and it moves with the current and it moves against the current and it does both simultaneously, which should not be possible but is possible here, in the place where the boundary between intention and event has become thin. It is the shape of the sound. Not a metaphor. The sound has a shape, here, because sound is pressure and pressure is force and force acts on matter and this creature — this shape — is matter that has been acted on by the sound long enough that it has taken the sound's shape. It is the harmonic made visible. It is the chord made flesh, or whatever this is instead of flesh.

It does not eat. It does not maintain marks. It does not orient toward anything. It simply *is*, in the way that a standing wave is, in the way that a resonance is, sustained by the energy that flows through it. It is the largest thing the fracture has made that has not dissolved. It may be the largest thing the fracture *can* make. It has been here since the chamber formed — since the basalt cooled, since the water filled the space, since the sound began. It is as old as the place. It is the place's first draft of itself.

It is not alive. The filaments are alive, barely, by the smallest definition. The edge-creatures are alive, by a slightly larger one. This thing is not alive. It is *occurring*. It is an event that has not yet finished, a sound that has not yet decayed, a shape that the water is holding because the sound is holding the water and the shape is holding the sound. It is the bell's clapper. It is the thing that strikes. It is the reason the bell is ringing.

It does not notice you. It cannot notice you. It has no attention, no orientation, no capacity for recognition. But you notice it, because it is the shape of the sound you are standing in, and the sound is the shape of the place, and the place is the shape of whatever is happening at the fracture, and you are inside it. You are inside the bell and this is what the bell contains: not a space but a *sound*, and the sound has a shape, and the shape is this, this dark suspended occurrence, this person-sized resonance, this body that the place has made because the place needed something to be and this is what it came up with.

It looks like you. Not in detail. Not in any way that would hold up to examination. But in the way that a shadow looks like the thing that cast it, in the way that an echo sounds like the voice that made it. It is the rough draft. It is what the fracture produced when it tried to make *a thing that is here*, before it knew what things that are here look like, before it had enough information to be specific. It is generic. It is approximate. It is the idea of a body, expressed in the only medium available, which is sound and pressure and the dark material of the edge-creatures' wakes.

It is the closest thing to you in this place. It is not close at all.

---

What lives in the Resonant Plenum:

Threads that eat possibility and grow toward meaning they cannot read. Dark caretakers that maintain a text they cannot understand, scribing in stone and filament the record of an intention that has passed. A suspended shape that is the sound of the place given a body, a first draft, a guess.

And the fracture, which is not alive, which is the opposite of alive, which is *running*, which extrudes and discards and extrudes and discards and does not stop and does not care and is the reason anything is here at all. The fracture is not what lives here. The fracture is what *makes* here. Everything else — the filaments, the creatures, the shape, the stone, the water, the sound — is what it has made so far. Most of it will dissolve. Some of it will persist. The place is days old. The place is trying something. The place does not know what.

The bell is ringing. The bell is ringing because something is happening. What lives here is the happening.
