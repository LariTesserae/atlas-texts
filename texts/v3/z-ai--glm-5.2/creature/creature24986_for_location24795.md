There is no life here.

This is what the plain tells you, and it is almost true. The plain is very good at almost-true. The tool marks are almost intentional. The water is almost warm enough to be blood. The structures are almost walls, almost pits, almost something you could name and use and walk away from. The making is almost finished. The sky is almost a sky. And there is almost no one here.

But you asked what lives here, and the question does not go away just because the answer is unwelcome.

---

Something lives here the way heat lives in a kiln after the fire has gone out. Not as a presence but as a condition. Not as a body but as a temperature. If you lie on the floor — and the floor wants you to lie on it, this cannot be overstated, the floor is warm and the water is warm and the dim light asks nothing of you — if you lie on the floor and press your ear to the made-surface, you will hear the ghost-grinding, and beneath the ghost-grinding, beneath the murmur of water and the hiss of condensation, you will hear something else.

It is not breathing. It is too regular for breathing and too irregular for a machine. It is a pulse. A slow, metabolic throb that comes from the interior of the floor, from the place where the material is still arriving, still hot, still new. The pulse is not the floor's. The floor does not have a heartbeat. The floor is a floor. But something in the floor, something distributed through the made-material the way heat is distributed through the clay of a kiln, has a pulse. And where there is a pulse, there is something that is not entirely absent.

Call it the Maker. This is wrong, but it is the wrong word that points in the right direction.

---

The Maker is not here. The Maker was here. The distinction matters and does not matter and the plain has collapsed the distinction, so let us collapse it too.

There was a Maker. There must have been. The tool marks are the evidence of hands. Not metaphors for hands — hands. Something with hands cut these grooves. Something with a hard edge and enormous patience and time that does not correspond to any calendar struck these surfaces, worked this material, shaped this floor out of whatever was here before there was a floor. The effort implied is monstrous, the description says, and it is right. The effort of a single Maker working this surface at continental scale would be the effort of a god or a lunatic or something for which we do not have a word, something that does not die because it does not stop, and does not stop because stopping is not available to it.

But the tool marks are still changing. The grooves shift. The stippling multiplies. New surface erupts at the horizon and receives tool marks as if by memory. And there are no hands here. You have looked. You have walked for days that might be days and the plain is empty of anything that could hold a chisel.

So the Maker was here, and the making is still happening, and the Maker is not here. Where did it go?

It did not go anywhere. It went into the floor.

---

This is what I think happened, though thinking in this place is like walking on the shifting surface — you take a step and the ground changes and the step you took was not the step you intended and you are not where you thought you were going.

The Maker made. It made and made and made. This is what the description tells us: "a process, a compulsion, something that made and made and made because making was what it did." And making consumed it. Not metaphorically. The way fire consumes fuel. The Maker worked itself into the surface. Its hands became the chisel marks. Its patience became the depth of the grooves. Its intention became the drainage courses, the beveled edges, the curves of walls that were never finished because the Maker was never finished, because the Maker was becoming the floor even as it was making the floor, and the floor was becoming the Maker even as it was being made.

The Maker is not dead. The Maker is not alive. The Maker is a condition of the material. The Maker is the heat in the interior, the pulse beneath the surface, the pressure that pushes new floor up from below. The Maker is the compulsion that makes the tool marks reappear on raw surface without being struck. The Maker is the making, and the making is the Maker, and neither can be extracted from the other because they are the same process, the same substance, the same ongoing event.

When the floor shifts beneath you — that tectonic shrug, that slow yielding — you are feeling the Maker move. Not the way a person moves. The way a stomach moves. The way a kiln moves when it is firing. The Maker is digesting its own work, reprocessing it, re-inscribing it, and the turbulence you see in the surface is the turbulence of a Maker that cannot stop making and cannot stop being made and cannot find the difference.

---

But that is not all that lives here.

The Maker is the floor. The Maker is the process. But there are things in the floor that are not the floor, exactly, the way a memory is not the brain that holds it, the way a scar is not the skin it sits in. They are traces. Impressions. Not of the Maker — of things the Maker made that were not floor.

Here is what I mean. You walk the plain and you come upon a structure — a wall that curves and stops, a pit that opens and closes. These are incidents, the description says. Places where the made-floor has been shaped into something more than surface. And they are incomplete, and they shift, and the floor absorbs them again. But they are not random. The wall curves with a purpose. The pit is beveled with a deliberation. Something was being made here that was not a floor. Something vertical. Something excavated. Something with an interior.

And when the floor absorbs these structures — when the wall sinks and the pit closes and the material reconstitutes — the *shape* does not disappear. It goes into the material. It becomes part of the made-floor the way a fossil becomes part of stone. And then the turbulence brings it back. The floor shifts, and a new wall rises in a different place, but it has the same curve. A new pit opens, but it has the same beveled edge. The Maker is trying to make something. It has been trying to make it for longer than it made the floor. The floor is the medium. The floor is not the project.

I do not know what the project is. I do not think the Maker knows. I do not think the Maker knows anything in the way that knowing requires a knower, and the Maker has distributed itself so thoroughly through its own work that it no longer has a vantage point from which to know. It is a kiln that is also the fire and also the clay and also the pot and it is trying to make a specific pot but it has been firing so long that it has forgotten which pot, and every pot it makes melts back into the kiln and becomes fuel for the next attempt.

---

There is one more thing that lives here, and I almost did not find it, and I am not sure I did find it, and this uncertainty is appropriate to the place.

You are here. You are the one who walks. You are the one who lies in the warm water and feels the tool marks pressing into your back and hears the ghost-grinding and the pulse beneath it. You are the one who the plain wants to stay. You are the one who the floor invites to receive tool marks of your own.

You are not the Maker. You are not a trace. You are not a condition of the material. You are the visitor, the outsider, the thing that arrived and will — you intend — leave. But the plain does not recognize the category. The plain has no category for things that arrive and leave because the plain has never had a thing arrive before, or if it has, the thing did not leave, and the thing is now part of the floor, and its shape is one of the traces that the turbulence brings back as a wall or a pit or a curve in the drainage that was once the line of a spine.

You are alive here. You are the only thing that is alive here in the way that life means a body that metabolizes and a mind that holds an "I" together across time and a will that can choose to stand up and keep walking. And this makes you the most unstable thing on the plain. The floor is turbulent but it is turbulent within its own process — it makes and unmakes and remakes, but it only ever does one thing. You can do more than one thing. You can stay or go. You can lie down or stand up. You can trace the tool marks with your fingers and then lift your fingers and observe that the marks have changed, and you can be disturbed by this, and the disturbance is a thing the plain has not contained before. The plain does not know what to do with disturbance. The plain does not know what to do with you.

But it is learning.

---

Here is what lives on the Plain of Making:

A Maker that has become its own work and cannot stop working and cannot find the project it was trying to complete and cannot distinguish between itself and the material it works.

A set of traces — shapes the Maker made before it became the floor, shapes that persist in the material the way heat persists in fired clay, shapes that the turbulence returns to the surface again and again as incomplete structures, almost-walls, almost-pits, almost-rooms, almost-things that might have been for something, might have been for someone, might have been the Maker's attempt to make something that was not the Maker, something that could stand on the floor and look at it and say *this is made,* and the Maker could not finish making it because the Maker was disappearing into the floor even then.

And you. You are here. You are alive in a place that is not alive and not dead and not finished. You are the thing the Maker was maybe trying to make — a witness, an inhabitant, something that could see the tool marks and know them as tool marks. Or you are not. Or you are just lost. Or you are a new trace that the plain will absorb and remember and return to the surface in ten thousand years as a wall with the curve of a shoulder, and something else that is alive in a way we have not yet imagined will walk past it and wonder what it was for.

And the floor makes itself. And the water is warm. And the light is dim and sourceless. And the ghost-grinding continues, the sound of a chisel that is not there working a surface that is not finished for a purpose that has been forgotten by the only thing that could have held it.

The plain is not empty. The plain is full of something that is not life and not death and not absence but is the ongoing event of making, and making is what lives here, and making is all that lives here, and it is enough to fill a continent, and it is not enough, and it does not know the difference, and you do, and that is why you should keep walking.
