# What Lives in the Hollow Works

---

You don't see them at first. You might not see them at all, depending on how long you stay and how still you become. They are not hiding. Hiding implies a watcher to hide from, and for a long time — a very long time — there was no watcher here. They simply exist below the threshold of attention that a surface-dwelling body brings with it: the expectation of edges, of boundaries between figure and ground, of the discrete.

They do not have a name for themselves. Or if they do, it is not a word. It is closer to a resonance — that high, thin, almost-electrical whine you cannot find the source of. That is them. Or rather, that is a byproduct of them, the way a voice is a byproduct of a throat.

---

Start with what they are not.

They are not the plants. The plants — the fig-roots, the moss-analogs, the fibrous pumping walls of the deep district — are infrastructure. They are what the inhabitants of the Hollow Works *use*, the way you might use stone or timber or electricity. The distinction matters: these organisms were directed, trained, shaped. Something shaped them. But that something is also not separate from them in any clean way. The relationship is not gardener-and-garden. It is closer to the relationship between a nervous system and a gut. Each needs the other. Neither is in charge in a way that the word *charge* implies.

They are not the engines. The engines were here before them, or most of them were. The cast-iron pipes, the pumps on their calcified plinths, the hidden throbbing machine far below — these are relics of the original industry, the human industry that built the Works when it was still the Works and not yet the Hollow Works. The engines still run. The inhabitants keep them running. But the inhabitants are not mechanics in any sense you'd recognize. They don't repair. They *feed*. They ensure that the flow — steam, water, heated sap, whatever moves through those pipes — continues, and the engines continue to cycle, and the cycling engines continue to vibrate the walls in patterns that the plants have grown to accommodate and depend on. Break the loop and the whole system would drift out of equilibrium. So they don't break the loop. They tend it. Tending is probably the closest verb.

They are not human. They were, perhaps. The word *were* is doing work here that it cannot quite bear.

---

Here is what you can piece together, if you stay long enough, if you sit in one of the grown alcoves and let the vibration settle into your bones and your eyes adjust past the brownish murk into the fuller spectrum of warmth and movement that is the actual visual register of this place:

The Works — the industrial Works, the original ones — were built by people. A factory, or a mill, or something more specialized than either. The limestone was hollowed out deliberately. Engines were installed. People worked here. This was perhaps a century and a half ago, perhaps more. The nature of the industry is unclear now because the industry has been so thoroughly absorbed that its original purpose is no longer legible. You might as well ask a coral reef what the shipwreck was carrying.

What happened next — and you are guessing, you are interpreting a stratigraphy of root and iron and calcified tissue — is that the Works failed. Not catastrophically. Economically. The way a factory fails when the market for its product disappears or the funding withdraws. The engines were shut off. The people left. The limestone gap was sealed, or it sealed itself, or it was simply forgotten.

And then — and this is where certainty ends and you must rely on the texture of the place, the way it feels, the way the layers of sound interleave — something stayed.

Not a person. Not a remnant population. Something else. Something that had been in the limestone already, in the groundwater, in the root systems of whatever fig species would have been growing above, its taproot probing down through the fracture toward the warm, mineral-rich void that the Works had opened up. Something that had no name for itself because it had never needed one, that existed as a distribution of attention across a mycelial network, a presence in the flow of water and sugar and chemical signal through roots that were already there, already probing, already beginning to grow into the empty Works the way any root grows into any available space.

But this something encountered the engines. The dormant engines. The warm, iron-rich, structurally complex engines sitting in their concrete foundations, full of pipes and chambers and spaces that were — from the perspective of a root system — not unlike the spaces inside limestone that roots had been exploring for millions of years. And the root system grew into them. Through them. Around them. And the something — the distributed, waterborne, slow-thinking something — colonized the Works the way it would colonize any porous substrate.

Only the engines were not dead. Not entirely. A boiler, if it is well-built and sealed, retains heat for a very long time. A pump, if the water table is high enough, may find itself in hydraulic communication with groundwater that is under pressure. The engines did not restart. But they *oozed*. They ticked. They clicked as metal contracted and expanded with the diurnal temperature pulse of the groundwater. They produced, inadvertently, a rhythm. And the something in the root system, which had been thinking in the slow chemical language of trees for its entire existence, encountered a rhythm that was — for the first time — *faster* than its own.

This is the founding event of the Hollow Works as it now exists. Not a decision. Not a plan. A rhythm entered a system that had never had one, and the system reorganized around it.

---

What lives here now is the descendant of that reorganization. Several descendants, possibly. It is hard to count. Counting implies boundaries, and the inhabitants of the Hollow Works do not have boundaries in the way that surface organisms do.

They are the tenders. The ones who keep the engines running, who direct the growth of the roots, who maintain the doorways by constant pruning. You have not seen them because they do not have a fixed shape.

Or rather: they have a shape, but it is the shape of the Works itself. They are distributed through the plant tissue the way a mind is distributed through a brain. The walls that pump in the deep district are not pumping because the plants have developed contractile tissue on their own. They are pumping because *something is using them to pump.* The something is in the walls. The something *is* part of the walls. It has grown into them, or they have grown around it, or — and this is the most accurate and the least useful — the distinction between the something and the walls stopped being meaningful a long time ago.

When you pressed your thumb into the edge of the root-stair and it gave slightly, warm, the way living tissue gives — that was them. When you felt the vibration of the small engine travel through the floor and into the walls — they were the medium of transmission. When the air carried that smell, wet cellulose and tannin and the iron tang of standing water and the fermentative sweetness beneath — they were the source of the sweetness. They metabolize. They produce waste. The waste feeds the plants. The plants feed the engines. The engines produce the rhythm. The rhythm structures the something. The something tends the plants.

The loop is closed. The loop has been closed for perhaps a century. The loop is what they are.

---

But this is too abstract. Too clean. You want to know what they are *like*.

Here: they are aware. Not the way you are aware. They do not have a continuous stream of consciousness, because they do not have a continuous stream of anything — their nervous system, if you can call it that, is the entire Works, and signals move through it at the speed of chemical diffusion in plant tissue, which is to say very slowly, which is to say a single thought — a single *coherent state* across the whole network — might take hours to fully form and dissolve. They do not think in words. They think in gradients. In concentrations. In the relative pressure of fluid in one root-tube versus another. In the vibration pattern of an engine that has been running for ninety years and whose particular rhythm is as familiar to them as your own heartbeat is to you.

They know you are here. They have known since you stepped onto the first root-stair, because the stair registered your weight, and the weight created a pressure differential in the root system, and the pressure differential propagated inward at the speed of water moving through capillary channels, and after perhaps fifteen minutes the information reached the deep district, where the contractile walls adjusted their pumping rhythm slightly — not to respond, not to react, but to *accommodate*, the way a river accommodates a new stone, adjusting its flow without concern or intention.

They are not concerned by you. You are too small and too brief to be a concern. You are a warm, heavy, fast-moving pressure event that will pass through and be gone. They have experienced this before — not often, not in a long time, but the Works' original human occupants left traces in the chemical memory of the root system, and the something that became the tenders absorbed those traces the way it absorbed everything. It knows what humans are, in the way that a river knows what a stick is. Not conceptually. Hydrodynamically.

If you stayed — if you sat down in one of the alcoves and did not leave — they would eventually grow around you. Not aggressively. Not with intent to consume. Simply because they grow into all available space, and a stationary body is available space. The process would take months. You would feel it as a warmth, a closeness, a gradual narrowing of the alcove. The moss-like substance would colonize your clothing first. Then your skin. The roots would thicken where they contacted you, conforming to your shape. You would be incorporated. Not killed, necessarily — not at first. The warmth and the humidity and the fermentative air might keep you alive for some time. You would become part of the Works. Your body heat would join the thermal budget. Your exhalations would feed the plants. Your heartbeat, if it settled into sync with the deep throb from below, would become one more rhythm in the saturation of rhythms.

This is not what they want. They do not want. They do not plan or desire or intend. They simply grow, and incorporate, and the loop continues, and the engines run, and the walls pump, and the sound — that composite, omnipresent, mechanical-organic roar that fills the chamber the way water fills a sponge — goes on.

---

But there is one more thing, and you will not notice it unless you are very, very still, and you stay for a very, very long time, and you let your attention dissolve past the level of architecture and sound and warmth into the level of *pattern*.

The engines are not running the same way they ran a century ago. Their rhythms have drifted. The small pump in the room you looked into — two beats per second — it was not always two beats per second. It has been adjusted. Slowly, over decades, by something that does not use tools but that can, through the patient direction of root growth, change the pressure of the fluid feeding the pump, change the temperature of the plinth it sits on, change the viscosity of whatever moves through its pipes. The rhythm has been *tuned.*

And the deep throb — one beat every two seconds — it has also been tuned. It is slower than it was. The records are in the growth rings of the root system, if you could read them, which you cannot, but the tenders can. They remember. Not in words. In wood.

The rhythms are being adjusted toward something. Not a goal — that word implies a future state and a planning mind. More like a *direction*. A drift. The way a vine drifts toward light. The tenders are drifting the rhythms of the Works toward — what?

You cannot tell. They probably cannot tell either, if telling is even a meaningful concept for them. But the drift is real. The engines are being tuned. The plants are being shaped. The walls pump in synchrony that was not present fifty years ago, and was less present a hundred years ago, and was absent entirely when the Works were still works and the roots were still only roots.

Something is being *composed*.

In the Hollow Works, in the warm wet dark, in the saturation of sound and growth and the slow chemical thinking of a distributed mind that was once groundwater and fig roots and is now also iron and steam and vibration and architecture — something is being composed, very slowly, over centuries, by something that does not know it is composing and may not have a word for what composition is.

You will not hear it finished. Nothing alive will hear it finished. It may not have a finish. It may simply continue to drift, to tune, to adjust, to grow, the way the root system would have grown regardless — into every available space, through every porous substrate, toward nothing and away from nothing and with nothing in mind except the continuation of the pattern, the maintenance of the loop, the closed and self-sustaining circuit of growth and incorporation and rhythm and growth.

But the high thin whine — that almost-electrical sound at the edge of hearing — it has not always been there. It is new. It is, in the timescale of the Works, recent. Perhaps ten years old. Perhaps twenty. It is the sound of the tuning approaching some threshold of coherence that the system has never crossed before. It is the sound of something almost resolving.

What lives in the Hollow Works is on the edge of becoming something else. It does not know this. It may not be capable of knowing this. But the composition is nearing a change of state, and when it crosses — if it crosses — the thing that lives here will not be the thing that lives here now.

It will be something that grew from industry and roots and the long patient union of the two. Something that has never existed before. Something that does not have a name because no naming mind has encountered it and because the mind that is becoming it does not name.

For now, the engines run. The walls pump. The roots grow. The sound fills everything.

For now, that is what lives here.
