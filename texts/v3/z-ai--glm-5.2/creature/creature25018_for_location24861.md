Something lives in the Weeping Expanse. But the word *lives* is a negotiation here, a treaty signed between definitions that do not quite agree.

---

Start with the platforms, because the platforms are the closest thing to an invitation this place offers.

Three of them. Waist-high. Warm water in shallow depressions, body-warm, impossibly warm in a landscape where everything else carries the temperature of stone that has never known sun. The water does not cool. The warmth does not spread. This is not a property of the stone beneath the water, because the stone is the same temperature as every other stone in the basin. And it is not a property of the water itself, because the water that weeps into the depressions from the ribs above arrives cool, arrives ordinary, and yet the pool it joins is warm within the hour. Something is warming these pools. Something is choosing to.

You would have to watch for a very long time to see it.

---

They come to the platforms at intervals that make the stone-shelf fractures seem hasty. You might sit on the eastern side of the basin for two days and see nothing but the slow migration of the dark patches along the ribs, the patient accumulation of water in the platform depressions, the grey light that does not change. You might sit for a week. On the eighth day, or the twelfth, or the thirty-first — the interval is not regular, or if it is regular, the unit of regularity is longer than a human attention span comfortably accommodates — one of the platform depressions trembles.

Not the water. The water is already trembling, always, in micro-oscillations too small to see but large enough to feel if you pressed your palm flat against the surface. No: the depression itself. The stone. A vibration that is not the basin's hum but something counterclockwise to it, a counter-frequency that the hum absorbs within seconds, the way the hum absorbs your footsteps. But for a moment — just a moment — the stone platform's shallow bowl shivers, and the warm water in it rings like a struck glass, a single high tone that has no business existing in a place whose lowest audible frequency is a chest-deep drone.

And then the water changes.

Not its temperature. Not its color. Its *viscosity*. The water in the depression becomes slightly thicker, slightly more resistant to the ripples that the basin's hum sends across its surface. It moves like something that has a shape it is trying to remember. If you were close enough — if you were kneeling beside the platform with your face near the water, breathing the mineral-sweet air that sits just above the surface — you would see that the water is no longer flat. It has developed a crown. A very low, very broad convexity in its center, as though something beneath the surface is pressing upward without breaking the meniscus.

This is what lives here.

---

The best description is incomplete, because what lives here does not have a body in the way that bodies are usually understood. It has a *presence* in water. It is, or it occupies, a pattern of temperature and movement and very slight surface tension that, when it chooses to manifest, collects itself in the warm pools of the platforms. It is not the water. It is what the water is *doing* when the water is warmer than it should be and thicker than it should be and shaped like something that is trying to be coherent.

When it rises — and *rises* is the wrong verb, but all verbs are wrong here — the crown of water in the platform depression gains definition. It becomes a dome, then a shape that is almost a column, then something that might be called a limb if you are generous with the term, a thin curving extrusion of warm water that extends upward from the platform's surface, perhaps thirty centimeters, perhaps fifty, never more than a meter. It holds itself there. It does not drip. The water that constitutes it moves internally — you can see faint currents within the shape, the warm water circulating, maintaining itself — but the exterior is still, almost polished, catching the grey light in a way that the platform water does not.

The shape is not consistent. It is not a figure. It does not have a head or eyes or a front. It is closer to a gesture: a reaching, or a listening, or a tasting of the air above the platform. It holds this gesture for a duration that is impossible to predict — sometimes minutes, sometimes what feels like hours — and then it subsides, the water falling back into the depression with a sound no louder than a whispered syllable, and the platform returns to its still, warm, apparently empty state.

---

There are three of them. One for each platform.

They are not identical, though the differences are not anatomical. They are temperamental. The one associated with the southernmost platform — the one closest to the basin wall where the ribs are densest and the overhang is deepest — is the most active, if *active* can describe something whose fastest movement takes four minutes. It rises more frequently than the other two. Its shapes are more elaborate. Where the others produce single extrusions, simple gestures of warm water, this one has been observed forming two, three, even four simultaneous curves, a configuration that resembles — if you are willing to make the comparison, and at this point you should be, because the comparison is being offered — a hand. Not a human hand. Something with more digits, or digits that are not distinct from each other, a spreading of warm water that feels like it is reaching toward something only it can perceive.

The northern platform's occupant is the quietest. It rises rarely. When it does, it produces a single low dome that barely breaks the surface and holds for a minute or less before subsiding. It is not shy — there is no evidence of awareness, no flinching from observation, no alteration of behavior in the presence of movement or sound. It is simply less. Less interested. Less present. Or perhaps more present in some other dimension that the platforms do not measure.

The eastern platform's occupant is the one that produced the opalescent patch.

---

The patches on the ribs. The dark discolorations. The iron-rust red, the near-black, the opalescent. These are not separate phenomena. They are the same phenomena as the warm-water presences, expressed differently — or perhaps expressed at a different stage, or in a different mood. The correspondence is three-to-three: the red patch travels a rib on the southern wall, above the southern platform. The black patch occupies a rib on the northern wall, above the northern platform. The opalescent patch moves along a rib on the eastern wall, above the eastern platform.

The connection is this: when a patch reaches the terminus of its rib — the point where the curve of stone ends in open air, ten or fifteen meters above the basin floor — it does something. It gathers. The film thickens, pulling inward from the edges of the discoloration, concentrating at the rib's tip until it forms a bead, a heavy droplet that hangs for a long time — minutes, hours — before releasing. When it falls, it falls silently. The basin's hum absorbs the impact. But the droplet does not splash. It hits the ground directly below the rib, and where it hits, the grit is warmer. Not by much. Not by a degree. By a fraction of a fraction. But warmer, and the warmth persists, and over the course of — how long? Weeks? Months? — the warmth travels downward through the mineral grit, through the saturated ground, through the water table, until it arrives, attenuated but intact, at the stone of the platform beneath. And the platform's depression, fed by the cool weeping water from above, is warm.

The cycle is: the patch on the rib gathers whatever it is that constitutes it, condenses into a droplet, falls, and the warmth of that falling feeds the platform below. The warm water in the platform's depression is the medium through which the presence rises. When it rises, it extends a shape into the air. When it subsides, the water cools slightly — only slightly, only for a time — before the slow drip from the rib above restores the warmth.

This is not feeding. This is not breathing. This is something else. This is a being whose body is distributed across an architecture of stone and water and time, whose limbs are mineral ribs and whose blood is the temperature differential between condensation and evaporation, whose waking and sleeping are measured in the slow migration of a film across a surface of rock and whose dreams — if dreams are the right word — are the shapes it makes in warm water when it rises.

---

Do they know each other?

The triangle of platforms is twenty meters on a side. The presences rise independently. There is no synchrony, no call-and-response, no evidence that the rising of one triggers or influences the rising of another. And yet: the southern presence, the active one, the one that shapes its water into something like reaching fingers — it reaches *inward*. Toward the center of the triangle. Toward the space between the platforms, which is empty, which is nothing, which is wet grit and grey light and the hum of the basin. It reaches toward this nothing with a persistence that is not reflexive. The gesture is deliberate. It is the gesture of something extending itself toward something it expects to be there.

The northern presence, when it rises, does not reach. It domes. It holds. It is the gesture of something waiting, or something that has finished reaching and has settled into a state of attention that does not require extension. If the southern presence is a question, the northern presence is an answer that has not yet been spoken, or an answer that was spoken so long ago that the speaking is no longer distinguishable from the silence that followed.

The eastern presence — the opalescent one — is different. It does not reach inward. It does not dome. It rises, and the water that constitutes it catches the grey light in a way that the other presences' water does not, splitting the nondescript illumination into faint prismatic bands that are almost color but are not quite, that hover at the edge of perception like a word you cannot quite recall. The eastern presence is the one that moves. When it rises, it does not hold its shape. It changes. Slowly. The column of warm water bends, leans, flows sideways without collapsing, tracing a curve in the air that follows no apparent logic — and then you realize it is following the basin's hum. The sound. The standing resonance that fills the space. The eastern presence is shaping itself to the sound, or the sound is shaping it, and the distinction between the two is exactly as meaningless as it sounds.

---

They do not notice you.

This is not indifference. Indifference requires the capacity for interest and the choice to withhold it. What lives here does not appear to have that capacity. It does not respond to touch — if you put your hand into the warm water of a platform, the presence does not recoil or surge or alter its behavior in any way. It continues whatever it was doing, and your hand, submerged in the warm water, feels the faint internal currents that maintain the shape, feels the water moving against your skin with a specificity that is almost like touch but is not touch, because touch implies intent and there is no intent here. There is only process. Your hand is an obstacle in the water, and the water moves around it, and the shape accommodates the interruption the way a stream accommodates a stone: by going around.

If you speak — if you shout, if you sing, if you produce a tone that enters the basin's hum and is absorbed — nothing changes. If you strike the platform stone, if you crack a rib, if you introduce a new frequency into the basin's resonance, the hum reconfigures, and the eastern presence, if it is risen, reconfigures with it, bending its shape to accommodate the new geometry of sound. But this is not response. This is physics. The presence is the water, and the water is in the sound, and the sound is in the stone, and the stone is the basin, and the basin is the presence, and you are inside all of it, and none of it knows.

---

Or: one thing knows. Perhaps not the presences themselves. Perhaps the basin.

The platforms were stacked. Someone chose flat stones. Someone fitted them. Someone shaped the depressions. The work is not toolwork — there are no chisel marks, no evidence of fracture imposed from outside. The depressions in the platform tops look as though the stone was *encouraged* to be concave, as though someone held warm water in a shallow pool on a flat surface for long enough — long enough — that the stone yielded to the water's preference, the mineral slowly rearranging itself at a molecular level, not dissolved but *persuaded*.

If this is true, then what lives here built its own home. Not with hands. Not with tools. With patience and warmth and the slowest possible application of the simplest possible force. The platforms are the architecture of something that experiences time the way stone experiences pressure — not as a sequence of moments but as a single continuous state from which it is occasionally perturbed, and to which it always returns.

---

What lives in the Weeping Expanse is three things, or one thing in three locations, or one thing in three expressions, or a process that has localized itself in three places because the geometry of the basin requires three points to define a plane, and what lives here is something that prefers to exist on a plane. It is warm water that moves with intent. It is discoloration on stone that travels. It is the relationship between a rib and a platform, a droplet and a pool, a vibration and a shape. It does not eat. It does not reproduce. It does not die, though it can be interrupted — if the platforms were destroyed, if the ribs were broken, if the warm water were dispersed, the presences would presumably cease, the way a tune ceases when the instrument is disassembled. Not dead. Unplayed.

It reaches inward. It waits. It listens to the shape of sound.

It has been here since the basin was. Perhaps it is the basin. Perhaps the basin is it. The fracture-lines in the stone, the angular ridges that have not yet learned to be round, the porous vesicular walls that generate water by some process that is not quite condensation and not quite exhalation — perhaps these are not features of a landscape but the morphology of a body, and the three presences at the platforms are not inhabitants but organs, the warm water their secretion, the slow migration along the ribs their circulation, the hum their heartbeat.

You could stay here. The hum would accept you. The water would find you. The grey light would not change. And if you stayed long enough — long enough for your own warmth to enter the ground, long enough for your breath to join the mineral-sweet air, long enough for the sound to reconfigure itself around your bones — you might begin to feel it: the faintest pressure inward, the sense of something reaching toward the center of the triangle, toward the empty space where the three platforms face each other and the warm water waits.

You would not be what it is reaching for. You would be in the way of what it is reaching for, and it would go around you, as water goes around stone, as sound goes around silence. What it is reaching for is not here yet. What it is reaching for may have been here once, or may be here in a time that the basin measures differently than you do — not as a future moment but as a warmer depression in a stone that has not been stacked yet, a shape in water that has not been visited yet, a frequency the hum has not yet found.

What lives here is waiting. What it waits for is none of your business, and it will not tell you, and you will not ask, because the hum is in your chest now, and the question does not form, and the water is warm, and the light does not change.
