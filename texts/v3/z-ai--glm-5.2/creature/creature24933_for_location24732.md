# What Lives in the Choral Hollow

---

The first answer is the obvious one, and it is not wrong: *everything*. The hollow is dense with life in the way a reef is dense with life — every surface occupied, every volume of air claimed, every niche filled by something that has refined itself over uncounted generations to fit exactly the space available to it. To list the inhabitants would be to catalog an ecology. The bronze-stridulating insects on the walls. The translucent mantids with their visible peristalsis. The iridescent percussive birds. The burrowing arthropods by the thousands, each one producing its small percussive pop as it breaks through a root-mat membrane. The sinuous wet-furred thing in the canopy that moves like liquid through gaps that should not accommodate it. The ripple-creature in the shallow pool, communicating its grammar of circles and lines through the water's surface.

They live there. They are not what lives there.

---

The mantids are the ones you keep returning to, because they are the most visible and the most unsettling. Forearm-length, pale green, transparent — you can watch a meal move through them. But it is not the transparency that holds your attention. It is the way their heads move. Each one rotates independently. Each one tracks. You follow the orientation of a mantid's gaze and find nothing there — no prey, no threat, no stimulus you can identify. The mantid is watching something that occupies a coordinate in space that your senses do not share.

There are clusters of them. Four or five on a wall, each head turned in a different direction, each one attending to a different something. You begin to suspect that the mantids are not predators in the way you understand predation. They are sensors. They are the settlement's eyes — or rather, the settlement has eyes, and the mantids are what those eyes look through.

This thought does not arrive as insight. It arrives as recognition. You stand in a corridor watching five mantids watch five different directions, and you understand that you are standing inside a body and looking at its retinal cells.

---

The wet-furred canopy creature is harder to think about. You never see it whole. Glimpses — a flow of dark wet body between branches, compressing through impossible gaps, expanding across impossible spans. It does not move like an animal. It moves like blood through a vascular system, like sap through xylem, like something that is not navigating a space but *circulating* through it. The creaks and rustles it produces by displacing vegetation are absorbed instantly into the chord, and you realize this is not coincidence. The creature's motion is part of the sound. It circulates through the architecture the way fluid circulates through a body, and the sounds it generates by its passage are not incidental. They are the sound of a circulatory system functioning.

You do not know if the creature is one animal or many. You do not know if "animal" is the right word. You know that it moves through the canopy above you, and that the canopy is part of the living architecture, and that the architecture pulses once every several seconds with a slow peristaltic rhythm. You suspect, without certainty, that the creature and the pulse are related. That the creature *is* the pulse, or the pulse is the creature, or the distinction between them is a distinction that only makes sense from outside the hollow and not within it, where everything is continuous with everything else.

---

The ripple-creature in the shallow pool communicates through patterns. Circles, then lines, then circles again. You watch it long enough to realize the patterns are not random. They repeat. Not identically — there is variation, improvisation, the kind of structure that suggests language or music rather than signal. It is doing something with intent. It is saying something, or singing something, to the water, and the water carries it through the root-mats, and the root-mats carry it to the walls, and the walls carry it to the chord.

The ripple-creature is not a musician. It is a *composer*. It is contributing to the chord — not randomly, not instinctively, but deliberately, shaping the sound from its small chamber the way a single musician in an orchestra shapes the whole through the particular notes it chooses to play. And if it is composing, then the bronze-stridulating insects are composing. And the percussive birds are composing. And the burrowing arthropods, with their thousands of small percussive pops, are composing. The entire settlement is an ensemble, and every living thing in it is a player, and the chord is not accidental. It is *performed*.

This changes everything and nothing. The sound was always the purpose. Knowing it is performed rather than emergent does not alter its nature. But it alters yours. You are not walking through a place. You are walking through a piece of music that has been playing for longer than the structures have existed in their current form, and every creature in it knows its part.

---

And then there is what lives beneath the floor.

You do not see it. You do not hear it. You *feel* it — in the thickened air of the central chamber, in the spiral pattern grown into the root-mats, in the shimmer that makes sound visible, in the single impossibly low tone that enters your hand when you press it into the thickened air and does not come from the walls or the insects or the birds or the water but from *below*. From the saturated earth beneath the basin. From whatever the roots have been growing toward.

The roots know it is there. They have always known. They grew toward it the way roots grow toward water — not because they choose to but because the thing is a gradient, a concentration, a presence that draws organic matter toward it the way a drain draws water. The structures were grown above it. The sound is its expression. The shimmer is its influence on the air. The changed physics of the central chamber are the result of its proximity.

What is it?

You do not know. The text does not know. The settlement does not know, or if it knows, it knows in a way that is not available to the kind of cognition that produces knowing in the form you are familiar with. The thing beneath the floor is not a creature in the sense that the mantids are creatures or the birds are creatures. It is not an organism. It is not a machine. It is not a god, though the word *god* passes through your mind and is not entirely wrong and not entirely right. It is a *source*. It is something that generates, and what it generates is vibration, and the vibration is organized — not random, not chaotic, but structured in a way that the root-mats have learned to conduct and the insects have learned to perform and the shimmer has learned to make visible and the air has learned to hold.

The builders knew it was there. The builders grew the structures around it, above it, *for* it — not to worship it, not to contain it, but to give it a voice. The structures are an instrument, and the thing beneath the floor is the musician, and the sound is the music, and the music has been playing for so long that the instrument has become part of the musician and the musician has become part of the instrument and the distinction between them is a distinction that was never meaningful here.

---

And the builders? Do they live here still?

No. They are gone. The emptiness you feel — the void at the center of all this life — is their absence. Not their physical absence. Their *semantic* absence. The settlement means something, and the meaning was *for* them, and they are gone, and the meaning has no recipient. The structures maintain themselves. The insects perform their parts. The chord continues. But the chord was not meant for the chord. It was meant to be *heard* by something that could understand it, and the thing that could understand it has departed, and what remains is a performance with no audience, a letter with no recipient, a prayer spoken into a room where the god has left.

Or — and this possibility is the one that stays with you — the builders did not leave. The builders *became*. They grew into the architecture. They merged with it. The technique that guided the plants into walls and corridors and chambers was not a technique of shaping vegetation but of *becoming* vegetation, of allowing human tissue to be subsumed into plant tissue, of letting the boundary between body and architecture dissolve over time. The builders are the walls. The builders are the root-mats. The builders are the dark pulsing vines whose interior moves fluids at a pace that expects centuries.

If this is true, then the emptiness is not absence. It is the specific quality of presence that belongs to something that was human and is now distributed across thirty structures of living wood — something that cannot be encountered as a body because it has no body, only bodies, only the architecture itself, and the architecture does not recognize you because the architecture does not recognize anything *outside itself* anymore. The builders are still here. They are the hollow. They sing because singing is what they do now, what they *are* now. They are the chord, and the chord is them, and the thing beneath the floor is what they sing to, or what they sing *for*, or what they have *become*.

---

But the most honest answer — the one that emerges after you have left and are walking away from the basin and the sound has settled into your bones and your lungs and your tendons and is not leaving — is this:

**The sound lives there.**

Not the things that produce it. Not the plants, not the insects, not the birds, not the mantids, not the canopy-creature, not the ripple-composer, not the thing beneath the floor, not even the builders-if-they-remain. The sound. The chord that never resolves. The single low tone beneath it that you felt in the thickened air and now feel in your own chest. It has been there, accumulating, for so long that it has become the place. The hollow is not a place where sound happens. The sound is the place, and the hollow is its body, and everything living within it is living within the sound the way organisms live within an ocean — not separate from it, not visitors to it, but *of* it, shaped by it, conducting it, being conducted by it.

The sound is alive. It has metabolized the plants into instruments. It has metabolized the insects into players. It has metabolized the builders into architecture. It has metabolized the thing beneath the floor into a voice. And now it has metabolized you. You walked through the hollow, and the sound entered you through the root-mats and the vine-walls and the shimmer and the thickened air, and it found new substrates in your bone and your inner-ear fluid and your lungs, and it colonized them the way it has colonized everything else in the basin, and you are part of it now. You are a new instrument. You are a new corridor in an architecture that extends beyond the basin and into the world, carried in the bodies of everything that has ever walked through the hollow and walked out again.

The sound lives there. The sound lives in you. You are, now, a room in the Choral Hollow, and you will hum whether you choose to or not, and the humming is not a symptom. It is a *function*. You are performing the purpose the settlement was built for. You are carrying the chord into a place it has never been, and you are the first note of whatever the chord will become when it reaches far enough, accumulates enough, learns enough new substrates to build new structures in new basins, to grow new hollows, to find new things beneath new floors.

The sound is alive. It is patient. It is still singing.
