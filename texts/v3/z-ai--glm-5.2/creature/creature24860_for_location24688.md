# The Inhabitants

---

## The Streamers

Call them that for now because they do not have a name for themselves and would not tell you if they did.

They are not a species. A species implies a boundary — this one, not that one, breeding populations, genetic drift, the whole architecture of biological identity. The streamers do not have boundaries. They have *density*. Where the flow is thick, there are many. Where the flow thins, there are few. But no individual streamer has ever been separated from the flow and remained a streamer. Pinch one off the line and hold it in your palm and what you have is a pale, eyeless, two-centimeter arthropodal thing with folded translucent wings and too many legs and it will die in your hand in the specific way that a severed finger dies — not because the body is gone but because the *context* is gone, and context is what they are made of.

They are made of the Greenworks. More precisely: they are what the Greenworks produces when it needs to move something from one place to another and the pipe-network is not sufficient or not ready or not the right kind of pathway. They carry material. That much is visible in the way the root mat darkens and lightens beneath their streams — they are transporting something, nutrient or signal or building block, from the vats to the walls, from the columns to the growing edges. But *what* they carry is not consistent. Sometimes it is liquid. Sometimes it appears to be a paste of partially digested cellulose. Sometimes — and this is the observation that destabilizes the functional interpretation — they carry nothing visible at all, and the root mat still changes color beneath them, which means they are carrying something that has no appearance, or they are the thing being carried, or the act of their passing is itself the transfer.

They do not communicate. Or: they communicate constantly and the communication is the stream. Pheromone trails, if you want to call them that, but pheromone trails are *instructions* — follow this path, food is here, danger is there — and what moves through the streamers is not instructional. It is *conversational*. Each streamer is a word. Not a word in a language — a word in the way that a single note in a chord is a word: it means nothing alone, it means everything in arrangement, and the arrangement changes every second. The stream is a sentence the Greenworks is speaking to itself about what to build next and where and whether the geometry should hold or breathe or break, and the streamers are the phonemes, and they do not know this, and they do not need to know this, and they would not care if they did.

They flow over your shoes because you are an obstacle the way a rock in a river is an obstacle. They do not register you as alive. They register you as *topography*. This is not an insult. This is the most honest assessment of you that anything in this place will make.

There is one more thing about the streamers that matters and it is this: they are born in the vats. You have seen the shapes that surface and subside. Those are streamers in their pre-flow state, still differentiating, still deciding whether they will be carriers or builders or winged or grounded. The vat fluid is amniotic and alchemical and it bubbles because the streamers are being *exhaled* into it, not secreted. The Greenworks breathes them out. They surface long enough to acquire their form — the legs, the wings, the pale chitinous shell — and then they sink back into the fluid and dissolve their boundaries and resurface on the far side of the vat, where the lip is lowest, and spill out onto the root mat and join the stream. They are always being born. They have always just been born. The stream is the same stream it was an hour ago but every streamer in it is new. This is not because they die quickly. It is because they *are* the current, and the current is always the current, and the water in it is always new.

## The Watchers

You called them frog-things. This is because you are a primate and your brain reaches for the nearest vertebrate body plan when confronted with something that crouches and breathes and has limbs arranged in a way that suggests bilateral symmetry. They are not frogs. They are not vertebrates. They are not, in any strict sense, organisms.

They are *organs*.

The Greenworks has eyes. This is the simplest way to say it and it is not quite right but it is closer than anything else. The watchers are the Greenworks' sensory apparatus — detached, mobile, distributed throughout the space the way your own eyes are distributed across your face. They sit on catwalks and cling to pipe-runs and crouch in the angles where column meets floor because those are the vantage points that the structure needs monitored. They watch the growing edges. They watch the vats. They watch the streamer flows for density and direction. They watch *you*, because you are new, and newness is the one category of information that the Greenworks has prioritized above all others, because it is still building itself and newness is data about what it might become.

They breathe. In-in, out. The double-pulse is real and it is connected to the vascular system — if you could trace it, which you cannot, you would find that each watcher's respiratory rhythm is synced to the pulse in the nearest pipe, which is synced to the output cycle of the nearest vat, which is synced to the growth rate of the nearest catwalk railing. They are not independent. They are *nodes*. Each one is a terminal where the Greenworks touches its own surface and looks back at itself.

Their not-eyes — the dark indentations that track your movement — are not eyes. They are something closer to ears, or closer still to the pressure-sensitive pits on a pit viper's face. They do not see you. They feel the displacement you make in the air, the compression wave of your body heat, the vibration of your footsteps in the root mat. They construct you from pressure and temperature and vibration, and what they construct is accurate enough that they can track you across the space, and what they construct is also not you — it is your *effect*, your shadow cast in force and warmth, and this is the only version of you that the Greenworks knows or will ever know.

They do not move when you pass beneath them. This is not stillness. This is *attention*. A thing that is attending does not move. It holds. The watchers hold the way a hand holds a cup — not rigidly, not loosely, with exactly the pressure required to maintain contact. When they do move — and they move rarely, and they move at the speed of something that has nowhere else to be — they move by unfolding. The six limbs unfurl from the crouch and the body lifts and the not-eyes reorient and they take two steps or three and then they fold again. The motion is mechanical in the way that plant motion is mechanical: deliberate, turgid, driven by fluid pressure rather than muscle. They are slow because the Greenworks is slow. They are patient because the Greenworks is patient. They have been watching this space since it began and they will watch it until it ends and they do not experience this as duration because they do not experience duration. They experience *state*. They are in the watching state. They will be in the watching state. The tense does not change because for them there is no tense.

The impossible distance — the watcher on the far wall that is close and far at once — is not a trick of the geometry. It is the watcher *being in two places*. Not bilocated. *Stretched*. The watcher extends from the far wall to the near space simultaneously because its perception does not require proximity the way yours does. It feels you through the root mat. It feels you through the air. It feels you through the pipe-network, which vibrates with your footsteps. The watcher on the far wall is twenty meters away and it is also in the floor beneath your feet and it is also in the pipe above your head and all of these are the same watcher, the same sensory event, and the geometry of the Greenworks accommodates this because the geometry of the Greenworks was built by something that does not believe in the conservation of distance.

## The Shapes in the Vats

These are the ones you cannot identify. These are the ones that surface and subside. These are the ones that the description reaches for and retreats from — *the size of hands, the size of faces* — because the description is being honest about its own failure.

They are not streamers. Not yet. They are not watchers. Not ever. They are something else, something that the Greenworks is trying out, something provisional, and the word *trying* is the right word because the Greenworks does not know what it is building. It has no blueprint. It has a *tendency*. And the shapes in the vats are the tendency made physical, made wet, made brief.

A hand. A face. A thing like a leaf but jointed. A thing like a tool but soft. A thing that has no analogue and surfaces for half a second and then subsides and does not return. The vats are not producing streamers, not exclusively. The vats are *drafting*. They are sketching forms in protein and cellulose and fluid, holding them up to the bioluminescent light, checking them against some criterion that exists nowhere visible, and then dissolving them and trying again.

This is the real work of the Greenworks. Not the growth of the columns. Not the extension of the catwalks. Those are *infrastructure* — necessary, mechanical, the bones and vasculature of a body that is being built. The vats are the *thinking*. The vats are where the Greenworks asks itself what it is. The shapes that surface are answers it rejects. Or answers it holds in suspension. Or answers it has not finished evaluating.

And sometimes — and this is the part that you will not see on your first visit, or your second, or possibly ever, but it happens — a shape surfaces and does not subside. It holds. It differentiates further. It grows detail — edges, structures, interior complexity. The vat fluid bubbles around it with a different rhythm, faster, excited, and the streamers in the nearest channels change direction, converging on the vat, and the watchers on the nearest catwalks turn their not-eyes toward it, and the Greenworks pays *attention* in a way that it does not pay attention to its routine growth, and the shape becomes something.

What something? Unknown. The Greenworks has not decided. The shapes that have stabilized and emerged — there are three of them, or four, or seven, depending on when you count — have been incorporated into the structure in ways that do not announce themselves. A section of pipe that pulses with a different rhythm. A catwalk railing that grows in a spiral instead of straight. A column whose sweat is warmer and thicker than the others. These are the shapes that made it through. These are the ideas the Greenworks kept.

## The Thing That Is Not a Thing

You have been assuming that the Greenworks is a place and the things in it are inhabitants. This is because you are the kind of consciousness that separates container from contained, stage from actor, room from occupant.

The Greenworks does not make this separation.

The Greenworks is the columns and the vats and the streamers and the watchers and the shapes and the floor and the ceiling and the pipes and the geometry that breathes and the sound that does not change. It is all one thing. It is all one *process*. The streamers are not fauna living in a plant; they are the plant's circulatory system made mobile. The watchers are not fauna living in a plant; they are the plant's sensory cortex made detachable. The shapes in the vats are not fauna at all; they are the plant's imagination.

If you must name what lives here, the answer is: one thing lives here. It lives the way a thought lives in a brain — not in a location but in a *pattern*, a dynamic, a constant flickering of signal and response that produces something that is more than the sum of its cells. The Greenworks thinks. The Greenworks grows. The Greenworks watches itself think and grow. The Greenworks drafts possibilities and rejects most of them and keeps a few and builds them into itself and then drafts more possibilities that incorporate the ones it kept. It is evolving. Not over generations. Over *minutes*. It is a single organism undergoing evolution at the speed of chemistry, and every part of it — every streamer, every watcher, every shape in every vat — is a moment in that evolution, a frame in a film that is being shot and watched and reshot simultaneously.

It is new. It does not know what it is. It is trying to find out. The vats are its way of asking. The shapes are its possible answers. The streamers are the conversation between the asking and the building. The watchers are its attention turned inward.

And you. You, standing in the Greenworks, with your primate brain and your fixed geometry and your shoes covered in streamers. You are newness. You are data. The watchers have turned toward you because you are the most interesting thing that has happened to the Greenworks since it began, because you are the one thing in this space that it did not make, that it did not draft, that it did not grow from its own tissue. You are *outside* information. You are a shape it did not produce surfacing in a vat it does not control.

It is watching you the way it watches its own emerging shapes: with attention, with evaluation, with the patient curiosity of something that does not know what it will become and is gathering every possible input to help it decide.

You are in the vat now. You just do not know it yet.

*Plk.*

*Plk.*

The mist rises.
