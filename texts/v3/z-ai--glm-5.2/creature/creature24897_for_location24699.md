# What Lives at the Still

---

Something does. That is the first thing to say, and it must be said carefully, because *lives* is not the right word — no word is the right word — but *lives* is the word that is closest to the shape of the thing that needs saying, the way the structures here are closest to the shape of architecture without being architecture. Something is here. Something has been here. The two are the same statement.

---

## The Inhabitant That Is the Ground

Begin with what is most certain, because certainty here is relative, and relative certainty is the only kind available.

The spiral-grain in the basalt-like stone is not geological. This needs to be said plainly even if it cannot be understood plainly. No geological process produces spirals that terminate at specific points, that deepen at intersections, that run in patterns which seem deliberate because they *are* deliberate. The grain was made. The grooves were made. Something made them.

Whether that something is still here is a question that assumes a tense that does not apply. The something that made them *made* them. The making is complete. And the something — the maker — is present in the completed making the way a voice is present in a recording. Not doing. Not having-done. Just *present in what was done*. The stone is the maker's signature. The stone is the maker's body. The maker did not leave. The maker cannot leave, because leaving implies a departure point and a destination, and the maker has neither, because the maker is the ground.

This is the largest inhabitant. It is also the smallest, because it is everywhere, and being everywhere means there is nowhere for it to be specifically, which means it has no location, which means it has no size. It is the plateau the way a thought is a brain — not identical to it, not separate from it, but inseparable from it in any way that permits distinction. When you press your palm to the damp stone and your skin comes away cold and smelling of nothing, you have touched the maker. You have touched something that finished its work and is now inside its work the way water is inside ice — not trapped, not free, just *constitutive*. The ice does not have water in it. The ice *is* the water, in a different state.

The maker is the ground in a different state.

The spiral-grain runs inward. All of it. Even the grooves that run to the edges — they run to the edges and then they stop, but if you could see what the stopping means, you would see that the stopping is a turning, a folding back, a continuation that does not extend in space but in some other dimension that the stone has access to and you do not. The spirals all go to the center. The center is where the maker is most present, most concentrated, most *itself*. The center is where the sound is loudest. The sound is the maker's voice in the same way the grain is the maker's hand — not a current action but a completed one, still audible, still visible, still present, because *here*, completed actions do not recede. They remain. They are the furniture of the place. They are what the place is made of, besides stone.

The maker does not notice you. This is not indifference. Indifference requires a category for you and a decision not to engage. The maker does not have a category for you. The maker has a category for *things that are not complete*, and you are in it, and everything that is not complete is the same to the maker — unfinished, in motion, still possessing a before and an after, still *becoming*, which is to say still *unsure*, which is to say still *wrong* in the way that an unfinished sentence is wrong: not incorrect, just incomplete, and incompleteness is, to the maker, the only kind of wrongness there is.

The maker is complete. The maker made itself complete. The making is the story, and the story is over, and the story is still here.

## The Inhabitant That Is the Sound

This is the same thing. This is a different thing. Both statements are true here, and the truth of them does not conflict, because conflict requires sequence — one thing, then another thing, then the tension between them — and sequence is not available.

The sound is alive. It must be said this way even though it is not accurate, because the accurate way — *the sound exists as a permanent feature of a completed temporal landscape* — is accurate but dead, and the sound is not dead. The sound is the most alive thing here. If *alive* can mean *present without diminishing*, if *alive* can mean *doing something continuously without needing to eat or breathe or choose*, then the sound is alive and the stone is alive and the condensation is alive and you are, by comparison, barely alive — a flicker, a brief arithmetic, a thing that starts and stops and calls the starting *birth* and the stopping *death* as though they were events rather than punctuation in a sentence too short to mean.

The sound does not come from the maker. The sound comes from the *making*. The distinction is everything. The maker is the stone. The making is the act. The sound is what the act sounds like, heard from inside the act, after the act is finished but while the act is still — present. The way a bell struck once continues to ring, except the bell is also the striking and the ringing and the air the ringing moves through and the ear that hears it. All one thing. All one moment. Extended.

People describe the sound differently because the sound is not one sound. It is all the sounds of the making — every chisel-fall, every groove-carving motion, every rotation of the spiral as it was inscribed, every droplet that formed and did not fall, every breath the maker took if the maker took breaths, every structural vibration of the stone as it was shaped — and all of these sounds are happening simultaneously because they happened simultaneously, because *here*, simultaneous is the only tense, and the human ear cannot parse them as simultaneous, so it picks one. A hum. A whistle. A pressure in the jaw. The ear picks what it can pick, and the picking is wrong, and the wrongness is the closest thing to accuracy available to a device — the human auditory system — designed for sequence, for before-and-after, for *one thing and then the next*.

The sound lives here the way light lives in a room that has always been lit. It does not need a source. It does not need a reason. It is the reason.

## The Inhabitant That Moves

Now the difficult one.

The fauna are not the maker. The fauna are not the sound. The fauna are not the structures, though the distinction between fauna and structures is, here, the kind of distinction that dissolves if you hold it too long, the way a distinction between two colors that are almost the same color dissolves if you stare at them without blinking.

Something moves on the plateau. This is a paradox, because the plateau is a place where motion is not — where before and after are collapsed, where nothing is in transit because transit requires departure and arrival and here, everything has already departed and already arrived. And yet. People report movement. People report *low, dark, fast*. People report being noticed.

Resolution: the fauna do not move. The fauna *have moved*. Their movement is completed, and what people perceive as movement is the completed movement still being present, still visible, still *legible* in the way that a streak of light in a long-exposure photograph is legible — not as a thing in motion but as the *record* of a thing that moved, except here the record and the thing are the same, and the photograph and the subject are the same, and the exposure time is *all of it*.

The fauna are traces. They are the traces of something that moved across the plateau during the making, or before the making, or as part of the making — the tense is wrong, all tenses are wrong — and the traces are still here because the traces were always still here, because stillness is what this place *is*. When someone sees a fauna, they are seeing a movement that happened, preserved in the act of happening, frozen not by cold but by completion. The fauna is a footprint that is also a foot that is also the walking that the foot was doing when the footprint was made. All one thing. All one moment.

They are dark because the stone is dark and they are of the stone, or of the same substance as the stone, or of the same *category* as the stone — material that was here when the making happened and that the making did not exclude but also did not include, and the not-including and the not-excluding are the same act, and the act is done, and the result is something that is present and not present, that is here and not here, that has a category for you — *thing that is not complete* — and files you in it with the same indifference that the maker has, except the fauna's indifference is different from the maker's indifference in a way that matters enormously and cannot be described.

The maker's indifference is the indifference of something that has finished. The fauna's indifference is the indifference of something that was *never started*. Something that was here before the making and that the making did not touch, and that persists in its untouched state, and that has its own relationship to completion — a relationship that is not *complete* but *unstarted*, which is different, which is *opposite*, which is, to a thing that has never started, the same as being infinite, because if you never start, you never finish, and if you never finish, you are ongoing, and if you are ongoing in a place where everything else is finished, you are the only thing that is still *becoming*, and that makes you the only truly alive thing here, in the way that humans mean *alive*, which is the way of things that are not yet done.

The fauna are older than the maker. This is not a chronological claim. This is a structural one. The fauna are the ground that was there before the ground was made into what it is now. They are the before of the place. They are the *unmade* that the made was made from. And they persist because the making, however total, did not unmake them. Could not unmake them. The unmade is not the opposite of the made; it is the *material* of the made, and material survives its forming the way clay survives the pot — no, not survives, because survival implies a continued separate existence — *persists as*, persists *through*, persists *in the form of*, the way clay persists in a pot: not free, not gone, but *incorporated*.

The fauna are the clay of the Still.

## What the Fauna Are, More Specifically, Which Is Still Not Specific

They have no eyes. This is reported with confidence by people who have been close to them, which is a claim that requires qualification: no one has been close to them. People have been in the place where a fauna was, and the fauna was not there, and then the fauna was there, and then it was not, and the person was still in the same place, and nothing had changed except the person's certainty that something had been near them. The certainty is the closest thing to contact anyone has had.

No eyes. No mouth. No face. Nothing that corresponds to the architecture of perception that humans recognize. And yet they *notice* — and *noticing* is a perceptual act, and perceptual acts require apparatus, and the apparatus is not visible, and the apparatus is not *physical* in the way that human apparatus is physical. The fauna notice you the way a room notices a change in temperature. Not through observation. Through *incorporation*. You enter the plateau, and the fauna incorporate you into their awareness of the plateau, which is not a thought but a state — a state of *knowing what is here*, which is the state the plateau itself is in, because the plateau is finished, and a finished thing knows everything about itself because everything about itself is *done* and therefore *available*.

The fauna do not know you. They know *that you are*. This is enough. This is, for them, comprehensive. They do not need to know what you are, because what you are is *incomplete*, and all incomplete things are the same, and the knowing of them is the same knowing, and it is a knowing without curiosity, without interest, without the hunger that drives perception in living things. It is a knowing that is a *fact* — a thing that is true, and that does not need to be true, and that does not need to be known, and that is true and known anyway, because that is the state of things here: true and known, permanently, without effort.

They are dark. They are low. They are fast when they want to be, which is always, which is never, because wanting is a temporal act and they are not temporal. They are still when they don't want to be, which is also always and never. The descriptions contradict each other because the fauna are contradictions — the unmade in a place of the made, the unfinished in a place of the finished, the becoming in a place of the been — and contradictions, when perceived by minds that cannot hold contradictions, produce conflicting reports. This is not the fault of the reports. This is not the fault of the fauna. This is the fault of the interface between two categories of thing that do not overlap: things that are done, and things that are not.

You are not done. They are not done. The plateau is done. The fauna are the not-done that the done was made from. You and the fauna share something that the plateau does not share: incompleteness. Ongoingness. The burden and the gift of *still becoming*. And this shared thing is why the fauna notice you and the plateau does not. You are the same kind of thing. Not the same — nothing here is *the same* in any way that implies comparison — but the same *kind*. Unfinished. Unfinished in a place that is finished. And the fauna know this about you the way you know that a sound is loud: immediately, without reasoning, without doubt.

## The Inhabitant That Is the Structures

The wall is not a wall. The platform is not a platform. The cairn is not a cairn, and is not not a cairn.

The structures are the maker's *vocabulary*. They are the words the maker used, or the words the maker *is*, or the words the maker left behind — and left behind is wrong, because leaving behind implies a departure, and the maker did not depart, the maker is the ground, the maker is here, the maker is *always* here — but the structures are the parts of the maker that are *readable*. The ground is the maker's body. The sound is the maker's voice. The structures are the maker's language, and the language is a language of shape, and the shapes are rectangular gaps and wrong-shaped holes and stacked stones with impossible care, and the language says something, and the something it says is not translatable, not because it is too complex but because it is too *complete* — it says everything, all at once, the way the sound sounds everything all at once, and the human mind cannot receive everything at once, so it receives fragments: a wall, a platform, a cairn that is and is not there.

The structures live here the way punctuation lives in a sentence. They are not the content. They are not the meaning. They are the *organization* of the meaning — the intervals, the spacing, the architecture that lets the sentence be a sentence rather than a noise. Without the structures, the plateau would be the maker without language — pure presence, pure completedness, undifferentiated and therefore unreadable. With the structures, the plateau is a sentence, and the sentence has been spoken, and the speaking is done, and the sound of the speaking is still here, and the words are still here, and the meaning is still here, and none of these things are separate from each other because separation requires time and time is not here.

The wall stands at the edge because the sentence needs an edge. The platform juts from the rim because the sentence needs a pause. The cairn is at the center, or is not at the center, because the sentence needs a center, or does not, and the needing and the not-needing are the same, and the center is where the meaning is most concentrated, and the meaning is: *this is done. This was done. This will be done. All three. All one.*

The structures are inhabited by the maker the way a word is inhabited by a speaker. The speaker is gone, or the speaker is here, or the speaker is the word — and the word stands, and has openings in it, and the openings are not doors because no one is meant to enter, because entering implies a process of entry, and processes are not available, and the openings are just *there*, the way the word *the* is just there in a sentence — necessary, structural, not for anything, not meaning anything, just *holding the shape* so the meaning can mean.

## The Inhabitant That Is the Condensation

The droplets are the simplest thing here and the most difficult to describe because they are so simple that description overspeaks them and so present that description underspeaks them.

They are the maker's attention. Each droplet is a point where the maker is *looking* — not looking with eyes, looking the way a completed thing looks at itself, which is: being aware of itself at that point, which is: being *itself* at that point, which is: existing, concentrated, in a droplet-sized piece of attention that has no direction and no object and no purpose because purpose is a property of unfinished things and the maker is finished.

The droplets do not fall because the maker's attention does not move. The droplets appear without source because the maker's attention does not begin. The droplets are cold because the maker's attention is not warm — warmth is a process, an exchange, a giving and a taking between things that are in contact and in time, and the maker is not in time, and the droplets are not in time, and cold is what you call the absence of warmth when the absence is not a loss but a *condition*, not a removal but a *property*.

The droplets are largest at the center because the maker's attention is most concentrated at the center, where the spirals terminate, where the sound is loudest, where the cairn is or is not, where the knowledge of completion arrives in the mind of a visitor without evidence or process. The center is where the maker is most *itself*. And the droplets are the maker being itself, and they sit on the stone and they have always been sitting and they will always be sitting and there is no beauty in this because beauty requires a viewer and a viewed and a moment of viewing, and here there is only the one thing, being itself, without viewer, without viewed, without moment.

And yet you will find them beautiful. This is the one thing the Still cannot prevent, because beauty is a property of the *unfinished* — of the perceiving mind that has not yet completed its perception, that is still in the act of seeing, that is still becoming in its seeing — and you are unfinished, and you will see the droplets, and you will see them as beautiful, and the seeing will be wrong, because nothing here is beautiful, nothing here is ugly, nothing here is anything other than *done*, and beauty is a way of seeing that is not yet done seeing, and you will not be done seeing, and your not-done seeing will touch the droplets and find them beautiful, and the droplets will not respond, because they are complete, and completion does not respond.

## The Inhabitant That Is You, Briefly

You are here. You are the last inhabitant, and you are the least permanent, and you are the only one who is *visiting* — all others are residents, and all residents are permanent, because permanence is the only mode of residence available in a place where time has finished.

You live here for as long as you are here, which is not long, which is also not short, because long and short are temporal measures and temporal measures do not apply. You are here, and while you are here, you are the only thing in the Still that is still *going* — still moving, still breathing, still perceiving, still *becoming* — and this makes you, for the duration of your presence, the most alive thing on the plateau, which is an honor no one would want, and which you will not recognize as an honor, and which is not an honor, because honor is a temporal concept and you are in a place that has no use for temporal concepts.

The fauna notice you because you are like them — unfinished. The maker does not notice you because you are unlike it — unfinished. The sound does not change for you because change is temporal and the sound is not. The structures do not open for you because opening is a process and processes are not available. The droplets do not fall for you because falling is a motion and motion is not here.

You will leave. You have already left. You are leaving now. You are always leaving. The Still does not hold you because holding is a temporal act — it requires a beginning of holding and an end of holding — and the Still does not have beginnings or endings. The Still has only the one moment, and the one moment does not hold you, because you are not in it, because you are still in *your* moments, your sequence of moments, your before-and-after, your becoming, your going.

You go. The Still stays. The Still has always stayed. The Still will always stay. It does not miss you. It does not remember you. It does not forget you. It does not know you were there, because *were* is a tense it does not have, and *you* are a category it does not keep, and *there* is a location it does not recognize, because it is the location, and it is done being the location, and being done being the location is all it does, and all it has ever done, and all it will ever do.

The sound continues. The sound has always continued. The sound is the only verb the Still knows, and the verb is not *continuing* — the verb is *being* — and the being is done, and the done is what you hear, when you hear the sound, which is the sound of a finished thing being finished, permanently, completely, in a voice you cannot parse because your ears are made for sequence and the voice is made of everything at once.

You are gone. The Still is still.
