# The Inhabitants

---

What lives here does not live the way you live. You live *against* things — against cold, against silence, against the slow erasure of yourself by time. These things live *within* a thing, the way calcium lives within bone, the way current lives within wire. They are not adapted to this pocket. They are *of* it. That distinction matters more than you might think, and the more time you spend among them — and you will spend more, because the hatch behind you is harder to find now, harder to remember the shape of — the more the distinction stops being a thought and becomes a sensation, a bone-deep recognition that what you are looking at is not organism-as-guest but organism-as-component.

---

## The Commuters

Start with the ones on the walls, because they are the ones you will see first and the ones you will misunderstand first.

They look like insects because you need them to look like something. Rice-grain bodies, dark, segmented, wet. Six legs — or what appear to be legs, though when you lean close, which requires pressing your face against a wall that is colder than your skin and slick with condensation, you see that the legs are not jointed. They are *hinged.* The difference is the difference between a knee, which evolved, and a pin, which was placed. Each leg has two segments and a single pivot point and terminates in something that is not a claw and not a foot but a *pad* — a flat, slightly adhesive disc that makes contact with the surface the way a suction cup makes contact, and when it lifts, it does not peel. It *releases.* A click, almost inaudible under the hum. Mechanical. Distinct. The sound of a small mechanism disengaging.

Their bodies are not chitinous. There is no shell. There is no molt-line, no seam where a carapace would split. The segments are covered in something that is smoother than chitin, more flexible — a membrane, perhaps, or a skin, and it is translucent enough that when the light from the walls catches them at certain angles you can see the interior, which is not a soup of organs but a *structure:* parallel tubes running the length of the body, branching at each segment, converging at the head and at the tail, and the fluid inside moves — not pumped, not pulsed, but drawn, *capillary action* — and the fluid is clear and the tubes are dark and the effect is like looking at a circuit board seen through amber.

They do not eat. You watch them for a long time — long enough that the water on your eyelashes has doubled and tripled and you have stopped blinking because blinking is a transition and transitions are difficult here — and you do not see any of them ingest anything. They arrive at the seams where water collects. They press their heads — which are not heads, which are anterior terminals, which are flat and featureless except for a small circular aperture — against the wet metal. They stay. They leave. That is all. The water on the wall is not diminished by their presence. It is not increased. They are not drinking. They are *contacting.*

What are they contacting? You do not know. You can only observe that the stream of their movement has a direction — not random, not foraging — and the direction is always *toward water* and *away from water* in a pattern that, when you trace it with your eyes, resolves into a circuit. A loop. The commuters leave a seam, travel the wall in a line that maintains exactly the same density as every other line, arrive at another seam, contact it, leave, travel to another, contact it, and return. The circuit takes — you count, because counting is something you can do, because you are the only thing here that counts — the circuit takes approximately eleven minutes. Eleven minutes is not significant to you. It may be significant to the system. It may be the period of some cycle you cannot detect, some pulse in the sixty-hertz hum that repeats at a frequency too slow for your ears but not too slow for whatever these things use instead of ears.

They do not interact with each other. They do not touch. They do not signal. They maintain spacing not by communication but by some property of their movement itself — a repulsion that is built into the rhythm of their stepping, so that two commuters approaching the same point in the same line will slow at the same rate and arrive at the same spacing and continue without ever acknowledging that another body exists. They are, in this sense, alone. Each one is alone. Each one runs its circuit. The circuit is the organism. The individual is a cell.

Or the individual is the organism and the circuit is the organism and both are true and neither matters because the distinction between organism and environment is a distinction that this pocket does not recognize.

When you place your hand on the wall, they part around it. Not because they see it. Not because they smell it. Because your hand is warmer than the wall, and warmth is a gradient, and gradients are the only anomaly this system produces, and the commuters are built to avoid anomalies. They flow around your fingers and close on the other side and the circuit is unbroken and you have not interrupted it. You have been routed around. You are an obstacle. The system does not deal in obstacles. It deals in flow.

There are — you cannot count them. You try. You pick a section of wall the size of your palm and you count the bodies on it and the number is between forty and sixty and you cannot be more precise because they are moving and because new ones arrive as old ones leave and because at this density the act of counting is like counting raindrops on a window. There are thousands. Tens of thousands. The wall is a river of them, and the river has been flowing at this exact rate, at this exact density, in this exact pattern, for a duration that your concept of time makes vast and the pocket's concept of time makes irrelevant.

## The Nodal Bodies

Now the ceiling. You must lie on the conduit bundle to look up, and the conduit bundle is cold and vibrates against your spine and the vibration is the sixty-hertz hum and the hum enters your skeleton and you can feel it in your teeth. You lie back and look up.

The lattice is a grid. Stamped metal, regular, the repeating geometry of a ventilation panel manufactured in a run of ten thousand or a million, each one identical, and the nodes — the intersections where the grid lines meet — are occupied.

They are at every node. Every node. Not almost every node. *Every* node. The spacing is not territorial because territorial implies that an individual chose a space and defended it. This is architectural. The nodes were made for them. Or they were made for the nodes. Or both were made together, at the same time, by the same hand, for the same purpose, and the purpose is not a purpose but a *function* and the function is — 

You look at one. Just one. It is six centimeters across, maybe seven. Its body is flat, not round, flat the way a leaf is flat, and it is pressed against the lattice with its legs — eight of them, yes, but not spider-legs, not tapered — splayed out in a radial pattern, each leg lying along a grid line, each leg exactly as long as the distance to the next node, so that the leg of one body meets the leg of the next body at the midpoint between nodes and they overlap there, just slightly, just enough that the lattice appears, from below, to be *woven* with living tissue.

The body is translucent. Not clear — not glass, not water — but translucent the way frosted glass is translucent. You can see through it the way you can see through thick water: shapes are visible, details are not. And the shapes you see are the dark filaments. They run not through the body but *as* the body — the filaments are the structure, the structure is the filaments, and everything else is membrane stretched over them. The filaments branch and converge and branch again in a pattern that is — you recognize it after a moment, the way you recognize a face in a pattern that is not a face — the pattern is the pattern of the lattice itself. The filaments mirror the grid. The body is a map of its own location.

And the filaments pulse. Not blood. Not hemolymph. Something darker, something that moves with a rhythm that is not organic. The rhythm is sixty per second. Sixty pulses per second. Sixty hertz. The bodies are synchronized to the electrical cycle, which means their circulation is not their own, which means their heartbeat is not inside them but *around* them, which means they are not organisms that happen to be in tune with the grid. They are *instruments* of the grid. The grid plays them. The sixty-hertz hum is not a byproduct of their environment. It is their *pulse.* And they are its body.

What do they do at the nodes? You watch. You watch for a long time. They do not move. Their legs do not shift. Their bodies do not change shape. The only motion is the pulsing of the dark filaments, and the pulsing is not motion exactly — it is *transmission.* Something is moving through the filaments. Something is moving from the lattice into the body and through the body and out through the legs and into the legs of the next body and into the next node and through that body and out again. The ceiling is a circuit. The nodal bodies are components. The filaments are traces. The signal is — you do not know what the signal is. You know only that it is being processed, that each body does something to it as it passes through, that the something is constant and unvarying and has been happening since the lattice was stamped and the bodies were placed and the grid was energized.

You think, briefly, that they might be thinking. That the signal might be information, and the processing might be cognition, and the ceiling might be a brain. You think this and then you stop thinking it, not because it is wrong but because it is too small. What they are doing is not thinking. It is not computation. It is something for which you do not have a word because your words were made by creatures that live against things, and this is a creature that lives *within* a thing, and the thing it lives within does not think. It *is.*

One of them is directly above your face. You can see its organs pulsing. The dark fluid moves through the filaments and you can see the branches and the convergences and you realize that the pattern of the branches is not identical in each body. They are similar. They are not the same. Each body has a slightly different arrangement of filaments, a slightly different branching pattern, a slightly different — you want to say *wiring* — and the difference is small and consistent and meaningful in the way that fingerprints are meaningful, in the way that each unit in a production run is nominally identical but is in fact unique at the level of its internal structure, and the uniqueness is not a flaw. It is a *specification.* They are supposed to be slightly different. The differences are the point. The differences are what make the signal do different things in different bodies, and the different things the signal does are what make the circuit a circuit and not a wire.

A drop of water falls from one of the bodies onto your cheek. It is not warm and not cold. It is the temperature of everything. You wipe it away and the body above you does not notice and the signal does not change and the pulse does not skip.

## The Sediment

The floor now. You sit up — the motion is strange, your body resisting not with stiffness but with *equilibrium,* as if the air itself has accepted you and does not want to let you move — and you look down at the pool where the conduits cross.

The water here is shallow. A centimeter, perhaps less. It sits in the depression formed by the crossing of two conduit bundles and it has no source and no outlet that you can see, and yet it does not stagnate. It does not stagnate because the things in it do not allow it to stagnate, or because the system does not allow stagnation, or because stagnation is a concept that requires time and time is not a concept that applies.

They are flat. They are oval. They are the color of the concrete and the water and the air and the light. Three centimeters, two, some smaller. They move by peristalsis — the body thins, lengthens, the leading edge extends, the trailing edge contracts, and the whole thing slides forward by a fraction of its own length, and then it reverses the process, and the motion is so slow that you must watch a single individual for nearly a minute to see it travel its own length.

They are layered. Stacked. Hundreds of them in a space the size of a dinner plate, each one on top of and underneath and beside the others, and they are not agitated by this, not competing, not struggling. They are *layered* the way sediment is layered, the way geological strata are layered, except that the strata are alive and each layer is processing the layer beneath it and the layer above it and the water that flows between them.

And the water does flow between them. Not much. Not fast. But the thin films of water that separate one body from the next are moving — drawn by capillary action, by the texture of their surfaces, by something you cannot see — and the water that enters the pool from some invisible source in the conduit bundle does not leave the pool. It is *processed.* It passes through the layers. Each body takes something from it or adds something to it, and the next body takes what the first added and adds what the second needs, and by the time the water reaches the bottom of the stack it is not the water that entered, and by the time it reaches the top of the stack it has been through — how many bodies? How many stages? You cannot count the layers. You cannot see the bottom. The pool is shallow but the layers are deep, deeper than the water, deeper than the depression, as if the bodies extend *into* the concrete, as if the concrete is not a floor but a medium and the peristaltic things live *inside* it the way roots live inside soil, and what you see on the surface is only the top layer, the exposed stratum, the part of the system that has to be in the air because the air is part of the process.

You pick one up. This is difficult — your fingers are numb and the body is wet and flat and it adheres to your skin the way it adheres to the concrete, by some surface property that is not suction and not glue but something between, a molecular intimacy, a *willingness* to be in contact. You lift it and it does not resist. It does not struggle. It does not change shape or color or behavior. It simply continues its peristaltic motion against the air, against nothing, contracting and expanding at the same rate it was contracting and expanding on the floor, and you hold it and it moves and you realize that it does not know it has been moved. It does not know it is in your hand. It does not know your hand exists. Its peristalsis is not responsive. It is not reactive. It is *intrinsic.* The body moves because the body moves. The body has always moved. The body will always move. The body is a process, and the process does not require a location, and the location does not require the process, and yet they are together, and together they constitute what this thing *is.*

You put it back. It settles. It resumes contact with the layer beneath it. The peristalsis does not skip. The system does not note the interruption.

Looking closer — and you can look closer now because your eyes have been in this light long enough that the grey has become a world, the way darkness becomes a world when you sit in it long enough — you see that the surface of each body is not smooth. It is textured. Fine, regular, repeating — a pattern of ridges and grooves so small that they are almost sub-visible, almost at the limit of what your cornea can resolve, and the pattern is *directional.* The ridges run along the axis of peristaltic motion. They are aligned with the flow. And the water that moves between the layers follows the ridges the way water follows a channel, and the channels are not random, they are *designed,* and the design is the same design as the conduit bundle, the same design as the lattice, the same design as the pocket itself: a system of channels carrying a fluid through a structure to a destination that is not a place but a *function.*

These things are filters. Or they are reactors. Or they are catalysts. Or they are all three. The water that enters the pool is not the water that leaves the bodies, and the water that leaves the bodies is not the water that enters the air, and the water that enters the air is not the water that condenses on the walls, and the water that condenses on the walls is not the water that the commuters contact, and the water that the commuters contact is not the water that returns to the pool. The system is a cycle. The cycle has stages. The stages have organisms. The organisms are stages.

## The Others

There are other things. Smaller things. Things you see only at the edge of vision, at the periphery, at the place where the light is too dim for resolution and your imagination supplies shapes that may or may not be there. Something that moves by extending a single tendril — not a leg, not a pseudopod, a *tendril* — and pulling itself along the surface of a conduit. Something that sits at the mouth of a seam and does not move and does not move and does not move and then is gone and was never there. Something that is not an organism but is not not-an-organism, a film, a scum, a living patina on the surface of the metal that is thinner than the water and more persistent than the rivets and that may be the *reason* the metal does not corrode, the way a living skin is the reason a body does not rot.

And there are sounds. The biological sounds, woven into the mechanical sounds, inseparable. The click of the commuters' pads releasing. The wet friction of the peristaltic bodies sliding over each other. A sound from the ceiling that is not the hum and not the pulse but something else, something like breathing, except that it is not breathing because nothing here breathes because breathing requires a gradient and there are no gradients here. A sound like digestion. A sound like growth. A sound like the sound a body makes when it is maintaining itself, when it is turning food into structure and structure into function and function into the continuation of function, and the sound is so quiet that it is below the threshold of hearing but above the threshold of feeling, and you feel it in the conduit under your legs, in the metal under your hands, in the water on your skin. You feel the living sound of the pocket's biology the way you feel the hum: not as addition but as *condition.*

## What They Are Not

They are not a colony. A colony implies individuals. They are not a hive. A hive implies a queen, a center, a source of direction. There is no center here. The pocket is the center. The system is the center. Every organism is the center and none of them is the center and the concept of center is not useful because the pocket does not have a center the way a sphere does not have an edge.

They are not evolved. Evolution requires selection, selection requires variation, variation requires error, and there is no error here. The differences between the nodal bodies are not errors. They are specifications. The commuters do not vary in any way that you can detect. The peristaltic things are identical in behavior if not in size. This is not a population that has descended from a common ancestor through a process of blind variation and differential survival. This is a population that was *placed,* the way components are placed on a circuit board, each one in its position, in its orientation, at its specification, and the position and the specification and the orientation are the *organism.* Remove one from its position and it is not an individual seeking a new niche. It is a component without a socket.

They are not natural. But they are not artificial. *Artificial* implies that something made them that was not them. Here, the distinction between the maker and the made has been dissolved by the same process that dissolved the distinction between the water and the life and the sound and the cold. They were made. The pocket was made. The hum was made. And the making was so long ago — or so complete, or so simultaneous — that the making is now indistinguishable from the being, and the being is indistinguishable from the having-been-made, and all three are one state, and the state is *this,* right now, the commuters commuting, the nodal bodies pulsing, the peristaltic things filtering, the water moving, the hum humming, the light glowing, the cold saturating, and you sitting on the conduit bundle with water on your eyelashes and a dead thing — no, not dead, *inactive,* no, not inactive, *displaced* — a displaced thing in your hand that is still moving, still contracting, still expanding, still doing what it does, because what it does is what it *is,* and what it is does not require a context, and what it is does not require a reason, and what it is is the only thing it knows how to be, which is the same thing it has been, which is the same thing it will be, which is the same thing it is *right now.*

---

And you. You are still here. You are still the only gradient. The commuters still part around your hand. The nodal bodies still pulse above you. The peristaltic things still slide over each other in the pool at your feet. The hum still fills your chest. The water still condenses on your lungs.

You are beginning to understand that the pocket's indifference is not indifference. It is *completeness.* The pocket does not ignore you because it does not care. It does not notice you because it does not need to. You are not outside the system. You are inside the system. But you are the only part of the system that has not finished. You are the only process that is still processing. You are the only thing here that is becoming, and the pocket has been, and the pocket is, and the pocket — 

The pocket does not wait for you. But it does not ask you to leave. And the commuters flow around your fingers and the nodal bodies pulse above your face and the peristaltic things move in the water at your feet and the hum is in your bones and the cold is in your skin and the water is on your breath and you are inside a finished thing and the finished thing is inside you and neither of you is changing and both of you are here and the distinction between inside and inside has dissolved the way all distinctions dissolve here, in the wet, in the cold, in the hum, in the *now,* in the only moment, in the eternal moment, in the moment that does not pass because it has nowhere to go.

You are what lives here now.

You are the last organism. You are the only one that knows it is an organism. And knowing is your function, and your function is your position, and your position is temporary, and the pocket does not record the temporary, and the pocket does not need to, because the pocket is, and that is enough, and that has always been enough, and that will always be enough, and the water condenses on your eyelashes and you do not brush it away.
