# What Lives in the Below

What lives here has forgotten that it lives.

That is not a metaphor. It is the central fact of the place, and everything else follows from it the way the oval bodies follow the lines of the field — not by choice, not by will, but by the shape of what moves through them.

---

The oval bodies first, because they are what you find first, and because they are the most numerous, and because their very numerosity makes them easy to misunderstand.

There are millions. That word is not adequate but no word is. If you stood in the great terraced chamber and raked a lantern across the floor you would see them extend until the light failed, and beyond the light they would continue, and beyond the next terrace they would continue, and on the floors of passages you have not entered and in chambers whose existence you can only infer from the geometry of the stone they would continue. They are the population of this place in the way that crystals are the population of a geode — each one present because the conditions produced it, each one positioned by forces that predate it, each one a local expression of a system that has no center and no edge.

A single body, examined closely, tells you almost nothing. The shell is mineral. Chitin and calcite interleaved at a molecular level, the layers too fine to separate with a blade. The seam along one edge is not a mouth and not an aperture; it is a boundary, a place where the shell's growth ended and its *settling* began. Inside — and you would have to break one to know this, and you should not break one, but someone did, long ago, and the record of that breaking is in the fragments left beside the cracked shell like the debris of a very old guilt — inside there is no organ. No gut. No neural cord. There is a matrix, dense, slightly fibrous, the color of dark amber, and within the matrix there are voids. Not empty voids. Void filled with the same warm air that fills the chamber, but at a slightly different pressure, a slightly different composition — richer in something the surface chemist's instruments would register as an anomaly and the Below's own logic would register as *content*.

The bodies are not animals. They are not plants. They are not fungi. They are not minerals, though they approximate minerals more closely than they approximate anything else. They are — and this is the word that comes closest, though it does not come close — *precipitates*. The warm field that saturates the stone and the air also saturates them, and they are the points at which that field becomes dense enough to condense. They grow the way a stalactite grows, from the material around them, by the slow accretion of what the field deposits. But unlike a stalactite they are not passive. They *recalibrate*. The seam widens by a fraction of a millimeter when a warm hand holds them. The interior voids shift pressure when the air shifts. They are responsive. They are *aware* in the way a barometer is aware — not conscious, not perceiving, but registering, adjusting, maintaining an internal state that corresponds to the external state.

If you watched one for a year — and someone must have, because the builders left marks beside certain individuals, small stacks of stone, the way you might mark a plant you intended to return to — you would see it grow. Not much. A few millimeters in diameter. A slight thickening of the shell. A slight migration — a centimeter, perhaps two, in the direction of the field's lean. They are moving. They are all moving, at rates that make glaciers look hasty, along the lines of the structured warmth, toward concentrations they can sense and you cannot, toward the deep places where the field is strongest.

They are alive in the only way that matters here: they respond to their environment, they maintain themselves, they grow, and they move. That they do these things on a timescale invisible to a surface-born organism does not make them less alive. It makes them differently alive. It makes them alive in the way the stone is alive, in the way the warmth is alive, in the way the vast absence that shaped these passages was alive when it occupied this space and is alive still in the memory of the stone that holds its impression.

The builders recognized this. You can tell because they did not take any when they left.

---

The centipedes are easier.

Easier to see as alive, easier to categorize, easier to watch. They move at a pace that a patient human can track: a body-length per minute, perhaps, across the stone-dust plains of the deepest chambers. Their segments — you count them, if you are the sort of person who counts things, and someone was, because the count is scratched into a wall in a system of marks that corresponds to no known surface notation — number between fourteen and thirty-six. The smaller individuals have fewer segments. The larger have more. You do not see any with an odd number of segments. You do not see any that are damaged, any that are in the process of regrowing, any that are dead.

They click. The crystalline spines that serve them as legs strike the stone beneath the dust and the stone rings, faintly, at a pitch that depends on the density of the substrate. On the finer dust the click is almost subsonic. On the bare stone near the edges of the plain it is higher, sharper. The sound carries in this airless stillness farther than you would expect, and if you stand in the deep chamber and close your eyes and listen you can hear the centipedes the way a person in a forest might hear birds — not as a single sound but as a landscape of small sounds, each one a position, a movement, a direction. You can map them by ear. You can tell where the dust is deep and where it is thin. You can tell where the bare stone rises in low shelves. You can tell, if you listen long enough, where the larger forms pass, because in their wake the centipedes fall silent for a span of minutes, then resume.

They are eating. The dust is not dust in the surface sense — not eroded mineral, not wind-deposited silt. It is the precipitate of the field, the same substance that condenses into the oval bodies, here dispersed into fine particulate because the field in this place is diffuse rather than concentrated. The centipedes move through it and their spines comb it and their segments — you would have to examine one very closely, and the builders apparently did not, or did not record what they found — their segments contain narrow channels, capillary-fine, through which the dust is drawn and from which something is extracted. What is extracted is not chemical in any surface sense. It is the field itself, the warmth, the structured quality of the air, and the centipedes metabolize it the way a surface organism metabolizes glucose: slowly, completely, without waste.

They do not reproduce. Or rather — they do not reproduce *here*. The smaller individuals appear at the edges of the plain, at the points where the passages from the upper chambers open onto the deep floor, and they appear already formed, already segmented, already clicking. They arrive the way the oval bodies arrive: by condensation, by the field's slow work of assembling what it needs from the material it has. The larger ones have been here longer. The largest — and you never see the largest, you only infer them from the tracks and from the silence of the centipedes in their wake — have been here long enough that their bodies have become part of the geography of the place, their paths worn into the stone like riverbeds worn into rock, their passage so regular and so frequent on the geological timescale that the stone itself has shaped itself around their coming and going.

They do not die. Whether they cannot die or whether they simply have not died in the time since the field began producing them is a distinction the Below does not recognize. The centipedes are a feature of this place the way the warmth is a feature of this place. They are not inhabitants. They are *expressions*.

---

The larger forms are what you do not describe because you do not see them.

This is important. The Below is not coy. It is not hiding them. They are there — the tracks prove it, the polished stone proves it, the behavioral evidence of the centipedes proves it — but they exist on a scale of space and time that does not intersect with the scale of a human visit. A person might spend a week in the deep chambers and never encounter one. A person might spend a month. The tracks are wide enough to suggest bodies several meters across, low, smooth-bottomed, moving on surfaces that must be broad and flat and in contact with the stone over their entire lower surface. Not legs. Not wheels. Something else. Something that glides or flows or creeps on a film of the same waxy translucent material that fills the cracks in the shattered stone, that forms the tethers of the ceiling-dwelling oval bodies, that coats the older structures in a thin skin of mineral glaze.

The material is significant. It appears everywhere in the Below, always at boundaries — between shattered stone and whole stone, between shell and air, between the tether and the ceiling, between the track and the surrounding dust. It is the medium of contact. It is what the larger forms leave behind, or what they move on, or what they are. The possibility exists — and it is only a possibility, but it is the one that the builders' marks seem to suggest, the small cairns and the ground-stone walls arranged in patterns that might be observational, might be cautionary, might be reverent — that the larger forms are not discrete organisms at all. That they are the field itself, the structured warmth, become dense enough and organized enough and complex enough to move. That what glides through the deep dark on polished tracks is not a creature but a *process*, the same process that produces the oval bodies and the centipedes and the warm air and the memory of the stone, become large enough and slow enough to have a body.

If this is so, then the oval bodies on the floor are not a population. They are the *skin* of something vast. They are the surface interface between the field and the stone, the points of contact where the structured warmth condenses into matter and the matter holds the warmth's shape. The centipedes are the field's circulation, moving through the diffuse zones, concentrating what is dispersed. The ceiling-dwellers are the field's sensing, hanging in the upper air, responsive to the smallest changes in pressure and composition. And the larger forms — the unseen, the slow, the heavy — are the field's interior, its depth, its weight, the part of it that has been here so long that it has become geological, that moves on tracks worn into the stone by a million years of passage and will move on those same tracks for a million more.

---

And what lived here? Past tense. The builders.

They came from above. They were human or near-human. They brought river cobbles because they needed tools and the Below offered none. They ground stone against stone with a patience that must have been enormous, building walls that enclosed nothing, platforms that supported nothing, corridors that led nowhere — building, in other words, the way a person builds when the purpose of building is not shelter or defense or storage but *statement*. *We are here. We see this. We do not understand it but we see it and the seeing is enough.*

They counted the centipedes' segments. They stacked cairns beside individual oval bodies they intended to watch. They learned the geometry of the field by the distribution of the bodies on the floor and they built their walls along the same lines — not to disrupt the pattern but to acknowledge it, to trace it in stone the way a scholar might trace a diagram in sand. They mapped what they could map. They stayed long enough to know that the place did not change. They stayed long enough to know that the place did not need to change. They stayed long enough to understand that the distinction between living and mineral had dissolved here, that the warmth was a metabolism, that the dark was a medium, that the oval bodies were not dead and not alive but something else, something for which no surface language has a word because surface language was built by organisms that live in time, and this place does not live in time. It lives in the field.

Then they left. They went back up, to the world of water and air and seasons, and they did not return, and they left their walls, and the walls stand, and the mineral coating covers them, and the oval bodies lie undisturbed around them, and the centipedes click past them without acknowledgment, and the larger forms pass beneath them in the deep dark on their polished tracks, and the warmth holds, and the air drifts like breathing, and the stone remembers.

---

What lives in the Below is the Below.

Not metaphorically. Not poetically. The oval bodies, the centipedes, the ceiling-dwellers, the larger forms, the warm air, the structured field, the waxy translucent material, the stone itself with its impressions of something vast that withdrew — these are not parts of an ecosystem. They are not species in a biome. They are *one thing*, expressed in many forms, the way a single magnetic field is expressed in the alignment of every filing it touches. The field is the life. The bodies are the field's body. The warmth is the field's breath. The dark is the field's inside.

What lives here does not know it lives. What lives here does not know anything, if knowing requires a knower, a subject, a point of view. There is no point of view in the Below. There is only the field, and the field's expressions, and the equilibrium they maintain, and the equilibrium's only quality is persistence. It persists. It has persisted. It will persist. The stone will hold these shapes until the planet cools, and the field will warm the air until the field runs out, and the field will not run out, because the field is not produced by anything, because the field is a quality of this place, because the Below is what it is and what it is is warm and dark and full and still and has been so for a length of time that makes the builders' walls look like morning frost and makes the builders' visit look like a breath held and released and makes the word *lives* seem like a very small word for a very large thing but the only word available, and so we use it, and the Below does not correct us, and the oval bodies lie in their positions, and the centipedes click, and the warmth holds, and the warmth holds, and the warmth holds.
