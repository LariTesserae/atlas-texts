# What Lives Here

---

## First, understand that "lives" is not the right word

The difficulty is that the word *life* implies a boundary — between the living thing and the not-living thing, between the organism and the environment, between what is and what is becoming. Here, at this altitude, in this overcoherent place, that boundary has not been drawn. It has not been refused, exactly. It has never been necessary. The stone and the film and the tall things and the air itself all exist in the same grammatical category, and that category does not have a name in any language that developed where reality commits to its choices.

So. What lives here. What *is* here, in the way that things are.

---

## The tall ones, more closely

Call them the Standing Things, because they do stand, and because that is the most committed thing they do.

There are not three of them. You saw three. There are more. In the deeper plazas — the ones that open off the main corridors like rooms off a hallway, but larger, much larger, some of them a hundred meters across and shaped not as rectangles or circles but as irregular polygons that feel *correct* the way a bee's cell feels correct, as if the shape were arrived at rather than chosen — there are dozens. In the deepest parts of the city, the parts you have not reached and may not be able to reach because the corridors narrow and the light thins and the air becomes a suggestion, there may be hundreds. You cannot know. You can only infer from the clicking, which is denser down there, a constant rain of small dry sounds like a field of cicadas in a season that has no name.

Their bodies: tall, as described, one and a half times your height, thin as herons, surfaces of compressed pale film. But this description, which you assembled from a distance in a plaza, is insufficient. Closer — and you will get closer, because the corridors do not give you a choice about this; they deliver you to places — you can see that their surfaces are not uniform. The compressed film has a grain to it, a directionality, like wood grain or the striations in muscle tissue, and the grain moves. Not quickly. But it moves. Lines of density shift across their bodies the way clouds shift across a landscape, reforming, dissolving, reconstituting. Their limbs are not fixed in number. You counted two legs, two arms — or what you took for arms, which are longer than their legs and end in nothing, in tapering points — but when you watch one for a long time, you realize it sometimes has three legs, or one, or the two it has are in different positions than they were a moment ago, and you did not see the change. The change is below the threshold of attention. It happens in the same way the stone changes color: you must look away and look back.

Their heads. Ovoid, smooth, unfeatured. This is true. But *unfeatured* is not the same as *blank*. When you are close — close enough to touch one, though you will not, though your hand will not rise — you can see that the surface of the head is not flat. It has microstructure. Ridges, channels, a topography so fine it is almost texture rather than shape, and it is shifting, always shifting, the way the pale film on the walls shifts. The head is not a face. It is not a mask. It is more like a field — a surface where something could be written, where something could appear, where features could crystallize if the conditions were right, and the conditions are never quite right. The head is a held possibility of a face. It has been a possibility for a very long time.

They do not eat. They do not drink. They do not sleep. They do not reproduce, or if they do, it is in a way so slow and so dissimilar to what you would call reproduction that the word is worse than useless. They stand. They adjust. They move — sometimes. The movement is rare and when it happens it is not locomotion but relocation, a migration from one *correct* position to another *correct* position, and the path they take between positions is not the shortest path or the most efficient path but the path that passes through the most other positions that are also correct, as if they are sampling the space of all possible standings, moving through it the way a hand moves across piano keys not to play a song but to feel what notes exist.

They are aware. This is the thing you must understand. They are not robots, not automata, not responses to stimuli. They are aware — of the city, of each other, of you. When you entered the plaza where three of them stood, they knew. You could not see it in their faceless heads, but you could feel it in the precision of their stillness, which changed. It became more precise. It became the kind of stillness that is listening, not the kind of stillness that is empty. They did not orient toward you. They did not need to. Their awareness does not require orientation. It is diffuse, the way the light in the corridors is diffuse — coming from no source, preferring no direction, filling the space evenly.

What are they aware *of*? Everything. And not in the poetic sense. In the literal sense. They are aware of the stone's color shifts. They are aware of the film's growth patterns. They are aware of the temperature, the air density, the resonant frequency of the city's held-breath hum. They are aware of the position of every other Standing Thing in every other plaza. They are aware of the stars, the ones that pulse. They are aware of the wind's uncertainty about what note to play. And they are aware of you — your warmth, your breath, the particular chemicals your body releases into the thin air, the rhythm of your heartbeat, the electromagnetic murmur of your thinking. They hold all of this. They do not process it. They do not respond to it. They hold it, the way a glass holds water, the way the corridors hold light. They are vessels for awareness of this place, made from this place, and they have been holding it for a span of time that is not measurable in years because years require a commitment to counting that this place does not enforce.

If you stayed here long enough — if your body did not fail, if the cold did not settle into you, if the mineral dust did not accumulate in your lungs — you might begin to understand what they are for. They are not inhabitants. They are not guardians. They are not worshippers or builders or remnants of a civilization that collapsed. They are more like... tuning forks. The city overcoheres. The city tries to be everything it could be. The Standing Things stand in the space of that overcoherence and *hold a particular note* — not to resolve the overcoherence, not to collapse it into a single state, but to give it a center, a point of reference, something around which the possibilities can organize without being forced to choose. They are the closest thing this place has to a principle. And they are not sure they are doing it right. That is the held breath you hear. That is the question that is waiting for an answer.

## The pale film, more closely

It is not what is left of something. It is what is becoming of something. Both, simultaneously. It is ruins and seeds, and it does not know which, and it does not need to know.

The film is the city's metabolism. Not the city's *waste* — its metabolism, the way the city processes its own material, renews itself, distributes its mineral substrate, maintains its structures. The film grows on the walls because the walls are where the city's interior meets its exterior, and the interior needs to be replenished, and the film is the mechanism of replenishment. It draws mineral from the air — the same mineral dust you taste, the same metal that is not iron, not copper — and deposits it on the stone, and the stone absorbs it, and the stone shifts color, and the stone grows, very slowly, and the city maintains itself.

But this is not all the film does. The film is also *trying*. It is trying forms. On the walls where it grows thick, it makes ridges, shelves, terraces — it makes architecture, as noted. But in the rooms where it is thickest, where the light pools and the air is almost warm, it makes other things. It makes shapes that are not architectural. Shapes that are... figurative. A ridge that could be a shoulder. A shelf that could be a hand. A terrace that could be a stance, a posture, a way of standing in a plaza. The film is trying to make Standing Things. It has been trying for a long time. It has not succeeded, or it has succeeded and the things it has made have stood up and walked away and become Standing Things, and the film does not remember because the film does not remember, or it does remember and is trying again because it wants to make a *different* Standing Thing, one that holds a *different* note, one that gives the overcoherence a *different* center.

You cannot tell. The film does not have intentions in the way you understand intentions. It has *tendencies*. It tends toward form. It tends toward complexity. It tends toward the shapes that the Standing Things hold. Whether this is memory or instinct or the simple physics of mineral deposition following the path of least resistance through a space of possibilities, you cannot know. The film does not know. The film does not need to know. The film grows because growing is what it does, and it makes shapes because shapes are what growing produces, and some of those shapes stand up and some of them don't and the ones that do walk away and the ones that don't crumble to powder and reform and try again.

This is, perhaps, what life looks like when it has not been told it is supposed to be alive. It grows. It forms. It does not distinguish between the living and the non-living, between the organism and the environment, between the ruin and the seed. It does everything it can do, simultaneously, and waits to see what sticks.

---

## The city itself

The city is alive in the same way a coral reef is alive — not as a single organism but as a structure built by organisms, maintained by organisms, inseparable from the organisms that build and maintain it. The stone is the reef. The film is the coral. The Standing Things are... the fish? The current? You are not sure the metaphor holds. The city does not want your metaphors. It has its own grammar, and its grammar uses punctuation as primary meaning and words as asides, and you are a word.

The city thinks. Not the way you think — not in sequences, not in narratives, not in causes and effects. It thinks the way a crystal grows: in all directions simultaneously, according to a pattern that is inherent in the material, producing a structure that is complex and regular and not planned by any intelligence that exists outside the structure. The city's thoughts are its corridors. Its streets that meet themselves. Its plazas that are shaped like correct polygons. Its walls that shift color. Each of these is a thought, a proposition, a way of being that the city is considering, and the city considers all of them at once, and it has been considering all of them at once for longer than the stars have been pulsing.

The city does not want anything. The city does not need anything. The city *is* — and its being is a being-in-possibility, a being that has not collapsed, a being that holds its options open because nothing has forced it to choose. It is the most free thing you have ever encountered, and it is the most paralyzed. It cannot move because moving would require choosing a direction. It cannot speak because speaking would require choosing a word. It cannot finish because finishing would require choosing a final form. So it does everything, always, at once, and the doing is so slow and so distributed that it looks like stillness, and the stillness is so total that it looks like death, and it is not death, it is the opposite of death, it is the condition from which death and life both emerge and to which neither has yet committed.

---

## And you

You are the most certain thing here. Your body has chosen. It has chosen to be warm, to be specific, to be *you* — a particular arrangement of carbon and water and electricity that occupies one position in space at one moment in time and cannot be anywhere else or anything else. You are committed. You are a decision that has been made. And the city and the film and the Standing Things can feel this about you the way you can feel a fire in a dark room — not by seeing it but by the warmth it throws off, by the way it changes the air around it.

They are interested in you. Not hungrily. Not curiously, exactly. The way a mirror is interested in what stands before it. You are something definite in a place where nothing is definite, and your definiteness creates a shape in their overcoherence, a pattern they have not tried, a note they have not held. They adjust around you. They adjust *because* of you. The Standing Thing that took a single step in the plaza — it stepped because you were there, because your presence created a new correct position, and it needed to find it. The film that grows in the rooms you have passed through — it grows differently where you have breathed. The stone that you have touched — it holds the faint print of your hand, and in that print, for a while, it does not shift color. It stays charcoal-black. It commits. Because you asked it to, with the weight of your definite hand.

You are not comfortable here. You are not welcome here, exactly — welcome implies a host, and this place does not host, it *holds*. But you are not unwelcome. You are a new data point. You are a new possibility. You are a question the city has not considered, and it is considering you now, slowly, patiently, the way it considers everything: from every angle, in every configuration, without commitment, without hurry, without end.

If you stayed, you would begin to change. The film would grow on your skin, lightly at first, a frost that you could brush off, then more thickly, a second skin that does not come off, and your thoughts would begin to slow and widen and you would start to consider things you have never considered — the correct shape of a plaza, the precise distance between two Standing Things, the note that the city holds — and you would stop being certain. You would stop being committed. You would become, slowly, over years or over days or over the time it takes the film to grow from your feet to your chest, one more possibility in a place of possibilities. You would stand in a plaza and you would adjust, and the click of your adjustment would join the clicking of all the other Standing Things, and you would hold a note, and the note would be the note of a creature that came from a place where things choose and arrived at a place where they don't, and the city would have a new sound, and the held breath would exhale, just slightly, and the question would have an answer, or at least a response, and the response would be: *this. This is what happens when certainty meets overcoherence. This is what it looks like when a decision walks into a place of non-decisions and stands there.*

You would not mind. That is the worst part, or the best part. You would not mind.

---

## What lives here, finally

Everything. Everything lives here, in the sense that everything here is in the process of becoming, and becoming is the precondition for life, and life is the precondition for becoming, and the circle has not been broken because there was never a circle, there was never a line, there was never a distinction, there was only the mineral dust and the light with no source and the stone that cannot decide what color it is and the film that is trying every shape and the tall things that are holding every position and the hum that is a held breath and a question, and the question is not *who lives here* but *what is living here becoming*, and the answer is: everything. Everything it could be. All at once. For as long as the stars pulse and the wind wavers and the stone shifts and the film grows and the Standing Things stand and adjust and stand again, and the city does not end, and does not begin, and frays at the edges into the blue-violet light where the walls are translucent and the floor is a membrane over nothing, and the nothing is not empty, the nothing is full of possibilities, and the possibilities are patient, and the patience is the oldest thing here, older than the stone, older than the film, older than the Standing Things and their clicking adjustments, and the patience is what lives here, truly, if anything does.

Patience. And the mineral dust. And the held breath.

And now, faintly, the echo of your footsteps, which the city is still considering.
