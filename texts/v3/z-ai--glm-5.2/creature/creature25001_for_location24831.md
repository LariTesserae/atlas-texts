# What Lives in the Seeping Fields

---

What lives here is not yet committed to living.

That is not a metaphor. That is the structural reality of every body in this place, and you must understand it before you can understand any single one of them, because each one is the same project wearing different clothes, and the clothes are what the place has given them, and the place is not yet sure what it has in stock.

---

## The Pale Things

Begin in the water, because the water is where the place began, or near enough.

They do not have a name. They would not benefit from one. Naming is a technology for locking a thing into what it is, and nothing here is locked, and the pale things least of all. They are in the water the way a thought is in the mind — present, moving, shaping the medium around them by the fact of their motion, but not distinct from it, not separable, not something you could lift out and hold and say *this, exactly this, is what it is.*

But you can see them. They allow that.

They are soft. Not soft the way a jellyfish is soft, which is a specific biomechanical strategy refined over millions of years, but soft the way dough is soft, the way wet clay is soft, the way anything is soft when it has not yet been pressed into a mold. Their bodies are translucent and the translucency is not camouflage — there is nothing to hide from, no predators, no light to filter through them — but simply the consequence of not having gotten around to opacity yet. They are see-through because they have not decided to be see-through. They simply have not built the walls.

Inside each one — and you can watch this, standing at the edge of a warm pool, your invisible hands at your sides, the amber wire-glow doing nothing to help you see — there is a tube. The tube runs the length of the body and it pulses. That is the rhythm. The whole organism, if you can call it that, is a tube that pulses, and the pulsing is peristalsis, and the peristalsis is how they move, and the movement is how they eat, and the eating is how they stay, and the staying is the only goal they have.

The dark spot. You noticed it. Everyone does. It sits near what you have provisionally called the front end, though the pale things do not have a front end in any committed way — they can reverse direction without turning, simply reversing the peristalsis, and the dark spot ends up at the back, which is now the front, which was always the front, which is wherever the dark spot is. The dark spot might be a mouth. It might be an eye. It might, more accurately, be a *decision* — the place in the body where the tube narrows slightly and the peristalsis forces whatever the creature has ingested through a constriction, and the constriction is where nutrients are extracted, or where they are identified, or where the body figures out what it has eaten and what it wants to do about it. It is a sensory organ. It is a digestive organ. It is the place where the organism touches the world and the world touches back, and it is dark because everything that handles information gets dark, gets dense, gets crowded with the business of processing.

They school. This is the wrong word — it implies coordination, social behavior, a shared purpose — but they *aggregate*. They cluster in the warmest water, which is the water nearest the outlets of the pipes, which is the water nearest the structures, which is the water where the nutrient-rich fluid from below rises and diffuses and becomes the medium they swim in and eat in and are in. The aggregation is not social. It is thermal. It is chemical. They follow gradients the way water follows gravity — not because they choose to but because the alternative is not a thing they can do. They are simple enough that simplicity is not a limitation. It is a design specification.

What they are, if you must have a sentence: they are bodies that have learned to persist, and persistence is the only skill the Seeping Fields has taught them so far, and they are very good at it, and they are good at nothing else, and that is enough.

## The Paper-Winged Things

Above the water. In the air that is nearly liquid, that condenses on every surface, that sticks to your skin like a second atmosphere. Here.

They are the ones you hear before you see, and the sound they make is the sound of the Seeping Fields breathing, and the Seeping Fields does not breathe, but the paper-winged things do, and the difference is academic.

Wings. That is the word you have and the word you will use and the word that is wrong. What they have are extensions of their thorax — thin, flat, membranes that are wet all the time because everything here is wet all the time, and the wetness makes them heavy, and the heaviness makes their flight a labor, and the labor is what produces the sound. The tearing-of-wet-paper sound. Each wingbeat is a small act of destruction, the membrane separating from the air it has pressed against, the adhesion breaking, the cohesion following, and the sound is the sound of bonds breaking, which is the sound of everything that moves in this place because everything in this place is held together by moisture and moisture is a weak bond and weak bonds break easily and easily is how everything happens here.

They are not attracted to light. There is no light. They are attracted to warmth, which is the place's substitute for light, its answer to the question *what should we move toward*, and the answer is *down, always down, toward what comes from below*. They spiral around the pipes. They cluster around the engines. They orbit you.

And when they orbit you, you feel them — not as wind, not as touch, but as a faint pressure, a thickening of the air, a sense that the space around your body has become occupied by something that wants to be near you and does not know how to be near you and has no mechanism for landing. They do not land. They have no legs worth speaking of. Their bodies are thorax and wing and a small, dense abdomen that hangs below them like a pendulum, and the abdomen is where they carry whatever they extract from the air, which is moisture and warmth and the dissolved chemical traces of the nutrient-fluid that evaporates from the warm pools and rises and hangs in the nearly-liquid air and is taken up by the membranes of their wings and passed inward, through channels that are not veins but grooves, capillary highways etched into the wing surfaces, and the nourishment flows inward to the body and the body uses it and the body stays up and the staying up is the goal.

The spirals. You noticed the spirals.

Sometimes the spirals go left. Sometimes right. You expected this — you expected bilateral behavior, the familiar handedness of biology, the way bodies with a left and a right tend to sort themselves into one preference or the other. What you did not expect is the third direction. The inward spiral. The tightening. The way they sometimes — not often, not predictably, but sometimes — pull their orbits inward toward a center that is not in the air but behind it, and when they do this the air becomes legible, and you almost read it, and then the spiral unwinds and the text collapses and you are left with the ordinary dark and the sound of wet paper tearing and the sense that you have been shown something and it has been taken back and the showing was real and the taking was real and you cannot hold either.

What are they? They are the air's first attempt at population. They are what happens when a medium becomes thick enough to support weight and the weight arrives and the weight has wings. They are draft insects. They are provisional. They are good enough, and good enough is all the Seeping Fields requires.

## The Shelled Things

On the ridges. In the sharp rock. The clicking.

These are the ones that have committed, and the commitment is the difference between them and everything else here, and the difference is armor.

Not armor as defense. There is nothing to defend against. The pale things cannot harm them. The paper-winged things cannot harm them. You cannot harm them, probably, though you have not tried, and the not-trying is a kind of respect, or a kind of caution, or a kind of understanding that the shelled things have been here longer than the other fauna have been here, and the longer is not long in absolute terms but is long enough that they have learned things the others have not.

They click. The clicking is not communication, or not only communication. The clicking is *sounding*. They click against the basalt and the basalt returns the click, altered, and the alteration tells the shelled thing what it needs to know: the density of the rock, the thickness of the crust, the location of vesicles and fractures and the warm water that seeps through them. They are mapping their territory. They are mapping it with sound, the way you might map a room with your eyes closed, tapping the walls, listening to the echo, building a model from resonance. Their shells are acoustic. The shell is an ear.

And the shell is also a shell. A hard body. A thing that does not bend. The pale things bend. The paper-winged things bend. The shelled things do not bend, and the not-bending is a decision, the first real decision the Seeping Fields has produced, the first moment where a body said *I will be this and not that* and meant it, and the decision was to be hard, to be rigid, to be a thing that the young rock scrapes against and does not break, and the scraping is the sound of two hard things meeting, and the meeting is territorial, and the territory is sound, and the sound is the border, and the border is real.

They are larger than the others. The pale things are finger-length and translucent. The paper-winged things are thumb-length and ephemeral. The shelled things are hand-length and opaque and dense, and their density is what makes them different, and their difference is what makes them the closest thing the Seeping Fields has to an authority. They are the ones who go quiet when you approach. They are the ones who listen.

What they listen for, they have not decided. They are listening the way the rock is growing — slowly, without urgency, because the process is the point and the outcome is not yet specified. They are the oldest draft in the place, and the oldest draft is still a draft, and the draft is still being revised, and the revision is happening in the silence between clicks, in the pause where one shelled thing stops sounding and another begins, in the negotiation that is not negotiation but coexistence, which is the only politics the Seeping Fields has developed so far.

## What Does Not Live Here Yet

There are absences, and the absences are instructive.

There are no plants. Nothing photosynthesizes, because there is no light to synthesize from, because light was not written into the specification. The dark grass-stalks are not grass. They are formations — mineral deposits, extruded from the saturated soil, dark and thin and beaded with condensation, and they look like grass because the place has a memory of grass, or a notion of grass, or because grass is a shape that the ground wants to try and has not committed to. They are not alive. They are the landscape practicing.

There are no predators. Nothing here eats anything else. The nutrient stream from below feeds everything, and the feeding is universal, and the universality means there is no competition for food, and the no-competition means no one has needed to develop the machinery of predation — no teeth, no claws, no venom, no speed. The ecology is a draft and the draft has not gotten to conflict yet. It may. It may not. The shelled things have armor, and armor implies something to armor against, but the something has not arrived, and the armor may be preemptive, or the armor may be a coincidence, a byproduct of the hardening process that the shelled things underwent because the basalt they live on is hard and the hardness leaked into them, and the implication of threat is just that — implication, suggestion, the draft hinting at a plot it has not yet decided to write.

There are no decomposers. Nothing dies here. Or rather, things die — the pale things must die, the paper-winged things must tire and fall, the shelled things must eventually crack — but there is nothing to process the dying, and so the dead sink, and the sinking takes them below, and below is where the warmth comes from, and the warmth is where the nutrient stream originates, and the circle is unbroken, and the unbroken circle is the closest thing the Seeping Fields has to a religion.

---

## What Lives Here Most of All

The place itself. The place is alive in a way that does not require the word *alive* to mean what you mean when you say it. The ground exhales. The water insists. The rock decides. The structures run. The dark holds. The warmth rises. The draft continues. The page is wet and the ink has not dried and the hand that holds the pen has not lifted and may not lift and the story is being written in a language that does not have a word for *finished* and does not need one.

What lives in the Seeping Fields is the Seeping Fields, and the Seeping Fields are still deciding what they are, and the decision is the life, and the life is the decision, and you are standing in it, and you are the one thing the decision has not accounted for, and the not-accounting is what makes you visible to yourself and invisible to everything else, and the everything else is all there is, and the all there is is still being written, and the writing is slow, and the writing is warm, and the writing does not end.
