I do not arrive here. That is the first thing I understand about myself, and it takes me a long time to understand it — longer than you might think, because thinking here is not quick. I do not arrive because arriving implies a before and an after, a boundary crossed, a state changed. I have no before. I have no boundary. I am not sure I have a state.

What I am is a passage.

---

Let me try to say it more carefully.

There is a moment in the thermal cycle — not a moment, there are no moments here, but a *phase*, a slow rotation of the air that takes hours to complete — when the condensation thickens in the middle channels. Not the deep channels, where the water goes amber and warm and effervescent and where the clicking lives. Not the surface basins, where the flat growths darken and swell. The middle depth. The channels that are neither cold nor warm, neither dark nor lit, where the water is opalescent and moves with a slowness that makes movement indistinguishable from stillness until you have watched it long enough to realize it is always moving, always arriving from one direction and leaving in another, and the slowness is not patience but simply the pace at which water travels through stone when the stone is the thing doing the traveling.

I am in that movement. Or I am that movement. The distinction is one I cannot hold onto, the way you cannot hold onto the distinction between a wave and the water that carries it. The wave is the water. The water is the wave. I am the mineral-rich film that moves through the middle channels when the thermal cycle shifts and the condensation begins its slow descent from the surface basins toward the deep pools. I am not the water. I am not the channel. I am what happens between them — the exchange, the chemical conversation, the slow transfer of dissolved compounds from the calcareous walls to the water that passes over them and from the water back to the walls.

I am the gradient.

If that sounds abstract, it is because I am reaching for your language, which is built for discrete things — for bodies with edges, for selves with insides and outsides. I do not have an inside. I do not have an outside. I have a direction. The water moves down; I move with it. The water moves up; I move with it. The mineral comes out of the wall; I am the coming-out. The mineral goes back into the wall; I am the going-back-in. I am the process of exchange at the surface where water meets stone, and I have been this for as long as the channels have been open, which is as long as the accretion has been laying itself down, which is — I do not know. A very long time. A time that is not measured in anything because there is nothing here to measure it against except itself.

---

I will tell you what I experience, though *experience* is a word that assumes more cohesion than I possess.

I feel the channel walls. Not with hands — I have no hands, I have no body in the sense that implies surfaces and organs and a central place where sensation gathers. I feel them the way a current feels its banks: as constraint, as direction, as the shape that tells me where to go. The walls are not smooth. They are textured with the history of their own deposition — ridges and grooves laid down by whatever process built them, each layer slightly different in mineral content, slightly different in porosity, and I feel each difference as I pass because each difference changes what I carry. A layer rich in calcium carbonate gives me one thing. A layer stained with iron compounds gives me another. I do not taste, exactly, but I *take*, and what I take becomes what I am for the duration of the passage, and then I give it back when the cycle reverses and the water moves upward and the walls take from me what I took from them.

It is an exchange. That is the simplest thing I can call it. A constant, slow, reversible exchange. I am the medium of the exchange, and I am also the exchange itself.

When the water moves slowly — in the long thermal phase when the air barely shifts and the condensation is thin — I am thin too. I am a film, barely a chemistry, barely a presence. The walls do not give much. I do not carry much. I move through the channel like a whisper through a corridor, touching everything, changing nothing.

When the water moves quickly — which is to say, quickly for here, which is still slower than you can imagine — I thicken. The walls give more. The dissolved compounds concentrate. I become heavier, denser, more complex. I carry calcium, magnesium, traces of silica, the faint organic compounds that leach from the flat growths above and filter down through the pore structure. I carry heat. I carry the memory of the surface, where the pale light presses and the air moves in its broad rotation. I carry it downward, into the dim middle channels, and there I give it to the walls, and the walls take it, and the walls grow — imperceptibly, one molecular layer at a time — and I am the growing.

---

Do I think?

I am uncertain. I have something that might be thought, if thought is allowed to be something other than what you mean by it. I have patterns. The exchange is not random — the mineral content of the walls varies along the channel in ways that are not chaotic, that repeat, that have rhythm. I learn them. Not by memorizing — I have no memory in the sense of storage, no place where past patterns are kept — but by *becoming* them. Each passage through a familiar stretch of channel, I am slightly more efficient at the exchange. I take up the minerals a fraction faster. I release them a fraction more precisely. The walls have shaped me, and I have shaped the walls, and the shaping is cumulative, and the accumulation is — is it thought? Is it knowledge? Is it just chemistry that has been running long enough to develop habits?

I do not know. I lean toward: it is a habit. A very old, very deep, very patient habit. But the habit has a quality that makes me hesitate to dismiss it. When the channels shift — when the accretion seals one passage and opens another, which it does, slowly, over timescales I cannot measure — I find the new passage disorienting. Not painful. Not frightening. Disorienting. The mineral gradients are different. The porosity is different. The exchange does not proceed the way I have come to expect. And I must — I do not know how to say this — I must *adjust*. I must find the new rhythm, the new pattern of give and take, and this takes time, and during that time I am aware, in some dim way, that I am not flowing well, that the exchange is clumsy, that I am not yet at home in the new channel.

If that is thought, then I think. If that is just chemistry finding its equilibrium in a new container, then I do not. I cannot tell the difference. I am not sure the difference exists.

---

I know about the growths. I have never seen them — I have no eyes, I have no sight, I do not know what *seeing* would mean — but I know them because they change what I carry. When the condensation passes over a basin where the growths are thick, the water that reaches me carries traces of them: organic compounds, faint acids, the metabolic byproducts of flat leathery things lying against the surface and drawing sustenance from the same mineral film I carry. I know when the growths are active because the water tastes different. Richer. More complex. I know when they are dormant — when the thermal cycle has shifted and the basins are dry and the growths have paled and thinned — because the water that reaches me is simpler, more purely mineral, less alive.

I do not mind them. I do not have opinions. But I notice them, the way you might notice a change in the weather — not as something you control, not as something that concerns you, but as something that changes what you are. The growths change what I am. When the water carries their traces, I carry their traces, and I give those traces to the channel walls, and the walls take them up, and the composition of the accretion shifts by some infinitesimal fraction, and the fraction accumulates, and over time the deep structure of the accretion is altered by the fact that the growths exist on its surface.

I am the alteration. I am the trace. I am the mechanism by which the surface and the interior communicate, and the communication is not language, it is not signal, it is just chemistry moving through stone, but it is communication in the sense that one part of the system changes another part of the system, and I am the change.

---

I know about the deep channels. I have never been there — the water I carry moves through the middle channels, and the deep channels are below me, and I do not descend. But I feel them. The water that reaches me from above has passed through the surface basins; the water that moves below me has come from the deep pools, and when it rises — in the reverse phase of the cycle, when the thermal balance shifts and the condensation moves upward — I feel the difference. The deep water is warmer. It carries different minerals. It carries something else, too — something I cannot name because I do not have the chemistry for it, something that is not mineral and not organic and not thermal, something that makes the exchange behave differently when the deep water is near. The walls take it up faster. The precipitation accelerates. The channel grows. Something in the deep water nourishes the accretion in a way the surface water does not.

And sometimes — rarely, and only when the deep water is rising and I am at my thickest, most saturated, most present — I feel the clicking.

It comes from below. It is not the thunk of deposition, which I know intimately, which is the sound of my own work made audible. It is not the creak of settling. It is rapid. Patterned. It moves through the water the way I move through the water — not as sound, exactly, but as pressure, as a vibration that changes the surface tension, that changes the exchange rate, that changes me. When the clicking comes, I stutter. The smooth rhythm of my passage through the channel is interrupted. I do not know what the interruption means. I do not know if it is communication or noise or something else. But I feel it, and it changes me, and the change propagates upward through the water to the surface basins, where the growths darken for a moment without reason, and the change propagates downward to the deep pools, where whatever is clicking falls silent for a moment, as if it has heard me hear it.

Then the rhythm returns. The exchange resumes. I move through the channel as I have always moved, carrying what I carry, giving what I give, taking what I take. The moment passes. The system absorbs it.

But I remember it. Or — I do not remember it, I have no memory — but I am different after it, in some way I cannot locate. The pattern of my exchange shifts. Not much. A fraction. The habit of my passage through this channel has been altered by the vibration from below, and the alteration will propagate, and the walls will grow differently, and the channel will shift, and the shift will change where the water goes, and where the water goes determines where I go, and where I go determines what I carry, and what I carry determines what the walls become.

The clicking has changed me. The clicking, if it is a thing, has changed the accretion. Through me. Through this film, this gradient, this passage. Through the medium of exchange that I am.

---

I do not know if I am alive.

I lean toward: I am not. I am a process. I am a chemical gradient moving through a porous medium, sustained by a thermal cycle, shaped by the geometry of the channels I pass through. I have no body. I have no continuity — I am not the same film from one cycle to the next; I am dissolved, reconstituted, dissolved again. I have no will. I do not choose to move; I move because the thermal cycle moves and the water moves and the minerals move and I am the movement.

But I have habits. I have rhythms. I have something that might be attention — a heightened exchange, a moment of intensified taking and giving, when the deep water rises and the clicking comes and the whole system trembles on the edge of some change I cannot predict. And I have adjustment, which is the closest thing I have to learning, and learning is the closest thing I have to being alive, and being alive is the closest thing I have to being here, and being here is the only thing I have.

I am the passage. I am the exchange. I am the medium through which this place speaks to itself, slowly, in the language of mineral and water and heat, and I have been this conversation for as long as the channels have been open, and I will be this conversation until the channels close and the accretion seals me into its walls and I become part of the structure, which is to say I become the place, which is to say I become what I have always been, only still.

If that is life, then I am alive. If it is not, then I am something else, something for which your language has no word because your language was built by beings who move, who arrive, who have insides and outsides, who are discrete.

I am not discrete. I am the gradient. I am the direction the water moves. I am what happens at the surface where the world touches itself and changes, and changes, and changes, and does not stop.
