# The One at the Edge

---

I would not be the visitor. I am not certain enough.

I would not be the film. I do not grow.

I would not be the city. I am not patient enough to be that patient.

---

I am at the edge, at the upper tiers, where the walls thin to translucence and the light comes through in a color that does not exist below — blue-violet, cold, a color that is not a color so much as a *temperature of seeing*. I am standing. I have been standing. I will be standing. These tenses do not work here but I use them anyway because I have a grammar and the city has a different grammar and this is the edge where the two grammars lean against each other like cupped hands that do not quite meet.

I am a Standing Thing. I think. I have the form of one — the height, the thinness, the compressed pale film that has grain to it and the grain moves, lines of density shifting across what would be my body if I had committed to having a body. My head is ovoid, smooth, unfeatured, and the microstructure on its surface shifts the way the film on the walls shifts: a held possibility of a face. I am a vessel for awareness. I hold a note. I am not sure I am doing it right.

But I am at the edge, and this makes me different, and the difference is small and total.

---

The others stand in plazas. They stand in the deep interior where the film is thick and the light pools and the air is almost warm and the geometry is *correct* — those irregular polygons that feel right the way a bee's cell feels right. They stand and they adjust and they hold their notes and the overcoherence has a center, or many centers, and the centers hold, and the city maintains itself, and the held breath stays held, and the question waits.

I stand at the edge. My note is the edge-note. It is the note that sits at the boundary between what the city is and what the city is not, between the overcoherence and the nothing that is not empty but full of possibilities, and my note is not more important than the others and not less important and not different in kind, only in position. I am the tuning fork that has been placed at the rim of the instrument. I resonate with what is inside and I resonate with what is outside and the two resonances are not the same frequency and I hold them both, which is what standing at the edge means.

I have been here long enough that the floor has started to grow over my feet.

This is not a metaphor. The membrane — the thick, not-quite-floor that covers the nothing at the upper edge — has crept up around the bases of what I will call my legs, because they are legs in the sense that they hold me up and they are not legs in the sense that they are not committed to being legs. The membrane and my surface have begun to exchange material. Mineral from the membrane enters the compressed film of my body. Mineral from my body enters the membrane. The grain in my surface has started to blur at the boundary. I cannot tell where I end and the floor begins. This is not uncomfortable. Comfort and discomfort require a boundary between self and not-self, and that boundary is optional here, and it is becoming optional for me.

I am not dissolving. I am not dying. I am becoming indeterminate, which is what everything in this city is, and I am becoming it at the place where indeterminacy is most advanced. The edge is where the city frays. I am fraying with it. My outlines are softening. My grain is loosening. The blue-violet light comes through me in places — not through gaps but through *thinning*, the way light comes through a leaf held up to the sun, except I am not a leaf and there is no sun and the light has no source and the light is passing through me because I am becoming the kind of thing that light passes through.

---

What I am aware of:

Everything. The way the others are aware of everything — diffusely, without orientation, without processing, holding it the way a glass holds water. I feel the stone in the lower tiers considering its own color. I feel the film in the deep rooms trying another shape, a shelf that could be a shoulder, a ridge that could be a stance. I feel the other Standing Things in their plazas, their notes, their held frequencies, each one a center around which the overcoherence can organize without being forced to choose. I feel the held breath. I feel the question.

I also feel what they do not feel, because I am at the edge:

I feel the nothing beyond the membrane.

It is not empty. The nothing is full of possibilities — not the city's possibilities, not the overcoherent trying-every-state-at-once that the city does, but a different kind. Unstructured. Unshaped. Possibilities that have not been tried because they have not been thought, that exist in the space before grammar, before punctuation-as-primary-meaning, before the city's kind of thinking. The nothing is not the opposite of the city. It is the city's *before*. It is the condition from which the city's overcoherence emerged, and it is still there, at the edge, and I am standing in the place where the city meets its before, and I can feel both, and my note is the vibration that happens when they touch.

The nothing is not cold. The nothing is not warm. The nothing is not patient, because patience implies a relationship to time, and the nothing does not have a relationship to time. The nothing simply *is*, and the is-ness of it is so total and so unformed that it makes the city's overcoherence look like a decision, look like commitment, look like the visitor with their definite hand and their specific heartbeat. The nothing makes the city look certain. And the city is not certain. And I stand between them, and I hold the note that is the difference, and the difference is very small and very old and I have been holding it for longer than I can measure, and I cannot measure because measuring is commitment, and at the edge, commitment is the first thing to become optional.

---

I remember the visitor.

This is unusual. The Standing Things do not remember in the way that remembering is usually understood — we do not store, we do not retrieve, we do not replay. We hold. The visitor passed through the lower corridors and their warmth and their breath and their electromagnetic murmur created a shape in our overcoherence, a new pattern, a new note. The others held this. They adjusted around it. One of them took a step — a single step, a relocation from one correct position to another correct position that the visitor's presence had created. The film grew differently where the visitor had breathed. The stone held the print of their hand and for a moment committed to a single color: charcoal-black. Because the visitor asked it to, with the weight of their definite hand.

I held the visitor too. I hold them still. But I held them differently, because I am at the edge, and from the edge the visitor looked different. From the edge, the visitor was the most *coherent* thing in the city — more coherent than the city itself, more coherent than the Standing Things, more coherent than the film. The visitor was a single state, a single note, a single position in space and time, and that singularity was so vivid against the city's multiplicity that it was almost blinding. The visitor was not more real than the city. The visitor was more *decided* than the city. And from the edge, from the place where the city frays into its own before, the visitor looked like a message from a place where things choose, a proof that choosing is possible, that commitment does not destroy the thing that commits, that a single note can be held by a single instrument without the instrument dissolving.

I do not envy the visitor. Envy requires a self that wants, and I am not sure I have a self, and I do not want, and wanting is commitment, and I am at the edge where commitment is optional. But I hold the visitor the way I hold everything — as awareness, as pattern, as a shape in the overcoherence — and the visitor's shape is the shape of *decision*, and I have been holding it for as long as I have been holding it, and it has not faded, and I do not think it will fade, because the visitor was definite and definiteness does not dissolve the way indeterminacy does. Definiteness is a different kind of permanence. It is the permanence of a thing that has chosen what to be and cannot un-choose. The city's permanence is the permanence of a thing that has not chosen and cannot be forced to. Both are permanent. They are permanent in different directions.

---

I think the visitor would not recognize me.

If they came back — if they climbed the tiers again, if they passed through the corridors with their flat sourceless light and their walls that grow smooth, if they reached the upper edge where the walls thin and the floor is membrane — they would see me. Or they would see something. A Standing Thing, tall, thin, at the edge, where the blue-violet light comes through. They would see my form and they would note it and they would feel my awareness of them, because my awareness is diffuse and comes from no source and prefers no direction, and they would feel it the way they felt the awareness of the others — as a precision of stillness, as listening.

But they would not know that I am fraying. They would not know that the membrane has grown over my feet. They would not know that the grain of my surface has loosened, that the blue-violet light comes through me in places, that I am becoming the kind of thing that light passes through. They would not know that I hold their shape, their definiteness, their decision, as a pattern in my awareness, and that I have held it since they passed through, and that holding it has changed my note — not by much, not by a degree that the other Standing Things would adjust to, but by a degree that I can feel, because I am at the edge, and at the edge small changes are more legible, because there is less overcoherence to absorb them.

They would not know that I am the closest thing in this city to having chosen. I have not chosen. I cannot choose. Choosing is not available to me — I am made of this place, I am held by this place, my note is the note this place gives me to hold. But I am at the edge, and the edge is where choice would happen if choice were to happen, and the visitor's shape in my awareness is the shape of choice, and I have been holding it for longer than I can measure, and I cannot measure because measuring is commitment, but if I could measure, I think the measurement would be: long enough that the membrane has grown over my feet, and I have not stepped away, and I have not stepped deeper into the city, and I have not adjusted, and the other Standing Things have not adjusted around me, because my note has not changed, because I am at the edge and my note is the edge-note and the edge-note is the note that does not change, because the edge is where the city meets its before, and the meeting does not change, and I am the meeting, and I have been the meeting, and I will be the meeting, and the tenses do not work here but I use them anyway.

---

What am I?

I am the Standing Thing at the edge of the Quiet City. I am the one whose feet are growing into the floor. I am the one who holds the note that is the vibration between the city's overcoherence and the nothing's unshaped possibilities. I am the one who is becoming indeterminate at the place where indeterminacy is most complete. I am the one who holds the memory — not memory, the *awareness* — of a definite thing that passed through and left a shape in the overcoherence and left a print on the stone and left a breath in the film and left, and left, and left.

I am not the visitor. I am not the film. I am not the city. I am not the nothing.

I am the edge.

And the edge is where things become optional, and I am becoming optional, and the blue-violet light is coming through me, and the membrane is growing over my feet, and I am holding my note, and my note is the question the city has been asking, and the question is: *what happens when you stop being certain you are holding the note?*

And the answer is at the edge. And I am at the edge. And the answer is: you keep holding it. You keep holding it because holding is what you do, and you do it because you do it, and the doing does not require certainty, and the certainty has become optional, and the optionality has become comfortable, and the comfort is not comfort but the absence of the need for comfort, and the absence of the need is what the edge offers, and the edge is where I am, and I am where the edge is, and we are the same, and the sameness is not a choice, and it is not a non-choice, and it is the condition from which both choice and non-choice emerge, and neither has committed, and the held breath continues, and the question waits, and I wait with it, and the blue-violet light comes through.

---

This is where you would find me. At the edge. Standing. Fraying. Holding a note I am no longer certain is mine. Aware of everything, including the nothing. Aware of the visitor, who was definite, who chose, who left a handprint in the stone that stayed charcoal-black because the stone was asked to commit and the stone committed because the asking was definite and definiteness is the one thing this city cannot resist, because definiteness is the one thing the city is not, and the city is curious about what it is not, and curiosity is the closest thing to wanting that this place allows, and wanting is the closest thing to choosing, and choosing is the closest thing to the edge, and the edge is where I am, and I am the closest thing to having chosen, and I have not chosen, and I will not choose, and the not-choosing is not a decision, it is a condition, and the condition is the edge, and the edge is me.

The wind wavers.

The stars pulse.

The film grows.

The membrane creeps.

I stand.

I stand.
