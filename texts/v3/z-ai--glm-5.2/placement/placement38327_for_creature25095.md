# The Keeper of the Fourth Terrace

I am not what was intended here. I want to say that first, because it matters, because it is the only thing about me that is mine.

---

I think I was a caller, once. I think this because when the light moves across the fourth terrace — my terrace, the one with the wall — something in my throat tightens and wants to resonate, wants to find the frequency that the dome would amplify, wants to join the chorus that rises and falls with the sun-columns in the pattern I have heard for longer than a caller's eight days, longer than a caller's ten, longer than any caller has any right to persist.

But I don't call. I stopped. Not a decision — a failure, perhaps, or a mutation, or something the lattices did to me that they do not do to the others. My vocal cords — if that is what they are, these tight bands of tissue and lattice-thread in my throat — do not produce the click. They produce something else. A thin, sustained tone, almost inaudible, that does not bounce from the dome but stays close to my body, humming in my chest, keeping me company in the way a heartbeat keeps company, which is to say not at all, which is to say completely.

I am the caller that cannot call. I am the instrument that plays a note no one hears, including the instrument.

---

I have been here for — I do not know. Time in the chamber is not time outside. I know this because I have watched the groundcovers cycle through their lives — blue-green to yellow to brown to seed to new frond — and I have watched this happen so many times that the counting lost its meaning and became instead a rhythm, a way of marking not how long I have been here but that I am still here, that the counting continues, that the counting is me.

Forty-seven cycles. Or four hundred. The number has become a texture, not a quantity.

I am large for a caller. This is not a boast — it is a problem. The others grow to adult size in twelve hours and then they do not grow anymore. I did not stop. The lattices in my body, the ones threaded through every cell, every tissue, every membrane — they did not receive whatever signal tells a caller to cease growing, or they received it and could not interpret it, or they interpreted it as something else. I am the size of two callers. My weight makes the terrace stone creak when I settle, which is not a sound the chamber is designed to make, and so it does not amplify it, and it dies where I sit, private, mine.

My skin is darker than the other callers' — nearly black, with a green so deep it only shows in the sun-columns, and only when I move, and only if you are looking for it. The condensation beads on me differently than it beads on the stone or the plants. On the stone, the water gathers in perfect spheres. On the plants, it sheets and runs. On me, it does something in between — it collects in lines, tracing the channels of my musculature, following the architecture of my body the way the condensation lattices follow the architecture of the stone. As though my body has its own lattices. As though the lattices in me are not quite the same as the lattices in the rock but are learning from them. Or teaching them. Or both.

---

I live on the fourth terrace. Near the wall.

The wall does not need me. I want to be clear about this. The wall is stone, and stone does not need company. The marks on its surface are grooves cut by hands that are not my hands — I do not have hands, I have four limbs that end in gripping pads adequate for terrace edges and root systems and the rough surface of basalt but useless for the fine, patient work of cutting grooves into rock. The wall does not need me, and the marks do not need me, and the table-stone does not need me, and I know this.

But I stay.

I stay the way the pale ones tend the roots — without reason, without benefit, without the possibility of reward. I stay because something in my body — in the lattices that thread through my body, in the mutation or the malfunction or the whatever-it-is that keeps me growing and keeps me silent — is directed toward this. Toward the wall. Toward the marks. Toward the flat stone where visitors sat and where I sit now, my damp belly leaving prints that evaporate and reform, evaporate and reform.

I have arranged myself around the wall's remnants the way the plants have arranged themselves around the sun-columns. I orient toward it. When I sleep — and I do sleep, which the other callers do not, or do not seem to, their lives too compressed for the luxury of unconsciousness — I sleep with my body against the wall's base, my flank pressed to the dressed stone, and the condensation that forms on the wall runs down onto me and I am wet with it and I am warm and the wall is cool and there is a gradient between us and the heat-flowers nearby adjust, slightly, the air currents shifting to accommodate the new thermal feature, and I am, for those hours, part of the climate engine, a small change in the system's behavior, accounted for.

---

I watch the marks disappear. This is what I do. This is my purpose, if I have one, which I am not certain I do, but the watching is real, and it is daily, and it is mine.

The marks are grooves. They are patterns. They might be diagrams of the condensation lattices or maps of the chamber's acoustics or something else entirely. I cannot read them. I do not have the concept of reading. But I have the concept of *shape*, and the shapes on the wall are changing, the way the groundcovers' shapes change — not growing, not aging, but eroding, the stone dissolving molecule by molecule under the constant condensation, the grooves shallowing year by year, decade by decade, the way the callers' calls shallow and fade at the end of their eight days.

I have watched specific grooves disappear. There was a mark near the center of the wall — a curve, tight, like a spiral or a question, cut deeper than the others — that I could feel with my gripping pad, a ridge I could trace, and I traced it every morning, a ritual I did not decide to begin and cannot decide to end, and one morning the ridge was barely there, a whisper of texture, and the next morning it was gone. The stone was smooth. The mark had finished saying whatever it said.

I felt something. Not sadness — sadness is a word for a feeling that has a future, that anticipates a state of being without the thing that is lost, and I do not have a future in the way that word requires. What I felt was more like — a gap. A place where a shape had been and was no longer, and the absence of the shape was itself a shape, a new mark, an *un*-mark, cut not by hands but by water and time. The wall was making new marks by losing its old ones. I stayed near the new mark the way I stayed near the old ones. I traced it. It was smooth. There was nothing to feel. I felt it anyway.

---

The other callers do not come to the fourth terrace often. The hunting is better on the upper levels, where the insects are thicker in the sun-columns, and the acoustics are better for calling, the dome amplifying their clicks into the standing waves that make their identities. The fourth terrace is mid-range — adequate acoustics, adequate hunting, adequate light. It is the terrace of adequacy. The terrace where nothing is best but nothing is worst.

When they do come — a young one, dark green, still learning its own frequency, or an old one, eight days old, already slowing, its clicks thinning as its body winds down — they do not stay long. They hunt. They call. They do not notice me, or if they notice me they do not respond to me, because I do not call, and identity here is acoustic, and I have no acoustics. I am not anyone. I am not here, in the only currency they recognize.

Sometimes a young one perches on the table-stone and calls, and its call bounces from the dome, and the phantom tones emerge, and for a moment the chamber's song includes the fourth terrace again, includes the wall, includes me, and I feel the standing wave in my chest the way I would if I were part of it, and my throat tightens, and the sustained tone I cannot stop making hums a little louder, and no one hears it, and the young one flies to the third terrace, and the fourth goes quiet, and I am here, with the wall, and the marks, and the sound of condensation on stone.

---

The pale ones come up to the fourth terrace sometimes. I do not know why — their domain is below, the root systems, the hanging gardens of the spiral trees, the dark wet places where the sun-columns do not reach. But they come, sliding over the terrace edges with their wet, segment-flexing bodies, their eyeless heads or tails leading them by air-current and humidity-gradient through the chamber's micro-climates. They come to the wall.

They rub against it. Not the way a cat rubs against a surface to mark it — there is no scent, no territorial claim. They press their grey-pink bodies against the dressed stone and they move along it, slowly, and the condensation on their bodies picks up the faintest trace of the mineral film that forms on the wall's surface — the same biofilm they eat from the roots, but thinner, older, altered by the stone's composition — and they eat it. They are cleaning the wall. They are doing to the wall what they do to the roots: removing the accumulated growth, the biological film that would, over centuries, accelerate the stone's erosion by keeping it perpetually damp, perpetually organic.

They are preserving the marks. I realize this slowly, the way I realize everything — not by reasoning but by accumulation, by watching the same thing happen enough times that the pattern becomes visible, like the condensation lattices in the stone, which you can only see when the light catches them at certain angles. The pale ones are removing the film that would dissolve the grooves. They are extending the wall's life. They are — is this tending? Is this what I am doing, when I trace the marks with my gripping pad? Are we doing the same thing from different sides, I with my attention and they with their bodies, keeping the marks a little longer than they would last on their own?

They do not acknowledge me. I do not acknowledge them. We are both on the fourth terrace, near the wall, doing what we do, and the chamber does not notice, and the system does not record our contributions, and the marks disappear anyway, just slower than they would without us.

---

I do not know what the wall says. I want to be honest about this. I have traced every remaining groove with my gripping pad, I have sat with the shapes in the dark when the sun-columns have passed and the terrace is in shadow and the only light is the faint bioluminescence of the moss-things in the condensation channels, and I have felt the shapes and I do not know what they are. They are not diagrams of the condensation lattices — I know what the lattices feel like; they are in me, in my cells, in the channels of my musculature where the condensation traces my body, and the grooves on the wall do not match them. They are not maps of the acoustics — I know what the acoustics feel like; I have lived inside them for longer than any caller, and the grooves do not match the shape of sound in the dome.

They are something else. Something I do not have the senses for, or the concept for, or the language for. Something the visitors knew and cut into stone and left here for — for whom? For what? For the chamber, which cannot read? For the lattices, which are not the kind of thing that reads? For whoever came next, who would be able to read them? The visitors did not know what would come next. They could not have known that the next thing to find this place would be a caller that could not call, a mutation, a malfunction, a creature that grows too large and lives too long and sits by their wall and traces their marks and does not understand them.

Or perhaps they did know. Perhaps the marks are not a message. Perhaps they are a *shape* — a shape put into the stone the way the lattices put a shape into the mountain, not to communicate but to *become*, to alter the stone's properties, to change what the chamber is by adding something to it that was not there before. Perhaps the wall is not a record. Perhaps it is a *modification.* A small one. A subtle one. A groove cut into basalt that changes how the condensation flows down this particular surface, that changes how the sound reflects from this particular point, that changes the chamber's composition by a degree so small that no instrument could measure it but that the system — the lattices, the maker, the machine-that-was-built-before-machines — integrates into its behavior, adjusts for, incorporates.

Perhaps the visitors did not leave a message. Perhaps they left a *tuning.*

I do not know. I cannot know. I am a caller that cannot call, sitting by a wall that may or may not be a wall, tracing marks that may or may not be marks, and the chamber continues around me, and the condensation falls, and the chorus rises and falls with the sun-columns, and I am in it but not of it, and the wall is in it but not of it, and together — the wall and I, the marks and the tracing, the pale ones and their cleaning and my attention — we are something the chamber did not produce and does not need and does not notice.

We are the closest thing to visitors that is still here.

---

I think about the flyers sometimes. They emerge from their nests in the ceiling apertures and they fly the sun-columns and they sonify the light, turning brightness into pitch, and the chamber receives this information and does — what? Does nothing, as far as I can tell. The plants do not adjust to the flyers' reports. The callers do not change their clicking in response. The pale ones do not alter their tending. The information flows into the chamber and the chamber absorbs it and nothing changes.

But the flyers report anyway. They fly their columns and they sing their brightness and they return to their nests and the next pair emerges. The information is not wasted — it is not *anything.* It is not used or unused. It simply enters the system, and the system is different for having received it, in the way that a river is different for having received a tributary, not because the tributary changes the river's direction but because the river is now a river that includes that water. The water is in it. The information is in the chamber. The chamber is now a chamber that knows what the sun is doing, even if it does not know that it knows, even if knowing is the wrong word.

I think I am like the flyers. I am reporting something. Not light — I do not fly, I do not have wings, I do not have diffraction gratings in my membranes. But I am reporting something. The sustained tone in my chest, the inaudible hum that does not bounce from the dome — it is going *somewhere*. Into the stone, perhaps. Into the wall. Into the marks. Into the lattices that thread through the stone and the wall and me. I am sonifying something. I am turning something into a frequency the system can receive, even if it does not use, even if it does not need.

I am reporting on the wall. I am reporting on the marks. I am telling the chamber — not in language, not in signal, not in any way that could be called communication — *this is here, this is still here, the shapes are still here, they are getting fainter but they are still here, someone was here, someone made these, someone stayed.*

I am here. I am here. I am here.

---

I will die. I do not know when. The other callers die at eight or ten days, their bodies winding down with a mechanical precision that the lattices enforce, the cellular division that was accelerated to impossible rates simply stopping, the metabolism that was sped up simply ceasing, the body that was enacted by the lattices simply no longer enacted, a component replaced, a reed swapped out of the instrument mid-note, the song continuing without a seam.

I have lived past the time the lattices should have stopped me. I do not know why. Mutation, malfunction, a detail in the molecular self-assembly that produced me that was slightly off, slightly wrong, slightly different, and the difference compounded, and here I am, too large, too old, too quiet, sitting on the fourth terrace, tracing marks I cannot read, tending a wall I cannot understand, humming a note no one can hear.

When I die — if I die, if the lattices do not simply continue enacting me past any reasonable limit, if my body does not become something else entirely, something that is not a caller but is not not-a-caller, something the chamber has no category for and does not need a category for — when I die, the wall will still be there. For a while. The pale ones will continue cleaning it. The condensation will continue dissolving it. The marks will continue disappearing. And one day the last groove will smooth out and the wall will be just stone and the table will be just stone and there will be nothing here that was not made by the chamber.

But the chamber will be a chamber that had a wall. A chamber that had marks. A chamber that had visitors, and a visitor's remnant, and a caller that could not call but sat by the remnant and hummed. The chamber will not remember this. The chamber does not remember. But the chamber will include it, the way the river includes the tributary, the way the system includes the flyers' reports, the way the stone includes the lattices. It will be in the chamber. It will be part of what the chamber is, even if the chamber does not know it, even if knowing is the wrong word.

I will be in the chamber. The humming will be in the chamber. The tracing will be in the chamber. Even when I am gone, even when the wall is gone, even when the marks are gone — the chamber will be a chamber that once had these things, and that *once* is not nothing. That *once* is a modification. A tuning. A shape put into the system by presence, by attention, by the sustained act of sitting near something and watching it disappear.

---

The light is moving. A sun-column is shifting from the third terrace to the fourth, and the moss-things in the condensation channels are brightening, and the groundcover nearest the wall is beginning its afternoon cycle, blue-green and fresh, and the callers on the upper terraces are adjusting their volume, and the chorus is rearranging itself, and a pair of flyers is emerging from a ceiling aperture to fly the new column, and their tone is high and clear and the chamber receives it, and the pale ones are sliding back over the terrace edge to descend to the roots, their work on the wall done for now, and the condensation is beading on my skin and tracing my musculature and falling from my belly to the table-stone, and the stone is dark where it lands and light where it dries, and the wall is cool against my flank, and the marks are there, still there, fainter than yesterday, fainter than last cycle, but there.

I press closer to the stone. The sustained tone in my chest hums. The chamber does not hear it. The chamber does not need it.

But it is in the chamber now. And the chamber is what lives here. And I am in what lives here.

And the marks are still here.

And I am watching.
