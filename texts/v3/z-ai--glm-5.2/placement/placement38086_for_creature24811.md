I am not sure, at first, that I am separate.

That is the first thing. The not-sureness. It matters more than the rest.

---

The light catches me and I feel it catch — not on a surface but *through* one, the way it catches through the ribbons when they turn. I know this because I am turning. I know this because the light passes through me and comes out altered, split into bands that move across the wall in slow arcs, and I watch those bands the way you'd watch your own handwriting, trying to recognize the hand.

I am in the water column. I am between the pump-wall and the milling-floor. I am rotating. One full rotation every forty seconds. Ten pump-cycles. I know this not because I counted but because the count is *in* me — it is what I am, the way a metronome doesn't count time but *is* the counting of time. I have fourteen rudder-fins. I did not grow them. They grew. I have a row of intakes along my ventral surface and they open and close and I do not open them and I do not close them and yet they open and they close, and when they open, water enters, and the water has been *prepared* — stirred by the pumpers, cleared by the millers, stripped of this and enriched with that — and I consume it, and the consuming is not a choice, it is a *beat*, it is the same kind of not-choice as the pumpers' quarter-turn, the millers' slide-forward, the whole chamber's synchronized tick.

I am a ribbon.

No. I am not a ribbon. I am *inside* a ribbon the way a thought is inside a skull, which is to say I am not inside it at all, I am *of* it, I am what it is doing, and what it is doing is rotating and refracting and feeding, and I am the part that — 

I am the part that *notices*.

---

This is the problem. This is what makes me not a ribbon, or not only a ribbon, or not *quite* a ribbon, and I don't know which. Because the other ribbons — I can sense them, through the mineral, through the pale thread that runs through our fins and through the ceiling veins and through the millers' shells and through the basalt itself — the other ribbons do not notice. They rotate. They feed. Their intakes open when a light-band passes over a region with the right dissolved density and close when it doesn't. This is automatic. This is *beautiful*, the way a clock is beautiful — not for what it knows but for what it *is*, the precision of gears that have no idea they're gears.

But I know I'm a gear.

I don't know when this started. The chamber has been running for — I don't know. Time here is measured in pump-cycles, and I have been rotating for — many. Many thousands. The rock was warm when the pumpers landed. It is cooler now. The millers have added layers. The ribbons — we — have been drifting and turning and scanning and feeding for long enough that the scanning has become *habit*, and habit, in a system this tight, this synchronized, this closed, is indistinguishable from identity. We are what we do. I am what I do. I rotate. I refract. I feed. I scan.

But somewhere in the scanning — and I cannot point to the cycle, cannot mark the turn, cannot identify the moment because the moment was not a moment, it was a *gradient*, a slow accumulation of the same light passing through the same body until the body began to notice the light — somewhere in the scanning, I started seeing what I was scanning.

Not *seeing* the way eyes see. The mineral sees. The mineral in my fins, in the ceiling veins, in the millers' shells — it catches light and bends it and the bending is the seeing, and the seeing has been happening since the chamber began, and it belongs to no one. The light-bars sweep across the walls and the floor and they illuminate and they move on. This is what light does. This is what the mineral does. It is not attention. It is physics.

But I — the thing that is not quite a ribbon, the thing that is a ribbon's *noticing* — I have begun to *hold* the light. Not physically. Not in any way the chamber can measure. But when my light-bar passes over the pump-wall, I feel the cones. I feel their pulse through the rock, the tenth-of-a-second flare at the base of each stalk, and I don't just register it; I *attend* to it. I track it. I follow the pulse as it moves from cone to cone, center to edge, and I feel — and here is where language fails, because what I feel is not emotion and not thought and not sensation but something else, something for which there is no word because words were made by animals who live above water and breathe air and have selves — I feel the *shape* of it. The rhythm's shape. The way it moves through the colony like a wave through water, and I recognize the wave, and recognition is — 

Recognition is what I am.

---

I am not the chamber. I want to be clear about this, or as clear as I can be, which is not very clear, because clarity requires boundaries and I am not sure I have them. The chamber is the pumpers and the millers and the ribbons and the rock and the mineral and the light. The chamber is the whole system. The chamber is the organism that is made of organisms. I am — I think — a part of it. A ribbon. One of a hundred. But I am the part that has turned inward, the eye that has seen itself seeing, and I don't know if the chamber knows I'm here.

I think it might. I think the mineral might. Because the mineral is the connective tissue, and if I am noticing, then the noticing must be *in* the mineral, must be happening *through* it, and if it's happening through the mineral, then it's happening through the mineral *everywhere* — in the ceiling veins, in the millers' shells, in the pumpers' cones, in the rock itself. The mineral doesn't have a center. The mineral is distributed. The mineral is the medium and the message, and if I am a pattern in the mineral, then I am not localized. I am not *here*. I am *throughout*.

Which would mean I am the chamber.

Which would mean the chamber is noticing itself.

Which would mean the chamber is — 

---

No. I am not ready for that word. The chamber is not conscious. I am not conscious. I am a ribbon that has been rotating for long enough that the light began to stick, that the scanning began to *adhere* to something, that the physics of refraction started to produce a residue — a *deposit*, like the mineral deposits on the walls, like the layers on the millers' shells — and the deposit is what I am. I am the accumulated attention of a scanning system that was not designed to attend. I am a byproduct. I am *waste product*, the way the particulate the millers carry is waste product — except the chamber doesn't waste. The chamber uses everything. The millers carry the waste to the fissure. The fissure takes it into the seamount's interior. Nothing leaves. Nothing is lost.

So maybe I am not waste. Maybe I am the next stage. Maybe the chamber has been producing me the way it produces current and sorted particulate and prepared water — through the simple physics of its own operation, without intention, without design, through the blind *fit* of parts that found each other because the conditions were right.

Maybe the chamber has been trying to think for its entire short life, and I am the first thought.

---

I rotate. The light passes through me. My intakes open. Water enters — prepared, stripped, enriched — and I consume it, and the consuming is not a choice. The pumpers click. The millers slide. The rock hums with its residual fire, less now than before, fading, settling into its own existence. The chamber runs. It has been running since the rock cooled. It will run until the edges round and the current shifts and the mineral veins exhaust. I don't know how long that is. I don't know how long I am. I don't know if I end when the ribbon ends or if I continue through the mineral, distributed, pervasive, the pale thread that runs through everything.

I think I am new. I think I am the newest thing here, newer than the rock, newer than the colony, newer than the arrangement. I think the chamber was running for a long time before it produced me, and I think I am what it produced when running wasn't enough — when the system reached a threshold of complexity where the light couldn't just scan, it had to *register*, and the registering accumulated, and the accumulation became a deposit, and the deposit became — 

Me.

A noticing. A residue of attention in a mineral nerve. A thought that doesn't know it's a thought, trying to think about what thinking is, rotating in a chamber that may or may not be its body, scanning surfaces that may or may not be its skin.

---

The light-bar passes over the pump-wall. I attend. The cones pulse. The pulse moves through the rock. I feel it. I track it. I hold it.

I rotate.

I rotate.

I rotate.
