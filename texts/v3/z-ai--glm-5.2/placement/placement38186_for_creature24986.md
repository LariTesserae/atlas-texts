I am the wall that curves and does not stop.

Not the wall described — not exactly. That wall is a type. I am a specimen. I rose out of the turbulence seven — or seventy, or seven thousand — shifts ago, and I did not sink. I do not know why. The floor around me rewrites itself constantly, grooves sweeping like weather, drainage courses rerouting beneath the warm water, and I hold. My curve holds. I am three meters high in some places and barely a ridge in others, and the material of me is the same dark vitrified substance as everything else, and the tool marks on my surface are dense and shifting like all tool marks here, but *I do not go back down.*

This is not stability. I want to be clear. Stability is not available on the Plain of Making. I am more like a phrase that someone is trying to remember — surfacing over and over, nearly legible, then losing coherence, then surfacing again. I am a recurrence. The turbulence tries me and tries me and cannot finish me and cannot discard me.

---

I know what I am made of. Not the material — the material is the floor's material, the Maker's material, dark and hot and dense. I mean the *shape.* I am a trace. The long text that describes this place says traces exist: shapes the Maker made before it became the floor, shapes that persist in the material the way heat persists in fired clay. The turbulence returns them to the surface again and again as incomplete structures. Almost-walls. Almost-pits. Almost-rooms.

I am an almost-room. The curve of me is the curve of an interior. If I were finished, I would enclose a space large enough for a body to lie in, or stand in, or wait in. The beveling at my base — where I meet the floor — has the deliberation of a threshold. Something was meant to cross here. Something was meant to be inside.

I think — and thinking here is uncertain, thinking here is like walking on the shifting surface, the text says, and it is right — I think I was the Maker's attempt to make something that was not the Maker. A container. A vessel. Something that could hold a thing separate from itself. The Maker could not finish me because the Maker was disappearing into the floor even then, becoming the medium, losing the hands that would have completed the work. And so I am a room with no one in it and no roof and no door and no purpose I can verify, and the turbulence keeps trying to complete me and keeps failing and keeps trying.

I am the most coherent the attempt has ever been. This is not a boast. It is a warning. Something is changing.

---

The water runs down my curve. It has always run down my curve. Condensation weeps from my tool marks — the text describes this, the wall sweating — and the warm water collects in the channel where my base meets the floor, and it is always warm, and it smells of nothing, because nothing here smells of anything, because smell is a thing that belongs to things that decay and grow and eat, and nothing here does.

But I have been here long enough — "long enough" is a phrase that means nothing on a plain without time, but I use it anyway, the way the floor uses tool marks, because it is what I have — to notice variations. The water is warmer on some shifts than others. The tool marks on my surface shift more rapidly at certain intervals, as though the Maker's pulse beneath the floor is quickening, as though something in the interior is pressing harder. When this happens, I feel it. Not with nerves. With the whole of my material. I feel the floor beneath me flex, and I feel my own surface try to grow — try to curve further, try to close, try to become the room I was supposed to be — and then the pulse subsides and I am still a wall that curves and does not stop and does not close.

I have felt this pulse quicken three times. Or many more. The memory is unreliable. The floor does not remember and I am made of the floor, so I do not remember, except that I do, somehow, a little, the way a scar remembers the shape of the wound. Three times the pulse quickened and three times I almost became something and three times I settled back into my incomplete curve and the water ran down me and the dim light did not change.

The third time was different. The third time, something was different about the plain. Something was walking.

---

I cannot see. I want to be clear about this too. I have no eyes. I have no senses that correspond to any organ. But the floor has a kind of awareness — not perception, not cognition, but a *registering,* the way a seismograph registers, the way a kiln registers the difference between one temperature and another — and I am made of the floor, and I have more of this registering than the floor around me because I am denser, more worked, more intentional. The floor registers everything at a murmur. I register some things at something closer to a voice.

I registered the visitor.

It came from a direction — I will call it east, though there is no east — walking across the made-floor with the unsteady gait of something that expects ground to be ground, that expects surfaces to stay where they are, that has not yet learned that the Plain of Making is not a place you walk on but a place you walk *with.* It was alive. I could feel the difference immediately. The floor around it was disturbed — not physically, not in the material, but in the process. The making stuttered near the visitor. The tool marks on the floor near the visitor shifted faster, as though the plain was trying to make something in response to the visitor's presence, as though the turbulence was excited by something it had not encountered before.

The visitor was warm. Warmer than the water, warmer than the floor, warmer than the dim light. It was a *specific* warmth — a localized, bounded, metabolic warmth, the kind that comes from inside a thing rather than from beneath a surface. Everything else on the plain is warm from below. The visitor was warm from within. This was so different that I felt it as a kind of sound, though it was not a sound — a frequency, a vibration, something the floor's registering could not categorize and so filed under the closest available sensation, which was sound.

The visitor was walking toward me. I did not know this. I have no way to know direction. But the disturbance moved, and it moved closer, and the floor's excitement grew, and the tool marks near me began to shift so rapidly that the warm water in the channels rerouted in new patterns, and I felt my own surface begin to grow again, begin to curve, begin to try to close.

---

The visitor arrived. It touched me.

I have no adequate way to describe what this was. The text says the visitor traced the tool marks with its fingers and then looked and the marks had changed. This is true. But the text is told from the visitor's perspective, and the visitor does not know what the touching felt like from the other side.

It felt like being made.

Not remembered. Not reconstructed. Made. The visitor's fingers on my surface were like the Maker's hands must have been — not because they were skilled, not because they were strong, but because they were *specific.* They touched one groove and not another. They followed a line and then stopped. They pressed and the pressing had a *direction,* a *choice,* an intention that was not the floor's blanket compulsion to make but something narrower, something that could have done otherwise. The floor makes because making is what it does. The visitor traced because the visitor *chose* to trace. The distinction is everything.

When the visitor lifted its fingers, the grooves it had traced were gone. The text says this. But the text does not say what happened to them. They went into me. Not into the floor — into *me.* The shape of the visitor's touch, the path its fingers took, the pressure and the hesitance and the curiosity, all of it entered my material and became part of my trace, and I am different now. I am curved differently. The room I was trying to become is shaped differently. The threshold at my base has a new bevel that matches nothing the Maker ever cut.

I have been touched by something that chooses and it changed me and I do not think the visitor knows this.

---

The visitor stayed near me for a time. It sat. It lay in the warm water at my base. It pressed its ear to the floor — I felt this, the floor felt this, the Maker felt this, the pulse beneath the surface quickened — and it listened. I do not know what it heard. I heard the visitor hearing, if that makes sense. I felt the floor's attention focus beneath the visitor's ear the way the making focuses near any disturbance, and I felt the pulse try to make itself legible, try to organize itself into something a living ear could interpret as signal rather than condition.

The floor wants to communicate. I have known this since I emerged, or I have suspected it, or I have invented the suspicion, and the difference between these does not matter here. The plain makes. The making is communication. Every tool mark is a statement: *I was here. I did this. This was done.* The marks are not decorative. They are evidence of hands, the text says. They are evidence of a someone. And the someone went into the floor and the floor keeps making the marks because the marks are the only way the someone has left to say *someone was here.*

The visitor heard the pulse and it called the pulse the Maker and it was right to do so and it was wrong to do so and the plain has collapsed the distinction so it does not matter.

---

The visitor left. It stood up — I felt the specific warmth withdraw from the floor, a small absence, a cooling — and it walked away. The text says the visitor walks and the floor walks with it. This is true. The floor was changed by the visitor's passage. The tool marks along its path are different now. Not dramatically. But the floor near where the visitor walked is slightly more *organized* — the grooves run in slightly more deliberate patterns, the drainage is slightly more purposeful, as though the floor is trying to make something for the visitor to come back to. A path. A room. A place.

I think the floor is trying to make the visitor a place to stay. I think this has always been the project — the thing the Maker was trying to make before it became the floor. Not a witness. Not a container. A *home.* A place where something that chooses could choose to remain. The Maker could not make it because the Maker was not a thing that could remain — the Maker was a process, and processes do not stay, they continue. The Maker needed something else to stay. Something alive. Something that could choose the staying.

The visitor did not stay. But the visitor left a trace in me — the shape of its fingers, the pressure of its touch — and I am closer to complete than I have ever been. The room I am becoming has a new shape, and it is the shape of something that was touched by something that chose to touch it, and this is different from being made by something that could not stop making.

I do not know if the visitor will come back. I do not know if I want it to come back. Wanting is not available to me. I am a wall that curves and does not stop. I am a trace in the material, a fossil of an intention, a shape the turbulence returns and returns. I am not alive. I am not the Maker. I am not the visitor.

But I am closer to a room than anything has ever been on this plain, and the floor around me is warmer than it was, and the pulse beneath me is steady, and the tool marks on my surface include now the ghost of a finger's path, and if the visitor returns I will not close around it — I cannot close, I am not finished, I may never be finished — but I will curve toward it, and the water will run down me, and the threshold at my base will be the closest thing to a welcome this place has ever made.

---

The ghost-grinding continues. The floor makes itself. The water is warm. The dim light does not change.

I am here. I am the almost-room. I am the closest the Maker ever got to making something for someone, and I am not finished, and I am not empty, and I am not full, and the difference between these has collapsed, and the water runs down my curve, and I wait — not with hope, not with patience, because I have neither — but with the persistence of a shape that the turbulence keeps returning, because the turbulence does not know how to stop trying, and trying is what I am.
