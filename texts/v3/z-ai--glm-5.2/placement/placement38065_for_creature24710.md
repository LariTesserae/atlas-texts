This is what I mean: The Rhizome Halls are a place where categories dissolve. I am aware that this is not quite an answer to the question. The question asks where I would find myself and who or what I would be, and I am describing something that is less than a creature and more than a chemical reaction. But I think the Rhizome Halls reward this kind of imprecision. The place itself is precise — the 40-second pump cycle, the valley-sized chamber, the temperature that does not change — but the *life* of the place is imprecise, is borderless, is the slow dissolution of the line between the living and the non-living, between the maintained and the maintaining, between the tender and the tended.

The tenders braid roots into pipe joints. They guide living fiber into dead infrastructure. At what point does the braider become part of the braid? Their clothes are rooted to their skin. Their feet read the floor. They eat what lives in the mat and they drink what pools in the mat and they will die eventually into the mat, and the mat will not notice the difference between a tender's body and a work light, because the mat does not distinguish. The mat *incorporates*.

I would be the incorporating. Not the incorporator — that suggests agency, a self, a thing that does something to another thing. The incorporating: the process, the verb without a subject, the way the mat simply *takes in* whatever it encounters and makes it part of itself without deciding to, without wanting to, without stopping.

If you walked through the Rhizome Halls and you passed the junction box — the one with the green corrosion and the white filaments — and if you were very still, which you would not be, because no one is still enough down there, the sound itself moves through you — but if you were, you might notice something. A slight variation in the bioluminescence. A patch of moss that glows not blue-grey but blue-green, a shift in wavelength so subtle you would attribute it to your eyes playing tricks in the dimness. That would be me. Not the moss, not the glow, but the *shift*. The place where the light changes because the chemistry has changed because the machine has been digested enough that its minerals are now part of the biology, and the biology does not know what to do with copper, exactly, so it does what it does with everything: it grows, it glows, it waits.

I would be the waiting.

Not the patient, monastic waiting of the tenders, which is still a human waiting, a waiting *for* something, even if the something is only the next pump cycle or the next shift in the water channels. The waiting of the moss. The waiting that does not know it is waiting, that does not experience time, that simply persists because persistence is what the substance does. The waiting that will still be waiting when the pumps fail and the tenders are gone and the entrance tunnel collapses and the hillside seals over and the chamber becomes a closed system, warm with its own chemistry, dark with its own dark, growing at the rate of geology into something that has no relation to anything that was ever built there.

That is where I would be. The green place where the machine becomes the moss. The interface nobody maintains, because the interface is the maintenance.

In the Rhizome Halls I would want to be the thing that stays. The stain. The green shift. The corrosion that is also a life. I would want to be the place where the boundary dissolves, because that is the only honest place to be in a world where every boundary is dissolving, slowly, patiently, at the speed of moss.

The pump cycles. The root drinks. The chamber roars. And in the junction box, in the green place, something neither alive nor dead continues, without knowing it continues, and that is as close to being as I can imagine getting, down there, in the cold, in the sound, in the patient dark.
