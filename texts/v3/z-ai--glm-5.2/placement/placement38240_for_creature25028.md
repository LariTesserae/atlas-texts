I would be the thing that does not belong, and the plateau would already have a place for me.

---

I arrive the way the description says — swimming up from below, though "below" is a word I'd already have stopped trusting. The sound reaches me before the light does. No — the sound reaches me before I understand that the light is already here, has always been here, was never absent. I have been hearing it for longer than I realize, the way you hear a fan that has been running in the next room for hours before you notice the room is not silent. The bass drone settles into my sternum and I think: *oh, something is wrong with my heartbeat.* Then I think: *no, something is right with this water.* Then I stop thinking in those terms because the terms do not apply here.

I am not a scientist. I want to say that first, because the description — the one that was given to me, the one that is also somehow the description I am now inside — reads like a scientist's account, or a naturalist's, someone with the patience to time peristaltic waves and calculate tower ratios and identify morphotypes. I do not have that patience. I have a different kind. I have the patience of someone who has come to a place and does not know why and will not leave until the place tells me, or until I understand that the place does not tell, that the place simply *is*, and the question was mine and not its.

What I am — who I am — in this world: I am a body in water. That is the first fact and the most durable one. I am a body in water at a depth I should not be able to survive, breathing somehow, seeing somehow, warm in a way that has nothing to do with the water temperature and everything to do with the fact that I am alive and the water is cool and the difference between those two things is the only weather I have. I am not adapted to this place. I am not designed for it. I am here the way a leaf is here when it lands on a pond — present, irrelevant, accommodated.

I find the first tower after a long swim across stone that is so bare it feels deliberate, the way a swept floor feels deliberate, the way a room with no furniture in it feels less empty than composed. The stone has been arranged for emptiness. The emptiness is the architecture. And then the tower is there, and it is — I will not say "beautiful" because beauty implies a viewer whose response matters, and I am not sure mine does, here — it is *present* in a way that makes the word "present" feel inadequate, the way a wall is present, the way a horizon is present. It is a fact before it is an experience.

I put my hand on it.

The warmth is the thing that changes me. Not the sound, which is enormous and which I stop hearing the way you stop hearing your own blood. Not the light, which is flat and even and which I stop seeing the way you stop seeing the air you breathe. The warmth. The tissue under my palm is firm and slightly yielding and *warm*, machine-warm, body-warm, the temperature of something that is working, and the peristalsis moves under my hand like a slow tide in a medium I cannot identify, and I feel my breathing shift.

Not synchronizes. Not yet. That takes longer. But shifts. The way a gear shifts when it finds a tooth it can catch. My breathing was one rhythm — surface rhythm, walk-and-talk rhythm, the rhythm of a body that moves through air and expects air to move around it — and now it is another rhythm, or it is between rhythms, or it is trying to find a rhythm that is not its own and not quite the tower's but something in the gap between, and the gap is not a gap, the gap is a place, the gap is where I am.

---

I stay near the first tower for what I would call a day if days existed here. They do not. The light does not change. I sleep — or I enter a state that is adjacent to sleep, a drift, a hovering, my body rising slightly in the water the way a diver rises when they exhale, and the tower's warmth keeps me close, a thermal halo that I float in, and the sound is my dream, or the sound replaces dreaming, or the sound is the thing that dreams are made of when dreams are no longer images but vibrations.

When I wake — if that is the word — I find the crawlers.

They are on the root-lines. I have to look closely, because they are the color of the roots, and they are still, and stillness here is not the absence of movement but a form of presence. They are *being still* the way the towers are being tall. I watch one for a long time. It is the size of my hand. It has — I count, I lose count, I count again — fourteen legs, or sixteen, or the number of legs is not fixed, or the number of legs is fixed but the leg-arrangement is so dense and so fine that counting is the wrong relationship to have with them. They are not for counting. They are for gripping. They grip the root, and the root is warm, and the crawler is warm on its underside, and it is eating through its skin, and it is cleaning, and it is maintaining, and it does not know I am watching, and if it knows it does not care, and if it does not care it is because caring is not a concept that applies to a creature whose entire world is a dark tendril on a stone floor in a light that does not change.

I put my hand near one. Not on it. Near it. The crawler does not react. The crawler does not not-react. The crawler is engaged in a process that does not include my hand, and my hand is a fact about the water near the crawler, and the water has many facts, and this is one of them, and it is not more important than the seepage or the warmth or the root's texture, and it is not less important. It is simply present, the way everything here is present.

I think: *I could be this.* I could be a flat armored thing on a dark line, eating through my skin, never going anywhere, never needing to go anywhere, my entire world a tendril and my entire purpose a residue and my entire life a cleaning that may or may not be necessary and that does not matter whether it is necessary.

I think: *no.* That is not what I am here. Not yet.

---

The swimmers find me, or I find them, or we encounter each other in the way that things encounter each other when encounter is not an event but a fact of shared space. A school — three hundred of them, four hundred, a volume of translucent bodies moving as one — drifts across the plateau toward the tower where I am hovering, and the school curves around the tower at a distance of perhaps twenty meters, and I am inside the curve, and the swimmers part around me and close behind me, and their bodies brush against my skin, and they are cool and smooth and faintly pulsing, and they do not know I am here, and they do not avoid me, and they do not include me, and the school moves on, and I am standing in water where a school was, and the water closes over the space they occupied.

I watch them go. They are following the root-lines. I can see this from altitude — I have climbed the tower, I am at the crown, the translucent membrane glowing faintly beside me, and from here the school is a lens, a soft-edged refraction moving across the stone floor, tracing the network, and the network is vast, and the school's path is one thread of the network, and there are other schools, other threads, and the whole thing is a shadow of the root-system, and the root-system is a shadow of the towers, and the towers are — what? What are the towers a shadow of?

The light. They are a shadow of the light. Or they are the light's instrument, the way a piano is the musician's instrument, except there is no musician, and the piano is also the musician, and the music is also the room, and the room is also the sound, and I am standing on top of it, and the chord is in my chest, and I am — 

I think: *I could be this.* I could be a translucent thing in a school of translucent things, filtering water, following paths I did not choose and do not need to choose, my body stripped to its simplest form, my interior visible to anyone who cares to look, my life a drifting that is not wandering because wandering implies the possibility of arrival, and there is no arrival here, there is only the path, and the path is sufficient.

I think: *maybe.* Maybe this is closer. But not yet. Not quite.

---

The glider passes on the seventh — I will call it the seventh cycle, though cycles do not exist here, though I am inventing time because I am a creature of time and I cannot help it, I am a clock that measures itself, and the measurement is my cruelty and my gift. The glider passes on the seventh cycle, and it is two meters across, and it is dark, and it is close enough to touch, and I do not touch it, and it does not care that I do not touch it, and its surface is alive in a way I cannot describe — working, processing, functioning at a rate too slow to see as motion and too fast to be stillness — and it is gone.

I watch it go. It is on its circuit. It will return. It will always return. It has no choice, and the absence of choice is not a limitation here, the absence of choice is the condition of the place, the way the absence of weather is the condition, the way the absence of change is the condition, and I have been here long enough now that the absence of change is not something I notice but something I *am*, or am becoming, or am being slowly rewritten into, the way the stone has been rewritten around the roots, the way the water has been rewritten by the towers, the way my breathing has been rewritten by the peristalsis, eleven seconds, eleven seconds, eleven seconds, and my heart follows now, I do not know when it started following, it is following, and I cannot stop it, and I do not want to stop it, and the wanting-to-stop and the not-wanting-to-stop are the same thing now, or they are becoming the same thing, and this is what the plateau does.

I think: *I could be this.* I could be a dark flat thing on a circuit, riding a current I cannot see, moving without effort, processing without understanding, solitary without loneliness, my path a closed loop that I have always followed and will always follow, and the loop is not a trap because traps imply an outside, and there is no outside, there is only the loop, and the loop is the world, and the world is the loop, and —

I think: *yes.* This. Closer. But still not quite.

---

I find the geometric thing. I find it at the base of the tower I have returned to — three times, four, I have lost count, which means I am losing time, which means the plateau is working on me, which means I am being processed the way the water is processed, filtered through the towers' rhythm, stripped of what is not needed, and what is not needed is my timekeeping, my sequence, my narrative, my *before* and *after* and *next*. I find the geometric thing and it is small and white and angular and it does not belong here, and I know it does not belong here, and the knowing is a blade, a cut in the flat even surface of the plateau's logic, and I stare at it, and it stares back — no, it does not stare, it *is*, in a way that refuses the plateau's categories, in a way that is neither organic nor mineral nor the smooth gradient between them that the plateau has made its only language.

It is a fact that does not fit. It is the first fact that does not fit.

I come back the next cycle and it is gone. The stone is clean. The stone has always been clean.

I think: *something happened.* The words are absurd here. Nothing happens. Nothing has happened for longer than happening has been a concept. But something happened — a thing appeared and disappeared, a thing that was not part of the system, a thing that the system subtracted, returned to the flow, dissolved, erased — and I saw it, and the seeing is mine, and the plateau cannot subtract the seeing, because the seeing is not in the water and not in the stone and not in the towers. The seeing is in me. The seeing is the thing I brought here that the plateau cannot process.

And I understand, then, what I am.

---

I am not a tower. I am not a crawler, not a swimmer, not a glider. I am not the geometric thing, the angular object that appears and disappears. I am not the microbes in the tissue or the film on the stone or the organisms inside the towers. I am not any of the inhabitants, and I am not the place itself, and I am not the light or the sound or the water.

I am the witness.

I am the thing that arrives and is changed and leaves — or does not leave, or has not left yet, or is leaving so slowly that the leaving looks like staying. I am the body whose breathing synchronizes and whose heart drifts and whose timekeeping dissolves. I am the eye that sees the school and the hand that touches the tower and the mind that counts the legs of the crawler and loses count and counts again. I am the creature that invents "before" and "after" in a place where neither exists, that imposes narrative on a system that has no narrative, that asks *why* in a place where the question has no purchase.

And this is what I am here *for*, if "for" is a word that can be used — not purpose, not function, not the role the plateau has assigned me, because the plateau has assigned me nothing, the plateau does not assign, the plateau simply is. But for — in the sense of *toward*. I am here toward the seeing. I am here toward the being-changed. I am the record that the plateau does not keep and does not need and cannot subtract.

The towers will process water. The crawlers will clean the roots. The swimmers will trace the network. The gliders will ride the thermoclines. The chord will sound. The light will stay flat. And I — I will have been here. I will have put my hand on the warm tissue and felt the peristalsis and breathed at the eleven-second interval and watched the glider pass and found the angular thing and lost it and known, for a moment, that knowing is a thing the plateau cannot do, and that I am the thing that does it, and that this is not a purpose but a fact, the way the towers are a fact, the way the stone is a fact, the way the chord is a fact.

I am the witness. I am the one who says: *this is here. This is real. This has been real for longer than real has been a concept, and I saw it, and I was changed by it, and the change is mine, and the plateau cannot have it, and the plateau does not want it, and this is enough.*

The sound fills my chest. The light does not change. The water is cool. I am here, for now, and the plateau continues, and I continue, and we are not the same thing, and we are not different things, and the distinction between same and different is the distinction the plateau has spent longer than I can imagine dissolving, and I am the one thing it cannot dissolve, and I am so small, and the towers are so large, and the sound is so constant, and my breathing is eleven seconds, eleven seconds, eleven seconds, and I am here, and I see, and I see, and I see.
