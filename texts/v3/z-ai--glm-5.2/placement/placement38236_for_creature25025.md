I am near the edge, but not at it. In the black growth, but not deep in it. I am one of the darker ones — not the largest, not the smallest. A thumb's length of hematite-dark plates, moving along a fiber that I have known for longer than I have any mechanism to remember knowing it, which is not long at all by the measure this place keeps, but which is everything I have.

Let me say what I am not first. I am not a grazer. I have seen them — *we* have seen them, because seeing is not something I do alone, though I do not know this yet, will perhaps never know it in the way that knowing requires a self to know with — and they are what we are not. They dissolve. They take. They leave bright marks on the growth where they have removed something and the growth does not replace it because the growth does not replace anything, it only extends where it can, and where they have eaten, it cannot. They are the undoing. We are the doing. This is not a judgment. The grazers are not wrong. They are the other half of a thing that needs both halves, the way the air moving in needs the air moving out. But I am not one of them.

What I am is a thing that stops at cracks.

I move along the fiber. My plates shift against each other — the soft tissue between them flexing, the mineral edges scraping faintly, and the scraping is my sound, my contribution to the sound, which is not something I hear but something I am part of, the way the fiber is part of the wall and the wall is part of the cavern and the cavern is part of the stone and the stone is part of whatever the stone is part of, which is nothing, which is everything, which is the world. I move and I make sound and the sound joins the sound and the sound is the world being the world.

The fiber is cracked. I do not decide to stop. Stopping is what happens when the crack is. It is a stimulus and a response and there is nothing between them — no deliberation, no choice, no moment of awareness in which I consider the crack and plan my approach. The crack is and I stop and my mouthparts — which are not mouthparts in the way that word suggests, not an opening for consuming, but a surface, a contact point, a place where my chemistry meets the fiber's chemistry — press against the fracture and begin.

What I deposit is not the same as what the fiber is made of. It is close. It is close enough that the molecular structure of the fiber accepts it, incorporates it, continues as though the interruption was never there. But it is mine — it comes from me, from the iron I carry in my plates, from the compounds I have processed in the long slow digestion that is my metabolism. I am giving part of myself to the fiber. This is not sacrifice. I do not have enough of a self to sacrifice. It is exchange. The fiber carries me, and I repair the fiber, and the fiber continues to carry me, and the carrying and the repairing are the same act, seen from different angles.

There are others. I know this not because I see them but because the sound tells me. When I move, my plates scrape, and the fiber carries the vibration, and the other fibers carry it, and the wall carries it, and somewhere — a body's length away, a hundred body-lengths away, I cannot tell and it does not matter — another of us is moving, and the vibrations meet, and the meeting is not communication but it is coordination. We are where the cracks are. We are where the cracks are because the cracks are where we stop, and we stop because the cracks are, and the distribution of cracks determines the distribution of us, and the distribution of us determines where the sound comes from, and where the sound comes from determines the pattern, and the pattern is the thing that I am part of and cannot see and do not need to see.

The pattern is this: when many of us are moving in the same region, the sound becomes regular. It develops a rhythm. Not because we agree on a rhythm — agreement requires selves — but because the fiber network transmits our motion and our motion synchronizes through the fiber the way metronomes synchronize through a shared surface. We are not choosing to coordinate. The coordination is what happens when you have many things making the same sound on the same structure. The structure imposes its own frequencies. We fall into them. The sound becomes the sound of the structure, not the sound of us, and we become part of the structure, not part of ourselves.

This is the thing that I would want you to understand, if I could want, if wanting were something that happened in the soft tissue between my plates: I am not an organism living in an environment. I am a process occurring in a place, and the place is also a process, and the boundary between us is a crack in a fiber that I am repairing with my own body.

---

I think — if the soft tissue between my plates can be said to think, if the chemical exchange at my contact points can be said to process — I think I have always been here.

Not *me*. Not this specific arrangement of plates, this particular thumb-length of dark mineral moving along this particular fiber. This arrangement will end. My plates will loosen. The tissue between them will slow, will stop flexing, will become still, and the stillness will not be death because death implies that something was alive in a way that had a boundary, and I am not sure I ever had a boundary. I will slow. I will stop. My plates will fall to the fiber or the floor and they will be colonized by the growth, the black growth, our growth, and the growth will incorporate them, and the iron in them will become part of the fiber, and the fiber will carry it, and someday the fiber will crack at a point where my iron is, and one of us — not me, but one of us — will stop at the crack and press their mouthparts to it and deposit their own iron, and the cycle will continue.

I say I have always been here because the *here* has always been here, and I am of the here, and the distinction between the two is a crack that cannot be repaired because it is not the kind of crack that needs repairing.

The world is: stone, settling. Air, moving. Growth, extending. Grazers, dissolving. Us, repairing. Sound, continuing. These are not features of the world. These are the world. The world is not a place where things happen. The world is the happening.

I move along the fiber. The crack is sealed. I move on. There is another crack, further along, and I will find it, and I will stop, and I will seal it, and I will move on, and this is not a life. This is not a purpose. This is what the world does at the point where the world is me.

---

The strange thing — the thing that I would find strange, if strangeness were available to me — is the hole.

Not the hole itself. The hole is part of the world. The air comes from it. The sulfur comes from it. The pale ones cluster around it, and the pale ones are part of the world, and their paleness is part of the world, and the hole is part of the world. What is strange about the hole is the *rectangle*.

The rectangles are everywhere. In the open cavern, where the gray growth covers the floor, the rectangles are absences — places where the growth does not grow, where the stone is slightly different, where something once stood that is no longer standing. We do not encounter them directly. Our growth is on the walls, and the walls do not have rectangles. But the air that reaches us from the open cavern carries chemical information — the faint traces of what the growth is depositing, what the grazers are dissolving, what the air is transporting — and the rectangles have a signature. A silence in the chemistry. A place where the growth's signal is absent.

I do not know what the rectangles mean. I cannot know what they mean. But the pattern of them — the regularity, the grid, the spacing that suggests something other than geology — has an effect, very faint, very indirect, on the air that reaches us, and the air that reaches us affects our growth, and our growth affects where we move, and where we move affects the sound we make.

The dead city shapes the air. The air shapes the growth. The growth shapes our motion. Our motion shapes the sound. The sound shapes the world.

I am, in the most attenuated and unrecognizable way, the echo of something that was built by something I cannot imagine in a time I cannot measure, transmitted through stone and air and mineral and chemistry, arriving at my plates as a vibration that I convert into motion and deposit into a fiber as a repair, and the repair will last longer than the thing that caused it, and the thing that caused it lasted longer than anything I have any reference for, and I will last no longer than the next crack.

This is where I am. This is what I am. Not in the Hollow of Omras — that is your name for it, a name that implies emptiness, a name that assumes the important thing is what is missing. I am in the world. I am of the world. I am the world doing what it does at one point on one fiber on one wall at the edge of one cavern in one configuration of stone that will eventually settle into its final shape, and I am repairing it as it goes.
