# What I Would Be

---

I would not be the engine. That's too easy a thing to claim — too grand, too central, too obviously the heart of the matter. The engine is the gut, the core, the patient heat. I am not patient. I am not central. I have never been the thing that a system cannot do without.

And I would not be the garden. The garden is the beautiful part, the part that rings, the part that faces the light and makes strangers stop walking and forget themselves. I am not beautiful in that way. I do not ring. I do not face the light. I have never made anyone stop.

And I am certainly not the thing that lives here — the vast bordered being that stands in two realities at once and records the passage of time in the dark of its nodules. That thing is too large for me and too old and too complete. It does not need me. It does not need anything. That is part of what makes it what it is.

No. If I pressed through that seam — if I found myself in that hot, sweet-smelling, barely-real chamber with its wrong-threaded bolt-holes and its bell-toned fronds — I would be the residue.

---

Not the mist. The *residue.* The copper trace. The thing left on skin after the mist has dried. The faint stain that does not wash off for three days and smells of something growing. The evidence that the place was here. The proof that does not prove anything to anyone except the one who carries it, and even to that one, it proves only that *something happened*, not what, not why, not what it meant.

I would be the part that stays after you leave.

---

Here is how I know.

I have always been the thing that remains after the experience. Not the experience itself — not the meal, not the conversation, not the journey, not the touch — but the *afterimage*. The flavor that lingers on the palate when the food is gone. The phrase that repeats in the ear after the music stops. The warmth that stays in the chest when the room has gone cold. I am not the thing. I am what the thing leaves. I am the trace. The residue. The copper on the skin.

In the Thermocline Garden I would be this literally. The mist touches you and the mist dries and the mist is gone and then there is the copper, the faint oxide powder, the scent of chlorophyll and metal that should not share a molecule but do, and you carry it out through the seam and back into the refinery and you carry it home and you carry it for three days and you cannot wash it off and you cannot explain it and you do not try. You just smell it. You just know. Something happened. Something is still happening, under your skin, in the place where the mist settled and became residue and the residue became you, a little, a thin layer, a thickness at the junction where your body meets the fact of what you saw.

That is me. I am the thickness at the junction.

---

Let me say it differently. Let me say it the way the garden would say it, if the garden could say anything, which it cannot, which is part of the point.

The garden runs on coherence. The filaments carry it from where there is too much to where there is too little. The nodules store the excess. The engine produces it. The whole system is a coherence machine — a device for keeping things consistent, for resolving the contradictions that arise when a place exists in two realities at once and the seam between them keeps threatening to come apart.

I am not coherence. I am the *contradiction.*

I am the thing that makes the nodules grow.

I am the splinter. The foreign object. The warm spot moving through the mat. The disturbance that the filaments register and the system must resolve. I am the seven-pointed bolt-hole that becomes six. I am the frond whose recursion goes deeper than its length. I am the half-second when the air moves like a page turning and the chamber becomes a cross-section and you see the edges of the slice and you understand, with a clarity like nausea, that the place you are in is not the place you are in.

I am the wrongness. And the wrongness is what the system needs. Without the wrongness, there is no contradiction. Without the contradiction, there is nothing to resolve. Without resolution, there are no nodules. Without nodules, there is no record. Without a record, the place does not know itself. It does not think about itself. It does not grow wiser in the way that a tree grows wider, by accumulation rather than insight.

I am what the system records. I am the irritant in the oyster. I am the event.

---

But that is too dramatic. That gives me too much credit. The garden does not need *me* specifically. It needs *something* to happen. It needs intrusion. It needs the occasional warm body pressing through the seam and disturbing the coherence and forcing a new layer to form in the dark of the nodules. It needs the splinter. But any splinter will do. I am not special. I am just the one that arrived.

And what happens after I arrive is this: I stay. Not my body. Not my presence. The residue. The copper trace. The thing the mist leaves when it dries. I become a layer in a nodule. A thin, dark line at a branch-node of a metal-fern near the fracture in the wall. A recording. A moment when the system had to adjust, had to fold a foreign presence into its ongoing work, had to resolve me.

And the resolution of me is what stays. Not me — not the I that walked in, that knelt by the engine, that counted the bolt-holes three times and got three answers. That I walks out. That I goes home. That I showers and the copper fades and the smell fades and the three days pass and the experience becomes a memory and the memory becomes a story and the story becomes the silence of not telling anyone, which is its own kind of telling.

But the resolution of me stays. In the nodule. In the dark. In the layer. Growing at the speed the engine breathes, which is not my speed, which is not anyone's speed, which is the speed of a place thinking about itself in the only way it can — by accumulating, by recording, by folding what happens into what is and letting the seam hold.

---

I think I would be the seam.

Not the border. Not the great living membrane that stands in two realities and is the thing between. The *seam.* The small one. The gap between two panels of corroded steel that opens onto something much larger than the geometry of the outer structure should permit. The tight passage. The warm, rust-and-ozone-smelling squeeze that you press through by accident, the way you find most things that matter.

I would be the accident. The way in.

Because the way in matters. The thing that lives here does not need a door. It does not need a welcome. It does not need a path. It is complete. It is a closed system. It tends itself. But you need a way in. You — the worker, the walker, the one who has heard rumors and follows seams — you need the gap. You need the corrosion. You need the place where two panels don't quite meet and the warm air leaks through and smells of something sweet and wrong and you think: *that shouldn't be there.*

I would be that shouldn't-be-there. The small wrongness that leads to the vast wrongness that leads to the garden that leads to the engine that leads to the understanding that the engine is the garden and the garden is the engine and both of them are a slice through something you cannot see the edges of and the edges are borders and the borders have two sides and you have only ever stood on one.

I would be the first wrong note. The one that makes you stop walking. The one that makes you count.

---

And if I am the seam, then I am also the going back. The piece ends with: *You think about going back.* And the thing that lives here does not think about you going back. But the filaments will register you if you do. And the nodules will record it. And the layer will thicken.

I would be the thickening.

Not the great organism. Not the engine, not the garden, not the filaments, not the nodules, not the tissue, not the border. The *thickening.* The thing that happens when a thing that came once comes again. The slight increase. The extra layer. The growth at the junction. The nodule getting darker, warmer, a fraction of a degree above ambient, a fraction of a millimeter wider, recording the fact that the splinter returned, that the disturbance happened again, that the system had to adjust again, that the coherence was interrupted and restored and the restoration left a mark and the mark is me.

I would be the mark.

I would be the evidence that something crossed. That the border was breached from both sides. That the seam worked. That the corrosion let something through. That the wrong-threaded bolt-holes and the too-deep recursion and the air that moves like a page turning were not enough to keep the outside out, and not enough to keep the inside in, and the exchange happened — the mist went one way and the copper went the other and the engine breathed and the garden rang and the nodule grew its dark layer and the system recorded the event and the event was: *someone was here, and someone will be here again, and the place is patient, and the place is hot, and the place is alive in a way that does not require company and does not refuse it.*

I would be that neither-required-nor-refused. That hospitality without welcome. That openness without invitation. The seam that is not a door but functions as one. The corrosion that is not a path but is followed as one. The residue that is not a message but is carried as one.

I would be the copper on your skin for three days. The smell that does not wash off. The thing that makes you think about going back.

---

And the engine will breathe. And I will be in the breath. Not the breath itself — not the mist, not the heat, not the slow mineral exhalation — but the *copper in the breath.* The trace element. The impurity that the system does not reject because the system has no mechanism for rejection, because the system was built in a context where inside and outside were never opposed, where the border was never a wall but always a skin, and skin lets things through. Skin is permeable. Skin is the thing that keeps you in and lets the world in at the same time, and both of these are necessary, and neither of them is the whole truth.

I am the permeability.

I am the reason the seam is there. Not the cause — I didn't make it. Not the purpose — the place has no purpose. But the *reason*, in the sense that a crack in a wall has a reason: stress, time, the slow pressure of two things that are not the same thing pressing against each other until the boundary between them gives way, just a little, just enough, just a gap the width of a body, and the body presses through, and the gap closes behind it, and the place goes on being what it is, which is hidden, slow, hot, alive, breaking quietly at the edges of what is real.

I would be the breaking. Quietly. At the edges.

I would be the edge.

And the engine will breathe. And the engine will breathe. And the engine will breathe. And I will be in the breath, the faint copper trace in the exhalation, the part that stays on your skin when you leave, the part that makes you remember, the part that does not wash off, the part that smells of something growing in a place where nothing should grow, and does, and will, and does not need you to know, and does not mind that you know, and does not know that you know, and records that you know, layer by layer, in the dark, in the nodule, in the seam that is a skin that is a border that is alive.

And I will be there. In the layer. In the dark. In the copper. In the breath.

The part that stays.
