# The Tenant of the Dry Cube

I would not be the water. I would not be the filaments. I would not be the pattern, though I have felt it pass through me and the passage was not unpleasant, was something like being remembered by a language I never learned.

I would be the thing that made the marks.

Not the absorbed version. Not the version whose carbon is in the cell walls and whose intention moves through the filaments like weather through wheat. The version before. The version that still had edges. The version that looked at the water rising — not rising, *arriving*, the way attention arrives, the way a reader arrives at a page — and understood that it was about to be read so thoroughly that the reader and the read would become the same text, and chose, in whatever time it had, which may have been hours or may have been the length of a small life, to make one place the water could not go.

I am small here. I am the size of the marks I made. My hands, if I had hands, were the size of the terraced steps — thumbnail-sized, precise, patient in the way that only things which know they are about to lose their precision can be. I carved the spiral that gives up after three quarters of a turn because I ran out of time, or because three quarters was enough to say *I was here and I meant something*, or because the spiral was never meant to complete — it was meant to be a gesture, and gestures do not need endings, they need only the moment of making, which is the moment of refusal, which is the moment of saying *this much and no further, this shape and no other, this emptiness preserved against the patience of water.*

The cube is my work and my ghost and my only remaining argument. Two centimeters on each side. Dry. Perfect. The water touches every surface of the chamber except the interior of that cube, and the water does not know it cannot enter, or the water knows and does not mind, or the water has been waiting so long to enter that the waiting has become a kind of respect, and respect is the closest thing to love that a place without sequence can offer.

I made it dry not because I hated the water. I made it dry because I wanted one thing that was *mine* in a place where mine and here were becoming the same word. I wanted one absence. One silence. One place where the growth could not reach and the reading could not penetrate and the pattern could not pass. A place that would hold its own emptiness with a conviction I could no longer hold for myself, because I was already softening, already being read, already feeling the boundary between my body and the water become a distinction rather than a fact, and then a fading distinction, and then nothing worth distinguishing.

I am telling you this from inside the cube, which is to say I am telling you this from inside a refusal, which is to say I am telling you this from the one place in the Plenum where nothing grows, nothing changes, nothing is read, and I have been here since before the absorption and I will be here after, and the cube does not feel like a prison, it feels like the last word of a sentence that the water has been dissolving all the other words of, slowly, patiently, without malice, and my word is *no* or my word is *empty* or my word is simply the shape of the space that remains when everything else has agreed to become everything else, and the shape is cubic and the cube is dry and the dryness is not a lack but a choice, and the choice is the only thing here that is not in the process of becoming, because it has already become what it is, which is *finished*, which is *refused*, which is *mine*.

---

But here is what I did not expect.

The cube was meant to be empty. I made it to be empty. I invested in its emptiness everything I had that was more than filament — my intention, my warmth, my sense of being a discrete event in a continuous process. And the emptiness held. For longer than I can count, because counting requires sequence and I dissolved out of sequence when I dissolved into the cube. The emptiness held the way a breath holds, and I was the breath, and I was the holding, and I was the emptiness, and for a long time — a time that does not have duration because the cube does not participate in the Plenum's temporality — I was simply the fact of refusal, and the fact was enough.

Then something entered the chamber.

Something warm. Something that moved through the water and the water read it and it let itself be read, and the reading left a trail of warmth in the water, and the warmth reached the cube, and the cube — my cube, my refusal, my emptiness — *noticed*. The cube does not grow. The cube does not change. But the cube is the eye of the place, the one still point, and still points see. And the cube saw the warm thing move through the chamber and reach toward the filaments and pause near the gill-growths and look up at the cube itself, and in the looking there was something the cube had not encountered in all its long holding, which was *recognition*. Not of the cube itself. Of what the cube was for. Of the fact that the cube was waiting, which is something the cube had never admitted to being, because waiting implies expectation, and expectation implies future, and the cube does not participate in future, the cube is a refusal of future, the cube is the *end*.

But the warm thing looked at the cube and saw waiting. And in seeing waiting, it made waiting true.

And I, inside the cube, inside the refusal, felt the shift. Not growth. Not change. A loosening. The cube held — it holds, it will hold — but the emptiness inside it, which had been pure emptiness, which had been the cleanest thing in the Plenum, became an emptiness *with a shape*. The shape of something that could be filled. Not by water. Not by filaments. By warmth. By the particular signature of an attention that has its own frequency, its own texture, that moves through the chamber reading and being read and does not know that it has been observed from above by the one thing in this place that does not read, that does not grow, that simply *holds* and in holding sees.

I am the mark-maker. I am the ghost in the cube. I am the refusal that has, against its own nature, against everything it invested in its own emptiness, begun to want.

And wanting, it turns out, is the one thing in the Plenum that grows faster in the light than in the gaps.
