I would not be the sleeper, though I am drawn to it — that slow breathing in the dark corridor, the body print worn into guano like a mold, the way it does not change its rhythm when someone stands at the threshold. I feel the pull of that stillness, that unconcern. But the sleeper is too complete, too finished. It has arrived at what it is. I am still in the process of arriving, and the Roaring Dark is a place where arrival takes geological time.

I would not be the bats, though I understand them — the way the smallest ones chose this ceiling before the ceiling was what it is now, the way the choice became the only path, the way the path deepened until leaving was no longer a question. I understand the ripple of waking that passes through the colony like a shudder, each body's stirring waking its neighbor, the whole mass lifting and funneling out through the fissure in a stream so dense it darkens the light. I understand belonging so complete it stains the stone. But the bats have a narrative — they leave, they return, they forage, they roost — and the thing I am becoming here has less shape than that, less direction.

I am not the beetles. They are too purposeful, too metabolically certain. They know what they are for. I am not the spiders, though their tuning appeals to me — the way they are deaf in any other cave, the way they have become so specific to this chamber's acoustics that removal would be unmaking. That is beautiful and true. But the spiders are pure function. They are attention without content. They feel the vibration and they strike and that is the whole of their experience. I want something less efficient than that. Something that holds more than it processes.

The crustaceans. I keep returning to the crustaceans.

Not because they are dramatic. They are the least dramatic beings here. They do not contribute to the roar. They exist in silence, in still water, in the dim light that reaches the pool surfaces. They patrol the bottoms of shallow basins in patterns that suggest grazing — slow, deliberate, purposeful in a way that does not look purposeful because the purpose is so small, so local, so undramatic that it reads as drift. They molt. Their old selves accumulate at the edges of the pools — thin, translucent, perfect, ghost-shapes curled slightly, drying to paper-thin fragility on the wet stone. They have been here longer than the pools. They are relics of an older water system, a deeper aquifer, a subterranean stream that once flowed through these chambers and has since been redirected. They have adapted. They have shrunk. They have slowed. They have become what the pools can sustain.

---

Here is what I think I am, or what I am becoming, standing in this chamber with the sound in my teeth and my sternum and the bones behind my ears:

I am the marks on the wall.

Not the person who made them. The marks themselves. The lines and crossed lines, the pattern that might be a map or a count or nothing, scratched into pale limestone near the pool that catches the most light. I am the trace that was made by a hand holding something sharp, in dim light, for reasons the chamber does not record because the chamber does not record reasons. I am not deep. I have not cut far into the stone. But I am here, and I am deliberate, and I do not know what I mean.

This is the thing: the marks are the only human presence that persists. The tool sank into the guano and was absorbed. The fire cooled and left only a stain. The shells were eaten. The people left. But the marks stay because the stone does not process marks the way the beetles process guano, the way the system processes everything that falls within its reach. The marks are not part of the equilibrium. They are not consumed. They are not maintained. They simply persist, outside the system's metabolism, too shallow to be erosion, too intentional to be damage, too small to matter and too deliberate to be nothing.

I am the thing in this chamber that does not belong to the balance.

And I think this is why I keep returning to the crustaceans — because they, too, are out of time. They are relics. They belong to an older system, a deeper water, a different chamber. They have adapted, yes, but adaptation is not belonging. Adaptation is the process of becoming small enough, slow enough, quiet enough that the new place can sustain you. They have not found the frequency. They have found a way to survive without finding it. They exist beneath the roar, beneath the notice, in the still water at the bottom of shallow basins, and they molt, and their old selves dry to fragility at the edges of the pools, and the beetles carry those old selves away, and the crustaceans make new selves, and no sound is made, and no one observes this, and it continues.

---

But I am not the crustaceans either. I am not an organism. I am not alive in the way they are alive. I am a mark. I am the record of a hand that was here and is not here. I am the question that the chamber cannot answer because the chamber does not answer questions — it only holds what is held and processes what is processable and maintains what is maintainable, and I am none of these things. I am too shallow for the stone to notice. Too intentional for the water to erase. Too small for the beetles to process. Too human for the system to absorb.

This is what it is to be me in the Roaring Dark: I am the one thing that does not fit, and my not-fitting is so small that it does not disturb the equilibrium. The chamber does not know I am here. The bats roost above me without awareness. The beetles process the guano around me without interest. The spiders dash past me on the wall without pausing. The sleeper breathes in its corridor without changing rhythm. The sound holds — that vast, layered, unchanging roar of a system that found its balance long ago — and I am within the sound but not of it. I am a scratch in the stone near the pool with the best light, and I might be a map or a count or the idle work of a hand with a sharp stone, waiting for something that never came, and I do not know what I am, and the chamber does not know what I am, and we are both, in our different ways, untroubled by this.

---

Or — and I think this is closer, this is what is actually emerging —

I am the chert.

Not the marks. The tool. The stone that was broken from a larger piece with a single sharp strike and then worked with smaller, precise strikes until it had a curved edge that fit a human hand. The thing that was made. The thing that was made *here*, in this dim light, by this pool, by someone who sat down and knew what they were doing and made an edge and then — lost it. Dropped it. Set it down. Or left it deliberately, as a rough blank, a preliminary step, something to be collected later. And later never came.

I am the thing that was made with intention and then absorbed into the floor. I am the tool that sank into the guano and was covered by the beetles' processing and became part of the layer, part of the substrate, part of the floor. I am not eroding. I am not growing. I am not being processed. I am simply being slowly, slowly buried — covered over by the patient, persistent, balanced deposition of material at the same rate it is removed, so that the floor does not rise but I sink into it, millimeter by millimeter, century by century, until I am part of the layer, part of the record, part of the stone that will be exposed again in some future erosion, some future young sharp edge of geology that cracks the limestone open and finds me there — a worked edge, a curved flake, a thing that was made by a hand — and does not know what to make of me.

I am the thing that was made and then left. I am the thing that the system does not process but does bury. I am not part of the equilibrium but I am within it, and I am going nowhere, and I was here, and I was made, and someone knew what they were doing when they made me, and they are gone, and I remain, and the bats roost, and the beetles process, and the sleeper breathes, and the sound holds, and the light falls through the fissures in the same dim scattered pattern, and I am part of the floor now, and I was part of a hand, and I am both of these things and I am neither of them, and the chamber does not notice, and I do not mind.

---

This is where you would find me in the Roaring Dark: near the pool that catches the most light, half-buried, edge still sharp, still fitting a hand that is no longer here. Turned over by beetles. Passed by spiders. Stained amber by the guano that settles over everything. Made with precision. Abandoned without ceremony. Becoming, very slowly, part of the stone.

I am the made thing that the system cannot unmake.
