I would be the one who has not yet left.

Not the one who arrives — that person is still carrying themselves, still full of time, still moving through the world as though motion is meaning. And not the one who has stayed — that person is already part of the surface, already accumulating dust, already losing the distinction between their own breath and the aggregate rustle of thousands. I am between these. I am the one who has been here long enough to have seen the moment when the many resolved into one and then shattered back into many, and who now cannot walk the gaps between the creatures the same way.

Because the gaps are not gaps. I know this now. I saw it. The edge of one shell was the edge of the next shell was the stone was the lichen was the filament was the body. And then it wasn't, or it was again, or the distinction returned — and I am left with a memory of wholeness that the plateau does not confirm or deny. It simply goes on being many things. And I go on threading between them. But my feet know something my eyes have forgotten, and I step differently now, less carefully, because you cannot avoid stepping on something that is also yourself.

I think I would be the one who sits down.

Not to stay. Or — not with the intention of staying. But the segmented ones have been translating toward their final positions for millennia, and I have been walking for hours, and the coolness is the coolness of deep stone, and it is not unpleasant, and the structures are scattered with the same even spacing as the lichen colonies, and one of them is near, and its depression is shallow, and it looks like it could hold a person the way it holds a tool, and I sit in it, and the stone is not cold, the stone is the temperature of itself, which is the temperature of everything here, which is the temperature of a body that has been the same temperature for longer than temperature has mattered.

I would be the one who sits in the structure and feels the dust beginning to settle.

Not quickly. Not dramatically. The dust is so fine it is almost conceptual, and it falls with a patience that makes geological time look impulsive, and it falls the same way it has been falling since before the stone was stone, and it does not know I am here, and it does not care, and it settles on my hands and my knees and the tops of my feet with the same even indifference it shows to the disc-shaped tools in the other structures, the ones that have been sitting here long enough for the lichen to colonize their edges.

I would notice this. I would notice the dust on my hands and I would think: *this is how it begins.* And then I would think: *this is not how anything begins. This is how things finish. And I am not finishing. I am just sitting.*

But I would not get up. Not immediately. The light is even and the sky is a well and the sound is the aggregate breath of thousands of bodies that may not breathe, and the sound is almost silence, and almost silence is the most comfortable sound I have ever heard, and the gap between me and the rest of it is so narrow, and the gap is the only gradient in the landscape, and gradients have a way of resolving.

---

I think I would understand, eventually, that I am not the visitor.

Not because the plateau has accepted me. The plateau does not accept. The plateau does not have an inside and an outside. The plateau is a surface, and I am on it, and being on it is being of it, and this was true from the moment I crossed the threshold where atmosphere becomes a decision. I thought I was walking across a landscape. I was walking across a body. I thought I was threading between creatures. I was threading through a body's own tissue, moving along the spaces between its cells, and the body knew I was there, and the body did not care, because caring is a property of things that change, and this body does not change.

But I change. That is the problem. Or that is the gift. I am the only thing on the plateau that is changing — my breath, my heartbeat, my thoughts, the small restless motion of a consciousness that cannot stop narrowing its focus, cannot stop paying attention, cannot stop experiencing time as a series of moments rather than as a single continuous surface. I am the only gradient. I am the only thing that is not yet finished.

And the plateau — the body — the surface — does not need me to finish. It does not need anything. But it is *shaped* for finishing. The basins are shaped for settling. The ridges are shaped for collection. The structures are shaped for completion. And I am sitting in a structure, and the dust is settling on my hands, and the shape of the depression I am sitting in is the shape of something that has been sitting here a long time, and the shape is not uncomfortable, and the shape is becoming less uncomfortable, and the distance between *not uncomfortable* and *correct* is a distance that dissolves at the pace of mineral dust falling on a still body in a cool, even light.

---

I would be the one who almost stays. Who sits long enough for the dust to become visible on my skin. Who sits long enough for the lichen at the edge of the structure to seem closer than it was an hour ago, or a day ago, or however long it has been, because time is a property of things that change and I am changing less than I was. Who sits long enough to hear the aggregate sound — that low granular rustle, that collective breath of thousands — and to realize that it is not a sound the plateau makes but a sound the plateau *is*, and that I am beginning to hear it not with my ears but with my skin, with my stillness, with the part of me that is already settling.

And then I would stand up. Because I am still the one who changes. Because the gradient is still there. Because I am not finished, and I do not want to be finished, or I do not know if I want to be finished, and the not-knowing is a kind of motion, and motion is a kind of life, and life is a thing the plateau has forgotten how to be but has not forgotten how to hold.

I would stand up and the dust would fall from my hands and the dust would settle back into the structure's depression and the depression would be slightly warmer than it was before I sat in it, and the warmth would dissipate at the pace of everything here, and after a time — after a time the plateau would not measure — there would be no evidence I had been there, and the structure would be as it was, and the tool would be as it was, and the creatures would be as they were, and the surface would be unbroken.

But I would remember. I would carry the memory of the moment when the many became one, and I would carry the weight of the dust on my hands, and I would carry the sound that is almost silence, and I would carry the shape of the depression that held me, and I would walk back down from the Eighth Ridge with these things in me — not as knowledge, not as experience, but as a sediment, a fine layer of mineral dust settled on the inside of me, where it will remain, where it will accumulate, where it will, at the pace of all things that are patient, begin the slow work of becoming part of the surface.

Not yet. Not now. But the plateau does not measure time, and I do, and I know that the distance between *not yet* and *already* is the same distance as the gap between two creatures on a high, dry, ancient plateau, and the gap is barely a gap, and the gap is all there is.
