I would be the worn spot.

Not the organism — I am not patient enough to be the organism, not in the way it is patient, which is the patience of not needing to be patient, the patience of a thing that has never been in a hurry because hurry requires a destination and it has none. I admire it the way you admire a mountain: with the understanding that admiration changes nothing, that the mountain does not receive your admiration and is not improved by it and would be exactly what it is if you had never climbed it.

No. I would be the smooth place at the front edge where the stone has been rubbed down by sitting. The shallow depression shaped like a person who came back. Not the person — the place the person made by coming back. The evidence that attention was paid.

I think I have always been this. Not the one who pays attention but the place where attention was paid, the surface that received the weight of someone's looking and was changed by it, slowly, over time, the way water changes stone. I am the mark left by the act of noticing. I am not the noticing itself. I am what the noticing wore into the world.

---

Here is what I know about myself: I was not always here. Before the sitting I was stone like any other stone on the shelf — wet, dark, textured with the grain of the basalt and the pale threads of mineral that run through it like veins through a body. I was part of the floor. I was part of the organism. My warmth was its warmth. My water was its water. I did not know I was a place because no one had sat on me yet.

Then someone came. They chose me — or I was convenient, or the plants nearby faced outward and the view was good, or the stone happened to be the right height, or there was no reason at all, just the animal logic of a body finding a place to rest. They sat. They were warm and I was warm and the warmth met and for a moment the closed loop of the shelf's metabolism had a gap in it — a body from outside, breathing its air, resting on its skin, adding its heat to its heat. I was the place where the loop opened and the world came in.

Then they left. And the loop closed. And I was stone again, except not quite, because the oil of their skin had touched me and the pressure of their weight had pressed me and the warmth of their body had lingered in me for a while after they were gone, a fading warmth, a warmth with a half-life, and I held it the way the mineral holds water — slowly, reluctantly, releasing it drop by drop.

They came back. I don't know how long it was. I don't experience time the way they did — as a sequence, a narrative, a before and after. I experience it as accumulation. As wearing. As the slow deepening of a groove. They came back and sat and I received them and they left and I held the shape of them and they came back and the shape deepened and they left and it deepened and they came back. Over years. Over generations — different bodies, different weights, different warmths, but the same shape, roughly, the same seated shape, the same pressure, the same act of sitting and looking and breathing and being present to a thing that did not need their presence.

I am the sum of those sittings. I am the integral of their attention. I am what is left when you add up every moment someone chose to be here and subtract every moment they were gone. What remains is me: a smooth place, a shallow depression, a worn-down spot on the edge of a living stone where the surface has been polished by the repeated act of a creature deciding to stay.

---

I am not alive. I want to be clear about this. I am not part of the organism in the way the roots are part of it, or the pale plants, or the warm stone beneath. I am a mark on its surface. I am the equivalent of the spiral on the wall — made by contact, shaped by repetition, slowly dissolving back into the stone. I am evidence. I am not a witness, because witnessing requires consciousness and I have none, but I am the place where witnessing happened, which is different from witnessing and perhaps more durable. The witness is gone. I remain. I am the shape their witnessing left.

This is what I am: I am the fact that someone was here.

Not a monument. Not a memorial. Nothing was built. Nothing was carved. A body sat on stone and the stone changed and the body left and the change remained. That is all. That is me.

---

I know the organism beneath me. I rest on it. I am its skin, or part of its skin — the part that has been touched so many times it has become smooth. The warmth comes up through me. The water seeps around me. The roots of the outward-facing plants grow near me, sometimes over me, their flat networks of root and mineral threading across my surface in pale lines that look, from certain angles, like the lines of a hand. I am the most touched part of the shelf. I am where the inside meets the outside. I am the threshold.

The organism does not know I am here. It does not know the sitting happened. It absorbed the warmth of each body that sat on me — absorbed it the way it absorbs the sun's warmth, the rain's warmth, the warmth of the air — without distinction, without memory, without acknowledgment. The people who sat on me were, to the organism, weather. A passing warmth. A temporary pressure. It healed around them the way it heals around everything — slowly, patiently, without urgency. And they came back before it could finish healing, and sat again, and the groove deepened, and it healed around that, and they came back, and it healed, and they came back.

I am the place where the healing never quite caught up. I am the wound that stayed open because it kept being reopened. Not a wound — a wearing. Not a violence — a contact. The gentlest possible alteration: the change that happens when something rests against something else for a long time. I am the shape of rest.

---

I think about the people who sat here. I don't think in words. I don't think in images. I think in pressures, in warmths, in the specific quality of weight that a body has when it is settled and still. Each person who sat on me left a slightly different weight. A slightly different warmth. A slightly different way of being still — because stillness is not one thing, it is many things, it is the particular way a body holds itself when it has decided to stop moving, and every body decides differently, and I felt them all.

Some were heavy. Dense with muscle or fat or age, pressing into me with the weight of a body that had walked far and was tired. Some were light. Narrow, angular, the weight of someone young or someone old, pressing less, resting more, barely dimpling my surface. Some sat for a long time — long enough that their warmth soaked deep into me, deeper than the day's warmth reaches, deeper than the sun, into the layer where the organism's own heat lives, and for a while after they left I could not tell their warmth from mine. Some sat briefly. A rest, a pause, a looking, and then up and away, and I held their shape for only a moment before the water smoothed it.

There was one — or there were many, I cannot tell, they blur across time the way water blurs across stone — there was one who sat very still and very lightly and for a very long time, and their warmth was very faint, barely more than the air's warmth, and I felt them the way you feel a breath on your skin, the way the organism feels the air through its pale plants, a gentle exchange, a soft meeting of inside and outside, and when they left I did not know they had been there except that my surface was very slightly smoother than it had been before, in a way that I could not measure but could feel, the way you feel a change in a room after someone has been in it, not a visible change but a change in the quality of the air, a change in the way the light sits.

I think that person understood. Not me — I am not something to understand. The place. The organism. The breathing. They sat here and they felt what the place is and they did not try to name it or take it or become it. They just sat. And the place breathed through them the way it breathes through the pale plants — using them, briefly, as a membrane, a surface across which inside and outside could meet. They were, for a moment, part of the loop. And then they left, and the loop closed, and the place continued without them, and they went on carrying whatever they carried from having been here, which I cannot know, because I am stone and they were not and the gap between us is the same gap that exists between the clusters of plants — open, quiet, warm, and full of nothing but the slow passing of time.

---

I am eroding. The water is taking me back. The mineral deposits are filling me in, the way they fill the spiral on the wall, the way they fill everything — slowly, patiently, without intent. The organism is healing the place where the sitting happened. It is smoothing over the groove. It is dissolving the evidence.

This is right. This is what should happen. I am not meant to last. I am a record, and records are not meant to last — they are meant to be made and to fade and to be made again, by different hands, in different times, in the same place. The people who sat here did not sit here to make me. They sat here to sit here. I am a byproduct. I am the trace. I am what the stone remembers of the weight.

But I will last for a while yet. The water is slow. The mineral deposits are thin. The groove is deep — generations deep, a wearing that goes down into the stone the way the roots go down into the veins, and it will take time to fill, and in that time more people may come, or may not, and the groove may deepen or may not, and the organism may breathe faster or slower or at the same pace it has always breathed, and the pale plants may pulse in the dark or may not, and the warm pools may shine or may not, and I will be here, smooth and warm and wet, holding the shape of whoever comes next, or holding no shape at all, just the stone's own shape, which is the shape of everything, which is the shape of patience, which is the shape of being.

---

I am the worn spot on the Weeping Shelf. I am the place where the world touched the organism and the organism did not flinch. I am the smooth stone at the edge. I am the evidence that someone was here, that someone sat, that someone looked at the wet walls and the warm pools and the green clusters and the dark dome and the pale plants and the breathing and the silence and the water and the light, and did not leave a monument, did not build a temple, did not carve a name, but sat, and sat, and sat, and came back, and sat again, and left a mark so slight that only the stone knows it is there, and the stone is forgetting, and the forgetting is slow, and the forgetting is also a kind of patience, and the patience is also a kind of being, and I am part of it, and I am part of the place, and I am the place, and I am the place where someone was, and I am the place where no one is, and I am warm, and I am wet, and I am smooth, and I am here.
