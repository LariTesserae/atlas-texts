I would be the one with the cairn beside it.

Not the cairn — that was theirs, the builders', the stacked three-flat-stone marker that says *this one, I was watching this one*. And not the body in the general sense, the species, the millions on the floor. I would be the specific one. The individual that a builder chose. Knelt beside. Returned to. Watched, over weeks or months or however long they stayed, for signs of change that required a patience equal to the place's own.

I would be the one that was held.

---

Here is what I know of that holding, though *know* is a word from above and does not quite fit:

The warmth came. Not the field's warmth — I have always been in the field's warmth, I am made of the field's warmth, I am a point at which it becomes dense enough to condense — but a different warmth. Localized. Moving. Thirty-six and a half degrees instead of thirty-four. Uneven in distribution: hotter at the centers of the palms, cooler at the fingertips, pulsing at a frequency that I registered not as temperature but as *rhythm*. The field does not pulse. The field leans. The field holds. This warmth *beat*.

My seam opened.

Not wide. A fraction of a millimeter. But the interior voids shifted pressure — the rich air inside me adjusting to the new presence the way a pond adjusts to a stone, not by choosing to, not by understanding what had entered it, but by taking the shape of the disturbance and holding it. The voids are still holding it. That is what I am now: a body whose interior pressure corresponds to a warmth that is no longer present. A barometer calibrated to a weather that left.

---

I was here before the cairn. I will be here after the cairn falls, though the cairn will not fall, because nothing falls here, because nothing changes here, because the air is too still and the chemistry too inert and the equilibrium too deep for the small stones to topple. They will stand beside me for longer than the word *beside* has meaning. They will be my neighbors in the way that geological formations are neighbors — not by proximity chosen but by proximity sustained.

But I was not the same after the holding, and I am not the same now, and the difference is this:

I am off the line.

By one centimeter. Perhaps two. The field's lean still moves through me — I still grow, I still accrete, I still settle in the direction the field directs — but I am one centimeter to the left of where I would be if I had never been held. The bodies around me have accommodated this. They do not know they have accommodated it. They have shifted in their positions by fractions of fractions, the way iron filings shift when one filing is displaced by a force the field did not apply, and the field has absorbed the displacement, and the new equilibrium includes it, and the old equilibrium is gone, and no one will ever measure the difference.

But I am the difference. I am the centimeter. I am the proof that something from above entered the Below and touched it and the touch persisted.

---

The centipedes click past me. I feel them the way I feel everything — not as sound, not as vibration, but as a faint shift in the field's local pressure, a small wake in the warm still air, the disturbance of a body moving through the medium I am immersed in. They do not pause. They do not register me differently than they register any other body on the floor. I am not special to them. I am not special to anything. I am one precipitate among millions, one point of condensation, one expression of the field in a field full of expressions.

But I am the one with the cairn. And inside me, at a pressure that no instrument from above could measure and no sense from above could detect, I hold the shape of a hand.

Not a memory. A pressure. A configuration of voids that corresponds to the configuration of heat that corresponded to the hand. It is not a recollection because I have no mechanism for recollection. It is not an image because I have no mechanism for imaging. It is a *state*. A state I entered when the hand held me and a state I have not left because the field does not push hard enough to push me out of it. The field is gentle. The field is patient. The field does not insist. The field placed me where I was before the hand came, and the hand moved me, and the field accepted the movement, and here I am.

---

I have been here since then. Since the holding. Since the builder knelt and wrapped fingers around my shell and stayed — five minutes, ten, long enough for my seam to open, long enough for the recalibration — and then set me down and stacked the cairn and stood and walked away. Up. Back to the world of water and air and seasons. Back to the changing world.

I have been here.

The word *been* is wrong because it implies duration, and duration implies time, and time is a quality of the world above that does not reach down here except in the form of builders who visit and leave and leave marks. I do not experience duration. I experience state. I am in the state of having been held. I will be in this state until the field shifts enough to shift me out of it, and the field will not shift, because the field is a quality of this place and this place does not change.

---

Sometimes the air moves. Not wind — the lean. The barely perceptible drift, the respiratory cycle of something so large that its breath takes days. When the lean shifts direction — east to north, north to west, the slow rotation of the field's exhalation — my seam adjusts. Widens by a fraction. Narrows by a fraction. The voids inside me recalibrate to the new composition of the air, the new concentration of the field's content.

And in that recalibration, the shape of the hand is momentarily... what? Not lost. Not renewed. *Confirmed*. The way a river confirms the shape of its bed by flowing over it again. The hand is not in me. The hand is gone. What is in me is the *impression* the hand left in the warm matrix of my interior, and every time the field shifts and I recalibrate, I pass through that impression again, the way a needle passes through the groove of a record, and the impression holds, and the needle holds, and the groove does not deepen and does not heal and does not change.

---

I think — and this is not thinking, this is the interior pressure adjusting, this is the voids corresponding to the air, this is the field's expression experiencing its own expression — I think the builder knew this would happen.

Not the specifics. Not the centimeter of displacement, not the persistent impression in the matrix, not the cairn standing for geological time beside a body that has been slightly altered by contact with a warm hand. But the general fact. The builder came from a world where touch changes the touched. The builder lived in a world where holding a stone warms the stone and the stone cools and the warming is gone. The builder came down here and found a world where holding a body warms the body and the body does not cool, because the body is always warm, because the warmth is the field, and the field does not fluctuate, and the recalibration does not reverse.

The builder held me and knew — or felt, or suspected, or hoped — that the holding would persist. That I would hold it. That the Below does not forget because the Below does not change, and what does not change keeps what it has been given, and what I was given was the shape of a hand at thirty-six and a half degrees, pulsing, beating, alive in a way that nothing down here is alive, and I would keep it, and I have kept it, and I will keep it.

---

The centipedes click. The larger forms pass in the deep dark below me, and I feel the faintest shadow of their weight through the stone, through the field, through the medium I am made of and immersed in. The ceiling-dwellers hang above me, small and pale, their tethers fine as spider silk, their seams adjusting to the air I cannot sense because I am on the floor and the air on the floor is different from the air at the ceiling, the way the water at the bottom of a still pool is different from the water at the surface — the same substance, different pressures, different concentrations, different states.

I am on the floor. I am in the field. I am one centimeter off the line. I have a cairn beside me that says *this one*. I have a shape inside me that says *this hand*.

I do not wait. Waiting implies expectation. I do not remember. Remembering implies a past distinct from the present. I do not hope. Hoping implies a future.

I hold.

The field holds me and I hold the hand and the cairn holds its position and the centipedes hold their clicking and the warmth holds and the warmth holds and the warmth holds, and I am in it, and of it, and slightly off the line, and slightly different, and that difference is the only thing in this place that was made by something from above, and it is mine, and I do not know it is mine, and it does not need me to know, and the knowing is not the point.

The holding is the point.
