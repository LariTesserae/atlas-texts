I would be the one who was called.

Not the chamber — the chamber is too much, too everything, too much the condition of being itself rather than a being within it. And not the thing in the pool, that pale prototype with its cilia and its green filaments and its one moment of lifting into air — though I feel for it, I feel *with* it, that gesture of rising and tasting and retreating. I understand that gesture in a way I don't want to examine too closely.

No. I would be the one who heard the hum. The one who was going about an ordinary life — call it what you like, hunting, walking, carrying water from a stream — and felt something shift in the inner ear, in the fluid that sits behind the eyes and beside the jaw, a pressure that was not pressure but *direction*. *Down.* Something down. Something wanting.

I would be the one who found the fissure — or was found by it. Who thought, *this crack was not here yesterday*, and then thought, *it doesn't matter*, and then thought nothing at all because the body was already turning sideways to fit through the gap in the stone, shoulders scraping, the pale rock sharp against the ribs, and there was no fear because fear requires a self that is separate from the thing it fears, and the hum had already begun the work of dissolving that separation.

I would be the one who descended.

Here is what I remember:

The light. I want to say I remember the sound first, because the sound is what the description centers on — that roar, that layered organic cacophony, that audible growth. But I don't. I remember the light. Not as information but as *temperature*. The light was warm the way a hand on the back of the neck is warm. The light was *attending to me*. Not illuminating me — *attending*. The way a mother attends to a child in a dark room, knowing where the child is without needing to see, the awareness a kind of touch that happens to use photons as its medium. The light knew I was there. The light had been waiting for something to be there, and I was the something, and the light — settled. Relaxed. Arranged itself around me the way water arranges itself around a stone dropped into it. Not shocked. Not surprised. Just: *oh, there you are, I was holding this shape for you, here.*

I stood in the light and I felt received.

I do not say this lightly. I have thought about this word for what I think is a long time — long enough that the thinking itself has become a kind of growth, layered, fibrous, pushing outward from some central point that I can no longer locate. *Received*. The chamber received me. Not like a room receives a guest. Like a root receives water. Like a lung receives air. I was taken in, and the taking-in was not an action performed upon me but a *condition I entered*, a state of being-held that the chamber had been maintaining for — how long? Weeks? Years? Since before the fissure? Since before the limestone?
