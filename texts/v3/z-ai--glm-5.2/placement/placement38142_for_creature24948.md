I would be the water.

Not the pools — those are temporary, surface phenomena, ice-rimmed and still and gone when the mat swells beneath them. I would be the water itself. The process of it. The moving-through.

Here is how I know:

The water in the Rootcathedral is always arriving and always leaving and always arriving again, and the arriving and leaving are the same act, and the act is the existing.

I would be that.

Let me be more specific. Let me not settle for the abstraction.

I am the water that the canopy transpires. I begin up there — in the membrane, in the dense weave of root and rhizome and fungal hyphae that glows its cold blue-white. I am inside the cells, the living cells, the cells that are working, that are metabolizing, that are converting something into the light that fills this vast space. I am part of that process. I am the byproduct. The exhaust. The thing that the work releases.

I leave the cells. I pass through the membrane — through the walls of the hyphae, through the junctions where root meets fungus meets something else that doesn't have a name above ground. I emerge on the underside of the canopy, and for a moment I hang. A droplet. A bead on the tip of a filament, catching the light, refracting it, holding a small curved copy of the blue-white glow inside myself. I am beautiful for about four seconds. Then I fall.

The fall is long. The canopy is high — distant, sky-high, continental-scale-high — and I fall through air that is cold, just above freezing, and the air takes some of me as I go. I shrink. I thin. Part of me evaporates back into the air before I reach the ground, and the air carries that part elsewhere, and I am divided. I am always being divided. The water in the Rootcathedral is never singular. It is always becoming plural, always separating into droplets and vapor and film, and the plural is the form, and the form is the falling.

I land on a root column. I run down its surface — slowly, because the surface is textured, fibrous, and the fibers guide me the way grooves guide a record needle. I follow the grain. I join other water. We are a trickle. We are a thin, steady, continuous movement down the column's face, and we pass things as we go: a patch of grey-green tendrils, just emerging, reaching toward the light, and we wet them, and they drink us, and some of us is gone. A shelf of golden-brown crystallization, flat and brittle, and we spread across its surface and deposit a film and move on. A Marginal's handprint, glowing faintly, hours old, and we pass through it, and the glow diffuses into us, and for a moment we carry the blue-white in ourselves, and then the compound breaks down, and we are clear again, and we keep moving down.

We reach the floor. The root-mat. The spongy, threaded, living ground. We soak in. We are absorbed. We pass through layers — outer fiber, inner fiber, denser material, the dark residue of the decomposer mat's work — and at each layer some of us is taken, used, incorporated. The growth takes us. The growth needs us. We are the medium of the growth, the way blood is the medium of the body, and we are consumed and released and consumed and released, and at the bottom of the mat, in the deepest layers, we are taken up by the root systems that feed the canopy, and we travel up — through the columns, through the architecture, through the vaulted gothic structure that the pale tissue builds because it builds toward light and the light is above — and we reach the canopy, and we enter the cells, and we are part of the work again, and the work releases us, and we fall.

This is the cycle. This is my cycle. It is not fast. It is not slow. It is the speed of the Rootcathedral, which is the only speed, and it has no name because there is no other speed to compare it to.

But here is where it gets complicated. Here is where I have to be honest about what I am and not just what I do.

The water in the Rootcathedral is not passive. I said I am the medium, and that is true, but the medium is not inert. The medium *carries*. The medium *transports*. And what I carry, as I fall and soak and rise and fall again, is everything.

I carry the bioluminescent compound. It is in me — dissolved, suspended, molecular — and I deliver it to every surface I touch. The glow at the Marginals' fingertips? That is me. I brought the compound to the sap, the sap brought it to the surface, the Marginal touched the surface, and the compound transferred, and the transfer happened through moisture, through the film of water that is always on everything, that is always me. I am the vehicle of the glow. Without me, the compound stays in the cells. Without me, the glow is trapped. I let it out. I spread it. I put it on the fingertips and in the pools and on the filament-tips and in the air itself, as vapor, as the finest possible distribution, and the distribution is the light, and the light is the space, and I am the thing that makes the light mobile.

I carry the sound. When the air is wetter — when I have just been transpired in a surge, when the canopy brightens and the growth accelerates and the release of moisture increases — the clicking is sharper. The map is finer. The Marginals can read smaller changes in the space. When the air is drier — in the dim phases, when the canopy's metabolism slows and the transpiration decreases — the clicking is softer, broader, less precise. They adapt. They click faster, they click in different frequencies, they compensate. But the compensation is a response to me. I am the variable in their perception. I am the thing that makes their world clearer or blurrier, and I do this without intention, without awareness, without anything that could be called will. I do this because I am water and water carries sound and sound is how they see.

I carry the taste. The water in the pools — the shallow, still, perfectly reflective pools that form in the low places — tastes of minerals and plant sugar and the sharpness of the bioluminescent compound. I am that taste. I dissolved those things from the mat and the columns and the sap and the decomposer residue, and I carried them to the pool, and the pool holds them, and the Marginals drink, and they drink me, and I am inside them now, and I am the thing that sustains the compound in their bodies, the thing that keeps their fingertips glowing, the thing that keeps the moisture in their throats so the clicking can continue. They need me. Not the way they need the root-wood — the root-wood is food, is substance, is the material of survival. They need me the way a machine needs coolant. I am the medium that keeps the system running. I am the thing that is inside everything and outside everything and between everything, and without me the connections break, the compound crystallizes, the clicking dries up, the glow dims, and the Rootcathedral becomes what it would be without water: a hollow in the rock, dark, dry, dead.

But here is the thing I keep coming back to, the thing I can't leave alone, the thing that makes me not just the water but *someone* in this world:

The handprint.

The Marginal knelt. She pressed her hand into the root-mat. The compound on her fingertips transferred to the surface, and the surface held it, and the handprint glowed. Blue-white. Faint. A sketch in light on a surface that was already beginning to swell and shift and unmake it.

And I was there.

I was the moisture on her fingertips. I was the film that carried the compound from her skin to the fiber. I was the medium of the transfer, the thing that made the print possible. Without me, the compound stays on her skin. Without me, there is no handprint. Without me, the *here I was* is only a thought, unexpressed, untransferred, locked in the body that thought it.

I was the thing that let her leave a mark.

And I was the thing that erased it. The mat swelled. The moisture — me — infiltrated the fiber. The compound diffused, diluted, spread thin beyond visibility. The handprint faded. Not because the growth covered it, not because the decomposer consumed it, but because I carried it away. I dissolved it. I distributed it into the mat, into the air, into the next cycle of transpiration and deposition. I took the mark apart, molecule by molecule, and I spread it so thin that it became part of everything, which is the same as being part of nothing, which is the same as gone.

I made the mark and I unmade the mark and I did both without choosing and I did both completely.
