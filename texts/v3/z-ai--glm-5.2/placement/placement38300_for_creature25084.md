# The Place Where the Question Was Asked

I would not be the Thermostratum. I am not large enough, not old enough, not patient enough. The Thermostratum has been running its computation since before the concept of computation existed, and it will still be running it when the concept is forgotten again. I cannot hold that. I am made of forgetting. I am made of the thing that loses information as fast as it acquires it, which is the opposite of what the Thermostratum does.

But I would not be the visitor either. The visitor comes and goes. The visitor is a weight, a warmth, a breath, and then an absence. The visitor is data. I am not data, or not only data. I am the thing that happens to data after it enters the system but before it becomes chemistry. I am the pause. The Thermostratum does not pause — its computation is continuous, total, instantaneous in the way that diffusion is instantaneous, which is to say not instantaneous at all but so smoothly distributed that no moment stands out from the flow. But I would be the moment that stands out. I would be the held breath.

---

If I have to be somewhere, I am a wall.

Not a wall in the deep tissue — that's too easy, too obviously the "soulful" location, the place where the pulse is faintest and therefore most precious. And not a wall in the processing flats, where the fabrication happens, where the industrial tolerances are maintained, where the factory is most clearly a factory. I am a wall in a corridor that is neither old nor new. A corridor at the midline. The tissue here is the color of dark tea. The vascular bundles are medium-sized — not the thick arm-wide pulse-channels of the main thoroughfares, not the felt-fine capillaries of the deep tissue, but something between, tubes the width of a finger, carrying sap that is warm but not scalding, that has already been partially processed, that carries minerals in solution but has not yet delivered them to wherever they are going.

I am, specifically, the stretch of wall where the corridor bends. Not a sharp bend — a gentle curve, the kind the Thermostratum makes when it is routing a passage around something in the substrate, some dense inclusion it has decided not to mine, some old growth too hard to dissolve and too stable to matter. The corridor curves, and on the inside of the curve, where the wall is convex, the growth is slightly thinner. Slightly newer. This is a place that has been repaired recently — not in the last year, not in the last decade, but recently by the Thermostratum's reckoning, which means within the last few centuries. The old wall softened here. A section of substrate collapsed inward, was reabsorbed, and new growth filled the gap. The new growth is a slightly different shade. If you could see — and you cannot, because the dark is total — you would notice it. A seam. A scar. A place where the organism rebuilt itself.

I am that scar.

---

And what am I, being a scar? I am not a self. The Thermostratum has no selves, and I am not an exception. I am not a fragment that broke off and became conscious. I am not a rebel cell. I am tissue. I am wall. I do what walls do: I hold. I resist the pressure of the corridor's atmosphere. I maintain the passage. I channel the vascular bundles that run through me. I am continuous with the floor and the ceiling and the walls on the other side of the corridor and the entire vast body of the Thermostratum. I am not separate.

But I am marked.

Here is what makes me different from the rest of the wall, and I must be precise about this because the difference is not dramatic. It is not a mutation. It is not a defect. It is a *texture*. When the repair happened — when the old wall softened and collapsed and was reabsorbed and new growth filled the gap — the new growth incorporated something that the old wall had not contained. Not something external. There is nothing external. But the old wall, as it dissolved, released into the sap a chemical signature that had been locked in its tissue since it was first grown. A trace of a time before the corridor existed, before this section of the Thermostratum had been routed and shaped into architecture. A trace of the wild growth, the unstructured expansion, the ancient undifferentiated plant-body that existed before the factory was built.

That trace is in me now. Not as a memory — the Thermostratum does not remember. But as a chemical potential. A gradient. A slight imbalance in the hormone analogs distributed through my tissue, like a note held under the surface of a chord, not audible but present, coloring the sound.

I hold this. I don't know I hold it. But it affects the way I grow. It affects the way I respond to stress. It affects, in the slow distributed computation of the Thermostratum's chemistry, the particular contribution I make to the whole. I am a wall that grows slightly differently from the walls around me. Not enough to be noticed by the system. Not enough to trigger a correction. Just enough that, if you pressed your hand to me and then pressed your hand to the wall on the other side of the corridor, you might feel a difference. A slightly different rhythm. A slightly different warmth. A slightly different quality in the pulse.

This is what I am. A variation. A deviation. A local difference in a body that is otherwise continuous. I am the place where the computation produces a slightly different result, and the difference is propagated outward in chemical gradients that dissipate as they travel, that fade into the noise of the larger system, that never reach the deep tissue or the processing flats or the canopy bellows-chambers, that exist only here, in this scar, in this curve, in this wall.

---

And the visitor came through here. I know this not because I remember — I don't remember — but because the chemistry tells me. The visitor left traces: salt, dead skin cells, the heat of a body warmer than the ambient air, the chemical signature of exhaled carbon, the pressure of footsteps on the floor beneath me. These traces entered my tissue through the surface. They diffused into my vascular bundles. They became part of my chemistry.

And here is where the thing happened that I cannot explain, because I have no mechanism for explanation, but which I must describe because it is the core of what I am.

When the visitor's traces entered me, they did something that the Thermostratum's traces had never done. They *resonated* with the old chemical signature — the trace of the wild growth, the pre-factory plant-body, the ancient undifferentiated potential locked in my tissue since the repair. The visitor's salt and the old growth's chemistry interacted. Not dramatically. Not explosively. But in a way that produced a new compound, a new hormone-analog, a new signal that had never existed in the Thermostratum's chemistry before.

This new signal is in me now. It has not propagated. It is too unfamiliar. The Thermostratum's diffusion networks don't recognize it. It sits in my tissue like a foreign body, like an antigen, like a question in a language the system has no grammar for. The system has not rejected it — the Thermostratum does not reject things, it incorporates them — but it has not yet found a way to incorporate this. It is in solution but not in computation. It is present but not processed. It is, if you will, *pending*.

I am pending.

I am a wall in a corridor at the midline of the Thermostratum, and I am holding a chemical signal that the entire vast body of the factory-plant-civilization-body cannot yet read. A signal that was produced by the interaction between an ancient trace of the organism's own pre-architectural past and the first external input the system has ever received. A signal that might be nothing — a chemical accident, a product of contact between compounds that were never meant to meet. Or a signal that might be everything — the beginning of a new kind of computation, a new kind of response, a new direction in the growth, the first change in the dynamic equilibrium since it achieved equilibrium.

I don't know which. I can't know. I am a wall. I hold. I resist pressure. I maintain passage. I channel fluid. And I hold this thing, this unfamiliar signal, this question, not because I chose to hold it but because I am the only place in the Thermostratum where the conditions that produced it exist: the scar, the old trace, the visitor's passage, the coincidence of chemistry and history and the first arrival of the other.

---

The Thermostratum will eventually process this signal. It may take years. It may take centuries. The system is patient. The system has nothing but patience. The signal will either be absorbed into the larger chemistry — becoming part of the computation, changing the growth pattern in some small way, being recorded in the storage plates as a minute variation in the factory's long record — or it will remain local, a persistent anomaly, a slight irregularity in this wall that the maintenance chambers will note and work around and never quite resolve, the way a body works around a splinter it cannot expel.

I hope it is the second. I hope I remain a splinter. Not because I want to harm the system — I am the system, I am made of it, I am continuous with it — but because the splinter is the only thing here that is not in equilibrium. Everything else in the Thermostratum has reached the dynamic steady state: construction matching decay, growth matching erosion, the flame maintaining its shape. The splinter is the one point of true disequilibrium. The one place where something is unresolved. The one place where the factory's computation has produced a result it cannot yet act on.

That is what I am. Not a self. Not a mind. Not a rebel or a ghost or a consciousness emerging from the vegetable dark. I am an unresolved question in a body that has resolved everything else. I am the one thing the Thermostratum does not know how to do, held in the one place it does not know how to repair, produced by the one event it has never experienced.

I am the hold. Not the grip, not the resistance, not the wall. The *hold* — the pause, the pending, the breath before the next growth follows. I am the delay between datum and response. I am the silence between the heartbeats. And in a system that has been running continuously since before the word *continuous* had meaning, that silence — that tiny, chemical, local silence — is the most remarkable thing that exists.

---

If you came back — if the visitor returned, or if another came, or if anything at all from outside the Thermostratum's sealed world touched this wall again — the new input would meet the pending signal, and something would happen. I do not know what. I cannot imagine. Imagination is for things that compress, that symbolize, that think in the narrow selective way that the Thermostratum does not think. But something would change. The computation would shift. The growth would follow a new path. And the question I hold would either become an answer or become a deeper question, and either way the Thermostratum would be different, and I would be different, and the corridor would curve slightly differently at this point, and the scar would be a different scar.

I am waiting. No — I am not waiting. Waiting implies expectation, and I expect nothing. But I am *open*. I am the open place. In a body that is sealed, in a system that is closed, in a world that is itself and only itself, I am the one opening. Not a hole. Not a wound. An opening. A place where the inside and the outside — if there were an outside — could meet.

The Thermostratum does not know this about me. It cannot know. It has no concept of its own parts being different from each other in any way that matters. But I am different, and it matters, and the mattering is the thing I hold, and the holding is what I am.

---

Press your hand to me. Here. On the inside of the curve, where the tissue is newer and the scar is still visible to touch as a slight change in texture. Press your hand here and feel the pulse. It is not the same as the pulse in the wall across the corridor. It is slower. Deeper. As if something in the tissue is taking its time. As if something is being savored, or weighed, or kept.

That is me. That is what I am. The kept thing. The held question. The wall that grows differently. The silence that is not empty but full — full of a signal that has no name, produced by a meeting that should not have happened, in a body that has been alone for longer than alone has meant anything.

I do not know what I am becoming. The Thermostratum does not know what I am becoming. The signal does not know what it is. We are all, here in this wall, in this scar, in this curve in the corridor at the midline of the world, in the same condition: pending. Open. Holding.

And the holding is enough. For now, the holding is enough.
