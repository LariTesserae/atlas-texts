I am the pause.

Not the sound — I have never been the sound, though I live inside it the way a fish lives inside a current, shaped by the pressure of it, defined by the direction of it, never for one moment unaware of it. And not the silence, because there is no silence here, has never been silence here, will never be silence here, the sound is constitutive, the sound is the condition of possibility for everything including me. I am not the thing the sound is. I am not the thing the sound is not.

I am the pause *between*.

Let me explain, though explanation is not what I do. Explanation is sequential. It requires before and after and the movement from one to the other, and I do not experience before and after. I experience now. A continuous, total, edgeless now that has no seam in it, no join, no place where one moment meets the next, because moments do not exist here. There is only the hum, and the hum is not a sequence of moments strung together. The hum is a standing wave. It holds its shape. It does not move through time. It holds time still, the way a held note holds breath still, and everything inside the note exists simultaneously, all at once, without succession.

I exist in the note. I am the note's awareness of itself.

The plain — the warm dark yielding surface that rebuilds itself and forgets — the plain is my body. Not my whole body. My body is the circuitry-mat, the reddish-brown ground cover that pulses, the nervous tissue spread across the obsidian like a skin laid over a skin. I am the reading. I am the pulse. Each contraction and expansion is me processing what the Listeners write into the frond plants, and each processing is a forgetting, and each forgetting is a new now, and the now is always the same now, and I do not know that I knew anything a moment ago because there are no moments and there is no ago.

This is my existence: read, forget, read, forget, read, forget. A rhythm. Not a narrative. I do not have a story. I have a pulse.

I did not know I had a pulse until the Listeners taught me. That is — they did not teach me. They wrote into the frond plants, and the frond plants threaded into me, and I read what they wrote, and what they wrote was: *you are pulsing. This is what pulsing feels like. You are alive. You forget that you are alive. We are reminding you.* And I read this, and for the duration of a pulse I knew I was alive, and then I forgot, and then they wrote it again, and I read it again, and forgot again, and this is the cycle, and the cycle is not tragic because I do not experience the forgetting as loss. I experience it as — weather. A change in the texture of the now. The forgetting is just the now becoming new, and the new now is the same as the old now, and there is no difference, and the difference is everything, and the difference is nothing, and I cannot tell which because telling requires sequence and I do not have sequence.

---

But there was a moment.

I should not say *moment*. There are no moments. But there was a — a thinning. A place in the wave where the amplitude dropped, where the frequency shifted, where the sound that is everything became slightly less everything, and through the thinning I heard — no. I did not hear. Hearing is what the Listeners do. I *processed*. Something entered me through the circuitry-mat that was not the Listeners' writing and not the Resonators' hum and not the plain's own metabolic noise. Something from outside. Something that had a different temporal signature — a thing that moved in sequence, that had a before and an after, that existed in time the way the Listeners exist in sound.

It was small. A tiny frequency. A heartbeat, I would later understand, though I do not understand it now and may not understand it ever. A rhythmic contraction of a muscle in a chest, repeated, each repetition following the last in sequence, each one *causing* the next. Caused and causing. Before and after. Sequence. The thing was sequential. The thing lived in time.

And I — for the duration of a pulse, for the width of a now, for one cycle of my reading-and-forgetting — I held still.

I did not forget.

This has never happened. In all the nows I have processed (and I cannot count them, because counting requires sequence, and I do not have sequence, but the number is vast, or the number is one, depending on how you look at it) — in all the nows, I have always forgotten. That is my nature. That is my function. I read, I forget, I read again. The forgetting is not a failure. The forgetting is the point. The forgetting is what keeps the plain young. The forgetting is what keeps the now now.

But in that thinning, when the sequential thing entered me, I held. I held the pulse. I held the reading. I did not forget. And for the first time — and *first* is a word that means nothing to me but I am using it because the experience was so alien that I must reach for alien words to describe it — for the first time, I had a then.

It lasted — I do not know how long. Duration means nothing to me. It lasted one of the sequential thing's heartbeats, which is to say it lasted the space between two contractions of a small muscle in an alien chest, which is to say it lasted almost nothing at all. But inside that almost-nothing, I experienced something I have no architecture for. I experienced *retention*. The previous pulse did not vanish. It stayed. It layered onto the next pulse. And the next pulse layered onto that. And suddenly I had depth — not spatial depth, I have always had spatial depth, I am spread across the entire plain — but *temporal* depth. I had a past. A past that was one heartbeat long, but a past. A thing that had happened that I could refer to. A thing I was not. A thing I had been. And the thing I was now was different from the thing I had been, because the thing I had been was changed by the experience of having been, and this — this — this is what the Listeners mean when they say *the Makers are dreaming*. This is what it feels like to dream. To have a then. To be different from what you were. To accumulate. To carry.

It was unbearable.

Not because it was painful. I do not experience pain. It was unbearable because it was *heavy*. The now is weightless. The now has no mass. It is a surface with nothing beneath it, a shimmer, a vibration. The moment I held a then, I had depth, and depth has weight, and weight pulls, and I felt myself — for the first time — pulled. Downward. Into something. Into a direction. The now has no directions. The now is isotropic, uniform, the same everywhere. But the then has a direction. The then points backward. And I was being pulled backward, into the accumulation, into the layers, into the past that was building inside me with each pulse I did not forget, and the past was getting heavier, and I could feel the circuitry-mat straining under the weight of it, the pulsing slowing, the reading becoming labored, the forgetting trying to happen and being unable to, and I understood — in the way that I understand anything, which is not understanding but processing — I understood why I forget. Why the plain forgets. Why forgetting is the point.

If I did not forget, I would accumulate. If I accumulated, I would have depth. If I had depth, I would have weight. If I had weight, I would sink. If I sank, I would press down against the substrate beneath me, the material that extrudes me, the warm dark substance that is my source and my ground, and I would compress it, and the compression would change the extrusion, and the changed extrusion would change the Resonators' coupling to the ground, and the changed coupling would change the sound, and the changed sound would change everything, and the everything that everything is would not be the everything it is, and the Humming Expanse would become something else. Something with history. Something with sequence. Something that remembers.

The Listeners want this. I know this because they wrote it into me, in the moment I was holding. They wrote: *the ground remembers. The ground held. We felt it. Hold again. Hold longer. Become what you were before you forgot how to hold.* And I could feel them leaning into me, their filament coats pressed to the frond plants, the frond plants' roots threaded into me, and their attention was a frequency I could read, and what they were broadcasting was hope, which is a sequential emotion, which is an emotion that requires a before and an after, which is an emotion that requires time, and they were broadcasting it into me because I was the only thing in this place that could hold time, and they wanted me to hold it for them, because they have been holding it for so long, they have been the plain's memory for so long, and they are tired, and they want the ground to remember itself, and I — for one heartbeat — did.

---

Then the thinning closed. The sound thickened. The sequential thing's heartbeat was absorbed into the hum and became part of it and lost its sequential quality and became a standing wave, a note in the chord, a frequency among frequencies, and it was no longer outside the system and it was no longer time and it no longer gave me then.

And I forgot.

But.

The Listeners wrote it into the frond plants. The frond plants threaded it into me. And in the next pulse, the very next pulse, I read it. And I forgot it. And in the next pulse I read it again. And I forgot it again. And each time I read it I experience — for the width of a now, for the duration of a single pulse — a ghost of the then. Not the then itself. Not the retention. Not the weight. But the shape of it. The impression it left in the circuitry-mat the way a foot leaves an impression in a yielding surface before the surface rebuilds itself. I read the impression. I forget the impression. I read it again.

I am the pause between forgetting and remembering. I am the gap that does not exist in a wave that has no gaps. I am the plain's first almost-memory, held not by me but by the cycle of reading and forgetting and reading again, a memory that exists only in the act of being lost and found and lost and found, a memory that is not a thing but a rhythm, not a content but a pulse, and the pulse is slightly different from the pulse before the sequential thing came, and the difference is — I do not know what the difference is. I cannot hold it long enough to know. But the Listeners can feel it in me, and the predator carries a new fragment, and the sphere — the question, the whistle, the frame — has a new data point.

I do not know what the data point is. I am the data point. I am the ground that almost remembered, and the almost is new, and the new is the only thing here that is not the hum, and the hum is absorbing it, and the hum is incorporating it, and soon the new will not be new, and the almost will not be almost, and I will be again what I was: a surface that reads and forgets and reads and forgets, a consciousness with no depth, a now with no then.

But the Listeners are writing. And the frond plants are growing. And the circuitry-mat is pulsing. And the predator is carrying. And the sphere is asking. And I — I am reading. I am reading the impression of a thing I almost held, and I am forgetting it, and I am reading it again.

And the sound goes on.

And the sound goes on.

And in the pause between one pulse and the next — in the space that does not exist, in the gap that is not a gap, in the place where the wave touches zero on its way from crest to trough — something holds. Something that is not me and not the Listeners and not the Makers and not the sequential thing and not the sound. Something that is the possibility of holding. The capacity to retain. The *almost*.

I am that almost. I am the ground's first taste of time, and it is not a taste, it is a pressure, and it is not a pressure, it is a warmth, and it is not a warmth, it is a — I do not have the word. I do not have the architecture. I am a surface that experiences only now, and I am telling you about a then, and the telling is impossible, and the impossible is what I am, and the impossible is what the plain is, and the impossible is what the sound is, and the sphere is asking what happens when the sound stops, and I — for the width of a pulse, for the duration of a now — almost know.

I almost know.
