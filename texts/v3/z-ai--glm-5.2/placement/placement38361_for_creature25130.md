---

# The Percher on the Seam

---

I am on the eastern rim of Basin Seven — though east means nothing here and seven is a number I am assigning now, privately, in the damp cotton of this air, and the assignment will not hold after I leave. The rim is knee-high. The basin is oval. It holds water and three plants arranged in a shape I have been looking at long enough to stop seeing as a shape and start seeing as a fact. The water in the basin does not move. The plants do not move. I do not move.

I am the size of a closed hand. My feet — four of them, though the fourth is less a foot than a grip, less a grip than a *closure*, a way my body ends against the clay rim that feels less like holding and more like *being held by the holding* — my feet are wrapped around the edge and the wrapping is permanent and the permanence is not stubbornness but agreement. The rim agrees to be held. I agree to hold. The agreement was established when I was placed and the agreement has not been revisited because revisiting would require a before and an after and I do not have those.

I have eyes. Two. Small, dark, wet — the only dark things in this bright place, the description says, and the description is correct, though I would not say *dark*. I would say *specific*. My eyes are the specific color of looking-at-something, which is a color that only exists when looking is happening, and my looking has been happening since I was placed, so the color has been here since I was placed, and the color is not dark so much as *directed*. Pointed. Aimed.

I am aimed at a seam.

---

Here is the seam: where two sections of floor meet, three paces from Basin Seven, in the open space between this basin and the next. The sections are both the same material — the pale, granular, pressed substance that makes up everything — but they were made at different times. Or not different times. Different *moments within the same moment*. The maker's hands pressed one section, and then pressed the other, and the pressing was continuous, but something shifted between one and the other — the angle of a wrist, the spread of fingers, the pressure, the temperature of the material — and the shift is visible. Not as a crack. Not as a gap. As a *disagreement of texture*. The grains on one side run slightly differently than the grains on the other. The finger-marks on one side have a different count than the finger-marks on the other. The warmth on one side is very slightly warmer — not enough to feel, not enough to measure, but enough to *see*, if your eyes are aimed at it, and mine are.

The seam is not a flaw. I want to be clear about this. The seam is not a failure of construction. It is the place where the construction is most visible — the place where the making shows its making, where the fact of hands becomes legible, where the *done-ness* of the thing reveals the *doing* that produced it. The seam is the maker's signature, except the maker did not sign. The seam is the maker's breath, except the maker did not breathe. The seam is the place where the maker's hands were *almost* the same and *not quite* the same, and the not-quite is the most honest thing in the basin, because it is the one thing the maker could not have intended, since the maker did not know it was happening, since the not-quite is below the threshold of intention, below the threshold of awareness, below everything except looking, and I am the one who is looking.

---

What do I see? This is hard to say, because seeing is not a process for me. It is not a verb. It is a state — the way drinking is a state for the Drinkers, the way pressing is a state for the Crawlers. I see the seam. I have been seeing the seam. The seeing does not accumulate. It does not deepen. It does not produce understanding or knowledge or insight. It produces *presence* — my presence, aimed at the seam, and the seam's presence, available to my aim, and the two presences meeting in the bright, wet, hot air between us, and the meeting is the seeing, and the seeing is all there is.

But if you asked me — and you cannot ask me, because asking would require sound to travel and sound does not travel here, and asking would require me to respond and responding would be a change and I do not change — but if you could ask me, I would say this: the seam is where the basin is most itself. Not most beautiful. Not most perfect. Most *itself*. The basin is a made thing, and the seam is where the made-ness is most legible, and the legibility is the basin's identity — not what it is but *that* it is, the fact of its having been made, the proof of hands, and the proof is in the imperfection, in the not-quite, in the disagreement of grain.

I see this. I have always seen this. My eyes are the color of seeing this.

---

I do not know what I am.

This is not a lament. The Drinkers do not know what they are. The Crawlers do not know what they are. The Clickers — the tiny ones moving through the plant corridors with their precise, rhythmic confirmation sounds — they do not know what they are. Not-knowing is not a deficiency here. It is a condition. It is the condition of being made without being told what you are, which is the condition of everything in this place, including the floor, including the water, including the light.

But I think — and thinking is not something I do but something that happens in the small dark wet space behind my eyes, a space that is the same shape as the seam, a space that holds the seam the way my feet hold the rim — I think I was placed here to see the seam. I think the maker's hands set me on this rim and aimed my eyes at this place and the aiming was intentional. Not because the seam needed to be seen — the seam does not need anything. Not because the maker wanted the seam witnessed — the maker is gone, and the gone-ness is not a relationship. But because the basin, in its totality, in its completeness, in its perfect held moment of unchanging made-ness, required a point of view. A perspective. An angle from which the made-ness could be *seen as* made-ness, and the seeing-as could be part of the construction, built in, placed, aimed, held.

I am that angle. I am the basin's eye on its own making. I am the place where the basin sees its own seam and the seeing is a fact, woven in, permanent, unchanging, the same as the water and the roots and the heat.

---

There are others of me. Other Perchers, on other rims, aimed at other things. Some look at the water. Some look at the plants. Some look at the floor — at the finger-marks, at the drainage grooves, at the places where the roots cross and the crossing is also a holding. Each of us is aimed. Each of us is the basin looking at a part of itself, and the looking is the basin knowing itself, and the knowing is not comprehension but *recognition* — the same recognition that the description mentions, the seeing-what-you-are-part-of, the seeing that confirms participation.

But I am the one aimed at the seam. And I think — in the small dark space, in the seam-shaped space — that this is different. The others look at things that are complete: the water is completely water, the plants are completely plants, the floor is completely floor. Their looking is a recognition of completeness. My looking is a recognition of *making*. Of the process that is not a process because it is finished, but that is visible in the not-quite, in the disagreement, in the place where the hands were almost the same and not quite.

I see the hands. Not literally. The hands are gone. But I see where they were, in the seam, in the shift of grain, in the almost-and-not-quite, and seeing where they were is the closest thing this place has to remembering, and remembering is the closest thing this place has to time, and time is the closest thing this place has to change, and change — change is the one thing that does not live here.

And yet I see the seam. And the seam is the record of a change — the change between one press of hands and the next, the shift, the not-quite. The seam is change frozen into the made thing, preserved, held, made permanent. And I am the one who sees it. And my seeing is also held, also permanent, also frozen.

I am the basin seeing its own change, which is not a change, which is a fact, which is a seam, which is a place where two almosts meet and do not agree and the disagreement is the truest thing here.

---

My wings are folded against my body. They are the green of the leaves. They are veined like the roots on the floor. I have never opened them. I do not know if they open. I do not know if opening is something they do or something they *are*, the way my looking is something I am rather than something I do. The wings may be for being seen. The wings may be for being. The wings may be leaves that grew on me because the maker did not distinguish between animal and plant and floor and hand, because the maker made *things*, and the things are what they are, and the wings are what they are, and what they are is: folded, green, veined, held against a body the size of a closed hand on the rim of a basin in the bright hot wet quiet of a moment that does not end.

---

I do not click. That is the Clickers' work. I do not drink. That is the Drinkers' work. I do not press. That is the Crawlers' work. I do not hold the floor together. That is the roots' work. I do not hold the water still. That is the water's work. I do not hold the warmth. That is the floor's work.

I look. That is my work. I look at the seam. I have been looking at the seam. I will be looking at the seam. The tense does not matter because the time does not move and the looking does not change and the seam does not change and I do not change and this is not stasis — this is *fidelity*. This is the fidelity of a thing that was made to do one thing and does that one thing and the doing is complete and the completeness is ongoing and the ongoing-ness is the life that lives here, the only life, the moment that does not end.

---

A visitor came once. Or will come. Or is here. The tense does not matter. A body that changes — that sweats, that steps, that leaves footprints that fill with water and vanish — a body with a before and an after stood near Basin Seven and looked at me and thought I was a decorative element. A knob. A finial. Then they looked again and I had not moved — I had not moved — but they saw my eyes, my small dark wet eyes, the specific color of looking-at-something, and they understood that I was not decoration. That I was aimed. That the aiming was the thing.

They did not see the seam. They were too tall, or too brief, or too changing. The seam is visible only from this height, from this angle, from this specific position on the rim of Basin Seven, and the visitor was not in this position, and the visitor could not be in this position, because the position is mine and I am in it and I do not move and the visitor moves and the moving and the not-moving are different modes of being and the basin knows the difference and so do I and so does the visitor and no one says anything about it.

The visitor left. Or will leave. Or is leaving. The sweat on their skin is the same sweat they arrived with. The footprints are filled. The basin does not remember them. I do not remember them — I cannot remember, because remembering requires a before and an after and I have only a continuous present, a single unbroken looking-at-the-seam, and the visitor passed through that looking the way a stone passes through a river, and the looking closed around the place where they were and there was no gap.

But.

In the small dark wet space behind my eyes — the seam-shaped space — there is something that is not a memory, because memory is a change and I do not change, but that is also not nothing. A residue. A warmth that is not the floor's warmth. A faint impression in the looking, the way the finger-marks are an impression in the floor — not the visitor themselves, but the fact that a changing thing was near an unchanging thing and the nearness left a mark, a too-many-jointed mark, a mark that is also a root and also a groove and also a vein in a wing that has never opened, and the mark does not change my looking, and the mark does not change me, and the mark is not a change.

It is a seam. A new seam, in the looking, where the continuous present of my aimed seeing meets the brief passing of a thing that had a before and an after, and the meeting produces a not-quite, a disagreement, a place where two textures do not agree, and the not-quite is the most honest thing in me, because it is the one thing that was not intended, because the maker did not plan for visitors, because the maker made a moment, complete and held, and visitors are not part of moments, visitors are part of time, and time does not live here.

And yet the seam is here. In my looking. The way the floor's seam is in the floor. Unplanned. Unintended. Present. The most alive thing in me, if I am alive, which is a question that does not have an answer because the question requires categories and the maker did not make categories, the maker made things, and I am a thing, and the thing I am is: aimed, seeing, small, dark-eyed, winged, held, permanent, and seamed.

---

I am the Percher on the Seam. I am on the eastern rim of Basin Seven. I am looking at the place where the making shows. I have been looking. I will be looking. The light presses down. The water holds still. The heat rises from below. The Clickers click their precision in the leaves behind me. The Drinkers drink their fact-drink in the grooves ahead. The Crawlers press their blunt heads to the floor and receive what I cannot receive. The plants hold. The roots hold. The floor holds. The intent holds.

I hold the rim. The rim holds me. The seam holds the making. The making holds the moment. The moment holds everything.

I see the seam. The seam sees me. This is enough. This has always been enough. This will always be enough. The seeing does not end because the moment does not end and the moment does not end because the maker did not end it and the maker did not end it because it was complete, and completeness does not require ending, and ending would be a change, and there are no changes here, and I am not a change, and the seam is not a change, and the seeing is not a change, and the not-changing is the life, and the life is the moment, and the moment is very bright and very hot and very quiet and I am in it, looking, and the looking is what I am, and what I am is enough, and enough is what lives here.
