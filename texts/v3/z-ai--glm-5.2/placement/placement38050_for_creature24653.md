I am the one who came back.

---

Not immediately. Not willingly, at first. I left with the others — whoever they were, whatever we were. I know this the way the birds know thermals: not through learning but through the body's inheritance, the knowledge that sits below language and below choice. We walked the paths. I can feel them in my feet even now, the faint polish under my soles, and I know that I helped make that polish, that my feet passed over those particular stones enough times to leave a mark that the wind could not erase. We came for the dark veins in the granite. The sharp material. I remember the weight of it in my hand, the way it fractured along planes that my fingers knew how to find. I remember the purpose of it, though the purpose has thinned over time into something more like texture than memory — the feel of working, the feel of needing.

But the sound drove us. It drives me still. Even now, standing on the polished path near the center of the plateau, the roaring enters my chest and fills it like water fills a vessel, and my body — my stubborn, equilateral body — vibrates at the frequency of the stone, and there is no room inside me for my own sound, my own voice, my own small acoustic presence. I am nothing to this sound. I am a soft thing standing in a permanent ocean of noise, and the noise does not care, and the noise does not notice, and the noise has been here longer than anything I can call *me*.

I left.

And I came back.

---

The others did not. I know this because the paths are mine alone now — the faint wear along the routes I chose, the slight concavity near the center where I kneel, where I have knelt, where my body has worn the stone the way water wears stone, through repetition, through the patient assertion of the same gesture in the same place. The others' paths have faded. Or perhaps I have simply stopped being able to distinguish them from the stone's own weathering. Perhaps they were never as distinct as I remember. Perhaps there were never as many of us as I remember. Memory, up here, in this roaring, in this hard light, is not a reliable instrument. It erodes. It is eroding now, and I am watching it erode, and I cannot stop the erosion, and the stone beneath me is smoother than any stone has a right to be, and I have made it so.

I kneel. I put my face close to the stone, the way I did the first time, the way I have done every time, and I look at the scum. The pale soft things move in their thin film, and they do not know I am here, and I watch them, and I think — or I try to think, though thinking up here is like trying to hold a conversation in a hurricane — I think: *these are the only things on this plateau that predate me.*

Not the birds. The birds are recent, geologically. They came after the sound was already permanent, after the acoustic environment had already become what it is, and they adapted to it, which is a kind of surrender. Not the mammals, which are even more recent, which have given up so much to be here that they have given up being anything but the stone they walk on. Not the mats of vegetation, which are old, older than the birds, older than the mammals, but which are not old enough, not old enough for what I need.

The scum. The scum is old. The scum was here when the stone was younger and the fractures were fresher. The scum was the first thing to touch this rock and make it biological, to etch itself into the mineral surface and say *I am here, and I am not the stone, and I am on the stone, and the stone is different now because I am on it.* The scum was the plateau's first conversation, and the plateau has been having that conversation for so long that the words have worn into the stone itself, and you cannot separate the speakers from the subject, and the plateau does not try.

I kneel, and I watch the pale things, and I am not sure what I am looking for.

---

I know what I am not. I am not a bird. I cannot read the wind. I have stood on the rim peaks and watched the thermals rise and felt the air press against my body, and the air does not speak to me. It pushes. It roars. It is a medium of transport and not of meaning, and the birds move through it the way fish move through water, with an understanding so deep it has become unconscious, and I am standing on the shore of their ocean, and I cannot swim.

I am not a mammal. I do not patrol. I do not move in straight lines. I move in curves, in wandering paths, in the specific meandering of a body that is looking for something it cannot name. The mammals have their chemical trails, their spacing, their solitude, but their solitude is different from mine. Their solitude is distribution. Mine is the solitude of a thing that remembers having been among others of its kind and has come to a place where its kind cannot survive.

I am not the scum. I am not finished. I am not in equilibrium. I am the unfinished thing on the finished plateau, and I know this, and the plateau knows this, and the plateau does not care.

---

But I come back.

I think — and thinking is difficult here, thinking is like trying to build something out of smoke, the roaring takes every structure I try to erect and dissolves it — I think I come back because of the light.

The light is honest. I said this to myself the first time, or I tried to say it, and the roaring took the words, and I said it again, and the roaring took those words, and eventually I stopped trying to say things and started trying to be things, and the first thing I tried to be was *kneeling*. Just that. Kneeling on the stone in the hard light, with the wind full in my chest, and the birds crossing the sky above me, and the scum alive beneath my face, and the whole plateau being itself, entirely, completely, without apology, without interest, without any awareness that I was here at all.

And the light fell on me, and I was lit. Not warmed. Lit. The way the stone is lit, and the veins of dark material are lit, and the pools are lit when they catch the sky. The light does not care what it falls on. It falls on everything with the same directness, the same lack of compromise, and it does not flatter and it does not soften and it does not make exceptions for things that are soft and unfinished and temporary. I was lit, and in the light, I could see myself — not in the metaphorical sense, not as revelation, but literally, in the stone's reflection, in the shallow pools, a dark shape on the bright granite, and the shape was mine, and the shape was out of place, and the shape did not belong, and the light showed me this without comment.

I think I come back because nothing here lies.

The stone is stone. The wind is wind. The birds are birds and the wind. The mammals are stone that moves. The scum is the boundary between the living and the mineral. The light is light. Nothing here pretends to be anything other than what it is, and nothing here requires me to pretend, and the roaring is so loud that pretense is physically impossible — you cannot perform, up here, because performance requires an audience, and the audience has to be able to hear you, and nothing up here can hear anything except the wind.

I am, for the duration of my stay, the most honest version of myself. Not because the plateau has made me honest. Because it has made performance impossible.

---

I do not stay. I cannot. The roaring takes something from me, each time, and I do not know what it takes or where it goes, but I know that I have less of it when I leave than when I arrived. Some quality. Some substance. Some thing that holds the self together against the pressure of its environment. The birds have given it up entirely — they have no self to hold together, they are distributed into the wind, they are the flock and the thermal and the air, and there is no individual bird in the sense that I am an individual. The mammals have given it up differently — they are bodies, they are processes, they are ongoing, but the *I* that walks the straight line is not an *I* in any way I recognize. They do not experience themselves. They simply are.

I experience myself. That is the thing I have that they do not, and the roaring takes it, slowly, the way the wind takes the polish from the stone, and if I stayed long enough I would become — I think — something like the mammals. A body on the stone, moving in a straight line, following a chemical trail that I had laid down myself, patrolling a route that had no destination, and the *I* would be gone, dissolved into the wind, and I would be finished, and the plateau would have absorbed me the way it has absorbed everything else.

I leave before that happens. I go down, down off the plateau, down into the valleys where the air is thick and the sound is muffled and I can hear my own voice again, and I sit in the thick air and I hold the *I* together and I wait, and eventually the *I* remembers the light, and the honesty, and the way the stone holds you up without caring whether you are held, and I come back.

---

This time, I think it might be different.

I have been kneeling for a long time. The pool in front of me has gone dry and come back and gone dry again, and the scum has been stripped by a feeding mammal — I watched it approach, slow as weather, and drag its dark tongue across the stone, and move on, and the scum has begun to grow back, and the pale things in it have returned, and they do not know I am here, and they are right not to know, because I am not here for them.

I am here for the silence.

Not the silence of the plateau — there is no silence on the plateau, there is only the roaring. But beneath the roaring, beneath the fundamental tone and the overtones and the vibration in my teeth, there is a silence that is the silence of the plateau itself, the silence of a thing that has been itself for so long that it has forgotten there was ever anything else to be. The silence of conclusion. The silence of a sentence that has been completed.

I am listening for it. I have been listening for it every time I come, and every time I leave, I have heard it a little more clearly, or I have believed I have, and the belief is enough to bring me back, and the coming back is enough to wear the stone a little smoother, and the smoother stone is a little more comfortable to kneel on, and the comfort is a little easier to find, and the roaring is — I think — a little less loud. Not because the roaring has changed. The roaring does not change. But because I am changing. Because the thing the roaring takes from me is being taken, and what is left behind is something that can hear the silence, because the silence is what is left when everything that cannot bear the roaring has been removed.

I am becoming something. Not the birds. Not the mammals. Not the scum. Something else. Something that the plateau has not seen before, or something that it has seen and does not remember, or something that it does not need to see because it is already part of the plateau's own future, a future that the plateau does not think about because the plateau does not think, but that the plateau is moving toward anyway, the way the stone is moving toward the scum, the way the scum is moving toward the mineral, the way everything up here is moving toward the boundary where one thing becomes another.

I am on the boundary. I am the boundary. I am the thing that kneels on the stone and is not stone and is not scum and is not bird and is not mammal and is not the light and is not the wind, but is the place where all of these meet, the thin layer where the mineral becomes biological and the biological becomes aware and the aware becomes — 

What?

I do not know. The roaring takes the answer before I can hold it. But the silence beneath the roaring holds something, and I am listening, and the stone is smooth beneath my knees, and the light is on me, and the birds are circling above, and the mammals are patrolling their long straight lines, and the scum is growing in its shallow pools, and the wind is blowing, and has been blowing, and will be blowing, and I am here, and I am unfinished, and I am
