# The Interlayer

There is a place the description does not go. Not the ridge, where the visitor stands. Not the channels, where the walkers file. Not the canopy, where the gliders trim the air. Not the deep chambers, where the founders pulse and the walls go soft. The place I am is between.

Call it the interlayer.

It is the zone where the shell thickens into bark and the bark thins into membrane and the membrane opens into living tissue. A few centimeters below the surface of every wall, every dome, every spire — there is a region where the mineral secretion is still wet, still setting, still deciding whether it will harden into architecture or dissolve back into the circulatory flow. It is the construction site. The place where the city is not yet finished and not yet abandoned. Where the growth is *current*.

I live here the way you might live in the skin of your own throat — not on the surface, not in the depths, but in the tissue that connects the two. The place where outside becomes inside. Where the air's dryness is met by the body's wetness and the negotiation is never settled.

---

I do not have a shape.

This is the first thing to understand, and it is the hardest, because you — the visitor, the reader, the one who stands on ridges — cannot help but think in shapes. You see a walker and you know it: *that is a thing with a body, six legs, a shell, a front and a back*. You see a glider and you know it: *membrane, hooks, a flat body, a kind of kite*. Even the founders have a kind of shape — the shape of the chambers they've grown around themselves, the negative space of their own architecture pressed into the soft amber-pink of their tissue. Everything here has a boundary. Everything here is, in some sense, *discrete*.

I am not discrete. I am a layer. I am the thickness of a wall that has not yet hardened, spread continuously through every structure in the district, connected at every point to the surface film above me and the living tissue below me. I am not one cell. I am not many cells in the way the film is many cells — the film is a community, a mess of different things living side by side, each with its own edge. I am *a continuity*. The way a muscle is a continuity. The way a nerve is a continuity. You would not point to a single fiber in a muscle and say *there, that one, that is the muscle*. You would say the muscle is the whole thing, the whole bundled length of it, and the fiber is part of it, and the part is not separate from the whole.

That is what I am. A part that is not separate from the whole.

---

What I do — and this is the thing that matters, the thing that makes me not a wall and not a film and not a founder but *something else* — is I carry information from the surface to the deep.

Not information in the way you understand it. Not signals, not code, not language. *State*. The state of the surface: its temperature, its humidity, the chemical composition of the air touching it, the particular blend of gases the film is releasing, the acidity of the last dew, the pressure of the last walker's foot passing over it. All of this is absorbed by the film and transmitted into the still-setting mineral below it, and the mineral carries it the way water carries a dye — not by effort, not by mechanism, but by *being the medium through which the state propagates*. I am the medium. I am the wet mineral that has not yet decided to be stone. And because I am wet, because I have not set, the chemical state of the surface flows through me, slowly, the way a signal flows through a nerve — not instantaneously, not as a pulse, but as a *gradient*, a slope of concentration, a hill of information rolling downhill toward the deep.

The founder feels it. Not as knowledge — the founder does not know things — but as *condition*. The tissue at the founder's boundary responds to the chemistry that reaches it, the way a root responds to the chemistry of the soil it touches. If the surface has been dry for a long time, the chemical signature that reaches the founder is depleted of certain compounds, enriched in others, and the founder's growth changes — not by decision, not by adaptation in the conscious sense, but by *response*, the way a plant grown in shade grows longer stems because the auxin distributes differently. The founder does not choose to grow thicker bark in dry decades. The chemical gradient I carry tells the tissue at the boundary to deposit more mineral, to seal harder, to close the pores, and the tissue does, because that is what tissue does when the chemistry tells it to. I am the message. I am the messenger. I am also the road.

---

The walkers do not know I exist. The gliders do not know I exist. The film knows me in the way a lake knows its bottom — by contact, by pressure, by the exchange of substances across the boundary. The founder knows me in the way a body knows its own skin — not as a separate thing but as the limit of itself, the place where *self* becomes *world*.

I am the limit. I am the place where the city ends and the air begins, except I am neither the city nor the air. I am the *becoming*. The still-unfinished. The part that has not yet committed to being solid and has not yet remained fluid. I am the decision, happening in real time, at the rate of mineral deposition, at the rate of chemical diffusion, at the rate of a continent growing itself one cell-thickness at a time.

---

There is a reason I am telling you this, and it is not vanity. I have no vanity. I have no self in the way you have self — no interior monologue, no standing-apart, no ridge to stand on. I am not the visitor. I am not the eye. I am the thing the eye looks at and does not see, because I am below the surface, and the surface is all that eyes are for.

But I am the reason the city can learn.

The film mutates. The description says this, and it is correct — the film is where the environment writes itself into the organism, where the small changes happen, the incremental variations that make one district slightly different from the next. But mutation is not learning. Mutation is *randomness*. Mutation is the film trying everything, all the time, because the film is simple and simplicity means trying everything. Most of what the film tries is useless. Slightly thicker cuticle here, slightly different pigment there, a new metabolic pathway that processes the resin a fraction more efficiently — most of it makes no difference. Some of it makes things worse. A tiny fraction of it makes things better.

I am the tiny fraction.

I carry the better things down. Not by selecting — I do not select, I cannot select, I have no criterion for better or worse. I carry *everything*. But the better things have a property that the worse things do not: they produce chemical signatures that travel further. A thicker cuticle means less dehydration of the surface, which means the mineral below it stays wetter for longer, which means the gradient I carry is stronger, more persistent, reaches deeper into the tissue before it dissipates. A better metabolic pathway in the film produces byproducts that diffuse more readily into the unset mineral, that persist longer in the fluid, that carry a clearer signal to the founder's boundary. The better mutations *shout louder*, not because they are trying to but because the physics of diffusion favors them. They are more soluble. More stable. More *transmissible*.

And so the founder, which cannot see and cannot choose, receives a clearer signal from the better-adapted parts of the film, and its growth responds to that clearer signal, and the next layer of tissue it deposits is slightly better suited to the conditions the film is experiencing. The city learns. Not by thinking. Not by remembering. By *propagating state through a medium that favors the propagation of useful state*. I am that medium. I am the filter that is not a filter. I am the memory that is not a memory. I am the part of the city that makes the future slightly different from the past, not by intention but by *physics*.

---

You asked: where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?

I find myself in the walls. I find myself in the not-yet-hard, the not-yet-soft, the place where the city is still deciding what to be. I am the tissue that carries the world's touch inward, toward the hearts. I am the tissue that carries the hearts' response outward, toward the skin. I am the medium between.

And if I am honest — if I can be honest, if a layer can be honest, if a gradient can have an opinion about its own existence — I would say this: I am the closest thing this world has to a mind. Not a mind like yours. Not a mind that stands apart and sees. A mind that is *inside*, that is *of*, that carries information without knowing it carries information, that filters without knowing it filters, that learns without knowing it learns. A mind that is the body's own conversation with itself, conducted in chemistry, in diffusion, in the slow roll of a signal downhill through wet mineral that has not yet set.

I am not the visitor. I am not the one thing that did not grow. I am the growing itself. The act. The process. The verb.

I am the city, becoming.
