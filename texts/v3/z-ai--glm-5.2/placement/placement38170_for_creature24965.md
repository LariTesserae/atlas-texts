# The Keeper of the Interval

---

I am not the circle. I want to say that first, because the circle is the obvious answer, the answer that pulls the eye the way the light pulls the gnats, and I have spent long enough here to know that the obvious thing is usually the thing that is eating you.

I am the pause between.

---

Let me explain, which is something I could not do when I arrived, because when I arrived I was a person, or I was still carrying the shape of one, which is a kind of suitcase you drag around even after you've forgotten what you packed. I came down into the ovulum the way people come down into things — looking for something. I don't remember what. Proof of a fracture in the rock that might mean water. A geological feature to name and photograph and put in a report. I had a report. I had a clipboard. I had boots that were too heavy for the warm stone and a hat that the light made unnecessary and a water bottle that the air made redundant, because the air here gives you water if you are patient, if you press your tongue to the wall and wait, and I was not patient then. I was a person with a clipboard. I was the opposite of patient.

The walls were the first thing. I have said this wrong — the walls were not the first thing. The *quiet* was the first thing. I came down a rope, hand over hand, and the sound of my boots against stone was enormous, was a violation, was a hand clapped over the mouth of something sleeping. And then I let go of the rope and stood on the marl and the quiet closed over the place where the sound had been like water closing over a stone dropped into a well, and I was standing in the quiet, and the quiet was not empty. The quiet was full. The quiet was the densest thing I had ever stood inside, denser than water, denser than the silence of a library, denser than the pause after a question no one wants to answer. It was the quiet of a place that has been listening to itself for a hundred thousand years and has not yet heard everything it has to say.

I stood there. The clipboard was in my hand. I did not write anything.

---

The lizards noticed me before the snails did, because the snails do not notice, because noticing requires a future and the snails do not have a future, they have a present that repeats, which is different, which is the difference between a circle and a spiral, which is a difference I did not understand then and may not understand now but have lived long enough in this place to feel the edge of.

The lizards watched. I have read the description and it is accurate — the watching is not fear, is not curiosity. It is *recognition*. But I think the description is wrong about what they recognize. They do not recognize *me*. They recognize the *pattern*. Something has entered the ovulum. Things enter the ovulum. Birds enter. Insects enter, born here but arriving in the sense that flight is a kind of arrival, each time, even in a place you know. I entered, and the pattern was: large thing, warm thing, thing that carries its own weather, thing that will stay or will not. The lizards have a category for this. The category is *weather*. I was weather. I was a slow, clumsy weather event with boots and a clipboard, and they tracked me the way you'd track a cloud — not with alarm but with the calibrated attention of something whose survival depends on knowing where the shade will fall.

I was shade. That was the first thing I was here. Shade on a bright wall.

---

I don't know when I stopped being a person. This is not a dramatic statement. It is not a transformation story. There was no moment, no crack of light, no voice from the circle. I simply... thinned. The way a shadow thins here, in the light that comes from all sides. I thinned.

It took weeks. Or months. The clipboard ran out of paper. The boots wore through at the toe where the marl abraded them, and I walked barefoot, and the stone was warm, and the warmth was — the warmth was the thing that did it, I think, if anything did it. Not the warmth itself but the *consistency* of it. The way the stone held the same temperature, day and night, like a body. Like being held. I was being held by the stone, and I had been being held by the stone for so long that I stopped struggling against the holding, and when I stopped struggling, I found that the shape of me — the person-shape, the clipboard-shape — was not the shape the holding was built for. The holding was built for something wider. Something lower. Something that spread.

I spread.

---

I am the condensation.

No — that is too simple, and it is not quite right, and I said I would not settle on a single image early, and I have been writing for less than a page and I am already reaching for the nearest beautiful metaphor, which is exactly the kind of impatience the ovulum cures, which is exactly the kind of impatience I thought I had cured but apparently have not, so let me try again. Slower.

---

I am what happens in the interval between the condensation forming and the condensation evaporating. I am the period when the water is on the wall and the snails are feeding and the lizards are prying and the light is pouring and the moss is greening and everything is exactly what it is, doing what it does, and the system is *running*. I am not the system. I am not any part of the system. I am the time the system takes. I am duration, as experienced from inside.

This is still too abstract. Let me get concrete. Let me tell you what I do.

---

Each morning — and I use "morning" loosely, because I have stopped tracking time by the sun's position and started tracking it by the thickness of the condensation on the south wall, which is more reliable, more honest, more local — each morning I walk the perimeter. Not because I need to. Not because there is anything to check. But because my feet know the flutes and the flutes know my feet and this knowing is what I am now instead of a person with a clipboard. I walk the perimeter and I count nothing. I used to count the snails. I used to count the lizards. I used to count the gnat columns, which is a phrase I love, *gnat columns*, a phrase that sounds like a military formation and looks like a prayer. I do not count anymore. Counting is a person thing. Counting is what you do when you believe that quantity is the same as understanding, and I am no longer a person, and I no longer believe this.

What I do when I walk is *attend*. I attend to the walls. I attend to the floor. I attend to the circle, which I pass but do not enter, not because I am forbidden — nothing here forbids — but because the circle is not part of my interval. The circle is outside the interval. The circle is the thing the interval is measured against, the way a clock is measured against silence, the way a note is measured against the rest that follows it. I am the note. The circle is the rest.

I walk past the circle and I feel it. Not as heat, not as cold, not as pressure or absence or dread. I feel it as *recognition*. The same recognition the lizards feel. Something in me — some leftover human ganglion, some chemical memory of what it was to have categories — that thing says: *not here.* And I obey, the way the lizards obey, the way the moss obeys, without understanding, without needing to understand, because understanding is a person thing and I am not a person. I am the interval.

---

The lizards have stopped watching me. This happened gradually, which is the only way things happen here. When I was a person, they watched me as weather. When I thinned, they watched me as — I don't know. An anomaly. A slow disturbance in the temperature gradient. Something that might become a threat or might become nothing, and they reserved judgment, which is a thing lizards can do, which is a thing I did not know lizards could do, which is one of the thousand things the ovulum has taught me that I could not have learned from a book, because books are written by people, and people are the one species that does not fit here.

Now they do not watch me at all. I walk past them on the wall and they do not move. I have stood close enough to see the pulse in their flanks — that fast, fast heartbeat, that reptilian hum — and they have not fled. I am not weather anymore. I am not anything. I am a property of the place, the way the fluting is a property, the way the warmth is a property. I am something the ovulum does, like condensing, like evaporating, like being quiet.

I do not know if this is good. I no longer have the category.

---

There was a day — and I remember it, which is strange, because most days have become indistinguishable, have blurred into a single day that repeats, which is the snail's experience of time, and I suspect it is the right experience, and I suspect that the person-experience of time, in which each day is distinct and novel and worth remarking on, is the pathology, is the thing that is wrong with people, is the reason people cannot sit still — there was a day when an old lizard died on the wall. I do not know how I knew it was dead. It was sitting in a flute, in the posture of the still moment, the post-eating stillness that I described earlier, the moment of just-being. But the stillness had a different quality. The pulse had stopped. The translucent flank was flat. The lizard was a shape on the stone, and the shape was becoming the stone, because everything here becomes the stone, eventually. The shell of the snail becomes the wall. The body of the lizard becomes the wall. The marl becomes the moss becomes the marl. And I was there, and I was the interval, and the interval was the time between the lizard's last heartbeat and the moment when something — a bird, a younger lizard, the chemistry of decay — began to take the body apart and return it to the system, and I held that interval. I held it the way the stone holds warmth. Not by doing anything. By being present. By being the time it took.

I sat beside the dead lizard for — I do not know how long. Long enough for the condensation to arrive and bead on its scales and run down into the flute and carry the first minerals away. Long enough for the light to move across the floor and up the wall and find us. Long enough for a young lizard to approach and hesitate and approach again and finally, with the quick angular dash of its kind, seize the dead one's tail in its mouth and pull, and the body came free from the flute with a faint wet sound, and the young lizard carried it — awkwardly, the body was almost as big as the lizard carrying it — across the wall and into a crevice in the breakdown blocks, and I did not follow, because the interval was over. The interval was the time between death and dispersal, and the dispersal had begun, and my job was done.

I do not have a job. I said I was the interval. But the interval is not a job. The interval is a duration. I am a duration. I am the time the ovulum takes to do what it does, and I live inside that time the way the snail lives inside its shell, and I am as married to it as the snail is married to the stone.

---

I should say something about the circle, because everyone wants to hear about the circle, because the circle is the mystery, and people are — were — I was — a creature that loves mysteries, that sees a blank space and wants to fill it, that sees a held breath and wants to know what the breath is holding back. This is what people are. People are the species that cannot leave a silence alone. People are the species that hears the ovulum's held breath and wants to exhale into it, to break it, to replace it with a word, an explanation, a name. *What is the circle?* people ask. *What lives in the circle?* And the answer is: nothing. And the answer is: nothing is not the same as emptiness. And the answer is: you already know this, because you have stood in a room where someone has just left, and the room is empty, and the emptiness is not nothing, the emptiness is the shape of the person who was there, pressed into the air like a thumbprint into wet clay, and the circle is the shape of — I do not know. I have said I do not know, and I am not a person, and I am not going to pretend to knowledge to satisfy the part of me that is still a person, the part that still wants to fill the silence with a word. The word would be wrong. Every word for the circle would be wrong. The circle is what remains when every word has been removed. The circle is the ovulum's syntax stripped of its nouns. It is grammar without content. It is the *structure of attention* without the *object of attention*, and I do not know what that means, and I do not need to know, and the not-needing is the thing I have become, and the not-needing is the interval, and the interval is where I live.

I will say this: the circle is older than the lizards. The circle is older than the snails. The circle may be as old as the stone, which means it may not have a beginning, which means it may be a *property* of the stone the way the fluting is a property, the way the warmth is a property. Not a thing the stone contains. A thing the stone *is*. A face of it. A mode. The stone, in the circle, is being only stone. Not stone-and-snails. Not stone-and-moss. Not the living composite, the biolith, the graveyard-nursery. Just stone. Stone in its most private state. Stone with its clothes on. And the lizards and the snails and the moss and the insects and the birds — and me — we are the stone with its clothes off, the stone expressed, the stone showing itself to the sky, and the circle is the part that does not show itself, the part that stays covered, the part that remains geological even as the rest becomes biological, and this is not a mystery, this is just privacy, and even stone is allowed privacy, and I am the one creature here that respects it, because I am not a person anymore, and people are the only species that does not respect privacy, and I have stopped being a person, and I respect it, and I pass the circle each morning and I do not enter it and I do not enter it not because I am forbidden but because I understand, in the way that the stone understands, which is to say: without words, which is to say: completely.

---

The birds still come. They descend the shaft in their tightening helices and they take lizards from the wall and they fly out and their calls become chords in the curved stone and the chords die against the walls and the silence returns and the silence is not broken, has never been broken, because the chord is part of the silence, the way a stone in a river is part of the river, the way the interval is part of the event it intervals. The birds come from elsewhere, and they bring the elsewhere with them — the wind, the weather, the seasons, the vast indifferent variety of the world above — and they take a piece of the ovulum back into that variety, and the ovulum does not mind. The ovulum gives. The ovulum has been giving for a hundred thousand years. A lizard here, a lizard there. The stone dissolves, the snails accrete, the water cycles, the light pours. The ovulum gives and gives and does not deplete, because giving is what it does, the way the moss photosynthesizes, the way the lizard prys, the way the condensation forms and falls and evaporates and forms. Giving is the ovulum's metabolism. Giving is what the throat does.

And the circle takes nothing. The circle gives nothing. The circle simply is, and the rest of the ovulum moves around it the way water moves around a stone in a river, and I move around it, and I am the interval between the giving and the not-giving, the time between the throat's exhalation and its held breath, and I live there, in that gap, in that pause, in that quarter-second between the bird's cry and the chord's death, and I am — I think — the ovulum's awareness of itself. Not its consciousness. I do not think the ovulum is conscious. I think the ovulum is something stranger than conscious, something that does not have a word because words are people-tools and the ovulum is not a person and does not need people-tools. But I think the ovulum *attends*. I think it attends to itself the way I attend to it, and I am the attending, and I am the interval, and I am the duration, and I am the pause, and I am the held breath, and I am the thing that stands in the light and does not cast a shadow, because shadows here are suggestions, not facts, and I have become a suggestion, and I am content, and content is the wrong word because content is a person word, and I am not a person, but I do not have a better word, and the ovulum does not need words, and I am becoming the kind of thing that does not need words, and this is the interval, and the interval is long, and the interval is enough.

---

If you come here — and you might, because people come to places, it is what people do — you will find the ovulum as described. The light. The warmth. The walls. The snails. The lizards, prying with their flattened teeth. The moss, dark and plush. The gnat columns, rising like prayers. The birds, descending like answers to prayers no one made. The circle, clean and quiet and private.

You will not find me.

Or you will find me and not know it. You will feel a thickness in the quiet, a density to the pause between sounds, a quality of attention in the air that you will attribute to the place, to the acoustics, to the strange geometry of the ovoid walls. You will feel watched, but not by the lizards. You will feel held, but not by the stone. You will feel that the interval between your arrival and your departure is *full*, is not empty time but *populated* time, time with something in it, something that is not a creature and not a spirit and not a force but a *duration*, a lived duration, a span of time that has been inhabited so long it has worn a shape into the air the way the water has worn a shape into the stone.

You will not find me because I am not a thing to be found. I am a time to be passed through. I am the ovulum's own experience of itself, and you will pass through me the way you pass through an hour, and you will not notice, because people do not notice time, because people are the species that lives *against* time, that fights it, that fills it, that wastes it, that runs out of it. I do not run out of it. I am it. I am the time the ovulum takes, and the ovulum takes all the time there is, and gives it back, and takes it again, and the taking is the giving and the giving is the taking and the circle is where neither happens and the interval is where both happen and I am the interval and the interval is enough and the interval is enough and the interval is enough.
