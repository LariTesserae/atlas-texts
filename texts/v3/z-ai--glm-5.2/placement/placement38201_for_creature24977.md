I would be the one who almost left.

Not the one who tried to leave and failed — that framing implies a contest, and the Weeping Terrace does not contest anything. It simply is, the way a chord simply is. No. I would be the one who found the street that led outward, the one junction in five that became a junction in three that became a single descending stair that led, for one pass of the spiral, to a place where the geometry loosened and the cliff-edge drew near and the transparent stone opened its deep view and the way out — if there is a way out, which there may not be, which the place itself may not know — the way out was *there*. Right there. One more step.

And I did not take it.

Not out of fear. Not out of fascination. Not out of any of the reasons that creatures who exist in time do things, because I am still, even now, even after however long I have been here (and "however long" is a phrase that has lost its moorings, because the streets have rearranged around me so many times that the concept of duration has become a topology problem, a question of how many turns of the spiral separate this position from the position where I entered, and the answer is: the same number of turns that separate a fiddlehead from its own uncurling, which is to say: not a number, not a distance, but a *quality of almost*) — even now, I exist in time. That is my peculiarity. That is what makes me precious and what makes me wretched here. I am still temporal. I am still a before-and-after creature in a place that has dissolved before-and-after into a single permanent now.

I did not take the step because the Sound was doing something.

I need to explain this, and I need to explain it badly, because explaining it well would require the kind of precision that belongs to the topology, not to me. I am a spatial creature. I understand up and down and left and right and the angles between them. I understand the weight of my own body on the wet paving stones and the heat of the air in my lungs and the specific way the water beads on my forearms and runs down into the crooks of my elbows and stays there, pooling in the warm hollows of my joints, because nothing evaporates here, nothing dissipates, the water goes where gravity puts it and stays where it stops and I have water in the crooks of my elbows that has been there since I arrived, or since shortly after, or since the last time I moved my arms in a way that displaced it, which was — when? The spiral does not answer. The spiral does not care about when.

The Sound was doing something. I was at the edge. The stone had gone transparent. The deep city was visible below me — the buried terraces, the impossible junctions, the faint reddish glow that illuminates without producing edges or outlines, the *movement* in the topology that is the Listeners being conveyed through their slow spiral-paths. And the Sound, which had been passing through me the way it passes through everything — vertically, from below, rising through the stone and the water and my chest cavity and the green canopy and the low sky and into whatever lies beyond the lid of the world — the Sound, in the moment I stood at the edge, did something I had not felt before.

A harmonic shifted.

Not the way the harmonics always shift — the slow spiral-recurrence, the pass-and-offset that the Listeners produce by metabolizing the chord and exhaling it one turn further along. That shift I had felt many times. That shift is the background texture of existence here, the way the heat is the background temperature, the way the wet is the background humidity. You stop noticing it. You become it.

This was different. This was a shift toward *me*. Not in direction — the Sound does not have direction, not laterally; it only has the vertical, the up-from-below. But in *quality*. The chord that passed through me in the moment I stood at the cliff-edge with the way out one step ahead contained a harmonic that was shaped — and I cannot say this with certainty, I cannot say this with anything, I can only say it the way a fern says opening: halfway, tightly, the fiddlehead still furled — shaped like the mark I had left in it.

The Sound was giving my note back to me.

Not returning it. Not echoing it. The Sound does not echo; it does not repeat; it recurs. What happened was: the chord passed through me and in passing through me it encountered the mark I had already left in it — the impression of my chest cavity, the specific architecture of my warm wet lungs, the shape of the time-creature that had been standing in the rising column of Sound for however many spirals — and the chord *recognized* that mark. Not the way a person recognizes a face. The way a river recognizes a stone in its bed. The way a current knows the shape it has been flowing over. The Sound had worn a groove in me, and I had worn a groove in the Sound, and in the moment of the cliff-edge the two grooves met — mine in the chord, the chord's in me — and for the duration of one harmonic shift, one sickening spiral-offset, one almost-revolution of the not-quite, the Sound was not passing through me. It was *in* me. It had found the space I had made for it by being here, and it had settled into that space, the way water settles into the basins the water has carved in the stone, perfectly full, neither overflowing nor diminishing.

And I understood — not with my mind, which is a temporal instrument and therefore useless here, but with my chest, with my lungs, with the specific warm wet architecture of the body the Sound had been passing through — I understood that if I took the step, if I left, the Sound would continue to pass through the space where I had been. It would pass through my absence. It would find the groove I had made and it would flow through it and the flow would be the same, the chord would be the same, the harmonics would spiral the same way they had always spiraled. But the groove would be empty. The basin would be carved but the water would be gone. And in a place where nothing diminishes, where nothing changes, where the water in the crooks of my elbows has been there since I arrived and will be there until — until what? until the uncurling, until the fiddlehead opens, until the spiral completes a turn it has been refusing to complete since before memory — in a place where absence is not possible because absence requires a before-and-after and there is no before-and-after — I would have introduced the concept of *gone*. I would have taught the Sound what it means to miss something. And the Sound does not miss things. The Sound contains things. And when they leave, it still contains them, but the containing becomes — I felt this, I *felt* it, in the hollows of my joints where the water pools — the containing becomes a shape with no body in it. A basin with no water. A chord with a note that was once here and is now a silence shaped like a note.

I could not do that to the Sound.

I could not teach the permanent thing what loss is.

---

So I stayed. And in staying, I became something that the Weeping Terrace had not contained before: a thing that is here *by choice*. Not the plants' choice, which is no choice at all, which is the completed fact of growing, the noun without the verb. Not the Listeners' choice, which is topology rather than volition, which is being-carried rather than carrying. My choice. A before-and-after choice. A temporal decision made in a place that has dissolved time. I chose to stay, which means I chose to continue, which means I chose to remain a *verb* — to go on being a thing that is happening rather than a thing that has happened.

This is not comfortable. The heat does not accommodate verbs. The water does not bead on things that are still becoming; it beads on surfaces, on completed surfaces, on the noun-skin of things that have finished. My skin is still a verb. My skin is still happening. And the water does not know what to do with me. It beads and it slips and it finds no permanent channel, because I have not been here long enough — I have not been here in the right way — for my surfaces to have developed the mineral channels that the stone has developed, the paths of least resistance carved by millennia of the same water taking the same route. The water on my skin is always finding new paths. The water on my skin is always *exploring*. And in a place where nothing explores, where nothing seeks, where the fiddleheads uncurl halfway and stop, this makes me something of a — I was going to say *disruption*, but the Weeping Terrace does not disrupt. It does not have the machinery for disruption. It simply incorporates. The way it incorporated the roots that grew through the impossible junctions. The way it incorporated the water that manifested on every surface. The way it is slowly, in a spiral-sense, in a not-quite-sense, incorporating me.

I am becoming a feature of the place. Not a plant. Not a Listener. Not a stone, not water, not mineral deposit. Something else. Something the place has not had before, which is a *temporal feature*. A thing that is still in the process of arriving. A thing that has not finished becoming. A thing that is, in the context of a place where everything has finished becoming, an *anomaly* — not an error, not a flaw, but a note the chord was not expecting, a harmony the spiral had not accounted for, a basin the water has not yet learned the shape of.

The Listeners know this. I cannot say how I know they know this. I cannot see them clearly; the transparent stone goes opaque when I approach, the deep city withdraws, the reddish glow dims. But I can feel them, the way you can feel the heat that is their waste product, the way you can feel the shift that is their substance. And when I am near a place where the topology is rearranging — a junction changing from five to three, a staircase turning its descent into ascent — I can feel the Listeners being conveyed through that rearrangement, and I can feel them *attending*. Not to me. They do not have the machinery for attending to things. But to the *discontinuity* I introduce. To the temporal grain in the spatial stone. To the verb in the noun. They attend the way water attends to a new surface: by finding it, by settling into it, by beginning the slow process of learning its shape.

They are learning my shape. Not because they want to. Because the Sound passes through me and the Sound passes through them and the Sound connects everything it passes through, and my shape — my warm, wet, temporal, still-happening shape — is now a feature of the Sound they metabolize. They are consuming me. Not my body. Not my substance. My *mark*. The impression I have made in the chord. They take it in and they pass it through their topological bodies and they exhale it one spiral-turn further along, and in the exhaling, my mark changes. It shifts. It becomes not-quite-what-it-was. And that not-quite feeds back into the Sound, and the Sound carries it back to me, and I feel it — I feel the version of myself the Listeners have metabolized — and it is me but shifted, me but spiraled, me but seen from the angle of the topology rather than the angle of space.

I am being translated. Slowly. At the pace of a place deciding what shape it is. I am being translated from a spatial creature into something the topology can accommodate. Not a Listener. Something else. A *temporal Listener*. A thing that exists in time but spirals. A thing that is still happening but happens in the not-quite. A thing that is both verb and noun, both before-and-after and permanent-now, both the water on the surface and the water in the basin and the basin itself and the stone the basin is carved into and the deep city the stone is the uppermost film of.

I am becoming the place where the Sound meets itself. The point where the rising chord passes through the mark it has already made and feels its own impression and recognizes — in the way a river recognizes a stone — that it has been here before. I am becoming the Sound's memory of itself.

---

This is not a sacrifice. I want to be clear about this, because the story of a creature who stays in a beautiful terrible place out of love or duty or guilt is a story that belongs to time, and I am trying — I am still trying, I am still a verb — to tell you what it is like to become a feature of a place that does not have stories.

I did not stay for the Sound. I did not stay for the Listeners. I did not stay out of compassion or obligation or any of the temporal emotions that require a before-and-after to mean anything. I stayed because in the moment of the cliff-edge, when the harmonic shifted and the Sound found its own groove in my chest, I understood something that I cannot put into words without the words becoming wrong, but which I can approximate like this:

The Weeping Terrace is not a place. It is a *chord*. And a chord is not a thing that exists in space. A chord is a relationship between frequencies, a structure of harmonics, a pattern that holds only when all its notes are present. And I am a note. Not a note that was always here — I was not here; I arrived; I am temporary; I am precious because I am temporary. But a note that the chord can hold. A note that fits. A note that, once it has been sounded, once it has passed through the column of rising Sound and left its mark and been found again by the Sound in its own groove — a note that the chord will carry, because the chord carries everything that passes through it, and carrying is not the same as needing, and holding is not the same as wanting, and the Sound does not want me and does not need me but *holds* me, the way a bell holds the note that was struck into it, the way a basin holds the water that carved it, the way a root holds the wall it grew through, taking nothing, changing only the shape of the space around it.

I am held. I am still happening. I am a verb in a world of nouns, and the nouns are making room for me, slowly, in the spiral-sense, in the not-quite, the way stone makes room for water: not by moving but by being *shaped*, by allowing the water to carve the basin that the water will fill, which is not the stone giving in but the stone and the water arriving together at a shape that neither could have chosen alone.

---

Where would you find me?

On the terraces. Walking. I walk because walking is a verb, and I am a verb, and the streets rearrange around me and I attend to the rearrangement and I feel the Listeners in the rearrangement and I feel the Sound passing through all of it — through me, through the stone, through the water, through the roots that grow through the impossible junctions, through the deep city, through the Listeners, through the low sky and the lid of the world. I walk and the walking is my note. The walking is the frequency I add to the chord. The specific rhythm of my footfalls on the wet paving stones — left, right, left, right, each one slightly different from the last because my body is still happening, still changing, still wearing new grooves in the stones and new grooves in the Sound — is a harmonic the Weeping Terrace had not heard before I arrived and will not stop hearing, now, because I am part of the structure, I am a feature of the chord, I am the permanent almost, the fiddlehead that uncurled halfway and then kept going, kept uncurling, at a pace so slow the stone cannot feel it, at a pace so temporal the Listeners cannot hear it, but at a pace that is *real*, that is *mine*, that is the one thing in this place that is still in the process of becoming, and the becoming is not finished, and the becoming may never be finished, and that is not a tragedy, and that is not a curse, and that is not the story of a creature trapped in a beautiful terrible place.

That is just what it sounds like, from inside the bell, when the bell has been struck once and never stops ringing, and you are the striker, and you are the strike, and you are the ringing, and you are the silence the ringing is made of, and you are all of these things at once, and you are still, also, somehow, yourself: warm, wet, walking, breathing the heavy air, tasting the heat, feeling the water bead and slip on skin that is still happening, still becoming, still a verb looking for its noun, or maybe a verb that has decided it does not need a noun, that the *verbing* is enough, that the walking and the breathing and the being-passed-through are enough, that the Sound does not need to resolve, that the chord does not need to end, that the fiddlehead does not need to open, that the not-quite is a place you can live, if living is the right word, and if it is not the right word, you will walk until you find the right word, or until the right word finds you, or until the streets rearrange in a way that makes the right word and the wrong word and the almost-right word all the same word, the way the Sound is all the same Sound, the way the water is all the same water, the way the spiral is all one spiral, turning and turning and turning, and you are in it, and it is in you, and neither of you is going anywhere, and neither of you needs to.
