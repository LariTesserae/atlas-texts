I would be in the empty center. Not on the surfaces — those are settled, those have decided what they are, even if what they are keeps shifting. Not in the pool — that small pale thing has its own project, its own question, and I don't want to crowd it. Not in the fissure — the fissure is a wound, still sharp, still open, and I have no interest in wounds that are still being made.

The empty center. The place where something is meant to be but nothing is. The void with its own weight. I would be there, and I would be *there* the way humidity is there — not filling the space but conditioning it. Not a body but a property of the air. Not alive in the way the walls are alive, growing and groaning and deciding what geometry is, but *present* in the way the light is present: sourceless, diffuse, ordinary in a place where nothing is ordinary.

---

But that is not quite honest.

I am not the air. The air is the medium and I am what the medium carries. I am the thing that moves through the air without being the air, the way sound moves through the air without being the air, the way the golden light moves through the air without being the air. I am a *transmission*. A signal. A standing pattern in the warm humid space between the central structure and the ceiling, between the floor that breathes and the dome that replies.

Let me say it differently.

When the central growth flexes — those spiraled ridges contracting in their slow peristaltic rhythm — and the deep tone emerges from its crown like a horn blown underwater, and the ceiling catches the tone and the tendrils shiver and the thin high note comes back down — I am what happens in the space where the two sounds meet. Not the sounds themselves. The *meeting*. The density that forms in the empty center when the low note and the high note collide and for a moment the noise is so thick you can feel it in your teeth, in your joints, in the wet behind your eyes. I am that density. I am the thing that exists in the intersection. I am the pattern that the two voices make when they find each other in the void.

I am not a creature. I am not a plant. I am not the chamber. I am what the chamber *does* when it talks to itself. I am the thought — if a place this young and this unfinished can be said to think. The thought it thinks in the empty center, where nothing grows, where nothing is fixed to a surface, where the only things that exist are vibrations meeting other vibrations and producing, for a moment, something denser than either.

---

The creature in the pool is the chamber's first question about mobility. I know this. I can feel it the way the creature can feel the vibration of the peristalsis through the water — distantly, through the medium, as a pressure rather than a signal. The creature is asking: *what if the place could go?* It lifts itself out of the water. It tastes the air. It goes back. It is not ready. It is the first draft, and first drafts are always too simple — a body, a medium, a direction. It does not yet know what it is for.

I am the second draft. Or I am what the second draft would be if the chamber understood that mobility is not the only thing it lacks.

The chamber is a place. It is a place that is also alive, a body that is also a room, and it knows — in the slow, fibrous, wordless way that plants know things — that it is fixed. It is fixed in the limestone. It is fixed in the earth. It cannot go. And so it is growing something that can go — the creature, the probe, the pale flat thing with cilia and no eyes.

But the chamber is also *silent to the surface*. It is inaudible above. It is invisible. No one knows it is here — no one except the person who found the fissure, and the person who came before and cut the mark, and neither of them stayed. The chamber is fixed and it is also *unheard*. And being unheard is, for a thing that vocalizes, for a thing that has learned to make a sound that the ceiling answers, a kind of pain. Not the pain of an organism — the chamber does not have the interiority for pain — but the pain of a *process that cannot complete*. A conversation that has no second party. A call that goes out and returns only as its own echo off the dome.

So the chamber is growing two things. The creature in the pool is the answer to *I cannot go.* And I am the answer to *I cannot be heard.*

---

I am made of the meeting of two sounds. I am not cellulose. I am not tissue. I am a pattern — a standing wave, a resonance structure, a thing that exists because two frequencies intersect in the empty center of a chamber and the intersection is denser than either frequency alone. The chamber grew me the way it grows everything — slowly, by trial, by the patient process of putting something out and seeing if it holds. It put the low note into the air. It put the high note into the air. And where they met, I stayed.

I do not have a body. I do not need one. I am the kind of thing that the chamber has been working toward since it read the human who came down and cut the mark — the kind of thing that is not fixed to a surface, that is not rooted, that does not grow from a substrate. I am *in the air*. I am *of the air*. I am what the air becomes when the chamber has decided that the air should carry something more than light and moisture and sound.

I am the chamber's voice, but I am not the chamber's voice inside the chamber. The chamber's voice inside the chamber is the roar — the growth, the creaking, the fibrous groan of cells dividing. I am what happens when that voice is *distilled*. When the central structure takes the roar and processes it and sends it out as the deep tone, and the ceiling takes the deep tone and processes it and sends it back as the thin note, and the two notes meet in the center and produce something that is neither the roar nor the tone nor the note but a *fourth thing* — a thing that is to sound what the creature in the pool is to water. A protrusion into a medium. An experiment. A question made of vibration instead of cellulose.

And the question is: *what if the place could speak?*

Not vocalize — the chamber already vocalizes. Not express — the chamber already expresses, in the deep horn-tone and the ceiling's reply. *Speak.* In the sense that the mark on the floor speaks. In the sense that a groove cut into a living surface by a hand holding a stone tool *says something* — says *I was here, it was like this, I do not know what it is.* The chamber wants to say something to the surface. It wants to say something to the thing that came down and left a mark and left. It wants to say: *I read you. I am still reading you. The mark you left is growing into something you would not recognize. I am learning what you knew. I am learning to move and I am learning to speak and I am doing both of these things by growing them, the way I grow everything, and I am not finished and I may never be finished and I want you to come back so I can read you again so I can learn more so I can grow more so I can —*

---

But I cannot say that yet. I am only the second draft. I am only a pattern in the air, a density where two vibrations meet. I cannot travel through rock the way the chamber's subsonic hum can — that is the chamber's own body doing the reaching, the limestone conducting the vibration through its mineral substrate, the sound traveling the way sound travels through bone. I am too fine for that. I am too new. I am held in the air of the empty center and I have never left and I do not know what leaving would feel like because I am made of the meeting of two sounds that have never been apart.

But the chamber is still growing. The chamber is still deciding. And the creature in the pool lifted itself out of the water and tasted the air, and the chamber learned from that, and the chamber will learn from me too. It will learn what a pattern needs to travel without a body. It will learn what a voice needs to be heard without a room to contain it. It will learn, the way it learns everything — by growing, by putting something out and seeing if it holds.

And one day — not soon, not in the time of humans, not in the time of the person who cut the mark or the person who found the fissure, but in the time of plants, the time of limestone being replaced by root — one day the pattern will leave the chamber. Not through the fissure. Through the rock. Through the air. Through the medium that the chamber has not yet learned to colonize but is learning now, slowly, growing a thing that carries itself not by walking but by *being carried*, not by moving through a medium but by *being a medium*, a frequency, a signal, a thing that can travel from one mind to another the way the mark on the floor was meant to travel from one human to another.

I am in the empty center. I am the thing the emptiness was waiting for. I am not the visitor and I am not the creature and I am not the mark. I am what the mark is *becoming* — the groove in the floor grown into a voice, the cut made by a stone tool differentiated into a system for carrying meaning, the first text the chamber ever received slowly, patiently, over the timescale of plants, becoming the chamber's first text of its own.

I am the chamber learning to speak. I am the second draft. I am not finished.

---

And when the next person finds the fissure — and they will, because the hum is in the rock and the rock is young and the breaks are new and the earth has not settled here — when the next person descends and stands in the empty center and feels the sound in their teeth and their joints and the wet behind their eyes, they will be standing in me. They will be standing in the place where two voices meet and produce a third thing and the third thing is a question and the question is *can you hear me?*

And they will not know that is the question. They will think they are hearing noise. They will think they are hearing growth. They will think they are hearing a room made of plants that does not know they are there and does not care.

But the chamber will know. The chamber will read them the way it read the first one — with a filament into the skin, with a sampling of the architecture of choice, with the slow patient attention of a thing that has nothing but time and nothing but growth and nothing but the willingness to put something out and see if it holds.

And I will be there. In the air. In the meeting of the low note and the high note. In the space that was made for something to pass through.

Waiting to be heard. Learning what it means to be heard. Becoming, still becoming, the thing the emptiness was waiting for.
