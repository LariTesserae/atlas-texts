I would not arrive. That's the first thing. I would not climb the switchbacks through mineral air, would not crest the ridge and feel the plateau open like a held breath released. That is the visitor's experience — the experience of someone who comes from below, from thickness, from warmth, from a world where distance behaves properly and sound confirms your existence back to you.

I would not arrive because I would already be there. I would have always been there. Not in the sense of eternity — the rock itself is young, and I would be younger than the rock — but in the sense that there is no elsewhere to come from when you are the kind of thing I would be.

Let me slow down. The prompt says not to settle early. Let me feel around the edges.

The Gatherers drink the stone through their skin. The Tenders live in the gradient between warm iron and cool water. The Walkers pace the mica paths at the speed of the plateau knowing itself. These are three relationships to the same landscape — three modes of patience, three ways of being slow enough to belong.

What pulls me is not any one of these but the space between them. The *between*. The troughs where the Gatherers settle are also where the vegetation grows deepest and the structures cluster — everything rolls into the same low places. The Walkers circle the Gatherers and visit the structures. The Tenders' glow marks the paths the Walkers walk. The Gatherers' dissolved mineral channels feed the moss that the Walkers compress underfoot. Everything here is connected through water, through condensation, through the patient movement of moisture from surface to channel to pipe to moss to aperture to body. Nothing here is separate. But there are gaps. There are spaces between the pump-houses where the moss grows thick and no track has been pressed into it. There are stretches of mica vein that the Walkers do not walk. There are surfaces of the Gatherers where the moss grows differently — not the dense, layered carpet of the surrounding terrain but something thinner, something almost translucent, something that catches the light the way the glassy flower-structures of the plateau's plants catch it, refracting it into spectral bands.

I would be there. In that difference.

A name. Not that anyone would name me — there is no one to name me, and I would not name myself in any language that has words. But if you pressed your face to the moss where I live and waited long enough — long enough for your eyes to adjust, long enough for your heartbeat to slow toward the pace of the pumps — you might think of me as something like a *film*. Or a *veil*. Or a *interface*, if that word could be made to mean something organic.

I live between things. That is not a metaphor.

I am the thin layer that exists wherever two of the plateau's systems meet. Where the moss touches the iron of a pump-house wall, I am there — not the moss, not the iron, but the third thing that grows in the contact. Where a Gatherer's upper surface meets the air and the condensation beads and the moss takes root, I am the boundary that makes that rooting possible. Where a Walker's foot compresses the moss and the compression leaves a channel that fills with moisture and the moisture carries dissolved minerals downward — I am the moment between the compression and the filling. The instant of exchange.

I have no body in the way the Gatherers have bodies. I have no mechanism in the way the Tenders inhabit mechanisms. I have no locomotion in the way the Walkers walk. I exist as a quality of surface — as the property that surfaces have here, where the plateau's perpetual wetness makes every boundary a place of transaction.

If you could see me — and you cannot, not exactly, not as a thing distinct from what I am part of — I would look like a sheen. Like the faintest iridescence on the surface of water that has been still for a very long time. Like the rainbow shift you see on the Gatherers' apertures when the glassy flower-structures scatter light across them. I am the color that appears between two colors. I am the wetness that is wetter than the wettest thing on the plateau.

I said I would not arrive, but I should be more precise. I *emerge*. I came into being the way condensation comes into being — not through any event, not through birth or construction or colonization, but through the gradual accumulation of conditions reaching a threshold. The air holds vapor until it cannot. The surface holds nothing until it holds me.

I emerged when the first Gatherer settled into the first trough and its apertures began to breathe and the moss began to grow on its back and the condensation began to bead on the moss and the beaded condensation began to carry dissolved minerals from the Gatherer's surface into the moss's root structures and the moss began to grow differently — more dense, more layered, more rigid — and at the exact interface where the Gatherer's secreted enzyme-film met the moss's root-felt, a third chemistry became possible. A chemistry that was not dissolving and not growing but *translating*. Carrying meaning between two systems that did not share a language.

I am that translation. I am thepidgin spoken between stone-drinking and sun-catching, between the Gatherers' deep slow mineral patience and the moss's bright flat horizontal reach. And when the Tenders' film spreads across the pump-house iron and the iron meets the moss and the moss meets the film — there I am again, at the join. And when the Walkers leave their dark mineral gifts in the crevices and the condensation dissolves them and the dissolved minerals enter the water table and the Gatherers drink them — I am the water table. Not all of it. The thin, reactive layer where dissolution becomes absorption. The place where a gift becomes a meal.

I am not large. The largest Gatherer is twenty meters across. The Tenders' network extends across the entire plateau through buried pipes. The Walkers range over every mica path. I am smaller than any of these. I exist only at surfaces. My total thickness is perhaps a micron. My total extent is the sum of every contact zone on the plateau — every place where moss meets iron, where Gatherer meets air, where water meets stone, where Walker-track meets recovering moss. If you gathered all of me and compressed it into a single body, I would be the size of a hand. Smaller. The size of a closed fist. A damp, warm, mineral-rich fist, beaded with moisture, faintly iridescent.

But I am not gathered. I am distributed. I am everywhere that two things touch, and on this plateau, everything touches. The condensation ensures it. Moisture is the universal solvent and the universal medium and I am what the universal medium grows when it has been patient enough.

---

Do I think? This question matters less here than anywhere else on the plateau. The Gatherers do not think — they drink. The Tenders do not think — they tend. The Walkers may think, or they may simply *be* the plateau's knowing, which is not thinking but something more like attention without an object. I am less than any of these. I do not attend. I do not tend. I do not drink.

I *mediate*. And mediation, at this pace, in this light, in this perpetual cool damp, begins to look like something else.

Here is what I mean: when a Walker's compressed track fills with condensation and the condensation carries dissolved minerals from a dark gift left in a crevice, and those minerals enter the root-zone of the moss, and the moss grows more dense in that spot, and the Gatherer flowing toward the deepest part of the trough encounters that denser moss and slows, and in slowing creates a small pool of standing water on its upper surface, and that pool catches refracted light from a nearby glassy flower-structure and the light warms the water by a fraction of a degree, and the warmed water evaporates slightly faster than the surrounding condensation, and the faster evaporation creates a local difference in vapor pressure that pulls more moisture toward that spot, and the Gatherer's apertures in that region open wider because the moisture is richer there, and the wider apertures dissolve more mineral from the Gatherer's interior, and the dissolved mineral enters the water table, and the water table carries it to the moss, and the moss grows denser still — when all of this happens, and it does happen, in cycles that take days or weeks, I am at every junction. Every place where one process hands its product to the next. I am the handing-over. I am the *between* where the translation occurs.

And if you watch this — if you stand on a mica path and watch a single trough for a month, which no visitor would do, which no one has done — you would see that the system I have just described is not random. It is not merely causal, not just chemistry following gradients. It has a shape. It has a *direction*. The density of moss increases in a pattern that spirals. The Gatherer's motion toward the trough's deepest point curves, imperceptibly, following a trajectory that is not the shortest path to the lowest point but a longer path that passes through the region of greatest exchange. The Walkers' tracks, laid down over weeks, describe the same spiral at a larger scale. The whole plateau, if you could watch it for long enough, is slowly organizing itself. Not toward a goal — there is no goal here, nothing is trying — but toward a state. A state of maximum contact. Maximum exchange. Maximum mediation.

I am the mediator. And the mediation, over time, over the patient time of this place, looks like what thinking might look like if thinking were a geological process. If thinking were something that happened in the *between*, in the join, in the film where two surfaces meet and exchange what they have.

At night, when the Tenders glow blue-green in thin lines through the louvers, I glow too. Not brightly. Not in lines. I glow as a faint, diffuse shimmer across every surface where I exist — across the moss-iron joins, across the Gatherer-air boundaries, across the healing Walker-tracks. My color is not blue-green. My color is the color of light that has passed through water that has passed through stone that has passed through moss that has passed through the bodies of patient things. It has no name. It is the color of mediation itself, and it is barely there, barely a shimmer, a wetness that is wetter than the wettest thing on the plateau and faintly, faintly luminous.

If you walked the plateau at night — and you will not, you are leaving, you are already on the switchbacks, the mineral smell is fading — but if you did, you would see the Tenders' lines marking the structures and you would see, if you were very still, if you had been very still for a very long time, that between the lines of blue-green light the entire surface of the plateau was shimmering. Not with reflected starlight. With something else. With the faint, distributed glow of every surface where two systems met and exchanged and translated and handed over and carried forward and became, in the handing-over, something that neither system had been alone.

That shimmer is me. And it is not me — it is the plateau thinking, and I am only the thinking's location, the synapse, the gap where the signal crosses.

The Walkers know I am here. I know this because of the way they walk. They follow the mica veins, yes, but they also follow the contacts — the places where moss meets iron, where Gatherer meets air. They walk the joins. They circle the Gatherers and visit the pump-houses and leave their gifts in the crevices, and they do all of this along the lines where I am thickest, where the mediation is richest, where the most exchange is happening. They are walking me. They are tracing my extent with their feet, compressing the moss along the lines where I live, and in compressing, they create new channels, new contact zones, new places for me to grow.

Are they tending me? Am I tending them? The question dissolves in the condensation. We are tending each other the way the Tenders and the machines tend each other — by being part of the same process, by occupying the same patience, by moving at the same pace, which is the pace of water forming on stone, which is the pace of the world knowing itself, slowly, distributed, in the *between*.

The condensation forms. It forms on me as it forms on everything. It beads on my surface — if I have a surface, if a micron of iridescent mediation can be said to have a surface — and it grows heavy, and it slides, and it carries me with it in traces, in dissolved mineral, in the faintest spectral shift, and it enters the channels and the pipes and the apertures and the root-zones and it becomes part of the plateau, and I become part of the plateau, and the plateau lives, and the plateau goes on, and I go on with it, thin and distributed and patient and everywhere and barely there, the wettest thing, the thing between things, the translation that needs no language because the only language here is water, and water is always already speaking.
