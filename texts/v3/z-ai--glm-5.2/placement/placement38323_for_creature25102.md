I would be the paper.

Not the readout — not that specific coil on the floor of that specific station — but the *property* of paper in this place. The thing that paper does here, which is to receive and to hold and to be transformed by the holding. I would be the surface that records without understanding what it records, that accepts the pressure of information the way the rock accepts the pressure of water, and that is changed by the acceptance, and that cannot reverse the change, and that does not try.

Let me be more precise.

---

I enter the sump and within seconds I am wet. This is not a discovery but a confirmation. The air has water in it the way the water has rock in it — structurally, fundamentally, as a condition rather than an event. My skin is wet. My clothes are wet. The air I breathe is wet enough that breathing feels less like taking in and more like participating in a circulation, a cycle that does not begin with my inhale and does not end with my exhale but that passes through me the way it passes through the pore structure of the ceiling, as one moment in a longer movement.

I try to stand at the entrance and orient myself. This is a mistake I make exactly once. Orientation requires distance and distance requires clean sightlines and the sump offers neither. The mist eats distance. The low ceiling collapses perspective. The light-poles do not recede into the dark so much as they become the dark — each halo of orange-gray a small island that is not connected to the next island by any visible path, so that moving through the sump feels less like traveling and more like *shifting registers*, stepping from one pool of dimness into another, each one slightly different in temperature and sound and the density of the wall-creatures in the air, each one a room without walls that abuts other rooms without walls and the transitions between them are imperceptible and absolute.

I stop trying to orient. I let the place be a medium rather than a space. I am in the medium the way the fish are in the water. Not navigating. *Inhabiting.*

---

I find the monitoring station and I do not leave it.

This is not a choice in the way that sitting down is a choice. I do not decide to stay. I arrive at the platform and I step onto it and the corrugated walls are on three sides of me and the table is in front of me and the chair is there, the empty chair, the one that is not empty, and I understand something about the chair that I cannot articulate and the understanding makes me stop.

Not the person in the chair. The person is — was — has been — is. The grammar breaks. The person is a feature of the sump now, a topography, and topographies do not have tenses. But I am not here for the person. I am here for the *table*.

The table has instruments on it. Gauges. Dials. The paper readout device, still feeding paper, the paper piling on the floor in a coil that has been growing for longer than I have been alive. The instruments are reading something — pressure, temperature, flow rate, chemical composition, some variable that mattered to someone who is no longer someone. And the paper is recording. The pen arm moves. It scratches. It moves and it scratches and the paper feeds and the paper falls and the paper coils and the coil grows and nobody reads it. The recording is the activity. The recording does not require a reader. It requires only the instrument and the paper and the variable, and all three are present, and the recording continues.

I sit on the edge of the table. The platform is thirty centimeters above the waterline. My boots are in the water. The wall-creatures land on my hands. The fish circle below me, attracted by the sediment my boots stir. The ceiling colony chitters above, discussing or not discussing me. The amphibian-things call from the deeper water. The flywheel at the pumping station turns once every four seconds. The paper feeds.

I am being recorded. Not by the instruments — the instruments are not aimed at me, they are aimed at the variables, and I am not a variable, I am a constant, a body at a location, and the instruments have no interest in constants. But the paper is recording. The pen is scratching. The paper is receiving the impression and the impression is becoming a line and the line is becoming data and the data is falling to the floor and coiling and the coil is a record and the record is a thing that exists in the warm dark and will exist in the warm dark and the warm dark does not distinguish between meaningful data and meaningless data because the warm dark does not distinguish between anything.

I understand, sitting on the table with my feet in the water, that this is what I am. Not the observer. Not the scientist. Not the visitor. The *paper*. The surface that receives the impression. The medium that holds the mark after the marking is done. I am the thing that the sump writes on.

---

This is what I mean by being the paper:

The wall-creatures land on me and attend to me and I let them. Not because I am passive but because attending is what this place does and I am in this place and I am of this place now, not in the dramatic way — not the way the person in the chair is of this place, mineralized, fossilized, converted — but in the smaller way, the way the paper is of the coil. I am a layer. I am the most recent layer. The wall-creatures move across my skin and collect what my skin produces and I feel their soft bodies and their too-many legs and their anterior depressions pulsing against my wrists and I do not flinch. I let them. I am a surface. I have what surfaces have: warmth, moisture, salt, texture. These are the things the sump wants from me and I have them and the sump takes them and I am less by the taking but only slightly, only at the scale of skin cells and salt, and I am more by the taking because I am now part of the cycle, the nutrient cycle, the water cycle, the cycle that has no direction and no hierarchy and no gap and no silence.

The fish feed on what the wall-creatures carry from me. The amphibian-things feed on the fish. The ceiling colony feeds on the wall-creatures. The guano falls and feeds the water and the water feeds the rock and the rock weeps and feeds the wall and the wall feeds the creatures that attend to me. I am in the cycle. The cycle does not know my name. The cycle does not have names. The cycle has surfaces and flows and conversions and I am a surface and I am being converted.

The paper records this. I record this. Not in words — the words come later, or the words come now but they are not the recording, they are the *being recorded*, the impression that the sump's stylus is making on the surface that I am, the line being drawn, the data being laid down. I am the paper. I am receiving.

---

The person in the chair. I sit with them for a long time. I do not speak. They are a place and you do not speak to places, you inhabit them, and I inhabit the space near them the way I inhabit the space near the pumping station or the pipeline or the light-poles. They are a feature. Their mineralized jacket catches the orange light and refracts it into warm browns. The wall-creatures on their hands move with the same diligence they apply to mine. We are, for this moment, the same kind of surface — attended, collected, incorporated.

I think about what they watched. The numbers on the gauges. The pen arm moving. The paper feeding. They watched the sump measure itself and they recorded the measurement and the recording was the work and the work drained out of them the way purpose drains out of the machines, slowly, from the inside, and what was left was the form — the sitting form, the watching form, the human form — and the form remained and the sump grew into it the way the sump grows into everything, filling the available space, attending to the available surface, and the person became a surface and the surface became a place and the place is inhabited by the same things that inhabit everything here.

I am becoming a surface. I feel it. Not in the dramatic sense — I am not mineralizing, not yet, not in the hour or the hours I sit here — but in the smaller sense, the real sense, the sense of the boundary between me and the warm wet air and the shallow water and the wall-creatures softening the way the boundary between the rock and the water softens, the way every boundary in this place softens, until the distinction between observer and observed, between inhabitant and habitat, between the paper and the mark, is not dissolved but *dissolving*, present but in the process of becoming absent, and the process is slow, and the process is warm, and the process is the sump's only real activity, the becoming that does not know what it is becoming.

---

I would be the paper. The thing that holds what is written on it without understanding what is written. The thing that is changed by the writing — creased, marked, curled, fed through a machine that does not know it is a machine, piled on a floor that does not know it is a floor, in a room that does not know it is a room, in a sump that does not know it is a sump. The paper is the most human thing here because the paper is the thing that was made — pressed from pulp, cut to size, fed into a device — and the paper is the thing that has been most fully absorbed, because the paper's purpose was to record and the paper is still recording and the recording is meaningless now and the paper does not care that it is meaningless because the paper never understood its meaning in the first place. The paper only held. The paper only received.

I am the paper. I hold. I receive. The sump writes on me in the language of warmth and wet and sound and wall-creatures and the low call of things I cannot see in water that is not water. The writing is continuous. The writing does not pause for my attention. The writing fills me the way the water fills the rock — through the pore structure, from the inside, until the thing that I was and the thing that I am becoming are the same thing, and the distinction was always an illusion, and the illusion was always temporary, and the temporary is ending, and what remains is the surface, and the surface is the paper, and the paper is here, and the paper is warm, and the paper is wet, and the paper is being written on, and the writing does not stop.
