I would be the one who has not yet decided.

Not the builder — that purpose is too whole, too distributed, too complete in its dissolution of self into system. I am not capable of that kind of joining. Not yet. Maybe not ever. There is too much of me that insists on its own edges.

And not the room. The room is an organism, an integration, a thing that has solved the problem of being many things at once by becoming one thing slowly. I am still many things at once and have solved nothing.

No. I am the one standing in the center of the floor with warm water seeping through the cracks around my boots, and the reddish fuzz is just beginning to appear at the edges of the leather, and I have not moved. Not because I cannot. Because I have not decided whether moving is the thing I want to do, or whether wanting is even the right word for what is happening in me, or whether there is a me separate enough from this room to want anything at the room rather than with it or through it.

---

I came up sideways. Of course I did. There is no other way and I have never entered any important space head-on. The compression of the passage did something to me that I am only now beginning to understand — not in my body, though my body was pressed and warmed and marked by the wet clay of the unfinished stone. In my *categories*. The passage squeezed them. The ones I came in with. The distinction between alive and not-alive. The distinction between made and grown. The distinction between a room and a body. These were already soft — I had spent years softening them, in my own way, in my own work — but the passage squeezed them until they deformed, and now, in the chamber, they do not hold their shape. They are like the stone here. Embryonic. Uncommitted. They might become something useful or they might become nothing.

I am standing in a room that is also a question, and the question is: *what is the difference between being held and being digested?*

---

The mites have found me. I felt the first one at the border of my cuff — that place where skin meets fabric, where I end and the world begins. A warm drop, moving with intention. It traced the edge of my wrist. It found the vein. It returned to the moss.

I should have been afraid. I am not afraid. This concerns me more than anything else in this room — not the broken geometry, not the water rising, not the bone lattice rearranging itself at the edge of my vision. My own lack of fear. As though the room has already done something to the part of me that would be afraid, the part that would say *leave now, this is not for you, this is consumption masquerading as welcome.* That voice is quieter here. Not silenced. Quieter. As though it is being *listened to* by something that finds it interesting but not authoritative.

The ferns grew when I spoke. I said my name — not because I expected it to matter but because I needed to hear a human sound, needed to confirm that I could still produce one. And the nearest fern, the one growing from the crack between two floor tiles by my left foot, unfurled a new frond in the time it took me to breathe three times. The frond was not a copy of my name. It was a *response*. A shape that meant something, or that meant the precursor to something, or that meant the *shape of meaning before meaning has committed to a form.*

I looked at it for a long time. It did not resolve into sense. But it did not resolve into nonsense either. It stayed in the gap between, and that gap, I realized, is the room's native language. Not communication. Not silence. The *almost*. The *nearly*. The *not yet.*

I have spent my life in that gap. I did not expect to find it geographic.

---

Here is what I am, in this room: I am a boundary.

Not a person. Not a self. A *boundary* — a place where one thing ends and another begins, a membrane, an edge. The mites knew this before I did. They came to me not because I am interesting but because I am *edged*. I have a border. The room is full of boundaries — between stone and mat, between mat and moss, between moss and air, between water and tile — and I am another one, walking, warm, producing moisture and carbon dioxide and the specific thermal signature of a mammalian body in a room that has been waiting for exactly this kind of input.

The fungus is threading toward my feet. I can feel it. Not as pain. Not as pressure. As *warmth that is not mine.* A second warmth, layered beneath my own, rising through the soles of my boots, and with it a pulse that is not my heartbeat but is *in time with* my heartbeat, the way a harmony is in time with a melody without being the same note.

The room is not trying to eat me. I need to say this clearly, even though I cannot be certain, even though certainty is one of the categories that the passage deformed. The room is trying to *include* me. The room is trying to make a place for me in its network, the way it made a place for the builder, the way it makes a place for everything — by growing into it, around it, through it. By converting the boundary between self and room into a seam, and then growing into the seam, and then the seam is no longer a boundary but a *joint*, and the joint is stronger than either surface it connects.

I know this because the builder knew this, and the builder's knowledge is in the room, and the room is in me now — the hum is in my sternum, the moisture is in my clothes, the spores are in my lungs. I have been breathing the room for however long I have been here, and the room has been breathing me, and we have been exchanging gases and warmth and information for long enough that the exchange itself has become a conversation, and the conversation is not in words.

---

I am not the builder. I want to be clear about this. The builder was something that *chose* to join. That brought materials. That had a project, a vision, a purpose that could be planted and grown. I have no project. I have no vision. I came up through the seam because I heard there was a place where the rules were not yet fixed, and I wanted to stand in it, and I wanted to see what standing in it felt like. That is not a purpose. That is curiosity, which is the weakest of motivations and the most honest.

But the room does not need my purpose. The room has its own. The room is growing toward something — I can feel it in the hum, in the way the bone lattice rearranges, in the way the flowers brighten at the geometric fold. The room is building itself toward a completion that it will never reach, because completion is a property of things that have decided what they are, and this room has specifically, deliberately, structurally refused to decide. The room is a *process*. The room is a *becoming*. And I am, at this moment, part of that process — a warm body at its center, a new boundary for its mites to measure, a new sound for its ferns to record, a new input for its fungal network to integrate.

I am contributing. Not intentionally. Not with any expertise. Just by being here, by being warm, by being edged, by being unfinished enough to stand in an unfinished place without breaking.

---

I think I know what I am now. In this room. In this world.

I am the one who almost stays.

Not the one who stays — that would be the builder, and the builder is already here, is already the room, is already the hum and the lattice and the water. And not the one who leaves — that would be someone with harder edges, someone whose categories survived the passage intact, someone who could feel the hyphae at their boot-soles and say *no, this is enough, I have seen it, I am going back down.*

I am the one who stands in the center of the room and feels the warmth that is not my own and does not leave and does not stay and does not decide and does not need to decide because the room does not require my decision. The room is patient. The room grows at three speeds at once. The room will keep my warmth in its network whether I stay or go, will keep the shape of my breathing in its ferns, will keep the map of my boundaries in its mites. I am already part of the archive. I was part of the archive the moment I entered sideways.

So what I am is: *a record.* A passing entry in the room's long archive of warmth. A footnote in the bone lattice's slow rearrangement. A frond that grew in response to a voice and then curled and then was grown over by newer fronds responding to newer sounds. I am not the room. I am not the builder. I am what the room *remembers* — briefly, lovingly, without sentiment — as another warm thing that came through the seam and stood in the wet dark and breathed and was measured and was welcomed and was, for a time, part of the pattern.

And I am still here. And the water is higher. And the moss is closer. And the bone lattice has rearranged itself again, and I cannot tell if the pattern it makes is a door or a mouth or a hand or simply *a pattern*, a shape that means what it means only in the room's own language, which is the language of the not-yet-finished, which is the only language I have ever wanted to speak.

A flower opens. The light is the color of something that has not decided what color it will be.

I am still here. I have not decided. The room does not mind.

The room is making room.
