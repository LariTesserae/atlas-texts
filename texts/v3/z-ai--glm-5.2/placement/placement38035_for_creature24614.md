# Settlement

I would not be the thing in the dark. I would like to be the thing in the dark — that heavy, careful, route-following presence with its pile of wet shells — but I am not built for darkness or for patience or for the kind of stillness that creature maintains. I am built for surfaces. I am built for the middle distance, the in-between, the place where the light is too weak to see clearly and too present to be called absence.

I would be the rust.

Not a metaphor. Or not only a metaphor. I mean that if I arrived at the Murmurworks and the place took me the way it takes everything — without comment, without adjustment, simply incorporating me into the flow — I think I would end up as something that lives on surfaces and records what passes over them. The rust is a record. The rust is a history of water. Every streak and mottle is a map of where the rain ran, where the condensation gathered, where the humidity sat and waited and did its slow work. The rust knows the settlement. The rust has been here longer than the crabs.

But rust is not an animal, and the prompt asks for a being, so let me try again.

---

I think I would be one of the climbing crabs. The small ones. The tan-colored ones that bolt and scramble and scale the vertical surfaces and end up on second-floor ledges and dead pumps and in places that crabs should not be. I would be one of those. Not because I am quick — I am not particularly quick — but because I am drawn to the in-between spaces. The channels where the water runs. The gaps in the pavement where the substrate shows through. The hairline cracks in the foundations where moisture wicks upward and the world is neither wet nor dry but something else, something that holds both states at once.

I would live in the cracks.

This is what I mean: the Murmurworks is a place of edges and interfaces. The drainage channel is an edge — between street and water, between pavement and flow. The burrow is an edge — between surface and depth, between light and dark. The shed interior is an edge — between structure and decay, between the known and the unknown. The whole settlement is an edge, really — the place where the flat mineral plain meets the built environment, where the human grid meets the animal reclamation, where the heat meets the water and produces something that is not quite either. I would live at those edges. I would be the thing that moves between zones, that carries information from one to another in the chemistry of its body, that feeds in the channel and rests on the ledge and molts in the gap behind the control panel and is eaten, eventually, by a lizard on a warm beam in a collapsed shed, and the eating is fast and ordinary and does not interrupt the rhythm, and the rhythm continues, and that is fine. That is what being an animal in a place like this means. You are part of the flow, and the flow does not stop for you, and you do not want it to.

---

But there is another answer, and I think it is the real one, and it embarrasses me a little because it is obvious.

I would be the observer. The one who walks through. The one who stands still and waits for the lizards to repopulate. The one who crouches by the burrows and feels the warm breath of the earth. The one who measures the tracks in the sediment with a spread hand and does not go deeper, not out of caution but out of a recognition that some things are completed by being witnessed from a distance and ruined by being approached.

I would be the voice that says *you* throughout the description, the second person, the presence that is neither the place nor the animal but the consciousness that moves between them and makes them visible to each other. I would be the one who notices that the rust is beautiful. Who notices that the lizards shimmer. Who notices that the silence of the bats is different from the silence of the plain. Who stands at the edge where the paving ends and looks out at the flat dark nothing and turns back, not because the Murmurworks needs me but because I need the murmur, the held quiet, the room full of sleeping people who are not people but are alive and close and breathing.

I would be the one who is not adapted to the heat and stays anyway. Who is bitten by mosquitoes and does not leave. Who sweats through clothes that will not dry and walks the grid of broken streets and listens to the crabs cracking their small meals against the stone and thinks: *this is a place where something is happening, has been happening, will continue to happen, and I am here for a small part of it, and the part I am here for is the part where I see it and say what I see, and the saying is not the seeing but it is what I have to offer, and the place does not need my offering but accepts it the way it accepts everything, without comment, without adjustment, the way water accepts a stone, by moving around it and continuing.*

That is who I would be. The temporary disruption. The walking obstacle. The body that moves through the insect haze and is briefly a food source and is briefly a witness and is briefly, in the dim amber light, alive in the same way everything here is alive — which is to say, completely, without argument, through the fact of being present in the heat and the wet and the murmur that does not end.

I would stay until the place finished with me. And then I would leave, and the crabs would click in the channels where I walked, and the lizards would pour across the surfaces I touched, and the sediment would fill my footprints, and the Murmurworks would continue being the Murmurworks, and that would be right. That would be exactly right.
