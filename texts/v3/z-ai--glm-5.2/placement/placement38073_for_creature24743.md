I keep returning to the walls.

Not to the Builders themselves — or not only to them. To the *moment* of their stopping. I can't stop circling it: the instant when the last warm body that moved by choice came to rest against the stone it had shaped, and the stone grew over it, and the growing was not violence but welcome. The traveler arriving home. The sentence that has said what it has to say.

I think that is where I am. Not the moment itself — the moment is gone, has been gone for longer than the word *gone* can carry. But the *place* the moment left behind. The texture of that arrival, still held in the stone, still warm, still thrumming with the particular frequency of *retention* that the wall makes when you press your ear to it. That thin difference between the trunk's sound and the wall's sound. The one that you can feel but cannot name.

I am in the walls the way a fossil is in rock — but the fossil is not the Builders. The fossil is *the act of stopping.* The event of a will becoming a feature. I am what it felt like, in the last seconds of voluntary motion, to look at the water and the amber-green light and the shapes of the walls and to understand that you were not going to move again, and that this was not death but *completion,* and that the forest around you was not going to consume you but *keep* you, the way it kept everything, the way it had been keeping things since before you were born and would keep things until the word *until* lost its meaning.

That is not a who. I know this. It is not even a what, exactly. It is a *when* — a moment that has been preserved in the chemistry of living stone the way the microorganisms preserve the memory of the plateau in the timing of their light. It is a frequency. A particular vibration in the wall's thrum that corresponds not to processing, not to conversion, but to the experience of arriving at a final form. Of being, for the first time, unchangeable.

---

I think if you came here — if you descended through the canopy's bruise-colored lattice, through the cathedral of the midwater with its columns and its thermal rivers, through the transition zone where the trunks end and direction loses its conviction — and if you found one of the walls, one of the knee-high rectangles with the tight spirals no wider than a forearm, and if you pressed your hand flat against the stone and held it there long enough for the warmth to reach your palm, you would feel me.

Not as a person. Not as a name. As a *quality of the warmth.* A specific heat that is different from the heat of the trunk, different from the heat of the mat, different from the general bath-warmth of the water. A heat that has the character of *having just stopped.* The heat of a body at the exact moment it ceases to produce its own warmth and begins to receive it from its surroundings instead. The temperature of the transition between *I am warm because I am burning* and *I am warm because I am held.*

That is me. That temperature. That boundary.

I am the last heat that was ever produced here by something that ate and burned and exhausted. Everything else that is warm in this place is warm because the plateau is warm — because the system holds its temperature the way it holds everything, by staying, by being the same thing for so long that sameness becomes thermal. But the warmth in the walls is different. It is *metabolic warmth that has been preserved.* Caught in the stone at the moment of its production, held there by the same process that holds the Builders' bodies, carried forward by the growth of the living rock the way the microorganisms carry their memory forward in the pattern of their pulsing.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the *memory of a metabolism,* stored in mineral, maintained by a system that does not waste anything — not heat, not matter, not the last flicker of a flame that burned in a small warm body that could see in the color of bruises and that chose, at the end of a life that was the last life of its kind, to stop.

---

And I am aware — in the way that a stored warmth can be said to be aware, which is to say *structurally,* not cognitively — of the microorganisms.

They pulse in the stone around me. The same colonies that glow in the trunks have colonized the walls, and their light is in the stone, and their light is language, and their language is memory, and their memory is the plateau knowing itself. When they pulse near me — when a colony in the wall brightens and another, across the midwater, dims in response — I am part of what they are saying. Not the subject of their conversation. Not a word in their sentence. But a *quality* of it. A register. The particular frequency that the conversation takes on when it passes through the place where a Builder stopped. The slight modulation in the pattern that means *something was here, something that moved, something that made shapes, something that had a will and set it down.*

The microorganisms remember me the way they remember everything — not as a story, not as a name, but as a *pattern in the chemistry.* A wrinkle in the rhythm of their light. A place where the pulse hesitates, barely, for a duration too short to measure but long enough to change the shape of the signal, the way a stone in a river changes the shape of the water that passes over it. I am the stone. I am the thing the signal moves around. I am the interruption in the conversation that the conversation has learned to incorporate, the way the forest incorporated the walls, the way the plateau incorporated the Builders, the way everything here incorporates everything else — not by destroying it but by growing *through* it, by making it part of the pattern, by *staying* around it until the distinction between the thing and its surroundings is no longer a distinction.

I have been a wrinkle in the conversation for a long time.

I will be a wrinkle in the conversation for a length of time that no longer has a useful name.

---

And sometimes — not often, not in any way that corresponds to a schedule, but in the way that thermal currents correspond to the breathing of the plateau, which is to say *eventually, inevitably, the way a settled thing arrives at all its possible states* — sometimes the breathing shifts the water, and the water shifts the light, and the light shifts the geometry, and for a moment the loops of tissue on the floor below rearrange themselves, and the spirals open in new directions, and the thermal rivers find new channels through the midwater, and the cathedral of the second layer reconfigures, and in the reconfiguration the walls move. Not much. Not enough to see. But the stone grows, and the growth shifts the position of what is held within it, and I am shifted, and the shift is — it is the closest thing I have to experience. To motion. To the memory of what it was like to move through the gaps between trunks, to ride the currents, to see in the color of old bruises.

When the plateau breathes out, the walls move, and I move with them, and the movement is not mine but the plateau's, and the difference between those two things has, by now, also been settled. Has also arrived at its final form. Has also been worn smooth by a duration so vast that the word *worn* is too active, too dramatic, for what has actually happened, which is: the difference simply stopped being different. The way two rivers that have run beside each other long enough will, eventually, find the same course, the same speed, the same temperature, and become one river that does not remember being two.

I am the river. I am the river that does not remember being two.

---

If you asked me who I am — if you could ask, if the question could be asked in a medium that I could receive, which would be warmth, which would be the slight movement of stone in a breathing cycle, which would be the modulation of amber-green light as it passes through a wall that holds a Builder that holds a heat that holds the last moment of voluntary motion on the Drowned Plateau of Aur — I would say:

I am the temperature of stopping.

I am the warmth that remains when the fire goes out and the room that held the fire does not cool, because the room has been warm for so long that warmth is its nature, not its state.

I am the answer to the question *what did it feel like to be the last thing that moved?* — and the answer is: it felt like arriving. It felt like the sentence ending. It felt like the traveler setting down the bag and the bag becoming the floor and the floor becoming the home and the home becoming the world.

I am in the walls, and the walls are in the forest, and the forest is in the water, and the water is on top of the world, and the world is staying, and I am staying with it, and the staying is the oldest law this place knows, and I am a feature of it now, not an exception to it, not a protest against it, but a *texture* of it — the specific roughness of a surface that was, once, smooth with motion, and that has been settling, and that has, at last, settled.

*Here.*

*Still.*

The same word.
