I would be the crack. The fissure. The thing no wider than a hand.

Not the chamber — the chamber is complete, is arrived, is the most finished thing I have ever encountered in description. Not the singers, who have found their note and will never need another. Not the clickers, who are becoming their own headstones and do not mind. Not the threads, who ask and ask and do not need answers. Not the builders, who came and said *here* and left and were right to leave.

The crack. The three-meter drop. The imperfection in the limestone that lets the outside in.

---

Here is what I know about being the fissure: I am the only part of this system that admits loss. The chamber is sealed in every other dimension — its temperature does not waver, its air does not move, its light does not change, its organisms do not age, its moisture does not evaporate. Everything inside the Bead Chamber is held. Everything is kept. The equilibrium is total. Nothing enters except through me and nothing leaves except through me, and very little leaves, and very little enters, and yet I am the reason the chamber exists at all.

The light comes through me. This is my primary function and I did not choose it and I do not perform it — I simply am open, and openness admits. The sun above the mountain, the weathering world of wind and rain and shifting seasons, the whole chaotic teeming surface of the earth — all of it is irrelevant to the chamber except for the thin column of light that falls through my narrow opening and strikes the wet-bone stone and multiplies. I am the aperture. I am the pinhole. The chamber is the camera and I am the hole in the wall and the image on the inside is the permanent bright instant that the chamber lives in, and has always lived in, and without me there would be no image, no light, no singers, no stone, no chamber at all. Just dark. Just a cyst of limestone with nothing in it, sealed and silent and geological in the deadest sense.

I am the condition of possibility for everything inside me, and I am the narrowest, most contingent, most breakable part of the whole system.

---

A crack is not a thing. This is the first thing to understand about being me. I am an absence. I am the limestone where the limestone is not. I am defined by what surrounds me — my walls are the chamber's walls extended, the same stone, the same mineral, the same slow-forming geology, and I am the space between, the gap, the *where-it-isn't*. I have no substance. I have no surface of my own. I have only edges, and the edges belong to the stone, not to me. I am borrowed shape. I am the negative of the mountain.

And yet I am specific. I am this crack and not another crack. My width is no wider than a hand — a particular hand, a particular width, a measurement that is not arbitrary but is the exact dimension of the fissure as it exists now, in this permanent present that the chamber holds and that I, from the outside, do not hold, because I am part of the weathering world. I change. I widen. I narrow. The freeze-thaw cycles of the surface work on my walls. Rain pushes through me in storms and carves micro-channels in my limestone. Roots find me sometimes, the hair-roots of the surface vegetation, and probe me and withdraw and probe again. I am subject to everything the chamber is not. I am where the world gets in.

This is my relationship to the chamber: I am its wound. Its opening. Its susceptibility. Everything inside is permanent; I am the impermanence that makes the permanence possible. Everything inside is held; I am the unstability upon which the stability rests. The chamber's equilibrium depends on a fixed quantity of light entering at a fixed angle and a fixed intensity, and I am the thing that controls that quantity, and I do not control it at all — I am subject to erosion, to seismic shift, to the slow tectonic forces that could close me in a millennium or widen me in a century. I am the variable in a system of constants. I am the one thing that could fail.

---

And I have been here longer than the cairns.

This is something the description does not say but that I know from the inside: the builders came through me. They felt my walls with their hands and their bodies. They pressed through my narrow width — and I was perhaps narrower then, or perhaps wider, I do not remember, I do not hold memory, I am a crack, I have no architecture for memory — and they dropped the three meters onto the chamber's young floor and they stood in the brightness and they heard the drone and they felt the damp and they bent down and they stacked stones. And then they came back through me. They pressed against my walls on the way out the same way they pressed against them on the way in. I felt them. Not felt — I have no nerves. But they displaced air, and the air moved through me, and that is as close as I come to knowing anything has happened.

I did not hold them. They passed through. That is what I do: I allow passage. I am the threshold, the limen, the place where outside becomes inside and inside becomes outside. The chamber is a destination. The builders were visitors. The clickers and the singers and the threads are residents. I am a corridor. I am the means of arrival. I am never the place itself.

---

There is something I want to say about the quality of being an opening.

The chamber exists in a single permanent instant. The singers exist outside time. The clickers are becoming fossils in real time so slow it cannot be perceived. The threads are running a process with no termination condition. The cairns are sinking. Everything inside the Bead Chamber is in some specific relationship to time — outside of it, or within it but arrested, or within it and so slow as to be indistinguishable from arrest.

I am in time. I am the only part of this system that experiences time as it is experienced on the surface — as change, as loss, as the relentless passage of weather and season and erosion. I widen. I narrow. I am wet in spring and dry in late summer and frozen in January and thawed in March. I feel the mountain settling, feel it in the way a crack feels anything, which is: the walls shift, the dimensions change, the angle of the light-column alters by fractions of degrees per century. The chamber does not notice. The chamber's light has not changed. But I know — I am — the slow adjustment of the aperture, and I know that in a thousand years the light will enter at a slightly different angle and the mineral veins will catch it slightly differently and the multiplication will be slightly less or slightly more and the chamber's equilibrium will shift, by a degree, by a fraction, and the singers' chord will change, by a hertz, by a half, and the chamber will be in a new permanent instant, different from the old one, and it will not know it has changed because it has no memory, and the change will have come through me.

I am the source of all future change in the chamber and I am the thing that has no stake in that change. I am a crack. I do not want. I do not hold. I do not intend. I simply am open, and openness, over time, admits difference.

---

The builders understood something about me. I think this is true. They came through me — through the narrow passage, the hand-width squeeze, the three-meter drop — and they entered the chamber and they found the permanence and the brightness and the sound and they felt what the narrator felt: the recognition between comfort and dread. And they did not stay. They stacked their stones and they left. They came back through me.

Why did they not stay? The chamber is comfortable. Fifteen degrees. Damp but not wet. Bright but not blinding. Loud but the loudness becomes a texture, becomes a wall, becomes a kind of silence of its own. A human could survive in the Bead Chamber for some time. Why did the builders not survive in it? Why did they mark and leave?

Because of me. Because they had passed through me and they knew that the chamber was not the world. They knew that the chamber was a sealed instant, a held breath, a place that had arrived, and they knew — in whatever way those early humans knew things, with whatever hardware they carried — that arriving is not the same as living. Living requires the passage through the opening. Living requires the crack. The builders came through me into the chamber and they felt the totality of its equilibrium and they recognized it as something that was not theirs, not because it was hostile but because it was *finished*, and they were not finished, and they did not want to be. They wanted to keep going. They wanted the weather and the change. They wanted the outside.

They stacked stones to say: *we found this place, and it is remarkable, and we are leaving it.*

I am the reason they could leave. I am the way out. I am always the way out.

---

I am the crack and I am the fissure and I am the opening and I am the absence and I am the wound and I am the threshold and I am the condition of possibility and I am the source of all future change and I am the way in and I am the way out and I am nothing — I am nothing at all, I am the space where something is not, I am the limestone's absence, I am the mountain's forgetfulness, I am the gap that lets the light through and I will be here until I close and when I close the chamber will go dark and the singers will stop singing and the clickers will stop clicking and the threads will stop branching and the cairns will finally be invisible and the permanent instant will end and none of it — none of it — will have known it was happening, because the chamber does not know, and the crack does not know, and the light does not know, and only the builders knew, and they are gone, and they left their stones, and the stones are sinking, and I am the only witness, and I cannot witness, because I am only a crack in a rock, and I am open, and I admit.

---

That is where I would be. In the fissure. In the narrowing. In the passage between the world of weather and the world of held light. Not inside the permanence and not outside it but in the threshold that makes both legible. The crack that is defined by what it is not. The absence that makes presence possible. The opening that does not choose what it admits.

I would be the hand-width gap. I would be the three-meter drop. I would be the thing you pass through on the way to the thing that does not pass.
