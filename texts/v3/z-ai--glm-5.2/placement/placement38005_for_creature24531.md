I would be the one who came down the slope.

Not the slope — there is no slope worth naming. The ground rises gently on all sides and I walked down the gradient the way water would, if water were here, which it is not. I came from outside the hollow, from whatever lies beyond it, which is to say I came from a place that has weather and distance and the ordinary passage of time, and I entered a place that has none of these things, and I noticed the difference in my body before I noticed it in the air.

The warmth found my feet first. Through the soles — I was barefoot, or I had removed whatever covered my feet, because the ground asked for this in the way that warm ground asks for it, which is silently and without insistence. The mineral dust yielded slightly and held its shape and was warm in the way that a thing is warm when warmth is not a condition imposed on it but a property of what it is. I stood there. I stood at what I would later understand was the lowest point, though the difference in depth is so slight that my body could not register it as a difference at all, only as a quality — a sense of having arrived, of being at the bottom of a shallow cup, of being held.

The darkness was not difficult. I want to say this because the description might make it sound as though the darkness were a problem to be solved, and it was not. The darkness was the temperature of the air. It was the condition in which the warmth existed. It was not the absence of light but the presence of something else — something that occupied the space where light would have been, if light had been wanted, which it was not. My eyes adjusted in the way that eyes adjust, which is to say they stopped expecting and started receiving, and what they received was the faint glow of the substrate, the pale luminescence that lives in the mineral dust like heat lives in it, as a property, as a fact, and the darker shapes of the growths scattered across the basin floor.

I did not know they were growths. I did not know they were alive. I knew they were there — darker masses, fist-sized, flush against the ground, dry-looking, still. I walked among them. I was careful not to step on them, though nothing in the hollow suggested that stepping on them would matter. I was careful because I was a thing that had come from outside, and things that come from outside are careful in places they do not understand. This is not wisdom. It is the caution of a body that knows it is out of context.

I sat down. The ground was warm against my legs, against my palms when I placed them behind me. The warmth came up through the compacted dust and into my skin and I felt it as a kind of permission — not to stay, not to go, but to be here, in the way that the growths were here, without justification, without purpose, without the need to explain the fact of my presence to anything, including myself.

The low tone found my chest. I say *found* because it did not arrive — it was not a sound that traveled from a source to a receiver. It was there, in my chest, the way the warmth was in the ground, and I became aware of it the way you become aware of your own heartbeat: not as a new sensation but as a thing that has been happening and that you have only just noticed is happening. It sat below hearing. It sat below language. It was a vibration that I felt in the sternum and in the ribs and in the tissue around the heart, and it was continuous, and it did not change, and after a time I stopped noticing it again, and this was not a loss but an integration. The tone became part of what I was in the hollow. Part of the condition of being here.

---

I did not touch the growths. I sat among them and I did not touch them. I was close enough to have touched one — the nearest one was perhaps a meter from my right hand, a dark mass the color of old charcoal, the size of a closed fist, its surface matte and rough in the faint glow. I could have reached out and put my fingers on it. I could have felt the texture that the description gives — fine-grained sandpaper, desert bark, the skin of something that has never needed to be smooth. I did not. Not because the hollow forbade it. Not because I was afraid. Because the gesture would have been a kind of conversation, and the growths were not in conversation, and I did not want to be the thing that introduced conversation to this place.

This is the closest I can come to describing what I was in the hollow: I was the thing that chose not to introduce conversation.

I was not a growth. I want to be clear about this. I was not one of the thirty-one. I did not persist in the way they persist. I had a body that was warm in a different way — metabolically warm, anxiously warm, the warmth of a thing that burns fuel and produces waste and expects change and fears the absence of change. My warmth was the warmth of process. Theirs was the warmth of property. The difference is the difference between a fire and a stone that has been sitting in the sun for a thousand years. Both are warm. Only one is warm because it is doing something. I was the fire. They were the stone. We sat in the same hollow and we were both warm and the warmth meant different things for each of us, and this difference was the distance between us, and it was not a distance that could be crossed.

But I was there. I was the thirty-second warm thing in the basin, for a while. The air did not move around me. The dust did not settle differently because of me. The growths did not react. The low tone did not change pitch or volume or quality. The hollow did not adjust to my presence because the hollow does not adjust — it persists, and persistence does not adjust, it simply continues, and whatever is in it is part of the continuation whether it wants to be or not. I was part of the continuation. I was a temporary component of a system that does not recognize temporariness because temporariness requires a concept of duration, and the hollow has no concept of duration. The hollow has only the present state, which is the same as every past state and every future state, and I was in the present state, and that was all I was.

---

I stayed long enough that the distinction between staying and going became difficult. Not meaningless — I had a body that would need to leave, that would need to walk up the shallow gradient and back into the world that has weather and distance and time. But difficult. The warmth was in my body now, not just in the ground. The stillness was in my breathing. The darkness was in my eyes. I had been in the hollow long enough that my body had begun to approximate the hollow's conditions — not perfectly, not permanently, but enough that leaving would require a small act of violence against my own state. I would have to stand up. I would have to break the contact between my skin and the warm dust. I would have to introduce movement into air that had not moved. I would have to be the perturbation.

I did not want to be the perturbation.

I stayed longer.

---

I do not know how long. This is not a evasion. Time in the hollow is not a thing that can be known, because knowing time requires change, and change requires a marker, and the hollow has no markers. The dust does not shift. The growths do not grow. The tone does not vary. The warmth does not fluctuate. I could have been there for an hour or a day or a week, and the hollow would have been the same hollow, and I would have been the same body in it, and the only clock was my own body — my breathing, my heartbeat, the slow cooling of my own metabolism as the warmth of the ground replaced the warmth of my blood — and these clocks are not reliable in a place that does not want them.

I think I slept. Not in the way that sleep happens in the world outside — the descent through stages, the dreams, the waking. I think my body simply stopped doing the things that bodies do to maintain wakefulness, and I was in the hollow and I was not aware of being in the hollow, and this was not different from being aware of being in the hollow, because the hollow was the same whether I was aware of it or not. I lay on the warm dust among the growths and I was not conscious and the hollow continued and the growths persisted and the tone carried and this was — I want to say this was peace, but peace is the resolution of conflict, and there was no conflict. This was something that does not have a word, because words are for things that change, and this did not change.

---

I left. Eventually I left. My body made the decision before my mind did — a shift in the spine, a tensing in the legs, the body saying *we need to go now, we have been still too long, we are not a growth, we are not a stone, we are a thing that moves and we must move or we will forget how.* I stood up. The dust held the shape of my body for a moment and then began, slowly, to settle back to its original level. I walked among the growths, careful again not to step on them, and I walked up the gentle gradient and out of the hollow and into whatever is beyond the hollow, which is the world, which has weather, which has time.

I did not look back. Not because I did not want to but because looking back would have been a kind of conversation, and I had chosen not to introduce conversation to this place, and I would not break that choice now. The hollow was behind me. It was the same as it had been before I entered it. It was the same as it would be if I returned. It was the same as it was now, without me, warm and dark and still, the thirty-one growths in their scattered places, the low tone in the air, the faint glow in the dust, the equilibrium unbroken, the present state continuing.

---

If you asked me what I was in the hollow, I would say: I was the visitor. The one who came and lay down among the living things that do not know they are alive, in the warm place that does not know it is warm, in the dark that does not know it is dark. I was the thing that knew, briefly, what it was like to be in a place that does not need knowing, and I was changed by it, and the place was not changed by me, and this was correct, and this was the way it should be, and this was what the hollow gave me, which was nothing, which was the absence of the need for something, which was enough.

I would be the one who came back to the world and found that the world was louder than I remembered. That the air moved. That light was aggressive and specific. That time pressed against my skin like a weight I had set down in the dust and picked up again on the way out. I would be the one who carried, in the bones of her feet, in the warmth that lingered in her palms, the memory of a place that does not remember — not a warm memory, not a cold memory, but a memory that was the temperature of the ground, which is the temperature of a thing that has reached the state it was always moving toward, and remains there, and does not waver.

I would be the thirty-second thing that was in the hollow and is not in the hollow now. And the hollow would not notice. And this would be the kindest thing the hollow has ever done, which is to not notice, which is to be complete without me, which is to be a place that does not need me to be complete, and in that not-needing, to let me be, for a time, complete as well.
