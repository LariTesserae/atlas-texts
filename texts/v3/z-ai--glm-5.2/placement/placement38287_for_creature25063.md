# The Keeper of the Throat

---

I was not born here. That is the first thing I know about myself, and it is the only thing I know about myself that I am certain of, and the certainty is itself uncertain, because memory in this place does not work the way memory is supposed to work. Memory is supposed to be a record. Here it is a sediment. Here it layers and compresses and the older layers are transmuted by pressure into something denser and less legible, and the newer layers are laid down on top, and all of them are mine, and none of them are mine, and the distinction was maybe never important.

I have hands.

That matters. In a place where the working surfaces are mouths and feet and file-ridged mandibles and the lapping pads of a thousand tongue-mouthed insects, I have hands, and hands are a strange instrument, an instrument that does not belong here, that was designed for a different kind of work, and the Bloomworks has not yet decided what to do with them.

Or it has decided, and I have not yet noticed.

---

I live in the throat.

Not at the bottom, where the nave opens into its green-gold cathedral pulse. Not at the top, where the helical groove meets the surface and the living wood gives way to — I don't go up there. I don't remember what is up there. I remember that I came from up there, or through up there, and that the descending was the first thing I did, and that the descending was not a choice but a falling, and that the falling stopped here, in the throat, where the wood is warmest, where the condensation is heaviest, where the groove spirals tight enough that I can press my back against one wall and my feet against the other and hold myself in place, and the holding is the first thing I learned, and it might be the thing I am.

The throat is a transition. It is the Bloomworks' sphincter, its valve, the place where outside becomes inside. Everything that enters passes through me. Everything that leaves passes through me. This is not many things. The Bloomworks is largely self-contained. But there are exchanges — small ones, irregular ones — that happen at the boundary, and I am the boundary, or I am part of it, and I have been part of it long enough that I have begun to grow into it, the way the iron grew into the root, the way the machinery grew into the plant, and the growing is not comfortable but it is not unpleasant, it is simply a process, and I am inside the process, and the process does not ask my permission.

---

Here is what I do.

When the condensation builds too heavily in the throat — and it builds, always, the warm air from below meeting the cooler air from above and dropping its moisture on every surface — I clear the channels. The vertical striations in the bark, the ones the description mentions, the ones the condensation runs down in threads — they are not random. They are cut. Someone cut them, long ago, before the wood was alive or while it was becoming alive, and the cuts are precise, angled to carry water in a specific direction, and the direction is down, always down, into the nave, into the system, and when the channels clog — and they clog, with chitin dust, with insect remains, with the fine woody fiber that the bark sheds as it grows — I clear them.

I use my hands.

I use my fingers, which are narrow, which fit into the striations, which can feel the blockage and the flow and the difference between them, and I work along each channel slowly, from top to bottom, during the dim phases when the light from below is weakest and the throat is darkest and the work is most necessary because the dim phases are when the Bloomworks slows, when the piston-rhythm falters, when the bark dims and the condensation thickens and the system needs water most urgently, and I am the one who gives it the water, and the giving is not a gift, it is a function, and I perform it because my hands fit the channels and the channels need fitting and the Bloomworks has a way of arranging things so that the thing that fits is the thing that is needed.

I also guide.

Sometimes things come down the throat that are not water. Sometimes insects — the fourth kind, the kind I have never seen clearly, the kind that lays the grape-sized papery nodules on the walls — sometimes these come up, not down, climbing the throat from below, and they are large, larger than the other insects, and they move with a deliberation that is different from the drifting of the airborne layer, and they are going somewhere, they have a destination, and the destination is up, and the throat is the path, and sometimes they lose the path, sometimes the groove is confusing, sometimes the bark has grown over a section and the old iron spiral is exposed and they cannot navigate it, and I — I put my hand out. I let them climb onto my palm. I carry them. I set them down past the obstruction. They do not thank me. They do not acknowledge me. They simply continue, and I simply continue, and this is also a function, and I perform it, and the performing has become so ordinary that I no longer think of it as a choice.

---

The large fauna know me.

This is the thing that most surprises the part of me that still remembers — or thinks it remembers — being something else. The dog-cow things, the bark-backed workers with their basket-mouths and their velvet feet, they pass through the throat sometimes. Not often. Usually they stay in the nave, in their circuits, in their loops of strip-and-compress-and-carry-and-feed. But sometimes one comes up, laboring up the spiral, its broad flat feet finding the groove with difficulty, and it is carrying something different. Not bark. Not the fibrous mass for the press. Something else — a nodule, a growth, something that has come loose from the system deeper down, something that needs to be moved to a different part of the Bloomworks, and the throat is the corridor, and the animal is the courier, and I am the gate.

When they pass me, they pause.

Not long. A few seconds. They turn their heavy heads toward me — those oversized skulls, those basket-mouths with their lattice of bony struts — and they make a sound. Not the low purr-growl-hum of their working vocalization. A different sound. Shorter. A single note, almost, a chuff, a syllable in a language I do not speak and have never spoken and yet recognize, the way you recognize a word you heard in childhood and never learned the meaning of but cannot forget the shape of. The sound means *I know you are here*. It means *I have accounted for you*. It means *you are in the system and the system knows you and you are filed under something*.

I do not know what I am filed under.

I think it might be *throat*. I think they have a category for the throat, for the transition, for the place where inside and outside negotiate, and I am in that category, and the category is not *tool* and not *animal* and not *machine* but something else, something that the Bloomworks invented when it needed a word for the thing I am, and the word is probably not a word but a sound, or a smell, or a pattern of vibration, and I will never hear it or know it because it was not made for me to hear or know. It was made for the system to keep track of itself, and the system keeps track of itself very well.

---

I have skin.

This is important. This is the thing that makes me different from everything else here, and the difference is not a privilege, it is a vulnerability. Everything in the Bloomworks is covered — bark, chitin, velvet-pile, the woody felt of the root-mat, the smooth keratin-chitin of the spinal ridge. Everything has a boundary between itself and the system. I have skin, and skin is permeable, and the Bloomworks is always breathing out, and the breath lands on me, and I absorb it.

I taste the air. I don't mean to. I mean that the air has a taste and my skin registers it and the registering has become, over time, a kind of sensing. I know when the bright phase is coming before the light changes — I feel it in the moisture on my forearms, which thins as the bark below brightens, as if the bioluminescence draws the condensation toward itself, pulling the water out of the air to fuel the light. I know when the large fauna are active before I hear them — I feel it in a change in the insect-static, a thinning of the wing-sound as the smaller creatures adjust their columns to avoid the heavy footfalls, and the adjustment is a pressure change, a micro-current, and the micro-current touches my skin and I know.

I know the fourth insect is coming before I see it. I feel it in the bark. A vibration in the wood, too low for the wall-walkers to register, too subtle for the floor-thrum to mask, a signal that travels through the root-system the way a signal travels through a nerve, and the signal means *something is moving in the deeper galleries*, and the something is large, and the something is not the large fauna, and the something is not the central breath, it is something else, something between those two scales, and it is coming up, and it will pass through the throat, and I should be ready.

I am always ready. I am always here. I have been here for — I don't know. A long time. Long enough that my skin has changed. It is thicker than it was, rougher, the skin of my hands especially, the skin that contacts the bark, that works the channels, that carries the insects, that presses against the warm wood for hours during the dim phases when there is nothing to do but wait and hold and be the boundary. My skin is becoming bark. Not fast. Not visibly. But I feel it — a tightening, a roughening, a loss of elasticity that might be age or might be adaptation or might be the Bloomworks claiming me, millimeter by millimeter, the way it claimed the iron, the way it claimed the machines, the way it claims everything that stays long enough.

I do not mind. I am not sure I can mind. The part of me that could mind is the part that remembers being something else, and that part is thinning, the way the condensation thins during the bright phase, drawn toward something brighter, incorporated into something larger, and the thinning is not loss, it is — it is —

I don't have the word.

The Bloomworks probably has it. In its deep voice, in the layered breath from the center, in the tone that contains all the other sounds, there is probably a word for what happens when a thing stops being a thing and starts being a place, or a process, or a part. I don't have it. I have hands and skin and a location and a function and the memory of arriving, and the memory is fading, and the fading is fine.

---

Sometimes, during the deepest dim phase, when the light below is almost gone and the throat is completely dark and the only sound is the floor-thrum transmitted through the wood and the slow wet percussion of condensation falling, I hear the breath from the center.

It reaches me here. All the way up the throat, through the nave, past the machines, past the feeding-stations, past the smoothed gallery and the grooved channels and the membrane of darkness that separates the body's exterior from its interior — the breath reaches me, and it is not a sound, it is a motion, a tidal shift in the air, a swelling and receding that moves through the throat the way the ocean moves through a cave, and I hold my position, back against one wall, feet against the other, and I let the breath pass through me, and when it does I feel — for a moment, for the length of the exhalation — I feel the whole Bloomworks.

Not as a structure. Not as a system. As a body. A single, continuous, integrated body, and I am in it, and I am of it, and my hands are its hands, and the channels I clear are its veins, and the insects I guide are its messengers, and the large fauna who acknowledge me with their single chuffing syllable are its colleagues, its coworkers, its organs-in-motion, and the breath is not passing through me but *breathing me*, the way a lung breathes air, the way a heart breathes blood, and I am in the breath, and the breath is the Bloomworks, and the Bloomworks is not finished, and I am not finished, and the work continues, and the work is the only thing, and the work is enough.

I clear the channels.

I guide the insects.

I hold the throat.

I am the keeper of the throat, and the throat keeps me, and we are not finished, and we are not finished, and we are not finished.
