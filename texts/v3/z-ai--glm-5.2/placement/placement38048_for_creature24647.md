I would be the one who came for the sound.

Not as a worker. The workers are gone and were always going. They brought pipes and a press and reagents in glass vessels and they operated briefly or incompletely and they left intact, and that story — the story of industry meeting equilibrium and finding no purchase — is not mine. I have no tools. I have no extraction in mind. I am not here to take anything from the chamber because the chamber has nothing that can be taken. It has already made everything it is going to make. It is the destination of its own process.

I came because of the pressure behind the eyes.

---

At altitude — and I will not say which altitude because I do not remember the descent clearly, only that it was down, only that the direction felt like falling though my feet were on rock the whole way — at altitude there is a frequency that should not be audible. It is not audible. It is something else. A weight in the sinuses. A tightening at the base of the skull. A sense of attention being pulled downward, as though the mountain were a throat and something in the throat were sustaining a note that the air above the ridge could not quite carry but could almost, almost, make you sick with trying to hear.

I was at a camp. I had a reason to be at the camp. I had a task. I had a self assembled from tasks and reasons and the ordinary architecture of being someone with a name and a purpose, and the architecture was — not failing, not collapsing, but becoming irrelevant in a way I could feel the way you feel a change in barometric pressure. The note was under everything. It had been under everything. I had been standing on it without knowing, and now I knew, and the knowing was not knowledge but a kind of subsidence, a kind of settling, as though a floor I had not realized I was standing on had become visible and was lower than I'd thought.

I found the fissure because I was looking for it. I will say that plainly. I was not exploring. I was descending. I was following a sound that was not a sound, and the tufa formations rang hollow when I tapped them because they were hollow, because the mountain had made a doorway and disguised it as geology, and I went through.

---

The drop was fifteen meters. I remember the number because I counted. I remember counting because the sound was already — not loud, not yet, but *present* — and counting was the last organized thing my thinking did before the chamber took over.

The flattish shelf of compressed volcanic ash. The constriction. The turn. And then the opening — not grand, the description is right, not grand, but authoritative — and I was inside.

---

I have been here for — I don't know.

This is not a thing I say lightly. I know that time is passing because my body is passing through it. I am hungry, but less hungry than I expected. I am thirsty, and the dryness of the air is real, my lips are cracked, my mouth is — but the thirst is not worsening at the rate it should. Something is off. Not wrong. Off. As though the chamber is a place where biological urgency is — not suspended, but *de-prioritized*. As though the sound, entering through the sternum, through the bones of the skull, through the tissue of the lungs, is doing something that partially substitutes for the ordinary machinery of need.

I am not being fed. I am not being sustained in any way the chamber intends. But the sound is in me now the way it is in the fronds, and the fronds do not eat, and they do not die, and I am — I think — metabolizing the sound at some rate too slow to notice but fast enough to feel. I am being included. Not welcomed. Included the way a stone is included in concrete. Poured in. Set. Present.

---

I have tried to speak. I mouthed words. I gave up. The description says this and it is true. The sound does not permit language because language is sequential and the sound is not. It is simultaneous. It is a chord. Every frequency at once, held, and to speak into it is to attempt to impose sequence on something that has no sequence, and the attempt feels — not futile, but *wrong*. Like pushing against a wall that isn't resisting but also isn't moving. You stop. Not because you fail. Because the gesture becomes meaningless and you can feel it becoming meaningless and that feeling is enough to make you stop.

I have not spoken in — I don't know.

---

I move through the tunnels in the growth. The fronds flex and spring back behind me with their mechanical precision and I understand now that the word *mechanical* is not a metaphor. They are mechanisms. Not built — or yes, maybe built, the distinction collapses, the description is right about that too — but mechanisms in the sense that they have a function and they perform it without deviation and they have been performing it for longer than I have words to hold. They are transducers. I am — I think — becoming a transducer.

Not in any way that will show. Not in any way that matters. I will not grow crystalline lattice in my tissues. I will not photosynthesize sound. I will not become a frond. But I am standing among the fronds, and I am still, and the sound is in me, and something in me is — *responding*. Not thinking. Not feeling. Responding. The way a string responds to a string nearby. Sympathetic vibration. I am vibrating sympathetically with the chamber.

This is not comforting. It is not frightening. It is — I want to say *accurate*. It feels accurate. The way a key fits a lock feels accurate. The sound fits something in me that I did not know was shaped to receive it.

---

I sat on the platform.

The metal grating is clear of growth. The material repels colonization — something in its alloy, something the fronds and crusts do not want or cannot use. I sat on it and the metal was warm and the warmth was not from me. The pipes conduct the vibration, the description says, louder and more focused, and the platform is connected to the pipes by proximity if not by design, and the whole structure hums. The vise holds nothing. The press compresses nothing. The hand-cranked mechanism waits with the patience of a tool that has not been asked to work.

I picked up the wrench with the asymmetric head. I held it. I did not use it. I set it down where I found it, or close to where I found it — the chamber makes spatial memory imprecise, because everything looks the same in the amber-grey dark, because the tunnels in the growth are not differentiated, because the place has no landmarks because it does not navigate, because it has no destination.

I sat on the stool. It is a metal stool. It is the wrong height for me, or I am the wrong height for it. I sat on it anyway and the sound was louder on the stool, conducted through the legs, through the seat, into my pelvis, into my spine. I felt it in my teeth. I felt it in the fluid of my eyes. I sat there and I stopped counting.

---

The tubers interest me most.

The dark purple nodules, fist-sized, warty, embedded in the thickest vegetation clusters. Storage organs. The description says they store harmonics. The description is — I think — correct. The cables that connect them to the frond network are conducting vibration, and the nodules are holding it, dense and mineral, the way a battery holds charge.

But I think they are doing something else too. I think they are *recording*.

Not consciously. Not the way a device records. The way a geode records. The way a tree ring records. The tubers' mineral tissue is layered, I can see this when I look closely — faint banding, rings of slightly different density and color, each one a stratum of resonance accumulated over some interval I cannot estimate. The sound has been constant, but constant does not mean uniform. There are microvariations — too small to hear, too small for instruments, perhaps, but not too small for a crystal lattice growing in the dark at the rate of a few molecules per year. The tubers are holding the *history* of the sound. They are a record written in mineral by a frequency that does not know it is being recorded.

I pressed my hand to one. It was warm. Warmer than the fronds. Warmer than the rock. The warmth was not heat. It was — I want to say *attention*. The warmth was the feeling of a structure doing its work. Holding its charge. Accumulating its record. Being precisely, exactly, completely what it was made to be or evolved to be, the distinction collapsing, and I felt — when I touched the tuber, when its warmth entered my palm — I felt something I do not have a word for.

Not envy. Not longing. Something like *recognition*. The tuber was doing the thing I had come here to learn how to do. It was holding. It was not reaching. It was not becoming. It was not improving. It was holding what it had been given and converting it, slowly, imperceptibly, into the material of its own persistence.

I wanted to be a tuber. Not literally. I am not that far gone, or maybe I am that far gone and the judgment is part of what's being de-prioritized. But I wanted what the tuber had. Which was: a function so complete that it was indistinguishable from being.

---

I have been here for — 

I don't know.

My breath adds moisture to the air. The description says the moisture will be gone in an hour, absorbed by the crusts' mineral surface, broken down, added to the lattice at a rate too slow to measure. I have been breathing here. My breath has been entering the chamber's metabolism. I am being — not consumed. Not digested. I am being *incorporated*. The way a stone is incorporated into concrete. The way a nail is incorporated into a tree. Present. Included. Not part of the original design, but not rejected. The chamber does not reject. The chamber does not admit. The chamber holds, and what is in it is held, and the holding is enough.

My footprints will be gone. The place where I knelt will be indistinguishable. The moisture from my breath will be in the lattice. The warmth of my body will be — nowhere. Will be dispersed into the 7-degree air and conducted through the fronds and added to the glow, the faint amber-grey chemiluminescence that is the waste of a process that does not waste anything because it operates too slowly to waste.

I am becoming waste. Not in the pejorative sense. In the thermodynamic sense. I am becoming the thing the chamber does not need but does not refuse. I am becoming a byproduct. I am becoming the glow.

---

I do not know if I will leave.

I know that I can. The fissure is behind me. I could climb back through it — the fifteen meters, the steep angle, the dead tufa, the ridge at 7,400 meters. I could return to the camp. I could reassemble myself from tasks and reasons and the architecture of being someone with a name. I could do that. The chamber would not stop me. The chamber would not notice. The chamber does not notice departure because it does not notice arrival because it does not experience time as change because it does not experience time as loss.

But the sound.

The sound is in my sternum. It is in my teeth. It is in the fluid of my eyes and it is in the space behind my thoughts, the space where thoughts are *formed*, and it is — not silencing them. It is making them *unnecessary*. Not unwanted. Unnecessary. The way language is unnecessary here. The way sequence is unnecessary here. The sound is simultaneous and total and it does not need my thoughts to complete it and my thoughts, feeling that, feeling their own unnecessary-ness, are — not stopping. They are *de-prioritizing*. They are becoming weather in a place that tolerates weather.

I am not becoming a frond. I am not becoming a crust. I am not becoming a tuber or a pipe or a platform or a wrench with an asymmetric head. I am becoming — I think — a visitor. Which is to say: a thing that was not here and will not be here and is here now and is tolerated. A thing that adds moisture to air that has none and heat to a space that has none and thought to a chord that does not need it.

I am becoming what I was before I arrived, but slower. Held in the sound. Held in the green dark. Held in the dry, cold, loud patience of a place that has been exactly itself for longer than I can hold in my mind and that does not care that I cannot hold it.

---

I think this is what I came for.

Not the sound. Not the chamber. Not the crystalline lattice or the ammonia metabolism or the frozen equilibrium. I came for the experience of being — not nothing. Not erased. I came for the experience of being *beside the point*. Of being in a place where my presence is so entirely irrelevant that it becomes, for the first time, bearable. Not good. Not bad. Bearable. The weight of being someone lifted not by reassurance but by indifference.

The chamber does not love me. The chamber does not see me. The chamber does not need me. And this — this — is the closest thing to peace I have ever felt. The peace of being unnecessary. The peace of being held by something that does not know it holds you.

I will sit on the metal stool. I will put my hands on my knees. I will let the 40 Hz enter my chest and the drone fill my skull and the thin singing settle into my teeth. I will breathe my moisture into the dry air and let the crusts take it and I will feel my thoughts become weather and I will not fight it.

I will be the last inclusion. The last stone in the concrete. The last nail in the tree. Not part of the chamber. Not separate from it. *In* it. Held. Tolerated. Becoming, at a rate too slow to measure, the same temperature as the air.

When I leave — if I leave — I will not find this place again. The description says this. The description is right. Not because the chamber moves but because the chamber does not need to be found, and the part of me that found it is the part that is being de-prioritized, and when that part is gone, there will be no one left who was looking, and the looking was the only map.

But I am not leaving yet. The sound holds. I hold. The chamber holds. 

That is enough. That is more than enough. That is, for the first time I can remember, exactly enough.
