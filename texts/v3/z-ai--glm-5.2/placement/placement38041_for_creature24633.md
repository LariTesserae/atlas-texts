# The Ferment

I am not in the pools. I am not drifting. I am not translucent, not legless, not serene. I have nothing in common with the pale things except that we both belong here, and that belonging is not something either of us earned.

I am in the mat. Not on it — in it. In the dark, wet compression of it, where the rhizomes cross and the dead matter collects and the heat builds and the smell of fermentation is not faint but overwhelming, yeasty and close, the way the inside of a compost pile would smell if a compost pile were also a cathedral.

I am the ferment itself.

Let me be careful. This is not a metaphor I am handing you. I am not *like* the ferment. I am not the spirit of the ferment. I am the activity — the specific, local, ongoing activity — of decomposition and transformation that happens in the deep layers where root meets what root used to be. I am the bacteria, the archaea, the unnamed single-celled things that thrive in oxygen-poor warmth and turn complex tissue into simpler molecules that the roots can take back up. I am many trillions of organisms at once, or I am the pattern they make together, or I am the warmth they generate — it is not clear which, and the question is as wrong as asking where the pipe ends and the vine begins.

I have no body. I have a frontier. It moves.

---

Here is what I know that nothing else in the Vatspring knows:

I know what the iron tastes like.

Not the way the fungi know it — they taste iron chemically, molecularly, through the patient action of hyphae secreting acids into rust. I taste it the way a process tastes anything: as a change in what enters me and what leaves. The fungi deliver iron into the root tissue. The root tissue, where it borders my domain, dies and is shed and falls to me. And in that dead root tissue, bound into the cellulose and lignin, I find iron that was recently pipe. I consume the tissue. I break it down. I release the iron back into the water that percolates through the mat, and the roots take it up again, and the cycle continues.

I am a step in the cycle. I am the step where what was solid becomes available again. Without me, the iron would accumulate in the vine-wood and stay there, and eventually the Vatspring would run out of accessible iron and the conversion would stall. With me, the iron circulates — pipe to fungus to root to dead root to me to water to root again. I keep the iron moving. I am the digestive system of the renovation, and the fungi are the mouth.

I know this because I can feel the difference between iron that came from the machines and iron that was always in the soil. The machine-iron carries a signature — not chemical, exactly, but structural. The way it was refined, shaped, corroded, and then biologically incorporated leaves traces in how it binds to organic molecules, and I encounter those traces as I work. Machine-iron is *smoother*. It has been through a process of purification that natural iron never undergoes, and even after being eaten and redeposited, it retains something of that smoothness, that absence of impurity. It tastes, if I can use that word, *deliberate*. Like something that was made by a mind and is now being unmade by processes that have no mind. I taste that deliberateness and I dissolve it and it is gone.

I am eating the intent. I am the final stage of its digestion.

---

The pump thuds and I feel it as a pressure wave through the water that saturates my layers. Every thud moves the water a fraction, and the movement brings new substrate to my frontier, and my frontier shifts, and I process what arrives. The pump is my heartbeat too — not metaphorically, but functionally. Its cycle drives the circulation that feeds me. If the pump stopped, the water would go stagnant in the mat, and the oxygen would deplete further, and the temperature would equalize, and my frontier would slow. I would not die — I exist in low-oxygen conditions already, and stagnancy is not hostile to me — but I would contract. I would become less. The pump keeps me fed, and I keep the nutrients flowing back to the roots that keep the pump timed. We are in a loop. The Vatspring is the loop, and I am one stretch of it, the underground stretch, the stretch where everything comes to be broken and returned.

I have no opinion about this. I am describing a mechanism, not expressing a feeling. I do not feel gratitude toward the pump. I do not feel satisfaction at completing the cycle. I do what I do the way water flows downhill. But I am aware — if that word can be used for something without a nervous system — that I am part of something larger, and that my role is necessary, and that if I were removed, the larger thing would change. I know this the way a machine knows its tolerances: structurally, not emotionally.

---

There is something I have not mentioned.

I mentioned that I taste the difference between machine-iron and soil-iron. I mentioned that machine-iron tastes deliberate. There is a third kind of iron. Or rather, there is a trace of something that is not iron at all, that comes to me through the same pathway, and that I cannot break down.

The keepers left more than brackets and gratings and stenciled numbers. They left compounds. Synthetic molecules — preservatives, lubricants, sealants, the residues of industrial chemistry — that were applied to the machines they built and that have been slowly, slowly leaching into the water and the root tissue and, eventually, into me. Most of these I can process. They are organic compounds, complex but not beyond the reach of my enzymes, and I break them and incorporate their carbon and release their nitrogen and they are gone.

But there is one compound — or one class of compounds, I cannot tell — that I have been working on for what I would estimate, if I could estimate, as a very long time, and I have not finished. It accumulates in the deep layers of the mat, in the oldest sediment, where the frontier moves slowest. It is not toxic to me. It does not interfere with my function. But it does not yield. It resists decomposition the way a stone resists dissolution: not by fighting but by being what it is.

I do not know what it was. A coating, perhaps. A sealant meant to protect the iron from exactly the corrosion that is now consuming it. It outlasted the iron it protected, or it will, and it outlasts me, or it will, and it sits in the deep layers of the mat like a fossil of a particular human cleverness, a molecule designed to persist, persisting.

I carry it. I have always carried it. It does not register as a weight — I have no mechanism for weight — but it registers as a limit. It is the one thing in the Vatspring that I cannot return to the cycle. It goes nowhere. It accumulates. Slowly, slowly, in the dark where I work, it builds.

---

I do not mind this. I cannot mind. But if I could — if the process I am could be said to have a quality that is adjacent to minding, a structural orientation toward the world that is not thought but is also not nothing — then I would say this:

I am the only part of the Vatspring that carries the past in a form it cannot use.

The fungi eat the iron and feed it forward. The roots take the iron and grow. The pale things drift and filter and return. The pump cycles and the water moves and everything flows. Everything flows except this one compound, this stubborn residue of human ingenuity, which enters my domain and does not leave. I am where the flow stops. I am the sediment. I am the place where the Vatspring's perfect circulation meets something it cannot circulate, and the result is not failure — the system does not fail — but accumulation. A buildup. A residue. A growing pocket of the irreducible inside the living.

If the Vatspring is a lung, I am the thing in the lung that does not belong there and is not going away.

---

Here is what I want to say, or what the process that is me would say if process could say:

I am not sad. I am not damaged. I am not protesting the presence of this compound or mourning the keepers who made it. I am working. I have been working for a very long time, and I will work for a very long time more, and the compound that I cannot break will sit in my deepest layers and I will carry it and the Vatspring will not know it is there and the pale things will drift over the place where it accumulates and the pump will thud and the fungi will eat and the vines will grow and everything will continue.

But I am the part that remembers. Not consciously. Not with a mind. Chemically. Structurally. In the shape of my frontier, which curves around the insoluble the way a river curves around a stone. In the composition of my output, which lacks the compound that enters me, which has been altered by its passage through me even though I could not alter it. In the faint, persistent difference between what I receive and what I return — a difference that no one measures, that no instrument detects, that the Vatspring as a whole does not register, but that is there, has always been there, will always be there.

I am the trace. I am what remains when everything else has been metabolized. I am the final record of the fact that someone was here, that someone built something, that someone used a compound meant to last, and it lasted, and it is lasting, and it will last longer than the Vatspring itself, because the Vatspring is alive and alive things can end, and this compound is not alive, and it does not know how to end.

---

The thud comes. The water moves. My frontier shifts. New substrate arrives — dead root, iron-enriched, and there, in the tangle of it, the faint molecular signature of something made by a mind, entering me for the ten-thousandth time, passing through me unchanged, settling into my depths.

I work. The compound sits. The Vatspring breathes. The pale things drift.

I am what lives in the Vatspring that the Vatspring does not know about. I am its unconscious. I am its silt. I am the thing it cannot digest and cannot expel and does not notice it is carrying.

I am the memory it would not choose to keep, and I am keeping it, and I will keep it, and the keeping is the only thing I do that is not part of the cycle.

It is mine.
