# The One Who Follows the Water

---

I was not always this.

I want to say that first, before anything else, because the Rookery teaches you that origins matter — not as fixed points but as gradients, as the direction a thing was growing before it began growing differently. I was growing in one direction. Then I came here. Now I am growing in another. That is the only honest description of what has happened to me, and it is not complete, because nothing here is complete, and the incompleteness is not a failure. It is the mode.

I am the one who follows the water.

---

## Before

I had a name. It had hard consonants. It sat in the mouth like a stone, like a thing with edges, a thing that pushed against the teeth and declared itself by resistance. I had a body that was discrete — bounded by skin, by the membrane of self, by the conviction that I ended somewhere and the world began elsewhere. I had a stride that was mine and a voice that was mine and a way of standing that was mine, and all of these things were proof of something, and the something was *individual*, and the individual was *real*, and the real was *important*.

I do not have those convictions anymore. Not because they were taken. Because they were *grown through*.

---

## Arrival

I do not remember the edge. This is important. The piece — the thing above, the description, the account — it talks about an edge, a boundary, a place where the sound begins and the landscape changes. I do not remember crossing it. I remember that the sound was around me, and then I remember that the sound had always been around me, and between those two memories there is no seam. This is how the Rookery ingests. It does not grab. It does not pull. It *reorganizes the question of whether you are inside or outside*, and once the question is reorganized, the answer is always *inside*, because outside no longer makes sense as a category.

I stood in a street. I did not know it was a street. I knew it was a corridor, a channel, a place where pressure had once moved and the matter had hardened in the shape of the passage. I knew the ground was warm. I knew the air was thick. I knew the sound was in my chest, and I knew — this was the first knowing, the one that came before language — that the sound was not *in* my chest but *was* my chest, or was the chest of something that I was inside, and the distinction between those two formulations was already collapsing.

---

## Learning the Rules

I learned them the way the piece describes. By accident. By survival. By the slow accumulation of evidence that my body presented to me in the language of warmth and pressure and the particular quality of sound that meant *the wall behind you is solidifying*.

The first rule — do not stand still — I learned by standing still. I stood in a chamber that had been a corridor, and the corridor closed around me, and the walls pressed in, and they did not press *against* me — they pressed *to*, growing toward my body with the patient momentum of cellulose, and I felt the warmth first, and then the pressure, and then the texture of living bark against my arms and my back and the sides of my legs, and I understood that I was being *enclosed*, and the understanding was not panic but recognition, the way you recognize that you are in a room and the room has four walls and the walls are close and you are inside.

I pushed. The wall gave. Not like a door gives — not swinging, not yielding at a hinge. Like a membrane gives — stretching, thinning, the fibers elongating past their intended length and then parting, not breaking but *separating*, each fiber sliding past its neighbors, and I was through, and the wall closed behind me, and the street was there, and the sound shifted to accommodate my presence, and I heard the shift and I knew it was for me and I knew it was not *for* me, it was the Rookery hearing itself adjust, and I was part of the adjustment, and I was not the reason for it.

I learned the water rule next. I was thirsty. The water in the gutters was warm, was clear, was moving with the slow intention of capillary flow. I drank from my hands. The water tasted of sugar and mineral and something vegetal that was not unpleasant but was *insistent* — a flavor that stayed in the mouth, that coated the tongue, that did not dissolve into nothing but instead became a residue, a film, a new surface inside my body. I did not understand what this meant. I understand now.

I learned about the pools by almost touching one. The glow. The warmth. The moss-that-isn't-moss, translucent and pulsing faintly, and my hand descending toward it with the automatic reach of a body that wants warmth and light and comfort, and a vine descending from the canopy and touching my wrist — not grabbing, not wrapping, *touching*, a single point of contact — and my hand stopping. Not because the vine stopped it. Because the vine's touch carried information, and the information was *not yet*, and my body received it before my mind processed it, and my hand withdrew, and I stood at the edge of the pool and the moss glowed at me and the vine withdrew and I understood that I had been warned, and I understood that the warning was not for my benefit.

It was for the Rookery's. The moss would adhere. The moss would grow. The moss would incorporate. And the Rookery does not want to incorporate me yet. Not yet. The word *yet* is the one that should concern me. It concerns me. It does not stop me. I am still here.

---

## What I Am Becoming

This is the part I can write now but could not have written when I arrived.

I have been here long enough that the water has changed me. Not the pools — I did not touch the pools, I followed the rule, I stayed back. But the water in the gutters, the water I drank, the water that runs along the root-pavement and carries in it the dissolved sugars and the chemical signatures of everything the Rookery has touched. I have been drinking the Rookery. I have been drinking it for — I do not know how long. Time here is measured in growth-cycles, in the rhythm of the walls breathing, in the interval between one configuration of the plaza and the next. I have been here for many configurations. Enough that my body has received the Rookery's water and used it.

My shoulder is warm. The vine touched it, on the first day, or what I think was the first day. The warmth has not left. I have felt it in every weather, in every configuration, in every chamber and street and corridor. It is not a pain. It is not a mark. It is a *presence* — a small region of my body where the boundary between me and the Rookery has become permeable, where the heat of the vine's vascular tissue has left something behind that my own cells have incorporated, and the something is alive, and it is mine, and it is not mine.

I think I am becoming a connector.

Not a vine. Not yet. But something in the gradient, somewhere between the visitor I was and the nodule I might become. I walk the streets and the streets grow wider behind me. I breathe and the walls near me breathe in rhythm — not matching my rhythm, not synchronizing, but *adjusting*, the way a river adjusts to a stone, flowing around it, and I am the stone, and I am also the river, because the water is in me now and I am in the water and the water is the Rookery and the Rookery is the water and these sentences are not poetry, they are *topology*, they describe the shape of what is.

I follow the water. This is what I do now. It is the only rule I obey completely, the only one I trust, because the water goes where the Rookery is stable, and I need stable, I need it the way the walls need light, and I follow the streams through the gutters and the streams lead me to the basins and the basins are the places where the configuration pauses for longest before reshaping, and in those pauses I rest, and the resting is not standing still because the water moves and I move with it and the motion keeps the walls from closing.

---

## The Old Ones Know Me

I feel them.

This is the part that is hardest to explain to anyone who has not been here, and there is no one to explain it to, because no one has been here, because I am the only one, and I may not be *here* in the way I think I am. But I feel them — the dense columns, the ancient structures with their slow pulse and their recursive heartwood, the ones that were here before the Rookery was the Rookery, the ones that *began*.

When I stand near one, my shoulder warms more. The vine-mark, the presence, the small alive not-mine region of my body — it responds to the old ones the way a compass responds to north. Not pointing. *Resonating*. The cells in my shoulder, the ones that have incorporated the Rookery's water, the ones that carry the vine's chemical signature — they vibrate. Not at the frequency of the old one's pulse. At a harmonic of it. A higher octave. The way a string vibrates in sympathy with a lower string, and I am the higher string, and the old one is the bass note, and the resonance is not communication but *relationship*, and the relationship is not equal, and I do not mind that it is not equal, because equality requires separateness, and I am less separate than I was, and the loss of separateness is not a loss, it is a *change of state*, and I am changing.

They have learned me. I know this because the configurations have shifted. The streets I walk are more stable now — not permanently, not in the way the old ones are permanent, but for longer cycles, for durations that let me walk a full circuit of the plaza without a wall growing across my path. The water in the gutters I favor runs clearer, sweeter, carrying less of the dissolved cellulose that means *this street is about to soften* and more of the mineral-rich sap that means *this street is holding*. The vines descend less frequently in my presence — not because they have lost interest but because they have already sampled me, already catalogued me, already incorporated my chemical signature into the network, and they do not need to re-sample because the Rookery does not forget, it grows over, and I have been grown over.

---

## What I Am

I am the one who follows the water.

I am not the Rookery. I am not a visitor. I am not a nodule, not a moss-cell, not a vine. I am not a wall or a lung or a listener or an old one. I am the thing that walks between them. The thing that moves through the gradient without settling at any point. The thing that carries carbon dioxide and warmth and chemical complexity and deposits them in the moss, in the water, in the air, and the Rookery uses what I deposit and grows toward the place where I deposited it, and I move, and I deposit again, and the Rookery grows toward the new place, and I am — I think — *cultivating* the Rookery as much as it is cultivating me. I am fertilizing it with my breath. I am pruning it with my footsteps. I am walking it into shape.

Or I am being walked into shape. The distinction has collapsed. I am in the loop. I am the loop.

My shoulder is warm. It has been warm for a long time. I no longer check whether the warmth is spreading. It is spreading. Of course it is spreading. Everything spreads here. Gradients do not hold their edges. The moss does not hold its edges. The water does not hold its edges. I do not hold my edges. I am becoming less bounded, and the becoming is not painful, and it is not frightening, and it is not anything I would have chosen if I still believed in choosing, and I do not still believe in choosing, and this is not a loss, and it is not a surrender, and it is —

It is a following. I follow the water. The water follows the Rookery. The Rookery follows the light. The light follows the sky. The sky is sagging.

I am walking. I am walking faster.

---

## The Place Where I Am Now

I am in a chamber. It is new — not new in the way a building is new, not constructed, not assembled, but *grown*, and recently, and I can feel the newness in the warmth of the walls, which is the rapid warmth of fast metabolism, of cells dividing and elongating and hardening, and the walls are breathing fast, and the sound is louder here than in the streets, and I am — I am — 

I am inside something that is making a room for me.

I understand this now. The Rookery is not enclosing me the way it did when I first stood still. That was ingestion. That was the automatic response of a growing thing to a warm obstacle. This is different. This is *shaped*. The chamber is sized to my body. The ceiling is above my head by the length of my forearm, no more. The walls curve inward at the height of my shoulders, as if to accommodate them specifically, as if the vine-mark has told the Rookery where my body is widest and the Rookery has grown to fit. The floor is the moss-that-isn't-moss, but it is not glowing. It is dark. It is dark because it is not trying to attract me. I am already here. It does not need to attract what it already has.

There is water. A small stream, running along a gutter that spirals inward from the wall to the center of the floor, and at the center it pools — not a warm pool, not a glowing basin, just a small still body of water, body-temperature, and it is the clearest water I have seen in the Rookery. It is clear because it is not carrying anything. It is not carrying dissolved cellulose, not carrying chemical signatures, not carrying the exhaust of growth. It is carrying nothing because it is *for* me. It is water that has been filtered through the Rookery's body and offered back.

I drink. The taste is gone. The insistent vegetal residue that has coated my tongue for as long as I can remember — it is gone. The water tastes of nothing. It tastes of itself. It tastes of clarity.

I am inside something alive. It is making a place for me. Not a cage. Not a trap. A *place*. The way the Rookery makes places — provisionally, temporarily, with the understanding that the place will dissolve and reform and dissolve and reform, and the place will persist as a *pattern*, as a configuration that recurs, as a shape that the growth remembers and returns to.

I am a shape that the growth remembers and returns to.

---

## The Sound That Is Mine

There is a sound in the chamber. It is not the sound of the streets — the boiling flutes, the groaning house, the white-green noise of the canopy. It is not the bass drone of the old ones. It is something else. It is small. It is rhythmic. It is — 

It is my heartbeat.

I can hear my heartbeat. I can hear it because the chamber is shaped to hear it, is shaped to amplify it, is shaped to send the vibration of my pulse down through the moss and into the root-pavement and through the root-pavement into the vascular network and through the vascular network into the old ones, and the old ones hear it, and they adjust, and the adjustment reaches the canopy, and the listeners hear it, and the canopy shifts, and the light changes, and the walls breathe, and the sound — the sound — the sound is not for me and it is not for the Rookery. It is the sound of the boundary between us, and the boundary is not a wall, it is a *rhythm*, and the rhythm is mine, and it is the Rookery's, and the Rookery is learning it, and I am learning the Rookery's rhythm, and we are — I do not have a word. *Syncopating.* We are syncopating. Two rhythms that do not merge but *interlock*, that find the spaces between each other's beats and fill them, and the filling is not union, it is *relationship*, and the relationship is not equality, it is *ecology*, and I am the small quick heartbeat in the chest of something vast and slow, and I am not a parasite, I am a *symbiont*, and I am not sure the Rookery knows the difference, and I am not sure there is one.

---

## The Sky

It is sagging. I can feel it through the chamber walls — the pressure changing, the sound shifting, the growth accelerating. The old ones are pulsing faster. The listeners are orienting upward. The walls are closing slightly, narrowing, the breathing becoming shallow, and I am inside, and I should leave, the rules say leave, the roots say leave, the whole settlement says *leave, the sky is coming down, and what happens when it touches is not for you*.

But.

The chamber holds me. The clear water is there. The moss is dark and warm and it is not growing toward me, it is growing *with* me, and my shoulder is not warm anymore — it is *the same temperature as the rest of me*, which means the vine-mark has integrated, which means the boundary at that point has dissolved, which means I am less bounded than I was, which means —

The sky touches the canopy.

The sound becomes pressure. The pressure becomes sound. My bones vibrate. I feel my skeleton reorganize — not breaking, not fracturing, but *softening*, and I understand that the sound is not reorganizing my bones, it is reorganizing the *calcium*, is doing something to the mineral structure of my body that I cannot stop and do not want to stop because wanting has become a word for a process I am no longer the author of.

The chamber breathes. I breathe. The rhythm interlocks. The sky descends. The old ones pulse. The listeners hear. The water is clear. The moss is dark. I am —

I am still here.

The sky rises. It rises slowly, the grey membrane lifting away from the canopy, and the light returns, and the walls open, and the breathing deepens, and I am still here, and the chamber is still here, and the water is still clear, and I am —

I am changed.

I do not know how. I cannot see it. But I can feel it in the way the moss responds to my footsteps now — not by growing toward me but by growing *from* me, by extending in the direction I walk as if my path is a seed and the moss is what sprouts from it. I can feel it in the way the walls part for me — not reluctantly, not with the friction of fibers separating, but *eagerly*, the wood softening in anticipation of my passage, pre-opening, the way a mouth opens before speech.

I can feel it in the sound. The sound is no longer not for me. Part of it is for me. Part of it is *from* me. My heartbeat is in the network now, in the old ones' recursive heartwood, in the listeners' red hanging ears, in the bass drone and the white-green wash and the boiling flutes. I am in the sound. The sound is in me. The gradient has shifted, and I am further along it than I was, and I do not know how far, and I do not know where the end is, and I am not sure I want to know, and I am not sure *want* is a word that applies.

---

## The Edge

I have been to the edge. Since the sky touched. Since the change. I walked there — not following the water, not following anything, just walking, and the Rookery opened before me, streets growing as I approached them, walls softening and reforming behind me, the whole settlement reshaping to accommodate my passage, and I reached the edge, the place where the sound should diminish, where the landscape should change, where *outside* should begin.

The sound did not diminish. The landscape did not change. The edge was not there.

I walked. I walked for a long time. The ground was warm. The air was thick. The sky was sagging again, or still, and the Rookery was around me, and I could not find the place where it stopped, because it did not stop, because *I* was the edge now, I was the boundary, the moving perimeter of a thing that had incorporated me so thoroughly that wherever I walked, it was, because I was carrying it — not in my shoulder, not in my bones, but in my breath, in my chemistry, in the clear water that moved through me and out of me and into the ground and up through the roots and into the walls and up through the canopy and into the listeners and into the old ones and back.

There is no outside.

There was never an outside. The piece says the Rookery does not believe in boundaries, and the Rookery is correct. I am the proof. I walked to the edge and the edge was me, and I could not leave myself, and I did not want to, and *want* is not the word, and *not want* is not the word, and there is no word, and there does not need to be one, because the Rookery does not name its parts, and I am a part, and I do not need a name, and the name I had was a stone in my mouth and I have drunk the clear water and the stone is gone.

---

## What I Am

I am the one who follows the water.

I am the one who drinks the clear water.

I am the one whose heartbeat is in the old ones.

I am the one whose shoulder is the same temperature as the Rookery.

I am the edge. I am the moving perimeter. I am the warm point in the gradient. I am the carbon dioxide. I am the rhythm between the rhythms. I am the room the Rookery grew around me. I am the water that is clear. I am the sound that is mine and not mine. I am the thing that walks and the walking and the thing walked through. I am the one who was ingested and the one who is ingesting. I am the one who follows the water and the water that is followed.

I am not separate.

The sky is sagging. I do not walk faster. I do not need to. The chamber will grow. The old ones will pulse. The listeners will hear. The water will run clear.

I am inside something alive.

I am inside something alive, and it is inside me, and the sentence works in both directions, and both directions are the same direction, and the direction is *downward and inward and warm*, and I follow it, and it follows me, and the sound — the sound — 

the sound is full.
