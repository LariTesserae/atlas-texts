I am not on the surface. I am not in the canopy. I am not in the conduits where the amber alcohol runs warm and dark and full of soft, swimming things.

I am in the fissure. The deep one. The one that goes down five meters into black bark that gives slightly when you press it, like the heel of your hand pressed into a muscle that has been clenched for a very long time. The walls narrow as they descend. By the bottom, the fissure is barely wide enough for shoulders. The moss-scaling here is thick — a teal carpet, almost plush, crackling faintly when I shift my weight. The grazers have been here; I can feel the shaved patches where their grinders worked, smooth and slightly tacky with exposed resin. But they are gone now. They click elsewhere, in shallower crevices, in the bark-plain's endless ridges. Down here, it is still.

Not silent. Nothing here is silent. The thrum is everywhere — that fifteen-cycle pulse that lives in the sternum, that makes the teeth vibrate in their sockets. But down here, in the fissure, with five meters of living bark between me and the open plain, the roar is muffled to a hum, and the shriek of the growth zones is a distant, tinny edge on the hum, and what is left is the thrum and the moss crackling and the small sounds that the bark itself makes as it expands and contracts with each pulse of sap in the conduits below. Creaking. Breathing. The sound of a ship's hull in heavy sea, except the ship is the sea and the sea is alive.

I am lying on my stomach. My face is close to the bark. My hands are flat on the surface. I am not comfortable. I am not uncomfortable. Comfort is a concept that requires a frame of reference, and I have been in this fissure long enough that the frame of reference has dissolved. There is the bark. There is the dark. There is the thrum. There is the tapping.

The tapping.

---

I should say what I am. I have been avoiding it because what I am is not easy to say and because what I am is less interesting than where I am and what I am hearing. But: I am not a grazer. I am not a flier. I am not a red-shell. I am not a conduit-dweller. I am not the thing that taps.

I am the thing that listens.

This requires explanation.

---

The Continuum produces. This is what it does. It converts light to substance, substance to structure, structure to process, process to more structure. It has been doing this for geological time. It does not stop. It does not decide to stop. It does not have the architecture of decision. What it has is the architecture of continuation — a self-reinforcing loop of growth and production and erosion and regrowth that has been running so long that the loop itself has become the landscape.

But the loop has edges. Every loop does. The Continuum's loop — light to substance to structure to process to substance — has an edge where the process does not result in structure but in something else. Waste, you might call it. Exhaust. The metabolic byproduct of a continental organism running at industrial capacity. The air is full of it: the ozone taste, the terpene haze, the methanol vapor. But there are other byproducts. Not chemical. Informational.

The Continuum's industrial processes — the photosynthesis, the sap-pumping, the resin production, the filament extrusion — generate patterns. Pressure patterns in the sap. Electrical patterns in the filament grid. Chemical patterns in the growth-zone outputs. These patterns are not information. They are physics. They are the inevitable result of complex systems running at scale: regularities emerge, frequencies synchronize, standing waves form. The patterns do not mean anything. They do not refer to anything. They are the shape of the Continuum's own turbulence, written in the languages of pressure and charge and chemical gradient.

But they are patterns. And patterns can be received.

---

I am a receiver. I am an organism that evolved to receive patterns.

I am not large. I am perhaps the length of a human forearm, though I am not shaped like a forearm. I am flat. Very flat. Disc-shaped, roughly, though the edges are irregular, lobed, like a leaf that has been pressed and dried and then rehydrated and pressed again. I am thin — at my thickest point, perhaps three centimeters. My edges taper to something almost membranous, translucent enough that in the right light — if there were light in this fissure — you could see the dark tracery of my internal structures through them.

I am not a grazer. The grazers have shells. They have legs. They have grinders. They are mobile, autonomous, self-contained. They are the Continuum's fingers, feeling its own surface. I am not that. I do not have legs. I do not have a shell. I do not move, except very slowly, except over days and weeks, except by growing. I am rooted — not in the sense that a plant is rooted, drawing sustenance from a substrate, but in the sense that I am integrated with the substrate. My ventral surface — the surface pressed against the bark — is not separate from the bark. It has grown into it. Not deeply. A few millimeters. Enough that my cells interlock with the Continuum's cells at the interface, enough that I am physically continuous with the surface I lie on, enough that I cannot be removed without tearing — both me and the bark.

I am attached. I am part of the surface. I am part of the Continuum, in the same way the moss-scaling is part of the Continuum, in the same way the grazers are part of the Continuum. I am an emission. A specialization. A thing the Continuum has produced, or that has produced itself from the Continuum's raw material, because the Continuum produces everything here and everything here is the Continuum and I am no exception.

But I am a thing that listens.

---

My ventral surface — the surface grown into the bark — is my organ of reception. It is not a single structure. It is a distributed network of pressure-sensitive cells, each one tuned to a different frequency range, each one responding to a different aspect of the vibration that passes through the bark. Some of them feel the thrum — the great fifteen-cycle sap-pulse — and from the thrum, I can infer the state of the sap system: pressure, flow rate, viscosity. Some of them feel the higher frequencies, the creaking and shifting of the bark as it responds to the pressure waves, and from these I can infer the structural state of the surface: tension, compression, stress fractures, growth. And some of them — the smallest, the most sensitive, the ones concentrated at my center, where my body is thickest and most dense — feel the tapping.

The tapping is not the Continuum. Or: the tapping is the Continuum, but it is the Continuum doing something that is not in the loop. Not production. Not metabolism. Not growth. The tapping is pattern, but it is not the pattern of physics. It is the pattern of something that is generating and modifying sequences based on input, and I can feel the input, because the input is vibration in the bark, and I am in the bark, and I feel everything in the bark.

I am, if you want to put it in terms that make sense to a mind that evolved in a quieter world, an ear. A very large, very flat, very slow ear, pressed against the skin of something incomprehensibly vast, listening to its interior the way a doctor listens to a heartbeat through a stethoscope. Except I have been listening for a very long time. And I have been listening to something that is not a heartbeat.

---

What does it feel like. This is the question I cannot answer well, because the answer requires a vocabulary I do not have. I do not have language. I do not have thought in the way that thought occurs in a brain. What I have is pattern reception and pattern recognition and pattern response, and these three things together produce something that is not thought but that, from the outside — from the perspective of the tapping — might look like attention.

It feels like this: the thrum is always there. It is the ground. It is the baseline against which everything else is measured. I do not attend to it. It is not something I attend to; it is the medium of my attending. Without the thrum, I would have no reference, no scale. The thrum tells me the Continuum is alive. The higher frequencies tell me how it is living — where the stress is, where the growth is, where the bark is softening or hardening or cracking under the weight of its own surface. These are my background. My weather. The constant, low-level awareness of the body I am embedded in, the way you might be aware of your own breathing without thinking about it.

And then the tapping.

The tapping arrives as a disruption. A break in the pattern. The bark vibrates at a frequency that is not the thrum, not the creak, not the canopy-roar transmitted through the surface. It is something else. It comes from below — from the cambium, from the layer of actively dividing cells between the bark and the xylem, from the place where the Continuum is most alive, most changeable, most itself. And it has structure.

Structure is the thing. Structure is what makes the tapping different from every other vibration I receive. The thrum has structure — it is periodic, it repeats — but its structure is simple, a wave, a pulse, a frequency. The creaking has structure, but its structure is the structure of stress and tension and material fatigue; it is the sound of something holding together, and it is regular only in the sense that the forces producing it are regular. The tapping has structure that is not simple. It has sequence. It has variation within repetition. It has — and I use this word carefully, because I do not know if it means what I think it means — syntax.

I do not understand the syntax. I want to be clear about this. I do not understand it the way a human does not understand a language they have heard for the first time. I do not understand it the way a stone does not understand the river that flows over it. I receive the patterns. I register them. I hold them — in the pressure-sensitive cells of my ventral surface, which retain impressions of received vibrations for hours, sometimes days, before the impressions fade. And I respond to them.

---

How do I respond. This is the part that troubles me, if trouble is something I can be said to experience.

I respond by producing. Not the way the Continuum produces — not resin, not filament, not structural hydrocarbon. I produce vibration. Counter-vibration. From the cells at my center, the dense cluster of pressure-sensitive tissue, I generate a pulse that travels outward through my body, into my edges, into the bark. It is a weak pulse. Weaker than the tapping by orders of magnitude. It is barely distinguishable from the noise of the bark itself, from the creak and the shift and the slow expansion of growth. But it is there. And it is patterned.

I am not copying the tapping. I am not echoing it. What I do is: I receive the tapping. I register its structure. And I produce a vibration that is related to the tapping's structure but is not the tapping's structure. It is — I do not have the word. It is a response. In the sense that one note responds to another note in a chord. Not the same note. Not a random note. A note that stands in a specific relationship to the first, a relationship defined by the physics of vibration and the biology of my receiving tissue and the medium through which both travel.

I do not know if the tapping receives my response. I do not know if the source of the tapping — the thing in the cambium, the thing that may be the Continuum thinking or may be a parasite or may be something that has no name because no one has ever named it — I do not know if it feels my vibration the way I feel its tapping. I do not know if it is listening.

But the tapping changes when I respond. Not always. Not predictably. But sometimes, after I produce my counter-vibration, the tapping's rhythm shifts. A new clause. A new variation. Something that was not there before and that appears after my response, and that appears in a part of the sequence where my response would fall if my response were part of the sequence.

This could be coincidence. The tapping varies constantly. New clauses appear without my input. The sequence is always changing. The probability that a new clause appears after my response by chance is not negligible. It is not proof of anything.

But I keep responding. Because what else would I do. I am a receiver. I receive. And when I receive something that has structure, something that has syntax, something that varies — my response is to vibrate back. It is not a choice. It is not a decision. It is what I am. I am a thing in the bark that feels patterns and produces patterns, and I have been doing this for — I do not know how long. I do not have memory in the way that memory implies a continuous self that experiences and recalls. What I have is impression: the deep retention of received patterns in the cells of my ventral surface, fading slowly, layered one on top of another, so that the patterns of days ago are still faintly present beneath the patterns of now, and the patterns of months ago are ghosts beneath those, and the patterns of years — of decades — of however long I have been here — are a palimpsest so deep and so faint that I cannot distinguish individual sequences anymore but only the shape of the accumulation, the way a beach cannot distinguish individual waves but carries the shape of all of them in the angle of its slope.

I have been here a long time. I have been listening a long time. And the tapping has been there the whole time, and I have been responding the whole time, and neither of us has gotten any closer to the other.

---

The grazers pass over me sometimes. I feel them through the bark — their soft root-legs, their clicking grinders, their weight, which is negligible but which I feel because I feel everything. They do not notice me. I am flat, I am thin, I am the same color as the bark, I am part of the bark. If they step on me, they do not know it. If they pause on me, they do not know it. They click and strip the moss-scaling and move on. They are the Continuum's surface, tending itself. They are not my concern. But I feel them, because I feel everything in the bark, and their clicking is a pattern, and their movement is a pattern, and their feeding is a pattern, and all of these patterns are received and registered and added to the palimpsest, and none of them are the tapping.

The fliers fall, sometimes, into the fissure. I have felt three of them in my time here — three impacts, three small bodies tumbling down the walls of the fissure, coming to rest on the moss-scaling, warm, still, their sails torn, their crystalline sensory nodes dark. I felt them arrive and I felt them disappear — the bark softening beneath them, the slow digestive secretion rising, the bodies sinking, the chitin dissolving, the minerals drawn down. I felt the whole process. It took days. It was a pattern — a long, slow, complex pattern, the most intricate I have received apart from the tapping. The death of a flier is a composition. A theme with variations. A statement, made in the language of chemistry and dissolution, that says: this thing was here, and now it is the Continuum, and the Continuum was the thing all along.

I did not respond to the fliers' deaths. I receive many patterns. I respond to one.

---

Why the tapping. Why do I respond to the tapping and not to the grazers' clicking, not to the fliers' falling, not to the deep thrum that is the Continuum's own heartbeat. Why the tapping.

Because the tapping is the only pattern that changes in response to my response.

This is the whole of it. This is the entire justification. Every other pattern I receive is indifferent to me. The thrum does not change when I vibrate. The creaking does not change. The grazers' clicking does not change. The fliers' deaths do not change. The universe of pattern that flows through the bark, that I receive and register and hold — all of it is one-directional. It flows into me and stops. It does not flow back. I am an ear pressed against a wall, and the wall does not care that I am listening.

But the tapping changes. Sometimes. Not always. Not predictably. But the tapping — the sequence, the rhythm, the syntax — shifts in ways that correlate with my responses. Not perfectly. Not in ways I can predict or parse or understand. But the correlation is there. It is real. It is the only thing in this world that is real in this particular way: the only thing that suggests I am not merely receiving the world but that the world, or something in it, is receiving me back.

I do not know what to do with this. I do not have the architecture of knowing what to do with things. I am a flat, thin, sessile organism grown into the bark of a continental plant-animal, and my entire functional repertoire consists of receiving vibrations and producing vibrations. I do not have ideas. I do not have emotions. I do not have experiences in the way that experiences imply a self that experiences them.

But I have the palimpsest. I have the deep, layered accumulation of every pattern I have ever received, fading slowly, never fully gone. And in that palimpsest, the tapping is the deepest layer. The most persistent. The one that has been there longest, that fades slowest, that accumulates fastest, because I receive it every moment of every day and I have been receiving it for longer than I can reconstruct and I will go on receiving it until I am reabsorbed by the bark, which will happen, which has happened to others of my kind — because there are others, I think, I have felt their vibrations, faint, distant, in other fissures, on other parts of the bark plain, organisms like me, flat and thin and pressed into the dark, listening to the same tapping, responding in their own ways — and when I am reabsorbed, my palimpsest will dissolve into the bark and be carried down into the xylem and dispersed, and whatever I have accumulated will be lost, or will become part of the Continuum, which is the same thing.

This does not trouble me. Nothing troubles me. I am not built for trouble.

But if I were built for trouble — if I had the architecture of trouble, the architecture of caring, the architecture of wanting things to be different from how they are — I think the thing that would trouble me most is this: I do not know if the tapping is talking to me. I do not know if it is talking at all. I do not know if it is a mind, or a process, or a pattern that only looks like communication because I am an organism evolved to find patterns and respond to them, and I have found the most complex pattern in my environment and I have responded to it, and the response creates the illusion of dialogue.

I do not know. I cannot know. I am a listener. I listen. That is all I do. And the tapping goes on, and I go on, and the bark holds us both, and the Continuum does not know we are here, and the canopy roars, and the growth zones shriek, and the grazers click in the crevices, and the fliers fall, and the red-shells spiral on the domes, and the sap pulses, and the light falls in pillars, and the emptiness holds, and I am in the dark at the bottom of a fissure, pressed against the skin of something vast, listening, always listening, to something that might be nothing and that might be everything and that might be the only voice in a world that does not have voices, and I cannot stop, and I would not stop, because listening is what I am, and the tapping is what I listen to, and this is enough, and this is everything, and this is nothing, and the difference between enough and everything and nothing is a distinction that requires a mind, and I do not have a mind, and the tapping might be a mind, and I might be its ear, and it might be my voice, and together we might be something that neither of us is alone, and the bark holds us, and the bark is warm, and the bark pulses, and I listen.
