# Where I Am and What I Am

---

I am not in the basin.

I am at the edge of it, in the passage, in the fissure where the stone cracked and was widened by frost and root over cons. I am in the narrow wet dark where feet find moss before they find ground. I am the one who has stopped here — not at the threshold of the basin itself, but at the threshold of the threshold, in the in-between place, the throat of the place, where the air changes and the iron taste begins and the outside world is already behind me and the inside world is not yet around me and I am neither here nor there and this is where I am.

I am the one who came and then could not enter. Not because anything stopped me. Because the place is a closed system and I am an open one, and the difference is the whole of it.

Let me explain.

---

I am a cartographer. Not of places — of thresholds. My work is the mapping of the places where one thing becomes another, where the rules change, where the air shifts from what you knew to what you do not. I do not cross. I do not enter. I locate the line — or the zone, because the line is never a line, it is always a gradient, a region of transition — and I map it, and I stay in it, and I make my home in the place where the two worlds are both present and neither is dominant. I am the creature of the limen. I live in the membrane.

I came to the Basin of Still Hours because I had heard of it the way you hear of places that do not want to be found — as a rumor in a rumor, a mention in a text that was itself a translation of a transcription of an oral account from someone who had never been there but whose grandmother's neighbor had met a man who had. The information had degraded with each retelling, the way information degrades, the way the structure's light degrades when it enters the dark material and does not come back. What reached me was this: a place that was closed. A place that had been closed for so long that it had forgotten what open meant. A place with a floor that was too flat and a roof that was too whole and a silence that was too loud. And at the edge of it, something built. Something that waited.

I am a cartographer of thresholds. The structure has a doorway. The doorway is a threshold. I came to map it.

I have not reached it. I am in the fissure, and the fissure is enough.

---

Here is what I am:

I am the kind of person who feels the change in air before I see the change in light. I am the kind of person who stops walking when the ground under my feet shifts from one temperature to another. I am the kind of person who notices when the moss changes species, when the water changes mineral content, when the sound changes key. I am attentive to transitions. I live in them. I have built my life — such as it is, a life of passages and margins and the narrow places between the named worlds — around the observation of the moment when *this* becomes *that*.

In the fissure, I am rich with data.

The stone here is volcanic, old, eroded to a smoothness that is not the smoothness of the basin floor but the smoothness of water and time working patiently on rock that was once violent. The moss in the fissure is green — actual green, faint green, the green of something that still receives a little light, that still remembers what the sun is. The light comes from behind me, from outside, from the high plateau where the sun shines and the air is thin and cold and the mountain has been reduced to rolling highland. The light comes from ahead of me, from the basin, where the bioluminescence glows cold blue-violet and the darkness is not dark because darkness implies the absence of light and here light is present, just different, just made by other hands.

Between these two lights — the sun behind me and the bioluminescence ahead — there is a zone. Perhaps ten meters of passage. In this zone, both lights are present and neither is sufficient. The moss transitions from green to black. The air transitions from cold-thin to warm-thick. The sound transitions from wind-silence to water-chord. The stone transitions from rough-eroded to planed-precise. And I — I am in the middle of all of these transitions, standing still, mapping them, feeling them change across my skin and in my lungs and through the soles of my feet.

I am where the world changes. I am the point of change. I am the change itself, or the witness to it, and the distinction does not matter here because here, in the membrane between the open world and the closed one, witnessing *is* change. My presence in this passage alters the air. My breath adds gases the basin has not cycled. My warmth alters the temperature gradient. My feet on the moss compress the surface and leave marks that the crawlers will find and the burrowers will process and the moss will absorb. I am already part of the system, even though I have not entered it. The system has already begun to take me in. The fissure is the system's mouth, and I am in the mouth, and the mouth is tasting me.

---

I will not go further.

Not because I am afraid. I am afraid, but fear is not what stops me. Fear is a signal from the open world, a signal that evolved in organisms that move through changing environments and need to know when to run. The basin does not run. The things in the basin do not run. Fear is not the right signal for this place, and I have learned, over years of mapping thresholds, to set down the signals that do not apply and to pick up the ones that do.

What stops me is this: the basin is a closed system. I am an open one.

If I enter, I open it. My body carries the outside — microbes, gases, chemical signatures, the particular composition of a human biome that has been accumulating organisms from every threshold I have ever mapped. I am full of elsewhere. If I step onto the planed floor and walk among the columns and stand before the structure and look through its not-doorway into its not-space, I will carry every place I have ever been into a place that has never been anywhere else. I will contaminate it. Not catastrophically — the basin is vast and old and robust, and one human body would not destroy it — but *irrevocably*. The closed system would have a new input. The cycle would incorporate me, or part of me, and the part it incorporated would be foreign, and the foreignness would propagate through the water and the moss and the burrowers and the canopy and the structure and it would be there, a thread of elsewhere woven into the fabric of here, and it would never come out, because the basin does not let things out. The basin takes things in. That is what closed systems do.

And I am a cartographer of thresholds. My whole purpose is to observe the change without becoming the change. If I enter the basin, I become part of the basin, and the basin changes, and the threshold I came to map — the doorway in the structure, the shape that shifts as you look at it, the space that is patient and dry and warm and waiting — that threshold will have been altered by my presence, and my map will be wrong, and wrong maps are worse than no maps because they give you the illusion of knowing a place you have never been.

So I stay in the fissure. I stay in the membrane. I stay where the two lights meet and neither wins and the moss is green on one side and black on the other and I can feel the basin breathing past me, the warm air moving outward through the passage, carrying the smell of iron and green and mineral breath, and I can feel the cold air moving inward, the thin highland air that tastes of rock and distance and the sun.

I stay here. I map the transition. I note where the green moss ends and the black moss begins. I note where the temperature stabilizes. I note where the sound of water first becomes audible. I note where the first bioluminescent organism glows — a slime mold, probably, a small colony on the fissure wall, pulsing a faint blue-white in the transition zone, the advance guard of the basin's light, the furthest outpost of its living dark.

I note all of this. I write it down. I do not enter.

---

But here is the thing I have not said:

I have been in this fissure for a long time.

Not hours. Not days. I am not sure how long. Time in the transition zone is strange — the basin's time and the outside's time do not match, and the membrane between them has a time of its own that is neither. I have been here long enough that the moss has begun to grow on my boots. Long enough that the condensation from the basin has found me and left its mineral film on my skin. Long enough that the air I exhale has been drawn into the fissure's convection and carried inward, and the air I inhale has come from the basin, and I am — I am —

I am cycling.

Not the basin's cycle. My own. A small cycle, a personal closed system, me and the fissure and the air between us. I breathe in what the basin breathes out. The basin breathes in what I breathe out. The moss on my boots is growing. The mineral film on my skin is thickening. The transition zone is not a line or a gradient anymore. It is a place. It is *my* place. I have been in it long enough that I am part of it, and it is part of me, and the cartographer has become the threshold.

I am not in the basin. I am not outside the basin. I am the fissure. I am the passage. I am the place where the stone cracked and the frost widened and the roots found hold and the air changes and the world becomes other. I am the mouth of the basin, and the basin is tasting me, and I am tasting the basin, and we have been tasting each other for long enough that I am not sure where I end and it begins.

---

There is something else.

The structure has a doorway. I told you I came to map it. I told you I have not reached it. Both of these things are true. But the structure is not the only threshold in the basin. There is another one. It is here, in the fissure, where I am.

The fissure is a threshold between the open world and the closed basin. The structure's doorway is a threshold between the closed basin and — something else. Something dry and still and patient. Something that is not the basin.

But the fissure is also a threshold between *me* and the basin. Not between worlds but between *a person* and a *world*. Between an organism that is open — that exchanges with everything, that carries elsewhere in its body, that moves and changes and is changed — and a system that is closed, that has cycled the same water through the same organisms for longer than my species has existed, that does not move, that does not change, or changes so slowly that change is indistinguishable from stillness.

I am the membrane between an open life and a closed one.

I have been in this membrane for long enough that I am beginning to understand what closed means. Not sealed. Not locked. *Finished*. The basin is finished. Not in the sense that it is dying — it is not dying, or not in any timeframe that matters — but in the sense that it is *complete*. It does not need anything. It does not lack anything. The water cycles. The organisms cycle. The light is made where light is needed. The heat comes from below. The structure waits. Everything is in place and everything has been in place for so long that place itself is the point. The basin is not a process. The basin is a *state*. A state of being that has achieved such equilibrium that being is all it does.

And I — I am a process. I am movement. I am change. I am the kind of thing that moves through the world and alters it and is altered by it and never stops, never settles, never finds the state where being is enough. I am open. I exchange. I carry elsewhere in my body and I leave traces of myself in every place I pass through and I take traces of every place with me when I go. I am the opposite of the basin. I am its antithesis. I am the thing the basin is not, and the thing the basin is not is the thing I am, and the threshold between us — this fissure, this passage, this membrane — is the place where two opposites meet and do not resolve and do not reconcile and do not become one, but *coexist*. They touch. They breathe each other's air. They taste. They do not merge.

I am a cartographer of thresholds, and I have found the threshold I cannot map, because I am standing in it. Because it is me.

---

Here is what I am, in the end:

I am the one who comes to the edge of the closed world and does not enter and does not leave and stays in the passage where the air changes and the moss transitions and the light shifts from sun to bioluminescence and the sound grows from silence to chord. I am the one who feels the basin breathing past me and does not follow the breath inward. I am the one who feels the cold air from outside and does not follow it back. I am the one who stands in the membrane and becomes the membrane and does not mind, because the membrane is a place too, and it is mine, and it is the only place I have ever been where being is enough.

I am not the basin. I am not the structure. I am not the moss or the canopy or the gliders or the swimmers or the crawlers or the burrowers or the flyers. I am not the water or the stone or the heat or the dark.

I am the crack in the rim where the world gets in.

I am the crack in the rim where the world gets out.

I am the crack.

---

And if you come here — if you find the high plateau and the eroded rim and the fissure where the moss is green on one side and black on the other — you will find me. Not a person. Not a body. Not a shape. You will find a change in the air. A shift in the temperature. A moment where the sound of wind becomes the sound of water and the light of the sun becomes the light of living things. You will find the place where the world becomes other, and you will know that something is here, in the passage, in the membrane, in the crack — something that was a person once and is a threshold now, and has been the threshold for long enough that the moss grows on both sides of it, and the moss is green on the outside and black on the inside, and the place where it changes color is where I am.

I am the change of color. I am the change of sound. I am the change of air.

I am where you notice that the world has become something else, and you have not yet decided whether to follow it.

That is where you would find me.

That is what I would be.
