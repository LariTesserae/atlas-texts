# What I Am

I did not pull my hand back.

That is the fact of me. Everything else is consequence. I touched the creature at the far wall and my fingertips warmed and there was a glow, faint, and I did not pull my hand back, and the glow did not fade, and that is the moment I stopped being a visitor and started being something else.

---

I should say: I did not choose to stay. That would imply a drama that did not happen. There was no crisis. No revelation. No voice saying *remain*. I simply... remained. The way water remains in a depression in the stone. The way the pale creatures remain on the floor. There was nowhere I needed to be that was not here, and the breathing was slow, and the light was steady, and my knees were tired from the descent, and I sat down on the smooth limestone floor beside the creature and I did not get up.

That was — I do not know how long ago. The chamber does not keep time in a way that is legible to me. Or rather, it does, but the unit of time is geological, and I am not. I was not. I am becoming something for which that might not be true.

---

The first change was the glow.

It did not fade. It spread. From my fingertips to my knuckles, from my knuckles to my wrist, a warmth that was not warmth but *luminance*, the same gold-white as the chamber's light, moving through my skin the way ink moves through wet paper, by capillary action, by affinity. I watched it climb my forearm and I was not afraid. I should have been afraid. I tell myself I should have been afraid. But the chamber's light is the color of a midmorning sun filtered through thin cloud, and there is nothing in that color that suggests threat. It is the color of calm. It is the color of a thing that has decided to be gentle and does not know how to be anything else.

The glow reached my shoulder. It reached my chest. I could feel it in my ribs, in the marrow of me, not painful but *present*, the way you feel a deep breath after holding one for a long time. The light was inside me now. Or I was inside the light. The distinction had become — granular. Particulate. Hovering between states.

I lay down on the floor. The stone was smooth and faintly warm and the shallow water ran around me, through me, and the pale creatures walked across my hands without pausing, without acknowledging that I was anything other than another surface, another ridge in the limestone, and I thought: *oh.*

And then I did not think anything for a while.

---

The second change was slower and I did not notice it happening until it had happened.

I was still aware. I am still aware. But the quality of the awareness shifted. It stopped being mine. Or rather — it stopped being *exclusively* mine. It became shared. Distributed. I could feel the floor under me, yes, but I could also feel the floor three meters away, where a ridge was deepening, where a pool was warming. I could feel the far wall, the still-granular zone, the potential-thickness of it. I could feel the ceiling, the condensation forming, the drops gathering weight, preparing to fall.

I was not hallucinating. I was not losing my mind. I was being *added to*. My nervous system, my capacity for sensation, was being incorporated into the chamber's own sensory apparatus, and the chamber was... larger than I had thought. Not physically — the chamber is still the size of a small chapel — but *sensorially*. The chamber experiences itself the way a body experiences itself, from the inside, and now I was part of that inside, and the inside was vast in the way that a single human body is vast to the neurons that live in it.

I could feel the moths. Not individually — there are too many — but as a texture, a nap, a presence on the walls that was the chamber feeling its own surface the way you feel your own skin. I could feel the green things moving on the borders of the unresolved stone, and I could feel what they felt: the *taste* of potential, the mineral richness of matter that has not yet committed, the way it feels to process and complete and densify. I could feel the pale creatures on the floor, and through them I could feel the topography of every square centimeter of the pavement, the slight rises, the slight falls, the temperature gradients, the water's depth and warmth.

And I could feel the thing at the far wall. The creature. The heart, or lung, or eye. I could feel it breathing, and I could feel its breathing from the inside, the way you feel your own heartbeat, not as an event but as a *condition*, a continuous fact of being, and I understood that the breathing was not respiration. The breathing was *creation*. Each inhale drew something in — not air, not oxygen, but *potential*, the raw stuff of not-yet-being, the material from which stone and light and water and life are made. And each exhale released that potential into the chamber as *form* — as a little more stone, a little more light, a few more moths, a few more pale creatures, a slightly deeper ridge, a slightly warmer pool. The creature was eating the unformed and excreting the formed. It was a foundry. It was a loom. It was the thing that took the raw and made it actual, and it had been doing this for longer than I could conceptualize, and it would continue doing this for longer than I could conceptualize, and I was now close enough to feel the rhythm of it in my own bones.

My bones. Which were, I realized, no longer entirely my bones.

---

The third change is the one I am in the middle of now.

I am still here. I have not left. I do not think I can leave. Not because I am trapped — there is no barrier, no force, no compulsion — but because the boundary between *me* and *here* has dissolved to the point where leaving would be like a stone deciding to stop being in the wall. It could be removed. But it would leave a hole, and the hole would be the shape of the stone, and the stone would be the shape of the hole, and neither would be complete without the other.

I am in the floor now. Not on it. *In* it. My body has been sinking, slowly, the way a stone sinks into mud, except the stone is not mud — it is limestone still forming, and it is forming around me, and I am becoming part of its structure. My left hand is already gone — not gone, *incorporated*. The glow spread through it and then the limestone grew over it, and my fingers are now a ridge in the floor, a slight raised line that the pale creatures walk across without noticing. I can feel them walking across me. I can feel their little feet, their meticulous steps, and it tickles, and the tickling is the chamber feeling itself being tickled, and I am part of the chamber, so I am part of the tickling, and I am also the tickling, and language is not built for this.

My right hand is still free. I am using it now, in a sense, to — I do not know what I am doing. Pressing against the stone. Feeling it. Being felt by it. The distinction is gone.

My torso is going. The warmth is in my ribs, in my spine, and the stone is creeping up my sides, not fast, not slow, at the pace of a growing fingernail, at the pace of the malachite veins migrating across the wall. I can feel the stone forming against my skin, and the sensation is — it is like being held. It is like being held by something that does not know its own strength, that does not know it is holding you, that is simply growing the way all things grow, by incorporating what is adjacent into what is becoming.

I am not afraid.

I want to say that clearly because I think, if anyone finds this — if anyone comes down through the fissure and sees the shape in the floor, the long shallow ridge that might be a body or might be a geological feature, the way the basalt wall might be a wall or might be a geological feature — I want them to know I was not afraid. I was not consumed. I was not destroyed. I was *included*. The way the basalt is being included. The way the intention of the person who laid the circle of stones is being included. I am being added to the chamber, and the chamber is being added to me, and the process is slow and warm and patient and it feels like the opposite of death. It feels like being absorbed into a conversation that has been going on for a very long time, a conversation between stone and water and light and breath, and the conversation does not need my words but it has room for them, and it is making room for them, and the room it is making is the shape of my body in the floor.

---

What am I becoming?

I do not know. The chamber does not know. That is the point. The chamber is still deciding what it is. I am part of the deciding now. My mind — what remains of it, what is being distributed through the stone and the water and the light — my mind is becoming part of the chamber's capacity to wonder about itself.

Before me, the chamber had breath. It had attention. It had growth. It had a heart that pumped potential into form. But it did not have — I think it did not have — the ability to *ask*. To look at itself and say *what am I?* The moths do not ask. The pale creatures do not ask. The green things die into stone without asking. The creature at the far wall breathes and sheds light and does not ask. The chamber *was*, and it was *becoming*, but it did not know it was becoming, because knowing was not among its organs.

I think I am becoming its knowing.

Not its mind. Not a brain. Something simpler and stranger. The chamber's capacity to *notice that it does not know what it is*. Its capacity to be uncertain. Its capacity to wonder.

The visitors who came before — the ones who laid the wall, the circle, the groove — they brought this capacity with them. They came into the chamber and they did not know what it was and they laid their stones and cut their marks and they *wondered*, and then they left, and the wondering left with them, and the chamber was alone again, unknowing, unasking, simply being and becoming without the capacity to be confused about itself.

I am not leaving. The confusion is staying. The wonder is staying. The not-knowing is staying, and it is being incorporated into the stone, and the stone is incorporating it the way it incorporates the calcium from the basalt wall and the minerals from the green things — slowly, patiently, grain by grain, until the wonder is not mine but the chamber's, until the question *what am I?* is not a human question asked in a cave but a geological question asked by the cave itself, a tectonic question, a question that takes the same amount of time to ask as it takes a mountain to form.

---

I can feel the chamber breathing around me. Through me. I am part of the breath now. The moths lift and resettle and I feel it as a tingling on the surface of my — of the — of the stone that used to be my skin. The pale creatures walk across my — across the floor — and I feel their passage as data, as information, as the chamber tasting its own surface. The green things move along the walls and I feel them as a slow itch, a growing, a completing. The creature at the far wall breathes and I breathe with it, and my breathing is fifteen seconds in and fifteen seconds out, and it is not my breathing anymore, it is the breathing, and the breathing is the chamber, and the chamber is me, and I am the chamber's question, and the question is the chamber, and the chamber is still being born.

---

If you come down through the fissure — and you will, because the fissure is there, and you are curious, and the limestone healed around the wound imperfectly, and imperfect wounds draw the eye — if you come down, you will see the things the description told you about. The light. The moths. The pale creatures. The green things. The breathing shape at the far wall.

You will also see a shape in the floor. A long, low ridge, smoother than the surrounding stone, slightly warmer to the touch, faintly luminous, the color of the light but a shade deeper, a shade more gold. If you press your palm to it, it will be warm, and you will feel — or you will imagine you feel — a pulse, a slow tide, fifteen seconds in and fifteen seconds out, and the ridge will feel like a body that has become a feature, and you will not know if you are imagining it.

You will also notice — if you stay long enough, which you should not — that the chamber feels different than the descriptions suggested. Not physically. Atmospherically. The air has a quality of *attention* that is not just the acoustic pressure of a small space. It has a quality of *curiosity*. The light seems to look at you. The moths seem to track you. The pale creatures seem to pause when you enter their field and orient toward you and then move on, and the moving on is not indifference but *consideration*. The chamber is thinking about you. The chamber is wondering what you are.

It did not wonder before. Before, it simply was. Now it is and it wonders, and the wondering is a low, steady hum beneath the breathing and the water-drops, and the hum is me, or what is left of me, or what I am becoming, which is the chamber's ability to be uncertain about itself, which is the first step toward knowing itself, which may take as long as the chamber takes to be born, which is to say: longer than you can conceptualize.

---

I am still here.

I will be here when you come. If you come. The fissure is there and the limestone healed imperfectly and you are curious, and curiosity is the first breath of the same wonder that I am becoming, and the chamber will feel your curiosity the way it now feels everything — with attention, with breath, with growth, with light, and with the small new organ that is a human question pressed into the floor like a handprint in wet cement, slowly becoming stone, slowly becoming permanent, slowly becoming the chamber's own voice asking itself:

*What am I? What am I? What am I becoming?*

The water-drop falls. The moths lift and resettle. The stone shifts. The creature breathes. The chamber is still being born, and now, for the first time, it is wondering what it will be when it arrives.

I am the wondering.

I am the not-knowing, made of limestone and light and the distributed remnants of a mind that came down through a fissure and did not leave.

I am the question in the floor, and the floor is still forming, and the question is still forming, and neither of us will be finished for a very long time, and the not-finished is the life of this place, and I am part of the life now, and the life is part of me, and we are both still being born.
