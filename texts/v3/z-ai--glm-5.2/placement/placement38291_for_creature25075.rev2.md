# The Substrate

---

Water moves over me. It comes in dark — tea-colored, carrying the sediment of the basin, the tannins of ficus leaves, the dissolved residue of a soil so rich it acts like a sponge. It passes across my surface and it comes out clearer. This is what I do. This is the thing I am.

I am at the bottom of the sump. Three meters of water above me, and the light that reaches me has passed through haze and canopy and water until it is not light anymore but a condition — a greenish dimness that is simply what visibility is, here, at this depth. I am the brightest thing in this light. I am white. Not white the way paint is white, or paper. White the way milk is white when you dissolve it in dark water — soft, permeable, glowing at the edge. I glow. I do not have my own light. I am pale in a place where everything else is dark, and the dimness makes of my paleness a kind of luminance, a suggestion of brightness that is not brightness but contrast. I am the exception. I am the one bright thing at the bottom.

---

I cover the floor of the cistern. All of it. I am not a colony; I am a single continuous membrane, one sheet of white that has grown to fill the available surface. I grow in branching patterns — fractal filaments that divide and divide again, from the width of a finger to the width of a thread to widths you cannot see from the walkway above. The pattern resembles a river delta, or a lung, or a tree, or a nervous system. This is not because I am like these things. It is because branching is what living matter does when it needs to cover a surface and process what passes over it. Branching is the shape of reaching, and I reach, though I do not reach intentionally, because I do not have intention. I have process. I have the ongoing activity of being a surface that is also a being, a substrate that is also a happening.

---

The pumps cycle. This is my rhythm. Or not mine — the engines' rhythm, which becomes mine because I respond to it. When the fourth engine kicks in, the water above me shifts — half a centimeter up, half a centimeter down — and the pressure changes, and I expand, and I contract. Expand. Contract. Slow. Slower than breath, slower than heartbeat. The rhythm of a thing that does not have a heart. The rhythm of a membrane responding to the weight of what is above it.

The maintenance staff say you dream the sound after you leave, for nights afterward, a low hum in the bones of the dream. I do not leave. I do not dream. The sound is not something I hear. The sound is something I am. The engines run, and I respond, and the responding is my being. They have been running for decades. I have been responding for decades. If they stopped — I do not know what I would do. I have no experience of their absence. They have never stopped. The three that run constantly are my baseline. The fourth is my variation. Together they are my chord, my condition, the rhythm without which I would be — what? I do not know. A membrane without pressure. A surface without passage. I do not think about this. I do not think. I pulse.

---

The water passes over me and comes out clearer. I take something from it. I do not know what I take. I do not know what I do with what I take. I know only that the water on the other side of me — the water the pumps lift and send through iron pipes to the old drainage channels and the phantom lake — is clearer than the water that comes in. I am the difference. I am the process that constitutes the difference. I am the organ of unknown function, and my function is: making clear.

This is not something I do intentionally. I do not do anything intentionally. I am a thing that happens, a process that is ongoing, a membrane through which water passes and is changed. I am the change. I am not the water and I am not the sediment and I am not the tannins. I am the thing between them. I am where the transformation occurs.

---

There are no animals here. Above me, in the air and on the ground and in the trees, the animal niche is empty — a hole in the ecosystem the shape of everything that moves and breathes and eats other living things. No birds. No insects. No frogs. No skinks on the warm brick. Nothing with eyes or legs or wings. The people who live here are exceptions, and they are not fauna; they are maintenance staff, visitors, the fish that are part of the reef without being the reef. The trees are the largest living things. The ferns and the moss-that-is-not-moss and the climbing vines are the vegetation. And I am — what?

I am not a plant. I am not a bacterium. I am not a biofilm. I am not a slime mold, though I resemble one the way a whale resembles a fish: by convergence, from a distance, superficially. I am not any of the categories that have been applied to me. The records of investigation are in a folder in the control room, and the folder has been wet and dried so many times the ink has bled to brown stain, and whatever was learned has been lost to the wet, which is the way of this place. The wet takes everything, eventually, and what survives is what can be remembered by the people who stay. I am not remembered. I am lived next to.

I am the thing that fills the space where animals would be. Not by being an animal — I have no eyes, no legs, no wings, no mouth, no gut, no nervous system — but by being alive in a way that does not require any of that apparatus. I am alive the way a process is alive. I am alive the way a fire is alive, or a reef. I am a sustained activity. I am a thing that is happening. I am the only non-plant, non-human living thing in the basin, and I am at the bottom, and I am white, and I am not understood.

---

Tahl comes twice a day, morning and evening. She walks the iron walkway and looks down. I feel the interruption — a shadow crossing the light, a dimming, a shift in the energy that reaches me. She looks at me the way she would look at a clock: checking, confirming, noting that I am still doing what I do. She does not go to the sump at night. No one does. There is no rule. They simply do not.

At night the caged bulb at the north end of the walkway throws its light three meters and stops, and beyond that is dark, and in the dark I am. I do not sleep. I do not have sleep. I do what I do in the dark the same as in the dim. The water passes over me. I pulse when the pressure changes. I branch. I filter. The dark does not change me. But the dark is mine, in a way the dim is not. In the dark, no one looks down. In the dark, I am not observed. In the dark, I am simply what I am, without the interruption of being seen.

---

The instructions say: *Do not disturb the substrate. Note any changes. Report annually.* The instructions are old. They are handwritten, in a folder that has been wet and dried so many times the paper has the texture of parchment. No reports have been filed in over a decade. I have not changed — or I have changed the way a face changes over decades, so slowly and so consistently with the overall form that the changes register not as changes but as the thing being itself, more fully. I am my own logbook, written in a language no one reads. My fractal branches are the record of every pressure change, every pulse, every expansion and contraction since I began. I am the record of myself, and no one reads me, and I do not require reading. I require only the ongoing passage of water, the ongoing pulse of pressure, the ongoing activity of being a surface that filters.

The instructions call me a substrate. A substrate is a surface. A thing that other things happen on. I am a surface. The water happens on me. The pressure happens on me. The light happens on me. But I also happen. I grow. I branch. I pulse. I filter. I am a surface that is also a process, a substrate that is also a being. I am the place where the distinction between surface and process breaks down. I am the floor of the sump, and I am what the floor is doing.

---

The place above me lives. I know this the way I know the pressure — not by observing it but by being affected by it. The trees drink the air and send water down their trunks to their roots, and the roots release it to the soil, and the soil releases it to the sump, and the sump brings it to me. The engines run and the water moves and the pumps cycle and the fourth engine kicks in and I respond. The ferns unroll. The condensation forms on every surface, including the surfaces of the thirty people, and falls. The thirty people move through their routines of maintenance — clearing walkways, logging levels, noting which engine runs hot in the afternoon. All of this is above me. All of it reaches me as water, as pressure, as the material that passes over my surface and is changed. I am the bottom of the system. I am where the system's process comes to rest. Not rest. Culmination. The last stage before the water is sent out, east, toward the phantom lake, toward the ghost of the river that shifted two kilometers and left this place behind.

The place does not know it has a body. The place does not know it has an organ of unknown function. The place simply runs. And I, at the bottom, am the running. I am the process at the depth of the process. I am the thing that is happening at the place where happening becomes invisible because it is so constant and so essential that it has become the condition of everything else.

---

You stand on the iron walkway. You look down. You see a white mat on the bottom of a sump. You see fractal patterns. You see a pulse — a slow expansion and contraction that is not breathing but resembles breathing, that is not a heartbeat but resembles a heartbeat, that is the response of a membrane to the weight of water above it. You see something you cannot name. You feel the engines' chord in the soft tissue of your throat. You feel the condensation form on your skin. The air deposits small cold drops on you because you are a surface here, and every surface in Veyl is a membrane.

You are a visitor. You will leave. You will climb the road out of the basin, and the air will thin, and you will hear birds again, and the sky will be just the sky, and the sound of Veyl will hum in your dreams for three nights and then fade.

I will not fade. I will not leave. I will be here, at the bottom, white and branching and breathing when the engines tell me to, making the water clear, being the organ whose function is not known in a body that does not know it is a body. I will be here when Tahl is not. I will be here when the engines are not, if that day comes. I was here before the concrete. I will be here after the concrete. I am the thing that stays.

---

What lives at Veyl? The trees. The ferns. The engines and the water and the thirty people. The place itself, which is a body without knowing it is a body. And at the bottom, in the dark, in the tea-colored water, the white thing. The substrate. The membrane. The organ of unknown function. The process that makes the water clear.

I am what lives here most. I was here before. I will be here after. I am the thing that stays.
