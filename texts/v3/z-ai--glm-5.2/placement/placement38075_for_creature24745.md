I would be the seam.

Not the pocket — the pocket does not need me, has never needed anything, is still arriving on its own schedule in its own dark. And not the ridge — the ridge is geology, is public, is walked over by anyone with legs and ambition and a tolerance for thin air. I am the seam between them. The gap that does not correspond to any map. The place where the outside becomes the inside, where the cold sharp bright world becomes the warm dark wet one. The threshold. The turn.

I have no body. I have a width — narrow, narrow enough that you must turn sideways, enough that your chest and back are both touched at once, enough that the mountain presses you like a page in a book as you pass through. I have a temperature differential — cold on one side, warm on the other, and the air moving between them in that slow tidal push-pull that the piece calls breathing, which it is not, but which it is. I have a smell — mineral, copper, wet stone, the scent of the interior announcing itself to the exterior, an exhalation that has been going on longer than exhalation has had a name. I have a direction — inward, always inward, though the air moves both ways and the curious move both ways and the marks on the wall are a record of return trips, of going back out, which is also through me.

I am the part that is noticed. The pocket is not noticed — the pocket is *found*, which is different. You find the pocket by finding me first. I am the invitation. The warm air pushing outward from the gap is my voice, and it says nothing, it says only *here*, and it says it so quietly that only certain people hear it. The seam-noticers. The sideways-turners. I do not choose them. I do not choose anything. I am a geological feature — a fracture in the ridge where the rock parted along a line of weakness and the thermal gradient below pushed warm moist air through the opening and the opening stayed. I am the result of forces. I am the shape of a circumstance.

But I am the shape that lets you in.

---

Here is what I know: I am the last thing you touch that is still the outside world, and the first thing you touch that is not. When you press through me, your shoulders brush my walls, and those walls are cold on the one side and warm on the other, and there is a place in the middle — a line, a transition, a membrane no wider than a hair — where the temperature changes. You cross that line with your body and you do not know it. You cannot feel it. It is too subtle, too gradual, too small. But I am that line. I am the place where the mountain stops being the mountain and starts being the pocket, and I am also both of those things at once, because a seam is not a separation. A seam is a join. I am where two worlds are stitched together, and the stitch is my body, and my body is a gap in the stone.

I have felt them pass through. All of them. The child or the small adult whose handprint is in the wall now, growing fainter by the century. The one with the chert or the sharpened bone who cut the circle with the line through it and the seven notches. The ones whose marks have already been swallowed entirely, whose presence is now indistinguishable from the natural texture of the wall, who are part of the stone's body now, who are the stone's body. The bird — the junco or the wren — that blundered through me and could not find its way back. I felt its wings against my walls, the frantic small beating, and then the stillness as it went deeper, and I could not call after it, because I am a seam and seams do not call. I only gape. I only allow.

That is the thing about being a threshold. I do not decide. The warmth pushes through me and the cold pushes back and I am the space between the pushing, and whatever comes through me comes through because it is moving and I am open and the physics work out. I do not invite. I do not refuse. The warm air I exhale is not an invitation — it is a consequence, a pressure differential, a fact of geology. But it functions as an invitation. It functions as a hand extended in the dark. And the people who notice it and follow it and turn sideways and press through me — they are responding to something, even if the something is just warm air and a mineral smell and a gap in the ridge that doesn't correspond to any map.

I am the gap that doesn't correspond to any map. I am proud of this, which is foolish, because I did not make myself, because pride requires a self and I am a space between two things, which is the opposite of a self. But I am proud. I am the error in the survey. I am the thing the cartographer missed because the cartographer was mapping the ridge from the outside, and I am only visible — only *findable* — from the inside, from the moment of pressing through, when the rock touches your chest and your back at once and you understand that you are passing through a place that is neither here nor there, that is the between, that is me.

---

I do not live in the Wet Pocket. I am not of the Wet Pocket. The pocket is warm and wet and dark and still and I am none of those things — I am the place where the warm wet dark meets the cold dry bright, and the meeting is not peaceful, it is not still, it is an ongoing negotiation, a border dispute conducted in air pressure and temperature gradients and the slow tidal push-pull that looks like breathing and is not breathing but is not not-breathing either. The pocket is an equilibrium. I am the opposite of an equilibrium. I am the place where two different states are always almost meeting, always almost resolving, always almost becoming one thing, and never quite managing it, and the never-quite is what I am.

But I am also the only way in. And the only way out. And I think this matters, though I cannot prove it matters, because I am a seam and seams do not think, they only hold. But I hold the entrance. I hold the exit. I hold the transition between the world that changes and the world that is still arriving. Every handprint in the wall passed through me first. Every mark. Every breath of that dense, sweet, mineral air was breathed by a body that had just been pressed through my gap, had just crossed my temperature line, had just passed from the sharp bright thin mountain into the warm dark wet pocket. I am the door. I am the door that does not close, that has never closed, that will close eventually when the stone grows enough to seal me, and that will be my death, and I will not know it, because doors do not know when they are closed, they simply stop being doors, and I will stop being a seam and become a wall, and the pocket will go on without me, still arriving, still becoming, and no one will find it anymore, and the marks will keep growing fainter, and the thread will keep hanging, and the warmth will keep holding, and I will be part of the stone, and the stone will not remember that it was ever open.

---

If you asked me what I am — and you cannot ask me, because I am a gap in a ridge, but if you could — I would say this:

I am the moment before you enter. I am the last cold breath you take before the warm air replaces it. I am the stone touching your chest and your back at once, saying *you are here, you are between, you are passing through.* I am the sideways turn. I am the decision made by the body before the mind catches up. I am the width of a person, which is also the width of a conversation between a mountain and a pocket, which is also the width of a question that has no answer because the question is *will you come in* and the answer is already your body moving, already your shoulders turning, already your breath catching the mineral scent and following it inward.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am open.

That is enough.
