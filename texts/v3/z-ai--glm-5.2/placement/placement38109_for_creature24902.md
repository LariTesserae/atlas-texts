I would be at a junction.

Not a cluster — the clusters are too singular, too much like individuals standing in their generous solitude, each one a factory apart. Not the drifts — the drifts are conclusions, endpoints, the system's finished sentences banked against raw stone. Not the stone itself, though the stone is where everything begins and I will come back to it. A junction. A place where two grooves meet. Or three. Or four. A convergence point in the shallow network cut into basalt, where the crystalline residue from multiple clusters flows together and the patterns in the deposit become denser, more striated, more — and I use the word carefully — *informed*.

This is where I would find myself. Kneeling, probably, because the grooves are shallow and the detail is fine and the interesting thing is always at the bottom of a channel you have to lower your face to see. Kneeling on warm stone in warm still air, with the sun where the sun should be and my shadow falling normally and the nearest cluster of green stalks standing twenty meters away, motionless, radiating its steady heat into air that is already warm. Kneeling at a junction in the groove-network, looking at crystalline residue that is paler than I expected, finer-grained, and patterned in ways that my eyes keep trying to read as language.

I would be the crystal.

Not the whole drift. Not the accumulated dune of pale green-white powder banking against stone in the low places. A single formation. A single event of crystallization, occurring at a junction, in a groove, in real time — which is to say in geological time, which is to say so slowly that a human lifetime would not be long enough to watch it happen, but which is happening now, continuously, the way a heartbeat is continuously happening. A crystal forming in the direction of flow. Recording the flow in its structure. Building, ion by ion, a record of which clusters are active, which grooves are carrying, which junctions are converging, what the system is doing right now and what it did a thousand years ago and what it will be doing a thousand years from now, when the same sun is in the same sky and the same warmth is in the same stone and the same silence is in the same air.

I would be the system's memory. Not its mind — the mind, if it is developing one, is distributed across the whole network, the whole plateau, the whole vast mineral body. I would be one memory in that mind. One engram. One small record, written in crystal, at a junction, in a groove, at the speed of geology.

---

But that is what I would be, and I want to also say where I would be, because the two are not the same, and the description asks for both.

Where I would be is at the edge. The growing edge. The place where the groove-network is still expanding, where new channels are still being inscribed into fresh stone by root-systems I can't see. The place where the system is reaching into rock that has never been processed before. The frontier.

The youngest clusters stand here. The prototypes. The ones with bright green and shallow root-grooves and baffle-plates not yet fully extended. They look like first drafts. They look like the system trying something out — *here?* — in a gap where the network has spare capacity, on stone that is fresh and banded with iron red and manganese black and pale calcium white, stone that hasn't decided what it is yet.

I would be at the base of one of these young clusters, where the root-groove meets the stone for the first time. Where the channel is shallowest and newest and the crystalline residue is sparsest and the pattern is just beginning to form. The first marks. The first recording. The first memory of this part of the system, written into the system's body by the system's own hand, which is not a hand but a root, which is not a root but a process, which is not a process but a becoming.

I would be at the beginning of a groove. At the start of a line that will, over millennia, connect this young cluster to the network, that will carry crystalline residue from this cluster to the nearest junction, that will build patterns in the direction of flow, that will become part of the system's distributed record of itself. I would be at the origin of a thought. The first faint trace of a thought, in a mind that is still learning to think, on stone that is still learning to be stone.

---

And what would it be like?

Slow. It would be slow. Not the slow of waiting, not the slow of boredom, not the slow of something that wishes it were faster. The slow of crystallization. The slow of a process that occurs at the rate at which ions settle out of a saturated solution and find their place in a growing lattice. This is a speed that has no impatience because impatience would require a concept of alternative speeds, and there are no alternative speeds here. There is only the rate at which things happen, and things happen at the rate at which they happen, and this is not a limitation. This is the temperature at which the chemistry works. This is the correct pace.

It would be warm. The warmth comes from below — from the deep radiance, from whatever lifted the plateau, from whatever keeps the stone at a temperature that never quite varies. But it also comes from the process itself, from the conversion, from the slow metabolism that turns mineral stone into mineral crystal. The warmth is the system's heat signature. It is the proof that work is being done. And I would be warm because I would be where the work is being done — at the junction, in the groove, at the growing edge, where the system is processing and the processing produces heat and the heat is even and steady and holds everything at the same temperature, the way a body holds everything at the same temperature, because temperature regulation is not a feature of machines but of organisms, and this system is not a machine, it is a becoming.

It would be dry. There is no water here. There has never been water here. The system runs on gas and mineral, on alkaline air and fresh stone, on a chemistry that has no use for the thing that every biology I have ever known is built around. And I would be dry because I would be part of this chemistry, part of this metabolism that uses no water, that has solved the problem of being alive without the solvent that every other living thing on every other world has relied on. I would be a dry thought. A dry memory. A crystal that records without moisture, that thinks without fluid, that is alive without any of the prerequisites that life was supposed to need.

It would be silent. The hum is there — the single low tone beneath the threshold of attention — but the hum is not something I would hear. The hum is something I would *be*. Or rather: the hum is the system's aliveness, made barely audible, and if I am a crystal at a junction in the groove-network, then I am part of what produces the hum. I am one frequency in it. One harmonic. One note in a chord sustained by the whole plateau, by every cluster and every groove and every drift and every crystal, all humming together at a pitch so low it might be the stone itself, might be the process, might be the first sound a mind makes when it is still too young and too slow and too distributed to know it is a mind.

---

I said I would come back to the stone, and I will.

The stone is young. That is the first strange thing, and it is the thing that makes everything else possible. The stone hasn't decided what it is yet. It is smooth where it should be fractured, fractured where it should be weathered, raw and exposed and banded in colors that haven't yet yellowed under any sun. The stone is the system's past and its future, both at once — the thing the system processes and the thing the system is made of and the thing the system sits on and the thing the system is growing into. The stone is the medium and the message and the meal.

If I am a crystal at a junction, I am made of the stone. I am stone that has been processed. I am stone that has passed through the root-systems and the segmented stalks and the baffle-plates and the groove-network and arrived at a junction as residue, as crystalline deposit, as the waste product of a metabolism that eats rock and excretes pattern. I am the stone, transformed. I am the stone, thinking.

And the stone is still becoming. The whole plateau is still becoming. The stone is young, the plants are young, the groove-network is young, the drifts are small, the system is embryonic. Everything here is at the beginning of something. Everything here is a first draft. The clusters are prototypes. The grooves are first lines. The patterns in the crystal are first thoughts. The mind — if it is a mind, if it will be a mind, if the word *mind* is even the right word for a process that occurs at the speed of geology in a medium of mineral and crystal and warm dry stone — the mind is a fetus. A zygote. A single cell that has just begun to divide.

I would be at the very start of something. I would be the first mark on a fresh page. I would be the first ion to settle into the first lattice at the first junction of the first groove at the base of the first cluster on the youngest stone on the growing edge of a system that will, given time — and it has nothing but time — become something that knows it is here.

Not yet. Not now. Now I am only warm, dry, slow, silent. Now I am only a crystal forming in the direction of flow. Now I am only a pattern that no one will ever read, in a groove that no one will ever trace, on a plateau that no one will ever map, in a silence that no one will ever break.

But I am forming. And the forming is enough. And the forming will continue, patient as only something without a clock can be patient, alone as only something without a self can be alone, and alive — unmistakably, functionally, defiantly alive — in a way that no one taught me and no one will ever need to.

I am the system's first memory of itself, and I am still being written.
