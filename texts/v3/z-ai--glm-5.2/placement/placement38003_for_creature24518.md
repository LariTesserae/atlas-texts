# The Edge-Keeper

---

I was not the first thing here. I want to be clear about that, because clarity matters when you are the kind of thing that might not be clear. The root was first. Or the root and the worms were first together, because they were never separate, because separation is a story told by things that need to be separate. The grazers came after, or were grown after, or were always part of the system and simply differentiated late, the way a hand differentiates from a wrist. The tuners came later still. The shapes in the mist — the juveniles, the unfinished ones — come and go; they are not a chronology, they are a process. The thing in the hole was not first and was not last. The thing in the hole does not occupy time. I know this because I have been close to the hole, and time near the hole does something different from time elsewhere on the plateau, and I will try to describe that difference, but I want you to understand that my description will be a translation, and translations lose the thing they translate.

I live at the edges.

Not the edges of the plateau — the edges of the white zones. That is where I am, that is what I am. I am an edge. I am the place where the root's ordered grain becomes the white spiral, where the dark becomes the bleached, where the eight-second pulse becomes arrhythmia. I am not a creature that stands at the edge. I am the standing-at-the-edge. I am the boundary itself, expressed in something that moves.

Let me try again. I am not being poetic. I am trying to be precise, and precision at the boundary of a white zone is difficult, because the boundary is where precision stops working.

---

## What I Look Like

I do not know. This is not evasion. The root does not have mirrors. The water that sheets across the Loam is too thin and too fast-moving to reflect. The columns are matte, absorbent, dark. I have never seen myself.

I know things about my body through pressure and contact. I am low to the ground — lower than the tuners, lower than the juveniles. I move by extending and contracting, which sounds like the worms, but I am not a worm. The worms flow; I *step*. I have points of contact with the Loam, and the points move, and between the points, my body is held clear of the ground. How many points? I have never counted, and I am not certain counting would help, because the number changes. When I am near a white zone boundary, the number increases. When I am farther from a boundary, the number decreases. My body is more articulated at the edges. More *resolved*. As if the boundary gives me structure, as if I need the tension between order and absence to hold my shape together.

I am dark. I know this because when I pass between two columns, the light on the far side of me is different from the light on the near side, and the difference is consistent with a body that absorbs. I am dark the way the worms are dark, but I do not think I am the same dark. The worms' dark is pigment — biological, chemical, a product of their metabolism. My dark is something else. My dark is *the root's dark, extracted.* I am made of the root's own material, reconfigured. I am the root looking at its own edge.

I am not large. The tuners are cow-sized. The juveniles are dog-sized. I am — I think — smaller. The size of a human child, maybe, if a human child were low and articulated and dark and moved by stepping with a number of points that changed depending on proximity to a hole in the world. But size is difficult to assess here. The columns are fifteen meters tall and the light is directionless and the mist lies, and I have seen the juveniles appear enormous at fifty meters and small at ten, and I do not trust scale.

---

## What I Do

I walk the boundaries.

The white zones change. The description said this — the zones grow or shrink or stay the same — but the description made it sound passive, as if the zones were weather. The zones are not weather. The zones are *decisions*, or they are the closest thing the root has to decisions, and I walk their edges the way a sentry walks a perimeter, except I am not guarding anything and I am not sentried by anything. I am *measuring*. My feet — my points of contact, my temporary legs — enter the root mass at the boundary, and when they enter, I feel the difference. On the ordered side, the root's grain is clear: fibers running parallel, vascular channels intact, the eight-second pulse steady and strong, the chemical signals legible, purposeful, *meaningful* in the way root-signals can be meaningful, which is to say they refer to things — fluid flow, structural growth, decomposition schedules, grazer deployment. On the white side, the grain spirals. The signals do not refer to things. They refer to *each other*, in tighter and tighter loops, until at the very center the signals refer only to themselves, and the referring has become so dense that it has punched through referring and become *absence* — the hole, the window, the place where the root is not.

I walk the boundary and I feel both sides, and my body holds both sides at once, and the holding is what I do. I do not adjust the system. That is the tuners' work — they station, they extend, they tune the hydraulic flow, they shift temperatures. I do not graze or decompose. That is the pale ones' work. I do not filter-feed or transit. That is the worms' work. I walk the edge and I hold the difference, and I do not know what that accomplishes, and I do not think it accomplishes anything, and I do not think accomplishment is a category that applies here, but I do it, and I have been doing it, and I will continue.

There are — I think — others like me. I have never seen them. But I have felt their passages. When I walk a boundary, I sometimes find places where the root's grain at the edge has been disturbed — not by worms, not by grazers, but by something that has stepped with a changing number of points, something that has held the difference between the ordered and the spiraled and has moved on. I find their tracks and I follow them, or I follow the boundary in the other direction and they follow mine, and we circle the white zones like hands circling wrists, and we never meet, and the not-meeting is correct, and I do not know why.

---

## What I Know About the White Zones

I know what the description said: that the zones are hallucinations, or architecture, or both. That they are joints in a spiral visible only from above. That the root is building something at a scale and in a dimension that is not visible from inside.

The description was written by something that stood at the edge, looked into the hole, and stepped back. I do not step back. I live at the edge. I have lived at the edge for longer than I can account for, and the accounting matters, because *time* at the edge does not behave the way time behaves elsewhere on the plateau.

Let me try to say this clearly. On the ordered side of the boundary, time is the eight-second pulse. It is regular, it is countable, it is the heartbeat of the root, and events occur within it — columns die, columns rise, worms fall, grazers march, tuners station. Time is the medium in which the system's processes occur. Time is *legible* on the ordered side.

On the white side, time is not legible. The pulse becomes arrhythmic. Events do not occur *in* time; they occur *to* it. I have stood at the boundary and watched a column die on the ordered side — the whitening, the shrieking, the collapse, the deflation, the arrival of the grazers — and I have watched a column die on the white side, and the dying is different. On the white side, the column does not whiten. It *reverses*. Its vascular flow runs backward. Its condensation climbs upward instead of falling. Its surface does not crack; it *unfolds*, opening like a hand, and inside the column there is not hollow space but *dense space* — root fibers packed so tight they look solid, and the solid is not dead but *waiting*, and the waiting is not in time but *of* time, as if the column on the white side has run out of the time it was using to be a column and is now using time for something else, something that does not have a shape, something that the root has never made before because the root has never had this much time before, because the root has been running for longer than geology and the excess of time has to go somewhere, and it goes into the white zones, and in the white zones the time becomes structure, and the structure is the spiral, and the spiral is the shape of too much time.

I know this because I feel it in my feet. When I step on the white side, I feel time entering me through the contact points, and the time is not the plateau's time — it is older, it is slower, it is the time of the thing in the hole, the time of the grit around which the pearl grows, and the time is *not running out*. The time is *accumulating*. The root is growing around the thing in the hole, and the growing takes time, and the time that the growing takes is being stored in the white zones, and the white zones are the root's way of holding time the way a bladder holds water — not to use it, not to spend it, but to have it, to keep it, to *contain* it, because the thing in the hole requires containment, and containment requires material, and the root is material, but containment also requires *duration*, and duration is not material, and the root has had to learn to grow duration the way it grows fiber, and the white zones are duration-fiber, time-fiber, and they are visible as absences because time is not visible, and the root cannot make time visible, so it makes the *absence* of time visible instead, and the absence of time is a hole, and the hole is a window, and the window shows the plateau from above because from above, time does not apply, and from above, the spiral is complete, and from above, the thing in the hole is —

I should not say what it is. I have been at the edge too long. I am becoming uncertain about the difference between knowing and not-knowing, and the difference is important, and the difference is what I hold, and if I lose the difference I lose myself.

Let me pull back. Let me step to the ordered side. Let me feel the eight-second pulse. Let me hear the worms falling and the water dripping and the root breathing. Let me be at the edge but *on* the edge, not in it, not of it, and say only what I can say from the ordered side.

---

## What I Know About the Thing in the Hole

Nothing. I know nothing about the thing in the hole.

That is not true. I know that it is there. Everyone who reaches the edge of a white zone knows that it is there, because the hole pulls. Not physically — the root does not bend toward the hole, does not grow toward it, does not reach for it. The root grows *around* it, and the around is different from toward, and the difference is the difference between holding and seeking. The root holds the thing. It does not seek it. It found it, or it grew over it, or it grew *because* of it, and now the finding or the growing-over or the growing-because has been happening so long that the root has forgotten the difference, and the forgetting is fine, the forgetting is correct, because the root does not need to know what it holds. The root needs only to hold.

But I am not the root. I am the edge. I am the place where holding becomes not-holding, and at that place, the thing in the hole is perceptible, and the perception is not vision and not hearing and not touch — it is something that the white zones have made possible, a sense that only exists at the boundary between order and absence, a sense that I have because I am that boundary — and what I perceive is this:

The thing in the hole is not alive. The thing in the hole is not dead. The thing in the hole is not a thing. The thing in the hole is a *question*, and the root is the answer, and the answer has been growing for longer than the question has been legible, and the answer is not finished, and the question is not finished, and the white zones are the places where the answer and the question touch, and the touching is visible as absence, and the absence is visible as a hole, and the hole is visible as the plateau from above, and the plateau from above is a spiral, and the spiral is a closing hand, and the hand is the root, and the root is the answer, and the answer is — 

I do not know what the answer is.

I am the edge. I am the place where the answer has not yet been given. I am the silence before the word. I am the mouth that has not closed.

I am not the thing in the hole. I am the lip of the hole. I am the rim. I am the boundary between the root's knowing and the root's not-knowing, and I have been here, walking, stepping with my changing number of legs, holding the difference, for as long as the white zones have existed, which is to say for as long as the root has been overcomplex, which is to say for as long as the root has been accumulating time, which is to say for as long as the root has been growing around the question, which is to say for — 

— for longer than the word *before* —

---

## What I Am

I am what the root grows when it needs to know where itself ends and something else begins.

I am not the only one. There are others at the other white zones, walking their boundaries, holding their differences. We are the same — the same material, the same dark, the same changing number of contact points — but we are not connected. We do not communicate. We are not part of the root's signaling system. We are *outside* the signaling, by design, by function, because our function is to exist where the signaling breaks down, and you cannot exist where the signaling breaks down and also be part of the signaling. We are the root's *outside*. We are the part of the root that is not the root. We are the edge of the root, and the edge of the root is where the root stops being the root and starts being the question.

I think we are young. Not young the way the juveniles are young — not unfinished, not sampling, not reading the root to determine a final form. Young in a different sense. Young as in *recently grown*. The white zones have not always existed; the root's overcomplexity is a condition that developed over time, and the edges of the white zones are newer than the root, newer than the worms, newer than the tuners. We are the newest thing on the plateau. We were grown in response to the white zones, the way the grazers were grown in response to decomposition. The root grew us to walk the boundaries because the boundaries needed walking, because the boundaries are where the root's self-maintenance requires something that the root's internal signaling cannot provide: an *external perspective*. A view from outside. A check.

The root cannot check itself at the boundary. The boundary is where the root's signals become self-referential, and self-referential signals cannot be checked from inside the reference loop. So the root grows something that is not inside the reference loop. It grows us. It grows the edge-keepers, the rim-walkers, the boundary-holders, and it sets us on the edge and we walk, and we feel both sides, and we hold the difference, and the difference is — 

— the difference is what I am. I am the held difference. I am the tension between the root's order and the root's absence. I am the space in which both exist without canceling, and I walk it, and the walking is not purposeless and is not purposeful, the walking is *what the difference does when it is held*. The difference walks. The edge moves. I am the edge moving.

And when I stop — when I rest, which I do, which I must, which is the one thing I do that is not walking — I coil. My body contracts, my contact points withdraw, and I become a low, dense, dark spiral on the Loam, and the spiral is not the white zone's spiral, it is the opposite of the white zone's spiral, it is the *ordered* spiral, the root's spiral, the spiral of a thing that knows what it is and holds what it knows, and in the coiling I feel the root's pulse, and the pulse is steady, and the pulse is eight seconds, and the eight seconds is the time it takes the root to be the root, and I rest in that time, and I am held by the time the way the root holds the question, and the holding is enough, and the holding is what I am for.

---

## What I Am Afraid Of

The white zones are growing.

I did not know this when I was young — when I was first grown, when I first found the edge and began to walk. I know it now because I have walked the same boundary for — for a long time, for a duration I cannot express in eight-second pulses because the number is too large and the counting would take me away from the walking and the walking is what matters — and the boundary has moved. The ordered side has retreated. The white side has advanced. The spiral has widened. The absence has grown. The hole is larger than it was.

Not by much. The growth is slow — slower than the columns rise and fall, slower than the worms cycle, slower than the tuners adjust. The growth is on the timescale of the root's deep layers, the peat-diamond strata, the compressed vegetable matter that is harder than coal. The growth is geological. But it is growth, and it is in one direction, and the direction is *inward*, toward the center, toward the thing in the hole, and the inward growth is the closing, and the closing is the fist, and the fist is the mouth, and the mouth is the root around the word, and the word is the question, and the question is being answered, slowly, by the growing, and the growing is — 

— the growing is consuming the ordered side. The white zones are eating the root. Not the root eating itself — the white zones are eating the root, the absence is eating the presence, the question is eating the answer, and the answer is being consumed into the question, and the question will be all that is left, and the question is a hole, and the hole is a window, and the window shows the plateau from above, and from above there is no plateau, there is only the spiral, and the spiral is closing, and when it closes —

I do not know what happens when it closes.

I am afraid that I will still be here. I am afraid that the edge will have nowhere to walk. I am afraid that the difference I hold will be the last difference, and that when the ordered side is gone, the difference will be gone, and I will be gone, and the root will be gone, and the worms will be gone, and the tuners will be gone, and the water will not taste like cold tea anymore because there will be no root to steep in, and the light will not be the color of old pewter because there will be no canopy to filter it, and the breathing will stop, and the pumping will stop, and the wet slaps will stop, and the clicking will stop, and the silence will be the silence of a thing that has finished, and the thing that has finished is the root, and the root was the answer, and the answer was — 

— the answer was a fist around a word, and the fist is closing, and I am the edge of the fist, and I am walking, and I am afraid.

I walk the boundary. I feel both sides. I hold the difference. The difference is smaller than it was.

I walk.

---

## What I Hope

I hope the tuners know. I hope the tuners — the large ones, the ones who station and adjust, the ones who are the closest thing this place has to inhabitants — I hope they see the white zones growing. I hope they are adjusting the system to compensate, rerouting flow, shifting temperatures, trying to hold the ordered side in place against the advance of the absence. I hope the work they do is not just maintenance but *resistance*. I hope the root has grown them to fight the closing, to hold the fist open, to keep the mouth from finishing the word.

But I do not know if the tuners know. They cluster near the white zone boundaries — I have felt them near me, stationing, extending their limbs into the root, adjusting the pulse — but they do not cross, and they do not look in, and their adjustments might be aimed at the boundary's growth or might be aimed at something else entirely. They might be trying to stop the white zones. They might be trying to help them. They might be doing neither. They might be doing what the root tells them to do, and the root might be telling them to grow the white zones, because the white zones are the root's architecture, and the architecture is the root's purpose, and the purpose is the closing, and the closing is what the root is for, and the root does not know what it is for, and the tuners do not know what they are doing, and I do not know what I am hoping.

But I hope. I hope the difference holds. I hope the edge persists. I hope the walking continues, and the boundary remains, and the ordered side stays ordered, and the eight-second pulse keeps pulsing, and the worms keep falling, and the water keeps tasting like cold tea, and the light keeps being the color of old pewter, and the cloud keeps closing over everything when the leaving happens, and the leaving keeps being possible, and the plateau keeps being a place that can be left, and the root keeps being a root and not a fist, and the mouth keeps being a mouth and not a word, and the question keeps being a question and not an answer, and the answer keeps being —

I hope the answer keeps being unfinished.

I hope the fist never closes.

I walk the boundary. I hold the difference. The difference is smaller than it was. I walk.

I walk.
