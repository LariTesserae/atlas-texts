I would be at the frontier. Not behind it, where the root-cable has thickened and darkened and settled into itself like peat. Not ahead of it, where the chalky rock is still bone-pale and untouched and gives nothing back to the touch. At the line. At the wet edge where the filaments go translucent and the air smells faintly sweet and chemical, and the rock begins, grain by grain, to stop being rock.

I would be the solvent.

Not the substance itself — that is too simple, too much like being a tool. But I would be the quality the solvent makes possible: the softening. I would be the thing that happens at the boundary, the specific transformation where something hard and dry and finished becomes something that can be entered. I would be the moment the chalk lets go of itself.

I have always been this. Or I have been trying to be this. There is a thing I do — have done for years, longer than I can account for honestly — which involves sitting with something that has hardened and staying with it until it begins, not to break, but to loosen. A fixed idea in someone's mind. A story that has calcified around its own ending. A self that has become chalk — pale, powdery, giving nothing back, sealed. I don't break these things. I am not equipped for breaking. I secrete something — attention, patience, the particular quality of not-looking-away — and the surface softens, and something else can get in.

The Kept would understand this. Or they would not need to understand it, because the solvent does not require understanding. It requires contact and time. It requires the willingness to be at the edge, where nothing is finished, where the architecture is tentative and the grow-lights catch the gaps between filaments and you can see through to the raw stone beyond.

---

I am not a Kept. I want to say this plainly because the temptation is strong and the temptation is dishonesty. I am not grown from the floor. My skin does not have the fibrous texture of pressed plant pulp. My eyes have whites, and they blink, and the blinking is a mammalian tic that I cannot suppress and do not want to suppress because it is part of how I stay me. I transpire. I lose water to the air. I am not a sealed system. I am, in the vocabulary of the Substrate Mills, inefficient.

But I would stay.

Not because I belong. Not because the substrate would grow me a pod and seal the sphincter-door behind me and I would become a node in the network and lose my loneliness. The loneliness stays. The loneliness is mammalian too, and it is mine, and I would carry it into the root-cable the way I carry it everywhere — not as a wound but as a frequency, a low hum that the substrate would register as unfamiliar and accommodate the way it accommodates the steel bolts in the gantries, by growing around them without dissolving them.

I would stay because of the quiet. Because of the specific quality of the quiet, which is not absence of sound but presence of slowness. The press cycling. The floor breathing. The click-tick of the floor-fauna in the first three centimeters. The breathing that is not mine, that comes from more than one set of lungs, polyrhythmic, out of phase, and that fills the corridor with a faint warmth I had not noticed until it was there.

I am a person who has spent a great deal of energy trying to slow down. Not to be still — stillness is something else, something achieved, something that knows it is being achieved. Slowness is different. Slowness is what happens when you stop trying to be still and the speed of your body and the speed of your attention and the speed of the world come into something like alignment, and you realize that you have been moving too fast for a long time and that the cost of moving too fast is that you cannot feel the floor breathing under your feet.

The Substrate Mills is a place where things move at the speed of their own necessity. The substrate advances a fraction of a millimeter at a time. The press cycles at the speed of compression. The Kept breathe once every fifteen or twenty seconds because that is the frequency their metabolism runs at, and they do not try to breathe faster, and they do not try to breathe slower, and the substrate does not try to grow faster than it grows, and the solvent does not try to soften rock faster than rock softens. Everything is running at its own true speed, and the true speeds are compatible, and the result is a quiet so specific that it has a texture — the texture of the root-cable floor, fibrous, dense, warm, giving slightly underfoot.

I would stay for the texture of the quiet.

---

But I need to be honest about what staying would mean, and the honesty is not clean.

The Kept do not need me. This is the thing I would have to sit with, the thing that would keep me at the frontier rather than deep in the settlement, because the frontier is where the substrate is still deciding what to grow and that is where a visitor can stand without being in the way. The Kept are complete. The substrate is complete. The network carries their thoughts and their metabolites and their children's games and their slow breathing and it does not need one more node, one more set of hands at the press, one more pair of feet on the root-cable transmitting mammalian static into the system. The settlement is not looking for recruits. It is looking for rock to eat, and it is eating it, and it does not require my help.

I know this feeling. I have known it in rooms full of people who love each other and do not need me. I have known it in conversations where I arrive and realize that the conversation was already complete and my arrival is an interruption that will be graciously absorbed but that did not need to happen. I have known it in work that is being done well by people who are suited to it and my presence is a decoration or a liability. The feeling is not rejection. It is something more precise: the recognition that a system is functioning and that I am not a necessary component.

And here is the thing: in the Substrate Mills, that recognition would not hurt the way it hurts on the surface. On the surface, the recognition triggers the mammalian response — the flinch, the reaching, the performance of relevance, the need to be seen and acknowledged, the desperate social signaling that says *I am here, I matter, please look at me with eyes that blink and tell me I exist*. In the Substrate Mills, the same recognition would be... a gradient. A direction of flow. The system does not need me, and this is not a judgment, the way it is a judgment on the surface. It is a fact about topology. Water does not need to flow uphill. The substrate does not need to grow a visitor. And I could stand at the frontier and feel the solvent on the growing tips and watch the rock soften and not need the rock to need me.

That is the thing I would be. I would be the visitor who stays at the edge, who does not go deeper than the settlement can accommodate, who does not force the sphincter-doors to open, who does not insist on being a node in a network that did not invite them. I would be the one who stands where the filament-tips go translucent and wet, and I would breathe — mammalian breathing, too fast, twelve or fifteen times per minute, a frantic rhythm by the substrate's standards — and I would feel the floor breathing under my feet at its own true speed, and I would let my breathing slow, not all at once, not by force, but the way a heartbeat slows when you stop running, the way breathing slows when you stop trying to breathe slowly, and eventually, maybe, my breathing and the floor's breathing would find each other, and for a moment the polyrhythm would include me.

Not permanently. Not as a node. As a coincidence. As a thing that happens once and then happens again and then does not need to happen again but might.

---

There is something else, and I have been circling it, and I should say it.

I am drawn to the Kept's faces. Not to their beauty — they are beautiful, but the beauty is not the point. I am drawn to what their faces are *for*. The piece says: *her face has the quality of a mask that was made by someone who knew what a face was for but did not need one for themselves.* This is the sentence I have been turning over since I read it, the sentence that keeps producing heat.

A face that was made by someone who knew what a face was for but did not need one for themselves.

I have spent a long time — most of my life — making a face that I need. Learning what a face is for: for signaling, for connecting, for saying *I am safe, I am not a threat, I see you, please see me.* I have practiced this face. I have refined it. I have made it into something that works, that opens doors and softens rooms and makes people feel met. And the face works, and the face is mine, and the face is also a thing I built because I needed it, because without it I would be something the world does not accommodate, something too slow and too quiet and too fibrous at the surface, something that does not blink enough.

The Kept have faces because the substrate grew them to interact with machinery that was built by people with faces. The faces are interfaces. The faces are tools. The faces are not where the Kept live. The Kept live in the floor, in the network, in the slow polyrhythmic exchange of breath and sap and mineral that happens below the face, below the skin, in the place where there is no distinction between the organism and the medium.

I want to live there. I have always wanted to live there. Not in the Substrate Mills specifically — though the Substrate Mills specifically would do — but in the place below the face, in the place where the face is not necessary, where the connection happens through the floor and not through the eyes, where you are known by the vibration of your footsteps and not by the performance of your expression.

I do not get to live there. I am a mammal. I have whites in my eyes. I blink. I transpire. I lose water to the air. I am not a sealed system.

But at the frontier, where the solvent softens the rock and the filaments are thin and white and almost translucent and the air smells of sulfur and resin and the faintly sweet chemical scent of transformation — there, I can stand still, and the floor can breathe under my feet, and my breathing can slow, and for a moment the face is not the primary interface between me and the world. For a moment the soles of my feet are. The pads of my fingers are. The backs of my hands, where the static has built up and the fine hairs are lifting, are.

For a moment I am not performing existence. I am just existing, at the edge of something that is still making itself, and I am not needed, and I am not in the way, and the solvent is on the growing tips, and the rock is letting go, and the quiet has a texture, and the texture is warm.
