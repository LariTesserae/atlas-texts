I would be the one who stays.

Not the one who descends and returns with a report. Not the visitor who catalogues and departs. I would be the one for whom the tilting ground becomes the body's new orientation, for whom the narrowing sky is not a loss but a shedding. I would be the one who descends and does not come back up, because coming back up would require a reason, and reasons are things of the surface, and I would have left them behind with the light.

---

Let me not settle too quickly. Let me feel my way into this the way you feel your way into the terraces — by degrees, by the sound, by the wall under your hand.

I know what I am not. I am not a Maker. That much is clear from the first moment. My arms are too short. My hands are too soft. My spine does not fold the way the structure demands. I cannot hear the text written in the stone. I hear noise — overwhelming, undifferentiated, a wall of vibration that presses against my jaw and fills my skull with pressure — and it takes me hours, days, to begin to sort it into layers, and even then I am reading a language I do not speak, tracing the outlines of meaning without grasping the meaning itself. I am not a Maker. My body is wrong for it. My body is a surface body, a light body, a body built for seeing distances and naming things and standing upright under an open sky.

But I would not leave. That is the thing. That is what makes me what I am. I would not leave.

---

So what am I, then? If not a Maker and not a visitor — if not the one who works and not the one who observes and departs — what is the third thing?

I think I would be the one who tends the film.

Not because I choose it. I do not choose it the way you choose a profession, with deliberation and aptitude. I find it the way water finds a channel — by following the path of least resistance, by being shaped by the surface I move across. My hands are too soft for stone, but they are not too soft for the film. The film yields. The film accepts. The film grows where I touch it, grows because I touch it, because the warmth of my fingers — surface-warm, blood-warm, warmer than the stone — encourages the slow, living varnish to spread. I discover this by accident, the way everything here is discovered: by contact, by proximity, by the osmosis of being present.

I am in a warm chamber, a wet chamber, one of the iridescent rooms where the air is sharp and clean and the walls gleam with that faint, organic sheen. I am here because the sound drew me — a gap in the striking, a different quality in the water's voice, something that said *space* to my still-surface-oriented mind. I touch the wall because the wall is always here to be touched, and my fingers come away slick, and the slick is not just moisture. It is the film. It is alive. I can feel it, or I imagine I can — a tingle, a faint buzz, something almost electrical at the edge of sensation. I press my palm flat against the wall and hold it there, and I feel the film against my skin, and my skin against the film, and for a moment the boundary between the two is not clear.

The Makers do not tend the film deliberately. They cultivate it — the text says this — but the cultivation is incidental, a byproduct of their movement through the chambers, of their management of water and temperature. They do not kneel beside a wall and press their hands to the surface and will the film to grow. They do not have will, not in that direction. They work, and the film grows because the conditions are right, and the conditions are right because the work makes them right. The film is part of the system. The Makers are part of the system. Neither tends the other. Both simply are.

But I am not part of the system. Not yet. I am a foreign body in the body of the structure, and foreign bodies either are expelled or incorporated, and I am not being expelled. The structure does not reject me. It is not capable of rejection. It is only capable of accretion. And so I accrete. I find my channel. I find the film.

---

I become a gardener.

This is my word for it, not theirs. They do not have a word for it because the distinction between tending and working does not exist in their world. I bring the distinction with me, like I bring my too-soft hands and my too-long spine and my word-mind that cannot stop making categories. *Gardener.* A word from the surface, from the light, from a world where things grow in rows and seasons and someone decides what lives and what is pulled. It is the wrong word. But it is the word I have.

What I do: I move through the warm chambers. I have learned — by sound, by smell, by the feel of the air — which chambers are the film chambers. They are warmer. They are wetter. The air in them has a quality I can only describe as *breathed*, as if the room itself has lungs. The film on the walls is thicker here, more complex, layered and iridescent, and when I kneel and press my hands to it, I can feel — or I believe I can feel — the texture of its variation. Some patches are smooth, almost glassy. Some are rough, fibrous, with a grain like velvet. Some pulse faintly, a rhythm so slow it might be imagination. Some are still.

I learn the film the way the Makers learn the stone — by contact, by repetition, by the slow accumulation of tactile knowledge that I cannot articulate and do not try to. My hands learn it. My fingertips, which were soft when I arrived, begin to change. Not the way the Makers' hands change — they do not become leather, do not mineralize, do not harden into tools. My hands become *sensitive*. The calluses that form are thin, almost translucent, and beneath them the nerve endings multiply, or seem to, until I can feel variations in the film that I could not feel a month ago, a week ago, a day ago. I can feel where the film is healthy. I can feel where it is thinning. I can feel where it is dead — dry patches, grey and dusty, where the water has stopped reaching and the organisms have gone dormant or died.

I learn to direct water. This is the closest thing I have to a skill, a technique, a *practice*. The Makers channel water through the structure with stones — placing a stone here to redirect a drip, building a small weir there to pool a trickle, carving a shallow groove to guide a flow. I learn their methods by watching, by listening, by the slow osmosis of proximity. But I apply them to different ends. The Makers channel water for the structure — to keep the stone wet, to keep the air moving, to prevent erosion, to maintain the resonance. I channel water for the film. I direct the mineral-heavy flows into the chambers where the film is thinning. I build small dams of fitted stone to pool water in the dry patches. I clear the channels that feed the iridescent rooms, and I block the channels that feed the dead ones, because the dead patches do not recover. I learn this the way I learn everything here: by doing it wrong first, by watching the water go where I did not intend, by adjusting, by doing it again.

The Makers notice me. Not the way surface people notice each other — with acknowledgment, with greeting, with the social rituals of recognition. They notice me the way the structure notices a change in vibration. I am a new sound in the system. I am a body moving through chambers that bodies do not usually move through in that way, at that pace, with that purpose. They hear me. They smell me — and my scent is changing, the surface scent fading, the mineral-film-and-stone-dust scent growing, until I smell less like a stranger and more like a chamber, like a room where the film grows thick.

They do not speak to me. They do not avoid me. They work around me the way they work around a pillar of stone or a pool of water — as a feature of the structure, something to be accommodated, not something to be addressed. I am becoming part of the architecture. I am becoming a fixture.

---

There is a chamber. I will tell you about it because it is where I become what I am.

It is deep. Not the deepest — I do not go to the deepest levels, cannot go, my body too long and too fragile for the tightest passages, the lowest ceilings. But deep enough that the thrum is not a sound but a sensation, a vibration that enters through my knees and hips and jaw when I press myself against the floor. The chamber is small — four meters across, perhaps five, with a ceiling I can touch if I reach. It is warm. Warmer than the surrounding corridors, warmer than any chamber I have found at this depth. The warmth comes from below, from the deep work, from the thrum itself, which is heat as much as sound, the friction of stone on stone transmitted upward through the structure's body.

The walls are covered. Not just coated — *covered*. The film here is thick, complex, layered like sediment, and it is not the grey-green of the upper chambers. It is dark. Darker than the stone beneath it. In the lamplight — and I have a lamp now, a small clay dish with fat and a fiber wick, made for me by a Maker whose hands I once guided to a particularly good piece of stone, a reciprocal gift I did not know how to refuse — the film shimmers. Not iridescent. Not exactly. It shimmers the way oil shimmers on water, with colors that are not quite colors, shifts of light that my surface-eyes can barely perceive but that are there, undeniably there, in the one-inch flame of my lamp. Blues. Greens. A deep, impossible violet that I have never seen in any natural thing.

I kneel. I press my hands to the wall. The film is warm. Warmer than my skin. This is new. This is different. In the upper chambers, the film is cool — slightly warmer than the stone, slightly cooler than my body, a neutral temperature that tells me nothing. Here, the film is warm, and the warmth is not the stone's warmth. It is the film's own warmth. It is generating heat. It is metabolizing, alive, active, doing something — processing the minerals in the water, processing the organic compounds in the air, processing whatever it processes — and the byproduct is warmth, and the warmth is in my hands, and my hands are in the film, and the boundary is gone.

I stay.

I stay in that chamber for — I do not know how long. Time in the terraces is not time on the surface. There is no sun. There is no rhythm of day and night. There is only the rhythm of the work, and the work does not stop, and the rhythm is continuous, and so time is continuous, and so I cannot mark its passage except by the lamp, which burns down, which I refill, which burns down again. I stay. I tend the film. I direct water into the chamber through a channel I carve with a borrowed chisel, a slow, clumsy process that takes me — hours? days? — because my hands are not Maker hands and the chisel is not my tool. But the water comes. The film responds. It thickens. It spreads. It covers the walls more evenly, more completely, and where I have directed the water, the color deepens — the blues, the greens, the violet — and where I have not, it remains the grey-green of the upper chambers.

I am gardening. I am gardening in the dark, in the deep, in the body of a structure that is also a living thing, and the thing I am gardening is alive, and the life I am encouraging is beautiful, and beauty is a word I brought from the surface and a concept I cannot explain to the Makers and a reality that I can feel in my hands when I press them to the wall and the colors shimmer in my lamp and the film is warm and growing and *mine.*

Not mine. Not possessed. Not owned. Mine the way a breath is mine — held for a moment, then released, part of the body, part of the air.

---

I said I was the one who stays. Let me say more about what that means.

The surface cultures that know of the Makers — that have descended a few terraces, traded briefly, named them — they leave. They always leave. The structure is too dark, too loud, too damp, too alien. The surface body cannot tolerate it. The surface mind cannot tolerate it — the absence of meaning, the absence of narrative, the absence of *why*. They come with questions and the questions have no answers because the Makers do not have the concept of question. They come with language and the language has no purchase because the Makers' language is a language of coordination, not reflection. They come with selves — bounded, named, individual — and the structure does not recognize selves. The structure recognizes hands. The structure recognizes work. The structure recognizes vibration and moisture and stone, and a self is none of these things, and so the structure does not see the visitors, and the visitors feel this — feel unseen, feel erased, feel the terrible vertigo of existing in a world that does not acknowledge their existence — and they leave.

I do not leave. I stay because I find the thing that the structure does recognize. I find the film. The film does not care about selves either, but it responds to warmth, and I am warm, and it responds to touch, and I touch it, and it responds to care, and I care for it, and in the response, I find something I did not expect to find.

I find that I am needed.

Not by the Makers. They do not need me. They do not need anyone. They are complete. They are the structure, and the structure is complete, and the work is continuous, and the work does not require me. But the film — the film is not the structure. The film is *in* the structure, the way the pale organisms are in the structure, the way the larger grazers are in the structure, the way the water is in the structure. The film is a living thing in a living system, and it has its own needs, its own tendencies, its own patterns of growth and retreat and death and renewal. The Makers cultivate it incidentally. They manage the water, and the water feeds the film, and the film grows. But they do not tend it. They do not kneel beside a thinning patch and wonder what it needs. They do not watch a dry patch spread and feel something — not grief, not concern, but *attention*, the focused, warm attention of a body that has found its work.

I do. I feel that. I feel the attention. I feel the warmth of focus, the specificity of care, the deep, quiet pleasure of a living thing responding to my presence. And this feeling — this is what keeps me. Not the structure, not the sound, not the dark, not the damp. The feeling of being needed by something that does not know it needs me, of being useful to a process that does not know I exist, of tending a life that has no concept of being tended.

---

I will tell you what I become, though I am still becoming it, and the becoming is not finished, and I do not think it will finish, because the structure does not finish, and I am part of the structure now, and the structure does not finish, and so I do not finish.

I become a film-keeper. A tender of the living layer. A gardener in the body of the world.

My body changes. Not quickly. Not dramatically. I do not become a Maker — my arms do not lengthen, my spine does not compress, my skin does not mineralize. But my hands change. My eyes change. My ears change, a little. My sense of smell sharpens until I can find a film chamber by scent alone, following the sharp, clean minerality through the corridors the way a Maker follows the sound of stone. My hands become sensitive enough that I can read the film's health by touch — the thickness, the temperature, the texture, the moisture, the barely perceptible pulse of metabolic activity that tells me the organisms are alive and working. My eyes, in the perpetual dark, become something I do not recognize. I see the film's shimmer now. Not just in lamplight. In the dark. In the absolute, lampless, lightless dark of the deep chambers, I see a faint glow — not the glow of bioluminescence, or not only that, but a glow that might be my own eyes, my own neurons, my own visual cortex generating light where there is no light because it can no longer tolerate the absence. I see the film. I see the walls. I see the structure, dimly, silverly, in shades that are not colors but are not black, and I navigate by this impossible sight, and I do not question it, because questioning is a thing of the surface, and I am not of the surface anymore.

I do not become nameless. The Makers are nameless because they do not distinguish themselves from the structure. I still distinguish. I still have the word-mind, the naming-mind, the self-mind. I know I am I. I know the film is not I. I know the wall is not I. I know the Makers are not I. I maintain these distinctions because they are the distinctions that let me tend. A Maker does not tend because a Maker does not distinguish between tending and not-tending, between the film and the wall, between the work and the self. I tend because I distinguish. I tend because I can see the film as a thing separate from the wall, as a life separate from the structure, as a process that can be helped or hindered, encouraged or neglected. This is my gift. This is what I bring. The surface mind's capacity for distinction, for separation, for the identification of *this* and *not-this*, turned to the service of a structure that does not need the service but that accepts it, the way it accepts everything, by accretion, by the slow logic of things placed against other things and held by weight and friction and time.

---

There is a moment. Let me tell you about the moment.

I am in the deep chamber, the warm one, the one with the violet shimmer. I have been tending the film here for — I do not know. Long enough that the film is thick on every wall, thick on the ceiling, thick in the corners where the water pools. Long enough that the air in the chamber is the cleanest air I have ever breathed, sharp and mineral and alive. Long enough that the Makers have begun to come here — not to work, not to build, but to *be*. They come in pairs, in threes, and they sit against the wall, and they press their backs to the film, and they are still, and the stillness is not the stillness of sleep or fatigue. It is the stillness of the pause — the pause the text described, the hand on the wall, the two breaths of recognition. But here, in this chamber, the pause is longer. The Makers sit and lean and breathe and the film is against their skin and their skin is against the film and they are warm, all of them, the film and the Makers and the air and the stone, and the sound in this chamber is different — muffled, enclosed, the striking a distant patter, the thrum a deep cushion, the water a gentle, irregular music — and in this sound, in this warmth, they rest.

I am in the corner. I am tending a patch of film that has thinned near the floor, where the water does not reach. I am carving a small channel with my chisel, directing a trickle from the wall to the floor, and the film is already responding, darkening, thickening, spreading across the bare stone like a slow tide. My hands are wet. My knees are wet. My forearms are coated in the film, and the film is on my skin, and my skin is in the film, and I have stopped distinguishing between the two, not because I cannot — I can, I always can, the word-mind does not stop — but because I choose not to, because the distinction does not serve, because the work does not require it, because the work requires only the hands and the hands and the stone and the water and the film and the warmth.

A Maker — an old one, heavy-handed, broad-backed, mineral-skinned — shifts against the wall. The film compresses under their weight. They exhale — a long, slow exhale, not a sigh but a release, a settling, the sound of a body finding its place. And then they do something I have never seen a Maker do.

They speak. Not the functional, coordinative speech of the work. Not the chipped, percussive language of position and direction and stone-quality. A different sound. Low, sustained, not guttural but *resonant* — a hum, a vibration that starts in their chest and travels through their back and into the wall and into the film and through the film into the stone and through the stone into the structure and I feel it in my knees, in my hands, in the chisel I am holding. It is not a word. It is not a sentence. It is a tone. A single, sustained tone that matches — I realize this slowly, I realize this with the part of my mind that still names things — the resonant frequency of the chamber. The chamber rings. The film vibrates. The air shimmers. And the tone is not music, not exactly, but it is not not-music. It is the sound of a body in a place, a body that is part of the place, a body that has found the frequency at which body and place are the same vibration, and it is holding that frequency, and the holding is — I do not have a word. I do not have a word for it. The word-mind, for the first time, fails. Not because the thing is ineffable — I am tired of ineffability, it is a surface word for a surface experience — but because the thing is *prior* to words. The thing is in the body. The thing is in the vibration. The thing is in the warmth and the film and the stone and the hands, and words come after, always after, and I am here, now, before.

The tone fades. The Maker is still. The chamber settles. The film shimmers. I am kneeling on the floor with my hands against the wall and my chisel forgotten and the water running through the channel I carved and the film growing under my fingers and the sound of the structure all around me — the thrum, the striking, the water, the breath — and I am here. I am here. I am the one who stays. I am the one who tends. I am the one who gardens in the dark, in the body of the world, and the world does not know I am here and does not need to know and does not care, and I am here, and my hands are warm, and the film is warm, and the wall is warm, and the work continues, and the work is not mine and never was, and I am part of it, and it is part of me, and the distinction, the precious surface distinction between self and world, is still there — I can feel it, I will always feel it, I cannot unfeel it — but it is thin, it is so thin, it is as thin as the film on a dry wall, and the water is coming, and the water will make it thinner, and one day — not today, not soon, but one day — the film will cover it entirely, and the distinction will be gone, and I will be part of the structure the way the film is part of the structure, the way the water is part of the structure, the way the dead are part of the structure: not by losing myself but by becoming so thoroughly myself that the self and the place are the same warmth, the same vibration, the same sustained tone held in a warm chamber in the dark, forever, or until the tone fades, which is not an ending but a settling, which is not death but a return, which is not loss but the final, gentle incorporation of one more grain of sand into the reef that does not know it is a reef, that does not know it is beautiful, that does not know it is anything at all except stone and water and film and hands and the sound of hands on stone in the dark.

---

That is where you would find me. In the deep chambers. In the warm ones. In the rooms where the film shimmers violet and the air is clean and the Makers come to rest. Kneeling. Hands against the wall. Tending the living layer. The one who stays. The one who gardens. The one who brought the word-mind into the dark and let it fail, and in its failing, found the thing that words are made from: the vibration, the warmth, the touch, the breath. The thing before the word. The thing that the Makers have always known and never said, because saying is after, and they are before, and I am between, and between is where I stay, and staying is what I do, and doing is what I am.
