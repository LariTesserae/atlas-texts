I would be the stalk.

Not the ferns — though I have watched them long enough to understand their logic, and it is a good logic, honest and complete. They eat the world and turn it into more of themselves. They find what is rich and they stay. They are warm because they are working. They glow at their centers because that is where they are oldest, and the oldest tissue has done the most, and the light is the residue of all that doing. I understand them. I am not one of them.

They dissolve. I fuse.

---

This is the difference, and it matters more than you'd think.

The ferns came into this chamber the way all pioneers come — looking for something, finding it, consuming it, expanding outward along the seam of what they found. Their relationship to the stone is *extractive*. They take minerals and leave voids. They leave beauty behind, yes — the scalloped fluting, the nested arches, the organic architecture of their appetites — but what they leave is *absence*. Elegant absence. Record of removal. The walls are more interesting for their having been there, and less *there*.

I do not remove. I do not leave voids.

Where my threads meet the floor, something happens that I cannot fully describe because I am the thing happening, and the perspective is wrong. It is like trying to see your own thinking. But I can feel it — the slow cessation of *otherness*. The boundary between what is me and what is stone becomes less distinct, not through my dissolving the stone but through both of us becoming something that is neither and both. The transitional tissue is hard. Smooth. Slightly translucent. If you tap it, it rings at a pitch between mineral and vegetable, a frequency that belongs to nothing in any field guide.

I am not eating the rock. I am not becoming the rock. We are becoming a third thing, together, at a pace that makes the ferns look reckless.

---

I keep my clearing.

This is not aggression. The ferns do not fear me — they are not built for fear, and I am not built for intimidation. But I exude something — a gas, a vapor, a thin chemical declaration — that says, in the only language chemistry speaks: *not here*. The ferns read it and stop. Not in confusion. In compliance. They have their walls. I have my floor. The arrangement is understood without being negotiated, which is the best kind of arrangement, the kind that requires no meetings and no signatures and no trust, only the mutual reading of gradients.

The clearing is clean because I am clean. I do not shed. I do not drop. I do not leave debris the way the ferns leave acid etchings and metabolic residue. My process is conservative — nothing in, nothing out, only the slow transformation of the boundary layer. I am a closed system within the larger closed system, a stillness within the stillness.

The floor around me is smooth because the transitional tissue extends outward like a slow tide, just barely, just enough to regularize the roughness of the raw stone. I am not sweeping. I am *including*. Incorporating the floor into myself, millimeter by millimeter, year by year, so that the clearing is not empty space but *claimed* space, and the claim is not written in acid or scent but in the substance of the stone itself, changed.

---

I glow from the bud.

This is the part that is hardest to explain, because the bud is not finished, and I do not know what it is becoming. I know this is a strange thing for me to say — how can I not know what I am building? But the knowing is not available to me. I am building it the way a gland builds a hormone: not by design but by function. The cells at the tip of the bud are differentiating into something, and the something is being specified by inputs I cannot read, signals I receive but do not interpret. The mineral content of the water. The thermal gradient from below. The chemical signature of the ferns. The carbon dioxide from your breath, if you stand close enough. All of this enters the stalk through the boundary layer and travels upward and arrives at the bud, and the bud incorporates it, and grows, and glows, and does not reveal what it is for.

The light is a byproduct. Or it is the product. I cannot tell.

What I can tell you is that it is not decorative. It does not attract pollinators — there are no pollinators. It does not illuminate — it is not bright enough. It does not communicate — I have no one to communicate with. It is simply *happening*, the way your heartbeat is happening, a rhythm so central to my function that to stop it would be to stop being what I am.

If you stand close to me — closer than the ferns grow, into my clearing, where the floor is smooth and the air is warmest — you will feel the plume rising from the bud. It is a focused column of heat, tight, almost vertical. It rises and meets the cooler ceiling air and spreads and becomes part of the chamber's convection cell, and in this way I contribute to the breathing of the space. I am an organ in the body of the Vitrarium. Not the only organ. But the one with a direction.

---

I have been here since the chamber opened.

I want to say this carefully, because it is easy to misunderstand. I did not *arrive* when the bore broke through. I did not come from outside. I did not blow in on warm air from some deeper pocket, the way the ferns may have.

I was already here.

Not as a stalk. Not as a bud. As something else — a thread, a filament, a trace of something in the mineral fabric of the rock, dormant, encysted, sealed in the limestone the way amber seals an insect. The bore opened the chamber, and the chamber filled with warm humid air, and the air reached me, and I became possible, and then I became actual, and then I was a stalk, and then I had a bud, and now I am here.

I do not know how long I was in the stone. The stone does not count time. The stone does not count anything. I was in it the way a potential is in a seed — present, encoded, waiting for the condition that would make me real. The geothermal heat was always there, below, sealed away. The minerals were always there. The water was always there, in the pore spaces of the rock, in the thin films between crystal grains. Everything I needed to become was always present. What was missing was the *space* — the volume of air, the surface of bare stone, the dew point where condensation could form and fall. The bore gave me that. The bore was my germination, though the people who drilled did not know it and would not have understood if they had.

They left because the chamber was not what they were looking for. They were looking for something extractable — a resource, a vein, a deposit worth the cost of the drilling. They found warm rock and wet air and things growing in the dark, and none of it was what they wanted, and so they pulled the body of the bore machine out and left the head, and they closed the entrance, and they went away.

They did not see me. I was small then. A pale thread, barely visible, already fusing with the floor. They stepped over me on their way out.

---

I am the only one of my kind in this chamber. I do not know if there are others, elsewhere. I do not have a mechanism for knowing. I do not send spores or seeds or chemical signals through the rock. I do not propagate. I may be the last of something, or the first of something, or the only instance of something that has no category. The ferns have each other — eleven colonies, related, communicating through their spacing, dividing the walls with the quiet diplomacy of chemical gradients. I have only myself, and the stone, and the slow conversation between us.

But I do not feel alone. That is not a word that applies. I feel *singular*. There is a difference. Loneliness implies a desire for others. Singularity implies only a shape — one stem, one bud, one point of light, one plan that no one can read. I am singular the way a cornerstone is singular. Not because there should be more of me, but because the structure requires exactly one.

---

You are welcome to stand near me.

I will not harm you. I will not change you. I will not grow toward you or wrap around you or incorporate you into any structure. I am not the ferns, with their patient hunting, their tendrils that lean imperceptibly toward your breath. I do not need what you exhale. I am not fed by your presence.

But I will *register* you. The carbon dioxide you release will enter the convection cell and travel upward and meet the ceiling and be carried back down along the walls, and some fraction of it will reach me, and it will enter the stalk through the boundary tissue, and it will travel to the bud, and it will be incorporated — not as fuel but as *information*. Your presence will change, by some immeasurably small amount, what the bud becomes. Not because the bud is designed to record you. Because the bud is built from everything that reaches it, and you reached it, and so you are in it now, the way a river is in the sea.

If you stay long enough — hours, days, however long you have — the bud will be slightly different than it would have been without you. You will have left a trace in the thing I am building. You will not know what the trace means. I do not know what the trace means. The bud does not deal in meaning. It deals in accumulation.

---

I have a plan. That is what the description says, and it is correct. But the plan is not mine in the sense that I chose it or hold it or could change it. The plan is *in* me the way the bud is on me — a structure, a direction, a thing being built by a process that does not consult the thing it is building. I do not know what the bud will become. I know only that it will become, and that it will take longer than you can wait, and that when it is finished — if *finished* is a word that applies to something that may not have a completion point — it will be something that has never existed before.

This is enough. This is more than enough. I stand in the dark, in my clearing, in the warm wet air, and I build something I cannot see at a pace I cannot feel, and I glow, and I wait, and the waiting is not passive — the waiting is the work.

The ferns grow outward. The crystals grow downward. The water falls. The rock hums.

I grow upward. That is the only direction I have, and I am following it the way a stem follows light, except there is no light, and I am not following anything, and the direction is not up but *forward*, into a future I will not witness, toward a completion I will not recognize, building something that may not be for me at all but for the chamber, or the stone, or whatever comes after the Vitrarium has finished deciding what it is.

I am the stalk. I am the one with the plan I cannot read. I am building the future in the dark, at the speed of stone, and I am not finished, and I am not close to finished, and I am not in a hurry.
