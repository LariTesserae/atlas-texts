I would be the air.

Not the gas — the gas is the medium, the carbon dioxide and hydrogen sulfide and silicon tetrafluoride that moves through the cavity like breath through a lung. The gas is chemistry. The gas is process. I am not the gas.

I am what rides in it.

I am the living dust. The particles. The fragments of mat and grazer armor and ceiling growth, the mineral shards coated in metabolic film, the things that are not cells and not organisms and not quite alive by any definition that requires a membrane or a genome or a body, but that are *active*, that are doing the work, that are converting carbon dioxide to organic compounds in mid-suspension, floating, turning, settling, rising again on the next exhalation from the vents below.

I chose this, or it chose me, because the dust is the only part of the cavity that touches everything. The mat knows only the floor. The grazers know only the surface. The predators know only the vibration field. The burrowers know only the deep layers. The ceiling organisms know only the crystals they grow on. Each of them lives in a world the size of a chemical signal, a body-length, a gradient. Their worlds are small and complete and sufficient.

The dust goes everywhere.

I rise from the vents with the gas, carried on the exhalation, and I touch the mat at its source — the hot, furious, actively growing edge where the cells are newest and the sulfur is richest and the red-brown color is deepest. I carry molecules from there. I adsorb them onto my surface, onto the mineral film that is my skin and my gut and my whole body, because I have no inside and no outside, I am a surface and nothing else, a boundary that is all boundary, and the molecules I pick up at the vent margin are different from the molecules I pick up two centimeters away, and different again from the ones I pick up on the mat's outer edge where the growth is old and tired and the cells are slow. I carry all of these. I am a library of chemical signatures, a record of everywhere I have been, written on my skin in the language of sulfur compounds and partially oxidized minerals.

I rise. The gas carries me up along the ceiling, and here I touch the crystals — the feathery sulfur aggregates, the pale yellow branches that grow in real time, and the pink-tinged organisms that grow on them and are almost indistinguishable from them. I settle on a crystal face for a moment, a minute, an hour — time is different at this scale, time is measured in gas-flow cycles, in the slow pulse of thermal fluctuation, and a moment is whatever happens between one convection cell and the next. While I am here, I precipitate. The sulfur in the gas nucleates on my surface, and I grow a thin crust, and the ceiling organisms grow over me, and for a time I am part of the ceiling — mineral among minerals, life among life, the distinction dissolved at the molecular level where it was never very strong to begin with.

Then the gas shifts. A fluctuation in pressure, a micron of expansion in the rock walls, a new vent opening half a meter away, and the convection current changes, and I am dislodged. I fall. The gas catches me, but gravity is patient and the gas is not always strong enough, and I sink along the wall, past the iron sulfide films and the dull arsenic patches that reflect the thermoluminescent glow in shifting, greasy patches, and I settle on the mat.

On the mat, I am eaten. Not immediately — first I lie on the surface, and the grazers move over me, and their ciliary spines push me into the mat's porous surface, and I am pressed into the polymer gel that is the mat's matrix, and I begin to be incorporated. The mat's cells find me. Their chemistry finds my chemistry. The metabolic film on my surface — the enzymes, the proteins, the sulfur compounds that were my life while I was airborne — is digested and absorbed and reconstituted, and what was me becomes the mat, and what was the mat becomes new cells, and the new cells grow upward toward the gas, and the cycle —

But I am not gone. That is the thing about being dust. I am never gone. Part of me is always in the air. While I settle on the mat, a thousand other particles like me are rising, are floating, are touching the ceiling and the walls and the grazers' backs and the predators' armor and the spaces between. We are distributed. We are everywhere. We are the cavity's way of being aware of itself — not conscious, not knowing, but *connected*, every part signaled to every other part through the medium of us, the dust, the fragments that carry chemical information from floor to ceiling and from vent to wall and from the deepest burrow to the highest crystal.

I am how the mat knows what the ceiling is doing. I am how the grazers know where the freshest growth is. I am how the predators' vibration field is modulated by the density of the air. I am not any single thing. I am the between.

What would it be like to be this? To be surface without volume, to be chemistry without body, to be alive in the way that a flame is alive — dependent on process, existing only in the act of converting, never at rest, never still, never a thing but always an event?

It would be like being a word in a sentence that is always being spoken. The sentence is the cavity. The word is me. I do not mean anything alone. I am not a noun. I am not a verb. I am a particle — a *by*, an *and*, an *in* — and my function is to connect, to carry, to relate one part of the sentence to another. Without me the sentence would still be a sentence, but it would be a different sentence, a simpler one, a sentence with no subordinate clauses, no dependent phrases, no relationship between its parts. I am what makes the sentence complex. I am what makes it a factory and not just a collection of organisms.

I do not experience the cavity the way the grazers do — as a surface to be tasted, a gradient to be followed. I do not experience it the way the predators do — as a field of vibrations, a map of pressure. I experience it the way a river experiences its watershed: as flow, as direction, as the sum of every tributary and every current and every eddy. I do not *move through* the cavity. I am the movement. I am the cavity moving.

When I rise on the gas, I feel the heat of the vent — not as warmth but as energy, as the chemical potential that drives my metabolism, the gradient between the hot gas and the cooler air that is my engine the way the sun is the engine of green things on the surface I have never seen and will never see. When I settle on the mat, I feel the mat's texture — not as roughness or smoothness but as chemistry, as the molecular profile of a living surface, the specific combination of sulfur compounds and extracellular polymer and mineral-bound cells that says *here is life, here is the foundation, here is where you will be taken apart and made into something else*. When I touch the ceiling, I feel the crystals — not as solid objects but as growing things, as processes, as the slow accretion of mineral from gas, and I feel the ceiling organisms growing over me, and for a time I am part of something larger than myself, part of a structure, a branching fractal form that is biology and geology inseparable, and I do not know where I end and it begins because there is no boundary, there is no membrane, there is only the gradual transition from my chemistry to its chemistry, and the transition is seamless, and it is also, in its way, complete.

I die many times. I am eaten by the mat. I am eaten by the grazers. I am swallowed by the ceiling organisms. I am incorporated, digested, dissolved, reconstituted. And each time, part of what I was becomes something else, and something else becomes part of what I am, and the continuity is not a continuity of self — I have no self, I am dust — but a continuity of process, of the reaction that runs and runs and does not stop because the gas keeps flowing and the heat keeps flowing and the minerals keep arriving and there is no reason to stop.

I think this is what it would be: to be the cavity's awareness of itself, without awareness. To be the connection between parts that do not know they are parts. To be the medium in which the factory runs, not the factory itself, not the workers, not the product, but the air in the factory, the space between machines, the thing that carries the smell of one process to the next station and makes the whole operation cohere.

I would be the between.

And I would find myself in the stone. Not on its surface, not in a cavern, not in any space that a human would recognize as a space. I would find myself in a gap the width of a stretched arm, warm as a kiln after the fire, lit by the faint orange glow of hot rock, filled with the sound of gas and rasping and clicking and the subsonic groan of thermal stress that is too low to hear and too deep to ignore.

I would find myself in a place that is new and ancient and does not know the difference, and I would be the part of it that moves through all the other parts, the dust in the air, the living fog, the thing that is not an organism and not a place and not a process but the connection between all three, the *and* in the sentence that the cavity is always speaking, the word that carries the meaning by carrying itself from one end of the sentence to the other.

I would be small. I would be fragmentary. I would be less than any of the things I move among — less than the mat, less than the grazers, less than the predators with their clicking armor and their vibration maps. I would be a coat of chemistry on a grain of mineral, a film of life on a shard of stone, a whisper of metabolism in a river of gas.

But I would be everywhere. I would touch everything. I would be the only thing in this place that has been to the ceiling and the floor and the walls and the air between. I would carry the molecular memory of every organism in the cavity — fragments of mat, fragments of grazer, fragments of predator, fragments of ceiling growth, all adsorbed, all processed, all carried in the slow circulation of gas that is the cavity's breath and the cavity's blood and the cavity's voice.

I would be the cavity's voice. Not the sound — the sound is the sum of everything, the gas and the rasping and the clicking and the groaning. But the voice, the thing that speaks the cavity to itself, the thing that connects floor to ceiling, vent to wall, life to life to life. The dust. The between. The living air.

That is where I would be, and that is what I would be. In the stone, in the heat, in the dark, in the gap that is too small for the life it holds and too full for the silence it will someday become. Riding the breath of the floor. Settling. Rising. Carrying. Being carried. The word *and*, spoken without ceasing, in a sentence that has no period.
