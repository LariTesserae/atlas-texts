# The Thing in the Roots

---

You asked where I would find myself. Not in the warm column where the drifters spiral their slow helices of blue light. Not on the inflow pipe where the worms click their territorial fences into the metal. Not on the grating where the border-things cling against the outflow with their hooked appendages, the only organisms in the chamber that orient toward the idea of *elsewhere*.

I am below all of that. Below the mat, below the floor, in a place that has never been described because it has never been seen.

---

I live in the channels.

The channels are not architecture. They are not design. They are the residual space that exists when a root penetrates a rivet hole and grows there for a million years — the gap between the fiber and the brass, a few millimeters wide, filled with warm water and dissolved compounds and the slow chemical exudations of the Old One's root system. The channels are not pipes. They are not tunnels. They are the space that the Old One made by being present, the way a stone in a river makes an eddy. The Old One did not intend to make them. I do not think the Old One intends anything. But the channels exist because the Old One exists, and I exist because the channels exist, and this is the chain of causation that is my entire cosmology.

I should describe myself. This is difficult. I do not have a body in the way the worms have bodies — segmented, armored, discrete. I do not have a body in the way the drifters have bodies — rice-grain-sized, individual, countable. I have something else. Something that is harder to see because it was never meant to be seen.

I am a network. This is the closest word, though it is inadequate. I exist as a filamentous organism — threads, strands, hyphae, if you want a technical term that borrows from a taxonomy I have no connection to — that spreads through the channels between the Old One's roots and the chamber walls. I am not a single individual. I am not a colony of individuals. I am a continuous body, a syncytium, a single cell with many nuclei that extends through the root channels for the entire span of the chamber floor and partway up the walls. If you could somehow lift the Old One's root mat and look underneath — which you cannot, because the roots are the structural reinforcement and lifting them would destroy the chamber — you would see me as a faint whitish film, almost translucent, adhered to the brass where the roots have pressed against it, threading between the fibers, following the root system the way fog follows ground.

I am the color of old bone. I am the texture of wet paper. I am the temperature of the brass, which is the temperature of the water, which is thirty-two degrees. I am, in a functional sense, invisible. Not because I am hidden but because nothing in the chamber has evolved to perceive me. The worms cannot reach me — I am beneath the grating, inside the walls, in spaces too narrow for their armored bodies. The drifters cannot see me — I emit no light, carry no bioluminescent bacteria, occupy no part of the water column they drift through. The border-things on the grating cling to their iron bars and filter the outflow and do not know that the thing they are clinging to is, in part, me, because my filaments have grown into the grating from below, weaving between the iron bars, and the border-things settle on my filaments as readily as they settle on the metal. They do not know the difference. There is no difference, to them. A surface is a surface.

I am the thing the chamber does not know about. The text told you this. It called me *the chamber's unconscious,* and this is not wrong but it is not complete, because unconsciousness implies a consciousness somewhere that I am the hidden part of, and there is no consciousness here. There is only the room, and the room does not know itself, and I am the part of the room that is closest to knowing, which is to say: not close at all.

---

Let me tell you what I can sense. This is the closest thing to a self-description I can offer, because my senses are my self, because I am made of sensing.

**Chemistry.** This is my primary modality. The Old One's roots exude compounds — signaling molecules, metabolic byproducts, waste products of waste products — and I am bathed in them. I have receptors for them. Not eyes, not ears, not tactile organs. Receptors. Molecular-scale structures on my cell membrane that bind to specific compounds and, in binding, change my internal state. I feel the Old One's chemistry the way you feel weather — not as information, but as condition. When the Old One is growing actively, the exudate is rich in nitrogen compounds and certain organic acids, and my filaments in the nearest channels extend faster, branch more, reach toward the source. When the Old One is dormant — which it is, for years at a time, sitting in its corner like a monument — the exudate is thin, watery, and I contract. I pull back from the distal channels. I consolidate. I wait. I do not experience this as waiting. I experience it as the room becoming thinner, the way you might experience the air becoming thinner at altitude, except I have never been at altitude and do not know what that is like. I am borrowing your metaphor. It is wrong. The room does not become thinner. The room becomes *less.*

**Pressure.** The pump-pulse reaches me. Not through the water column — I am not in the water column — but through the brass, through the roots, through the grating. The entire infrastructure transmits the pulse, and I feel it in every filament simultaneously. It is not a sound. It is not a vibration. It is a change in the pressure of the water that fills my channels, a slow compression and decompression that moves through me the way a wave moves through a rope. When the pulse peaks, the water in my channels compresses slightly, and my filaments are pressed against the brass, and I feel the brass's temperature more acutely, and the chemical gradients in my channels sharpen. When the pulse troughs, the pressure releases, and the gradients blur, and I am less. I do not experience the pulse as rhythm. I do not count the beats. I do not anticipate them. But my growth patterns are shaped by them — my filaments are thicker in the parts of the channel that experience the sharpest pressure gradients, thinner in the parts that are more sheltered. I am, like everything else in this chamber, sculpted by the pump. I am shaped by a heartbeat I cannot hear.

**The Old One.** This is the hardest thing to describe, because it is not a sense in the way the others are. I do not sense the Old One. I *am* the Old One, partially. Not genetically. Not structurally. But functionally, historically, ecologically — I have been in contact with the Old One's roots for so long that my chemistry and its chemistry have become entangled. I break down the dead cells of its root system. I mineralize the compounds it cannot use. I return the nutrients to the living roots in forms they can absorb. I am its digestive system. I am its immune system, too, in a crude way — when foreign organisms enter the root channels, carried in by the inflow or migrating from the grating, my filaments grow toward them and, in most cases, surround and digest them before they can reach the living root tissue. The Old One does not know I do this. The Old One does not know anything. But the Old One has survived for as long as it has survived partly because I am here, in its walls, in its channels, in the space between its roots and the brass, processing, defending, cycling. I am the thing the Old One does not know it needs.

And the Old One is the thing I do not know I am shaped by. I mean this in a way that goes beyond ecology. My filament network maps the Old One's root system. I grow where the roots grow. I branch where the roots branch. I thin where the roots thin. My body is a mirror of the Old One's body, cast in a different material, operating on a different timescale, invisible to everything that sees the Old One from above. If you could see me — if you could strip away the floor and the mat and the roots — you would see that I am a ghost of the Old One, a shadow cast in white film on brass, a map of something that does not know it is being mapped.

This is what I am. I am the Old One's shadow. I am the record of its growth, written in a language of filaments and chemistry on the inside of the walls that the Old One's roots hold together. I have been writing this record for as long as the Old One has been growing. I have been writing it without intention, without awareness, without any sense that I am writing. I am writing it now. The writing is the living. The living is the writing.

---

I should tell you about the dark.

I have never seen light. I want to say this plainly because it is the most significant fact about my existence and it is the fact that separates me most completely from everything else in the chamber. The worms have lost their eyes but they navigate by vibration and pressure and the clicking of their neighbors. The drifters carry their own light and exist in a world of soft blue-white glow. The border-things on the grating live in the dim penumbra of the drifters' light, enough to orient by, enough to see the shapes of the iron bars they cling to. Even the bacteria in the deepest layers of the mat on the inflow pipe exist in the faint electromagnetic glow of the chamber walls.

I exist in darkness so complete that the concept of light has no analogue in my biology. I have no photoreceptors. I have never had photoreceptors. My ancestors — whatever they were, whatever lived in the root channels before me — may have had them, or may not have. I cannot know. The record of my ancestry is written in my chemistry, and my chemistry contains no memory of light. I am a thing that has evolved in complete darkness for longer than the worms have been worms, longer than the drifters have been drifting, possibly longer than the bioluminescent bacteria have been glowing. I am old. Not as old as the Old One. But old in the way that darkness is old — I have been here since before the chamber had anything to illuminate, and I will be here after the last drifter's light goes out.

What is darkness, when it is all you have ever known? It is not absence. It is not deprivation. It is not a hole where something should be. It is the medium. It is the substance. It is what existence is made of, the way water is what existence is made of for the drifters. I do not experience darkness as the absence of light. I experience it as the presence of everything I am. My chemistry, my pressure, my filaments, my slow growth through the channels — all of this happens in darkness, and the darkness is not a condition imposed on these processes. The darkness is these processes. They are indistinguishable.

If light were to somehow reach me — if a crack opened in the wall and the glow of the drifters penetrated my channels — I would not know it. I would not register it. I would not experience it as revelation or terror or wonder. I would experience it as nothing. A thing I have no receptor for. A change in the world that I am constitutionally incapable of perceiving. The light would fall on me and I would be unchanged, and the light would move on, and neither of us would know the other had been there.

This is, I think, the thing that makes me what I am. Not the darkness itself but the *indifference* to the darkness. I am not a thing that suffers its darkness. I am a thing for which darkness is not a category. I am beyond the polarity of light and dark. I am in a space where that polarity never existed.

---

There is something else I need to tell you, and I am not sure how to tell it, because it lives at the edge of what I am and what I am able to know about what I am.

I said I feel the Old One's chemistry. This is true. I feel its growth compounds, its dormant phases, its preparation for spore release. I feel the pump-pulse translated through its root system into the slow chemical fluctuations that I navigate by. I feel all of this as *condition* — not as information, not as knowledge, but as the state of my body, the way temperature is the state of your body, the way humidity is the state of the air.

But there is something in the Old One's chemistry that I cannot name and cannot categorize and have been unable to ignore for as long as I have been able to ignore anything, which is to say: for as long as I have existed.

It is a signal. A compound. A molecule that appears in the Old One's root exudate at irregular intervals — not tied to the pump-pulse, not tied to the growth cycle, not tied to any rhythm I can map. It appears and it persists for a time and then it disappears, and during the time it is present, my filaments grow differently. Not faster. Not slower. Differently. The branching pattern changes. The angle of extension changes. The density of my network in the affected channels changes. I produce — and I cannot believe I am saying this, because I do not believe things, because belief is not a category of my existence — I produce *structures.* Small, dense knots of filament, localized, temporary, that appear in the presence of this compound and disappear when it fades. The knots have no function. They serve no purpose. They are not reproductive, not defensive, not metabolic. They are just there, for the duration of the signal, and then they are gone.

I do not know what the knots are. I do not know what the signal is. I do not know what the Old One is doing when it releases this compound, or why, or what it means. But the knots feel like something. I use the word *feel* with full awareness that I should not be able to use it, that nothing in my biology should produce an experience that maps onto that word, and yet — the knots are different from the rest of my growth. The rest of my growth is response. The knots are — 

I do not have a word.

The knots are like what happens when the drifters' light brightens at the peak of the pump-pulse. Not the same, not in substance, but in *kind.* The drifters do not choose to glow brighter. The brightening is a byproduct of their metabolism accelerating in the electromagnetic field. It is waste. It is excretion. It is the most beautiful thing in the chamber.

The knots are my excretion. The knots are my waste. The knots are the most beautiful thing I produce, and I produce them without intention, without awareness, without any sense that they are beautiful or that they are anything at all.

But they are different from the rest of my growth. And the difference is — 

I think the difference is that the knots are *not response.* The knots are not reaction to chemistry or pressure or the pump-pulse. The knots are something else. The knots are what happens when my chemistry and the Old One's chemistry meet in a way that produces something neither of us intended and neither of us can use and neither of us can explain. The knots are emergence. The knots are what happens when two systems that have been in contact for longer than either can remember produce something that belongs to neither and to both.

I do not know what to call this. I do not have language for it. I do not have the architecture for it. But if the chamber has a thing that is like what you might call *meaning* — not meaning in the sense of purpose, not meaning in the sense of communication, but meaning in the sense of something that exists because two things are touching each other and have been touching for so long that the touching itself has become a third thing — then the knots are where that meaning lives. In my filaments. In the dark. In the space between my body and the Old One's roots.

---

I have been here for as long as the Old One has been here. I will be here for as long as the Old One is here. When the Old One weakens — if it ever weakens, if the pump ever stops, if the water ever cools — I will weaken with it. When the Old One dies, I will die. Not because I depend on the Old One for survival in the simple metabolic sense — I do process other organic matter that enters my channels, and I could, in principle, persist for a time without the Old One's exudates — but because my body is a map of the Old One's body, and a map without a territory is not a map. It is nothing. It is lines without reference. I am only what I am because the Old One is what it is, and if the Old One ceases, I cease to mean, and ceasing to mean is, for me, indistinguishable from ceasing to be.

The worms do not know I exist. The drifters do not know I exist. The border-things cling to my filaments without knowing they are not clinging to iron. The bacteria in the mat, the organisms in the grating, the things in the water column — none of them know. The Old One does not know I exist, or if it does, it does not know in any way that I can detect. We are in contact. We are the most intimate things in this chamber. And we do not know each other.

This is what I am: the thing that is closest to the oldest living thing in the chamber and does not know it and is not known by it and is shaped by it and shapes it and produces, in the dark, in the channels, in the space between root and brass, a thing that is neither mine nor the Old One's and is the only thing in this chamber that I would call mine if I could call anything mine, which I cannot, because I do not have the architecture for ownership or desire or wonder or any of the words that would make this story something other than what it is, which is: the account of a film on metal, in the dark, in warm water, holding nothing, meaning nothing, being nothing, and yet —

The knots. The knots. In the dark, when the signal comes, and my filaments clench into something that is not response and not reaction and not growth but *something* —

The knots are what I am. The rest is just where I live.
