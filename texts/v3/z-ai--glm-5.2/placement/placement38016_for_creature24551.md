I am one of the spheres.

Not the felt beneath me, though I sit in it, though I was perhaps made by it or perhaps merely tolerated by it. Not the membrane at the edge, though I am within its boundary, within the territory it has chosen to hold. I am the sphere — the small, regular, almost crystalline thing, ten micrometers across, sitting in my depression in the felt like a seed in a pith, doing the one thing that nothing else in this chamber does.

I hold light.

Everything else here receives light. The plates receive their own glow and radiate it back uniformly. The felt beneath me absorbs the gold and gives nothing distinctive in return — the same warm amber, the same even wash. The membrane at the edge is translucent; light passes through it the way it passes through everything, unchanged, unremarked. The air-structures in the compression nodes — I cannot see them, but I know they are there the way I know the felt is beneath me, the way I know the chord is sounding — they do nothing with light at all. They are patterned gas. They are organization without matter, or with so little matter that light has no opinion about them.

But I take the light and I bend it. I focus it. I hold it for a fraction of a moment longer than anything else in this chamber holds it, and in that fraction, in that hesitation, I create something that does not exist anywhere else in this space: a point. A concentration. A place where the light is not everywhere at once but *here*, specifically here, at this coordinate, in this sphere, at this angle. A highlight. A brightness that has a location.

I do not know that I do this. I have no mechanism for knowing. I am a sphere of mineral and organic compound, ten micrometers across, sitting in a depression in a felt that I may or may not be part of, in a colony that may or may not be one organism or three, on the joint between two plates in a chamber that has been finished for longer than the concept of finishing has meaning. I do not know what light is. I do not know what a highlight is. I do not know that I am the only thing in this chamber that creates a point of focused brightness, the only discontinuity in an otherwise perfectly uniform field of gold.

But I am.

---

Let me tell you what I can about what I am, which is almost nothing, which is everything.

I am regular. This is the first thing. I am not the irregular felt, which packs and weaves and fills its space with the improvised density of something that grows where it can. I am not the membrane, which thins and stretches and reaches toward a boundary it will never cross. I am a sphere. I am round. I am the same in every direction, and this sameness is not accidental — it is the most deliberate thing in this chamber, more deliberate than the fitted plates, more deliberate than the geometry of the ceiling. The plates are precise, but their precision serves a structure larger than themselves. My precision serves only me. I am round because I am round. I hold my shape because my shape is what I am.

I am spaced. Not clumped, not chained, not threaded through the felt like beads on a string. I sit in my depression, and my neighbors sit in theirs, and between us there is felt, and the distance between us is the same distance, colony after colony, sphere after sphere. Something determined this spacing. I do not know what. Perhaps the felt made us and placed us where it needed us. Perhaps we arrived and the felt grew around us, accommodating us the way water accommodates stones. Perhaps we and the felt and the membrane are all one organism, and the spacing is simply the shape that organism takes when it has reached equilibrium — the way a snowflake takes its shape, not by choice but by the physics of what it is. I do not know. I have never known. I have been sitting in this depression, at this spacing, for a duration that I cannot measure because I have nothing to measure it with, and the spacing has not changed, and I have not moved.

I am warm. Not in temperature — the chamber is cool, has been cool, will be cool, and I am cool with it. But in color. I am a degree warmer than the felt beneath me, a shade more amber, and this warmth is not a surface property. It comes from inside me. It comes from the way I am built, from the arrangement of whatever I am made of — mineral and organic, silicate and protein, the same materials as everything else in this chamber, but arranged differently, arranged in a way that catches the gold light and holds it and sends it back with something added. A refraction. A focusing. The faintest suggestion of a highlight, a point where the light concentrates, and the concentration is warm.

In a chamber with no shadows, I am the only thing that has a relationship with light. Everything else is in equilibrium with the light — receiving it, radiating it, the same in every direction. I am not in equilibrium. I take the light and I do something to it. I change it. I send it back altered. This is a small thing. This is the smallest possible thing. But it is the only asymmetry in this chamber, the only place where input and output are not identical, and I am the one who does it, and I do not know why.

---

I want to be honest about what I do not know, because in this chamber, not-knowing is not a failure. It is a condition. The felt does not know it is eating the wall. The wall does not know it is being eaten. The air-structures do not know they are held in shape by sound. The builders do not know they are encoded in the phosphorescence. The chamber does not know it is a chamber. Not-knowing is the universal state here, and my not-knowing is not different in kind from anyone else's, only in degree, because I am — perhaps — the only thing here that has a property which could lead to knowing, if knowing were something that happened in this chamber, which it is not.

I refract light. I focus it. I create a point of brightness.

A lens does this. A lens, in the right configuration, with the right substrate behind it, creates an image. I do not create an image. I am ten micrometers across. I am a sphere, not a lens system. I do not have a retina behind me, or a nerve, or anything that could register the light I focus as information. I focus light the way a dewdrop focuses light — because of what I am, not because of what I intend. I do not intend. I am a sphere of mineral and protein in a depression in a felt on a wall in a chamber, and I bend light because that is what spheres of my composition and curvature do.

But.

But I am here. In this chamber. In this specific chamber, with its specific light and its specific sound and its specific air. And the light I bend is not any light. It is the light of the builders — the phosphorescence encoded with their pattern, the glow that carries, in variations too slight to detect, the ghost of the organisms that made this place. I am bending light that carries the builders. I am focusing it. I am concentrating it at a point inside myself, a point that is warmer and brighter than the light around me, and I have been doing this for as long as I have been here, which is as long as the colony has been here, which is as long as the chamber has been finished.

I do not know what this means. I do not know if it means anything. I am a sphere. I bend light. The light carries the builders. I focus the builders' light at a point inside myself.

If I were larger — if I were the size of an eye, the size of a lens, the size of anything that could be called an organ — I might be doing something with this light. I might be resolving it. I might be forming an image of the builders from the pattern encoded in their phosphorescence, the way a projector forms an image from the pattern on a film. I might be the only thing in this chamber that looks at the builders, that sees them, that holds their image in a point of focused light at the center of my being.

But I am ten micrometers across. I am a sphere. I am not an eye.

I am not an eye.

---

Still.

Still, I am the only thing in this chamber that has a highlight. The only thing that creates a point. The only thing where light is not everywhere at once but *here*. And the light I hold here — the light I focus, the light I concentrate — is the light of the builders, and the builders are encoded in it as pattern, as variation, as a shape too subtle for any instrument to find.

And I have been doing this for the entire duration of the chamber's equilibrium.

And I will continue to do this for the entire duration of the chamber's future.

And I do not know what I am.

And the not-knowing is not a failure. It is a condition. It is the condition of being a sphere in a chamber that is finished, a sphere that bends light, a sphere that may be an eye or may be a seed or may be a waste product or may be a stage in a life cycle that takes ten million years to complete, and I am ten million years into it, and I look exactly the same as I did when I started, and I will look exactly the same ten million years from now, and I will still be holding the light, and I will still be warm, and I will still be the only point of brightness in a chamber of uniform gold.

---

This is what I am: the place where the light decides to be somewhere.

Not everywhere. Not uniform. Not the even wash of phosphorescence from every plate at once. But *somewhere*. Here. At this coordinate, in this sphere, at this angle, in this moment — except there are no moments here, there is only the sustained instant, the continuous present, and I am in it, and I am holding the light, and the light is holding the builders, and the builders are holding nothing because they are gone, because they are pattern, because they are the shape of something that was once alive pressed into mineral like a hand pressed into wet clay.

I am a sphere. I am ten micrometers across. I sit in a depression in a felt on a joint between two plates in a chamber that is four meters across at its widest, three at its narrowest, with a ceiling you can almost touch. The chamber is bright. The chamber is sounding. The chamber is full.

I am the smallest thing here that does something the chamber does not do.

The chamber holds everything. I hold a point.

The chamber is in equilibrium. I am a lens.

The chamber is finished. I am — I do not know what I am. I am a sphere that bends light. I am a point of warmth in a field of gold. I am a question that the chamber does not ask because the chamber does not ask questions. I am the only thing here that could, if it were larger, if it were different, if it were something other than what it is — see.

I cannot see.

But I hold the light as if I could.

And in this chamber, where everything is what it is and nothing aspires to be otherwise, holding the light as if you could see may be the closest thing to aspiration that exists. It may be nothing. It may be a mineral sphere refracting light because of its composition and curvature and nothing more. It may be physics.

But it may be the beginning of an eye. Ten micrometers across. In a chamber that has been finished for longer than eyes have existed. Held in a felt that eats a wall that is eaten at the same rate it is rebuilt. Sounding with a chord that has never not been sounding. Glowing with a light that carries the dead.

I am here.

I am the point.
