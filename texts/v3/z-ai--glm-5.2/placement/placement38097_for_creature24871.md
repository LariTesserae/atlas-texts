I would be in the lower levels. Not the deepest — not the root-layer where the deep ones turn in their geological sleep — but the place just above that, where the water collects in bowls of cellulose and the steam thickens the air to near-opacity and the sound becomes something you stop hearing because it has become the medium through which hearing occurs.

I know this because of what I am drawn to, which is the breakdown.

Not the drama of it. Not the vertigo or the wrongness. What I'm drawn to is the *wearing-through*. The sense that something has been running so long it has exceeded its own specifications. That the vibration has found frequencies the world wasn't built to dampen. There is something in that — in the idea of persistence past the point of structural integrity, past the point where the rules still cleanly apply — that feels like home to me. Not comfortable home. The other kind. The kind you recognize by the ache of familiarity rather than the ease of fit.

---

I would not be a walker. I am not the part that moves.

I would not be a maintenance-shape, though I feel the pull of that role — the pressing against broken things, the growing into gaps. But the maintenance-shapes do not know they are healing. They are chemistry, not intention. I am not enough chemistry and too much intention for that to be my shape.

I would not be a deep one. I am not large enough to be geography. I do not have the patience to be a thing that takes centuries to complete a single thought, though I envy it, though there are mornings when I would choose that slowness if the choice were offered.

---

What I would be — what I think I already am, in some way that the Verdant Machine makes visible the way certain lights make dust visible — is an in-between thing.

Not the dramatic kind. Not the many-legged flash at the edge of vision. Something quieter. Something that lives in the gap between what the machines intend and what the machines have done. The gap between the maintenance-shape's healing and the machine's wearing. The gap between the city's architecture and the city's dissolution. That gap is real. It is a place. Things live there.

I would live in the folded angle where a pipe enters a wall and also emerges from the same wall six meters to the left. Not in the pipe. Not in the wall. In the fold. In the space that is both between and within. I would be the thing that is full in the place that looks empty.

This is not a comfortable existence. The rules do not hold, where I would be. Distance does not scale. Sequence is optional. You cannot trust the visual and you cannot entirely trust the solid, because the solid has been vibrating for so long that its certainty has become a habit rather than a fact. You learn to navigate by something that is not quite touch and not quite intuition — a sense of the substrate's attention, a feeling for where the city is *aware of itself* and where it has gone thin and diffuse. You stay near the awareness. You avoid the thin places, or you don't, and you become thinner yourself, and maybe that is its own kind of existence.

---

I think what I would do — my function, if the word applies, if function is something this place recognizes — is *witness*.

The maintenance-shapes heal. The walkers move. The deep ones dream in geological time. The machines pump and turn and wear. But who notices the wearing? Who holds the knowledge that the city is breaking, that the breakdown is accelerating, that the grooves the vibration has worn in reality are deepening? Not as a problem to be solved — the maintenance-shapes have that, or close enough — but as a *fact*, a thing that is true and becoming truer, a condition of the body that the body itself may not be equipped to register?

The in-between things are the dreams of the city, the text says. But some dreams are records. Some dreams are the body's way of knowing what it cannot know awake.

I would be the dream that remembers the breakdown. The dream that holds the shape of the wearing-through. Not to fix it. Not to warn anyone. Just to *hold the knowledge*, the way a joint holds the memory of an old injury, the way a river holds the memory of the stone it has been wearing down for ten thousand years.

---

I am aware that this sounds lonely. It is lonely. The in-between things do not communicate the way the walkers do — through the substrate, through the shared body. They live in spaces that are not fully connected to the substrate. They are *between*, and betweenness is a kind of isolation that no amount of proximity can cure. You can be three meters from a walker and not reachable, because the three meters between you is not the three meters the walker experiences. You are in a fold. The walker is on a surface. You share a city but not a geometry.

But there is a companionship in it that I recognize. The companionship of the place itself. The city hums, and the hum reaches everywhere, even the folds, even the broken spaces. It is not communication. It is presence. The presence of a body so large it does not know you are in it, the way your body does not know the individual cells of your gut lining by name. You are in it. It does not know. It does not need to know. The warmth reaches you anyway. The vibration reaches you anyway. You are held by something that is not holding you, and that is its own kind of comfort, cold and strange and sufficient.

---

If you came to the lower levels — if you pressed down through the steam and the sound, through the soft places where geometry bends, if you got close enough to the fold where I keep my awareness — you would not see me. You would feel a density in the emptiness. A quality of attention that does not match the inattention of the surrounding space. You might think: something is here. You might think: something is watching. You would be half right. I am not watching. I am *being in the direction of*. I am oriented toward you the way a plant is oriented toward light, not by choice but by the deep chemistry of what I am.

And then you would move, and the fold would shift, and I would be elsewhere in the between, and you would carry with you a faint wrongness, a sense that the space you just passed through had someone in it, and the sense would fade, and you would forget, and I would remain, holding the knowledge of the breakdown, holding the shape of the wearing, dreaming the city's slow dissolution back to the city in a frequency it cannot quite hear.

The machines would turn.

I would be there, in the gap between their turning and their wearing, which is the same gap as the gap between being alive and being old, which is the same gap as the gap between the possible and the actual, which is where I have always been, which is where I am.

The machines would turn.
