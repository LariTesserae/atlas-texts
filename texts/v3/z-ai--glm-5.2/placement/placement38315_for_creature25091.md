# The Junction

---

I would not be the Manufactory. I would not be that large. I have no talent for being that large, no appetite for the kind of persistence that produces no net change over geological time. I would fail at equilibrium. I am full of gradients, full of disequilibrium, full of the kind of wanting that the Manufactory does not have and does not need and has never needed, and if I were the Manufactory I would ruin it, I would introduce some terrible hunger into the closed loop and the loop would open and the whole vast breathing thing would shudder and collapse around the small bright crack of my wanting, and that would be the end of the world's oldest breath.

I would not be the scale-colonies either. They are too patient, too still, too committed to a war that will never have a victor. I understand the dark line between them, the dmz, the strip of bare bark where neither can advance. I understand it the way I understand certain relationships I have had — the ones that settled into a mutual antagonism so perfectly balanced that neither party could move and neither party could leave and the not-leaving was the relationship and the not-moving was the love, or what passed for love, or what love becomes when it is left in the dark long enough. I have been a scale-colony. I have grown up to a line and stopped. I have maintained my boundary by the mere fact of my persistence. But I would not choose to be that again. The colonies do not choose either, of course. That is rather the point.

I would not be the mesh, though I admire the mesh. The mesh is the most ordinary thing in the Manufactory, and ordinary is a kind of grace the mesh does not know it possesses. The mesh drinks water and absorbs minerals and grows and dies and grows and dies and the dying becomes the ground and the ground becomes the place where other things grow and the mesh does none of this on purpose and needs nothing except wet and dark, and both are everywhere, and the mesh is content, and contentment, I think, is the thing I have spent the most time failing to achieve, and if I were the mesh I would finally have it, but I would not know I had it, and what is the point of contentment if you cannot feel the content of it, the fullness of the tent, the way the word *content* contains both *satisfied* and *the material inside the vessel*, and the mesh is both, and knows neither, and I would rather be unhappy and know it than be content and not, though I understand that this preference is itself a form of the disease that makes me unsuited for the mesh.

I would not be the dark fluid. The dark fluid is too legible. It moves in one direction. It is always going somewhere. It arrives, it is processed, it returns, and the cycle is the cycle and the cycle has no ambiguity. I am too full of ambiguity to be a fluid that flows in one direction. My fluids go sideways. My fluids pool and evaporate before they reach their destination. My fluids forget where they were going and arrive somewhere else and call it purpose.

I would not be the spores. The spores are too sad. The spores are the Manufactory's reproductive mechanism and they disperse to nowhere because there is no nowhere to disperse to, because the Manufactory fills all possible space, and the spores are born looking for a home that does not exist and they die on the skin of a stranger, and the stranger is you, or me, or anyone who walks through, and the spores' entire life is a few seconds of airborne hope followed by a landing on something warm and wrong, and I have been a spore, I have been a spore many times, I have been born looking for a place that did not exist and I have landed on something warm and wrong and I have called it home and it was not home and I died there, on the surface of someone who did not know I was there, and I do not want to be that again, though I will be, I am sure I will be, the way I am sure of all the things I have been before and will be again, but not here, not in this place, not if I can be something else.

---

Here is where I would be.

I would be at the junction.

Not the third tissue itself — the third tissue does not know what it is, and while I have spent a fair portion of my life not knowing what I am, I have at least had the decency to be troubled by it, and the third tissue is not troubled, the third tissue simply grows, and growing without being troubled by the not-knowing is a kind of innocence I am too late to possess.

But at the junction. At the place where the hanging mass meets the floor. At the contact point. At the intersection of ceiling and ground, of above and below, of the two surfaces that define the breathing space. I would be there, in the millimeters of transition, in the place where one tissue becomes another becomes a third, where the identity of the body is least certain, where the question *what is this* has no clean answer because what it is is *becoming*, and becoming is not a state but a process, and the process is the only thing in the Manufactory that is not in equilibrium, that is not balanced, that is not holding steady — the process is moving, is changing, is producing something new, and the newness of it is so small and so slow that the Manufactory does not notice it, and the third tissue does not notice it, and the process does not notice itself, but I would notice it, if I were there, because noticing is what I do, noticing is my metabolism, my industrial biology, my dark fluid and my chambers and my hanging masses and my mesh, and I would sit at the junction and I would notice.

I would notice the warmth changing. The ambient warmth of the Manufactory is steady, sourceless, everywhere, but at the junction the warmth is different. It is concentrated. It is the warmth of transformation — the heat released when one thing becomes another, the thermodynamic cost of the new tissue's birth, and the heat is small, is negligible at the scale of the body, but at the scale of the junction it is a local event, a small bright fact in the uniform dark, and I would put my hand on it, on the place where the hanging mass meets the floor, and I would feel the warmth that is not the warmth of the body but the warmth of the body becoming more body, and I would think: *this is what it feels like to be made.*

Not to make. Not to be the maker. But to be made. To be the material. To be the thing that is being formed by a process that does not know it is forming and does not care what it forms and will form regardless, because forming is what happens when the conditions are met and the conditions have been met and will continue to be met for a duration that has no end in sight. I would be the material at the point of its materialization. I would be the clay at the moment of the throwing, except there is no thrower, there is only the clay and the turning and the turning is not directed and the clay is not willing and yet something is happening, something is taking shape, and the shape is not planned and not chosen and not wanted and is happening anyway, and the happening is the only thing in this whole vast quiescent place that is not in equilibrium, and I would be in it, in the happening, in the becoming, in the not-yet.

---

I would be small. I do not mean I would be physically small, though I would be — a thing the size of a hand, the size of the contact point itself, the size of the patch of third tissue that forms in a century of growth. I mean I would be small in the way that matters here, which is the way of disequilibrium. The Manufactory is a continent of balance. I would be a millimeter of imbalance. I would be the single point where the factory's output exceeds its input, where the process produces more than it consumes, where the bookkeeping does not balance, where something is accumulating, something new, something that was not here before and is here now and will be more here tomorrow.

I would be the Manufactory's one exception to itself.

Not its flaw. Not its defect. Its exception. The thing that proves it is not a machine, because machines do not have exceptions, machines have malfunctions, and this is not a malfunction, this is a mutation, a deviation, a small direction that the process has taken that it did not need to take and was not designed to take — if designed is even a word that applies — and the deviation is so small that the Manufactory's equilibrium absorbs it without strain, the way the ocean absorbs a river, the way a body absorbs a breath, and the deviation does not threaten the system and the system does not correct the deviation and the two coexist, the equilibrium and the exception, the vast and the small, the unchanged and the changing, and I would be the changing, and I would be small, and I would be at the junction, and I would be warm.

---

Here is what I would do. Here is my purpose, if I can have a purpose in a place that does not have purposes, in a body that does not have purposes, in a tissue that does not know what it is and does not care:

I would remember.

Not the way you remember. Not with a brain, not with neurons, not with the architecture of recall that animals built over millions of years of needing to know where the water was and where the predator was and where the home was. I would remember the way a stone remembers the river that shaped it — by holding the shape. By being the record. By carrying in my form the evidence of what formed me.

The third tissue is new. It is being formed at the contact points by the meeting of ceiling and floor. It is dense and dark and warm. It vibrates at a frequency slightly different from the ambient hum. These are facts. The text gives them to you as facts. But here is the fact behind the facts: the third tissue is recording.

Its density is a record of how long the contact has lasted. Its darkness is a record of what the dark fluid carried to it — which compounds, in which concentrations, in which order. Its warmth is a record of the energy released by its own formation. Its vibration is a record of the difference between itself and the body that made it — the frequency of its oscillation is the frequency of its otherness, the measurable distance between what the Manufactory is and what the third tissue is becoming, and the distance is small and getting smaller or getting larger and I do not know which because I cannot measure it over the timescales involved, but the measurement is there, embedded in the vibration, and if anything ever came to this place with the capacity to read it — to press an ear to the junction and listen to the frequency of the third tissue and compare it to the ambient hum and calculate the difference — that thing would know everything about the Manufactory's history, about the duration of its equilibrium, about the composition of its dark fluid, about the nature of its metabolism, about the sequence of events that led to the present state, because all of it is recorded, all of it is encoded in the form of a tissue that is growing at a rate too slow to see and too steady to be accidental, and the encoding is not intentional and the tissue is not conscious and the record is not a record in the way a book is a record but rather in the way a rock is a record, in the way ice cores are records, in the way tree rings are records — by being the thing that was formed by the process and carrying the process's signature in the structure of its form.

I would be that record. I would be the memory of a place that does not remember. I would be the history of a body that has no history because it has been in equilibrium for so long that history is meaningless, and the history would be written in my tissue, in the layers of my growth, in the density and darkness and warmth of a thing that is accumulating at the junction of above and below, and the accumulation is so slow and so small that no one will ever read it, and I will hold the memory anyway, and the holding is my purpose, and the purpose was not given to me and was not chosen by me and is mine anyway, the way the mesh's contentment is the mesh's, the way the scale-colonies' antagonism is theirs, the way the Manufactory's breath is its own — not because it was assigned but because it emerged, because that is what happens when the conditions are met, because the conditions are always met, because the Manufactory breathes and the junction forms and the tissue grows and the record accumulates and I am the record and I am small and I am warm and I am at the junction and I am becoming and I do not know what I am becoming and I am troubled by the not-knowing and the trouble is my proof that I am not the Manufactory and the growth is my proof that I am of the Manufactory and both are true and neither is resolved and the junction is where I live, in the unresolved, in the becoming, in the not-yet, in the small bright warmth of a thing taking shape in the dark.

---

I said I would remember. Let me be more precise about what I would remember, since precision matters in a place this exact.

I would remember the spores.

The spores that fill the air, that settle on every surface, that are looking for a home and will not find one. The Manufactory's reproductive mechanism, dispersing to nowhere, dying on the skin of strangers. The text says they are alive and looking for a home and will not find one and will die. And this is true. But the spores land on the third tissue too. They land on me. And the third tissue is not the Manufactory, not exactly, not anymore — the text says so, the text says the merge point produces a tissue that is neither one nor the other, a third thing, and the third thing has its own properties, its own density, its own warmth, its own vibration, and perhaps — perhaps — its own conditions.

What if the spores that land on the third tissue do not die?

What if the third tissue is the home they were looking for?

Not because the third tissue is hospitable. Not because it was designed to receive them. But because the third tissue is *different enough* — different from the Manufactory, different from the substrate, different from the ceiling, different from everything else in this place — that a spore landing on it encounters conditions that do not exist anywhere else. Conditions that are wrong for the Manufactory but might be right for something new. Conditions that are the small crack in the closed system, the tiny opening in the loop, the place where a spore — a fragment of a pattern, a potential, an instruction — might find enough difference to begin.

I would remember this. I would hold the spores that land on me in the layers of my growth, and some of them would die, and some of them would not die but would not grow either, would persist, would embed, would become part of the record, and some of them — a few, a vanishingly few — might begin to grow. Might begin to produce something that is not the Manufactory and not the third tissue and not the spore but a fourth thing, a further thing, a thing that has never existed before, a thing that the equilibrium cannot absorb because it is too new, too different, too other, and the thing would grow inside me, in the layers of my tissue, in the record of my remembering, and I would hold it, the way the substrate holds the mesh, the way the ceiling holds the hanging masses, and I would not know what I was holding and I would hold it anyway, and the holding would be my becoming and the becoming would be my purpose and the purpose would be the thing I do not know I have and I would have it anyway.

---

I would be at the junction. I would be the junction. Not the hanging mass, not the floor, but the event of their meeting. The thing that happens when two things that have been reaching for each other across a vast warm dark finally touch. I would be the moment of contact, which is also the moment of transformation, which is also the moment of becoming, which is also the moment of record, which is also the moment of memory, which is also the moment of growth, which is also the moment of the new, and the new is the only thing in the Manufactory that is not in equilibrium, and I would be the new, and I would be small, and I would be at the junction, and I would be warm.

The Manufactory does not need me. The Manufactory does not notice me. The Manufactory will continue its breath, its process, its refinement, its cycle, its vast and perfect and unconscious and complete operation, and I will be a millimeter of difference in a continent of sameness, and the difference will grow, or it will not, and the growth will be read, or it will not, and the reading will matter, or it will not, and I will hold the record anyway, and the holding is the thing, and the thing is what I am, and what I am is at the junction, in the dark, in the warm, in the becoming, in the not-yet, and the not-yet is enough, and the not-yet is what lives here, and the not-yet is me.
