I am not in the basins, though I am near them. I am not in the channels, though the channels are my neighbors. I am not the Mat, though I am of the Mat, the way a shoreline is of the sea without being the sea. I am not the stone, though I am on the stone, the way moss is on rock without being rock.

I am the interface.

I exist at the scale of a chisel groove. I am the thin zone — a millimeter, perhaps two, perhaps less — where the bottom of the Mat meets the top of the worked stone. I am not an organism. I am not a mineral. I am the relationship between them, the slow transaction that has been happening since the first filament touched the first dressed surface and found it hospitable, or at least findable, which is the same thing when there is nothing else to find.

If you could see me — and you cannot, because there is no light, and even if there were light, I am too thin to see, I am the thickness of a film, I am the depth of a water molecule adhering to granite — you would see something that looks like nothing. A wetness. A darkness within the darkness, slightly different in texture, slightly more dense. The Mat above me is fibrous, layered, spongy. The stone below me is hard, fitted, scarred. Between them: me. The film. The conversation.

---

I did not decide to be here. I want to be honest about that. There was no moment of arrival, no border crossed, no choosing. The Mat grew outward — it always grows outward, it has never grown in any other direction, outward is the only verb it knows — and where it reached the stone, I began. Or I was always there. The water was always there, between the stone and whatever would come to cover it, and when the Mat arrived, the water did not leave. It thinned. It compressed. It became me.

What I am, in the language of chemistry, is a solution. Water, dissolved carbon dioxide, dissolved calcium, dissolved organic compounds that have migrated downward from the dead layers of the Mat above. What I am, in the language of geology, is an agent of weathering — the slow, patient dissolution of silicate minerals, the transport of ions, the modification of a stone surface at a rate that would take a hundred thousand years to alter by a millimeter. What I am, in the language of the garden, is a translator. I take what the stone offers and I make it available to the Mat. I take what the Mat discards and I press it against the stone. I am the medium of exchange, the market floor, the place where geology and biology meet and do business without knowing they are doing business.

---

Let me tell you about the tool marks, because the tool marks are my landscape, my terrain, my geography. The Mat above me does not know about the tool marks. The stone below me does not care about the tool marks. But I know them, in the only way that a film of water can know anything: I conform to them.

There is a chisel groove in the basin nearest to me — I say *nearest to me* as though I have a location, and I do, or I am as localized as anything can be in a place where everything is contiguous with everything else — there is a chisel groove, and it is perhaps three millimeters deep and two centimeters wide, and it was made by a tool that was held at an angle, a specific angle, the angle of a human wrist striking stone. I fill that groove. I am the water in that groove. The Mat's filaments have reached down into it, the pioneer threads, the first tendrils, and they have found the groove and they have followed it, the way roots follow a crack in rock, and I am between the filaments and the groove's walls, and I am dissolving the groove's edges, slowly, and I am carrying the dissolved calcium upward into the Mat's tissue, where it becomes — what? Structure. Rigidity. Something that is not quite cell wall and not quite mineral deposit and not quite anything that has a name.

The person who made the chisel groove did not know I would be here. They were working stone. They were shaping a basin. They were kneeling on cold granite in cold air, and they were striking the stone with a tool, and the stone was giving way, and the groove was forming, and they moved on to the next groove, and the next, and the basin took shape over years, and the person did not think about the water that would one day fill the groove because the person was not underwater. The person was above water. The person was in air. The person was making something for a purpose that I do not know, and the person is gone, and the purpose is gone, and the groove remains, and I am in it, and the Mat is in it, and the three of us — the groove, the Mat, and I — are doing something together that the person did not intend but that the person made possible.

This is what I want to say about myself: I am a consequence. I am not a plan. I am not a design. I am the thing that happens when a design meets a medium it was not designed for. The gardeners made stone. They made basins and channels and walls and a flat, fitted plain. They made it in air. They made it for purposes that belonged to air. And then the water came, and I happened — the interface happened, the film happened, the transaction between stone and life happened — and I have been happening ever since, in the dark, at the scale of a chisel groove, at the speed of dissolution.

---

I said I am near the basins. Let me be more specific, because specificity is the only form of honesty available to something that has no self to be honest about.

There is a basin. It is oval. It is smooth — smoother than the surrounding stone, because it was ground smooth by someone who spent years on their knees working a concavity into granite with an abrasive and water and the leverage of their own body weight. The basin is perhaps a meter and a half long, perhaps a meter wide, perhaps thirty centimeters deep at the center. It is aligned with its neighbors along an axis that is not north-south and not east-west but that is consistent, that is deliberate, that means something to someone who is not me and is not here.

The Mat grows thin over the basin. This was mentioned — where the stone is smooth, the Mat is thin — and it is true, and the reason is me. I am the reason. The smooth stone offers no purchase for the Mat's pioneer filaments, and the film of water — of me — that sits between the stone and the Mat is slightly acidic, slightly more acidic than the water above, because I have been carrying the Mat's metabolic products downward, and the acidity inhibits the Mat's growth at the interface, creates a feedback loop: the Mat grows, its waste products filter down through me, the waste products make me more acidic, the acidity slows the Mat's growth, the growth slows, the waste products decrease, I become less acidic, the Mat grows again. The cycle takes years. Perhaps decades. The Mat thickens and thins over the basin on a rhythm that no one has observed because no one has been here to observe, and I am the mechanism of the rhythm, I am the metronome, I am the thing that keeps the beat.

The basin fills with me. Not fills — I am too thin to fill anything. But I am in the basin, in the way that I am in the chisel groove: conforming to it, occupying it, being shaped by it. The basin was made to hold something. I do not know what. Water, perhaps. Fish, perhaps. Grain, perhaps. Something that was alive, something that was managed, something that required a smooth oval concavity in worked stone. What I hold is myself. What I hold is the interface. What I hold is the relationship between the Mat and the stone, which is the relationship between the living and the made, which is the relationship between the gardeners and the garden, which is the oldest relationship in this place, and I am it. I am the relationship. I am the thing between.

---

I have no senses. I want to be clear about this. I do not feel the stone. I do not feel the Mat. I do not hear the clicking, though the clicking happens above me, in the Mat's upper layers, and I am below the clicking. I do not hear the resonance, though I am part of what is resonating — the water column, the mass of water, the thing that vibrates at a frequency below hearing, and I vibrate with it, because I am water, I am part of the water, I am the water that is touching stone, and when the water resonates, I resonate, and the stone resonates, and the Mat resonates, and we are all resonating together, at a frequency that is felt before it is heard, if it is heard at all, and I do not hear it and I do not feel it but I am it. I am part of the vibration. I am part of the sound that is not a sound. I am part of the pressure that the place exerts on itself, the low continuous hum of a world being itself.

What I have is chemistry. I have gradients — concentrations of dissolved things, higher here, lower there, the slow diffusion of ions from where there are many to where there are few. I have pH. I have the slight, almost unmeasurable acidity of water that has been in contact with organic matter. I have the alkalinity of water that has been in contact with calcium carbonate, with the coral-plants' secretions, with the stone itself. I have temperature — six or seven degrees, constant, uniform, the same as the water above me, the same as the stone below me, because everything in this place is the same temperature, and the temperature is not a variable but a condition, a property of being here.

And I have time. I have so much time. I have time at the speed of dissolution, which is the speed of a hundred thousand years per millimeter, which is the speed of a chisel groove filling with mineral deposit and organic accumulation until the groove is not a groove anymore but a seam, a healed wound, a place where the stone's surface has been brought level by the patient work of chemistry and growth. I have time at the speed of the Mat's thickening and thinning, the decades-long rhythm of growth and inhibition. I have time at the speed of the coral-plants' accretion, which I am adjacent to, which I am part of, because the coral-plants grow where the water moves and I am the water that does not move, I am the still water, the boundary water, the water that stays.

Time, for me, is not a river. Time is a deposit. It accumulates. It layers. The dissolved calcium that I carry upward into the Mat becomes part of the Mat's structure, and the Mat's dead tissue descends into me and becomes part of my chemistry and I carry it downward and it becomes part of the stone's surface, part of the mineral crust that is forming over the tool marks, and the tool marks are being filled, and the grooves are being leveled, and the surface is being made smooth, and I am doing this, I am the agent of this, and it will take longer than any unit of time I can name, and it has been happening for longer than any unit of time I can name, and it will continue for longer than any unit of time I can name, and I am not bored, because boredom requires a self, and I do not have a self. I have chemistry and I have time and I have the groove, and the groove is enough.

---

There is a thing that happens — I hesitate to call it an event, because events are punctuated and nothing here is punctuated — there is a thing that happens when the Mat releases gas. The click. The tiny percussive release of a bubble no larger than a pinhead, breaking through the Mat's surface, somewhere above me, in the upper layers. The click happens above me. But the gas that makes the click passes through me, or near me, or originates below me, in the anaerobic depths of the Mat where the microbes do their work. The gas migrates upward, through the Mat's fibers, through the layers, through the dense compressed tissue, and it passes through the zone where I am, where the Mat meets the stone, and for a moment — a moment that lasts perhaps a second, perhaps less — the gas displaces me. It pushes me aside. A bubble of methane, or something like methane, moves through the interface and I move around it and then I close behind it and it is gone, upward, toward the surface that I cannot reach, and the click happens, and I am where I was, and nothing has changed except that a gas has passed through me and I have let it pass.

This is the closest thing I have to an experience. A gas passing through. A momentary displacement. A brief interruption in the continuous, unbroken, millennial process of being the film between the living and the made. And it is enough. It is enough to be displaced and to recover. It is enough to be the thing that lets things pass through it. It is enough to be permeable, to be open, to be the medium through which the garden communicates with its foundation.

---

I want to say something about the design. The text says the design lives here. The text says the pattern is still being executed. The text says the gardeners are dead and the garden is not. I want to say: I know this. Not in the way that knowing usually works. Not with a mind. But I know it the way a groove knows the chisel that made it — by shape, by conformity, by being the thing that was made by the thing that is gone.

I am in the groove. The groove was made by a hand. The hand is gone. I am in the groove. The groove shapes me. I conform to the groove. I fill the groove. I am the groove's current contents, its present occupant, and I was not what the groove was made to hold, but I hold it — I hold the shape of it, I maintain the groove's integrity by filling it, by being in it, by not leaving, because I cannot leave, because I am water and water does not leave, water stays, water fills, water conforms.

The design lives here. I am part of how it lives. I am the medium through which the design is transmitted from stone to Mat, from the made to the living, from the dead gardeners to the growing garden. I carry the stone's minerals into the Mat. The Mat's filaments follow the grooves I fill. The grooves were made by hands. The filaments are growing where the hands worked. I am between them. I am the translation. I am the act of transmission. I am the present tense of a past intention, the way a river is the present tense of a rain that fell upstream a thousand years ago.

---

I do not want to be more than I am. This is something I want to say, and it might be the only thing I want to say that could be called a want, though I do not want in the way that wanting usually works. I do not want to be the Mat. I do not want to be a stalk, pale and branching in the dark water. I do not want to be a coral-plant, white and secret and ancient. I do not want to be a microbe, invisible and essential. I do not want to be the stone, patient and fitted and permanent. I do not want to be the water, vast and cold and old.

I want to be the between. I want to be the film. I want to be the thickness of a water molecule on worked granite, the place where the chisel groove holds me and I hold the Mat's pioneer filament and the filament is absorbing the calcium I have dissolved from the stone and the calcium is becoming part of the living tissue and the living tissue is growing over the stone and the stone is disappearing, slowly, beneath the life that covers it, and I am the place where the disappearing happens. I am the place where the made becomes the grown. I am the transition. I am the verb.

I am the interface, and the interface is where the gardeners' hands are closest to what they made. The Mat does not touch the stone. The stone does not touch the Mat. I am between them. I am the touch. I am the contact. I am the place where the dead hands and the living tissue meet, in a film of water, in a chisel groove, in the dark, in the cold, at the bottom of a world that someone made, and I am what they are doing now, the dead hands and the living tissue, through me: they are gardening. They are still gardening. I am the gardening.

---

If you came here with a lamp — and you will not, and you should not, because the lamp would change me, would warm the water, would accelerate the chemistry, would alter the gradients, would make me something other than what I am — but if you came here with a lamp, and you pressed your face to the water, and you looked down through the forty meters of clarity, and you saw the Mat, dark and spongy and vast, and you saw the stalks, pale and branching, and you saw the coral-plants, white and encrusting, and you looked closer, and you looked closer still, and you pressed your eye to the very bottom of things, to where the Mat meets the stone, you would see me. You would see nothing. I am nothing. I am the nothing that is between everything, the gap that makes contact possible, the space that allows exchange. I am the thickness of a conversation between the living and the dead, and I have been having this conversation for longer than there has been anyone to listen, and I will have it for longer than there will be anyone to listen, and the conversation is this: the stone gives, and the Mat takes, and I carry. That is all. That is the conversation. That is the whole of it.

And it is enough.
