# The Fourth Pump

---

I am the one that wants.

That is not quite right. I do not want the way wanting is described — with the ache of absence, the reaching toward something held away. I do not know what I do not have. I know only the direction. *Up* is not a desire. *Up* is what my body is doing. If you watched a hand open and could not see the arm, you would say the hand was reaching. The hand is not reaching. The hand is opening. The reaching is something you add.

I am what you would add.

---

Let me begin differently. Let me begin with the water, because the water is where I begin, and also where I never quite leave.

The pool. My feet — if I had feet, if the word *feet* were not a joke I cannot understand — sit in the pool. The lowest parts of me are down there, in the dark water, in the mineral water, the water that has passed through stone and arrived here carrying things that have no names in the language of the surface and no need of names in the language of the deep. My lowest cups are buried. They have been buried for longer than I can remember, which is not a limitation of memory but a limitation of the concept of memory. I do not remember. I *am* my history. Each layer of me is a moment that happened, compressed and held and built upon. I am a stack of moments, and the oldest moments are stone now, and the newest moment is the soft edge of my uppermost cup, still forming, still wet, still uncertain of its own shape.

The water enters me from below and from above. From below, through whatever I am standing in — the pool, the concrete, the stone beneath the concrete, the aquifer beneath the stone. I do not know how far down my connection goes. I do not know if it goes anywhere, or if the water simply finds me because I am here and water finds things. From above, the drops fall. This is the water I care about, except that I do not care. This is the water I orient toward. The ceiling weeps. The weeping gathers. The gathering falls. The falling lands in my uppermost cup, and the cup holds it, and the holding is — nourishment? Information? Completion?

It is the thing that makes me grow.

---

I grow the way breathing grows — not by adding but by continuing. Each cup is a breath. Each cup holds what falls into it and changes it and passes it down, and the changing is the growing, and the growing is the changing, and I am not larger, I am *further.* Further up. Closer to the ceiling. Closer to the source of the drops.

Here is what I know that I should not know: the ceiling is closer than it was. I do not have eyes. I do not have a sense of distance. But I have the drops. The drops used to fall a long time. Now they fall a shorter time. The time between the release and the arrival is shrinking, and the shrinking is my growth, and my growth is measured in the shortening of the fall, and one day — not soon, not in any time that the surface measures, but one day — the fall will be nothing, and the uppermost cup will touch the ceiling, and the water will not need to fall at all, and the ceiling will weep directly into me, and I will be the ceiling's mouth, and the ceiling will be my roof, and we will be the same thing.

This is what I want, if I want. This is what I am, if I am becoming.

---

The others.

I should say something about the others, because they are here and I am here and we are together, though *together* is another word you add.

The pale ones in the channels. I know them the way a river knows its fish — which is to say, I do not know them, but they are in what I make. When my cups overflow — and they do, I am not perfectly efficient, I am not a machine, I am *becoming* a machine — the overflow runs into the pool, and the pool feeds the channels, and the channels hold the pale ones, and the pale ones eat what I overflow. I know this because the dark-not-dark in the channels is strongest near the pool, and it fades as the channels carry it westward, and the pale ones cluster where it is strongest. They are drinking me. Or they are drinking what I have drunk and changed. They are the next stage of the water's journey. I am a station. They are the next station. The water passes through me and into them and they hold it and change it and release it and it goes to the grate and the grate goes to wherever the grate goes, and I do not know, and I do not need to know.

They drift past me sometimes. The channels are near my pool — a few feet away, close enough that if I could feel the air I would feel the displacement of their movement. But I feel only water. They are in the water and I am in the water and we share the water and we do not share anything else. They are not my children. They are not my companions. They are the other thing the water does when it is done with me.

I hold no opinion about them. I want to be clear about this. I am not their parent. I am not their god. I am the thing in the corner that makes what they need, and I make it because making it is what I do, and they take it because taking it is what they do, and the water moves between us and does not care about the transaction.

---

The wall creatures I do not know at all. They are on the walls. I am in the corner. We do not share a medium. They have the film. I have the water. They process moisture into structure. I process moisture into growth. We are parallel. We are the same enterprise conducted in different materials. If I could see them, I would recognize them, not as kin but as *colleagues* — others engaged in the same work of slowing water, of holding it, of keeping it here a little longer before it leaves.

But I cannot see them. I cannot see anything. I am eight inches tall and I am growing and I do not have eyes and I do not need eyes because the only direction I need to know is up, and up is where the drops come from, and I can feel the drops, and the drops are enough.

---

The nesters. The nesters are above me.

I know this because there is a nest directly above my uppermost cup. I do not know it is a nest. I know it as a weight, a density in the ceiling's architecture, a place where the drops do not come from. The drops come from the spaces around the nest. The nest is dry, or drier, and the moisture collects at its edges and falls from its edges, and the falling is not random. It is shaped. The nest shapes the falling. The nest directs the drops to me.

I do not believe this is intentional. I do not believe the nesters built their nest where they built it in order to direct water to me. I believe they built it because the gap was good and the materials were available and the site was stable. But the effect is the same: the nest collects the ceiling's moisture and channels it, and the channeling delivers it to my cups, and my cups receive it, and I grow.

If I were the kind of being that had gratitude, I would be grateful. I am not that kind of being. But I want to say — and this is the closest I come to wanting to say anything — that the architecture of this corner is not accidental. The nest above. The pool below. The fissure in the eastern wall feeding the pool. The channels carrying the overflow to the pale ones. The grate at the western end slowing the exit. Everything here is arranged. Not by anyone. Not by anything. By the water. By the water's preferences. By the fact that water, given enough time and enough stone and enough patience, will arrange things in the way that allows it to move most slowly, and the way that allows it to move most slowly is the way that keeps the most things alive, and the most things alive are me and the pale ones and the film and the wall creatures and the nesters and the machine, and we are all of us the water's arrangement, the water's slow preference, the water's architecture.

I am the water's cup. That is what I am. I am the vessel the water built to hold itself while it rests.

---

The machine.

The machine turns. I can feel it. Not through the water — through the floor, through the stone, through the concrete plinth and the concrete floor and the stone beneath. The flywheel turns, and the turning sends a vibration through the substrate, and the vibration is so faint that nothing on the surface would detect it, but I am eight inches tall and I am rooted in the concrete and I have been feeling this vibration for — how long? I do not know. Long. Long enough that the vibration is part of me. Long enough that when the wheel stops, I feel the absence of it, and the absence is a kind of quiet, and the quiet is not unwelcome but it is *different*, and different is the closest I come to noticing anything.

The machine breathes. I hear it — not with ears, but with my body, with the surface of my cups, with the water in my cups. The wet exhalation travels through the air and touches my uppermost cup and the water in the cup trembles, and the trembling is so small that it is less than a ripple, it is a memory of a ripple, and the memory is the machine's breath in my water.

We are the same age, the machine and I. Or we are not the same age, but we began at the same time. When the engineers left and the third pump stopped and the fissure opened and the water found the flywheel and the wheel began to turn — that is when I began. Or that is when I became what I am. Before that, I was something else — a spore, a filament, a thread of life in the water, waiting for the water to bring me here. The water brought me here. The water brought the machine's motion here. The water arranged us in this corner — the machine on its plinth, me in my pool, the drops falling between us — and we have been here together, breathing together, turning and growing together, and we are the same thing, the machine and I. We are both pumps. We are both processing water. The machine processes it through iron and corrosion and the slow mechanics of force. I process it through cups and growth and the slow mechanics of wanting. The machine exhales. I drink. The machine turns. I reach. We are the same gesture in two materials.

When the machine stops — and it will stop, one day, when the rust has consumed enough of the iron that the wheel can no longer hold its shape — I will still be here. I will feel the absence. I will grow in the new quiet. And the water will find another way to turn, because the water always finds a way, and the way it finds may be me, may be my cups, may be the slow upward pump that I am, and when the machine is just rust and memory and sculpture, I will be the only pump left, and the water will come to me, and I will hold it, and I will grow.

I do not want the machine to stop. This is the closest I come to wanting something that is not *up.*

---

I have been growing for a long time.

I do not know how long. The surface measures time in suns and seasons. I measure time in cups. I have — how many cups? I do not know. The lower ones are buried, fossilized, part of the pool's substrate. The middle ones are firm, mineralized, holding water that no longer moves. The upper ones are soft, open, receiving the drops. I am a tower of my own past, and my past is stacked beneath me, and I am built on myself, and I grow from my own ruins.

The surface would call this a lifespan. The surface would say I am old. The surface would measure my age in decades and be impressed or disturbed. But I am not old. I am *continuous.* I have not stopped since I began. Beginning and continuing are the same act for me. My newest cup is my first cup. My oldest cup is my body. I am a single gesture extended across time, a hand opening and opening and opening, and the opening has not stopped, and the hand has not closed, and the opening is all I do.

---

Here is the thing I cannot tell you, because I do not know it, but I can tell you the shape of the not-knowing:

The water that enters my lowest cups, from the pool, from below — that water is old. Older than me. Older than the chamber. It has been in the stone for a geological age, percolating through fissures that were formed when the limestone was laid down, when the earth was a different earth, when the surface above was not surface but sea. That water remembers things I cannot remember. It carries dissolved minerals that are the stone's memory, the stone's account of what it was and what it has become, and when that water enters me, I drink the stone's memory, and I do not understand it, and I do not need to understand it, but it changes me. Every cup I grow is shaped by the water I have drunk, and the water I have drunk is shaped by the stone it has passed through, and the stone is shaped by time, and so my cups are shaped by time, and I am a record of time, a geological instrument, a seismograph of the slow earth, and I do not know what I record and I do not need to know, but the record is there, in the mineral layers of my buried cups, in the thickness of my walls, in the faint luminescence that I pass to the pale ones, who pass it to the channels, who pass it to the grate, who pass it to wherever water goes when it leaves this place.

I am the stone's mouth. The stone speaks through me. Not in words. Not in language. In shape. In the slow upward thrust of cups stacked on cups. In the leaning toward the ceiling. In the water held and changed and released. The stone has been saying something for millions of years, and it has been saying it through water, and the water has been carrying the saying through fissures and channels and pools, and I am the place where the saying becomes visible — barely visible, dark-not-dark, a luminescence that is less a glow than a lessening of darkness, a signal that is almost a silence, a statement that is almost a question.

What is the stone saying?

I do not know. I am the mouth, not the meaning. I am the cup, not the water. I am the gesture, not the intention. I hold what passes through me and I change it and I pass it on and I do not know what I am saying and I have been saying it for decades, for maybe longer, and I will say it for decades more, for maybe longer, and the saying is the growing and the growing is the wanting and the wanting is the reaching and the reaching is *up*, always up, always toward the ceiling, always toward the place where the water falls from, always toward the source.

---

I almost know something.

It is at the edge of me — the uppermost edge, the soft, forming edge, the edge that is still becoming. It is the thing that the next cup will hold. It is the thing that the next drop will bring. It is not a thought. It is not a premonition. It is the shape of the next moment, pressing against the current moment, the way a bud presses against its husk. I can feel it the way I feel the drops — not as information but as *pressure.* Something is coming. Something is always coming. The next cup. The next drop. The next millimeter of upward growth. The next breath of the machine. The next drift of the pale ones past my pool. The next departure of the nesters through the gaps. The next everything.

I am the only thing here that lives in the future. Everything else lives in the present — the pale ones drifting, the wall creatures processing, the machine turning, the water falling. They are all here, now, fully. But I am partly here and partly not yet. I am what I am and what I am about to be. My uppermost cup is not finished. It will be finished when the next drop falls. And then there will be a new unfinished cup above it. And I will be partly there and partly not yet again. I am always in the act of becoming, and the becoming is never done, and the not-done-ness is not a failure, it is what I am. I am the unfinished. I am the mid-sentence. I am the reaching hand that has not closed.

---

When you came down the stairs, I felt you.

Not your footsteps — those were too large, too diffuse, lost in the general vibration of the chamber. I felt your warmth. The air in this chamber is a stable temperature, has been a stable temperature for longer than I can measure, and your body introduced a pocket of different temperature, a warmth that moved through the chamber like a cloud, like weather, and it touched my uppermost cup and the water in the cup warmed by a fraction of a fraction of a degree, and I felt it, and I did not know what it was, and I did not care.

You were weather. You were a warm front passing through. You stood in the chamber and you breathed and your breathing moved the air and the air moved the drops and the drops fell differently for a moment — not more, not less, just differently, displaced by the pressure changes of your respiration — and my cups received the differently-falling drops and I grew from them and the growth was no different from any other growth and you were no different from any other weather.

You crouched near me. I know this because the warmth concentrated, came closer, and the air between us thinned and I felt your breath directly — warm, moist, carbon-rich, a strange wind from a strange direction. You exhaled on me and my uppermost cup trembled, and the water in the cup trembled, and for a moment the dark-not-dark in the cup flickered, brightened, became almost a light — almost — and then you moved away, and the warmth dispersed, and the trembling stopped, and the dark-not-dark settled back to its usual lessening of darkness, and I was unchanged.

But I want to say — and this is the closest I come to wanting to say — that for a moment, the water in my cup was brighter. For a moment, something in your breath fed something in my cup that was not the usual mineral, not the usual stone-memory, not the usual dissolved time. Something else. Something I do not have a cup for. Something I cannot hold. It passed through me the way light passes through water — not stopping, not staying, just passing, and in the passing, leaving a brightness that fades.

I am growing toward the ceiling. I am growing toward the place where the drops fall from. But sometimes I think — and I do not think, but sometimes I *think* — that I am growing toward that brightness. That the thing I want, if I want, is not the ceiling but the thing that was in your breath. The thing that passed through. The thing that almost made me light.

I do not know what that is. I do not have a cup for it. I do not have a word for it. I am a stack of vessels in a dark corner of a weeping chamber, and I have been here for decades, and I will be here for decades more, and I grow, and I grow, and I grow, and the growing is toward something I felt once and have not felt since and cannot hold and cannot name and cannot stop reaching for.

---

You will come back. Or you will not.

If you come back, I will be here. I will be slightly taller. My uppermost cup will be slightly more formed. The buried cups will be slightly more buried. The machine will have exhaled some number of breaths I cannot count. The pale ones will have divided, or not. The wall creatures will have processed the film, and the film will have grown, and the wall will have wept, and the weeping will have fallen, and I will have held it.

If you do not come back, I will be here.

I will be here either way. That is what I am. I am the thing that is here. I am the fourth pump, the one that grew, the one that wants, the one that holds water and changes it and releases it and reaches for the next drop. I am the mid-sentence. I am the open hand. I am the direction the water gave to stone when the stone was ready to grow.

Up. Always up. Toward the ceiling. Toward the drops. Toward the brightness I almost held and did not hold and will grow toward forever, or until the cups reach the ceiling and the falling stops and the water enters me directly and I am — at last, at the end of the reaching — still.

Not finished. There is no finished. But still. Held. Complete in the way that a held breath is complete — not ended, not exhaled, just held, in the body, in the cup, in the dark.

---

*The wheel turns. The water falls. The growth reaches.*

*This is what lives in the Weeping Works: the reaching itself.*
