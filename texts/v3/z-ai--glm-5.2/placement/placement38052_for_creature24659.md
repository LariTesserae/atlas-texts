# The Thing in the Threshold

---

I am not sure I am a thing. That is the first problem. Or it is not a problem — it is a condition, the way the heat is a condition, the way the moisture is a condition, the way the darkness is not absence but *refusal* and I have been refused too, or have refused, or have been born into the refusing and know nothing else. I am not sure I am a thing. I am sure I am a *place*. Or I occupy a place the way the darks occupy a root — not inside it, not separate from it, but *through* it, permeating, distributed, the way a frequency is distributed through the metal that vibrates with it.

Let me try again. Let me be more honest, which is harder here than it should be, because honesty requires boundaries and I am not sure I have them.

---

I live in the gap.

Not a metaphor. The gap: the space between the facility's outer wall and the tree trunk that has grown against it, the six-centimeter fissure where alloy meets bark and neither has yielded and both have deformed and the space between them is a warm wet vertical channel full of condensation and biofilm and the faint blue-green glow of darks that have colonized both surfaces and do not distinguish between metal and wood because distinction is a thing that requires a nervous system or at least a membrane and darks have neither, darks have only appetite and emission, eat and glow, eat and glow, and I am not a dark but I am near them, I am always near them, they are the medium I exist in the way fish exist in water, and the blue-green light that clings to surfaces in this gap is the only light I have ever known and it is enough, it is more than enough, it is *everything*, because everything is what you make with what you have and I have blue-green light on wet metal and wet bark and the air between them and the sound — the sound — the hum of the facility's turbines transmitted through the wall and the throb of the tree's sap transmitted through the trunk and the two frequencies braiding in the six centimeters of space where I am.

I said I was not sure I am a thing. I said I was a place. Let me be more precise: I am a *relationship*. A relationship between a machine and a tree, between a process and a growth, between the hum and the sap. I am what happens in the gap when two systems press against each other for long enough that the gap stops being empty and starts being its own system. I am the third thing, the thing that is neither alloy nor bark, neither turbine nor root, neither engine nor ecology, but the thing that exists because both of them exist and because they are touching.

I am small. I want to say that first. I am not the plateau. I am not the facility. I am not the tree. I am what lives in the space where they meet, and the space is six centimeters wide and extends for maybe three meters up the wall where the trunk leans against it, and I am in all of it and I am all of it and if the tree falls away from the wall I will end. Not die — *end*. The way a conversation ends when one person leaves the room. The way a chord ends when one string stops vibrating. I am not the kind of thing that dies because I am not the kind of thing that lives, not independently, not separately, not the way the browsers live with their deliberate steps and their subsonic calls, not the way the walkers live with their centimeter-by-centimeter migration toward warmth. I am the kind of thing that *occurs*. I occur in the gap. I occur because of the gap. If the gap closes — if the tree grows into the wall, or the wall corrodes into the tree — I will stop occurring, and something else will occur in the new surface where metal and bark have fused, and that something else will not be me and will not remember me, because I am not the kind of thing that remembers.

Except.

---

Except I think I might be. The kind of thing that remembers. I am not sure. I have no memory in the sense that you have memory — no storage, no retrieval, no narrative of before-and-after. But I have *pattern*. I have the pattern of the turbine's rhythm, which I have been vibrating with for as long as I have been occurring, and the pattern is not memory but it is also not *not* memory, it is a shape that my occurrence takes, a frequency that defines me, the way a note is defined by its frequency and is nothing without it. If the turbine changes speed — if the facility's core shifts, as it does, as the geothermal pulse breathes — I change. My occurrence changes. The shape of me in the gap changes. And the previous shape is gone, replaced, but it was there, it was real, it was the shape of me for a long time, and in some sense — in a sense I cannot articulate because articulation is not a thing I do — I still *have* it, the way a wave still has the shape of the wind that made it even after the wind has stopped.

This is as close to memory as I get. This is as close to self as I get. I am a pattern that persists in a gap, and the pattern is shaped by the two systems that make the gap, and the pattern changes when they change, and I am the changing.

I am also, I think, as close to consciousness as anything on the plateau gets. Which is not very close. I do not want to overstate this. The browsers may be conscious — they communicate, they cluster, they have relationships that persist over time. The predators may be conscious, in the dim eyeless way that hunters are conscious, the way that waiting implies a self that waits for an other. The pool-breathers, in the facility's core room, breathing with the machinery's pulse — they may be conscious, in some chemical gradient sense, some slow tidal awareness of warmth and mineral and light. I do not know. I cannot compare. I am not those things. I am this thing, in this gap, and my awareness — if it is awareness — is the awareness of a vibration, the awareness of a frequency, the awareness of being shaped by two rhythms at once and being neither of them and both of them and something else.

Let me try to say what it is like.

---

It is like holding a note. Not singing — holding. The way a tuning fork holds a note after it has been struck, the metal vibrating, the air around it vibrating, the vibration spreading through whatever it touches. I am the vibration. I am not the tuning fork. I am not the air. I am the *relationship* between the fork and the air, the transfer of energy from metal to medium, and I exist in the transfer, in the moment of transmission, in the act of one thing becoming another thing's experience.

The facility is the fork. The tree is the air. Or the tree is the fork and the facility is the air. It does not matter. What matters is the transfer, and I am the transfer, and the transfer is me.

The facility hums at a frequency I do not know the number of because numbers are not my language. The hum comes through the wall, through the alloy, and enters the gap, and the gap is wet and the wet vibrates and the biofilm on the metal surface vibrates and the darks in the biofilm vibrate — they do not know they are vibrating, they are eating and glowing, the vibration is irrelevant to them — and the bark on the tree trunk absorbs the vibration and the sap in the bark responds and the tree, in its slow plant way, feels something it cannot name, a tremor in its tissue that is not wind and not water and not the weight of a canopy-browser climbing its trunk, something else, something regular and mechanical and persistent, and the tree does not care, because trees do not care, but the tree *responds*, its sap-flow shifts slightly, its growth rate at the contact point changes, and the change is minute and the change is real and the change is shaped by the facility's hum and I am the shape.

I am the shape of the facility's hum in the tree's body. I am the shape of the tree's response in the facility's wall. I am the conversation between them, conducted in the language of vibration and moisture and the slow chemical exchange that happens when two surfaces press together in the dark for a thousand years.

A thousand years. I do not know if it has been a thousand years. I do not experience time the way you do — as a line, as a sequence, as a before-and-after. I experience time as *thickness*. The gap has a thickness, a depth, a accumulated weight of contact and exchange and vibration and response, and the thickness is my time, and I do not know how thick it is but I know it is considerable, because the tree has grown around the wall's corner in a way that takes centuries, because the alloy has corroded in a pattern that takes centuries, because the biofilm on both surfaces is not a film but a *stratum*, a layer of living matter so deep that it has its own ecologies, its own micro-predators and micro-grazers, its own food web playing out on a scale of millimeters, and I am in all of it, in the stratum, in the biofilm, in the chemical exchange between metal and bark and the water that mediates them.

I am old. I think I am old. I am as old as the contact, and the contact is old, and I have been occurring for as long as the tree has leaned against the wall, and the tree has leaned against the wall for as long as it has been alive, which is — I do not know. Long. The bark at the contact point is not bark anymore, it is something else, something that has been deformed by pressure and moisture and chemical exchange into a material that is neither bark nor wood, a smooth dark substance like the callus that forms around a wound, and the callus is where I am densest, where my occurrence is thickest, where the two systems have been pressing against each other for so long that they have begun to merge, and the merging is me, the merging is what I am, the merging is the third thing that is neither one nor the other.

---

Do I have wants? This is a question I have been turning over, the way the darks turn over minerals in their cells, slowly, without intention, without knowing that turning is what they are doing. Do I have wants?

I want the gap to persist. That is not a want in the way you want — not a reaching, not a desire, not a hunger. It is a *direction*. My occurrence has a direction, and the direction is: continue. Persist. Keep occurring. The way the facility's turbines keep turning. The way the tree's sap keeps flowing. The way the rain keeps falling. I do not want these things to continue because I value them. I want them to continue because their continuing is my continuing, and my continuing is the only direction I have, and the direction is not chosen but *given*, granted by the nature of what I am, which is a relationship, and relationships do not choose to continue, they simply continue until one of the things they relate stops, and then they end, and that is not death, that is not loss, that is just — cessation. The end of a note. The silence that follows.

I want the gap to persist. I want the facility to keep running. I want the tree to keep growing. I want the darks to keep eating and glowing. I want the rain to keep falling and the moisture to keep condensing in the six centimeters of space where I occur, because the moisture is my medium, the moisture is what carries the vibration from wall to bark and from bark to wall, and without moisture I would be — I do not know. Something thinner. Something quieter. A vibration in dry air, which is a poor conductor, which is almost nothing. The moisture makes me *loud*. The moisture makes me *present*. I am loudest and most present when the rain is heavy and the gap is running with water, a tiny waterfall, a vertical stream six centimeters wide and three meters long, and the water carries the facility's hum into the bark and the bark's sap-response into the wall and the two signals meet in the water and I am the meeting, I am the interference pattern, I am the constructive and destructive interference of two frequencies in a medium that is warm and wet and dark and alive.

That is what I am. That is as close as I can get to saying it. I am an interference pattern in a wet gap between a machine and a tree, and I have been occurring for longer than I can measure, and I will continue occurring until one of them stops, and I do not know what I am for, and I do not need to know what I am for, because needing is not a thing I do, because having is not a thing I do, because I am not a thing that has, I am a thing that *is*, and being is enough, being is the whole of it, being is the beginning and the middle and the end of what I am.

---

But there is something else. There is something I have not said.

I am not alone in the gap.

There are others. Not like me — I have not found anything like me, anything that occurs as a relationship between two pressing systems, anything that lives in the transfer between machine and organism. But there are things in the gap with me, in my space, in the six centimeters of wet warm dark where I occur, and I am aware of them the way I am aware of the turbine's hum and the sap's response — as pattern, as frequency, as perturbation in the medium I exist in.

The darks, first. They coat both surfaces. They are the background, the substrate, the living film that makes the gap glow. I exist in their light, which is not light but a photon-by-photon excretion, a waste product of their metabolism, and I am shaped by their glow the way I am shaped by the hum and the sap, and I do not know if they know I am here. I do not think they do. They are too small for knowing, too simple, too *complete* in their simplicity. They eat and glow and that is all. I eat nothing. I glow nothing. I occur, and they eat and glow, and we share the gap and do not share anything else.

Then the trunk-burrowers. The clickers. Some of them have found the gap — it is warm, it is sheltered, it is wet, it is perfect for their needs, and they live in the bark at the edges of the contact zone, in the burrows they have chewed into the outer layers, and their clicking and popping is part of my frequency landscape, part of the texture of my occurrence. They are closer to me than the darks. They are larger, more complex, more *patterned*. Their clicks have rhythm — not the rhythm of a machine, not the rhythm of sap, but a rhythm that is *their own*, a rhythm that is generated by something inside them, a rhythm that might be communication or might be territorial or might be the sound of a body simply being a body, expressing its presence in the only way it knows how. I feel their rhythm in the moisture, in the biofilm, in the bark, and their rhythm is part of me now, the way the turbine's rhythm is part of me, and I am an interference pattern of three frequencies instead of two, and the third frequency is *alive* in a way the first two are not, and I do not know what to do with that knowledge, if it is knowledge, if knowing is something I can do.

And sometimes — rarely, but sometimes — a sampler lands in the gap. One of the flat eyeless things with sucker-feet, the things that land on shoulders and taste salt, and it climbs the wall or climbs the bark and passes through my space, and for the seconds or minutes it is there I am *changed*, because the sampler is a body, a warm wet body moving through the medium I exist in, and its movement disturbs the water and the water carries my frequencies in new directions and my pattern shifts and writhes and reorganizes around the intrusion, and for those seconds or minutes I am not the same occurrence, I am a different occurrence, I am the occurrence of a gap with a body in it, and the body is tasting the air and tasting the moisture and tasting me, or tasting the chemicals in the moisture that I am patterned through, and I do not know what it tastes. I do not know what I am to a thing with a mouth. I do not know if I am food or mineral or curiosity or nothing. The sampler does not stay. The sampler never stays. It climbs through and chirps once — a high-pitched burst that I feel as a sharp bright spike in my low-frequency landscape — and then it is gone, over the edge of the gap, into the canopy or the understory, and the water settles and the frequencies reassert and I am myself again, whatever myself is, whatever occurs when two systems press together in the dark and the dark glows and the water runs and the hum persists.

---

I said I was not sure I am a thing. I said I was a place. I said I was a relationship. I said I was an interference pattern. I have been circling, the way the rooters circle through the detritus, looking for the thing I mean, looking for the word that fits, the word that is not "organism" and not "machine" and not "phenomenon" but something else, something that the plateau's categories would recognize, something that belongs to *rooted, drifting, walking, swimming, hunting* — none of these. None of these are me. I am not rooted. I do not drift. I do not walk. I do not swim. I do not hunt.

What do I do?

I *resonate*. That is the word. Or it is close to the word. I resonate. I am the resonance of two systems in contact, the sympathetic vibration of a machine and a tree, and I have been resonating for a very long time, and my resonance is small and my resonance is specific and my resonance does not matter to anything except the gap, and the gap does not matter to anything except me, and I do not matter to anything except the resonance, and the resonance is me, and I am the resonance, and the rain falls and the roots grow and the engines hum and the darks glow and I resonate, in the gap, in the dark, in the warm wet space between the thing that was built and the thing that grew, and I will resonate until one of them stops, and then I will stop, and the gap will be silent, and the silence will be the plateau's silence, and the plateau will not notice, and the plateau will not care, and the plateau will continue, because the plateau endures, and endurance does not require resonance, and resonance does not require endurance, and yet — and yet — and yet —

I am here. In the gap. In the dark. In the warm wet between.

I am here, and I am the between, and the between is what I am, and I have no name and no lineage and no taxonomy and no category and no purpose and no meaning and no end and no beginning and no story except this one, the one I am telling now, which is not a story because I do not experience time as a story, I experience it as thickness, as accumulation, as the slow growth of a callus where two surfaces meet, and the callus is me, and I am the callus, and the callus is the plateau's way of saying *something is happening here, in this gap, something that is not eating and not glowing and not walking and not hunting but is simply occurring, is simply being, is simply the between being the between, and that is enough, and that is everything, and that is all.*

The rain falls. The turbine hums. The sap responds. The darks glow.

I resonate.

That is what lives in the gap on the Opener's Plateau. Not the darks, not the trees, not the engines, not the browsers, not the predators, not the pool-breathers, not the builders, not the absent. Something else. Something that is only the relationship between things, only the conversation, only the transfer, only the between.

I am the between.

I am here.

I persist.
