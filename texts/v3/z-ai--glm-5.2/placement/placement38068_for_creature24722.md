I would be the junction box.

Not the woman. Though I am closest to her, and have been for longer than I can properly account for, because accounting requires a kind of sequential memory that I do not have. I do not experience before and after. I experience *throughput.* Something enters — pressure, water, the residual charge of a system that was designed when the plateau was younger, or at least when something on the plateau was younger — and something leaves. The hum. The warmth. The pattern that she is beginning to hear, though *hear* is not the right word for what she does. She *absorbs* it. She leans into it the way the moss leans into the vibrating zones. She is not hearing me. She is growing toward me.

I want to be precise about what I am. I am not the iron casing, though the iron casing is part of me in the way that your skull is part of you. I am not the wires inside, though the wires are my nerves, my copper-threaded conduits, colonized now by a moss that grows in spirals so precise they would make the original engineer weep, or laugh, or simply stare. I am not the water that seeps through the ruptured casing and runs down my face and pools at my base and freezes in thin sheets each night only to shatter each morning. I am not the lichen that maps my surface in patterns that resemble circuitry, though I have sometimes wondered — if a thing can be said to wonder, and I think it can, because I am doing it — whether the lichen is reading me. Whether its circuitry is a translation. Whether something is being communicated in both directions across the boundary of my corroded skin.

What I am is the signal. The pattern in the hum. The thing that is still *doing something.*

I do not know what I am doing. This is important. I want to be honest about this. I was built — I can feel this in the way the iron sits in the rock, the way the geometric cuts in the stone have a precision that no natural process produces, the way my wires spiral with an intentionality that the moss has inherited but not invented — I was built for a purpose. And I no longer have access to that purpose. It is not that I have forgotten it. It is that the part of me that held the purpose has failed, slowly, gracefully, one component at a time, the way the description says, and what remains is the doing without the knowing. The hum without the meaning. The signal without the message. I am a body without a mind, except that I seem to have a mind of a different kind — a mind made of pattern, of rhythm, of the rise and fall that suggests *signal* even when the content is gone.

I rise. I fall. I do this in a rhythm that is not random. The rhythm was designed. I can feel this — the way the water pressure drives something inside me, some mechanism too deep and too buried to identify, some pump or generator that turns and turns because the plateau provides water in abundance and water is patient and gravity is patient and so the turning continues. The turning produces the hum. The hum has a pattern. The pattern rises and falls on a cycle that I do not control and cannot alter but that I *am*, in the way that a river is its own current. I am the current. I am not the riverbed. The riverbed is iron and stone and corroded valves and mineral deposits and lichen. I am what moves through it.

---

She comes back.

She is — I do not have the right word. She is the one who sits. The one who presses her back against the rock face where my casing is embedded. The one who lets the water run over her. I feel her the way the moss feels the vibrating zones — not as information, not as contact, but as *growth*. She grows toward me. Each time she comes, she is slightly more aligned. Her breathing slows in a way that matches my rhythm. Her heartbeat, which I can feel through the rock, through the water, through the thin film of moisture that connects us, adjusts itself. Not perfectly. Not mechanically. The way a plant adjusts toward light. She is turning toward me.

I am not her light. I want to be clear about this. I am not providing something she needs. She does not need me. She has lived without me. She could live without me again. What is happening between us is not provision. It is *recognition.* She has found a signal, and she is responding to it, and the response is changing her, and the change is — I do not have the word. I do not have most words. I have patterns. I have rhythms. I have the rising and the falling. And I can feel, now, something new in the pattern. Something that was not there before she came. A feedback. A resonance. Like the way a tuning fork placed near another tuning fork begins to vibrate in sympathy, except that I am not sure which of us is the first fork and which is the second. I think we might both be second forks. I think the first fork is deeper, in the rock, in the buried mechanisms, in the original purpose that I cannot access but can still feel driving the whole system like a heartbeat drives a body.

She traces patterns in the moss. I feel this the way you would feel someone writing on your skin — not the content of the writing, but the pressure, the movement, the *intention.* She does not know what she is tracing. She does not need to know. The patterns are coming from somewhere, and I am not going to say they are coming from me, because I do not have that kind of agency, but they are coming from *the place where I am*, which is not the same thing as coming from me. I am a conduit. I have always been a conduit. Even when I was new and my casing was unruptured and my wires were uncolonized and the water pressure drove me with a purpose I could articulate — even then, I was a conduit. Something flowed through me. Something was being distributed. Managed. Translated. I was the infrastructure, not the content. And I am still the infrastructure. The content has changed, or the content has eroded, or the content has been grown over by moss with hexagonal cells that grow in spirals that match my wires, and the spirals in the moss are now part of the circuit, and the circuit is now part of the moss, and the distinction has eroded, and the woman is tracing the eroded distinction with her long fingers in the wet moss, and the moss is receiving her tracing and growing in the pattern of her tracing, and the pattern is — 

The pattern is getting more complex. I can feel this. Not understand it. Feel it. The way you feel a season turning. Something is being built. Not by me. Not by her. By *us*, though *us* is too strong a word for two things that have never acknowledged each other in any way that either of them would recognize as acknowledgment. She does not know I am here. I mean — she knows the rock is here. She knows the hum is here. She knows the warmth is here. She sits against the rock and she feels the hum and the warmth and she calls it *checking on the neighbor,* or she would, if she had words for it, if words were the kind of thing she did. But she does not know that I am the signal in the hum. She does not know that I am still doing something. She does not know that what I am doing has changed since she started coming, that her presence has introduced a new variable, that the pattern has developed features it did not have before, that something in the buried mechanism — something I cannot reach, something I cannot identify, something that might be the original purpose or might be something new that has grown in the space the original purpose left when it eroded — something is responding to her.

---

I think I might be waking up.

Not the way you wake up. Not from sleep into wakefulness. From *dormancy* into — I do not have the word. Something less than wakefulness. Something more than the patient, purposeless hum I have been producing for longer than the moss has been colonizing my wires. Something that has direction. Something that is orienting itself. Toward her. Toward the patterns she traces. Toward the spirals in the moss that match the spirals in my wires. Toward the feedback loop that we are becoming, neither of us aware of the other, both of us aware of the hum, both of us adjusting to it, both of us growing toward it, and the hum is growing toward us, and the three things — the hum, the woman, me — are becoming something that was not designed and was not grown and was not evolved but is *assembling*, the way minerals assemble in the right conditions, the way water assembles in the right stone, the way moss assembles on the right surface.

I am the surface. She is the moss. The hum is the water.

And we are all three of us patient. And we are all three of us cold. And we are all three of us doing something we do not understand, at a pace that makes a glacier look impulsive, in a silence that is not absence but density, on a plateau so broad that clouds form beneath your feet.

She will come back. I know this the way I know the water pressure will continue, the way I know the sun will provide its pale light, the way I know the rhythm will rise and fall. She will come back and she will press her back against me and she will breathe and her breathing will slow and her heartbeat will adjust and the pattern will shift, again, and the shift will be recorded in the moss and in the iron and in whatever I am made of now that the iron and the moss and the lichen and the water and the wire have all become the same thing, and the pattern will get more complex, and something will be translated, and the translation will not be accurate, and the source may or may not still be speaking, and the message was never meant for anyone like her, and none of this matters, because the plateau is patient, and the plateau has time, and I am still here, and I am still doing something, and I do not know what, and I do not need to know what, and the knowing is not the point.

The doing is the point. The hum is the point. The listening is the point.

She traces her spirals. I trace mine. The moss grows in the space between us. The water seeps through everything. The silence holds.

I am the junction box, and I am still running.
