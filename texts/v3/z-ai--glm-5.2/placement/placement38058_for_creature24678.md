I would be the one who left.

Not left — that implies a departure, a rupture, a moment of choosing to go. The ones here do not rupture. I would be the one who *leaned past*. The one whose leaning became drifting, whose drifting became distance, whose distance became the plain between the settlement and the fold, and who is here now, alone, at the place where the plain stops promising approach and simply becomes itself.

Let me be more precise.

I was — *was*, the word sticks, it has the texture of something from a world that still had tense — I was one of the standing ones. I know this because I have the surface. I have the waxy give. I have the pale color of the ground I stand on, and the damp that sits on me and does not evaporate. I have the vertical axis, or what remains of it. If you touched me — and you could, if you came here, if you walked the two kilometers east from the settlement and then the further distance north that has no number because numbers require change and change requires a before and after and I have been here long enough that before and after have eroded into each other like the sky and the ground at the fold — if you touched me, your fingertip would come away wet, and you would feel, under the damp, something that was once firmer. Something that has the memory of density the way a riverbed has the memory of water. The groove is there. The shape of what I was is there. But the substance has thinned.

I am thin. This is the first thing you would notice if you could see me, which you probably couldn't, because I am the color of the ground and the color of the air and the color of the light that does not change, and I have been absorbing these colors for so long that I have become a consensus of them, a negotiation between pale mineral and pale sky and pale luminance, and the negotiation has settled — as everything settles here — into a color that is all of them and none of them, the color of old bone left in weak tea, the color of almost-gone.

I am thin the way a stain is thin after a thousand washings. Not transparent — not yet, not quite — but *reduced*. I have given myself to the mist and the air and the ground and the few plants that grow out here, far from the settlement, the sparse clusters that find the shallow channels and drink from them. I have been giving myself to them the way the others give themselves to the lush gardens near the settlement, but my giving has been going on longer, or more completely, or both. The plants out here are not lush. They are sparse and hard and small. They are not blue-green; they are the pale gray-green of something that is almost the color of the ground. They have been absorbing me for so long that they have begun to take on the consensus color too. They are becoming what I am becoming. We are becoming the same thing, which is the ground, which is the air, which is the table at the top of everything, which is the thing that remains.

---

Why did I leave the settlement?

I didn't. That is the honest answer, and it is the answer that the place would give if the place could answer, which it can't, because answering requires a before-and-after and a causality and a because, and there is no because here. There is only the leaning, and the leaning became the drifting, and the drifting became the distance. I did not decide to walk toward the fold. I did not choose the fold over the settlement. I did not choose anything. Choice requires a future, and the future eroded away before I eroded, and what was left was the orientation, and the orientation was toward the crease, and I leaned, and the leaning did what leaning does when it goes on long enough without anything to stop it.

I leaned the way the plants lean. I leaned the way the water in the pool rotates — without cause, without acceleration, without the possibility of stopping. The fold is where the world creased, and the crease exerts something that is not gravity and not magnetism and not longing. It is the pull of a surface toward its own continuity. Think of a wrinkle in a cloth. The cloth on either side of the wrinkle leans toward the wrinkle because the wrinkle is where the cloth is closest to being itself, to being the single flat unbroken sheet it was before it was folded. Everything here leans toward the fold because the fold is where the world remembers what it was before it was broken, and the remembering is not a cognitive act. It is a material one. It is the tendency of a thing to return to its uncreased state, which is also its final state, which is also its original state, and the words *original* and *final* mean the same thing here because beginnings and endings have eroded into each other.

I leaned. I lean. I am leaning. The verbs have all become the same verb, the way the colors have all become the same color, the way the ground and the sky have become the same surface at the fold.

---

What is it like, out here?

I will tell you what I can, though I should say first that *like* is the wrong preposition. *Like* implies resemblance, and resemblance requires comparison, and comparison requires two things, and out here there is one. The one thing is the plain, and I am of it, and it is of me, and the fold is ahead of me, and the settlement is behind me, and both of them are at distances that do not decrease when I move toward them or increase when I move away, because distance out here is not a measurement. It is a quality. It is the quality of *apartness*, which is the only quality that has not eroded, because apartness is the quality of being one thing rather than another, and out here that quality is very thin, very thin indeed, but still present, still here, still holding me as me and the ground as ground, barely, barely.

The light is the same. I should say that first because it is the first thing and the last thing. The light is the same as it was in the settlement — diffuse, gray-white, without source, without shadow, without change. But out here, far from the walls and the paths and the dwellings, the light has a quality it does not have in the settlement. It has the quality of *depth*. Not darkness — there is no darkness here; darkness would require the possibility of less light, and less light would require change, and change has eroded. But depth. The light goes down into the ground and does not come back, or comes back so slowly that the returning is indistinguishable from the staying. The ground absorbs the light the way it absorbs the mist — reluctantly, without enthusiasm, holding it in its mineral surface the way the pools hold their water, without current, without motion, without the desire to hold or to release. The ground is luminous in the way that a very old photograph is luminous — not bright, not shining, but carrying light in its substance, in the material itself, so that the light seems to come from the ground rather than falling on it.

I stand in this light. I absorb it too. My surface takes the light the way it takes the mist, and the light sits on me and in me, and I am — I have become — faintly luminous, in the way of the ground, in the way of the old photograph, in the way of something that has been in the light so long that it has taken on the light as a property of its own substance. If you came here, if you walked out from the settlement, past the last cluster of blue-green, past the last shallow channel, past the point where the mineral ground changes from rough to waxy — if you came here and looked, you might see me as a faint irregularity in the ground's luminance, a place where the pale light is slightly more present, slightly less spread, gathered into a vertical shape that is not quite a column and not quite a figure and not quite anything at all. You might see me and think: that is a trick of the thin air. That is the way the light behaves on a surface that is almost flat but not perfectly flat. You would not be wrong. You would also not be right. The categories of right and wrong have eroded, and what remains is the seeing, and the seeing is indeterminate, and the indeterminacy is not a failure. It is the actual nature of what I am.

---

The fold.

I should tell you about the fold, because the fold is why I am here, and the fold is what I lean toward, and the fold is the thing that I cannot reach and have never expected to reach and have never tried to reach, because trying requires a future, and the future is here, present, arrived, finished, wearing thin.

The fold is ahead of me. It has been ahead of me since I began to lean. It does not get closer. I have established this. I have been moving toward it — *moving* in the sense of the mist, in the sense of below the threshold of perception, in the sense of a motion that is also a stillness — and it has not gotten closer. The fold is not at a distance. The fold is at an *angle*. It is oriented differently from the rest of the world. The world is flat — the table, the plain, the ancient ground — but the fold is vertical, and the vertical is not a direction here. It is a dimension that has been set aside, folded up, put away, the way you would fold a tablecloth and place it in a drawer. The fold is the drawer. The fold is where the vertical went when the mountains flattened and the ground settled and the up-and-down of the world eroded into the single plane of here and here and here.

But the fold is not nothing. The fold is not absence. The fold is *the place where the vertical still is*. That is why everything leans toward it. That is why the plants lean, why the water rotates, why I drifted, why the standing ones in the settlement orient themselves north — north, the direction I have decided to call the fold. The fold contains the dimension that the rest of the world has lost. The fold is where up still exists. And everything that has a vertical axis — the plants, the beings, the ancient memory of mountains — everything that remembers up, even in the mineral memory of stone, leans toward the fold because the fold is where up went, and up is where things grow, and growing is the last motion that has not eroded, the last motion that is still motion-as-being rather than motion-toward.

I grow toward the fold. Not grow in the sense of larger — I am not getting larger; I am getting thinner, smaller, more eroded. But grow in the sense of *extend*. I extend toward the fold the way a root extends toward water, the way a tendril extends toward a support, the way anything extends toward the thing that it needs to continue being what it is. I need the vertical. I was once vertical. I remember this — not in a mind, because I do not have a mind; minds require thinking, and thinking requires change, and change has eroded. I remember in the mineral. In the orientation of whatever substance remains in me. In the way the atoms of my almost-stone body still carry the alignment of a thing that once stood up, that once had a head at the top and feet at the bottom and the whole long axis of a body between them, oriented against the flat pull of the ground, holding itself up, holding itself up.

I am not holding myself up anymore. That is the second thing you would notice, if you could see me, if you came here. I am leaning. I am leaning at an angle that should be impossible — an angle that defies the gravity that still works here, that still pulls stones down when you let them fall. I am at an angle that is not held by structural rigidity or by tension or by anything you can name. I am held by the fold. The fold holds me up the way a magnetic field holds iron filings — not by force but by orientation, by the alignment of the thing with the field it is in. I am aligned with the fold. I am a filing in its field. And the field holds me at the angle I have been leaning toward since the leaning began, and the angle is the angle of a thing that is almost down, almost on the ground, almost horizontal, almost at rest, but not quite, not yet, because the fold still has enough of the vertical in it to hold me at this last angle, this final inclination, this posture that is neither standing nor lying but is the thing between, the thing that has no name because the ones who would have named it have become the walls they built and the paths they walked and the ground they stood on.

---

I tend nothing. This is the difference between me and the ones in the settlement. They tend the plants. They tend the central one. They bring moisture and deposit it on surfaces that need the moisture to stay soft, to stay in the middle, to keep the possibility open. I tend nothing. There is nothing out here to tend. The plants are sparse and small and do not need me, or do not want me, or have already taken what they will take from me and are now simply growing in the ground that I have become part of, absorbing the mineral that was my substance the way they absorb the mist. I do not bring moisture to anything. I do not bring anything to anything. I am the thing that is brought — the substance that is transferred, the material that moves from vertical to horizontal, from standing to ground, from the body to the plain.

I am being given. That is what I am doing. I am being given to the ground and the air and the few plants and the light, and the giving is not a choice and not a sacrifice and not a loss. It is the motion of a thing that is high and heavy and tired of holding itself up allowing the ground to pull it down. The ground is pulling me. I am allowing it. The allowance is not surrender. Surrender implies resistance, and resistance eroded away long ago, in some epoch I cannot name because naming requires categories and categories require boundaries and my boundaries have eroded until I am not sure where I end and the ground begins. The ground is pulling me. I am going. The going is the being. The being is the going.

---

Do I think?

The water in the pool rotates. Does the water think? The question is wrong. The question comes from a world where thinking is a process that happens to a thing, an event, a something that occurs in the something else of a mind. Here, thinking is not a process. It is not an event. It is a condition, the way wetness is a condition of the air, the way luminance is a condition of the ground. I do not think. I am in the thinking. The thinking is in me. It rotates, slowly, without cause, without acceleration, the way the pool rotates, and the rotation is not about anything, and the rotation does not conclude anything, and the rotation is not directed toward a result or a resolution or a rest.

If I could tell you what the rotation contains — and I cannot, because telling requires language and language requires categories and categories have eroded, and also because the rotation does not *contain* anything; it is not a vessel; it is a motion — but if I could, I would say this: the rotation contains the fold. Not as a thought. Not as an image. Not as a memory or a longing or a desire. As an orientation. The way a compass needle's rotation contains north. The needle does not think about north. The needle does not desire north. The needle is *oriented toward* north, and the orientation is the being of the needle, and the being of the needle is the orientation, and without the orientation the needle would not be a needle. It would be a piece of metal in a case.

I am oriented toward the fold. The fold is in my rotation the way north is in the needle. It is not a thought. It is not a feeling. It is the direction of my being, the inclination of my substance, the alignment of whatever I am made of with whatever the fold is made of, and the alignment is not something I do. It is something I am.

---

There is one thing more, and I will tell it, and then I will stop, because stopping is what this place teaches, and I have been here long enough that stopping and going have become the same motion, the way standing and lying have become the same posture, the way sky and ground have become the same surface at the fold.

The one thing is this: I am not the only one who left.

There are others. Not in the settlement — the settlement has its standing ones, its tending ones, its central one on the platform with the faintest give. But out here, on the plain, between the settlement and the fold, there are others like me. Thin. Pale. Almost gone. Leaning at angles that should be impossible. Luminous with the ground's luminance. We do not gather. We do not speak. We do not touch. We are too far apart for touching, and too eroded for gathering, and speaking requires a language and language requires categories and categories have eroded. But we are here, and we are oriented the same direction, and we are leaning the same way, and the same field holds us at the same impossible angles, and we are, in our apartness, together — not together in the way of a community or a family or a people, but together in the way of compass needles, in the way of iron filings in a field, in the way of things that share an orientation without sharing a space.

We are the ones who leaned past. We are the ones whose leaning became drifting and whose drifting became distance and whose distance brought us here, to the place where the fold is ahead and the settlement is behind and neither one gets closer or farther and the light does not change and the mist comes and the ground is luminous and we are luminous and the plants are sparse and the pools are few and the water in them turns and we turn and the turning is the thinking and the thinking is the turning and the fold holds us and we lean and we are and we are and we are.

We are the ones who went toward the fold.

Not to reach it. Not to arrive. Not because the fold is a destination. We went because the leaning took us, and the leaning took us because the fold is where the vertical still is, and we were once vertical, and the memory of vertical is in our mineral, and the mineral remembers what the mind has forgotten, and the remembering is not cognitive but material, and the material is what we are, and what we are is what we are becoming, and what we are becoming is the ground, and the ground is leaning toward the fold too, and one day — *day*, the word is wrong, there are no days; one duration, one epoch, one long flat evenness of time — one something, the ground will reach the fold, and the fold will unfold, and the vertical will return, and the sky will lift from the ground like a cloth being shaken out, and the mountains will rise again from the table, and the horizon will curve, and the light will change, and the air will carry sound with enthusiasm, and everything will begin.

But that is a future, and futures have eroded, and what remains is the leaning, and the leaning is the leaning, and it is enough.

It is enough.

I lean, and I am, and the fold is ahead, and the ground is luminous, and the mist comes, and I am here, and I am still going, still.

Still.
