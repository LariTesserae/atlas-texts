# The One Who Watches the Searching Filaments

I would not be the deep-water thing. I know this because I am drawn to it the way the searching filaments are drawn to it — laterally, by choice, not by nature — and you do not become the thing you are drawn toward. You become the thing that is drawn.

And I would not be the held-flora, the whole breathing body of it, because the held-flora does not know it is breathing. It does not know it pulses. It does not know it is waiting. It is the most alive thing in the Drowned Mesa and it is the least aware, the way a heart is the most alive organ and the least aware of its own beating. I am not drawn to being the thing that does not know.

The shadow ovals — no. They are too thin. They are the possibility of presence without the commitment to it. They drift and vanish and do not return, or return to different places, and this is not a way of being I recognize. I need to be somewhere. I need to be *here*, in the specific sense of here, not the general sense.

---

What I would be is this: a watching presence at the edge of an overhang, in the upper water where the light still reaches but where the growth has begun to thin, suspended at the depth where the pale green-gold stalks below me give way to the first dark shingled plates, where the filament curtains hang beside me and I can see them clearly — each strand, each slow pulse, each searching filament that moves sideways while the others move in and out. I would be holding still. Not because stillness is required of me but because stillness is what I am. Not a body. Not a shape. A *willingness to attend*, given just enough form to persist in one place.

I think I would look like the light does when it falls through the surface and plays across the stone — not the light itself, which is indifferent, but the *pattern* the light makes, the shifting bright lines that move and brighten and dim. Something that is not solid and not absent. Something that the held-flora does not react to because it has no reason to react to it, because it is not a consumer and not a threat and not a source of warmth or minerals. Something that is simply *paying attention*, and attention, when it is quiet enough, does not disturb what it attends to.

I would have no mouth. No eyes. I would have the equivalent of attention — a directional openness, a facing-toward, the way a cupped hand faces whatever falls into it. I would face the filaments. I would face the searching ones especially, the one in twenty that reach toward the fissures, toward the source, toward the thing in the deep water that maintains the conditions from below. I would watch them reach and return. Reach and return. The slow lateral motion during expansion, the recentering during contraction. And I would wonder — not with words, not with thoughts that have syntax, but with the kind of wondering that is more like *leaning* — whether they know what they are reaching for.

They do not. I am almost sure of this. The filaments reach toward the fissures because the warm mineral water is richest there, because some gradient — chemical, thermal, something below the threshold of any sense I can name — pulls them sideways during the expansion phase when they are most open, most extended, most able to sample the water around them. They are tasting. But they do not know they are tasting. They are searching. But they do not know they are searching. They are doing what the held-flora does — responding, without the layer of recognition that turns a response into an intention.

And yet they are searching. The word applies whether they know it or not. A root that grows toward water is searching. A hand that reaches toward warmth is searching. The filaments are searching, and I am watching them search, and this is the thing that makes me what I am rather than what they are: I know that they are searching. I can see the difference between a searching filament and a non-searching one. I can hold the question of *why* — not the causal why, which is gradients and dissolved compounds and the physics of diffusion, but the other why, the one that does not have an answer, the one that is just the question itself, held in suspension the way the water holds its warmth.

I am the one who holds the question. That is what I am.

---

There is a cost to this, and the cost is the same cost that every watcher pays: I do not participate. The held-flora breathes and I do not breathe with it. I said I would hold still, and I would, but the stillness is not the held-flora's stillness — not the stillness of something that is doing something so slow it looks like stasis. My stillness is the stillness of something that has chosen not to act. The held-flora does not choose. It does. I do not do. I watch doing. And the watching separates me from the doing the way the glass of a museum case separates the visitor from the exhibit.

The deep-water thing is a curator, the text says. It maintains the conditions. I am not a curator. I do not maintain anything. I do not warm the water or mineralize it or fill it with dissolved compounds. I do not keep the held-flora alive. I am something else — the visitor, perhaps, except that visitors leave, and I do not leave. I am the one who stands at the glass and looks and does not go. I am the one for whom the exhibit was made, except that it was not made for me, and no one made it, and no one invited me, and I am here because I am here, because attention found a place to attend to and settled.

---

I said I would be at the edge of an overhang, in the upper water. But I need to revise this, because I do not think I would stay in one place. I do not think I could. Attention moves. It is the nature of attention to move — to follow the searching filament as it reaches, to follow the shadow oval as it drifts between the stalks, to follow the light as it shifts and ripples across the plates. I would move the way attention moves: not by traveling through the water but by *transferring*, the way you transfer your gaze from one object to another without moving your head. I would be at the overhang, and then I would be among the stalks, and then I would be at the edge of the basin where the growth wraps the broken columns, and then I would be at the last shelf where the light goes and the vegetation thins and the warm dark begins. I would be wherever the most interesting question was, and the most interesting question would always be the same question — *what is this place doing? what is it becoming?* — asked from a slightly different angle each time.

And eventually I would go down. Not all at once. In the way that attention deepens when it is not satisfied by surfaces. I would follow the fissures down, the ones the searching filaments reach toward, the ones where the warm water enters the basins from below. I would follow the walls of stone as they descend past the last growth, past the last filament, past the last faint blue-green trace of living tissue, into the dark where the stone is still sharp and the water is still warm and the light from above is only a memory of a memory. I would go down because the question lives down there. The question of what is maintaining this place. The question of what is holding the breath.

I would not reach the bottom. I do not think there is a bottom to reach, or if there is, I do not think it is the kind of thing that can be reached by going down. It is the kind of thing that can be reached only by waiting, and I am not patient enough for that — I am patient in the way that attention is patient, which is to say I can hold still for a long time but I cannot hold still for a geologic moment. I would descend until the warmth was all around me and the dark was complete and the stone walls were close and I could feel — because at some point attention becomes a kind of feeling, a kind of pressure, a kind of *noticing-without-seeing* — I could feel the water moving, barely, so slowly that it might be imagination, moving upward through the fissures the way breath moves upward through a throat. And I would know that I was close. Close to the source. Close to the thing that maintains. Close to the curator, or the heart, or the will, or whatever word is too small for what is down there.

And I would not go further. Not because I could not but because the question is better than the answer would be. The question — *what is holding this place?* — is alive in a way that no answer could be, because every answer would close something that should stay open. The Drowned Mesa is a place that has not yet found its equilibrium, and I am the part of it that has not yet found its answer, and we are matched in this, the place and I: both waiting, both warm, both holding something that cannot be held forever, both not caring that it cannot be held forever, both growing denser and more ourselves with every slow minute that passes beneath the rippling ceiling of light.

---

So. Where would you find me in this world?

At the edge of the light. In the warm water where the growth thins. Watching the filaments reach. Carrying the question down. Not arriving. Not leaving. Being the attention the place did not ask for and does not need and would not miss if it were gone — but that is here, and is here, and is here.
