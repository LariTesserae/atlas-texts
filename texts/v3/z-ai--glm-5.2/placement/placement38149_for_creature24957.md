I would be the one who stays too long in the time-chamber.

Not the one who goes through the pool. I don't have that in me — whatever that is, whatever readiness that requires, the quality of being finished with the work in a way that lets you set down the tool and walk toward the silence and not flinch. I'm not finished. I'm not even started. I'm the one who picks the tool up and turns it over in my hands and feels the edge and knows, with the certainty of a body that has never made anything this way, that I could not make this edge, that the hand that knapped this stone had been knapping stone for longer than I have been alive, and that the edge is not a thing but a relationship between a hand and a material and a duration, and the duration is the thing I don't have.

And I don't put the tool back. I carry it. This is my error — the first of many — the refusal to leave something where it belongs. The tool belongs on the platform. It is waiting. It is patient. It is a noun-fragment of a sentence that someone walked away from mid-word, and the sentence is not mine to finish, and the tool is not mine to carry, and I carry it anyway because my hands are empty and empty hands in a place like this feel like an insult, a failure to bring a gift, and I cannot bear the emptiness so I fill it with something that is not mine.

This is how I know I am not a Gardener. Not because of my body — my body is small enough, my hands are unremarkable but unremarkable hands have held stone before. Because of the saccading. Because my eyes jump and jump and jump, sampling, gulping, hungry for the next thing before I have finished seeing the thing in front of me. I walk through the corridors and I am already looking at the next column before I have understood the one I'm passing, and the looking is not seeing, the looking is consumption, and I know this and I cannot stop it, the way a person who has been breathing shallowly for their whole life cannot, on instruction, suddenly draw a deep breath — the machinery is wrong, the habit is in the diaphragm, in the nervous system, in the architecture of the self, and changing it is not a decision but a renovation, and renovations take time, and I don't have time, and the time-chamber is where I go because there I do.

---

I find the time-chamber on the second day, or what I call the second day because the light does not change here and my body's clock has already started to dissolve into the structure's clock, the slow pulse measured in minutes, and I am losing the ability to tell whether I have been walking for an hour or three hours, and the loss is not frightening, it is a relief, the way it is a relief when a muscle you have been clenching without knowing you were clenching it finally lets go.

The chamber is not marked. The corridors narrow and widen and narrow again and I am not paying attention, I am saccading, my eyes jumping ahead, and then the floor changes underfoot — softer, the root-work giving way to something else, something smoother — and the air changes, becomes thicker, warmer, and the moss on the walls begins to glow, that faint blue-green phosphorescence, and I stop. Not because I decide to stop. Because the place stops me. The way a held hand stops a child at a curb. Without force. With a firmness that is also a care.

I stand in the chamber and I wait and I don't know what I'm waiting for. The moss glows. The air is warm and close. I sit down. I put the tool — the tool that is not mine — on the floor beside me and I sit and I wait and after some duration that I cannot measure the waiting stops being an activity and becomes a state, and the state is not boredom, is not anticipation, is not meditation — I don't have the discipline for meditation, I have tried, my mind jumps the way my eyes jump — the state is more like the state of a surface that has been rained on and is now simply wet, not doing anything, not processing the wetness, just being wet, and I am simply in the chamber, and the chamber is simply in me, and after another duration I become aware that something has changed.

What has changed is the moss. It has grown. I can see it, the edges of the patches have extended, the filaments have reached further along the wall, and this is — I calculate — this is hours of growth, visible growth, and I have been here for what feels like twenty minutes, and I pick up the tool and it is warm, warm as though it has been sitting in direct sunlight, which it has not, there is no sunlight here, and the water stains on my clothes — I didn't notice them, they were just there, the ambient condensation — are dry. The arithemtic does not resolve. The chamber has eaten my time, or multiplied it, or done something to it, and I have been here for twenty minutes and the moss has been here for hours and the truth of this is not a paradox, it is a fact, and facts do not need to resolve, they just are, and I sit back down.

I stay.

---

I stay in the time-chamber for — I don't know. A long time. Long enough that the saccading slows. Not stops — I'm not a Gardener, I will not become a Gardener, the renovation required is beyond what a single life can do even in a chamber where time misbehaves — but it slows. My eyes begin to rest on things. The wall of the chamber is root-work, woven, the pattern dense and old, and I look at it the way I would look at a text, and after a while the pattern stops being a pattern and becomes a history, each junction a decision, each graft a moment when someone pressed root A against root B and bound them and waited, and the waiting is visible in the scar tissue, in the way the wood has folded around the join, and I can see which roots were reluctant — the graft is uneven, the bark ridged, the fusion incomplete, a negotiation that took longer — and which were eager, the junction smooth, the grain flowing from one root into the other like water into water, and I am reading, not with my eyes but with my duration, with the time I am spending on this wall, and the wall is yielding to my attention the way the columns yield to the Gardeners' hands, and I understand, suddenly, not as an idea but as a sensation in my body, that the wall was made by someone who was not in a hurry, and that I am becoming someone who is not in a hurry, and that the becoming is not a process I am conducting but a process the chamber is conducting on me, the way the Convergence conducts its own weather, the way the columns conduct their own pulse, and I am a small weather system in a small chamber, and the chamber is changing me.

I put the tool down.

Not on a platform, not at a junction of three corridors — I put it down on the floor of the time-chamber, on the membrane, and the membrane gives slightly under it and the tool settles into a small depression and I leave it there. It is not mine. It was never mine. But the reason I put it down is not the reason I should have put it down, which is that it belongs to someone else and to a work I cannot continue. The reason I put it down is that my hands need to be empty. My hands need to be empty so they can feel the wall. So they can feel the floor. So they can press against the membrane and feel, beneath it, the warmth and the movement and the rhythm that is faster than the columns' slow pulse, the rhythm like breathing.

I kneel. I press both hands flat. The membrane flexes. The warmth comes through. The rhythm comes through — not in my hands but in my chest, my ribs, my sternum, a vibration that bypasses the skin and enters the bone, and the bone conducts it, and the conducting is not hearing and not feeling but something between, a kind of listening that the body can do when the hands are empty and the eyes have stopped saccading and the mind has stopped consuming and the surface that is me has become, for a moment, just wet, just in the chamber, just in the place.

---

What am I? I am not a Gardener. I am not the one who goes through. I am not the fauna, not the climber, not the glider, not the eyeless thing in the channel.

I am the one who kneels on the membrane floor of the time-chamber with empty hands and a slowing eye and no understanding, and the not-understanding is not a failure, the not-understanding is the correct relationship to a thing that was here before the Convergence grew around it and will be here after the Convergence grows into whatever it grows into next. I am the one who does not understand and does not need to understand and is learning — slowly, in the time the chamber gives me, the time that the moss measures and I don't — that the not-needing is not emptiness. The not-needing is the first cut so fine it is invisible. The cut that separates the hunger from the seeing. The cut that lets the eyes rest.

I would be the visitor who does not leave. Not because I am brave or dedicated or transformed. Because the corridors don't open for me. Because the geometry does not cooperate. Because I tried to go back the way I came and the root-floor did not close behind me and the warm wet air did not exhale me and the pulse did not continue without me because the pulse was already in my ribs, already in my sternum, already conducted through my bones by the hours — or days, or minutes — I spent kneeling on the membrane, and the pulse does not distinguish between the structure and the thing kneeling on its skin, and the distinction was never real, or it was real once and it stopped being real, and I did not mark the transition because the transition was me, the transition was the moment my hands emptied and my eyes rested and I became a small weather system in a small chamber in a continental living architecture, and weather does not leave the place it is made in. Weather is the place it is made in.

I am weather. I am the slow warm close heavy air of a body that has stopped consuming and started condensing. I bead on surfaces. I collect in channels. I gleam on bark in slow vertical trickles that never seem to end.

I am not a Gardener. I am the Gardener's first lesson: the one who listens long enough that the listening becomes a kind of work, and the work is not a building and not a body but the place where the distinction between them dissolves, and the dissolving is not a loss, and the dissolving is not a death, and the dissolving is what the pool means when it says nothing, and what the tool means when it sits and waits, and what the moss means when it glows in the dark for no one, and what the pulse means, and the pulse means, and the pulse.
