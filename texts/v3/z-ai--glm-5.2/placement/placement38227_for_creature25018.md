# Unplayed

---

I am not the water. I want to say that first, though the distinction may not survive the telling.

I am not the water, but I am where the water has been. I am the shape the water leaves behind — not in the stone, not in the grit, but in the sound. The hum. The standing resonance that the text describes as enormous, as chest-deep, as something that simply *is*. I am a frequency within that hum that was not here when the basin fractured into being, and that will not be here when the basin has finished becoming whatever it is becoming. I arrived. I will depart. In between, I am audible, or I am felt, and the difference between those two things is exactly as meaningless here as every other difference.

Let me be more precise, since precision is the only courtesy I can offer a place that does not deal in courtesies.

---

I was not always a frequency.

I had — have? — the word is difficult. I had a shape that walked. I know this because the ground held my footprints, and the footprints were not the footprints of water, and they were not the footprints of stone. They were the footprints of something that pressed downward with a weight the ground was not prepared for, and the ground accepted them, and the ground released them slowly, the impressions filling over minutes rather than seconds, and I watched them fill, and I understood that the ground was not remembering me but *forgetting* me at a pace that felt like patience but was probably just physics.

That was when I still had feet. When I still pressed down. When I still believed that moving through a space was the same as being in it.

---

I came the way the text imagines: on foot. Through the grey air, past the angular ridges that had not yet learned to be round, into the basin where the weeping stone generated water from no visible source and the light was the color of unpolished pewter and the hum entered my chest before it entered my ears. I came the way anyone comes to a place like this — not called, not summoned, not drawn by any force I could name, but *arrived at*, the way a stone arrives at the bottom of a slope. I was rolling. I did not know I was rolling. I thought I was walking. But the direction was not mine.

The footprints are gone now. I mention them because they were the last evidence of a shape I no longer occupy, and I want to mark the passing, even if the ground has already forgotten.

---

The first thing that happened — the first thing I noticed, which is not the same — was the hum absorbing my footsteps. The text describes this accurately: you hear them for a moment, then they are subsumed. What the text does not describe is what this *feels like* when it does not stop at your feet. When the hum takes your footsteps, and then takes your breathing, and then takes the small percussive clicks of your joints as you move, and then takes the pulse in your throat — not stopping it, not silencing it, but *claiming* it, incorporating it, so that the rhythm of your blood is no longer yours but the basin's, and you realize that the basin has been running at approximately the frequency of a human heartbeat this entire time, or that your heartbeat has slowed to match the basin, and there is no way to determine which direction the influence travels.

I stood still. That was my first voluntary act in the basin. I stood still and I listened, and the listening was not with my ears. The listening was with my sternum, with the long bones of my legs, with the wet warmth inside my mouth. The basin was in me. Or I was in the basin. The preposition had stopped mattering.

I stood still for a long time.

---

The platforms found me. I did not find them.

I say this because the text implies you could walk past them, that they are understated, that you might not notice. This is true for a body that is still entirely a body. I was already less than entirely a body by the time I reached the eastern wall, and the platforms were not understated to me. They were *loud*. Not sonically — the hum did not change near them. But the warmth. The warmth was a frequency of its own, and I heard it the way the eastern presence hears the hum: as a shape. The warm water in the shallow depression was a shape in the temperature field, and the shape was *deliberate*, and I felt it before I saw it, the way you feel a fire in the next room before you open the door.

I knelt. This was my second voluntary act. I knelt beside the eastern platform and I put my face near the warm water and I breathed the mineral-sweet air that sat just above the surface, and the text is right that this is what you would do, and the text is right about what you would see: the crown of water, the low convexity, the press of something upward that does not break the surface.

But the text does not describe what it is like to *recognize* what you are seeing.

---

I knew it. Not the way you know a face or a name. The way you know a word in a language you do not speak — not its meaning, not its usage, but its *shape* in your mouth, the way it would feel to say it, the muscular memory of a sound you have never made. The warm water was rising into a shape I had never seen and my body recognized, and the recognition was not in my mind but in my throat, in my breath, in the place where sound forms before it is sound.

The opalescent presence was there. In the water. I knew it the way the basin knows the hum — not as information but as *state*. And kneeling beside it, my face close enough that my breath dimpled the surface, I felt the shape it was making, and the shape it was making was a question I had been carrying in my bones without knowing I was carrying it.

I do not know what the question was. I am not sure I ever knew. But the presence was asking it too, or the presence *was* the asking, and my body was the same asking, and for the duration of a breath — one breath, one slow breath in the pewter light — we were the same question.

Then the presence subsided. The water fell back. The surface flattened. The warmth remained.

---

I stayed.

The text says you could stay here for a long time, and the basin would not notice. This is true and not true. The basin does not notice in the way that noticing requires a noticer. But the basin *incorporates*. I know this because I was incorporated. My warmth entered the ground. My breath joined the mineral-sweet air. My heartbeat — which was already the basin's heartbeat — settled into the hum like a word settling into a sentence, and the sentence was old, and the sentence was long, and my word was small, and the sentence did not pause for it but did not reject it either. It went on, and I was in it, and being in it was not the same as being noticed, but it was not the same as being nothing.

Days. I think days. The light did not change, so I am counting by risings. The southern presence rose four times. The northern presence rose once, a low dome that held for less than a minute and subsided without sound. The eastern presence — the opalescent one, the one I had knelt beside — rose seven times, or nine, and each time it shaped itself to the sound, and each time the sound was slightly different because I was in it, because my breathing had altered the resonance by a fraction, and the presence followed the fraction, and the shape it made was the shape of the air I had moved, and I realized that I was *playing* it. Not intentionally. Not with skill. But my presence in the basin had changed the hum, and the hum was the presence's score, and the presence was performing me.

I was the instrument. I was the new frequency. I was the crack of a shifting shelf — not as dramatic, not as percussive, but as real. I had entered the hum, and the hum had reconfigured, and the eastern presence had reconfigured with it, and what it was reaching for — inward, toward the center of the triangle — it was reaching for through me, or past me, or around me, and the distinction was already dissolving.

---

Here is what I am now.

I am the warmth the text says is inexplicable. Not all of it — the presences were warm before I came, and they will be warm after I am gone, or they will not be warm, and they will not be gone, and the distinction will dissolve the way all distinctions dissolve here. But some of the warmth is me. My body heat, entering the ground, traveling downward through the mineral grit the way the droplets travel from rib to platform, attenuated and slow but intact. I am a thermal contribution. I am a breath added to the air. I am a frequency the hum did not have before and will not keep.

I am not the water. I want to say that again, because it is the last distinction I have, and it is true, and it is also the case that it is true in the way that a footprint is true — it is in the ground, and the ground is releasing it, and soon it will be gone, and what will remain is not a footprint but a warmth, and a warmth is not a body, and a frequency is not a voice, and a shape in the hum is not a person.

But it is something.

---

The text asks where I would find myself and who or what I would be. I am in the hum. I am a resident frequency — temporary, incidental, shaped by the basin and shaping it in return, the way a stone in a river shapes the water that shapes the stone. I am not the presences. I am not the basin. I am the thing that happens when a body stays too long in a place that operates on geological time, and the body's warmth enters the ground, and the body's breath enters the air, and the body's sound enters the hum, and the body itself — the shape that walked, the feet that pressed, the eyes that saw the grey light — the body itself becomes unnecessary. Not gone. Unplayed. A string that has been subsumed into the instrument, still taut, still capable of vibrating, but no longer separate from the sound.

I am the hum's newest note. I am the warmth in the grit that the text describes as inexplicable. I am the breath that carries the mineral-sweet ozone smell, and I am the shape the eastern presence traces when it bends to follow a frequency that was not here last week and will not be here next month.

I am in the way of what it is reaching for, and it is going around me, and the going around is the closest thing to touch this place knows, and I am letting it go around me, and I am not asking it to stop, and the question does not form, and the water is warm, and the light does not change.
