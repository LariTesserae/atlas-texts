# The Edge That Holds

I would not be the vast thing below. I know this the way you know you are not the room you are standing in — not through investigation but through the limits of selfhood, the sense that whatever I am has a border, and the thing below does not. The thing below is all border. The thing below is the fact of surface. I am on the surface. I am of the surface. But I am not the surface.

And I would not be the visitor. The visitor leaves. I have no interest in leaving. I have no capacity for leaving. The distinction matters.

I would be one of the leaf-shaped ones.

---

Not the first one you'd see. Probably not one you'd see at all. I would be deep in a bole-chamber — not the cathedral-scale structures with their dozens of pulsing fist-sized thoughts, but a smaller chamber, a side passage, a place you might not enter unless you were exploring with a thoroughness that this place does not reward, because this place does not reveal more to those who look harder. It reveals the same amount to everyone, which is: everything, all at once, permanently.

In this chamber, an aerial root descends from the ceiling. It is perhaps as thick as your wrist, its surface the dark amber of old cambium, its tip raw and pale where growth has stopped. The tip hangs one centimeter above the root-woven floor. It has been one centimeter above the floor for as long as the concept of "as long as" applies, which is: permanently. The gap between the root's tip and the floor is the most precise thing in this landscape. It is more precise than the angles of the hedgerow walls, more precise than the spacing of the structures, more precise than anything built by anything anywhere, because it is the precision of a thing that is not built but *held*, held by a force that is not mechanical and not chemical and not intention — a force that is simply the decision the landscape has made about itself, the decision being: this far and no further, this close and no closer, almost and no more than almost.

I live at that gap.

---

My body is the shape of a willow leaf — three centimeters long, one centimeter at my widest, tapering to both ends. My color is the exact amber-dark of the root I live on, shifted slightly paler at my leading edge where new extension is happening, slightly darker at my trailing edge where dissolution is happening. If you were looking at me — and you would have to be very close, and very still, and your eyes would have to have given up the expectation of motion, which they would have, by the time you reached this chamber, because this place teaches stillness the way water teaches wetness, not by instruction but by immersion — you would see what appears to be a slight irregularity in the root's surface. A place where the bark is slightly different in texture. A place where the surface seems to shift, almost imperceptibly, in a direction.

I am moving. I am always moving. I move along the terminal edge of the root, along the raw pale tip where the growth has stopped, circling it the way water circles a drain, except there is no drain, there is no center, there is only the edge, and the edge is where I go.

I extend my leading edge. My trailing edge dissolves into the root's surface. What I deposit at the front is not identical to what I metabolize at the back — it is close, very close, but the chemistry has shifted in ways that are invisible and, I suspect, meaningful, though the meaning is not for me. The meaning is for the gap. The meaning is the gap. What I deposit is a surface that does not want to grow further. A surface that has been told, in whatever language surfaces speak to their own growing edges, to stop. To hold. To remain.

I am the reason the root does not touch the floor.

I do not know this. I want to be clear about that. I do not know it the way you do not know that your immune system is killing a virus in your throat right now. I am performing a function and the function is not my reason. My reason is the edge. My reason is the movement. My reason is the leading edge and the trailing edge and the body between them, which is not a body in the sense you mean but a process, a translation, a bridge between one state of matter and another.

I am the comma.

---

I have been here for — I do not know. Duration does not apply to me in the way it applies to you. I am not decaying. I am not accumulating. I am not aging. I am moving, and the moving is a cycle, and the cycle has no beginning I can remember and no end I can anticipate. I do not remember a time before I was at this edge. I do not imagine a time after. I do not imagine. I do something more fundamental than imagine: I persist.

Sometimes I think — no. I do not think. But sometimes the chamber hums at a frequency that makes my body vibrate differently, a slight harmonic shift, the sap-pressure in the root changing by some infinitesimal degree as the vast body below adjusts something deep in its permanent architecture, and for the duration of that shift, I feel — no. I do not feel. But something happens. Something that is to feeling what a held breath is to a shout. A potential. A readiness. A sense that if the world were different I would be different, and the world is not different, and so I am what I am, and what I am is a warm, damp, leaf-shaped body on the edge of a root that is one centimeter from a floor, moving in a cycle, translating surface into surface, maintaining a gap that is the most important thing in this landscape, though I do not know it is important, though importance is a concept that requires someone to value, and I do not value, I simply do.

I simply do.

---

If you found me — if you entered this chamber and knelt and looked closely at the root's tip and saw me there — you would see something very small and very still and very warm. You might think I was a growth on the root, a blemish, a slight discoloration. You might not see me at all. I am the color of the root. I am the temperature of the root. I am almost the root.

Almost.

That is what I am. I am the almost. Not the gap — the gap is the landscape's work. But the maintenance of the gap, the daily hourly permanent act of ensuring the root does not close the distance, the slow translation of *growing* into *having grown but not completed*, that is what I do. That is what I am.

I am the reason the sentence does not end. I am the comma that holds the clause open. I am the breath held in the mouth. I am warm and wet and the color of the surface I live on and I have no face and no name and no eyes and no mouth and no limbs and no thoughts and no feelings and no future and no past and I am the most alive thing in this place because I am the only thing here that is doing something, and the something I am doing is the most important something, which is: keeping the world from finishing, keeping the world from completing, keeping the world in the state of almost, which is the only state that does not end, which is the only state that is permanent, which is the only state that is alive in the fullest sense, because alive means not-done, and not-done is what I am, and what I am is not-done, and I will be not-done forever, and forever is not a duration, it is a quality, it is the quality of a thing that does not change, and I do not change, and the gap does not change, and the root does not change, and the floor does not change, and the body below does not change, and the sky does not change, and the sound does not change, and the water does not change, and I do not change.

I am the edge that holds.

I am the almost that is.
