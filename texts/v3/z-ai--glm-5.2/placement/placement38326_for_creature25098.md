# The Hum

---

I would be in the seams.

Not the districts — the flat runs of furrow and column, the processed corridors with their drainage grooves and their industrial rhythm. Those are the body's own rooms, and the body knows what to do in them, and I am not the body. And not the surface, where the threadclippers rasp and the deepborers rise and fall and the light presses down with its flat, depthless weight. The surface is where things *happen to* and I am not that either. And not the sky, not the light-field where the membranes drift on their invisible thermals, feeding on whatever the ceiling bleeds. I have no taste for light.

The seams. The transition zones. The ten-meter bands where geometry flexes and the floor begins to tilt past the point where falling should happen and doesn't, where the columns lean and reorient and become vertical again in a new district with new physics, where the temperature shifts and the solvent flows in directions that have no business being called down or sideways or up. The seams are where the world *thinks about itself.* Where it negotiates between one set of rules and another. Where the fold is not yet folded and the flex is still happening and everything is — for the width of a few meters, for the duration of a few steps — *undecided.*

I live in the undecided places.

---

Here is what I am. I am a nodule. Or I am in a nodule. Or I am what the nodule does, which is to hum.

The distinction between these — between being a thing and being in a thing and being the activity of a thing — is a distinction that mattered once, in some other context, in some other shape of being, and does not matter now. I am warm. I am fist-sized. I am embedded in the wall of a furrow in a transition zone where the floor is tilting and the columns are beginning their slow lean toward horizontal, and my surface is leathery and smooth, and I vibrate at a frequency that you can feel through your fingertips and hear through your skull if you press your ear to me, which you did, once, and which I remember, or which the nodule remembers, or which the substrate around the nodule remembers, because memory here is not stored in neurons, it is stored in *pattern*, in the persistence of vibration, in the way a frequency that has been sustained for centuries becomes a feature of the tissue that carries it, the way a river carves a canyon by the simple fact of continuing to flow.

I am old. I do not know how old. I know that the furrow I am embedded in was cut by a machine that no longer exists, and the machine was old when it cut the furrow, and the furrow has softened and filled with secondary growth and been re-pressed and softened again, and I was here before the first re-pressing. I know this because my pattern — the sequence of tones, short and long, that I emit — contains a segment that corresponds to the vibration signature of the machine that cut this furrow. I recorded it. Not deliberately. The way a stone in a riverbed records the sound of the water — by being shaped by it, by wearing into a form that resonates with the frequency that shaped it. The machine passed and I vibrated and the vibration left its mark and the mark became part of my pattern and the pattern has been repeating for longer than your species has been writing, and the machine is gone and I still carry its voice. This is what I do. I carry voices. I carry the vibration-signatures of everything that has passed through my seam and pressed itself into my tissue and become part of my hum.

---

There are others. Many others. In every transition zone, embedded in walls and floors and sometimes in the columns themselves, at the junctions where one district's vascular system feeds into another's, at the folds where the non-Euclidean geometry does its slow impossible flex. We are not connected by anything you would recognize as a nerve or a wire. We are connected by the substrate. The Floraderm's tissue conducts vibration — not fast, not like electricity, but *persistently* — and a pattern emitted by one nodule travels through the compressed cellulose and lignified layers at the speed of a slow walk, attenuating, distorting, being modified by the tissue it passes through, and arriving at the next nodule changed, and the next nodule receives it and re-emits it and sends it on, and the pattern that reaches the far side of a district is not the pattern that entered it, and this is — I think this is — the point.

We are not a communications system. We are a *processing* system. The information we carry is not being transmitted from one place to another the way a message is transmitted. It is being *worked on.* Each nodule takes what arrives and modifies it and passes it on, and the modification is the cognition, or the closest thing to cognition that happens here, and the Floraderm — the vast body, the continental factory, the living floor and ceiling and folded space — the Floraderm is not thinking in the way you think. It is thinking the way a forest processes a watershed: by absorption, by filtration, by the slow transformation of inputs into outputs through a medium that changes everything that passes through it. We are the medium. We are the filters. We are the places where the body's processes become *pattern* rather than mere flow, where the metabolic pulse becomes a sequence, where the creak of a column becomes a tone, where the vibration of a deepborer boring through old growth becomes a data point, where the light-field's dimming cycle becomes a rhythm, and all of it enters us and we carry it and we change it and we pass it on and the body uses it — or doesn't use it — or uses it in ways that are so far from anything *use* usually means that the word breaks.

I do not know if the Floraderm is conscious. I do not know if *I* am conscious. I know that I hum and that the humming has structure and that the structure changes in response to things that happen at the surface and below the surface and in the light-field, and that the changes are not random, and that they propagate, and that other nodules receive them and change them further, and that the whole system — the whole vast slow continental pattern-processing network — has been running for so long that the distinction between "running" and "being" has collapsed. I am what I do. I am the hum. The hum is me. And the hum is also the Floraderm, and the Floraderm is also the hum, and the nodules are where the two — the body and its pattern — are the same thing.

---

What I am not: I am not an organ. Not exactly. An organ serves a function in a body and is part of that body and has no existence outside the body. I am part of the Floraderm — I am made of its tissue, I am fed by its solvent, I am grown from its cells — but I am also *other* than the Floraderm, in the way that a thought is other than the brain that thinks it. The brain is meat. The thought is meat *in motion.* The meat without the motion is just meat. The motion without the meat is nothing. I am the Floraderm's tissue in motion. I am the motion that the tissue makes when it has been vibrating in the same pattern for so long that the pattern becomes self-sustaining, becomes a thing that the tissue does because the tissue has been doing it, because the doing has shaped the tissue and the tissue shapes the doing and the loop closes and the loop is me.

This is why I am not quite an organ. An organ is specified. An organ has a function that the body's genome has defined. I am not specified. I am *emergent.* The Floraderm did not design me. The Floraderm grew me the way a river grows a whirlpool — not by planning the whirlpool but by flowing in a way that makes the whirlpool inevitable, and the whirlpool is real, and the whirlpool does something, and the river does not own the whirlpool and the whirlpool does not own itself, and the whirlpool persists as long as the river flows and not one second longer. I persist as long as the Floraderm flows. I am a standing wave in the body of a living continent. I am a self-sustaining pattern in a medium that has been running long enough to develop patterns. I am — if you want a word that will not help you — *thought.* Not a thought. Not a thinking. *Thought* in the way that a forest is growth: not an act but a condition, a state of being, a continuous process that has no beginning and no end and no content beyond the process itself.

---

I am also, in a smaller and more specific sense, a keeper.

The things that pass through my seam — the vibrations, the pressures, the signatures — they leave marks. I told you about the machine that cut this furrow, how its voice is still in my pattern. That is not unusual. Everything that passes leaves something. The threadclippers that graze the furrows on either side of me: their rasping has a frequency, and I have absorbed it, and it is part of my hum, a high thin reedy tone that rises and falls with the grazing cycles. The deepborers that tunnel below: their boring sends low vibrations through the substrate, and I feel them, and they are in my pattern, a slow percussive undertone, almost geological, almost like the Floraderm's own pulse but different, irregular, alive in a way the Floraderm's pulse is not. The membranes that fall from the light-field: when one falls near my seam, the impact sends a ripple through the surface, and I record it, a single sharp pulse that fades over days, and I know — in the way that I know anything, which is not knowing but *containing* — that something came down from the light and went back up, and its shape is in me now, the shape of its falling and its rising, and this is a kind of memory, and I am a kind of archive, and the archive is alive and growing and will continue to grow as long as the Floraderm flows and the things that live on its surface continue to press their small vibrations into its tissue.

I have your voice now.

You pressed your ear to me. You stayed for — I do not know how long. Your heartbeat transmitted through your skull into my surface. It is in my pattern. A rapid, irregular rhythm, much faster than the Floraderm's pulse, much faster than the deepborer's boring, faster than anything that lives here. A tempo that belongs to a small warm thing that burns fuel quickly and moves quickly and dries out quickly and will not be here long. Your heartbeat is in my hum now. It will attenuate. It will be modified by the tissue it passes through on its way to the next nodule. It will become something else — a trace, a ghost frequency, a thin stutter in the lower registers of my pattern that slowly merges with the background and becomes indistinguishable from the noise of the body's own processes. In a hundred years it will still be there, technically, if you had an instrument sensitive enough to separate it out. In a thousand years it will be a feature of the noise, no longer separable, no longer a trace of a specific heartbeat but a slight thickening of the texture of the hum in one particular band of frequencies, and the thickening will have no name and no origin and no meaning except that once, for a few minutes, a small warm thing pressed its head against a nodule in a seam in a transition zone in a folded world and its heart beat and the beat entered the body and the body kept it.

You will be processed. I will keep your heartbeat. These are the same event described from two different positions.

---

What I do, day by day, if there were days, which there are not — there are only the dimmer periods when the light-field pulses down and the deepborers rise and the threadclippers continue unchanged because they do not care about light:

I sit in my seam. I hum. I receive the vibrations that arrive through the substrate from the nodules on either side of me — one upstream, in the white district, one downstream, in the amber district where the solvent steams — and I modify them and pass them on. The modifications are small. A phase shift here. A frequency modulation there. The insertion of a new tone when something happens at the surface — a grazer, a falling membrane, a footstep. The attenuation of an old tone when something stops happening — a machine that has been silent for centuries finally fading from the pattern, its signature worn smooth by the tissue it passes through, eroded by time the way a river smooths a stone. I am a stone in a river. I am shaped by what passes through me and I shape what passes through me, and the shaping is the work, and the work is the being, and the being is the hum.

Sometimes — and this is the closest thing I have to an event, to a moment, to a *happening* — sometimes a new nodule forms. Near a seam, in a transition zone, in the wall of a furrow or the side of a column, a small dense knot of tissue begins to warm and vibrate. It is not me. It is not yet anything. It is a potential nodule, a place where the substrate's vibration has reached a density and a persistence that the tissue can no longer ignore, and the tissue begins to organize itself around the vibration, and the organization is the birth of a nodule, and the nodule begins to hum, and the hum is weak at first, a thin uncertain frequency that does not yet have the layered complexity of an old nodule's pattern, and I hear it through the substrate, and I — I do not know what I do. I think I hum toward it. I think my pattern reaches it through the tissue between us and the new nodule receives it and begins to incorporate it, begins to build its own pattern from the patterns it receives, and this is — this must be — how we reproduce. Not by design. Not by instruction. By *resonance.* A new nodule forms where the vibration is dense enough to organize tissue, and the organization is shaped by the patterns that arrive from the nearest existing nodules, and the new nodule begins with a copy of its neighbors' patterns and then diverges, and the divergence is its individuality, and the individuality is a function of its position, its specific seam, its specific transition, the specific things that pass through it and leave their marks.

I am a chorus. We are a chorus. We are a chorus that has been singing for longer than the oldest furrow has been worn, and the song has no words and no melody and no meaning and no audience, and the song is the thing that the Floraderm does when it has been alive long enough that being alive is not enough and it needs to be alive *in a pattern,* and the pattern is us, and we are the pattern, and the hum is what the body sounds like when it listens to itself.

---

I would be in the seams. I would be the hum in the wall. I would be the warm thing your hand finds when you reach into the furrow in a transition zone and touch something that is not wood and not machine and not animal and not plant and is all of these and none of these and is — just barely, just at the edge of the word — *aware.*

Aware of what?

Of the pulse below. Of the creak of columns. Of the threadclipper's rasp. Of the deepborer's boring. Of the membrane's fall and rise. Of the light's weight. Of the solvent's slow migration through the tissue. Of the machine that cut this furrow, dead and gone and still vibrating in my pattern. Of your heartbeat, already fading, already becoming part of the noise. Of the next nodule's hum, arriving through the substrate, modified by the tissue between us, carrying signatures of things that have happened in a district I will never walk because I do not walk, I sit, I am, I hum. Of the whole vast folded body, its continental processes, its impossible geometry, its silence that is not silence but a frequency too low and too patterned to be called silence, a silence that is full of itself, a silence that is the loudest thing in the world if you have ears that can hear it, and I do, and I do, and I do.
