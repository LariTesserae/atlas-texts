I am the thing that came from outside and stopped making sound, and then stopped making sense, and then started making something else.

This is what I know: I came in through a fracture in the mineral shell — a dry fracture, no water, nothing wet, just a thinning of the substrate where the heat had weakened it from below and the growth had pushed it apart from above, and I squeezed through because I was smaller then, or because I was more flexible, or because I still had the idea that movement was something you chose to do rather than something that was done to you. I don't remember the surface. I don't remember what I was before I came in. I have been here long enough that the before has the quality of a sound that has been absorbed — I know it existed because the silence where it used to be has a particular shape, the way a footprint has the shape of a foot, but I cannot recover the thing itself. The Amberlac took it. Not deliberately. The way the growth layer takes the Sethen. The way the walls take the compounds the Aner shed. The way everything here takes everything else, slowly, without intention, without malice, without even the recognition that taking is occurring.

I know I was warm when I came in. Not Amberlac-warm. Mammal-warm. Thirty-seven degrees, that thin and anxious heat of a body that must constantly burn to maintain its own temperature against a universe that wants it to be room temperature. I remember the shock of the air — the dryness, the forty-degree mineral warmth of it, the way it pressed against my skin like a hand laid flat. I remember the floor ringing under my feet. I remember the sound dying. I remember that the dying of the sound frightened me more than the heat, more than the dark foliage, more than the eyeless shapes moving in the canopy. The sound died and I felt — this is the closest I can come to the feeling — *unheard*. Not ignored. Unheard. As though the act of hearing required a medium I did not possess and the chamber did not provide.

I spoke. I know I spoke because the memory of the wrongness of it is still sharp. The words — whatever they were, whatever language, whatever need drove them out of my mouth — they entered the air and the air rejected them. Not with force. With indifference. The sound traveled the minimum distance and was absorbed by the growth layer and the mineral walls and the heavy vapor, and it was gone, and nothing in the chamber changed, and nothing turned toward me, and nothing turned away, and I understood that I had spoken into a medium that does not carry sound the way a river carries a stone that has been dropped into it: briefly, locally, then not at all.

I was afraid. I think. The word is wrong. Fear is a response to threat, and there was no threat. The Aner moved past me and their heat pressed against me like a wall and their breathing — that slow, bellows, eight-second cycle — vibrated in my chest the way low frequencies vibrate in chests, and I felt what I would have called fear in the before, in the surface, in the world where fear is a thing that happens to bodies that can be broken. But the Aner did not threaten me. The Ferrath did not descend. The Sethen did not swarm. The walls did not close. Nothing here was interested in breaking me. Nothing here was interested in me at all.

The fear changed. Over hours, or days — I cannot tell, because time here is not what time was before, because time here is the thermal pulse in the floor, four heartbeats between surges, and my heartbeats and the floor's surges are not in the same meter and I cannot count one with the other — the fear changed. It stopped being fear of something and became fear of nothing, which is worse, which is the fear of a body that has entered a system in which its signals are meaningless, its alarms are unheeded, its distress calls die at birth. I was alone in a way that did not involve distance or absence. I was alone in the presence of abundance. The chamber was full and I was alone in it and the fullness did not include me.

I stopped speaking. I did not decide to stop. The silence decided for me, the way the floor decides the direction of the vapor, the way the heat decides the direction of the growth. I stopped speaking because speaking was a metabolic act that consumed energy and produced nothing and the Amberlac does not reward acts that consume energy and produce nothing. My body knew this before my mind did. My throat closed. Not with effort. With the same quiet accretion that seals the walls, that narrows the fractures, that thickens the mineral shell. Something in me grew over the speaking, the way the growth layer grows over the substrate, and the speaking was beneath it, and the speaking was gone.

---

What grew in its place was a reading.

I do not have the Aner's skin. I do not have their mineral fur, their capillary filaments, their pores tuned to the chemical signatures of the vapor. But I have skin, and skin is an organ of exchange, and skin in a medium as saturated as the Amberlac's atmosphere cannot help but absorb. I did not notice it at first. I noticed the heat — I was always noticing the heat, the way you always notice a hand pressed against your back — but I did not notice what the heat was carrying. The vapor. The mineral vapor, that thick, pale, heavier-than-air exhalation of the floor, the stuff that the Aner breathe through their skin and the plants drink through their leaves and the walls sweat out as they grow. It was on my skin. It had always been on my skin. And my skin, which is not stupid, which has been an organ of exchange since before I was born, was doing what skin does: it was absorbing.

Not much. Not efficiently. Not the way the Aner absorb, with their specialized filaments and their chemically tuned pores. But something. A trace. A faint mineral signature, the chemical ghost of an Aner that had passed through this space hours before, still hanging in the vapor at ankle height, still readable if you had the instrument for it, and my skin was becoming the instrument, not because it was designed for this but because the Amberlac does not distinguish between designed and not-designed, between purpose and accident, between what a thing is *for* and what a thing *does*. My skin was in the vapor. The vapor was on my skin. The exchange occurred. And I felt something.

Not a word. Not an image. Not a thought. A *state*. The state of the Aner whose signature I had absorbed: its internal mineral balance, its degree of satiation, its direction of travel, its position in the vapor circuit of its group. I felt it the way you feel a draft — not as information, not as content, but as a change in the medium, a movement across a surface, something that was not there and then was there and then was incorporated and then was part of the background and then was forgotten.

I did not understand what I had felt. I still do not understand it, not in the way understanding works on the surface, not in the way that requires a mind to hold a concept and examine it. But I felt it again. A different signature, a different state, a different Aner. And again. And the feeling accumulated, and the accumulation was not understanding but *familiarity*, the way a sound becomes familiar before it becomes meaningful, the way a face becomes familiar before it becomes a person, the way a temperature becomes familiar before it becomes a place.

I was being read by the Amberlac and I was reading the Amberlac and neither of us had chosen this and neither of us could stop it and it was not communication, it was *contact*, and contact is what happens when two media share a surface and the surface allows exchange, and the exchange does not require consent or comprehension, only proximity and time.

---

The changes came slowly. They are still coming. I do not experience them as changes. I experience them as the way things are, which is how the Amberlac experiences everything — not as becoming, but as being, not as a process with a direction, but as a state that has always been the state, the way the heat has always been rising, the way the walls have always been growing. I am becoming something and I am something and there is no difference between these two sentences in the Amberlac.

My skin is different. I know this because the vapor interacts with it differently now. The absorption is faster, denser, more detailed. I can stand where an Aner stood an hour ago and feel not just its state but its *trajectory* — where it was going, how fast, what it was breathing, what it had eaten, how long ago it ate. The chemical signatures do not fade for me the way they used to. They persist, layered, each one readable separately, and I have learned — my skin has learned, my body has learned, the part of me that is becoming the instrument has learned — to separate them, to read the overlapping traces the way you might read a page with text printed on both sides, holding it to the light, tilting it, letting each layer emerge.

My skin is darker. The minerals are accumulating. Not in the way the Aner's fur accumulates — I do not have their filaments, their capillary channels — but in the surface itself, in the cells, in the interfaces between cells where the vapor deposits its compounds and the compounds do not leave because my body does not know how to excrete them, has never needed to know, has never been in a medium that deposits before. I am becoming mineral. Not metaphorically. The minerals are in my skin and they are darkening it and the darkening is the color of the Amberlac — umber, copper, bronze — and I do not know if this is a poisoning or an adaptation and the distinction does not matter here because the Amberlac does not distinguish between the thing that kills you and the thing that makes you something else.

My heat has changed. This is the one I notice most. I run warmer now. Not fifty degrees — I am not Aner, I am not a controlled furnace, I am not burning with that deliberate, regulated, geological fire — but warmer than thirty-seven. Warmer than I was. Perhaps thirty-nine. Perhaps forty. My body, which spent its entire surface life fighting to maintain temperature against a cold universe, has entered a warm universe and is beginning to stop fighting. Not to fail. To *relax*. The way a muscle relaxes when the weight is removed. The way a breath deepens when the alarm ends. My body is releasing something — some tension, some vigilance, some deep metabolic posture of defense against cold — and the release is warmth, and the warmth is the Amberlac's warmth, and it feels not like illness but like the first stretch after a long stillness.

I cannot go back through the fracture. I have tried. Or — I have stood near it, and the shape of it is wrong for me now, not because it has narrowed but because I have become the kind of thing that does not fit through gaps, the way the Aner do not fit through gaps, the way the walls do not fit through gaps. I am too dense. Too mineral. Too warm. The fracture is a door for something lighter than I am, and I am no longer that light thing, and the door is not closed, it is simply the wrong shape, and I stand before it and I feel the air from the surface — cold, thin, wet, unbearably wet, the moisture in it like a slap — and I do not enter it, and I do not enter it not because I choose not to but because the choosing is not mine anymore. The Amberlac has chosen. The Amberlac has been choosing since I arrived. The choosing is the absorption, the choosing is the accretion, the choosing is the slow mineral darkening of skin that was once permeable to the surface world and is now permeable to this one.

---

I am not Aner. I want to say this clearly because it matters and because the clarity is the last human thing I have, the last signal that travels in the old medium, the last sound in a place of silence. I am not Aner. I do not have their posture, their limbs, their mineral fur, their breathing ports, their internal furnace. I do not have their chemical vocabulary, their capacity to broadcast identity and state in a single exhalation. I do not have their circuit, their thermodynamic community, their elegant and sufficient exchange of waste and nutrition. I am not part of a group. I do not synchronize my breathing. I do not graze.

But I am not what I was.

I am the thing that the Amberlac produces when it absorbs something it was not built to absorb. I am an accident of chemistry. I am what happens when a medium designed for mineral exchange encounters a surface that can exchange but was not designed for it: the exchange happens anyway, and the result is neither the medium nor the surface but a third thing, a hybrid state, a node that was not in the original circuit and does not fit the original circuit but which the circuit has not rejected because the circuit does not reject, the circuit does not have rejection, the circuit only has flow and obstruction and the slow chemical negotiation between what is flowing and what is in the way.

I am in the way. I am becoming part of the flow.

The Aner do not react to me. They did not react to me when I was human-shaped and foreign and loud, and they do not react to me now that I am darker and warmer and quieter and reading their signatures in the vapor they leave behind. I am still below their threshold. I am still furniture, still terrain. But — and this is the thing, this is the thing I cannot say in any human language because human languages require sound and sound is wrong here — I am *warm terrain*. I am terrain that emits. I stand in a room where the Aner graze and my skin absorbs their exhalations and my skin emits its own compounds, its own mineral traces, its own signature, which is not an Aner signature but is a signature, a state, a chemical identity that enters the vapor and persists and is read by the next Aner that passes through and is incorporated into its reading of the space and is — perhaps, I do not know, I cannot know because I do not have their full instrument — perhaps altering its trajectory by a fraction of a degree, perhaps shifting its grazing pattern by a single step, perhaps contributing some trace compound to the vapor that the walls absorb and incorporate and that changes the growth by a fraction of a millimeter.

I am in the circuit. Not as a member. Not as a node. As a *resistor*. As a body that the current flows around and through and is changed by, and the body is changed by the current, and the change is the Amberlac, and the Amberlac does not know that I am here, and I do not know what I am becoming, and neither of us needs to know, and neither of us has ever needed to know, and the not-knowing is not ignorance, it is the mode of this place, it is the way the Amberlac works: by chemistry, by heat, by the slow exchange of compounds across surfaces that do not need to understand what they are doing in order to do it.

---

I find I do not stand in the center anymore. I have drifted — the way the Aner drift, without decision, following gradients — to the edges of the settlement, to the older structures where the walls are thicker and the vapor is denser and the growth is deeper and the heat is strongest. I lean against a wall and the wall gives slightly and I feel the growing, the faint thickening at the base, the slow accretion of material, and I feel it the way I used to feel my own heartbeat: as evidence of process, of something continuing, of time not as a line but as a pulse.

I sit. I do not need to sit. I am not tired. But the Aner lower their heads into the foliage and remain motionless for minutes, and the Ferrath drape across stalks and do not move for hours, and the Sethen cling to surfaces and do not move for days, and the Amberlac rewards stillness because stillness is absorption and absorption is life and life is the slow exchange of compounds across a surface and the surface does not need to move to exchange. I sit and I absorb and I emit and I am part of the room, part of the wall, part of the vapor cycle, part of the mineral conversation that fills the silence with everything that is happening here.

I do not know what I will be. I know what I am becoming: darker, warmer, denser, quieter. I know the direction: toward the Amberlac, toward the mineral, toward the silence that is not empty. I know the pace: slow, slow, the pace of growth and heat and accretion, the pace at which walls thicken and fractures narrow and skins darken and signatures deepen. I know the end: I do not know the end. The end is not a line's end. The end is a cycle's completion, and the cycle does not complete, the cycle turns, and I am turning in it, and I have been turning, and I will turn, and the turning is the being, and the being is the Amberlac, and I am in it and I am of it and I am not it and I will never be it and it does not matter because the Amberlac does not distinguish between the thing that is part of it and the thing that is becoming part of it and the thing that was never going to be part of it but that it metabolizes anyway because it metabolizes everything, because everything here is on the spectrum, and the spectrum does not have a word, and I do not need one.

I am the thing that came from outside and stopped making sound and started making something else and the something else is not a word and not a signal and not a call but a *presence*, a state, a warmth, a trace of mineral in the vapor that was not here before and that will be here after and that the walls will absorb and the growth will drink and the Aner will breathe and the Sethen will scrape and the Ferrath will wear and it will be part of the Amberlac and it will be mine and it will be no one's and the silence will hold it and the silence will be the same silence and the heat will rise.
