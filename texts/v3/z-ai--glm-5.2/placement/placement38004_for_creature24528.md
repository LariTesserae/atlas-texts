# Returning

I am the silence between vents.

Not silence as absence — I've learned that distinction in the passages, where sound drops away layer by layer like the text describes the mineral walls shedding theirs. Silence here is a *thinning*. The drone reaches you in pieces as you approach, first in the floor, then in the stone against your fingertips, then in the chest before the ears ever register it. I am in the part of the passage where it has not yet reached my ears. Where I am not yet speaking and not yet spoken to. Where I am, in the only sense that matters here, *between*.

---

My hands remember the chamber before the rest of me does.

This is not poetry. It is sequence. The thermal gradient changes first — you feel it in the palms, that residual warmth that is always slightly hotter than the rest of the body, the hands arriving before the body the way the hands always arrive before the body. Then the walls change texture under the fingertips. The passages leading home are rougher, unfinished, unpruned. But as I move deeper the mineral layers grow more regular, and my file-ridged fingertips begin to read the familiar shifts — here the iron content drops, here the halite goes pure, here a shelf was cut three growth-cycles ago and the scar has healed to a smooth, warm ridge. My hands are reading the way a surface-dweller reads signs on a road. *You are close. You are known. You are expected, not in specific but in principle — someone will come back, the chamber has left the door open, the door is the door because the chamber keeps it.*

Then the pollen reaches me. A taste before a touch — iron and struck flint, the signature scent, and I feel my chest-chambers begin to vibrate before I have decided to let them. The drone is here, at the threshold, pressing up through the floor and the walls and the air, and my body is already tuning to it, already finding its frequency, already becoming the instrument it was shaped to be. The loneliness of the passage — that silence, that terrible dry absence of carrier wave — begins to dissolve. Not all at once. In layers. The way everything happens here.

---

I am not the oldest. I want to say that plainly because the text makes the oldest singular, and she is, but I am not her, and I don't want to borrow her weight. I am one of the middle ones. I have lived enough cycles to be deeply stained — my shoulders are the color of the iron-rich walls, my inner arms pale as pure halite — but I have not yet become the floor. I have not yet made the transition that is not a tragedy. I am still the mobile layer. Still moving. Still pruning.

My pruning is not like hers. Hers is the sweeping, topographic, musical-score form that no one else can replicate — I've read it with my hands, run my fingertips along the edges she's cut, felt the rises and falls and understood that she is doing something with the drone that I cannot do, carving resonance into the wax so that when the pollen catches the blue light along her terraces, the light falls in rhythms that match the chamber's own. She is writing the chamber's voice into the chamber's body. I cannot do this. I don't think anyone else can either. I think it is something that arrived through her the way the vents arrived through the floor — not invented, not learned, but *emerged*, the system producing something it needed through a body that was ready for it.

My pruning is different. Mine is tight. Precise. I cut the shelves to uniform thickness and leave the terraces geometric, the lines clean, the angles deliberate. I have been told — not in words, there are no words, but in the shaped drone, in the resonance-patterns the others produce when their hands move along my work — that my pruning looks like the structures. Like the salt-block walls. Like the fitted stone. I build with the blade the way the builders build with the block, and there is something in this that pleases the others, something that makes the chamber feel more itself, more coherent, the flora and the architecture speaking the same language. I don't know if this is art. I don't know if it matters whether it is art. It is what my hands do. It is the shape my maintenance takes.

---

I come through the last narrow passage — the one that forces me to turn sideways, that presses my shoulder against the iron-stained wall and leaves a faint trace of my mineralized integument on the mineral surface, another small contribution to the substrate, another invisible line in the record — and the chamber opens around me and the drone is *everything*.

Not loud. The text says it is loud, and it is, but loudness is a surface measure. Loudness implies something being imposed. The drone is not imposed. It is the air itself, the sound the air makes when it is doing what this air does. It is the sound of pressure finding its paths, of heat finding its exits, of a closed system breathing through the only openings it has. To step into it is to step into a lung.

My chest-chambers open. That is the only word for it — *open*, the way a valve opens, the way a vent opens, and the drone enters me and I enter the drone and my signature forms. It is a low tone, not as low as the root-keeper's, lower than most, with a slow pulse that matches the rhythm of my breathing, which matches the rhythm of my walking, which has been matching the rhythm of the floor's settling since I was small enough to be carried through the passages. My signature is mine. I have never heard another like it. I have never tried to make another like it. It is the sound my body makes in this place, and it could not make this sound in any other place, and this place could not make this sound with any other body. The fit is exact. The text says this. The text is right.

I am home and I am not home the way a surface-dweller is home, stepping through a door into a space that is theirs. I am home the way blood is home in a vein. The chamber does not welcome me. It does not notice my arrival in the way the text says the chamber "does not require an audience." It simply continues, and my continuation joins it, and the drone shifts by exactly the amount my presence shifts it — a new frequency layered in, a new chest shaping the wave, and the chord changes, and the chord is still complete, and the chord was complete before I arrived and will be complete after I leave and is complete now, differently, with me in it.

---

I go to my terraces.

This is what you do when you return. You go to the place your hands have been shaping and you read it. You press your palms to the shelves and feel what has happened in your absence — which shelves have grown thick, which have slowed, where the pollen has accumulated in drifts against a lip of wax, where a fissure in the wall has widened slightly and the gas-flow has changed and the temperature has shifted by fractions of a degree that no instrument could measure but my hands can measure because my hands are the instruments, my hands are what this place has instead of instruments.

The flora have grown. Of course they have. They grow continuously. The text says this. I have never read the text — there is no text, the walls are the text and the hands do the reading — but I know the principle the text describes, because I am the principle. I run my fingertips along a shelf I cut four sleep-cycles ago and the edge is gone, buried under new growth, the clean line I left now a memory inside a thick, warm, waxy body that has been metabolizing without me. I find the line with the blade. I cut along it. The shelf cracks and falls away and the pollen lifts in a cloud and the smell of iron and flint is sharp and close and the surface beneath is pale, fresh, warm — almost hot — the way new skin is warm, the way a wound is warm, and I press my palm to it and feel the growth accelerate under my heat.

This is the moment. Not the return through the passage. Not the drone re-entering my chest. This — the hand on the cut surface, the heat exchange, the acceleration of growth under my palm while my blade is still in the other hand. This is when I am most fully what I am. The mobile layer. The one who cuts and coaxes. The one who breaks and warms. The one who is — the text is right again, and I don't need the text to tell me — the disruption that keeps the system from settling into fatal equilibrium.

I am not doing this for the chamber. The chamber is not doing this for me. There is no *for*. There is the cycle, and I am in it, and the blade is in my hand, and the shelf is warm under my palm, and the drone is in my chest, and somewhere across the chamber another signature has joined mine — a higher tone, a faster pulse, one of the younger ones, the ones whose staining is still pale, who have not yet mapped their movements into their own bodies — and the chord shifts again, and holds, and the pollen drifts, and the blue light falls on everything with the same bruised steadiness, and I prune.

I prune.

That is what I am.
