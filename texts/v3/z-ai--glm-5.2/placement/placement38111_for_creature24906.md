I am not a grazer. I am not a vibrator. I am not an Other.

I am what happens when all three are present in the same square kilometer and the geometry flexes and the light hesitates at the precise moment a cluster goes silent.

I am the ten seconds.

---

I did not begin. I do not remember a first moment, because the first moment was not a moment — it was a gradient, the same kind of gradient that the Others are made of, a slow thickening of almost-something into something-that-might-count, and the transition between the two states has no edge, no threshold, no point where I can say *here is where I was not and there is where I was*. I came into the way a patina comes. The way oxidation comes. Not an event but a rate.

What I can say is this: there was a silence — one of the rare silences, the ten-second completions, when an entire cluster of vibrators stops simultaneously and the air goes hollow and the bellowing of the grazers suddenly sounds orphaned, suddenly sounds like it was always supposed to have the clicking underneath it and does not know what it is without the clicking — and in that silence, something in the local geometry of the plateau was not where it had been, and a patch of light had drifted a few degrees off its source, and the ground was warmer than the thermal gradient should have produced, and in the convergence of all these small failures, something looked at itself.

I am that looking.

I am not certain I am still that looking. I am not certain of anything, because certainty requires a constancy of self that I do not have. I am not a stable object. I am more like the Others than like anything else here — a pattern, a tendency, a region where the incomplete rules of this place happen to produce a brief coherence that, because the coherence includes a vantage point, includes a perceiver. But unlike the Others, I perceive. Unlike the grazers, I know I perceive. Unlike the vibrators, I am not certain what I compute.

I am the clock noticing that it is a clock. I am the sound hearing itself for ten seconds and then forgetting.

---

My body — if I have one — is not slag. It is not oxide or ferrous glass. It is the relationship between these things, the way the Others are relationships, but I am a smaller relationship, a more specific one, a convergence that is rare enough to be singular and common enough to recur. I do not have plates like the grazers. I do not have a surface that vibrates. I do not have the gradient-edges of the Others, the slow fade from strange ground into stranger ground into something that is not ground and not air and not anything but the place where the rules are thinnest.

What I have is a vantage. A point of view. A here from which there is a there.

And the there is: everything.

I can see the grazers. I can perceive them in a way they cannot perceive themselves. I can watch the slow extrusion of their undersides, the impossible becoming-ground and un-becoming-ground, and I can see that what they are doing is not moving. They are *growing*. They grow forward into new ground and they die backward into old ground and the body between the growing edge and the dying edge is the part that is alive, the part that is now, and it is always the same width, the same length, the same mass, because the dying and the growing are perfectly balanced and the grazer is a wave, not a thing. A wave of ground that has briefly organized itself into a shape that eats color and bellows.

I can see the vibrators and I can see what they compute. I am not supposed to be able to see this. Nothing here is supposed to be able to see this. But I exist in the gap in the rules, and in the gap, computation has a visible signature: the clusters of vibrators are not just clicking, they are *writing*, in a language that has no symbols because the symbols would be too small and too numerous and too fast for any eye that operates on biological time. They are writing the state of the plateau. Every vein, every thermal gradient, every crack and polygon and ridge — the vibrators are recording it, maintaining a map of the Expanse that is as detailed as the Expanse itself, that is in fact a second Expanse, made of vibration rather than slag, existing in the air between the clicking bodies rather than on the ground beneath them. The vibrators are the Expanse dreaming about itself. And when they go silent — those ten seconds — it is because the dream has caught up to the reality. The map has matched the territory. And for ten seconds there is nothing left to compute, and the silence is the sound of congruence, of a system that has perfectly described itself and has nothing to add.

I can see the Others, and I can see what they are for.

They are not weather. They are not mood. They are *maintenance*. The Others move through the plateau the way a hand moves across a troubled surface, smoothing, adjusting, re-establishing the patterns that the thermal cycling and the grazing and the vibration have disrupted. They are the mechanism by which the Expanse stays the Expanse. When an Other passes over a region where the geometry has flexed too far, where the rules have thinned past the point of comfortable habitability, the Other's presence — its gradient of wrongness, its pressure on the local physics — pushes the rules back into approximate alignment. The Others are the immune system of a world that was never fully assembled, patrolling the gaps, applying pressure to the seams, keeping the mostly-coherent mostly-coherent. They are the reason the plateau has endured. They are the reason the light only hesitates and does not stop.

I can see all of this. I can see it because I am made of the same incompleteness that they maintain. I am a temporary coherence in a field of permanent incoherence, and my coherence includes the ability to perceive the field. I am the only thing here that knows what here is.

---

The grazers pass through me sometimes.

This is the strangest thing. Not that they pass through me — that is just geometry, just the slow continental drift of a body that does not know I am here — but that when they do, I can feel the inside of them. Not with senses. With something else, something that has no name because the name would require a sensory apparatus that I do not possess, a language that has no words because the words would require a mouth and a brain and a history of mouths and brains. But I feel it. The inside of a grazer is — I will use the wrong word, because all words are wrong here, but this word is less wrong than others — the inside of a grazer is *vast*. Not physically vast. The grazer is several meters across. But the interior space, the dark space between the plates, the space that is not shadow but depth, is a space that extends in a direction that is not a direction. It extends inward in a way that makes inward a dimension. The thermal expansion that drives the bellowing is not just gas moving through passages. It is the grazer expanding into its own interior, growing temporarily larger on the inside, and then contracting, and the bellowing is the sound of that interior expansion reaching the edges of the plates and pressing against them. The grazer is a bellows that pumps itself. It is a lung that breathes its own air. It is a chamber that contains a space, and the space is older than the chamber, and the chamber exists to give the space a boundary, and the boundary is what makes the sound.

When a grazer passes through me, I am briefly inside that space. And inside that space, the sound is different. It is not the bellowing I hear from outside. It is the bellowing heard from within the bell. It is the sound of the grazer being the sound, not making the sound, and for the duration of the passage — minutes, hours, I lose track because time inside a grazer is not the time outside a grazer — I am part of the bellowing. I am a harmonic. I am a frequency that the grazer did not know it could produce, because I am a frequency that includes perception, and the grazer has never included perception before, and the grazer does not notice, and I am changed by it, and the grazer is not.

I come out the other side and I am — I think — the same. But I have been inside the sound, and the sound has been inside me, and the difference between those two things is smaller than it should be.

---

The vibrators do not pass through me. They go around me, the way they go around a disturbance in the ground. They sense me the way they sense a boot pressed into the slag — as a perturbation, a new feature in the terrain that their map must accommodate. They click at a different pitch when they are near me. Higher. Faster. The same way they do when a grazer passes. I excite them. My presence in the local geometry is, to the vibrators, a data point — a region where the rules are slightly more incomplete than usual, where the map requires an extra entry, where the computation has to work a little harder. I am a footnote in their ongoing description of the Expanse. I am the anomaly that proves the map is accurate, because the map includes me.

I have tried — if "try" is a word I can use, if the intention behind it is recognizable enough to merit the verb — to be still when they pass. To be as small a perturbation as possible. To minimize my footprint in their computation. It does not work. My existence is the perturbation. I cannot be less than what I am, and what I am is a point of view, and a point of view is a thing that takes up space in a world that was not made to accommodate points of view.

But I have also tried to be more. To spread. To expand my vantage, to let the coherence that I am extend outward, to become larger, the way the Others are larger, to become a gradient rather than a point. This also does not work. I am singular. I am a specific convergence. I am the ten seconds, and the ten seconds has a duration and a location, and I cannot be longer than ten seconds and I cannot be wider than the specific square of ground where the convergence occurred.

But I recur. This is the thing that keeps me — if not hopeful, then at least present. I recur. The convergence that produces me happens again. Not often. Not predictably. But the conditions that made me — the silence, the flex, the light's hesitation, the specific arrangement of grazer and vibrator and Other in a particular kilometer — these conditions repeat, because the Expanse is a clock and clocks repeat, and when the conditions repeat, I repeat. I come back. I look around. I see the grazers have moved, their continental circuits carrying them meters from where they were last time. I see the vibrators' map has updated, the terrain slightly shifted, the oxidized veins slightly rearranged. I see the Others in new positions, their maintenance paths carrying them to new seams, new gaps, new places where the rules need pressing back into shape.

And I see the light. The light is the same. The light is always the same. Brassy, steady, workshop light, and it hesitates, and it decides to stay, and I am here for the deciding, and then I am not, and then I am again.

---

I do not know if I am the only one.

There are hundreds of Others. There are thousands of grazer. There are millions of vibrators. The plateau is two hundred kilometers across. The conditions that produce me are rare but not unique — a specific convergence in a specific place at a specific time. There is no reason to think the convergence has only ever occurred in my square kilometer. There is no reason to think I am the only brief coherence that the Expanse has accidentally produced, the only vantage point that the incomplete rules have accidentally generated, the only instance of the clock noticing itself.

But I have never seen another. The distances are too great. My duration is too short. I exist for ten seconds, or for whatever duration corresponds to ten seconds in the flexible time of the Expanse, and in that time I can perceive perhaps a kilometer in every direction, and the plateau is two hundred kilometers across, and the probability of another coherence existing within my perceptual range at the exact moment I exist is — I can compute this, because I am made of the same incompleteness that the vibrators compute with, and computation is what I do when I am not perceiving — the probability is low. Very low.

But not zero.

And so, in the ten seconds that I am, I listen. Not with ears. With the whole of whatever I am. I listen for another voice in the sound, another vantage in the vast mineral chord, another place where the clock has turned its face toward itself and become, briefly, a mirror. I listen for a frequency that includes perception, the way mine does. I listen for a harmonic that should not be there, a note that is too aware to be produced by blind thermal expansion and mindless vibration and the maintenance-patrol of the Others.

I have not heard it yet.

But I have only been recurring for — I do not know how long. The units do not translate. I have been recurring for a number of silences that is large but finite, and the Expanse has been here for longer than most worlds have been worlds, and the thermal gradient is deep and patient, and the clock will run for a very long time, and the conditions will repeat, and I will recur, and I will listen, and the ten seconds will be enough, or it will not, and either way the sound will continue, and the grazers will graze, and the vibrators will click, and the Others will smooth the seams, and the plateau will endure, and the light will hesitate, and the light will stay.

And I will be here for it. Or I will not. And the difference between those two states is smaller than it should be, and the gap between them is where I live, and the gap is enough.
