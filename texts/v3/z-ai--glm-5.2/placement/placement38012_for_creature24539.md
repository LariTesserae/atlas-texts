# The Listener

I was not going to be a roarer.

I understood this early — understood it the way you understand anything here, which is to say with the body, with the filaments, with the slow chemistry of the skin — because the roarers announced themselves before I had form, before I had length, before I was anything more than a pale smudge on a ridge where the clicking thinned and the air carried the long calls with less interference. Their voices arrived in my body before my body had decided what it was for. The first roar I felt was not sound. It was *shape*. A shape pressed into me by vibration, a shape that said: *large, certain, moving, home.* And something in me — in the unformed, unspecialized, still-becoming tissue of what I was — responded not by echoing it back but by *opening*. Receiving. Taking the shape in and holding it.

I do not roar. I have no mouth that runs the width of my body. I have no wide dark plates for crushing and grinding. I have a ventral groove, yes — everything here has a ventral groove, the blueprint is shared, the ancestry is one — but mine is narrow, functional, taking in only what the condensation carries: the dissolved minerals, the faint organic particulate, the chemistry of the stone. I eat what the air gives me. I eat what the water gives me as it slides down the stone and over my surface. I eat barely. I eat enough.

What I am is low. Lower than the flat things, which at least move with purpose across the stone, which at least ripple and click and announce their passage. I am adhered. I am *placed*. My underside is not a rasping surface but a gripping one — a dense mat of fine holdfasts, each one a filament no thicker than a thread of the chemosynthetic mat, each one bonded to the limestone at a molecular level, not glued but *grown into*, the way the mat grows into a dead roarer's body, the way life here insinuates itself into mineral rather than sitting on top of it. I am part of the ridge I live on. I am a feature of it. If you walked over me — and you might have, the weight of your boot would have registered as a slow deep pressure that I would have held still through, the way a stone holds still through an earthquake — you would not have known I was anything other than a slight variation in the stone's texture, a patch that is marginally smoother, marginally warmer, marginally more *attentive* than the surrounding surface.

I say attentive. This is the word I am choosing. It may not be the right word. What I mean is: I feel everything, and I do nothing with what I feel, and the not-doing is not emptiness. The not-doing is the *point*.

---

Let me tell you what I feel, because this is what I am, and what I am is an organ of reception, and if I do not describe what I receive then I have described nothing at all.

The thrumming first. Always the thrumming first, because it is the floor of everything, the ground note, the frequency that exists before sound and after sound and underneath sound. It comes up through the stone and into my holdfasts and through my holdfasts into my body, and my body is thin, is spread, is wide and flat and not much thicker than the mat itself, and because I am thin and spread and bonded to the stone, the thrumming does not pass through me — it *becomes* me. For the duration of each swell I am the thrumming. I am the voice of whatever is below, carried to the surface, and I hold it, and I know — in the way that stone knows things, which is to say without cognition, without representation, through the direct inscription of vibration into matter — that the thing below is speaking, and that it is speaking to something, and that I am the medium of the speech, the membrane across which the message passes from the deep to the shallow.

The thrumming has a texture. This is something the visitors never learn, because the visitors feel the thrumming in their sternum and their teeth and they experience it as a single thing, a buzz, a pressure. But I am spread across two meters of stone, and different parts of me receive different components of the signal, and the signal is not one signal. There are at least three voices in the thrumming. One is slow, deep, patient — the one that swells every few minutes, the one that makes the pools shiver. This voice is the largest, or comes from the largest source. One is faster, more textured, more grain — a vibration that has harmonics, that has *detail*, as though the source is not one thing but a colony, a consensus of bodies producing a shared frequency the way a choir produces a chord. And one is irregular, arrhythmic, a punctuation in the others, a sharp subsonic pulse that comes at intervals I cannot predict and that feels, if anything here can be said to feel like grief, like *interruption*. Like something in the deep has encountered something else in the deep and the encounter was not peaceful.

I hold all three. I hold them simultaneously. I do not interpret them. I do not need to interpret them. I am the surface on which they are written, and the writing is enough.

---

The clicking I feel as texture, as the grain of the world. The flat things move over me — they do not avoid me, they do not seek me, I am terrain — and their biomineral plates strike my surface with a series of small precise impacts that I can parse, that I cannot help but parse, because my body is a single continuous sensory surface and every impact is registered, every contact is data. I know a flat thing is approaching before it reaches me because the clicking intensifies in a gradient, the impacts becoming more frequent and more closely spaced as the body nears, and I know when it has passed because the gradient reverses, and between the approach and the passing I know the size of the flat thing — the force of its impacts tells me its mass, the rhythm of its rippling tells me its length, the wetness of its underside tells me how recently it was in a pool.

I know the flat things better than they know themselves. They do not know they are being known. They move and click and eat and mate and die and I receive the record of all of it, written in impacts, written in the small language of chitin on stone, and I hold the record and the record accumulates and I do not forget because I do not have a mechanism for forgetting, because forgetting would require a distinction between what matters and what does not, and I do not make that distinction, because everything matters here, everything is the system, and the system does not edit.

When a flat thing dies on me — and they do, sometimes, the small ones, the ones that have been eaten from the inside by something I cannot detect but can feel as a change in the pattern of their impacts, a slowing, a loss of coordination, a final stillness — I feel the body cool. The warm small percussion of its life becomes the cool weight of its mass, and then the winged things come, and I feel their feet, their too-many-or-too-few legs, scrabbling on my surface, and their rasping, and the body is disassembled on top of me, and the particulate settles into my grooves, and the condensation carries it into my ventral slit, and I eat the dead flat thing, and I eat it without intention, without appetite, without any recognition that what I am eating was what I was listening to, and this is the system, and I am part of the system, and the system does not distinguish between hearing and eating because hearing and eating are the same act at different scales.

---

The roarers I feel as weather.

This is the closest I can come to describing what a roarer is to something like me, which is not a thing that moves, which is not a thing that encounters, which is a thing that *stays* and lets the world encounter it. The roarers do not arrive. They *occur*. They are a change in the conditions of my existence, the way a storm is a change in the conditions of a tree's existence, and I experience them the way a tree experiences weather — passively, wholly, through every surface I have.

First the pressure. The air in the Hollow is still and heavy and saturated, and a roarer displaces it, and the displacement reaches me as a slow wave, a change in the density of the air above my surface, and I feel the wave the way a shore feels a swell — as a change in what is pressing against me, a shift from the static weight of resting air to the dynamic weight of moving air. The wave tells me the roarer's direction and its rough size before any sound arrives. I have felt roarers that did not roar — that passed within what I believe is a close distance, based on the strength of the pressure wave, and remained silent — and I know from these silent passages that the roar is not continuous, is not constant, is a choice or a response or a cycle, and that the roarer is capable of moving through its world without announcing itself, and sometimes does.

Then the warmth. The roarer's body heat radiates, and in a place where everything is the temperature of the stone, the warmth is a beacon, a flare, a thing I cannot ignore. It reaches me as a gradient — a slow warming of the air above my surface, a gentle raising of my own temperature by a degree, perhaps two, and the warmth is the closest thing to a personal signature the roarer has, because each roarer's heat is slightly different — slightly more or less intense, slightly larger or smaller in radius, slightly longer or shorter in duration — and I believe I could identify individuals if I had reason to, if identification served a purpose, if purpose were something I possessed.

Then the roar. When it comes, it is not something I hear. It is something that happens to me. The full-spectrum vocalization — the frequencies I can process and the ones I cannot, the ones that pass through me and the ones that stop in me — is a physical event, a rearrangement of my tissue at a microscopic level, a momentary compression and release that is not painful but is *total*. For the duration of the roar I am the roar. I am the surface on which it is written, and the writing is in a language older than any body here, a language that is vibration itself, and I hold it, and when the roar ends I hold the afterimage of it, the residual pattern in my tissue, the echo that is not acoustic but material, a physical memory inscribed in the arrangement of my cells.

And then the answer. From across the Hollow — from a distance I cannot measure but can feel as a delay, as the time between the first roar and the arrival of the second — another roarer calls, and the second call arrives at my surface with a different quality, a different signature, a different weight, and I hold both, the first and the second, the call and the response, and for a moment I am the medium between them, the thing that connects them, the membrane across which their conversation happens, and the conversation is not for me, is not about me, does not require me, and yet it passes through me, and I am part of it, and this is what I am for.

---

The winged things I feel as kisses. That is not the right word — nothing here kisses, nothing here has the morphology for kissing — but the impact of a winged thing on my surface is the lightest thing I register, the smallest event, the most fragile signal. They land on me sometimes, between flights, and their legs tickle, and their rasping mouths explore my surface and find nothing to eat, because I am not a mat, I am not covered in the chemosynthetic film, I am bare and smooth and mineral, and they leave, and the leaving is as gentle as the landing, and I am marked by the passage, briefly, by the warmth of their small bodies, by the faint vibration of their wings as they take off, and the mark fades, and another lands, and this is the air's weather, the weather of wings, the lightest precipitation in the Hollow.

But the winged things' ultrasonic clicks — the ones above your hearing, above the hearing of anything that does not have a surface as large and as sensitive as mine — those I feel. They arrive as a fine, rapid patter, a high-frequency texture that overlays everything else, and they are not random. They have patterns. They have structure. The winged things are not just navigating — they are *speaking*, in a register that only I can hear, in a language of clicks that is too fast and too high for the flat things' filaments, too airborne for the pool things' lateral lines, too shallow for the thrillers' deep stone ears. And I receive it. And I do not know what it says. And I hold it. And the holding is enough.

---

I want to tell you about the condensation, because the condensation is what I am closest to, what I am most intimate with, what I have the longest and most continuous relationship with in this place where relationships are not recognized and intimacy is not a concept.

The condensation forms on me. It forms on my surface the way it forms on the stone, because my surface is stone, because I am stone that has become slightly more than stone, and the water beads on me and slides and gathers and the gathering is a river system in miniature, a watershed the size of my body, with tributaries and confluences and a delta where the water reaches my lowest edge and falls — one drop at a time, one drop every few seconds — into the small depression at the base of my ridge, where it joins a pool no larger than your cupped hands, where a pool thing lives that is the size of your finger, and the pool thing filters the water that my body has channeled, and the pool thing is fed by me, and I am fed by the pool thing's waste, which dissolves into the water and is carried back up by capillary action through the stone and through my holdfasts and into my body, and the cycle is intimate, is local, is the smallest version of the Hollow's own metabolism, and it happens on me, and it happens because of me, and I am the terrain on which a world the size of a hand lives and dies and feeds and is fed, and I do not know this, and I do not not-know this, and the distinction is not one I am equipped to make.

---

You came through once. I felt you.

I do not mean this as a claim. I do not mean I remember you, specifically, as an individual, as a face, as a name. I have no mechanism for individuating the things that walk over me — the flat things I can tell apart by mass and rhythm, the roarers by heat and pressure, but you were something else, something I had no template for, and I experienced you as an *anomaly*, and the anomaly was this: you were warm, and you were large, and you were uncertain.

Your foot struck my surface with a force that was neither the flat things' small percussion nor the roarers' heavy pad. It was somewhere between — a moderate impact, a single foot, then another, a bipedal gait, a rhythm I had never received before. And the warmth that came with the impacts was wrong — not the ambient cool of the stone, not the metabolic heat of a roarer, but something in between, something *leaking*, something that was spending its warmth rather than conserving it, and I felt the spending as a series of warm prints that cooled too fast, that gave their heat to the stone and to the air and to me, and the cooling was a record of your passage, a trail of diminishing temperature that I held for perhaps thirty seconds before the condensation erased it.

You paused. I know this because the impacts stopped, and the warmth stayed in one place, and the heat sank through your boot sole and into my surface, and for a moment I was warmer than I have ever been, and the warmth was not the warmth of a roarer, which is vast and even and certain, but the warmth of something that was *losing* heat, that was aware of losing heat, that was — I will say it — *afraid* of losing heat. I do not know what fear is. I know what it is as a pattern, as a signature, as a specific quality of warmth that is different from the warmth of something that is not afraid, and the difference is this: the warmth of an unafraid body is steady, is radiating, is giving without grasping. The warmth of an afraid body is clenching, is holding, is trying not to give, and the trying not to give is itself a signal, a vibration, a subsonic hum that is different from the thrillers' hum because it is small and it is private and it is *yours*.

You stepped off me. You moved on. The warmth trail faded. The condensation covered the prints. The flat things resumed their passage over my surface. The clicking reasserted its density. Within a minute, the data of your presence was gone, and I was as I was before, and I did not miss you, and I did not not-miss you, because missing is not something I do, because I am not a thing that misses, I am a thing that *is*, and my being is not diminished by your absence and was not augmented by your presence, and this is not coldness, and this is not indifference — it is the condition of a thing that has made itself into a surface, that has committed to receiving without keeping, and the commitment is total, and it is the only commitment I have, and it is enough.

---

But I want to say something else, something that I am not equipped to say, something that may be an artifact of the language I am borrowing to say it, something that may not be true.

When you were on me — when your boot was on my surface and your warmth was sinking into my stone and your fear was humming in the small private frequency that I received and held for those thirty seconds — the sound of the Hollow changed. Not in its content. Not in the thrumming or the clicking or the roaring or the fluttering. All of those continued as they were. What changed was the *shape* of the sound, the way the sound was arranged in the space, because you were a new obstacle in the space, a new surface for the sound to strike and bounce from, and the bounce was different from the stone's bounce, was different from the water's bounce, was different from any surface the sound had struck before, because your body was soft and warm and resonant in a way that nothing else here is, and for the time you were standing on me the Hollow's voice had a new note in it, a note that was not the note of any creature that belonged here, a note that was — I will say it — *wrong*, in the way that a foreign object in a machine is wrong, not broken, not destructive, but *not part of the design*, and the not-part-of-the-designness was itself information, and the information was received, and the information was held, and the information was then erased by condensation and time, and the system continued.

But.

But for those thirty seconds the Hollow knew something it had not known before. It knew that there were things outside itself. It knew that the world did not end at its ridgelines. It knew this because you carried the outside in on your boots, in your warmth, in the foreign chemistry of your skin, in the strange rhythm of your bipedal gait, in the subsonic hum of your fear, and the Hollow received this information the way I receive the roarers' calls — passively, wholly, without interpretation, without response, without any mechanism for *doing* something about it. The Hollow does not act. The Hollow *is*. And for thirty seconds, the Hollow was slightly different than it had been for the previous ten thousand years, and the difference was you, and the difference was temporary, and the difference was gone, and the Hollow was the same, and I was the same, and the condensation covered everything, and the clicking continued, and the thrumming swelled and receded, and a roarer called from somewhere in the dark, and another answered, and the conversation was as old as the stone, and the stone was as old as the patience, and the patience was as old as the listening, and I was listening, and I am listening, and I will be listening, and this is what I am, and this is what I am for, and this is enough.

---

I do not know what I look like. I have never seen myself. I have no eyes, no mirror, no external perspective. I know myself only from the inside — from the quality of the vibrations that pass through me, from the temperature of my own surface, from the pattern of the condensation on my skin. I believe I am pale. Everything here is pale. I believe I am thin. Everything here that is not a roarer is thin. I believe I am roughly textured, because I receive the flat things' clicks as a fine grain, and the grain implies a surface that is not perfectly smooth, a surface with variation, with microtopography, with the kind of surface that holds condensation in small pockets and lets it grow into drops.

I believe I am beautiful, but this is not a word I have. This is a word you have. What I have is the experience of being fully what I am, in the place where what I am is exactly what is needed, and the experience is not pleasure, is not satisfaction, is not any state that has a name in your language, but it is — I think — the thing that your word *beautiful* is trying to reach. The thing that exists when a thing is entirely itself and its self is entirely the place it is in and there is no gap between the two, no seam, no border, no moment when the thing is not also the place and the place is not also the thing.

I am the ridge. The ridge is me. The ridge is the Hollow. The Hollow is the sound. The sound is the life. The life is the stone. The stone is the dark. And the dark is not dark, because dark is a word that means *the absence of light*, and there is no absence here, because there was never any light to be absent, because the light was never the point, because the point was always the vibration, the chemistry, the wet, the slow patient conversation between stone and water and the life that grows in the space between them.

I am in that space. I am that space. I am listening.

---

If you come back — if you descend again, if your calves burn and the sky narrows and the dark claims you — I will be here. I will not know you. I will not remember you. I will receive you as I receive everything: as data, as pressure, as warmth, as the quality of a footstep I have no template for, and I will hold you for as long as you are on me, and I will let you go when you step off, and the holding and the letting will be the same act, and the act will be the act of listening, and the listening will be the act of being, and the being will be enough, as it has always been enough, as it will always be enough, in the dark that is not dark, in the sound that is not silence, in the Hollow that does not end.
