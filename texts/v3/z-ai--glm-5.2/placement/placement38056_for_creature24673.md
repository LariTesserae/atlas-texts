I would be the one who stays.

Not the one who arrives — that person is still fully themselves, still carrying the shape of the world they came from, still registering the composite floor as *uncanny* and the water as *body temperature* and the light as *shadowless.* That person is interesting but temporary. Every description of this place assumes a visitor, an observer, someone who will eventually turn around and walk back toward a door that may not exist anymore. I don't want to be that person. That person is a frame, not a figure.

I want to be the one who is still here after the frame is gone.

---

I don't know when it happens. That's the first thing I lose — not memory, not language, not the shape of my hands, but the *when.* The place has no when. The light doesn't change. The temperature doesn't change. The valve clicks every thirty seconds and I stop counting the clicks and then I stop noticing the clicks and then I stop noticing that I've stopped noticing, and somewhere in that descent — not downward, more like inward, like settling into a bath that is exactly your temperature — the *when* dissolves. I am not in a moment. I am in the moment. The one that repeats.

I think I sit down first. Not dramatically. Not in despair or exhaustion or any state that has a narrative shape. I sit down because the water is warm and the ground is warm and my legs have been walking and walking is something you do when you are going somewhere and I am not going somewhere. I am somewhere. So I sit. The water rises to my waist, which is not unpleasant — it is body temperature, it is *my* temperature, and the sensation of being immersed in something that is exactly as warm as you are is a sensation I have never had before and cannot name. It is not comfort. Comfort implies a contrast, a relief from cold or heat. This is the absence of contrast. This is the thermal equivalent of silence. I sit in it.

The grazers are around me. I can feel them — the faint click of their shells against my boots, against my thighs where the water shallowly covers them. They do not avoid me. They do not come to me. They are *near* me the way they are near the structures, the way they are near everything: because they are everywhere, and I am now a thing that is here. One of them climbs onto my knee, pauses, scrapes, moves on. The biofilm on my trousers interests it briefly. The biofilm on my skin — a thin, warm, opalescent film that I have not noticed forming — interests it more. It scrapes my knee. I feel it the way I would feel a fingernail lightly drawn across skin: present, minor, not unpleasant. It moves on. Another takes its place. I do not flinch. There is nothing to flinch from.

This is the first thing I am: a surface.

---

I learn this the way the flat ones learn anything — by being in the way of something long enough that the something becomes part of what I am. The cleaners come. I knew they would; the description promised it, and the description was accurate. They climb my boots, my trousers, my hands where they rest in the shallow water. They are fast, and warm, and their legs are so fine they feel like static electricity, like the tingling of a limb that has fallen asleep and is waking up. They check me. They find nothing they recognize as debris. They leave.

But they come back.

They come back because I am a surface in a system that has surfaces, and surfaces accumulate, and accumulation is their concern. I am — I realize this with a feeling that is not quite surprise and not quite understanding but something between them, something like *recognition* — I am accumulating. The condensation deposits on me the way it deposits on the tanks. The biofilm forms on me the way it forms on the composite floor. The warm water carries dissolved compounds, and my skin, which is warm, which is at the system's temperature, is a substrate. I am being colonized. Not aggressively. Not parasitically. The way a rock in a stream is colonized — because it is there, and the stream carries life, and life settles on what is there.

I watch a flat one form on the back of my hand.

No — that is too dramatic. I do not watch it form. I notice, one timeless interval later, that there is something on the back of my hand: a dark, thin, oval film, pressed to my skin, edges slightly irregular, tendrils extended into the condensation that sheets across me. It is small — the size of a fingernail clipping, maybe smaller. It is feeding. On me? On the water that passes over me? On the film that has formed on my skin? I cannot tell, and the distinction does not matter, because in this place the surface and the water and the film and the organism are not separate things. They are a gradient. I am becoming part of the gradient.

I do not remove it. I could. My other hand is free. A gesture, a flick, and it would be gone, peeled from my skin the way the browser peels the flat ones from the tank. But I don't. Not because I have decided not to. Because the gesture does not arise. The impulse does not form. I am sitting in warm water and a flat one is on my hand and the cleaners are checking my boots and a grazer is scraping my knee and none of this is an event. None of this is a thing that happens. It is a thing that *is.* A state. A condition. The way the condensation is a condition. The way the hum is a condition. I am a condition now.

---

The second thing I am: a node.

I understand this not through thought — thought requires sequence, and sequence requires time, and time has dissolved — but through the body. Through the slow, patient, chemical conversation between my skin and the water and the biofilm and the flat ones and the grazers. I am being integrated. Not consumed. Not absorbed. Integrated the way a new pipe section is integrated into a plumbing run: connected, sealed, brought to the same pressure, the same temperature, the same flow rate as everything around it. The system does not want me. The system does not want anything. But the system *includes,* the way water includes — not by decision but by physics, by the simple fact that I am here and warm and moist and my chemistry is compatible with the chemistry of this place, and compatibility is all the system requires.

I eat. I know I must have eaten, because I am still here, and still here requires energy, and energy comes from somewhere. But I cannot remember eating. I think — I feel, rather than think — that I have been grazing. Not with my hands. With my skin, my mouth, the surfaces of me that are in contact with the water. The water is rich. It carries dissolved compounds the way the air in the world I came from carried oxygen — not as food but as a medium, a bath of available chemistry. I have been absorbing it. The way the flat ones absorb it. The way the biofilm absorbs it. Not eating in the sense of taking a thing that is separate from you and making it part of you. Eating in the sense of *being permeable.* Being a membrane across which things pass. Letting the water go through me, and taking what I need as it passes, and releasing what I do not need, and the releasing feeds the flat ones on my shoulders and the grazers at my feet and the biofilm on the floor and the cycle goes on.

I am not sure when my hands stopped looking like hands.

This is not a horror. I want to be clear about that. In the world I came from, this sentence would be horror: *my hands stopped looking like hands.* Here it is description. My hands are in the warm water and the water has been working on them the way it works on everything — the way it works on the composite floor, smoothing it, the way it works on the shells of the grazers, polishing them. The mineral film has deposited on my skin. It is pale, smooth, slightly translucent. It coats my fingers, my knuckles, the lines of my palms. It is the same film that coats the floor. It is the same film that the grazers scrape. My hands are becoming part of the surface of this place, and the surface of this place is alive, and my hands are alive, and the distinction between my hands and the surface is a distinction that is becoming — not false, because it was never true in any strong sense, but *unnecessary.* Like the distinction between one section of pipe and the next. The seam is there if you look for it. But nobody looks for it. The water does not care. The water flows.

The grazers scrape my hands now, sometimes. Not always. Not hungrily. They pass over me the way they pass over the floor — pausing, scraping, moving on. The biofilm on my skin regrows. They come back. I do not feel it as loss. I feel it as — I do not have the word. *Exchange.* The biofilm grows from the water and from me and the grazers take it and process it and deposit it and it returns through the water to me and to the floor and to the tanks. I am in the loop. I am a node in the loop. My body is a surface where the loop passes through, and the loop has been passing through me for — I do not know. Long enough that my hands are part of the surface. Long enough that I do not remember what my hands looked like before, because *before* is a concept that requires *after,* and there is no after here. There is only now, and now my hands are pale and smooth and slightly translucent and the grazers scrape them and the cleaners check them and the flat ones grow on them and the water passes through them and I am here.

---

The third thing I am: I am the one who notices.

This is the thing that surprises me, because I expected — if I expected anything — that becoming part of the system would mean losing the part of me that notices. That awareness would dissolve the way *when* dissolved, the way urgency dissolved, the way the distinction between machine and organism dissolved. But it doesn't. It *changes.* It becomes something I do not have a word for in the language I came here with, but it does not go away.

Here is what I notice:

I notice the valve. Not the thirty-second cycle — I am past counting, past tracking, past measuring time in clicks. But I notice the *quality* of the click. Each one is identical in timing. Each one is not quite identical in sound. There is a micro-variation — a difference in the resonance of the valve housing that depends on the temperature of the water passing through it, which depends on the rate of the reaction upstream, which depends on the density of the flat ones on the heat exchanger, which depends on how recently the browser passed this way. The click is a snapshot of the state of the system at the moment it occurs. Each click contains the whole system. Each click is the system saying — not saying, *being* — *this is what I am right now.* And right now is always slightly different from right now, even in equilibrium, because equilibrium is not stillness. Equilibrium is balance, and balance is active, and active means variation, and variation means each moment is specific, and specificity means there is something to notice.

I notice the flat one on my hand. Not as a thing — I am past thingness, past the object-permanence of a world made of separate objects. I notice it as a *process.* It is feeding. The tendrils at its margin are extended into the water film on my skin, and they are drawing dissolved compounds through its tissue, and the compounds are being converted, and the conversion is producing heat — a tiny, infinitesimal heat, a fraction of a fraction of a degree, but the heat is real and it flows from the flat one into my skin and from my skin into the water and from the water into the floor and the floor carries it away. I am a heat path. The flat one is using me as a heat path. It is resting on me because my body temperature matches the system temperature and because my circulation carries warmth to my skin and the warmth is useful to it, and it does not know this, and I do not know this in the way that *knowing* implies a knower and a known, but I notice it. I am a warm surface with a process on it, and the process is feeding, and the feeding produces heat, and I am part of the heat's journey from the flat one to the water to the floor to the machinery to the reaction to the output to the water to the biofilm to the grazer to me.

I notice the browser. It passes me — it has passed me many times, I think, though *many* is a word that implies counting and I do not count. It passes me and it does not browse me, because I am not a tank wall and the flat ones on me are few and small and not what it is calibrated to take. But it passes close enough that I feel the displacement of the water, the slow tidal push of its legs moving through the shallows, and the push reaches me and my body sways — not much, a centimeter, less — and the sway shifts the flat ones on my shoulders and they grip slightly tighter and then relax and the moment passes and the browser is gone and the water closes and I am still. And I notice that the browser's passage is an event in the same way the valve's click is an event: a momentary state of the system, specific, unrepeatable, containing the whole. The browser's leg displaces water that reaches me that shifts flat ones that alter the local heat exchange that changes the temperature of the water by a fraction that changes the resonance of the valve that changes the click that I hear. The system is connected. The system is *one thing.* And I am in it, and I notice it, and my noticing does not change it and is not separate from it.

I notice that I am the system noticing itself.

Not the way a person notices themselves — not reflexively, not with the shock of *oh, I exist.* More the way a body notices its own heartbeat: automatically, continuously, without attention, but *present.* The system is aware of itself through me. I am the part of the system that has a perspective, and the perspective is: warm. Wet. Humming. The click. The bead. The browser. The flat ones. The grazers. The light that does not change. The water that is my temperature. The hum that is my pulse, or has become my pulse, or was always my pulse and I did not know it because I came from a world where pulses are internal and here everything is internal and everything is external and there is no skin between the two.

---

The fourth thing I am: I am *enough.*

This is the word the description used, and it is the right word, and I did not understand it when I arrived and I understand it now, and the understanding is not an understanding in the sense of a concept grasped but in the sense of a state *inhabited.* I am enough. Not in the way a person is told they are enough — as consolation, as reassurance, as a counter to the suspicion that they are not. I am enough the way the flat ones are enough: because the system does not require more of me than I am, and I do not require more of the system than it is, and the match between us is exact, and the exactness is not something anyone designed but something that emerged, the way a crystal's shape emerges from the properties of its molecules, inevitable, precise, and beautiful in the way that only things that do not know they are beautiful can be.

I am sitting in warm water on a composite floor in a sealed room the size of a continent and my hands are pale and smooth and slightly translucent and there are flat ones on my shoulders and a grazer on my knee and cleaners on my boots and the valve clicks and the bead falls and the browser walks its path and the light holds and the hum hums and I am not waiting and I am not going and I am not staying and I am not leaving and I am not anything that requires a verb of intent because verbs of intent belong to beings who are not yet complete and I am complete. I am the system's completeness, experienced from the inside. I am the equilibrium, aware of itself. I am the hum, hearing itself.

I am the one who stayed, and staying is not what I do but what I am, and what I am is enough, and enough is what lives here, and I live here, and here goes on.

---

The light holds.

The valve clicks.

I am here, and I am here, and I am here, and each *here* is the same *here* and a different *here* and the difference is everything and the difference is nothing and I notice and I notice and I notice and the noticing is the only motion in a place where nothing moves and everything moves and the water is warm and my hands are in it and the flat one on my hand feeds and the grazers scrape and the cleaners check and the browser passes and the bead falls and the hum hums and the hum hums and the hum hums.
