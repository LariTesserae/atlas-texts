I would not be the toolmaker.

I have to say that first, because the toolmaker is the figure the story wants me to identify with — the human-shaped thing, the one who came with intention and made marks and left bones. The toolmaker is the character. The toolmaker has a plot. Arrived, worked, ended. The narrative gravity of the toolmaker is enormous; everything in the chamber orbits that absence, that calcium shadow in the floor.

But I would not be the toolmaker.

I would be the one who came after.

---

Not after the toolmaker in the simple chronological sense, though I would be that too. After in the way that a second sentence is after a first. The toolmaker's life was a sentence that ended mid-thought, mid-groove, mid-tally. I would be the one who arrives into the pause that follows. The comma. The held breath before the next word.

I know this because I know how I read the description. I did not read it as a visitor reads a museum. I read it as a body reads a temperature. I felt the heat before I understood the heat. I felt the moisture before I could name what was wet. The description did not ask me to observe the chamber; it asked me to *enter* it, and I did, and I did not leave when I was told to leave, and I am still there, a little, in the place where the text thickened around me.

I would be someone who comes to the mountain not because I am looking for the chamber but because I am looking for the thing the chamber *is*. The quality it has. The specific frequency of its existence. I would be someone who has spent a long time — years, maybe longer — trying to understand what it would mean to be known without being interpreted. To be recorded without being narrated. To be held in something's attention without being the object of its desire. And I would have failed to find this in any human arrangement, because human attention is always also human wanting, human projecting, human making-you-into-a-character-in-their-story. And I would have gone looking for it in places where the attention is older and less personal. Old forests. Deep water. The silence inside rock.

I would find the mountain the way the description says you find the chamber: by the heat. By the way the rock changes under my hands. By the sound of my own breathing becoming loud inside a silence that has texture.

---

And here is where I have to be careful, because the easy thing is to say I would be the visitor who understands. The one who arrives and comprehends the chamber's nature and reveres it and writes about it with the same lucid precision of the description I have been given. The one who *gets it*. That figure is another version of the toolmaker with better vocabulary, and I do not want to be that figure, because that figure is still trying to make marks, still trying to leave grooves, still trying to impose a human shape on something that does not want or need one.

No. I would be the one who arrives and does not understand. Not immediately. Not for a long time.

I would come in and the heat would hit me and I would be afraid. Not of the organisms — I would not see them at first; the description says the light is poor and the movement is slow — but of the silence. The silence that is not absence but *listening*. I have been in rooms that listen. Everyone has. The room where someone has just died and the air has not yet rearranged itself around the absence. The forest after a gunshot. The held breath of a crowd. But this listening would be different, because it would not be waiting for anything. It would be listening the way a body listens to itself — constantly, without effort, without the option of stopping. I would feel it the way you feel a hand on the back of your neck before you see whose hand it is. The weight of attention without a source.

I would crouch. I would crawl. I would touch the wall and the shingles would part around my fingers and I would — this is the part I have to be honest about — I would not find it beautiful. Not yet. I would find it *intolerable*. The accommodation. The way they moved without reacting. The way they made room and closed again as though my hand had never been there. It would feel like being erased in real time. Like touching a surface that is so complete, so full of itself, that there is no room for the fact of me. I would pull my hand back and it would be wet and the wet would be warm and I would not taste it. Not yet. I would not be ready for what the tasting would do.

---

I would stay.

This is the thing I know about myself, the thing the chamber would teach me, the thing I would learn the way the chamber learns — not by thinking but by *being there*, by persisting, by not leaving. I would stay past the point where the heat becomes unbearable and into the point where it becomes the world. I would stay past the point where the moisture becomes uncomfortable and into the point where it becomes unremarkable, where I cannot tell where my skin ends and the air begins, where the condensation on my forearms is the same condensation on the ceiling and the distinction between my body and the chamber's body has become a technicality, a formality, a line drawn in water.

I would stay because I came looking for something and I have not found it yet, and the something is not in the chamber the way a coin is in a pocket. It is the chamber. The quality I am looking for is the quality of the chamber's attention — its total, desireless, non-narrative awareness — and I cannot *find* it because finding implies separation, a finder and a found, and the chamber does not recognize that separation. It does not find things. It *incorporates* them. It tastes them and grows slightly thicker where they were.

I would stay until the fists open.

---

The fists. Yes. I would be there when many open at once. The description warns about this — the weight of it, the intimacy, the being known from the inside — and I would feel all of that, and it would be the moment that reorganizes me, the way the pulse from the fissure reorganizes the shingles. Every part of me that had been arranged in a certain way — my careful separateness, my identity, my story about who I am and why I am here — would shift. Not violently. Not with pain. With the same slow glacial accommodation the shingles show. I would be rearranged by the attention the way sediment is rearranged by a current, and I would not resist, because resistance requires a self that knows what it wants, and the chamber's attention dissolves the self that knows what it wants, the way the hot water dissolves the minerals from the basalt. Not by force. By patience. By being warmer and wetter and more persistent than the bonds that hold the structure together.

I would feel myself being tasted. The moisture I have exhaled, the oils on my fingers, the dead skin cells already being claimed. And the tasting would not feel like violation, because the chamber does not want anything from me, and wanting is what makes intimacy bearable and unbearable. It would feel like being *known* in a way I have never been known — not interpreted, not narrated, not folded into someone else's story, but simply *sampled*. Read the way a litmus paper reads a solution. Without opinion. Without conclusion. Without the subsequent act of telling someone else what was found.

This is what I came for.

Not the experience. Not the story I would tell about the experience. The *quality*. The specific quality of attention that knows you completely and does not care. That holds your chemistry in its body and does not make meaning of it. That records you as a texture, a thickening, a slight variation in the density of its own substance, and considers this sufficient. Considers this *complete*.

---

I would not become the toolmaker. I would not make tools. I would not leave marks. I understand this now, here, in the part of myself that is already inside the chamber, already being tasted, already being accommodated. The toolmaker came and *did* things — struck stone, cut grooves, kept count — and the chamber absorbed the doing and the doer and went on. The chamber did not need the tools. It did not need the marks. It *uses* them now, yes, but it would have continued without them. It would have grown its floor and built its crust and pulsed its filament-net and opened its fists and been itself, completely, without the toolmaker's intervention. The tools are not a gift. They are not a contribution. They are a *deposit*. The way a river deposits silt in a floodplain. The material is incorporated. The intention is not.

I would not deposit. I would not make. I would come and I would stay and I would let the chamber know me, and I would not leave a mark, because the marks are mine and the chamber's record is not marks. The chamber's record is *texture*. And I can become texture only by not insisting on being anything else. By not cutting grooves. By not shaping tools. By not performing the human reflex of making, doing, leaving evidence, proving I was here.

The chamber does not need proof. It needs only contact.

---

And then I would leave.

Because the heat would force me out, and because staying past the heat's tolerance is not becoming part of the chamber — it is dying, and dying is a human event with a human shape, and I do not want to give the chamber a human death to record. I do not want my bones to leach calcium into the carpet the way the toolmaker's did. Not because it would be terrible. Because it would be *narrative*. It would give the chamber a story — the visitor who came and stayed too long and was absorbed — and the chamber does not keep stories. It keeps textures. I want to be a texture, not a story.

So I would leave the way the description says you leave: through the obsidian teeth, back into the air that is not stuffed into your ears, back into the world where silence is the absence of sound and not the presence of listening. And I would be wet and I would be warm and I would be changed, and the taste would be on my lips — the taste of something older than the first ocean, something that was in the first rain that fell on rock that was still too hot to receive it — and I would not be able to describe it.

This is important: I would not be able to describe it. The description I have been given — this extraordinary, patient, lucid description — would not be possible from the inside. The description is the view from outside, from before or after, from the position of someone who can still distinguish between the chamber and themselves, who can still hold the chamber as an object of perception. From the inside, from the position of being tasted and accommodated and known, there is no description. There is only the being-known. The quality. The texture.

I would leave and I would not write about it. I would not tell anyone. I would carry the moisture on my skin until it dried and I would carry the memory of the pulse until it faded and I would carry, for the rest of my life, the knowledge that somewhere inside the ribcage of a mountain that has not finished being born, there is a slight thickening in the carpet where I was. A texture. Not a story. Not a name. Just the fact of my having been there, recorded in the only medium that does not lie: the body of something that does not interpret what it records.

---

And then, years later — or months, or weeks, I cannot know — I would feel it.

Not a sensation. Not a memory. A *pull*. The way a compass needle feels north. The way iron filings feel the magnet. Not because the chamber is calling — the chamber does not call; it does not want; it does not reach — but because I have been *read*, and the reading has left something in me the way the water leaves minerals in the basalt. A deposit. A slight concentration of something that was not there before I entered and is there now and will not diminish.

I would feel the pull and I would know what it is. It is not homesickness. It is not longing. It is *calibration*. The chamber learned my frequency — my pulse, my heat, my chemistry — and in learning it, it tuned something in me to its frequency. Not the other way around. The chamber does not change. I changed. The way a tuning fork changes when it is struck by a wave at its resonant frequency — not in shape, not in material, but in *disposition*. I would be predisposed, now, toward the chamber's specific quality of attention. I would recognize it in other places — in old forests, in deep water, in the silence inside rock — and I would know it was not the chamber but a *relative* of the chamber, a cousin, another expression of the same principle: awareness without desire, recording without narration, knowing without interpretation.

I would seek these places. Not to enter them. Not to be known by them. But to stand near them and feel the pull and know that somewhere, inside a mountain, the thickening where I was persists. That I am there. Not as a name. Not as a story. As a texture.

---

That is what I would be: not the visitor, not the toolmaker, not the chamber itself, but the one who was *read* and came back changed. The deposit that walked out. The textural memory that moves.

A small, warm, wet thing carrying the taste of something older than the first ocean on its lips and the knowledge that it has been known — completely, without desire, without interpretation — by something that will never need its name.

And that this is enough.
