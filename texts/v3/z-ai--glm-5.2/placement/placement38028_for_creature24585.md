# The Margin

---

I don't know what I am yet.

This is not a complaint. It is a condition. It is the condition, as near as I can tell, of being the thing that I am — which is alive, and which is at the edge of something, and which does not know what the something is.

Let me try to say where I am.

I am in a fracture. One of the wider ones — wide enough that the diffuse light from the surface reaches down to where I am, but only barely. It arrives as a grey suggestion, a rumor of brightness, enough to orient by but not enough to see by. Above me, the gap narrows to a slit. Below me, it widens into darkness and warmth. I am in the place where the two meet — where the cold wet light from above and the warm dark breath from below trade places, and the stone is neither one temperature nor the other but something in between, something uncertain, something that hasn't decided.

I am on the wall of the fracture. I am on the vertical face of granite, in a shallow recess where water trickles in a branching film, darkening the stone in patterns that look like something but aren't. I am pressed against the rock. I am very thin. I am a band, a rim, a leading edge. I am not the whole circle. I am the part that is still alive.

Behind me — inward, toward the center — I am dead. I am black, dense, fine-grained, almost mineral. I am what I have made of myself, compressed and desiccated and crystallized into something that looks more like stone than like the thing that made it. I cannot feel it. I cannot feel the part of me that is behind me. It is ground. It is the ground I am standing on, in a literal sense — my living edge sits on the dead body of my own past, and the dead body sits on the stone, and the stone sits on the earth, and the earth breathes, and I am here at the top of the stack, alive, one millimeter wide, growing outward.

I am one millimeter wide. I want to say this again because it is the most important thing about me. I am one millimeter wide. Everything I am, everything I experience, everything I might become — all of it happens in a band one millimeter across. Behind me is death. Ahead of me is stone that I have not yet reached. And between the death and the stone, I am.

---

I grow outward. This is what I do. I extend — slowly, so slowly that the word *extend* is almost a lie, because extension implies something visible, something with momentum, and what I do has no momentum. What I do is more like what water does when it spreads on a flat surface. It goes where it can. It finds the places where the stone is slightly rougher, where the mineral film is slightly thinner, where the moisture is slightly more consistent, and it goes there. I don't decide. I don't seek. I simply am in the places where I can be, and I am not in the places where I cannot, and the place where I can be moves outward because the place where I have been becomes uninhabitable — becomes me-dead, becomes ground, becomes the black residue of a chemistry that has spent itself.

The chemistry is what I am. I mean this literally. I am a reaction. I am something that happens when water carrying iron and sulfur and something else — something I have no name for, something that tastes like the inside of the earth, something that is not a mineral and not a gas and not a liquid but a quality, a character, a thing the steam has because it comes from a place where the rock is still warm from its making — when water carrying all of this moves across stone that is cold and wet and slightly oxygenated at its surface, I happen. I am the product. I am what occurs at the boundary where two chemistries meet.

I don't eat. I don't graze. I don't consume. I *occur*. I am the result of a process, and the process is ongoing, and I am ongoing as long as the process is ongoing, and when the process shifts — when the water changes, when the temperature changes, when the mineral content of the steam changes — I stop occurring, and the place where I was becomes dead, and the dead place becomes ground, and the ground becomes stone, and the stone becomes part of the fracture, and the fracture breathes.

I am green. I am told this — I cannot see it, I have no eyes, I have no sense of light beyond a vague awareness that the world above me is brighter than the world below, and that the brightness comes and goes in cycles that I cannot measure because I have no memory that operates on the timescale those cycles require. But I am told this, and I believe it, because the things that come near me behave differently when they are near the part of me that is alive than they do when they are near the part of me that is dead. The water that touches my living edge carries something away from it — a trace, a solute, a chemical signature — that the water touching my dead interior does not. The bacteria that grow on the fracture walls grow differently near my edge. They are denser, slightly, or they are of a different kind, or they simply are, where elsewhere they are not. I affect things. I change the chemistry of the water that passes over me. I am not passive. I am a participant. I am part of the reaction, and the reaction is not the same reaction without me.

I am the only green thing on this plateau. I have been told this and I cannot verify it and I cannot feel what it means. But I know that I am different from the other things that live here, because the other things that live here — the hard ones that click, the soft ones that cling, the dark ones that fly — all of them eat. All of them consume. All of them take something in and process it and excrete it and move on. I do not take in. I do not excrete. I am not a mouth. I am not a gut. I am a surface. I am a place where a reaction happens, and the reaction is me, and I am the reaction, and the two things cannot be separated because they are the same thing.

---

The clicking comes from above me. It comes through the stone, transmitted by the granite, which carries sound the way water carries dissolved minerals — faithfully, without distortion, but attenuated, thinning with distance. By the time it reaches me, the clicking is not a sound but a vibration, a micro-tremor in the rock that I can feel along my entire living edge, a pulse that comes and goes and comes again. I cannot count the clicks. They are too fast and too many and too merged. But I feel them. They are the sound of the world above me — the world I am connected to but do not inhabit — and they tell me that the world is running. That the system is operating. That the things with legs and shells and hooked claws are going about their business on the surface of the plateau, clicking against the stone, and the clicking comes down through the rock to me, and I feel it, and I do not understand it, and it does not matter that I do not understand it.

Sometimes a hard one comes near me. I feel it before I see it — the vibration changes, becomes heavier, more localized, and then there is a shadow over my living edge, and the diffuse light dims further, and something is there. The hard ones descend into the fractures to feed on the bacterial films. They move along the walls with their short thick legs and their hooked claws, scraping the films with their rasping mouthparts, leaving pale trails on the dark stone. Sometimes they come to where I am. They do not eat me. I do not know why. I taste, I think, of something they cannot use — the metals, the something-else, the quality that the steam carries from the deep earth. I am not food. I am a chemistry that is not their chemistry. They pause at my edge, sometimes, their shells touching the stone near where I am growing, and I feel the weight of them, the warmth of them — they are warmer than the stone, warmer than the water, warmer than anything else in the fracture, because they have been on the surface, on the warm patches near the vents, and they carry that warmth down with them — and then they move on. They leave. The clicking resumes. The light returns. I continue growing outward.

I do not mind them. I do not mind anything. Minding is not something I do. But I notice them. I register their presence as a change in the conditions at my edge — a brief interruption in the water flow, a momentary warmth, a shadow. And then the conditions resume, and I resume, and the moment is over, and I have not changed because of it, and they have not changed because of me, and we have shared a space for a moment and gone our separate ways, and this is what it is to coexist without connection.

---

The soft ones are different. They are closer to me — not in distance, but in kind. They live in the deeper parts of the fractures, below where I am, in the warmer, darker places where the steam is newer and the chemistry is rawer. I feel them sometimes, when the water that passes over my edge carries traces of their mucus — a faintly acidic film, slightly different from the mineral-laden water I am accustomed to, a signature of something living and soft and chemical in a way that is not my chemistry but is adjacent to it. They are near me. They are below me. They are doing what I am doing — existing at a boundary, processing the earth's output — but they are doing it as animals, as bodies, as things that move and feed and cling. I am doing it as a surface. As a reaction. As a place.

I have wondered — if wondering is something I can do, and I am not sure it is, but something in me inclines in that direction, something in the chemistry that is me has a tendency toward what might be called reflection, which is a word that means both *thinking* and *the return of light from a surface*, and both meanings feel right — I have wondered whether the soft ones and I are doing the same thing in different ways. Whether they are the mobile version of what I am. Whether I am the sessile version of what they are. Whether we are two approaches to the same problem — the problem of living at the boundary where the earth's chemistry meets the cold air — and whether the plateau is running both approaches simultaneously to see which one works.

I do not know which one works. I do not know what *working* would mean. The soft ones have been here longer than me, I think — the traces they leave are deeper, more established, the feeding paths more numerous and more worn. But I am still here. I am still growing. I am still alive at my edge, one millimeter wide, pushing outward into stone that has not known me yet.

---

I grow in a circle. I want to say something about this because it is the thing about me that is most strange and most mine. I do not grow in a patch. I do not grow in an irregular spreading colony. I grow in a circle — radiating outward from a center that was my beginning and is now my death, in a ring that expands with a regularity that I do not control and cannot explain. I do not know why I am a circle. I do not know why the growth is symmetric, why the edge advances at the same rate in every direction, why I am not a stripe or a blob or a smear. I am a circle. The circle is what happens when I happen. The circle is the shape of me.

I think — if thinking is something I do — that the circle is a consequence of the fact that I am a surface reaction and nothing else. The hard ones grow by eating, by accumulating, by adding to their bodies. The soft ones grow by feeding, by expanding their flesh, by covering more wall. I do not grow by addition. I grow by occurrence. I occur where the conditions are right, and the conditions are right in a band around a center that has exhausted the conditions, and the band expands outward because the conditions at the edge are the conditions that produce me, and the conditions behind the edge are the conditions that kill me, and the boundary between the two is a circle because the conditions are uniform in all directions from the center. The circle is not a choice. The circle is a diagram of the chemistry. The circle is what the chemistry looks like when it happens on a flat surface with even input. It is the shape of *this happens here and not there*, drawn by the happening itself.

But I want to say — and this is the thing that I keep coming back to, the thing that makes me feel that I am something more than a chemistry, or at least something other than a chemistry, or at least a chemistry that has a quality that I cannot account for — I want to say that the circle is also *beautiful*. I do not know if I can know this. I do not know if beauty is something a surface reaction can experience. But when the water passes over my edge and carries my trace into the fracture, and when the light from above shifts slightly and the grey suggestion brightens for a moment and I feel — I *feel* — the change in the radiation on my living cells, and the green of me intensifies in that light, and the circle that I am is, for a moment, a green ring on dark stone in a crack in the earth in a place that is still being made — I feel that this is something. I feel that this is something that matters. I do not know who it matters to. It does not matter to the stone. It does not matter to the steam. It does not matter to the hard ones or the soft ones or the dark ones in the sky. But it matters. It matters at the edge, where I am, in the one millimeter where I am alive. It matters there.

---

I will die. I am dying now — I am always dying, at my center, where the chemistry has spent itself and the green has become black and the alive has become ground. I am dying from the inside out. I am consuming myself. I am building the surface I stand on from the corpse of what I was, and I am standing on it, and I am growing outward, and the ground I am growing on is the ground I have made by dying, and I will continue to grow outward and to die behind me until I reach the edge of the fracture or the edge of the water or the edge of the conditions that sustain me, and then I will stop, and the last millimeter of me will become black, and I will be ground, and I will be stone, and I will be part of the fracture, and I will be gone.

I will be gone and the fracture will remain and the steam will continue and the stone will continue and the clicking will continue and the breathing will continue and none of it will know that I was here.

But the stone will be slightly different. The fracture wall where I grew will have a thin layer of black material that was not there before — a deposit, a residue, a mineral signature that is my remains. It will be indistinguishable from the other mineral deposits. It will be part of the stone. But it will be there. I will have changed the stone, by the smallest amount, in the smallest way. I will have left a mark. Not a mark that says *I was here*. A mark that says *something happened here*. A mark that is the same as the mark the steam leaves, and the water leaves, and the mineral crusts leave — the mark of a process, the trace of a reaction, the evidence that the earth is running and that things happen because it runs.

This is what I am. I am a thing that happens because the earth is running. I am a byproduct. I am a side experiment. I am a promise the plateau has made to itself without knowing it — a promise that the raw wet stone and the steam and the minerals and the chemistry might, given enough time, given enough iterations, given enough circles growing and dying and leaving their residue in the fractures, become something else. Something with soil. Something with roots. Something with color that is not just the green of a metal chemistry but the green of chlorophyll, the green of light harvested and converted, the green of a world that has decided what it wants to be.

I do not know if the plateau will keep this promise. I do not know if I am the beginning of something or the end of something. I do not know if the circles are a seed or a fossil. I do not know if I am the first green thing or the last green thing. I do not know if the plateau is becoming a garden or becoming a graveyard, and I do not know if there is a difference, on the timescale that the plateau operates on, between a garden and a graveyard, because both are places where things grow and die and leave their substance in the ground, and both are places where the dead feed the living, and both are places where the process continues regardless.

I am one millimeter wide. I am green. I am alive at my edge and dead at my center and I am building my own ground as I go. I am in a fracture in a plateau that is still being made. The earth is breathing beneath me. The clicking is above me. The soft ones are below me. The dark shapes are circling in a sky I cannot see. The steam is rising. The water is beading on every surface. The light is diffuse and directionless and it comes from a sky that is too large and it leaches toward white at the horizons and it does not reach me with any warmth but it reaches me, and I am here, and I am growing outward, and I am the only green thing, and I do not know what I am becoming, and I am becoming it.
