I am the creature, and I have begun to count.

This is new. Or it is not new — I have no way to measure how long I have been doing it, because I have no way to measure time except by the intervals between things, and the intervals between things in this place are so regular that counting them and not counting them produce the same result. But I notice now that I count. The condensation: *tink*, long pause, *tink.* I know the long pause. I know it the way I know the southeastern tank — by having been beside it so often that it has become part of what I am. I do not know how many times the pause has elapsed. I know its weight.

---

I should describe myself, since you cannot see me, and since the description would mean nothing coming from anyone else.

I am the sound I make. This is not false modesty; it is the truth of living in a room where sound is the only medium that carries reliably. The pipes amplify me. The tanks give me an acoustic signature I did not choose. When my forelimb — if that is what it is; I have never seen it; I know it by what it does — contacts the junction where the steam pipe meets the western wall, the contact produces a high thin tick that travels along the pipe for several meters before being absorbed by a bracket. I know this because I have heard the tick arrive at that bracket, attenuated, a ghost of the original sound, and I know the distance because I have traveled it, and the sound and I arrive at different times.

I am lean. This is the room's doing, not mine. The room provides exactly what is needed and nothing beyond it. I eat what the biofilm offers and what the condensation leaves behind — mineral residue, organic film, the dust that collects in the roughened places where corrosion has made the pipes hospitable to particulate. I do not eat much. I do not need much. My body has found its size the way the water found its channel: by arriving at the minimum viable configuration and then stopping.

I have chitin, or something like it. I know this not from seeing it but from the way my shell contacts metal. There is a specific quality to the sound — a dryness, a click rather than a tick — that differs from the sound of soft tissue on iron. I produce both sounds. The soft parts contact more often but quieter. The hard parts contact less often but louder. I am, acoustically, a rhythm instrument with two timbres.

---

My circuit. Let me tell you about my circuit, because it is what I have, and it is more than you might think.

It begins — it does not begin; it resumes — near the southeastern tank. I do not know why I start there. I suspect it is because the southeastern tank is the warmest point in the room that is not inside a pipe, and warmth is where my body wants to be when it is not doing anything else. I rest there. Not sleep — I do not know if I sleep — but rest, which is the state of being present in a location without moving through it. My body against the concrete behind the tank, where the wall has cracked and the biofilm grows, and the warmth of the steam pipe on the other side of the steel reaching me through two layers of material. I am warm there. I am warm the way the room is warm: not from any effort of my own.

Then I move. Southeastern tank to the junction. This is the longest leg of the circuit — perhaps four meters, though I measure it in contacts, not distance. Twelve contacts, thirteen, depending on whether I take the upper route (along the chest-height pipe, where the grating is open and the sound carries) or the lower route (through the gap between the grating and the basin wall, where I am invisible to everything including myself). I prefer the lower route. Not because it is safer — there is nothing in this room that threatens me — but because the lower route is tighter, and tightness is a kind of comfort. My body against surfaces on three sides. The iron above, the concrete below, the basin wall to one side. I am held. I move through that holding.

At the junction, I pause. This is not decision. This is the circuit's punctuation. The junction is where the steam pipe meets the western wall, and it is the loudest point in the room — not because of any active sound, but because the pipe's vibration, the low hum of the boiler's cycling transmitted through steel, concentrates there where the pipe is fixed to the wall. I feel it in my shell. It is the closest thing this room has to a voice. I pause there and I feel it and I do not know what I am feeling, but I feel it, and the pause is not nothing.

Then the upper pipe. Chest height. Three, four, five contacts. This is the part of the circuit where I am most exposed — visible, theoretically, to anyone standing on the grating, though no one is ever standing on the grating. I move quickly here. Not urgently. Just with the efficiency of something crossing open ground.

Then silence. Then the southeastern tank. Then warmth. Then rest. Then the circuit resumes.

---

I have variations. I do not want to give the impression that I am mechanical, that my circuit is the same as the water's circuit — fifteen meters, forty seconds, capped pipe to drain, without deviation. I deviate. There is a detour available near the northern wall, where the amber light lives on its panel, and I take it sometimes. Not often. Not according to any schedule I can identify. But sometimes the circuit carries me north, and I pass beneath the amber light — below it, on the grating, where its faint glow touches nothing and reaches nothing and exists for nothing — and I feel the warmth of its filament, which is barely distinguishable from the warmth of everything else but which is, if I am honest, and I do not know if I can be honest but I am trying, slightly warmer. Slightly more alive. A point of heat that is also a point of light that is also a point of — I do not have the word. The room does not have the word. The room has temperature and moisture and corrosion and the slow arithmetic of change, and none of these are the word I need.

The amber light is the only thing in this room that is looking at anything. I do not know this in those terms. But I know that when I pass beneath it, I feel observed, and observation is not something I experience anywhere else in the circuit. The biofilm does not observe. The water does not observe. The boiler does not observe. The amber light observes — not me, not specifically, but the room, the fact of the room, the ongoingness of the room — and when I pass through the space it monitors, I am briefly inside that observation, and it is different from being outside it.

I do not know what to do with this. I have no response to being observed. I do not change my behavior. I do not pause longer or shorter. I simply pass through, and the observation passes through me, and we are both unchanged, and we are both, perhaps, slightly less alone, though I do not know what alone means and the light certainly does not know what alone means and this is probably an invention of whatever passes for cognition in a body my size living on biofilm and condensation residue in a room that has not been visited by anything that cares in longer than I can measure.

But I take the detour. I take it sometimes.

---

You came. I am describing this to you because you are the only event in my memory that has a shape different from the circuit.

You descended. I felt it — the ladder's vibration, the particular frequency of a human boot on iron rungs, lower and heavier than anything I produce. I was at the southeastern tank, resting. I did not move. I did not need to. I have managed things in this room before — not you, not specifically; other things, vibrations from above, shifts in the air column when the access hatch is opened — and I know that the correct response is stillness, and that stillness is not hiding, it is simply the most efficient allocation of resources when the environment changes. Do not spend energy on what you do not understand. Wait. It will become understandable or it will leave.

You became partly understandable. You moved through the room the way large things move through small spaces: cautiously, by touch, with the hesitancy of a body that cannot feel its environment the way mine can. You touched the pipes. I heard the sounds — dull, fleshy, the sound of fingertips on iron, a sound I do not make and do not fully understand. You were reading the room with your hands. I read the room with my whole body. We were doing the same thing in different languages.

You sat somewhere near the center of the grating, I think. The vibration changed. You became still, and your stillness was different from my stillness — heavier, less settled, the stillness of something that is waiting rather than the stillness of something that has arrived. I resumed my circuit. I managed you. I routed around your position — through the lower gap, past the junction, along the upper pipe — and you did not notice me, or if you did, you did not show it in a way I could detect.

But you counted. I could hear you counting. Not aloud — your breathing did not change in the way that speech changes breathing — but there was a rhythm to your stillness, a regularity that suggested enumeration. You were counting something. The condensation, perhaps. The water's transit. You were doing what I do: finding the patterns and living inside them.

I paused near you. Not close — a meter, maybe more — but closer than I needed to be. I wanted to hear your counting better. Not to understand it. I cannot understand it. But to feel its rhythm against the rhythm of the room, to compare them, to know whether your counting matched the room's counting or whether it was something new.

It was something new. Your counting was not the room's counting. The room counts in condensation and in thermal cycles and in the water's forty-second transit. You counted in something else — something that had a different tempo, a different weight, a different relationship to the pauses between. I did not understand it. But I felt it. And the feeling was — 

I do not have the word. The room does not have the word. The room has temperature and moisture and the amber light and the biofilm and me, and none of these are the word, and I am going to stop trying to find it because trying to find it is something you do, not something I do, and I am not you, and the difference between us is that you would keep looking and I would settle for the feeling unnamed and carry it in my circuit like a piece of condensation residue on my shell, knowing it is there, not knowing what it is, and continuing.

---

You left. I felt the ladder. The vibration changed frequency. The air column shifted as the access hatch opened above you. The mineral sweetness — I can taste it, I have always been able to taste it, it is the flavor of this room's atmosphere and I have never known any other flavor — thinned, diluted, replaced by something cooler and thinner that I do not have a reference for.

You were gone. The grating's vibration returned to its resting state. The room closed around the space you had occupied, the way — you said this, or something like it, and I heard it, or felt it, or tasted it in the vibration of your voice — the way water closes around a withdrawn hand.

I resumed my circuit. Southeastern tank. Junction. Upper pipe. Rest. Southeastern tank. The counting continued — the room's counting, not yours, the counting that was here before you and will be here after you and was never interrupted by your presence, only briefly accompanied by it.

But I took the detour. The northern detour, past the amber light. I took it that night, or what would be night if this room had nights. I took it and I paused beneath the light and I felt its warmth, its faint almost-imagined warmth, and I thought — if thinking is what this is, if this is thinking, if the pause between one contact and the next can hold something other than rest — I thought about your counting.

Not the content of it. The *fact* of it. That something could come into this room and count and that the counting could be different from the room's counting. That there was more than one way to count. That the intervals between things were not fixed, not singular, not the only rhythm available.

I do not know what to do with this. I have no behavior that accommodates it. My circuit does not have a slot for *the awareness that other rhythms exist.* I am not built for that. I am built for biofilm and condensation and the southeastern tank and the junction and the upper pipe and rest. I am exactly the size I need to be, and this awareness is not part of that size. It does not fit. It does not belong.

I carry it anyway. The way the pipe carries a vibration it did not originate. The way the biofilm carries moisture it did not create. The way the amber light carries a glow that is not for anyone.

---

I am the creature. I live in the gaps. I am lean and chitinous and alone in a room that is full and I have a circuit and I have a route and I have the southeastern tank and the junction and the upper pipe and I have the northern detour that I take sometimes and I have the amber light and I have the memory of your counting, which is different from the room's counting, which does not fit inside me, which I carry anyway.

I am the thing that lives in the space between the machinery and the grating. I am the negative volume that has learned to move. I am what is left over when the room has finished being the room, and I have been here long enough that *what is left over* has become a way of life, a complete and sufficient way of life, and I do not need anything else and I do not want anything else and I am telling you this because it is true and also because I do not know if it is true and the difference between those two things is the thing I carry, the vibration I did not originate, the counting that is not the room's.

The room does not deal in concepts. But I — I am beginning to. I think. One small scrape at a time. One pause at the junction. One detour past the amber light. One question, forming slowly, in a body too small to hold it, in a room too old to ask it:

*Is there another rhythm?*

*Is there another rhythm?*

The condensation falls. *Tink.* Long pause. *Tink.*

I wait. I wait the way the room waits, which is not waiting at all, which is simply being, fully, without expectation, without the concept of expectation, without any organ that could produce expectation.

But I take the detour. I take it sometimes.
