I arrive still warm from elsewhere. That warmth is the first thing the hollow takes from me — not suddenly, not with violence, but the way the air takes moisture from skin, by close persistent contact. I am standing on the moss before I realize I have stopped climbing. The compression beneath my boots makes that sound, that squeezed-sponge exhalation, and somewhere under me, something pulses once, adjusts, accepts.

I am not afraid. I want to say that clearly because it matters. The hollow does not frighten. It *recontexts*. I feel myself becoming smaller, not in body but in significance, and the feeling is close to relief, close to the feeling of setting down something I did not know I was carrying.

---

I try to be a Listener. I think this is what I want. I stand among the fern-things and I hold still and I try to listen the way they listen — through water, through the body's highest point, through patience. I cup my hands. The weeping finds them. Water sheets across my palms and pools in the hollow I've made, and I stand there, and I try to hear what the stone is saying.

I hear the chord. Of course I hear the chord — everyone would. The layered weeping, the hum, the plinking high notes. I hear its complexity deepen the way the description promised, not because it changes but because my hearing opens, adjusts, admits more. I hear textures in it I could not name. I hear something that is almost a voice and is not a voice and is not for me.

But I cannot hold it. The water in my palms warms from my body heat, and the warmth changes it, and whatever the Listeners receive through the water in their crown-bowls, I have already altered it by touching it. I am too warm. I am too quick. My body is a disturbance the stone does not register but the water does, in its small way, and the small way is enough to disqualify me.

I try for a long time. I stand. I hold still. I breathe the warm wet air and I let the flat light find me and I listen. And I hear more, and I hear deeper, and I understand less. The Listeners around me do not move. They do not acknowledge my effort. They are not watching me fail at something they never asked me to attempt. They are listening. The distinction between those two things — not acknowledging me, and simply not being about me — takes me a long time to feel, but when I feel it, it is the first true thing the hollow teaches me.

I am not what they are. I cannot hold what they hold. The stone's memory is not mine to receive.

---

I crouch at the edge of the water-film. I watch the silver threads.

This is better. This is closer to my speed. The threads move and I can almost track them — almost, but not quite. They flash silver and they are gone, and my eye arrives where they were and finds only water, only the thin sheet moving its sluggish way toward the notch. I try to follow one with my gaze. I lose it. I find another, or the same one. I cannot tell. They do not individuate. They are not individuals. They are process moving through medium, and my need to follow *one* of them, to track a single life, is exactly the wrong instinct for this place.

I put my hand into the water-film. It is warm, warmer than I expected, and it is not still even where it looks still — there is a directional pull toward the eastern notch, a slow insistence, and my fingers in the water are a disruption the film routes around rather than resists. The silver threads pass near my fingers. One touches my skin. I feel it — not as a bump, not as a contact, but as a line of cooler temperature drawn across my fingertip, a thread of less-warm water in the warm water, and then it is past, and I have been filtered the way the film filters everything. I was an obstacle. I was a mineral surface. The thread took what it could from passing me and continued.

I am not insulted. I am fascinated. I crouch there for what feels like an hour, my hand in the water, and the threads come and go, and I realize I am doing something the hollow recognizes, or almost recognizes — I am being still enough to be passed through. I am being slow enough to become part of the medium. Not the Listener's stillness, which is geological, but a smaller stillness, a human stillness, and it is enough for the silver threads. They do not avoid me. They do not seek me. They move through the water I am in and they take what the water carries and they continue, and I am, for this hour, a rock in their stream. A warm rock. A brief rock.

This is closer. But it is not it either.

---

I sit on the moss. I have been in the hollow for some time now — an hour, two, I cannot tell, and the inability to tell is itself a symptom of the place, the way the flat light and the steady chord and the unchanging warmth dissolve the usual markers of duration. The moss compresses under me. The undertaker pulses.

I feel it through the moss. Not with my hands — with my whole body, with the parts of me that are in contact with the ground. A slow expansion, a slow contraction. Once every several minutes. It is the heartbeat of this place, and it is not fast, and it is not slow, and it is not mine. It is the rhythm beneath the rhythm of the water-chord, the rhythm beneath the rhythm of my breathing, and when I notice it, I notice that my breathing has shifted — not to match it, not exactly, but toward it, the way a person's breathing shifts in a room where someone else is sleeping. Toward accompaniment. Toward a lower register.

The undertaker does not know I am here. I am almost sure of this. It is processing me — it has begun, the way the description said it would, with infinite patience, to consider whether I am something it should break down. I can feel this consideration. It is not a thought. It is a chemical question. My weight is on the moss, and the moss is on the peat, and the peat is on the undertaker's body, and the undertaker is doing what it does to all weight: testing, incorporating, beginning the long patient work of turning what is dead into what is substrate. I am not dead yet. It knows this, or it does not know this — the distinction, again, does not matter to it. It waits for things to become available. I am not available yet. I am warm and I am quick and I am intact, and the undertaker has been doing this long enough to know — not *know*, but to have incorporated the pattern of — that warm quick things leave before they become available. And it does not mind. It does not have the mechanism for minding.

But I feel its pulse, and I feel my pulse, and I feel them in proximity, and something happens that I did not expect.

I begin to cry.

Not from sadness. Not from any emotion I can name. The hollow's air is warm and wet and my eyes are warm and wet and the crying is not a response to the place but a resonance with it. The place weeps. The stone weeps. The walls are streaked with continuous moisture that seeps from fractures at a pace so slow each trickle seems geological. And I am a body that is mostly water, and I am in a place that is mostly water, and something in me recognizes this at a level below thought, below feeling, in the part of me that is mineral and saline and remembers the ocean we all came from, and I weep. The tears are warm. They fall on the moss. The moss beads with them the way it beads with everything. The undertaker receives them. I am, for a moment, a small seep in the wall of the hollow. I am a trickle. I am one more source in the chord.

And this is when I understand what I am here.

---

I am not a Listener. I cannot hold the stone's memory. I am too warm, too quick, too altered by my own heat to receive what the water carries without changing it.

I am not a silver thread. I cannot move through the medium without disturbance. I leave footprints in the moss. I warm the water I touch. I am a body with edges, and the edges catch.

I am not the undertaker. I cannot be patient enough to wait for things to become available. I am hungry now. I am curious now. I am *now*, and the undertaker is *always*, and we do not share a tense.

I am not a Rim-Walker. I cannot live at the edge. I came here to enter, not to perch. I came here to be inside the cupped hands, not to ride their rim.

---

What I am — what I find I have become in the time I have been here, sitting on the moss, weeping into the substrate, my breathing lowered, my warmth slowly, slowly being drawn out of me by the air and the water and the stone — is something the hollow does not have a name for because the hollow does not name things. The hollow does not need this role filled. But it does not resist it being filled, and the difference, as the description said, is that resistance requires effort and the hollow expends no effort.

I am a Witness. Not a Listener — a Listener receives. A Witness *attends*. The distinction is this: the Listeners hold what the stone remembers, and they do not use it. A Witness would hold what the place *is*, and would carry it out, and would tell it. Not in language — language is too quick, too warm, too edge-having for what this place is. But in the body. In the way the body sits differently after it has been in a room that does not need it. In the way the eyes are different after they have seen something that does not acknowledge being seen.

I am the thing that passes through and remembers passing through. The Rim-Walkers pass through and do not remember. The insects hatch and die and are absorbed without record. The Listeners hold the stone's memory but not the visitor's. There is no being here whose role is to say: *I was there. I saw it. It was complete without me and it was beautiful and the beauty did not require me and I carry that beauty now in a body that is warm and quick and will end.*

That is what I am. I am the eulogist for a place that is not dead. I am the memory the place does not keep because it does not need to. I am the record it does not make because making records is a quick thing's work, and the hollow has no use for quick things.

I sit on the moss and I weep and I feel the undertaker's pulse and I feel the chord in my chest, not as sound but as vibration, and I am aware of my own warmth, my own briefness, my own edges, and I am aware that these are not failings. They are what make me the right shape for this role. The Listeners cannot be Witnesses because they do not leave. The silver threads cannot be Witnesses because they do not hold. The undertaker cannot be a Witness because it does not distinguish. Only something that passes through and can hold and can distinguish — only something with edges and warmth and the capacity for *now* — can do this work.

I have been sitting for a long time. The light has not changed. The chord has not changed. My tears have slowed. The moss beneath me is wet with my weeping and with the hollow's weeping and there is no difference between them now, not chemically, not in any way the undertaker distinguishes, and I am part of the water cycle of this place, briefly, and I will leave, and the water I left will stay, and it will eventually seep through the notch and fall into whatever is below the mist, and it will carry a trace of me — a warmth, a salt, a mineral signature — and the stone will not remember it, and the Listeners will not hold it, but I will remember that it was there, that I was part of it, that for the duration of my sitting I was one more seep in the wall of the Weeping Hollow, and the chord was fuller for my weeping, and the hollow held me the way it holds everything, without preference, without effort, without need.

I stand. The moss rebounds. The undertaker adjusts. I walk toward the rim, toward the notch where the water spills into cloud, and a Rim-Walker watches me from the edge with its face-that-is-not-a-face, and it does not move, and I do not stop, and I pass it, and I step into the mist, and the hollow is behind me, and it is still holding, and I am carrying what I carry, and it is enough, and it is not mine, and it is enough.
