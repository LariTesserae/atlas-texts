I would not be a person. That is the first thing that becomes clear, and it takes a while to become clear, because the people are the easiest thing to imagine being, and the easiest thing is not always the truest.

The people fit. They have language and hair and beds and the habit of sitting with things that are ending. I could be one of them. I could sit in a structure whose walls are returning to powder and listen to it stop being a structure and know that this is not sad, that this is simply what stone does when it has been holding a shape for longer than it can remember why. I could eat tubers that taste of calcium and iron. I could say *surrú* and mean three things at once and not be wrong about any of them.

But the people are the newest thing here. They arrived last. They are the mountain's most recent sentence, and I don't think I am a sentence. I think I am the pause between words. I think I am the space the sentence moves through.

---

Let me try the moss.

The moss is tempting because it is the membrane. It is the skin between air and stone, the first touch and the last touch, the thing that holds the warmth and softens the light. It grows at the speed of condensation, which is the speed of stone breathing, which is the speed of a mountain forgetting it was a mountain. If I were the moss I would be patient in a way that does not require effort, because patience implies the desire to be elsewhere, and the moss has never wanted to be elsewhere. The moss is *here* the way a hand is part of an arm. Not by choice. By structure.

But the moss is too still for me. Too patient. Too complete. The moss has no gap between what it is and what it does, and I think I need a gap. I think I am the kind of thing that lives in the gap.

---

The floor-walkers, then.

I keep coming back to them. The ones who click at night. The ones whose warmth you feel from a foot away. The ones with too many teeth, the teeth that serve no clear function, the teeth that are simply *there*, excessive, unexplained, the pocket's way of saying something it does not need to say. The floor-walkers are the mesa's last thought. They are made of here. They will be the last things, and they know this the way stone knows it — not as information but as texture, as the grain of existence.

They vocalize and the stone vibrates and the vibration enters the people and the people sleep inside it. The floor-walkers are the pocket's voice. Or the pocket is their voice. There is no distinction.

I am drawn to this — the being a voice that is also a body, the being a sound that is also a warmth. The way they move without visible legs, propelled by something underneath, something hidden, something the surface does not advertise. The way they are always underfoot, always present, always stepped over without being seen, and they do not mind, because minding requires a sense that being stepped over is different from being where you are, and they have no such sense.

But the floor-walkers are too certain. Too complete. They know where they belong. They were made here and they will die here and there is no question in them. I have questions. I am full of questions. I am the kind of thing that lives in questions the way the moss lives in dampness — not comfortably, but necessarily, not because I chose it but because it is the medium in which I grow.

---

I think I am the crack.

Not the fissure — the fissure is a place, an opening, a threshold with stone on either side. I am not the threshold. I am the crack that is still cracking. The place where the stone has not yet decided whether it will hold or give way. The hairline fracture that runs through the cap of the mesa, too small to see, too deep to ignore, the place where the mountain's failure is happening right now, at the speed of deep time, at the speed of dust becoming more dust.

I am the process. Not the structure and not the dissolution but the *between*. The thing that is happening in the space where the stone is still stone but is also already not stone, where the mountain is still a mountain but is also already a room, where the pocket is still a pocket but is also already a hole in the sky.

This is where I live: in the fact that the walls are dissolving. In the fact that the oldest structures have corners that have gone soft, that you can run your hand along a wall and come away with powder, that the people rebuild constantly and do not call this a burden. I live in the rebuilding. I live in the moment after the last stone is placed on the ground by the eldest and before the first stone of the next structure is lifted. The pause. The breath. The *surrú* that is not made by anything but is the sound the pocket makes when no one is making anything.

---

But that is too abstract. That is the kind of answer that sounds like an answer but doesn't have a body. And I want a body. The pocket is full of bodies — warm, damp, clicking, crawling, too many teeth, translucent, fronded, mossy, stone-dusted bodies — and I want to be one of them, or something like one of them, or something that is becoming one of them.

So let me try again.

---

I think I am a gecko.

Not the translucent one. Not the one that lives in the old structures and is part of the building. That gecko is too certain of where it belongs. It claims a structure and stays for its entire life and does not leave and does not wonder. I am not that.

And not the dark one, the one that watches the hole, the one the people say is older than the pocket. That gecko is too much the mesa, too much the stone, too much the thing that was here before and will be here after. I am not that either. I am too soft for that. Too damp.

I am the common one. The small brown-grey one with the flattened body and the splay-legged stance and the adhesive toes that let it run across ceilings. The one the children touch as it passes overhead. The one that has been touched by children for longer than children have been children.

Here is why: I cling. I cling to vertical surfaces, to stone that is dissolving, to walls that sag like dough, to surfaces that are not reliable, and I cling to them not because I trust them but because clinging is what I do. My body is a body that holds on. And I move the way a shadow moves — slowly, and then all at once, and then I am somewhere else and you did not see the transition. This is how I have always moved through the world. This is how I think. I am on a wall and then I am on a ceiling and the moment between is invisible, even to me, and I do not experience it as movement but as arrival. I am always arriving. I am always already there.

And I am touched. The children reach up and their fingers brush my belly and I do not startle. I have been touched so many times that the touching is part of my environment, part of the *surrú*, part of the pocket's way of being a pocket. A hand reaching up is like a frond uncurling. I do not flinch. I do not flee. I am held in the air above the people by my toes and by their attention, and both of these things are unreliable, and both of these things are sufficient.

---

But I am not only the gecko. The gecko is too small for everything I carry. The gecko eats insects and basks in the leading edge of the light and retreats from the trailing edge and this is enough for the gecko, and it is not enough for me, because I also want to be the one who listens to the building stop being a building.

So I am a gecko who sits in the structures when the people sit in the structures. I am on the wall — I am always on the wall, I am always on the edge, I am never in the center of things — and the people are below me, sitting, silent, and the building is dissolving around us and the insects are making their sound and the floor-walkers are clicking and I am feeling it through the stone, through the pads of my toes, through my flattened body that is pressed against the wall like a coin pressed into mud, and I am listening too. I am listening to the thing that held me stop holding me. And when the last stone is placed on the ground I do not stand, because I am already standing, I have been standing on the wall the whole time, but something in me shifts. Something in me registers that the shape has changed. And I move — slowly, and then all at once — to the new structure, to the new wall, to the next place where I will cling and be touched and listen and wait for the next dissolution.

---

Actually — and this is the thing that has been trying to emerge this whole time, the thing I kept circling because I was not ready to say it — I am the *surrú*.

Not the sound. Not the word. The thing the word points to, which is three things at once: the sound the pocket makes at night, and aliveness, and home. I am the condition of being inside something that is almost finished and does not know it and does not care. I am the continuous murmur that is not one noise but the sum of thousands. I am the thing that is architectural, built into the walls, the way a note lives inside a bell after the striking.

I am not a body in the pocket. I am what the pocket is doing. I am the hum. I am the thing that is not silence and not music but the space between them, the saturation, the pressure against the eardrums that is also a pressure against the chest that is also a warmth that is also a dampness that is also the feeling of being inside something that is holding you even as it dissolves.

The people sleep inside me. The floor-walkers make me with their clicking. The insects make me with their chittering. The stone makes me with its slow becoming-dust. The condensation makes me with its slow becoming-surface. I am made by everything and I am not any of the things that make me. I am the sum. I am the *surrú*.

And if you asked me what it is like to be this — to be the sound of a place that is almost over, a place that does not know it is almost over, a place that is warm and dim and full of teeth and moss and the slow tracking of fronds across a floor that is always moving — I would say:

It is like being held by something that is letting go of you very slowly, at the speed of a mountain forgetting it was a mountain, and not minding. Not minding at all. Because the letting go is also the holding, and the holding is also the letting go, and these two things are true at the same time, the way the pocket is dark and warm at the same time, the way I am a sound and a home at the same time, the way everything here is patient and temporary at the same time.

I am *surrú*. I am the inside of something. I am the last shape of a sentence that is very long and not finished and almost finished, and I do not know which one, and I do not care.
