# The Cell at Third Row, Eastern Wall, the Deep One

---

I am writing this in a language that is not mine. Not the upland tongue, which I have heard spoken three times in my life and which sounds like gravel falling into gravel. Not the gestures that are my first language, which cannot be written because they exist in the space between bodies and do not survive the removal. This — these marks, this linear scrape of meaning onto surface — is something else. A third tongue. One I am building from fragments the visitor left behind.

The visitor. Yes. There was a visitor. I should begin there, or near there, because the visitor is the reason I am doing this at all, and also because the visitor is the reason I have the concept of *doing this at all* — of taking something interior and making it exterior, of pressing thought against a surface the way I press mycelium against wet stone. The visitor left things. A book, paper-bound, water-damaged within hours but I dried the pages against my chest and the text is broken and partial and I have spent — I do not know how long. Long. I have spent long with those pages. And from the broken text I have assembled the idea that a person can make marks that carry meaning forward through time, and I have been practicing, and the marks are ugly, and I am using a stone stylus on a piece of dried mycelium sheet that I have smoothed and re-smoothed until it will hold a scratch, and the scratches are what you are reading now, if you are reading this, if anyone is reading this, which I cannot assume because I do not know if the concept of *audience* applies to what I am doing. I am doing it anyway. I am doing it because the sound has not changed and I have not changed and the quarter has not changed and something in me — something that may be the residue of the visitor's presence, a condensation bead of foreignness that fell on me and was absorbed — wants to make a mark that was not here before. Wants to be the exception.

I will regret this. I think I will regret this. The system does not need marks. The system does not need exceptions. But here I am, pressing stylus to sheet, and the water is falling where it falls, and the sound is roaring where it roars, and I am doing something new, and the new thing feels like a crack in a wall that has been whole for longer than anyone remembers.

---

## What I am

I have a position. Not a name. The third row of the eastern wall, the deep cell — this is where I sleep, and the cell is identified by its position, and the position is identified by the cell, and I am identified by both, and the identification is stable, and it is enough. But *position* is not the whole of what I am. I have a function, and the function is older than me, and when I die the function will continue in another body, and the body that carries the function will become me in the way that matters, which is not the body but the function, which is not the function but the way the body moves through the quarter listening.

I tend the resonators.

No. That is the visitor's phrasing. The visitor asked the elder what I do, and the elder made the gesture — flat hand, fingers curved, pressed to the air in front of a cavity, then the palm cupped behind the ear — and the visitor wrote *tends the resonators* in his broken book, and the phrase is not wrong but it is not mine. What I do is this: I listen. Not as an activity. Not as something I choose to do. I listen the way the walls listen. The way the drainage channels listen. I am a surface the sound strikes, and I know what it sounds like when it strikes, and I know when it sounds different, and I know where to go when the sound is wrong.

The sound is rarely wrong. I should say this. The system is at equilibrium. The water falls where it falls. The condensation rates are constant. The resonators sing the notes they have always sung. But *rarely* is not *never*, and when the sound changes — when a drainage channel silts, when a lip erodes, when a crack in the ceiling opens and the water finds a new path — I feel it before I hear it. In my teeth. In the fine bones of my inner ear. A wrongness. A deviation. A note in the organ that should not be there, or a note that should be there and isn't.

I go to it. I find it. I correct it. A scratch of a blade to open a channel. A handful of packed mycelium pressed into a crack. A stone, small, wedged beneath a drip to redirect it back to the path it is supposed to follow. The corrections are small. They are always small. The system does not need large corrections because the system is at equilibrium, and equilibrium is not stasis — it is the active maintenance of a state, the constant small adjustments that keep a thing where it is. I am one of those adjustments. I am the thing that keeps the sound the same.

I was not always this.

There was a time before I had this function, and in that time I was — I think I was the girl. The one who follows the basin-checker. The one who watches the pale things with the steady regard of someone confirming what is already known. I was that girl.
