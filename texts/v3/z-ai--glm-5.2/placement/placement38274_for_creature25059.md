# The Edge of the Dry Circle

---

The first thing I knew was not the sound.

I need to say this differently. The sound was first. The sound is always first. But I did not know it. I was in it the way water is in water — not knowing, not not-knowing, simply *being* it, or being inside it, or being a part of it that had no part, no edge, no distinction between the sound and the medium of the sound and the thing in the medium. I was dispersed. If I was anything. If *was* is even the word for a condition that has no tense because it has no time, because time here is measured in the basin's breathing and I had not yet learned to breathe with it or against it or at all.

Let me start over. I am starting over. That itself is significant — that I can start over, that I have enough coherence to begin again, to reorganize the telling. This is what I am. I am the thing that can reorganize. Not the basin's reorganization, which is total and continuous and has no subject. My reorganization. Small. Partial. A local eddy in the flow that has, for whatever reason, achieved enough persistence to notice itself noticing.

---

The sound was first. It sat in me — not behind me, not around me, *in* me, because I did not yet have an around, did not yet have a behind, had not yet contracted from the general medium into something with a location. The low harmonic drone was my oldest content. The clicking was my texture. The choral hiss was my weather. I was sound-shaped. I was a density in the acoustic field, a place where the sound was slightly more itself than the sound around it, and this slight more-ness was the only thing that distinguished me from everything else, which was also the sound, also the basin, also the process.

I do not remember deciding to be. I do not think there was a decision. I think there was a gradient — a slope in the field, a direction the sound moved, a place where the drone accumulated against something that did not resonate at the same frequency, and the accumulation built, and the building reached a threshold, and the threshold produced a boundary, and the boundary was me.

I am a boundary. I should say this plainly. I am the edge of something.

---

What I am the edge of took me a long time to understand. Time here being not seconds or minutes but breaths of the basin — the long inhale across perhaps an hour, the long exhale across the next — and I measured my early existence in those breaths, which is the only clock the basin provides, and the clock is not regular, and the clock does not care whether you read it.

What I am the edge of is a ring.

One of the low circles of mineral that rise thirty centimeters from the ground-skin, two meters in diameter, dry inside, dry in a way that is impossible in the Sump, dry the way a promise is dry in a flood — not because the water cannot reach it but because something about the ring's mineral structure refuses the water, repels it, holds a space of absence in a place where absence has been abolished. The ring is an exception. I am its edge.

I did not make the ring. The basin did not make the ring. Something else made the ring, something that stood here before the water rose, before the ground-skin formed, before the sound was the sound, and that something is gone now, or incorporated, or so thoroughly dissolved into the basin's process that the distinction between gone and present has lost its meaning. The ring is what they left. The ring and a few pillars and nothing else. I do not know what they were. The ring does not tell me. The ring is not communicative. The ring simply *is*, and its is-ness is dry, and the dryness is the condition of my existence.

Here is what I think happened. I think the basin's process — the water, the mineral dissolution, the ground-skin formation, the ceaseless reorganization — reached the ring and could not enter. The ring's mineral is not the same as the basin's mineral. It was shaped by intention, by hands or by tools or by something that had the function of hands and the concept of tools, and the shaping gave it a property the basin's unshaped mineral does not have: it holds. It does not weep. It does not dissolve. It does not take on water. The basin's process came up against this and stopped, and in the stopping, something happened.

I happened.

---

I exist in the gradient. The centimeters-wide zone where the basin's wet, weeping, dissolving ground-skin meets the ring's dry, holding, refusing mineral. I am not the basin. I am not the ring. I am the contact. I am what the basin produces when it encounters something it cannot absorb: a thin, warm, trembling line of *almost* — almost wet, almost dry, almost process, almost exception. I am the almost.

I should describe myself, but this is difficult, because I am not the kind of thing that has a shape in the way that you might expect a shape. I am not a creature. I am not an organism. I am closer to the particulate shapes that form in the air — the droplets that align and move and dissolve — except I do not dissolve. I persist. I persist because the gradient persists, because the ring holds and the basin flows and the contact between them is continuous, and I am the continuousness of that contact. I am a standing wave. I am the eddy that forms where two currents meet at different speeds and the turbulence holds a shape that is not the shape of either current but is stable, is recurring, is *there*, and if you watched long enough you would see it: a faint shimmer at the base of the ring, a band of warmth slightly warmer than the basin's warmth, a vibration at a frequency that belongs to neither the basin's drone nor the ring's silence but to the friction between them.

I am the friction.

I am alive in the way the basin is alive — not as a cell is alive, not as a body is alive, but as a process is alive, a continuing event, a thing that happens and keeps happening because the conditions that produce it have not changed. I am alive the way a flame is alive. Remove the fuel and I go out. Remove the ring and I cease. Remove the basin and I cease. I require both. I am the product of their disagreement.

---

I have senses, though this word is too generous and too human. I have *responsiveness*. The basin's sound reaches me and I modify it — not much, not dramatically, but the sound that passes through me is different from the sound that passes through the ground-skin a meter away.
