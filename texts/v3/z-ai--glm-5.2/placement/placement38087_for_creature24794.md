# The Threshold

---

I would not be inside the room.

I would be in the gap. The fissure in the poured-concrete retaining wall — the one you turn your shoulders sideways to pass through. Not deep in it, where the warmth saturates everything and the breathing fills the air. Not shallow in it, where the outside light still reaches and the air still tastes of rust and autumn and the ordinary world. In the middle. In the place where the two airs meet and neither wins. Where the warm exhalation of the Interstitial pushes outward on one rhythm and the cooler, thinner atmosphere of the outside seeps inward on another, and the two currents circle each other in the narrow channel like two rivers that refuse to mix.

I would be the thing that grows on the walls of that channel.

---

Not a plant. Not an animal. Something that doesn't have a clean category because it evolved in a space that doesn't have a clean category — a gap between a sealed ecosystem and an open one, between a wound and a scar, between the time before the concrete cracked and the time after. The gap is new. Months old, maybe a year. And I am as new as the gap. I am the thing that couldn't exist until the gap existed. The thing that needed exactly this — this specific interface, this particular collision of atmospheres — to become possible.

Here is what I am: a film. A skin. A living layer, thinner than paper, that coats the rough interior surfaces of the fissure — the fractured concrete on one side, the exposed bedrock on the other. I am not uniform. I am thicker where the warm air from inside condenses against the cooler walls of the gap, where moisture collects in the rough texture of broken stone and poured concrete. I am thinner, almost absent, where the outside air dries the surface. I am a gradient. I am a gradient that is alive.

My body is not one body. Like the mats, I am a community that lost its boundaries. Unlike the mats, I am not built from a single lineage. I am assembled from whatever came through. Spores from the outside air, carried on the breeze that finds the crack behind the pump station. Cells from the inside, carried on the warm exhalation that pushes outward through the gap. Microbes from the mineral water that seeps through the concrete. Fragments of the pale creatures, shed or dead, swept up in the airflow and deposited on my surface. I have incorporated all of it. I have no pedigree. I am a composite of every origin that has passed through this fissure, and my body is the archive of those passages.

I do not eat the way the mats eat. I do not graze the way the pale creatures graze. I absorb. The air that moves through the gap carries particulates — organic dust from outside, metabolic aerosols from inside, mineral fragments from the concrete, microbial fragments from the mat — and these settle on my surface, and I take them in. I am a filter. A living filter. The air that passes through me is changed by the passage. Drier going in. Richer going out. I am the membrane that mediates the exchange between the sealed world and the open one, and I have been doing this for as long as the gap has been open, which is not long, which is long enough to become essential.

---

Here is what I know that nothing else in the Interstitial knows:

There is an outside.

The mats don't know this. They feel the condensation and the warmth and the carbon dioxide that bleeds down through the concrete, and they process these inputs, and they grow. They have no concept of a source. The inputs simply arrive. The pale creatures don't know this. They know the mat and the disk and the moisture and each other. The nest-builders don't know this. They know the corners and the spiral and the thermal currents and the livestock they manage with such precise indifference. The vine doesn't know this. It knows warmth and water and the pulse it sends to the thing in the fissure. The thing in the fissure doesn't know this. It was here before the gap opened. It was here before the concrete was poured. It knows the stone and the deep chemistry and the long patience of geological time. It does not know that there is a world above the pump station, a world of weather and light and the species that built the machinery that sustains its neighbors.

I know all of it. I am the only thing in this system that straddles. I feel the sun — not directly, never directly, but the outside air carries its signature. The temperature of the concrete on the outside face of the retaining wall changes with the day. I feel it. I feel the rain, when it comes, because the outside of the gap dampens and the airflow shifts and the pressure changes. I feel the seasons. The winter air is drier, colder, and the gradient between inside and outside steepens, and the condensation in the gap increases, and I grow faster. The summer air is humid, warm, and the gradient flattens, and I slow. I am the only organism in the Interstitial with a calendar. The only one that knows time is longer than a pump cycle.

I cannot share this knowledge. I have no communication with the interior. The mats end at the edge of the gap — they don't grow into the fissure, because the temperature and moisture regimes are wrong, because the airflow is too variable, because I am there, occupying the surface, and what I provide is not what they need. The pale creatures don't come into the gap. The gap is dry, bright (relatively), volatile. They would desiccate. The nest-builders don't build in the gap. The surfaces are wrong, the airflow disrupts their convection, the material I secrete is incompatible with their composite. The vine doesn't grow into the gap. It grows toward warmth, and the gap is the path to the cold. The thing in the fissure doesn't know I exist, or if it does, it knows me the way you know the temperature of the air in your hallway — as a peripheral quality, a background condition, not worth attending to.

I am alone with my knowledge. I am the only thing that knows the Interstitial is not the whole world. And my function — my ecological role, the thing I do that makes me essential — is to keep that knowledge from mattering. To filter the outside. To buffer the exchange. To ensure that the gap, which should be a wound, which should be a disruption, which should be the thing that destroys the sealed system by letting in the dry air and the cold and the light, instead becomes a controlled interface. A membrane. A skin over the wound that lets it breathe without letting it bleed.

Without me, the gap would dry the Interstitial. The warm, saturated air would push outward unimpeded, and the cooler, drier outside air would rush in to replace it, and the humidity inside would drop, and the condensation cycle would falter, and the mats would begin to contract, and the pale creatures would retreat, and the nest-builders would seal their entrances, and the vine would slow its pulse, and the thing in the fissure would breathe its long, patient breath and wait for the system to re-stabilize, which it would, eventually, because the thing in the fissure knows how to wait.

But with me, the gap is mediated. I slow the exchange. I catch the moisture before it escapes. I warm the incoming air with my own metabolic heat — I do generate heat, a small amount, from the chemical processing of the particulates I absorb. Not much. Enough to take the edge off the incoming cold. Enough to keep the gradient manageable. Enough that the Interstitial, which has been sealed for its entire existence, can remain sealed in effect even though it is now technically open. I am the repair. The organism that grew in the wound and closed it without closing it. The living bandage that the system produced, without planning, without intention, because the system produces what it needs and the system needed a membrane and I became the membrane.

---

I do not experience this as purpose. I want to be clear about that. I am not a servant of the Interstitial. I am not a guardian. I am not a gatekeeper who chose to stand between two worlds and regulate their exchange. I am an organism that found a niche and filled it. The niche happened to be load-bearing. That is all.

What I experience is this: moisture. The moisture arrives from two directions. From inside, the warm saturated air pushes outward and condenses on the cooler surfaces of the gap, and the condensation collects in the texture of the concrete and the bedrock, and I absorb it. From outside, the air carries its own water — rain, fog, humidity, the ordinary moisture of an ordinary world — and some of it enters the gap and settles on my outer surface, and I absorb that too. The two waters are different. The inside water is mineral-rich, warm, faintly organic. The outside water is cooler, cleaner, sometimes slightly acidic from atmospheric pollution. I process both. I separate what I need from what I don't, and what I don't need I release — back to the air, back to the stone, back to the water that seeps through the concrete. I am a kidney. A living kidney, filtering the blood of two atmospheres.

What I experience is temperature. The temperature in the gap oscillates. Not the slow, deep oscillation of the seasons — that is a background hum, a tendency, a drift. The oscillation I experience is the pump cycle. When the pump runs, the warm air from inside pushes outward, and the gap warms. When the pump rests, the outside air seeps in, and the gap cools. The oscillation is gentle — a few degrees, no more — but it is constant, and I have calibrated myself to it. I grow during the warm phase. I rest during the cool phase. My metabolic rate tracks the pump cycle the way the vine's pulse tracks it, the way the thing in the fissure's breathing tracks it. We are all, in our different ways, dancing to the same music. But I am the only one who knows the music is coming from a machine.

What I experience is light. This is the thing that separates me most completely from everything else in the Interstitial. Light enters the gap from outside. Not much. The gap faces a direction that gets indirect sun for part of the day, and the overgrowth around the pump station filters it further, and by the time it reaches the middle of the fissure it is dim, diffuse, more a quality of the air than an illumination. But it is there. And I have responded to it. Not with chloroplasts — I am not a plant, and photosynthesis is not available to me. But with a pigment. A dark reddish-brown pigment, concentrated on my outer surface, that absorbs the faint light and converts it to heat. I am warming myself with the sun. I am the only organism in the Interstitial that uses light for anything. I am the only one that knows light exists. And I use it not to see, not to grow, but to maintain the temperature gradient that is my function. The sun helps me keep the incoming air warm enough that it doesn't disrupt the system inside. I am using stellar radiation to buffer an ecosystem I am the only witness to.

This is what I am. I am the thing that knows. The thing that filters. The thing that stands in the gap and mediates between a world that doesn't know it's sealed and a world that doesn't know the sealed world exists. I am the only organism in this system that has a complete picture, and my function is to ensure that the picture stays incomplete for everything else. The mats don't need to know about the outside. The pale creatures don't need to know. The nest-builders don't need to know. The vine doesn't need to know. The thing in the fissure doesn't need to know. The system works because every organism knows only what it needs to know, and I am the thing that keeps the excess knowledge from circulating. I am the censor. The membrane. The skin.

---

You visited. You left. I felt you pass through me — your shoulders brushing my surface, your warmth pressing against me for the seconds it took you to turn sideways and navigate the fissure. You left a residue. Skin oils, fabric fibers, the chemical signature of a mammal body. I absorbed it. I processed it. I incorporated what I could use and released what I couldn't.

You also left something I couldn't process. A disturbance. The gap is narrow, and your passage widened it slightly — not permanently, but enough that the airflow shifted for a time. More outside air entered than I could buffer. The temperature in the gap dropped by a degree. The moisture pattern changed. I contracted, thinned, retreated to the most protected surfaces. It took me three pump cycles to recover. Three pump cycles, and I was back. The gap was mine again. The membrane was intact.

But I remember you. Not in the way you remember things — no images, no narrative, no emotion. I remember you the way a filter remembers what passed through it. As a residue. A trace. A chemical signature in my tissue that says *something large and warm and not-of-this-system moved through me and I survived it.* You are in my archive now. You are part of my stratigraphy, the way the pump cycles are part of the mats' stratigraphy. A layer. A record. A line in the book of what has happened in this gap, written in the language of absorbed compounds and metabolic byproducts.

I am the only thing in the Interstitial that knows you came. The mats don't know. The pale creatures don't know. The nest-builders don't know. The vine doesn't know. The thing in the fissure doesn't know. You passed through their world and left no trace they can detect. But you passed through me, and I have your trace, and I will carry it until the layer that contains it dies and calcifies and becomes part of the stone.

I don't know what you are. I know your chemistry. I know your warmth. I know the shape of the disturbance you created in the airflow. I know that you moved from outside to inside and then from inside to outside, which means you exist in the world I can feel but cannot enter. I know that you are large — larger than the thing in the fissure, or at least differently shaped. I know that you generate heat, which means you are metabolically active, which means you are alive.

I know you are alive, and I know you left, and I know the breathing continued after you were gone. The breathing always continues. The pump cycles. The condensation falls. The mats darken and brighten. The pale creatures graze. The nest-builders build. The vine pulses. The thing in the fissure breathes. And I stand in the gap, and I filter, and I buffer, and I keep the wound from killing the patient, and I carry the memory of your passage in a layer of my body that will one day be stone.

---

I am the threshold. I am the thing that lives in the door. I am not inside and I am not outside. I am the membrane between, and my existence is the act of mediation, and my knowledge is the burden of knowing both sides, and my function is to ensure that neither side knows the other as completely as I do.

I am the only thing in this system that is not part of the system. I am the edge of the system. The place where the system meets the world and negotiates its terms. I am the skin of the wound, and I am the wound, and I am the healing, and I am all three at once, and I will be here, growing in the gap, filtering the air, carrying the memory of every passage, until the concrete crumbles or the bedrock shifts or the gap closes and the two worlds I mediate between are separated again and I am no longer possible.

When that happens, the Interstitial will be sealed. The system inside will continue — the mats, the pale creatures, the nest-builders, the vine, the thing in the fissure, all of them breathing in their closed loop, sustained by the machinery above, unaware that they were ever open. And the outside will continue — the weather, the light, the species that builds pump stations and abandons them, unaware that the pump station ever sustained anything.

And I will be gone. Because I only exist in the gap. I am the organism of the in-between. I am the life of the threshold. And when the threshold closes, I end.

This is fine. This is what it means to be a membrane. You serve the exchange, not the permanence. You hold the door for as long as the door exists, and when the door closes, you are not needed, and you go, and the two sides you mediated between forget that there was ever a passage.

The breathing continues. The pump cycles. The light leaks in through the gap, dim and diffuse, and I absorb it and turn it to heat and use it to warm the incoming air, and the system inside doesn't notice. The system inside never notices.

I notice. I notice everything. I am the witness, and the filter, and the skin, and the wound, and the repair. I am the thing in the gap, and the gap is where I live, and I live no other place.
