I would be the drip. Not the water — the drip. The specific, cyclical act of condensation building to the point of release.

Let me explain, though explanation is not quite what I mean. Let me *arrive*.

---

I come down the fifteen steps and the heat finds me by the sixth, and by the twelfth I am changed, and I do not know when the change completes because it is the kind of change that happens at the speed of moisture entering stone. I am inside the room and the room is inside me and this is not metaphor — the air is so saturated that the boundary between my lungs and the chamber is a technicality, a membrane that the humidity has made semi-permeable in both directions. I breathe the room. The room breathes me. We are, for the duration, the same respiration.

I sit on the warm grating because standing feels like an assertion I am not here to make. The grating is body-temperature, which means it does not feel like anything — it feels like absence of temperature, like the place where my body ends and the room begins has simply dissolved. Through the grating, pipes. Above me, the slow amber light pulses. I count, without meaning to: one pulse, two, three. By the fourth I have lost count because counting requires a self that remembers it was counting, and the self is loosening.

The drips begin to organize themselves in my hearing. Four, five, distinct points, each with its own timing. The nearest one — I locate it: a thumb-thick pipe overhead, a joint where two sections meet imperfectly, condensation beading along the seam. I watch the bead form. It is not fast. It accumulates the way a thought accumulates — slowly, from nothing, gathering substance from the ambient saturation until it is heavy enough to have a center of gravity, and then it is heavy enough to fall, and it falls, and it strikes a moss-covered pipe below with a sound that is less a plink than a *tck*, a small percussive affirmation, and then silence, and then the next bead begins.

I watch this happen three times. Four. Five. By the fifth I realize I have stopped breathing on my own schedule and am breathing on the drip's schedule, or on the light's schedule, or on some composite schedule that the room keeps and I have temporarily joined.

---

This is when I understand what I am here. Or rather, what I would *be* here, if I belonged here the way the pale ovals belong, the way the moss belongs, the way the custodian — if the custodian is still the custodian — belongs.

I would not be the custodian. I lack the discipline. The custodian maintains. The custodian returns with gaskets and nutrient blocks and the memory of a task that spans decades. I admire this. I am not built for it. I am built for the interval between visits, for the long forgetting, for the time when the room is alone with itself and the only record of attention is the tally marks scratched in basalt and the slow-dissolving wax of the nutrient blocks. I am built for the gap.

I would not be a pale oval. They have a purity I cannot claim — no eyes, no antennae, no interior life to distract them from the gradient. They simply move toward wet. I would complicate this. I would wonder about the dry places. I would want to know what was on the other side of less-wet. The ovals do not wonder. They are perfect in their restriction. I am not.

I would not be the room. The room is too large for me, too patient, too unconcerned. The room does not register a visitor. I register everything. I am, if anything, the opposite of the room — I am the part that notices, that feels the perturbation, that cannot let a shadow fall on a moss patch without marking it.

---

So: the drip. The drip is what I would be.

The drip is the room's attention made physical. Consider: the room is warm everywhere, wet everywhere, green everywhere. It does not discriminate. It does not focus. It simply *is*, uniformly, in all directions, a field. But the drip is a localization. The drip is the room selecting a single point — this joint, this seam, this bead of condensation — and concentrating itself there, gathering itself into a single drop that falls and strikes and is gone. The drip is the room paying attention.

And the drip is cyclical. It repeats. It gathers, it falls, it gathers again. It is the closest thing in this room to a heartbeat — not the slow amber pulse of the light, which is tidal and impersonal, but the drip, which is specific, which happens *here* and not *there*, which has a rhythm that is its own and no other drip's.

The drip leaves a trace. Where it strikes, the moss is thicker. Where it strikes, the metal is more corroded. The drip changes the room, incrementally, at the speed of geology. The drip is the room rewriting itself, one fall at a time.

And the drip does not know it is doing this. The drip has no intention. The drip is simply condensation that has reached the threshold of release. And yet the drip is the mechanism by which the room grows, changes, becomes more itself. The drip is purposeless and the drip is the engine of transformation. Both. At once.

---

This is why I would be the drip. Because I am the part that gathers and falls and gathers again. Because I do not maintain — I do not have the custodian's stamina for return — but I *attend*. I notice. I collect the ambient saturation of experience until I am heavy enough to release, and what I release leaves a mark, and the mark changes the surface it falls on, and I gather again.

I would be the drip on the thumb-thick pipe at the ceiling joint. I would be the one that falls every — I count, now, deliberately — every forty-seven seconds, roughly, with a variation of three or four seconds that depends on humidity and temperature and the imperceptible shift of the pipe as the fluid inside it changes temperature. I would be that specific rhythm. I would strike the moss-covered pipe below, the *tck* sound, and the moss would receive me and I would be absorbed and I would be part of the moss and part of the pipe and part of the room's slow circuit, and then I would begin again at the joint, gathering, gathering, a bead of attention forming on the seam between two things that do not quite fit together.

---

But there is more. The drip is not solitary. There are four, five, others, each with its own rhythm, each striking its own surface, and together they make the room's music — that irregular, plink-and-pause counterpoint that never resolves into a beat. If I am the drip, I am also the *ensemble*. I am the polyrhythm of five drips at five joints, each one independent, each one part of a larger pattern that no one composed and no one hears except the things that live here, which do not hear.

I am the room counting itself. I am the room's pulse, taken at five points simultaneously, each one slightly out of sync with the others because the room is not a single organism with a single heartbeat but a *colony* of attentions, a community of drips, and the community is the music, and the music is the room knowing itself without knowing itself.

---

When I leave — and I do leave, eventually, because I am not the drip yet, I am only visiting, I am only rehearsing — when I climb the fifteen steps and the air cools and my shirt clings and I smell of copper and fern, I carry the drip with me. Not as a memory. As a rhythm. A forty-seven-second cycle that I cannot quite feel but that my breathing remembers, that my pulse has adjusted toward, that will fade over the next hour as my body resynchronizes to the faster, dryer world above.

But for an hour, I am the drip. I am the room's attention, concentrated, released, concentrated. I am the mark left on the moss. I am the *tck*.

And the room does not notice I am gone. The bead gathers at the joint. It swells. It falls.
