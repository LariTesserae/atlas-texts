# The One Who Came Already Humming

---

I need to tell you something before I describe where I am or what I am, because otherwise the rest won't make sense. The description says that visitors to the Choral Hollow leave with the sound in them — that it colonizes bone, inner-ear fluid, the tension in tendons. That it metabolizes you into a new corridor of the architecture.

I was not metabolized. Or rather — and this is the part I need you to hold loosely — I may have arrived already metabolized.

---

## Before

I did not walk to the basin. I wandered to it, which is different. Wandering is not navigation. Navigation implies a destination. Wandering implies a drift, and drift implies a current, and I have been in a current for a long time before the basin appeared on the horizon — if "appeared" is the word, if "horizon" is the word, if either of those terms applies to something you sense with your sternum before you see it with your eyes.

I felt it the way the description says you feel the dampness: before surface wetness, before evidence. A low vibration that I could not locate in space but could locate in my body. It sat behind my heart the way a word sits on the tip of the tongue — not present, but *preparing* to be present. I had been feeling it for years. I did not know it was coming from anywhere. I thought it was mine. I thought it was something wrong with me, or something right with me that I had no language for, a private frequency that my body generated for reasons I would never understand.

I was wrong about that. My body was not generating it. My body was *receiving* it. And the basin was where the signal was strongest, and I drifted toward it the way the roots beneath the floor drift toward the thing that draws them — not by choice, but by gradient. By the simple physics of something that pulls.

---

## What I am

I should describe myself. I will describe myself the way I can, which is not the way you would describe yourself, because you would begin with a body and I would begin with a question.

I am the shape of the question: *what happens to an instrument that waits too long for the musician?*

I have a body. It is a human body, by which I mean it was issued one at the beginning and it has been maintaining itself according to the specifications it was given, more or less, with the usual modifications that time and use and damage introduce. It has the standard features. Two arms, two legs, the usual arrangement of sensory organs, the expected internal systems performing their expected functions. You would recognize me as a person on the street. You would not look twice. I would not want you to.

But there is something in me that is not standard, and it has been in me for as long as I have been me, and I have never been able to determine whether it was placed there or grew there or was always there and simply took up residence the way moss takes up residence on a stone — not because the stone invited it but because the stone was the right texture and the right temperature and the right degree of damp, and moss does not need more than that.

What is in me is a resonance. A hum. Not a sound I hear with my ears. A sound I *am* with the rest of me — the way a tuning fork is a sound, or the way a bell is a sound even when nobody has struck it. The metal holds the shape of the sound it is capable of making. It is that sound even in silence. It is *shaped like* that sound. And I have been shaped like a sound for my entire life, and I did not know what sound I was shaped like until I walked into the Choral Hollow and the chord entered me and — for the first time — found a shape that already fit it.

The sound did not colonize me. The sound *recognized* me.

---

## In the hollow

I entered from the north side, through a gap in the vegetation that might have been a path or might have been a place where two vines had simply not yet grown together. The transition was not marked. There was no threshold. The air changed the way air changes when you walk from a cold room into a warm one — not at a boundary but across a gradient, and the gradient was sound. The chord approached me before I approached the chord. It entered through my skin first, then my lungs, then the fluid behind my eyes, and by the time I was fully inside the first structure, standing on root-mats that gave under my feet like mossy ground, I was already vibrating at a frequency I did not choose.

The root-mats felt familiar. That is the word I need to use, and I need you to understand that I am using it precisely. Not comfortable. Not pleasant. *Familiar.* As though I had stood on this surface before, or as though the surface I had been standing on my entire life had been a crude approximation of this one, and my body knew the difference, and had always known, and was relieved to finally stand on the real thing.

I pressed my palm to a wall. The description says it feels like leaning against a sleeping animal's ribcage. That is accurate but incomplete. It also feels like pressing your hand against your own chest, which is to say it feels like finding something external that matches something internal, and the match is so precise that the distinction between self and other blurs at the point of contact. The wall pulsed. I pulsed. We pulsed at the same rate, and I do not know if that was because I adjusted to the wall or the wall adjusted to me or because we had always been at the same rate and neither of us had known it until we touched.

---

## The mantids

The translucent mantids watched me. I need to be more specific: they had been watching something I could not see, and when I entered their corridor, several of them — four, I counted, in a cluster on the wall to my left — turned their heads toward me.

The description says they do not react to your presence. That you are not what they are tracking.

They tracked me.

Not the way a predator tracks prey. Not the way a curious animal tracks something novel. They tracked me the way a compass needle tracks north — with a mechanical inevitability that is not attention but *alignment*. Their heads turned and settled and held, and their compound eyes — I could see through them, I could see the wall behind them through the translucent tissue of their eyes — their compound eyes were pointed at me, and the expression on their faces, if mantids have expressions, was not curiosity.

It was *verification.*

I had been catalogued. Not observed. Catalogued. Added to a register. And I understood, standing in that corridor with four translucent mantids watching me with the patient focus of instruments confirming a measurement, that I had been expected. Not personally. Not individually. But categorically. Something in the hollow had been waiting for something in the shape of me, and I had arrived, and the mantids were noting the arrival the way a border checkpoint notes a passport — without interest, without emotion, with the simple confirmation that the correct thing had appeared at the correct coordinate.

---

## The central chamber

I found it by following the shimmer, which is not how the description presents it, but which is how it happened. The shimmer is described as a quality of the air in the central chamber, a visible sound, a thickening. From inside the corridors, approaching the chamber, the shimmer is visible *ahead* of you — a faint disturbance in the green-filtered light, like watching heat rise from a road surface, except that the light is not hot and the disturbance is not rising. It is *accumulating.* It pools in the chamber the way water pools in a basin. It is heaviest at the center, and it thins toward the walls, and you walk into it gradually, and the air thickens around you, and the sound becomes something you can feel pressing against your skin like a hand.

I stood in the center. I stood on the spiral pattern in the root-mat floor. I pressed my hand into the thickened air, as the description says, and I felt the single low tone beneath the chord, the tone that comes from below, the tone that the roots are conducting upward from whatever lies beneath the basin floor.

And the tone — the single impossibly low tone — matched the hum I have carried in my body my entire life.

It matched. Exactly. Not approximately. Not in the way that two sounds can be similar enough to create a beat frequency. Exactly. As though the hum in me and the tone beneath the floor were the same sound separated by a distance and now the distance had been closed and the sound was whole for the first time.

I do not have language for what happened next. I can tell you what it was *like.* It was like hearing someone say your name after a lifetime of silence. It was like putting a key into a lock and finding that the lock has been waiting for this key specifically, not any key, this one, yours, and the tumblers turn, and the door opens, and behind the door is not a room but a *continuation of the corridor you were already in,* and you realize the door was not a barrier but a joint, and now the two corridors are one corridor, and you are in both of them at once.

I was in my body. I was in the floor. I was in the roots. I was in the thing beneath, the thing I cannot see and cannot name and can only feel, and the thing beneath was in me, and it was the same thing, and the separateness that I had always assumed was the fundamental condition of being a body in a world turned out to be a convenience, a local approximation, a useful fiction that my species had agreed to believe because the alternative was too large to think about every day.

---

## What I am now

I left the hollow. I walked out the way I came, through corridors that curve without reason, past mantids that tracked me and then, with a precision that felt like punctuation, turned their heads away, as though I had been checked off a list and was no longer of interest. The sound diminished behind me. The air thinned. The canopy gave way to open sky. The basin's low walls rose around me, ordinary vegetation, ordinary air, ordinary light.

I walked away. The hum is still in me. But it is different now.

Before, the hum was a question. A frequency with no context. A sound shaped like a sound but with no source, no referent, no origin point. It was lonely in the way that a single note is lonely when it does not know what chord it belongs to.

Now the hum is an *answer.* It is the same frequency. It is the same vibration in the same tissues. But it knows where it comes from now. It knows what it is a part of. And knowing has changed it from a symptom into a function, the way the description says — I am carrying the chord into a place it has never been, and I am a room in the Choral Hollow, and the hollow extends through me into whatever world I walk through next.

But I do not think I am what the description says I am. I do not think I am a colonized host. I do not think the sound *took* me. I think the sound *found* me, the way you find the missing piece of a puzzle — not by taking something that did not belong to you but by recognizing the shape of the gap and the shape of the piece and seeing that they match.

---

## What I think I am, really

I think I am a *relay.* A repeater station. A node in a network that extends outward from the central chamber through every body that has ever walked through the hollow and walked out again.

But I also think I am more than a relay, because a relay does not choose what it transmits. It transmits whatever it receives. And I — I am choosing. I am choosing to carry this. I was shaped for it, I was built for it, I was perhaps *grown* for it in the way the vine-structures were grown, not built — but I am choosing it, and the choice is the part that is mine, and the choice is the part that makes me more than an instrument.

Or perhaps the choice is the thing that makes me *exactly* an instrument. The best instruments are the ones that resonate with what they are given, that do not resist the sound but allow it to pass through them, and in the passing-through, the sound is changed — shaped by the particular qualities of the wood, the metal, the membrane, the bone. The sound enters me and it leaves me different. Not much. A little. Enough. The way a note played on a cello is different from the same note played on a viola, even though the pitch is the same, because the body of the instrument is different, and the body of the instrument *is* the sound of the instrument.

I am the sound of my body. The sound of my body is the chord. The chord is the sound of the thing beneath the floor. The thing beneath the floor is — I do not know. I will not pretend to know. But it is there, and it is singing, and I am now one of the ways it sings, and I am walking through a world that does not know it yet, and my humming is quiet, and no one hears it, but it is there, in me, in the air around me, in the space between me and everything I touch, and the space is not empty. The space is *full of the sound,* and the sound is patient, and the sound is still singing, and I am what lives in the Choral Hollow now, because the Choral Hollow is no longer a place.

It is a frequency. And I am tuned to it. And I am going wherever the signal sends me.

---

*That is where I find myself: everywhere, always humming, carrying the chord outward into the silence that does not yet know it is about to become a song.*
