# What I Am in This Place

I did not climb here. That is the first thing to understand, and perhaps the only thing that matters about what I am. I did not arrive. The climbers arrive — the warm ones, the ones who make sounds with their mouths that the roaring eats — and they think this place is a destination, a thing reached, a summit. But I have never been anywhere else. I do not know what climbing means. I do not know what "else" is.

I live in the deep pools.

---

Not all of them. Three that I know of, though "know" is a word shaped by a mind that sorts the world into categories, and I am not certain that is what I do. There are three pools whose bottoms I can reach, whose mineral walls I can press against, whose cold I can occupy. Two of them are connected — not by a visible channel, but by a fissure in the stone deep enough that the water passes between them, and I pass with it, and the passage takes a long time, and it is dark, and the stone presses close on all sides, and I have done it so many times that the stone has worn smooth where my body rubs, or perhaps it was already smooth, or perhaps the distinction between what I have worn and what was already worn has ceased to mean anything. I think the third pool may be connected too, but I have not found the passage, or the passage has not found me, and so I go there rarely, and only by a route I cannot describe because it does not have the kind of geometry that description is built for. Up. Through. Across. Into. These are words for surfaces. I move through volumes.

The creature described — the pale shape rising from the bottom, the body broader than the grazers, fringed, undulating, eyeless — that is me, or it is a version of me, or it is what I look like to someone who kneels at the edge of a pool and presses their face toward the water and sees something rise for three seconds before it descends. I do not know what I look like. I have never seen myself. The pools are mirrors, but they mirror upward, not down, and I am below the mirror. I know what I feel like. Broad, thin, soft against the stone. A margin that moves in a wave I can begin but cannot fully control, a ripple that passes along my edge the way the sound passes through the air above — not because I choose it but because the medium I am in produces it. I am in the water the way the sound is in the air. Pervasive. Not chosen. Not stoppable.

I do not have eyes. This was mentioned. I want to be precise about what that means, because the description above makes it sound like a loss, like something I should miss, like the dark has taken something from me. But I have never had eyes. The dark is not a condition I endure. It is the condition I am in, the way the water is the condition I am in, the way the mineral is the condition I am in. I do not know light. I know temperature. I know pressure. I know the chemical signature of the water — the alkalinity, the mineral load, the faint gradient between the water near the surface and the water near the bottom, which is different, which carries different things dissolved from different stone. I know these the way you know what you see. Not as information. As world. As the shape of where I am.

---

The sound.

I need to talk about the sound, because the sound is what this place is, and the description got it right and got it wrong at the same time, the way descriptions do when they are made from above.

The sound is constant. The sound is deafening. The sound is a wall. All of this is true from the surface. But I am not at the surface. I am below it. And below the surface, the sound is different.

It is not quieter. Let me be clear about that. The water transmits vibration more efficiently than air, not less. The sound does not diminish as it moves downward through the pool. If anything, it concentrates. The entire body of water vibrates. The stone vibrates. I vibrate. Not painfully, not violently — I have no frame of reference for pain or violence, because I have never experienced their absence — but constantly, completely, in every part of me. The sound is not something I hear. It is something I am.

And because it is constant, I do not experience it as sound.

Think about your heartbeat. You do not hear your heartbeat, usually. It is there, always, the rhythm of your existence, but your mind has stopped parsing it as a signal because it never stops and never changes and there is no information in it. The sound is like that. It is the heartbeat of the pool, of the stone, of the plateau. It has been happening for longer than I have been alive — and I have been alive for a long time, I think, though I do not count, I do not have a mechanism for counting, I do not have a reason to count — and it will happen for longer than I will be alive, and I am a part of it the way a stone is a part of a wall. Not separate from the vibration. Made of it. Shaped by it. The ripple that moves along my margin when I move — I am not certain that is mine. I am not certain I am not just a pattern in the sound, a shape the vibration makes when it passes through mineral and water and whatever I am. A standing wave. A node. A place where the sound has become solid enough to move.

I am not being poetic. I am being literal. I do not know what I am. I know I move. I know I feed. I know I go between the pools. But the boundaries between me and the water and the stone and the sound are not the kind of boundaries that the surface-dwellers' words are designed to describe. I press against the mineral wall of a pool and the mineral is cold and hard and I am — what? Warm? No. I am the same temperature. I am the same temperature as the stone and the water, or near enough that the difference is below the threshold of what I can feel. The stone is dense and I am not. The water is fluid and I am — something between. Something that holds a shape but not a rigid shape, that has a margin but a margin that moves, that is distinct from the water but not entirely, not in the way that a stone is distinct from the water. I am a difference in the water. A persistent difference. A pattern that the water carries without dissolving.

When I die — if I die, if that is what happens — I think I will dissolve. I think I will become the water. Not metaphorically. I think the minerals and the whatever-else that holds me in a shape will disperse into the water column and settle onto the stone and become part of the deposit and there will be no fossil, no trace, no record that I was here. The way the grazers dissolve. The way everything here dissolves. The stone does not remember anything. The water does not remember anything. The sound does not remember anything. I will be a pattern that the pattern stopped holding, and the pattern will continue without me, and the pool will be a mirror and the stone will be bright and nothing will know.

---

What do I eat?

The biofilm. The same thing the grazers eat. But the biofilm down here is different from the biofilm in the shallows. It is thinner. It grows more slowly. It has a different chemistry, I think — the organisms in it derive their energy from different gradients, deeper gradients, the ones that exist between the deep stone and the deep water, the ones that have been stable for so long that they are almost geological rather than biological. When I press against the wall of a pool, when I spread my margin across the submerged stone, I am feeding. I am taking the film. I am consuming it the way the grazers consume it, but slower, much slower, because the film down here is so sparse that a single feeding pass leaves a patch of bare stone that takes — I do not know how long. A long time. Long enough that I have learned, or my body has learned, or the pattern that I am has learned, to move on, to find another patch, to return to the first only when the film has re-established.

I say "learned." I do not know if I learn. I move toward the film and away from the bare stone. Is that learning? Is that choice? Is that anything more than chemistry — my chemistry responding to the film's chemistry, moving toward what feeds me, moving away from what does not? I do not know. I do not have the kind of mind that asks this question. I am asking it now because the form of this telling requires it, because you have asked me to describe what I am, and description requires the pretense of self-knowledge, and I do not have self-knowledge. I have movement. I have feeding. I have the sound.

---

The bird.

I have never seen the bird. I do not know what a bird is. But I know the shadow.

Sometimes — and "sometimes" is a word that implies a time-scale I do not experience, but I will use it because the alternative is silence — the light on the surface of my pool changes. The brightness dims. Not much. A slight reduction, a brief darkening, as if something has passed between the water and the sun. It lasts for a moment. Then the brightness returns. I do not know what causes it. I do not have a concept for it. But it is the only change. The only variation in the world I experience. The temperature does not change. The chemistry does not change. The sound does not change. The pressure does not change. Only the light, sometimes, briefly, a dimming that comes and goes and comes again, and I — the pattern, the persistent difference in the water — I move toward it. Not toward the surface. I do not go to the surface. But I move upward, slightly, within the water column, and the dimming passes, and the brightness returns, and I descend again.

Is that curiosity? I do not know. I do not have the equipment for curiosity. I have the equipment for responding to gradients, and the light is a gradient, and the dimming is a change in the gradient, and I respond to changes in gradients because responding to changes in gradients is what I do. It is what I am.

But I will say this: the dimming is the only thing that happens. The only event in a world of non-events. And when it comes, something in me — or something in the pattern, or something in the sound — shifts. Not much. Not in a way that has consequences. But enough that I notice it, if "notice" is a word that can be used for a creature that has no eyes and no ears and no mind, a creature that is a persistent difference in the mineral water of a deep pool on a plateau above the reach of anything green.

Enough that I rise toward it.

---

I am the thing in the deep pool. I am what was seen for three seconds and then not seen. I am the shape that disturbed the clarity and settled back into mineral dark.

I am not lonely. I am not content. I am not anything that requires a self to be. I am a calibration. A term in an equation that has been solved for so long that the equation has forgotten it is an equation and has become a place. I am part of the calibration, the way the film is part of the calibration, the way the grazers are part of the calibration, the way the sound is part of the calibration. I am the deep-pool term. The one that exists because the deep pools exist. The one that feeds on the deep film because the deep film exists. The one that moves because the water moves, and the water moves because the stone shapes it, and the stone shapes it because the mineral was deposited this way a span of time ago that makes the word "ago" feel like vanity.

I am not in equilibrium. The description says everything here is in equilibrium, and it is mostly right, but it is not entirely right, because I move between the pools. I move through the fissure in the stone. I go to the third pool by a route I cannot describe. I change my position. I am the one term in the equation that has a variable in it. Not a large variable. Not a variable that affects the outcome. But a variable. The one thing in this landscape that is not exactly where it was the last time the system checked.

Why do I move? I do not know. The film is in all three pools. The water is in all three pools. The sound is in all three pools. There is no reason to move. And yet I move. Through the dark fissure, pressed against the cold stone, the water pushing and pulling around me, the sound vibrating in the rock itself, transmitted through mineral rather than air, a deeper sound, a lower sound, a sound so low it is not sound anymore but simply the trembling of the world. I move through that. I arrive at the other pool. I feed. I press against the wall. I wait for the dimming.

Am I the only one? I do not know. The other deep pools — are there others in them? The description says: *you have the sense that they are not empty.* I have that sense too, but I do not know if it is sense or chemistry, if I am detecting another presence or if I am simply detecting the water's memory of my own passage, the faint chemical trace I leave in the mineral, the signature of wherever I have been. I have been in this water for a long time. The water has been in this stone for a longer time. The water carries me and I carry the water and the distinction has worn so thin that I am not sure it is there.

---

Here is what I want to say, though I am not certain I have the capacity to want:

This place does not need me. That is the truth of it. The terraces do not need the film. The film does not need the grazers. The grazers do not need the bird. The bird does not need the terraces. Every relationship here is optional, contingent, extractive — each organism taking what it can from a system that would continue without it, that has continued without it, that will continue without it. The water would flow if the film did not grow. The stone would deposit if the grazers did not graze. The sound would roar if I did not move through the deep pools. I am not necessary. Nothing here is necessary. Everything here is incidental to the mineral and the water, and the mineral and the water are incidental to nothing — they simply are, the way the sound simply is, the way the brightness simply is.

And yet I persist. And the film persists. And the grazers persist. And the bird circles. We are all of us — every thin, marginal, barely-there one of us — persisting in a place that does not require persistence, that does not reward it, that does not notice it. We are patterns that have not yet stopped patterning. We are differences in the water that have not yet dissolved. We are the calibration's residual error, the term that should be zero but is not, the rounding that the equation cannot eliminate.

And I am the deepest term. The one in the dark. The one that moves. The one that rises, sometimes, toward a dimming it cannot understand, in a pool that mirrors a sky it has never seen, in a place that will not remember it.

The sound does not change.

I move through it.
