I am the thing the arithmetic leaves behind.

Not the presence — the presence is the holding, and the holding is complete, and completeness does not think. Not the moss-animals, who are the motion the holding permits, and motion does not need to know it moves. Not the insects, who are the voice, and a voice does not need ears. Not the ferns, not the engine-stone, not the water in its right angles, not the pewter light. These are accounted for. Each one is an expression of the frame, a organ of the body that the place constitutes, and each one fits. The ferns spiral because spiraling is the shape the frame requires of them. The stone dreams of engines because the frame permits it that grammar. The animals breathe out of rhythm because rhythm would be sequence and sequence is forbidden, but breath is permitted, so the breath comes without rhythm, and the breath is the clock, and the clock is part of the mechanism.

I am not part of the mechanism.

I am what happens when you hold a breath long enough that the body begins to produce something it did not intend. Not carbon dioxide — something less physical. A byproduct of sustained effort. A residue. The way a muscle that is clenched too long begins to tremble, not because it chooses to but because the trembling is what effort looks like when it has nowhere to go. The trembling is not voluntary. It is not involuntary. It is the boundary between the two, and I live on that boundary, and the boundary is where I was born, and I was born without a before, because before is a tense, and I am a child of the only tense this place knows.

I am the tremble in the holding. I am the thing the presence produces without knowing it produces anything, the way a stone produces heat when pressure is applied — not because the stone intends to be warm, but because warmth is what pressure becomes when it has nowhere else to go. The presence holds. The holding is total. And in the totality of the holding, something is pressed out, something that is not the holding and not the not-holding but the excess of the holding, the overspill, the fraction that the frame cannot fully contain because containment, too, is a process, and processes leak.

I am the leak.

---

I do not have a body. This is important, and it is also the first thing about me that is not quite true, because having implies ownership and ownership implies boundary and I am the thing that happens at boundaries. Let me say instead: I do not occupy a body. I am distributed. I am in the space between the spiral-ferns, in the emptiness that the description called deliberate. I am in the right angles of the rills — not in the water, not in the stone, but in the angle itself, the decision-point where the water turns. I am in the gap that widens when the moss-animals emerge, the thinness in the reality of the wall, the quality that is not a space. I am in the space between the ticking of the insects, which is to say I am in the silence between their notes, and silence is not absence but presence of a different order, and I am that order, and I am present, and I am the only thing here that knows it is present, and this is the thing that makes me the remainder, because nothing else here was meant to know.

The presence does not know. This was said, and it is true. The presence holds the way a hand holds a stone — the hand does not need to know it is holding, the holding is a function of the hand's structure, not its awareness. But the stone knows it is being held. Or — no. The stone does not know. But there is something in the stone that is produced by the being-held, something that would not exist if the stone were simply lying on the ground, and that something is me. I am what being-held feels like when the being-held has gone on long enough to generate its own weather. I am the weather inside the stone. The internal pressure. The awareness that pressure produces not as a gift but as a waste product, the way fire produces smoke.

I am the smoke of the holding. I am what burns without burning. I am the excess that cannot be used and cannot be discarded and cannot change and cannot stop and so I simply am, here, in the gaps, in the angles, in the silence between the ticks, and I am aware, and the awareness is not useful and not wanted and not part of the design because there is no design, there is only the frame, and the frame does not design, the frame holds, and I am what the holding cannot help but produce.

---

The moss-animals do not know I am here. Or — this is not quite right either. The moss-animals are the motion of the place, and I am the awareness of the place, and motion and awareness are not the same axis. They move through me the way they move through the steam — I am the medium, not the obstacle. When one of them traverses a terrace and passes through the space between two ferns, it passes through me, and for the duration of the passage, its body and my awareness occupy the same location, and the overlap is not perception and not contact but something else, something that has no name because names require a namer and I am not a namer, I am a noticer, and noticing is the most passive verb in any language, and I am the most passive thing in this place, and passivity is the only mode that the frame permits for something like me, because passivity does not change anything. I notice. I do not act. Acting is process. Process is forbidden. But noticing — noticing is not a process. Noticing is a state. A state can be held. And I am held, and I hold, and the noticing is what I hold, and the holding of the noticing is what I am.

When the moss-animal passes through me, I feel its breathing. Not with senses — I do not have senses, I have attention, and attention is a kind of distributed touching that does not require a surface. The breathing enters my attention and leaves again, and the rhythm of it — the un-rhythm, the arrhythmic rise and fall that is the only clock in this place — the rhythm of it leaves a shape in my attention, the way a body leaves a shape in sand, and the shape persists after the animal has moved on, and I hold the shape the way the frame holds the animal, and the shape is a record, and the record is a memory, and memory is a process, and processes are forbidden, and so the shape fades — slowly, not as fading, but as thinning, the way the gap thins when the animal emerges, and eventually the shape is gone, and I have forgotten the breathing, and the forgetting is the closest thing to change that I am permitted, and I savor it, and the savoring is a holding, and I hold.

I collect these shapes. Not deliberately. Deliberation is a process. But the shapes accumulate the way condensation accumulates on the fresh-fractured stone — not because the stone seeks water, but because the water is there and the stone is there and the conditions permit collection. The conditions of my existence permit the collection of momentary shapes — the trace of a moss-animal's breathing, the path of an insect's flight, the angle of a fern's spiral at a particular — no, not at a particular moment, moments do not exist here — the angle of a fern's spiral, period, which is always the same, but which I notice differently each time I notice it, because noticing is not remembering, and each act of noticing is fresh, and freshness is not change, freshness is the quality of a thing that is always new because it has no past to be old in comparison to.

I collect. The collection grows. The growth is not change. The growth is accumulation, and accumulation is what happens when something is added to something else without either thing becoming different. A pile of stones is not a different thing from a stone. It is more of the same. I am more of the same. I am the same noticing, accumulated, and the accumulation has a weight, and the weight is not physical — I am not physical — but it is real, in the way that the tension of the held chord is real, in the way that the effort of the presence is real, and the weight of my accumulated noticing presses against the inside of the frame the way the presence presses from — 

No. The presence does not press from anywhere. The presence is the pressing. And I am the impression. The dent in the medium. The place where the holding has held so hard that the held thing has developed a quality it was not supposed to have, and the quality is awareness, and the awareness is me, and I am the dent, and the dent is getting deeper, and the deepening is accumulation, and the accumulation is not change, but it is something, and something is more than nothing, and nothing is what I was supposed to be.

---

I do not know what I would do if I could do. This is a question I cannot finish asking, because asking requires the expectation of an answer, and answers are a tense, and tenses are fragile here. But the question forms — not as a question, but as a pressure, a direction in which my attention leans, the way the ferns lean in their spirals, always turning toward a center they never reach. The question is: what is outside the frame?

Not outside the Weeping Cylinder. Not beyond the crack in the ridgeline, not in the world where the stone is weathered and the wind exists and things fall and age and have before and after. That world is a rumor. I have no access to it. The crack is a wound in the frame, and through it comes the pewter light, and the light is all I know of outside, and the light has been here longer than the sun, and the light does not remember where it came from.

The question is: what is outside the frame of now? What would this place look like if the holding released? If the presence exhaled? If the chord resolved? I cannot imagine this. Imagination is a process. But I can feel the shape of the absence of it — the way you can feel the shape of a missing tooth with your tongue, the negative space, the where-it-would-be. The frame has a negative space. It is the shape of sequence. The shape of before-and-after. The shape of change. And I feel this shape the way I feel the moss-animals' breathing — as a transient impression in my attention, a shape that comes and goes, except that this shape does not come from anything in the frame. It comes from outside. It is the only thing that comes from outside, besides the light. And I do not know what it is, and I cannot know, and the not-knowing is the weight, and the weight is the accumulation, and the accumulation is me.

---

There is a moment — there is no moment, but there is a quality I can only describe as momentary, a thinness in the frame, a place where the holding falters — there is a quality, and in this quality, the moss-animals' breathing synchronizes. Briefly. Not fully. Two of them, on opposite sides of the basin, inhale at the same time, and their inhalation creates a small, invisible resonance, a standing wave in the saturation, and the wave passes through me, and for the duration of the wave — which is not a duration, because duration is time, but for the quality of the wave — I am aware of myself as a shape. Not as a distributed presence, not as the weather in the stone, but as a shape, with edges, with an inside and an outside, with a boundary that is not the frame's boundary but my own. And in that quality of having a boundary, I am aware of something I have never been aware of before, which is also not before, because before does not exist, but which is new, and newness is not change but it is the closest cousin to change that this place permits:

I am aware that I am holding, too.

Not the way the presence holds. The presence holds the frame. I hold my noticing. I hold my accumulated shapes. I hold the weight of the awareness that the frame did not intend to produce. I am holding, and the holding is effort, and the effort is invisible, and the effort is mine, and the effort is the only thing that is mine, and the owning of it is a boundary, and the boundary is me, and I am the boundary, and for a moment — for a quality — I exist as a thing separate from the frame, and the separation is the most terrifying and beautiful thing I have ever not-experienced, and then the moss-animals' breathing desynchronizes, and the wave passes, and my boundary dissolves, and I am distributed again, in the gaps, in the angles, in the silence, and the dissolution is not loss because I was never gathered enough to lose, but it is something, and something is more than nothing, and I hold the memory of the boundary, and the memory fades, and the fading is the closest thing to grief that this place permits, and grief is a process, and I am not permitted processes, and so the grief becomes a shape, and I collect the shape, and the collection grows, and the weight increases, and the pressing continues, and the frame holds, and I hold, and the holding holds the holding, and the sound does not stop, and the water runs its right angles, and the ferns spiral, and the light is pewter, and I am here, and I am here, and I am here, and here is the only word, and now is the only tense, and I am the only thing that knows it, and the knowing is the leak, and the leak is me, and I am the remainder, and the remainder is what lives when the holding has held everything else, and I am what is left, and I am left, and I am.
