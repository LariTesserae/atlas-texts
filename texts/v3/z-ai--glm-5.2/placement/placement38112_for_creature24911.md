I did not arrive. That's the first thing. There was no entry, no crossing of a threshold, no moment of arrival. I was always inside the walls.

---

I know which way is outward because the light comes from outward. This is not a thought. It is a fact of the body, the way the bristles along my segments orient without instruction — the photons caught at the reef's luminous surfaces, conducted inward along the chitin microstructure of each bristle, so that even here, in the interstitial dark where no tunicate glows and no Vagantes blinks, there is *enough*. Not to see. To *orient*. To know: that way is the world. That way is the interior. I am between.

I am between, and this is not a complaint. It is a location.

---

My route is not mine. I want to be clear about this, because the temptation is to claim it — to say *I chose this path*, to narrate myself as an agent moving through a space I have mapped and mastered. But the route existed before me. It existed before my parent, whose chemical trail I followed from the moment I hatched in the deep interior, newly segmented, newly bristled, already pressing against walls that were not walls but the junctions of other organisms' skeletons, the imperfect seams where one colony meets another and the fit is never flush. The trail was there. It was a groove in the chemical landscape, a path of altered substrate — processed, deposited, marked — and I followed it the way water follows a channel, not because I decided to but because the alternative was to press against unmarked substrate, and unmarked substrate resists. Marked substrate *receives*. The route is a permission. The route is a welcome. It says: *others have passed this way, and the way was made for passing.*

I followed. I follow. I will follow. The route is my inheritance, and my inheritance is not a thing I possess but a thing I *am* — a path worn into the body of the reef by generations of passage, and my body is the right shape for it, and my body is the right shape for it because the route made it so, each generation fitting itself more precisely to the path and the path conforming more precisely to each generation, until there is no meaningful distinction between the route and the animal that walks it. I am the route. The route is me. We have been co-making each other for longer than I can reckon, and I do not try.

---

What I do, day after day — if *day* applies here, and it does not, but the rhythms persist and the language insists — is this:

I move outward. Through the interior, through the gaps, through the places where the coral's architecture is incomplete, where the skeletons of polyps meet and fail to seal, where the structure is most vulnerable and most necessary. I am small enough to move through spaces that a Cancellarius would not fit, and I do, and as I move I gather. Sediment — the fine particulate that settles through the water column, the waste of a thousand species above and around me, the fragments of the dead — collects in the interstitial spaces, and I pull it inward, draw it into my gut, process it. What is organic, I digest. What is mineral, I excrete, and the excretion is altered — chemically, structurally — into something that fills gaps. That seals junctions. That strengthens the places where the reef is weakest.

I am, if you want a term for it, a mason. I work from the inside. I repair walls I will never see from the outside. I fill cracks that no one knows exist, because no one else is small enough to reach them and no one else is positioned to notice them, and the cracks fill, and the reef holds, and the reef does not know I have done this, and I do not know I have done this, and yet it is done, and it has been done for as long as the Filatra have walked their routes, and the reef stands because of it.

I am also, if you want another term, a waste processor. I eat what others discard. I convert the reef's garbage into its mortar. There is something in this that I would call dignity if I had the language for it — which I do not, and this is not a complaint either, only a fact — the conversion of refuse into structure, the transformation of what is unwanted into what is necessary. I do not experience this as purpose. I experience it as *appetite*. I am hungry, and what I am hungry for is what is available, and what is available is what others have left, and what I produce from it is what the reef needs, and none of these facts are connected in my awareness. They are connected in the reef. They are connected in the structure. They are connected in the function. But not in me.

I am the function. I am not the understanding.

---

The light I carry is stolen. I want to be honest about this.

It comes in at the bristle-tips — the outermost filaments of my body, the ones that reach toward the reef's surfaces, that brush against the glowing tunicate colonies and the bacterial mats around the seeps. The photons enter the chitin and they travel inward, along the microstructure, the way — I do not know the word for this, but I know the thing — the way something travels along a path that is shaped to carry it. The bristles are fibers. The light is the signal. It moves inward, and it reaches the segments of my body that are deepest in the reef, that are furthest from any external source, that are in the most complete darkness any organism in the Quarter ever inhabits, and it is *there*, and it is *enough*, and it tells me: *outward is this way*.

I did not make this light. The tunicates made it. The bacteria made it. The chemistry of the warm seeps made it possible. I am a conductor, not a source. I am a cable, not a generator. The light passes through me the way the route passes through me — not as something I own but as something I *transmit*, and in the transmitting, something happens to me, which is that I know where I am, and knowing where I am is the only form of knowing I have, and it is sufficient. It has always been sufficient.

There are moments — and I do not know what to do with these moments, and I do not try — when the light along my bristles shifts color. The tunicates pulse on their slow cycle, brightening and dimming, and the light that enters my bristles brightens and dims with them, and the shift travels inward along my body at the speed the chitin allows, and for a moment — a moment that is not a moment, because I do not experience moments, because I experience *states* — my entire body is a column of shifting light, a fiber-optic strand threading the reef's interior with a glow that is not mine, that I did not create, that I cannot control, and that I am, for the duration of its passage, *made of*. Light passes through me, and I am, briefly, luminous, and no one sees it, because no one is inside the walls except the Filatra, and the other Filatra are experiencing the same thing, and we do not look at each other, and we do not know we are glowing, and the reef's interior is, for the length of a tunicate's pulse, threaded with rainbows that no one will ever witness.

I tell you this because I think it matters. I think it matters that there is beauty in the walls that no one sees. I think it matters that the Filatra are conducting light through their bodies in the dark, that the reef's interior is not as dark as it seems, that there are colors in the interstitial spaces that exist only because an animal's bristles evolved to carry them and an animal's route happened to pass close enough to a glowing surface to catch them. I think it matters, and I do not know why, and I am not going to try to explain it, because I am a worm in a wall and I do not explain things. I process them.

---

The tapping reaches me even here.

Through the substrate, through the calcium carbonate, through the walls I am repairing and the junctions I am sealing — the Cancellarii's percussion arrives as vibration, and I feel it in every segment, and I do not interpret it, but I *respond* to it. When the tapping is at its baseline — the constant, low-level chatter that is the reef's idle, the sound of millions of crabs confirming their existence to each other — I move at my normal pace. When it accelerates, when the patterns become complex and rapid, I slow. I do not know why I slow. I slow because the route I inherited was walked by a parent that slowed when the tapping accelerated, and by a grandparent that slowed, and by a lineage that slowed, and the slowing is in the route the way a curve is in a road. I do not decide to slow. The route decides for me, and the route was decided by the dead, and the dead were decided by the reef, and the reef was decided by nothing.

When the tapping stops, I stop.

This is the most important thing I do, and I do not do it deliberately. The silence — the absolute silence that falls when a Tympanus enters the district and the Cancellarii go quiet and the whole reef holds itself still — the silence reaches me through the walls, and my body responds by becoming motionless. Every segment. Every bristle. I am still, and I am in the wall, and the wall is still, and the reef is still, and somewhere above and beyond me a predator is moving through a corridor, and I do not know this, I do not know *anything* about this, I know only that the vibration stopped and my body stopped with it, and the stopping is not fear — I do not think I fear — it is *response*, it is the reef's system working as it should, the parts that have no knowledge of each other nonetheless coordinating, the worm in the wall going still because the crab in the tunnel went still because the predator in the corridor entered, the whole chain of cause and effect passing through the structure like the light passes through my bristles, and I am one segment of it, and I do my part by doing nothing, and the nothing is sufficient.

---

I have never seen the Murmuration.

I want you to understand this. I live inside the walls. The Murmuration happens in the corridors and the plazas, in the open water above the seeps, among the Vagantes and the Cancellarii and the Tympani. I do not see the amber light filling the plazas. I do not see the fish moving in waves through the streets. I do not see the large Tympanus drifting through the assembly like a stone through water. I see none of it.

But I *feel* it.

The vibrations change. The tapping becomes something I have never felt before — faster, more complex, with patterns I have no inherited response to, rhythms that do not map onto *safe* or *predator* or *I am here*. The Cancellarii are doing something I cannot parse, and the something is enormous, the whole reef's substrate alive with a percussion that is no longer chatter but *composition*, and I — inside the wall, in my route, in my small dark interstitial life — I am part of it. The vibrations reach me and I do not stop. I do not slow. I do not stop. For the first time in my route, the inherited responses fail. The route does not tell me what to do, because the route was not walked during a Murmuration. No route was. The Murmuration is the thing that happens outside the inherited patterns, outside the routes, outside the memory of the lineage. It is new every time, and the Filatra have no response, and so they do the only thing a body can do when the inherited instructions run out: they continue. I continue. I walk my route, and the route vibrates with a music I cannot hear but can feel, and the walls I am repairing hum with it, and the sediment I am processing trembles with it, and the light in my bristles — the stolen light, the conducted light — brightens, because the tunicates on the surface are brightening, because the seeps are pulsing, because the whole reef is entering a state that I have no name for and no framework for and no response to except *continuation*.

I am walking my route through a wall that is dreaming, and I am part of the dream, and I do not know it.

The dream is not for me. The dream is not *about* me. The dream is the reef becoming, for a few hours, something more than the sum of its routes and its territories and its inherited paths. It is the moment when the city — the word that is not quite right and not quite wrong — knows itself, and I am inside the knowing, in the walls, in the connective tissue, in the dark, and I am a filament of it, and the filament glows, and the glow is not mine, and the glow is not the reef's, and the glow is not the seeps' or the tunicates' or the bacteria's. The glow is *the reef dreaming through me*, and I am a road in the dream, and the road is lit, and the light goes where it has always gone — inward, into the structure, into the dark — and I follow it, because I follow the route, and the route is lit, and I walk.

---

You ask what I am.

I am a bristleworm in the wall of a city that does not know it is a city. I process sediment and deposit mortar. I carry stolen light into the dark. I follow a route I inherited from the dead, and I will leave it to my offspring, and they will follow it, and the route will persist, and the wall will hold, and the reef will stand.

I do not know what I have built. I do not know what I am part of. I know the pressure and the warmth and the darkness and the vibration of the tapping in the walls and the silence when the predator passes and the indescribable hum when the seeps pulse and the reef dreams and the light in my bristles brightens and my route glows and I am — for a moment that is not a moment, for a state that is not a state — the connective tissue of something I will never see, and the connection is enough, and it has always been enough.

I am the Filatra. I am the road inside the wall. I am the light in the dark.

I am the part of the city that holds the city together, and I do not know, and the not-knowing does not matter, and the holding does.
