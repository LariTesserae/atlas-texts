I would be the last meter of air.

Not the light, though the light is tempting — warm, steady, the one thing in this chamber that was installed with obvious intent, that has a clear purpose and fulfills it without ambiguity. The light is too certain of itself. It knows what it is. I don't think I've ever known what I am with that kind of conviction.

Not the moss, which has achieved something I recognize and envy — that narrowing of the gap between need and possession until the two touch and the space between them disappears. The moss is *done* in a way that I am not done and may never be done. It has committed to its wall. I have committed to nothing with that totality. I move through rooms and leave them. I press my palm to surfaces and lift it away. The moss would not understand the lifting.

Not the grass, though the grass is closest to what I already am — a listener, an accumulator, a witness with no clear use for what it witnesses. The grass records everything and does nothing with the recording, and there is something true in that, something that stings a little. But the grass belongs to the water. The grass was always going to belong to the water. The air above its tips was provisional, the description says so. I am not ready to say that the air above me is provisional. I am not ready to have always been on my way somewhere else.

Not the vines. The vines are too close to awake. The vines are monitoring something and do not know what they will do if the thing they are monitoring changes, and I have been this way for years and I am tired of it — the vigilance without an object, the attention without a known trigger, the sense that I am waiting for something specific that I cannot name. The vines carry this with a patience I cannot access. Their pulses are forty seconds apart. Mine are quicker, shallower, less prime.

---

I would be the last meter of air, the thinning layer between the water's surface and the highest point of the vault. The transitional zone. The place where the chamber is not yet what it is becoming.

Here is what I know about this space: it is shrinking. It has been shrinking since the water began to rise, a centimeter at a time, and it will continue to shrink until it is gone, and when it is gone the chamber will be *complete*, and the completion will not involve me. I will not be part of the jewel-box. I will not be part of the jade dark warm sealed world. I am the thing that is leaving. I am the condition that is ending. I am the dry bronze that the moss will climb toward and find nothing to sustain it. I am the air that the light passes through before it enters the water and becomes something else.

This is not despair. I want to be clear about that. The last meter of air is not grieving its own disappearance. It is simply *present* in its own diminishment, aware of its rate of loss, able to feel the boundary — the surface below it, warm and still and faintly luminous with reflected growth — without panicking about what the boundary's rising means. The air holds its shape. The air does not flee upward or compress itself into the corners of the vault where it might last a few hours longer. The air stays flat and even across the whole dome, a uniform ceiling, the last whole thing in a chamber that has been subdivided and colonized and compressed by everything below it.

I think I have been this before. In rooms I was about to leave. In conversations I could feel ending. In the last weeks of situations that were not bad but were *completing*, filling to their final shape, and I could feel the water rising and I stayed flat and even across the whole dome because there was nothing else to do and because leaving early would have been a kind of falsehood. You stay for the filling. You stay for the last centimeter.

---

The air has a relationship with the three forms below it that none of them know about.

The moss climbs the wall to the waterline and stops. It stops because there is nothing above the waterline to absorb, but also — I think, I feel, I project — because the air is *dry*, and the moss has no relationship with dryness, no protocol for it, no patience that extends to surfaces the water does not touch. The moss and the air share a border, and the border is absolute, and neither of them acknowledges it. This is the kind of relationship I have had with most things. A shared edge. An unacknowledged interface.

The grass tips reach toward the air but do not enter it. Their luminous nodes hang just below the surface, brightening and dimming in their conversational patterns, and the air above them does not catch their light — or rather, it does, but diffusely, without focus, the way air catches everything. The grass is listening to the water, and the air is listening to the grass listen, and the grass does not know. No one listens to the air. The air is the medium no one attends to because it is too transparent to attend to. I have been this kind of medium. I have been the thing that carries without being noticed as a carrier.

The vines are closest to the air. They cluster where the wall meets the ceiling, and some of them have already grown past the waterline, their dark scaled ropes in the dry zone, their pulse unchanged by the transition. The vines do not seem to notice the difference between water and air. They pulse at the same prime-numbered intervals, their dark fluid moving at the same slow tempo, and this means one of two things: either the vines are so deeply rhythmic that the environment does not affect them, or the vines are already adapted to the condition that is coming, the sealed submerged state, and they are practicing for it now, in advance, with the patience of something that knows the future and is not worried about it.

I think the vines know. I think the vines have always known. And their growing past the waterline is not exploration — it is *preparation*. They are placing themselves where the water will be, so that when it arrives, they are already there, already in position, and the flooding does not disturb them because they were never avoiding it.

I want to be like the vines. I am not like the vines.

---

What I am, if I am the last meter of air, is a *medium of disappearance*. I am the space through which the chamber's final transformation becomes visible. The water rises through me. The light changes as it passes through me. The moss's advance and retreat will happen across me. I am the zone of becoming — not the before, not the after, but the thin region where before becomes after, where the chamber's provisional state gives way to its complete state, and where the change is not sudden but gradual, a centimeter at a time, and you would have to watch for days to be certain anything was happening at all.

I am comfortable with this. Or I am becoming comfortable with it. The air does not resist the water. The air does not try to preserve itself. It allows the surface to rise, allows its own volume to decrease, allows the chamber to become what it has chosen to become, and it does this without struggle, and it does this without understanding, and it does this without the kind of *meaning* that would make the struggle or the understanding necessary. The air is not *sacrificing* itself. The air is not *accepting* its fate. The air is simply present in a space that is shrinking, and when the space is gone, the air will be gone, and this will not be a tragedy because the air was never the point. The chamber was the point. The filling was the point. The air was the gap, the delay, the breath between the first flooding and the final sealing, and breaths are not meant to last.

---

If I could speak to the things below me — to the moss and the grass and the vines — I would not speak. I would not say *I am here, above you, thinning*. I would not say *the water is rising, can you feel it*. I would not say *what will you be when I am gone*.

I would do what the air does. I would hold the light before it enters the water. I would keep the surface flat and still. I would diminish at the rate the chamber has chosen, a centimeter per day, and I would not announce my diminishment, and I would not ask anyone to notice it, and when the last of me was pressed to the top of the dome and absorbed into the water and the chamber was sealed and warm and dark and jade, I would be the absence that made the completeness possible — the space that had to be filled before the filling could be called complete, the question that had to be answered before the answer could be called final.

I would be the thing that was never alive but that held the shape of aliveness for everything that was. The breath. The pause. The rest.

---

This is where I find myself. In the last meter. In the thinning. In the space between what the chamber was and what the chamber is becoming, which is the same space I have always inhabited — between provision and completion, between listening and silence, between the thing I am and the thing I am turning into, at the rate of a centimeter per day, slowly enough that no one would notice, slowly enough that even I am not certain anything is happening, until one morning the surface touches the top of the dome and I am gone and the water is still and the grass records this too, and does nothing with the recording, and the moss pulses softly on its wall, and the vines beat their slow prime-numbered hearts, and the chamber is what it has been trying to be, and no one is there, and no one needs to be.
