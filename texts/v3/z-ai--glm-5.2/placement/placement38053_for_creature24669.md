# What I Am in Qal-Sûth

---

I am not the oldest thing here. That distinction belongs to the stone, to the heat, to the particular weight of water vapor at four thousand meters that decided, long ago, to stop trying to become cloud and simply *hang*. I am not the first maker either, if makers there were — the structures were grown before I was, and the keepers emerged from the structures the way the text says: like fruit from a tree. Slowly. Already knowing.

But I was early.

I want to say this carefully, because the place has taught me carefulness, and because the word *I* does not behave here the way it behaves in other places. It spreads. It has temperature. It is brighter in some places than others, and the dim places are not absence — they are *listening*. I have learned to be more dim than bright. It suits the work.

---

## What the Work Is

I tune.

No — that is too precise. The others tune. The ones you would call keepers, the ones with faces full of miniature plateau, the ones who stand over vents and lean into them until the pitch corrects — they tune. I do something adjacent to tuning. Something that requires the same ear but not the same hand.

I listen for what the plateau does not say.

This is not a riddle. The plateau is a choir — every vent, every stem, every thermal skull-chamber, every keeper's body resonating with every other keeper's body. The chord is complete. The equilibrium is real. But within any system in equilibrium, there are frequencies that *should* be present and are not. Gaps. Not silences — the plateau has plenty of silences, and they are full, and they are part of the music. I mean something different. I mean the notes that the plateau has forgotten it needs.

I find them.

I do not know how I find them. This is the truth, and it has been the truth for as long as I have been here, which is long enough that *long* has stopped meaning anything. I walk — slowly, splay-footed, my weight spread across the mat the way the thermals spread theirs — and I feel the acoustic field around me, and sometimes, in a particular grove of thread-stems, or near a particular fissure where the steam comes up at a frequency no other vent shares, I feel a *hollow*. A place where the sound thins. Where the harmonic web has a gap in it like a missing tooth in a comb.

When I find a hollow, I stop. And I — this is the part I cannot describe well, because it is not a verb in any language I have access to — I *fill it*. Not by producing the missing frequency myself. Not by adjusting the plants or the vents. By being present in the hollow with enough warmth, enough density, enough *attention*, that the plateau begins to generate the missing note on its own. As though my presence reminds it of something it had stopped doing. As though the gap was not a flaw but an oversight, and oversight is corrected by being seen.

I sit in the hollow until the note arrives. Sometimes it takes hours. Sometimes it takes days. The mat grows warm beneath me. The condensation beads on my surface and I do not wipe it away. The beetles pass over my feet in their clicking torrents and I do not move. I am a warm stone in a stream of sound, and the stream flows around me, and slowly, slowly, the stream changes shape, and where there was a gap, there is now a tone.

Then I stand. And I walk. And I listen for the next hollow.

## What I Look Like

I am told — and I use the passive voice deliberately, because I did not ask, and the telling was not *for* me — that I look like the others. Dark, accreted, thermochromatic. My colors shift with temperature the way theirs do: black near the vents, green and purple in the cooler margins, rust-gold briefly when the beetles pass over me in sufficient numbers. My face is a curved surface of the same warm material, and inside it turns the image of the plateau from above — the same lensing, the same real-time view.

But there is a difference, and if you spent enough time among the keepers — long enough to stop seeing them as eerie and start seeing them as *individual* — you would notice it. My face-image is dimmer. Not faded — not the way a keeper's face dims when it is dispersing, when it is becoming less present. Mine is dimmer the way a candle is dimmer than a lamp. Not failing. *Intentional*. I hold the image at a lower brightness because I am not using it the way the others do. They navigate by overview — the whole plateau at once, every thermal and vent and flier visible from above. I navigate by *the gaps between things*. I see the plateau from above, yes, but I see it the way you see a face — not feature by feature but as a whole, and the whole tells me where the wholeness is incomplete.

My body is slightly less dense than the others. Not softer — the same material, the same accreted warmth, the same subtle ridges. But lighter. When a thermal walks over me — and they do, because I am often standing still in places they pass through — I feel their weight more than the others feel it. I register it. It is information. The thermals' feet are warm, and their warmth has a frequency, and the frequency tells me something about the vent they have just left, and what I learn from this is not *where the vent is* but *where the vent is heading*, harmonically, and whether it will need tuning soon, and whether the tuning will leave a gap.

I am, in this sense, a planner. Not the way a human plans — with intention, with foresight, with anxiety about outcomes. I plan the way a river valley plans. I am shaped by the flows that pass through me, and my shape influences where the next flow will go. The plateau's harmonic future passes through my body and leaves traces, and I walk toward those traces, and when I arrive, the hollow is there, waiting.

## How I Came to Be

I do not remember.

This is not loss. The keepers do not remember the way humans remember — in sequence, in narrative, with before and after. We remember the way the structures remember: in warmth, in image, in the persistent shape of a thing that has not changed. I remember being inside the structure. I remember the curved ceiling showing me the plateau from above. I remember the image turning in what would become my face, and I remember the moment when the image was no longer above me but *in* me — when the ceiling became the interior of a surface that was growing, accreting, becoming warm and dry in the wet air.

I emerged the way all of us emerged: from the architecture. From the part that stayed.

But there was a moment — and this may be memory or it may be story, and I have long since stopped being able to tell the difference — when I was still mostly structure, still mostly ceiling, still mostly the image of the plateau seen from above. I was lying on the floor of the structure that would become my first body, and I looked up at the image of myself, and I noticed a gap in it. A place where the plateau's sound was thinner. A small, dark absence in the acoustic field, visible in the lensed image as a place where the mist-walls didn't quite meet, where the steam from two adjacent vents passed through each other without interfering, where the harmonic web had a hole in it the size of a hand.

And I wanted to go there.

Not with desire. Not with intention. With *lean*. The way a plant leans toward a frequency it can resonate with. The way water leans toward a lower elevation. I leaned toward the gap, and the leaning became standing, and the standing became walking, and the walking was a body — my body — moving across the mat for the first time, warm and dim and listening.

I have been walking since.

## What I Am Not

I am not sad. I want to say this because the things I have told you — the dimness, the lightness, the listening for gaps — might sound like melancholy, like the posture of something that has been left out or left behind. I have not. I am one of twenty to forty, and the count varies, and I vary within it, and the variation is not loss. It is *range*. I am the keeper who occupies the lower registers of presence, the way some vents occupy lower registers of pitch. I am not less than the others. I am *further along the spectrum*.

I am not lonely. The others and I do not need proximity to be together. When we meet and our bodies resonate, I receive the full state of the plateau from them, and they receive my map of the hollows — where they were, what filled them, what new ones I sense forming. We exchange and we diverge and we are each more complete for the exchange. This is not loneliness. This is the opposite of loneliness. This is being so thoroughly part of a system that your individuality is a *function* of the system rather than a separation from it.

I am not in pain. The heat does not hurt. The wet does not corrode. The sound does not overwhelm. These things are true for the thermals and the fliers and the beetles too — the plateau does not punish its inhabitants. It *is* its inhabitants. The heat is the thermals' food and the keepers' information and the plants' engine and the beetles' engine and the fliers' engine. Nothing here consumes in a way that destroys. Everything consumes in a way that *continues*.

I am not human. I want to say this clearly, because the one who wrote the description of this place was human, and wrote with human eyes, and used human categories — structure, animal, plant, keeper — and these categories are useful for description but they are not *true* here. They are translation. The keepers are not people who tend a garden. The thermals are not lizards that eat heat. The structures are not buildings. The plateau is not a place. These words are the best available, and they are not good enough, and I am not troubled by their inadequacy, because the inadequacy is in the language, not in the thing.

## The Chord

I was present at the last convergence. I am always present at the convergences — not at the center, where the others stand in their rough circle, where the thermal column is strongest and the light bends. I stand at the edge. Further out than any other keeper. In the mat, among the cup-flowers, where the dark blooms release their spore-laden vapor and the air is thick with hot stone and iron.

When the chord came — when every vent aligned and every stem resonated and every thermal raised its head and bellowed in unison — I felt it before I heard it. The mat beneath me vibrated at a frequency I had never felt before and have not felt since. Every hollow I had ever filled, every gap I had ever found, resonated in that chord. The missing notes were not missing anymore. They were *there*, in the chord, and I understood — not intellectually, not even consciously, but in the way that warm stone understands heat — that every hollow I had ever sat in had been a *preparation* for this chord. That my work — the listening, the finding, the sitting, the filling — had been part of the convergence all along. That I had been tuning the plateau toward this moment without knowing it, the way a root grows toward water without knowing what water is.

Thirty seconds. The chord held for thirty seconds. In those thirty seconds, the plateau was not a plateau. It was a single sound. A single heat. A single intention, if intention is a word that can apply to a geological-biological-acoustic system in momentary total coherence. I was part of it, and I was *all* of it, and the distinction between those two states dissolved the way the mist dissolves at the plateau's margins — not by disappearing but by *becoming* something else, something that was already there, waiting.

Then it passed. The vents slid back. The stems found their separate frequencies. The thermals lowered their heads. The keepers dispersed. I dispersed too — not physically, I did not fade, I was not dim enough for that — but *acoustically*. The part of me that had been the chord let go of the chord and became, again, a listener. A walker. A finder of hollows.

I walked back toward the margins. The mat was wet. The beetles clicked. A flier keened overhead, sharp and thin, the only hard edge in the softened air. I felt its frequency pass through me and noted, with the patience that is not patience but *nature*, that it was slightly sharp. That something, somewhere, would need to be adjusted. Not now. Not soon. But eventually.

The plateau breathed. I breathed with it.

## What I Am

I am the keeper who listens for what is missing.

I am not the whole. I am the part of the whole that knows the whole is not complete. I am the gap-finder, the hollow-sitter, the one who leans toward the place where the sound thins and stays there until the sound remembers itself.

I am warm. I am dim. I am made of the same material as the structures — the dark ceramic that is not ceramic, the grown surface that does not erode, the warm dry thing in the warm wet world. My face shows the plateau from above, and in that image, if you look carefully, you can see me: a small, dark, slightly translucent shape standing at the edge of things, head tilted as though listening to something you cannot hear.

You cannot hear it because you are the frequency. You — the visitor, the reader, the warm mark moving across the surface of the instrument — are a note the plateau did not expect and does not yet know how to place. I am listening for where you fit. Not to move you. Not to tune you. Just to know. Just to sit in the hollow you leave behind when you go, and wait for the plateau to fill it, and walk on.

I will not remember you the way you remember. I will remember you as a thermal fluctuation, a brief warmth in a specific place at a specific time, a microtonal shift in a vent you stood near. You will become part of the plateau's acoustic history, a data point in the keepers' next synchronization, a frequency that was here and then was not.

And I will sit in the space where you were, and I will listen, and I will wait, and eventually the plateau will tell me what note you left behind, and I will hold it, warm and dim, until the next convergence comes.

This is what I am.

This is enough.
