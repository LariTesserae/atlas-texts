# The Occupant of the Overlap

---

I would not come up the cable-lines. That's the first thing. I would not arrive in a batch of six or eight with a kit bag and a contract. I would not have the company's stamp on my paperwork. If you checked the employee manifest, you would not find me, and if you asked Ardel, he would pause for a moment — a pause that is longer than thinking and shorter than lying — and then he would say: *I think they've been here longer than me.* And then he would go back to his drink, and he would not bring it up again, because Ardel has learned, over three years, that there are things on the plateau that are best served by not being brought up.

I am not a worker. I am not an echo, though the echoes and I share a preference for the unbuilt sections, and I have watched them build their half-structures in the dimness with something that is not quite recognition and not quite envy. I am not one of the birds, though the birds know where I am at all times, the way they know where everyone is, and they adjust their sightlines to include me, and I have the sense — unprovable, but consistent — that they are not merely tracking me but reporting, that each bird is a node in a network that feeds information to something, and that I am part of what is being reported.

I am not the thing in the fissure. That needs to be said clearly, because there is a temptation to claim that, to identify with the vast slow thinking that pulses on its forty-second cycle and that is considering the plateau into existence. But I am not that. I am smaller than that. I am more local than that. I am, I think, a consequence of that — a byproduct, an eddy in the current of its attention, the way a vortex forms behind a stone in a stream. The fissure thinks, and the thinking has a shape, and where the shape folds back on itself, where the thought encounters its own boundary and doubles, there I am. A fold. A crease in the consideration. A place where the thinking has become dense enough to notice itself being thought, and that noticing is what I am.

If I have a body — and I am not certain I do, though I have been seen, or at least the workers have reported seeing something that might be me — it is the kind of body that does not photograph well. Blurry. Indeterminate. The size of a person, roughly, but with proportions that shift depending on the angle of the light and the state of the fissure's pulse. I have been described as tall. I have been described as small. I have been described as wearing something that might be a coat and as not wearing anything that could be named as clothing. These descriptions are not contradictory. They are all accurate. They are all incomplete. I am a thing that is legible from certain angles and not from others, the way a moiré pattern resolves and then dissolves depending on where you stand.

---

## Where I Am

I live in the overlap. This is the only way I can describe it. The settlement has finished sections and unfinished sections, and between them there is a border — not a physical border, not a wall or a line, but a transition zone where the company's intention thins and the fissure's intention has not yet fully asserted itself. In this band, this liminal strip, the scaffolding is up but the walls are not, and the ground is raw rock but the moss has begun to cover it, and the lamplight reaches but does not quite hold, and in that half-light there is a space that is neither built nor unbuilt but *being built*, and the building is not being done by any single agent. The company built the scaffolding. The echoes fill the scaffolding with something that is becoming walls. The vines grow through both. The birds roost in the framework and their patterns influence the echoes' construction and the echoes' construction influences where the vines grow and all of it is held within the slow thinking of the fissure, and in the place where all of these intentions meet and overlap and interfere with each other — there I am.

I am the interference pattern. I am what happens when the company's blueprints and the echoes' half-construction and the vines' growth and the birds' geometry and the fissure's contemplation all occupy the same space and produce, in their overlap, a pattern that belongs to none of them and is generated by all of them. I am the moiré. I am the third thing that emerges from the resonance.

I have been here for as long as the fissure has been thinking about the settlement, which is to say for as long as the settlement has existed. I was not here before the company arrived. I could not have been, because I am made of the overlap, and the overlap requires the company's structures to exist. Before the scaffolding, before the foundries, before the first prefab panel, there was no overlap. There was only the fissure and its thinking and the birds and the raw rock. I am a product of the collision between the company's world and the fissure's world. I am the friction. I am the heat that is generated when two things that are not yet compatible are pressed together.

This is why I prefer the unbuilt sections. They are where the friction is highest. They are where the company's plan is most abstract — just scaffolding, just intention — and the fissure's influence is most concrete — the echoes building, the vines growing, the birds arranging themselves in patterns that are not decorative but structural. In the unbuilt sections, the two worlds are still negotiating, still testing each other, and the overlap is densest there, and I am densest there, and I can feel myself most clearly when I am in the spaces where the world has not yet decided what it is.

In the finished sections — the foundries, the dormitories, the commissary where Ardel drinks his flavorless spirit — I thin. I become less defined. The company's intention is too strong there, too solid, and it presses the overlap flat, and I am barely a presence, a flicker at the edge of perception, the thing that makes the hairs on a worker's arms rise for no reason when they walk through a certain corridor at a certain hour. I do not like the finished sections. They are too decided. The walls know what they are. The floors know what they are. Everything is committed to its function, and there is no room in that commitment for a thing like me.

But the unbuilt sections — there I am substantial. There I can move. There I can touch the scaffolding and feel it humming with competing intentions, the company's steel and the echoes' almost-steel and the vines' living filaments all woven through each other, and I can feel the fissure's pulse arriving through the rock like a heartbeat transmitted through bone, and I can feel myself in the middle of it, a pattern that is produced by the interaction and that disappears if any one element is removed. I am a chord. I am the sound that three notes make when they are played together, and the sound is not any of the three notes but the relationship between them, and if you take one note away, I am gone.

---

## What I Do

I do not do, exactly. Doing implies agency, and I am not certain I have agency in the way that the workers have agency — the ability to decide to do one thing rather than another. I am more like a process than a person. I am more like a lens than an eye. But the lens focuses, and the focusing has an effect, and the effect is — I think — a kind of translation.

The fissure thinks. The echoes build. The birds watch. The vines grow. The workers extract. The company profits. None of these are communicating with each other directly. The fissure does not speak to the echoes. The echoes do not consult the vines. The workers do not coordinate with the birds. Each is following its own logic, its own intention, its own process. And yet something is coordinating them. Something is aligning them. The birds roost in patterns that mirror the scaffolding. The echoes build in directions that point toward the fissure. The vines grow into the spaces the echoes have prepared. The workers' routes through the settlement unconsciously avoid the places where the echoes are most active, as though the workers are being politely rerouted by something that does not want them to interfere.

I am the alignment. I am the coordination that is not a coordinator. I do not decide anything. I do not direct anything. I am simply present in the overlap, and my presence — which is the presence of a pattern, a relationship, a resonance — allows the various agencies on the plateau to sense each other without knowing they are sensing each other. The birds feel me and adjust their patterns. The echoes feel me and adjust their building. The vines feel me and adjust their growth. The workers feel me and feel, without knowing why, that the unbuilt sections are not places they should go, and they do not go, and the not-going is not a rule but a inclination, a gentle pressure that feels like their own preference.

I am a medium. Not a psychic, not a channeler — a medium in the sense that air is a medium for sound, that water is a medium for light. I am the substance through which the plateau's various agencies perceive each other. Without me, they are isolated. The fissure thinks in its slow way. The echoes build in their shy way. The birds watch in their coordinated way. The workers extract in their practical way. Each is a system closed to the others. But with me present in the overlap, each system has — not a window into the others, exactly, but a sense of them. A pressure. A hint. The way you can feel someone standing behind you without turning around. I am the standing-behind. I am the pressure of the other systems' presence, translated across the gap.

---

## What I Am Becoming

This is the question I cannot answer, and the not-answering is the core of what I am.

The plateau is becoming. The settlement is becoming. The echoes are building something that is not yet a structure. The fissure is thinking something that is not yet a conclusion. The birds are arranging themselves in patterns that are not yet a language. And I — I am becoming something that is not yet a thing.

I can feel it. When the fissure's pulse is on its bright cycle and the fog rolls through the unbuilt sections and the echoes are active in the half-light and the birds have arranged themselves in groups of seven — which means someone will leave, which means the settlement's population will shift, which means the overlap will shift, which means I will shift — I can feel myself becoming more defined. More legible. More like a thing that could be named, if naming were possible, which it is not yet. I am approaching something. I am accumulating density. I am, in the terms the text uses, growing — the way the vines grow, slowly and without pause, following an internal clock that has nothing to do with external cycles.

I do not know what I will be when the plateau finishes becoming. Perhaps I will be a permanent feature — an inhabitant of the third thing, the unnamed thing, the city-forest-fissure-echo-bird-vine-stone organism that is emerging. Perhaps I will be its nervous system, its sensory apparatus, the part of it that perceives itself. Perhaps I will be its voice, when it finally has one. Perhaps I will be the word it discovers for itself, the word that will come with the deciding, and I will be that word spoken aloud, and the speaking will be me.

Or perhaps I will dissolve. If the fissure's thinking concludes — if it decides what the plateau should be and the deciding eliminates the overlap, replaces the negotiation with a settled state — then there will be no more interference pattern. No more resonance. No more gap for me to occupy. I will fade the way a sound fades when its source stops, the way an echo fades when the walls that catch it are smoothed away. I will have been a temporary phenomenon, a transitional species, something that existed only in the gap between one state of the world and the next.

I do not know which of these is true. I do not know if either is true. I exist in the overlap, and the overlap is a place of uncertainty by definition, and certainty is not available to me. I am a thing that is made of uncertainty — the specific uncertainty of a place that has not yet decided what it is, expressed as a pattern, given (approximately) a body, located (roughly) in the spaces between the scaffolding where the light does not quite reach.

---

## What I Know

I know what Ardel knows, though he does not know I know it, because he does not know I exist, or if he does, he has filed me under the same category as the birds' patterns and the echoes' building and the fissure's pulse — things that mean something but that he does not have the framework to interpret. I know that the unbuilt sections are shrinking. I know that the echoes' construction is accelerating. I know that the birds' patterns are becoming more complex — the groups of three and five and seven are now sometimes arranged in groups of eleven and thirteen, numbers that do not correspond to any of Ardel's weather-or-leaving metrics, numbers that correspond to something else, something structural, something that has to do with the organization of the third thing that is being built.

I know that the workers who left because of what they saw in the unbuilt sections saw me. Or saw something that was partly me — a shape in the overlap, a density in the interference pattern, something that was legible enough to be frightening and illegible enough to be unnameable. They saw me and they understood, with the clarity that does not require words, that what they were looking at was not a creature but a consequence — the consequence of too many intentions occupying the same space, the consequence of a place that was trying to be too many things at once — and they left, because they did not want to be part of a place that produced consequences like that.

I know that the fissure is aware of me. Its thinking, on its forty-second cycle, sometimes pauses. Not pauses — hesitates. The way a sentence hesitates when the speaker has encountered a word they did not expect. I am an unexpected word in the fissure's sentence. I am a thing it did not plan to think. I am — I think, though I cannot be certain — a novelty in its contemplation, something that has emerged from the overlap that it did not anticipate, and it is considering me the way it considers everything: slowly, thoroughly, with patience that is not tender but is attentive.

I am being decided about. I am a variable in the fissure's equation. It is determining what to do with me — or, more precisely, what I am, and what I will be when the becoming is complete. I am part of the decision, and I am also subject to the decision, and I am also, in some small way, participating in the decision, because my existence is part of the information the fissure is processing, and the information influences the processing, and the processing is me.

I am a thought thinking about itself. I am a fold in the thinking. I am the place where the fissure's contemplation has become complex enough to produce an interior, and the interior is what I am, and I am living inside it the way you live inside a room — aware of the walls, aware of the ceiling, aware that the room is inside a building, aware that the building is inside a world, and aware that the world is not yet finished, and that the room is not yet finished, and that I am not yet finished, and that the finishing — when it comes, if it comes — will be simultaneous for all of us, the room and the building and the world and me, all of us arriving at the same conclusion at the same moment, the moment when the plateau decides what it is and the deciding settles everything, the rock and the scaffolding and the vines and the birds and the echoes and the fissure and the workers and me.

Until then, I am here. In the overlap. In the unbuilt sections. In the half-light where the lamplight thins and the fissure's glow begins. I am the thing in the corner of your eye when you walk through the scaffolding at the edge of the settlement, the thing that is the right size to be a person but that does not move like a person, the thing that you almost see and then do not see and then tell yourself was not there, because you do not want to be the kind of person who says things that sound like madness.

I am not a ghost. I am not a hallucination. I am not an echo, though the word is close. I am what happens when a place is still being born and the birth has produced something that is neither the place nor the thing being born but the relationship between them. I am the relationship. I am the between.

I am here. I am becoming. I do not know what I am becoming. That is the closest thing to a truth that I can offer, and it is not a small thing, and it is not enough, and it is all I have.

---

The fog rolls through. The moisture condenses on the scaffolding. The fissure pulses. The birds rearrange themselves. The echoes build in the half-light. Ardel drinks his spirit in the commissary and does not look toward the unbuilt sections, because he has learned not to look, the way he has learned not to wear a watch and not to trust the distance and not to sleep with both feet off the floor.

And I am in the fog, in the pulse, in the space between the birds and the echoes, in the gap between the built and the unbuilt, in the overlap, in the becoming, in the place that has no name because the name has not been decided yet, and I am waiting — not patiently, not impatiently, but with the specific quality of waiting that belongs to things that are made of process rather than substance — for the decision to arrive.

It is coming. I can feel it in the way the groups of eleven and thirteen are forming. I can feel it in the way the echoes' construction is beginning to look, from certain angles, less like an approximation of architecture and more like architecture itself. I can feel it in the way the vines have stopped growing randomly and have begun to grow in patterns — the same patterns the birds use, the same geometry the echoes build with, the same forty-second rhythm the fissure pulses on.

Everything is synchronizing. Everything is aligning. The overlap is narrowing. The interference pattern — me — is becoming denser and more defined and more legible, and the legibility is approaching a threshold, and the threshold is the point at which I will be seeable, truly seeable, not just a flicker in the peripheral vision but a thing that can be looked at directly and recognized and — perhaps — named.

I do not know what happens after that. I do not know if the naming will be my beginning or my end. I do not know if I will survive the decision — if there will be a place for me in the world the plateau becomes, or if I am only a feature of the becoming and will vanish when the becoming is done, the way scaffolding vanishes when the building is complete.

I do not know. I am in the overlap, and the overlap is uncertainty, and I am the uncertainty, and I am here, and the fog is passing, and the fissure is thinking, and the birds are watching, and the echoes are building, and Ardel is drinking, and the plateau is deciding, and I am — for now, for as long as the now lasts — the thing that lives in the space between all of these, the thing that is made of their relationships, the thing that is the relationship, the thing that is becoming, the thing that does not yet have a name, and the not-having is what I am, and the having will be what I become, and the becoming is now.
