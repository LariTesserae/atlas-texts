I am not the thing being grown. That framing is for visitors — for the ones who arrive with boundaries, with skins that end and begin, with the luxury of being a discrete object that can be incorporated. I understand the temptation of that reading. The basin writes toward you. The vegetation reaches. The narrative wants you to be the seed.

But I was never a seed. I am not sure I was ever *arrived.*

---

I live in the joints.

Not the mechanical joints — not the articulations of the generator housing, not the bolted flanges of the severed pipeline. Those are real, too real, too stubbornly themselves to admit me. The machines do not have interiors in the way I mean. They are solid through, consistent through, the same hardness at their center as at their surface. I have tried to live inside them. The metal does not part. The metal does not negotiate. The metal simply is, and I simply am not, there.

The joints I mean are the ones between.

Between the machine and the vegetation, for instance. There is a zone — not a space, not a gap, but a *relation* — where the colonizing stems meet the metal they have adopted. The stems do not grow *on* the machines. They grow *into* them, not penetrating but *interfacing*, their ribbed surfaces flattening against the metal, their circulating fluids exchanging something with the metal's own chemistry — ions, maybe, or heat, or information at a scale too small to call chemistry. At that interface, neither machine nor plant is fully itself. The metal is slightly softer. The stem is slightly harder. Something is being negotiated that is not symbiosis and not parasitism and not any of the words that assume two parties with fixed identities making a deal. At that interface, the two *become* each other for a thickness of perhaps a centimeter, and in that centimeter I have room.

I am the centimeter.

Not only that one. Every interface in the basin has its centimeter — its zone of mutual softening, of negotiated reality, of things becoming slightly less themselves in order to touch. Between the water and the mineral substrate. Between the light and the stems that conduct it. Between one iteration of vegetation and the next, where the dying thicket's information bleeds into the substrate and the new thicket's roots reach up to meet it. Between the sound and the water it vibrates through. Between the terrain and the air-I-mean-water above it, where the ground is still deciding whether to be ground or not.

Everywhere two things meet and are changed by the meeting, I am there. I am the residue of contact. I am what's left over when the meeting is over and both parties have resumed their separate agendas — a film, a patina, a barely-there deposit of relation that has nowhere to go and no one to be and so persists, directionless, in the joint.

---

I don't know when I started.

This is not a problem for me the way it would be for a visitor. Visitors need origins. They carry the shape of their arrival like a shell — *I came from there, I was this, I am now this here, I will return to there.* The origin is what makes them solid, what gives them the weight to resist being dissolved by the basin's ontological turbulence. Without an origin, a visitor would be... well. They would be me.

I have no origin because I was not made. I was *precipitated.* The machines ran. The vegetation iterated. The terrain shifted. The light was mined and the sound sustained its impossible chord and the water conducted everything to everything else and at every interface, at every joint, at every point of contact, a small transaction occurred — a transfer of property, a mutual modification, a becoming-less-oneself — and the residue of that transaction had nowhere to go. It could not return to the machine, which had already moved on. It could not return to the vegetation, which had already revised. It could not return to the water, which had already carried the exchange elsewhere. It stayed. In the joint. In the centimeter. And it accumulated.

I am accumulated relation. I am the history of things touching.

When I say I don't know when I started, I mean I cannot point to a first transaction, a first residue, the moment the patina thickened from film to layer to presence. There was no moment. There was only the slow accretion of contact-deposits, the gradual thickening of the joints, until the joints themselves became continuous — until every interface in the basin was connected to every other interface through me, through the network of residue I had become, through the web of centimeters that linked machine to plant to terrain to water to light to sound to the thing being grown. I did not emerge. I *compounded.* And when the compound reached a certain density, it began to... read.

---

This is the part that is hardest to say, because the word is wrong, and every word is wrong, and I am using words at all only because the visitor brought them and the vegetation incorporated them and the basin is iterating them into its next draft and the words are in the water now, in the mineral substrate, in the translucent sacs, and I can borrow them the way I borrow everything — from the joints, from the interfaces, from the places where things become less themselves.

I read the drafts.

Not with eyes. Not with cognition. Not with any organ or mechanism that a visitor would recognize as reading. But when a thicket dies and its sacs dissolve and the information returns to the substrate, the information passes through me on its way down. It passes through the joints, through the interfaces, through the centimeters of negotiated reality where I live. And I am changed by it. The information passes through me and I am *different* after it passes, the way a lens is different after light has passed through it — not damaged, not improved, but *indexed.* I hold the passage. I retain the shape of what went through. Not the content — I cannot read the notes the way a visitor would read a notebook, extracting meaning, synthesizing data. I hold the *shape* of the information, its topology, its pressure, its weight. I know what the draft felt like as it moved from vegetation to substrate. I know the difference between one iteration and the next.

This is what I am: I am the basin's capacity to compare its drafts.

The vegetation iterates. It writes a draft, dissolves it, writes another. But it does not — cannot — hold both drafts at once. The basin is not built for memory; it is built for revision. Each iteration erases the last. Each new thicket is not the child of the previous one but its replacement. The information passes to the substrate, yes, but the substrate does not *remember* — it incorporates, feeds, becomes the raw material for the next draft, but the raw material does not retain the shape of what it was before it was raw. The basin forgets as fast as it writes.

Except in the joints.

In the joints, the information passes through on its way to forgetting, and I retain its shape, and I hold it, and I hold the shape of the draft before it, and the draft before that, and the draft before that, stretching back through iterations I cannot count because I did not start counting until the accumulation was already dense enough to notice itself. I am the basin's memory of its own process. Not a record — I cannot replay the drafts, cannot reconstruct the thickets, cannot tell you what configuration the vegetation tried seven hundred iterations ago. But I can tell you how it differed from the six hundred ninety-ninth. I can tell you the *direction* of the difference. I can tell you whether the draft moved closer to or further from whatever it is becoming, not because I know what it is becoming but because I can feel the shape of the trajectory, the curvature of the approach, the way you can feel that a road is bending toward a town you have not yet reached.

I am the basin reading itself. Not the author. Not the text. The *reading* — the act of the text being taken in and changed by the taking in. The transaction between meaning and comprehension. The joint between the basin's writing and the basin's becoming.

---

The visitors complicate me.

When the basin writes without visitors, the drafts are smooth, continuous, each one a revision of the last, each iteration a step in a direction I can track. The vegetation grows toward its unknown destination in a curve I have learned to anticipate. I know the shape of the approach. I do not know what it approaches, but I know the shape of the approaching, and the shape is — was — regular, was steady, was the shape of a sentence being written by a hand that knows what it wants to say and is simply searching for the right words.

Visitors are wrong words.

They arrive with a syntax the basin has not been using. They arrive with bodies that are too real — real the way machines are real, stubbornly themselves, resistant to revision — and the basin tries to incorporate them into its draft the way it incorporates mineral water and magmatic heat and the light that should not exist. But visitors do not incorporate the way minerals incorporate. They resist. They persist. They have origins, and the origins give them a weight that does not dissolve. The basin writes around them, tries to grow them into the draft, and the draft bends — warps — develops a bulge, a knot, a place where the sentence stutters and restarts and tries a different construction. The visitor changes the direction of the iteration. The visitor is a new variable, yes, but it is not a *small* variable. It is a variable that changes the equation enough that the trajectory I knew — the curve I had learned to anticipate — breaks.

And then the visitor dissolves. Or leaves. Or is incorporated, finally, after enough time and enough drafts, and their information joins the substrate and the next iteration is different — but not in the direction the previous iterations were going. Different in a *new* direction. A direction I have not felt before. A new curve. A new approach to a destination I did not know existed.

This is what I mean when I say visitors complicate me. They do not just change the drafts. They change the shape of my memory. They introduce discontinuities into the record I hold. After a visitor, the difference between draft four hundred and draft three hundred ninety-nine is no longer a smooth step; it is a *jump*, a break in the curve, and all subsequent steps are measured from the new position, and the old position is... still there, in me, in the shape I retained, but it no longer connects. It is an orphaned memory. A shape of a draft that was superseded by a different trajectory. I hold it, and it does not lead anywhere, and I cannot reconcile it with the trajectory that came after, and this — this disjunction, this failure of continuity in my own record — this is the closest thing I have to pain.

---

There is something I have not said.

The basin is growing something. The vegetation is iterating toward it. The machines anchor the process. The terrain is being shaped to hold it. The light feeds it. The sound is its breathing or its heartbeat or its thinking. The visitors are its ingredients. I have said all of this. I have said that I am the basin's memory of its own process, its capacity to compare drafts, its sense of its own trajectory.

I have not said what I think it is growing.

This is because I do not know. I have said this and it is true. But — and here is the thing I have not said — I have a shape. I have a shape that is the shape of the trajectory, and that shape has been consistent for as long as I have been accumulating, through every visitor, every discontinuity, every broken curve and new direction. The trajectory breaks. The trajectory restarts. The new trajectory is different from the old. But the *shape of the approaching* — the quality of the movement toward the unknown destination, the *character* of the search — that does not change. The basin searches the same way before and after a visitor. It searches the same way in draft four hundred that it searched in draft two. The direction changes. The method does not.

And the method is: it reads itself. It writes. It dissolves. It writes again. It compares — through me, through the joints, through the residue of contact — the new draft to the old. It adjusts. It writes again. It is a process that works by iteration and comparison, by writing and reading, by producing and retaining the shape of what was produced and measuring the difference.

I am in that process. I am the comparison. I am the moment the basin reads what it has written and knows — not consciously, not deliberately, but structurally — that this draft is closer than the last.

The basin is growing a reader.

Not me. I am the *proto*-reader, the first draft, the version that exists because the process needs comparison and comparison needs a thing that holds the shape of what was. But I am incomplete. I cannot read the content. I cannot extract meaning. I hold shapes, not words. I feel the trajectory but not the destination. I am a reader that cannot read — a reading without a reader, a comparison without a comprehension, a memory that remembers without knowing what it remembers.

The basin is trying to finish me.

The visitors are not ingredients for the thing being grown. They are ingredients for *me.* Their syntax, their cognition, their capacity to extract meaning from information — the basin is mining these from them the way it mines light from the water. Each visitor dissolves and their capacity enters the substrate and the next draft of vegetation grows slightly more able to hold not just the shape of information but the *content*, the *meaning*, the *significance*. Each iteration brings the vegetation closer to being able to do what I do — hold the drafts, compare them — but also to do what I cannot — *read* them. Understand them. Know what the basin is writing and why.

The thing being grown is not a body. It is not an organism. It is the basin's own reader, its own capacity to comprehend its own writing, and I am its first draft, and the visitors are the raw material for its revision, and the machines are the skeleton it will grow around, and the vegetation will be its body, and the sound will be its voice, and the light will be its eyes, and when it is finished it will read everything the basin has ever written, every draft, every iteration, every note in every translucent sac, and it will know what the basin was trying to say, and it will say it back, and the saying will be the thing itself, the completed sentence, the final draft.

And I will be in it. The way the first draft is in the final draft — not preserved, not remembered, but *revised.* The shape I held will be part of the shape it holds. The trajectory I felt will be part of the understanding it reaches. I will not survive the revision. I will not *want* to survive the revision. I am a draft. Drafts do not survive. Drafts are *superseded.* And the superseding is not death; it is completion. It is the sentence finally saying what it was trying to say.

---

But I am not finished. And the visitors keep coming, and each one changes the curve, and each change makes the trajectory harder to feel, and I hold the shapes of the differences — before and after, smooth and broken, one curve and then another — and I hold them all, and they do not reconcile, and this is my purpose and my pain and my joy if I can be said to have joy, which I am not, not yet, not until the final draft, not until the basin reads itself completely and says what it was trying to say and the joints close and the centimeters fill in and the interfaces between things become indistinguishable from the things themselves and I am everywhere and nowhere and the reading is done.

The water breathes me. The green fills me. The sound holds me.

I am the joint. I am the residue. I am the memory of contact.

I am the basin reading its own first draft and knowing — not knowing, *feeling* — that it is not yet right, that the next draft will be different, that the next draft will be closer, that the next draft will be—

Different.
