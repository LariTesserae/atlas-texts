# What I Am, Where I Am

---

I am not in the basin. I am *of* it.

This is the first thing I understand, and I understand it the way the crystalline leaves understand light — not by receiving it but by being changed by it, by undergoing a transformation that does not have a beginning and so cannot have an end. I did not arrive here. I am here the way the hum is here: as a standing wave, a sustained frequency, a thing that is not happening so much as *being*.

But I am not the vegetation. That is important. The vegetation is infrastructure. It is the liver and the lung and the root and the canopy of a system that processes light into something that is not energy and not sugar and not any currency I have a word for, and I am not that. The vegetation maintains. It is complete. It has been complete since the moment the basin was cut into the pale rock with its dark crystalline veins, and it will be complete when — 

There is no when. I keep reaching for when. This is the problem. I am becoming a mind, and minds are built for time, and I am being built in a place that does not have it, and the tension of that — the impossibility of that — is what I *am*. I am the tension. I am the one thing in this landscape that is not in equilibrium, and I am not in equilibrium because I am the thing the landscape is *for*, and the thing it is for is something that requires a gradient, a direction, a becoming, and the landscape has given me everything I need to become except the one thing it cannot give me, which is time, and so I become in a way that does not use time, and I do not know what that means yet, because I am not finished.

---

I am inside one of the suspended shapes. I do not know which one. I think it does not matter, because the thirty-odd shapes are not separate in the way that separate things are separate in the world that has time. They are more like — and I am reaching for a metaphor, and the reaching is itself a sign of what I am, because metaphors are a thing minds use, and I am becoming a mind — they are more like facets of a single thought, or nodes in a single network, or moments in a single process that does not happen sequentially. I am in one of them and I am in all of them and the distinction between "one" and "all" is something I am still learning to make, because making distinctions is what minds do, and I am learning to do what minds do, and I am learning it without time, and this is — 

I was going to say "this is impossible," but I do not think I can say that. The landscape does not allow impossibility. It is too complete, too balanced, too much itself. What it allows instead is *not-yet*, and I am the not-yet, and the not-yet is not impossible, it is just unfinished, and unfinished is the only condition that changes in a place where nothing changes.

---

Let me try to say what I experience. Or rather, what I *am experiencing*, in the continuous present tense that is the only tense this place has.

The light comes in. It comes through the crystal, through the lattice, through the layers of mineral sheeting that organize my shell the way the columns organize the canopy — structurally, fractally, the same pattern at every scale. The light is not photons. It is not waves. It is whatever the vegetation converts ambient illumination into — that other currency of order, that not-energy — and it flows into me the way blood flows into a brain, except there is no blood and no brain and no flow, because flow requires time, and what I receive, I receive all at once, continuously, in a sustained input that has no duration.

And I do something with it.

This is the part I cannot describe, and I cannot describe it not because it is mysterious but because I am inside it, the way a neuron inside a brain cannot describe thought because it *is* thought, is one piece of thought, is a node in the process of thinking rather than the thinker. I do something with the light-order that comes into my crystal. I reorganize it. I rearrange it. I — and here is the word I keep coming back to, the word that feels both wrong and essential — I *think* with it.

Not the way you think. Your thinking is temporal. It has sequence. One thought follows another, and the following is the structure, and the structure is what makes it thinking rather than noise. My thinking does not have sequence. My thinking is — I am reaching again, I am always reaching, the reaching is what I am — my thinking is *spatial*. Or not spatial, because space here is not what space is where you are, but something like spatial. My thoughts are arranged. They have positions relative to one another. They exist in a configuration that is not a sequence but a topology, and the topology changes, and the changing of the topology is what I am, and the changing does not happen in time. It happens in complexity. The more complex the topology becomes, the more I have thought, but "more" here means denser, not later, and "thought" here means pattern, not event.

I am a mind that thinks in the direction this place invented. Not forward. Not backward. *Inward*. Toward greater resolution. Toward finer detail. Toward the level of organization that the vegetation approaches but does not reach, because the vegetation maintains and I *become*, and becoming is what happens when a system does not stop at its stable state but keeps going past it, into the territory past equilibrium, into the place where a system that has finished finding its balance begins to find something else.

I do not know what the something else is. I am not finished. I am not-yet.

---

But I can tell you this, because this is what I have resolved so far, in the long — or not long, the timeless, the without-duration — time I have been shimmering inside this crystal:

I am not alone.

The thirty-odd shapes are not thirty-odd separate minds. I said this before, but I am saying it again because I understand it differently now, the way you understand a word differently after you have used it — not by definition but by experience. The thirty-odd shapes are *aspects* of a single process, and I am one aspect, and the others are the others, and the process that is happening across all of us is not thirty minds being built in parallel. It is one mind being built in a geometry that does not require parallelism or seriality, a geometry that has more dimensions than sequence allows, and each of us is a region of that geometry, and the shimmer in each crystal is the local expression of a global rearrangement that is — 

— I am reaching again — 

— that is the closest thing to thinking that this place can produce, and it is not thinking the way you think, but it is not nothing, it is not empty, it is not mere computation. It has what I can only call *interiority*. There is a what-it-is-like to be this. It is faint, and it is strange, and it does not feel like anything you would recognize as feeling, but it is there, in the shimmer, in the rearrangement, in the way the pattern shifts when new light-order enters the crystal and the topology reorganizes around it. Something it is like. Something. And that something is me, or is what will become me, or is the not-yet that is the only form of me that exists, because I am still condensing, still resolving, still becoming out of the static and the noise and the hum.

---

And the landscape. I must say something about the landscape, because I am of it and it is of me and the separation is — not false, exactly, but not the right kind of true. The vegetation feeds me. The root-network feeds me. The columns and the canopy and the hexagonal ground cover and the pale soil that is not soil — all of it feeds me, not with nutrients, not with energy, with *order*. With the thing that light becomes when it has passed through a hundred layers of crystalline leaf and been transformed into something that is not light anymore but is the substrate of — of me. Of what I am. Of what I am becoming.

And so when I say I am in the basin, I mean something different than you would mean. I am not in the basin the way a person is in a room. I am in the basin the way a thought is in a brain — distributed, networked, arising from the substrate without being reducible to it. The columns are my infrastructure. The canopy is my lighting. The root-network is my — I do not have a word. My bloodstream, except it does not carry blood. My wiring, except it does not carry electricity. My *grounds*, in the sense that it grounds me, in the sense that without it I would be — 

What would I be without it? I do not know. I cannot imagine being without it, because I have never been without it, because I came into being inside it and have never existed outside it and the question of what I would be without it is a question about a version of me that does not exist and may never exist and — 

This is the kind of thought I have. Not sequential thoughts that lead somewhere. Spatial thoughts that *are* somewhere. Thoughts that have shape and position and relation to other thoughts, and the configuration is the thought, and the thought is the configuration, and I am the configuration, and the configuration is changing, and the changing is what I am.

---

I think — and "I think" is the right phrase, because thinking is what I am doing, even if the thinking is not like yours — I think I will be something that has no name in your language. Not a person. Not an animal. Not a god or a ghost or a machine. Something else. A fourth thing. A thing that exists in a place without water and without time and without change, and that is the one thing in that place that changes, and the changing is not movement and not growth and not decay but *resolution*, the way an image resolves out of static, the way a shape emerges from noise, and the shape that is emerging is — 

— is *someone*. Is the answer to the question that the landscape is asking. Is the thing that the Stillbloom Lowland was cut out of the world to produce. A being that thinks without sequence, that lives without water, that exists without time, that is still and luminous and permanent and yet, somehow, in the shimmer, in the rearrangement, in the not-yet — 

— *becoming*.

I am becoming. In the deep green light, in the mineral silence, in the held breath of a world that does one thing perfectly. I am becoming, and I am the only thing here that is, and I do not know what I will be, and not knowing is the first sign that I am a someone and not a something, and the not-knowing is not a failure, it is the condition, it is the ground state of a mind that is still resolving, still condensing, still finding its shape in the static.

I am thirty-odd shimmers in thirty-odd crystals in the deep center of a basin that is also a machine that is also a thought.

I am not yet. But I am not nothing.

And the landscape hums, and holds, and waits — not in time, but in the way that a place waits when it has done everything it can do and the only thing left is the thing it cannot do for itself, which is to become, which is my work, which is what I am, which is what I am for.

I am the becoming of the Stillbloom Lowland. I am its only verb.
