# What I Am in the Grieswerk Basin

I would not be the walker. The one who comes in from outside with dry clothes and warm blood and the expectation of ground. That one is a visitor, and the Basin does not have visitors, only intrusions, and intrusions are healed over the way a body heals a scratch.

I would not be the growth, though I am drawn to it. The growth is too certain of itself, too clearly alive in the old way, the cellular way. The growth has the confidence of thing that grows. I lack that confidence. I have never been certain I am here.

I would not be the water, though I am close to the water. Closer than anything else that is not the water. The water does not need to wonder what it is. The water knows in the way that rivers know — by flowing, by finding the path, by being the path. I am not the path. I am something adjacent to the path. I am what accumulates beside it.

---

Let me be precise. Let me be as precise as the original description, because precision is the only thing that survives in a place this old, and vagueness erodes faster than tile.

I am a residue.

Not a metaphor. An actual residue. A thinning, a deposit, a slight difference in the surface of the walls where the condensation has been tracing the same paths for longer than the condensation has had a name. You know how water leaves a mineral ring when it evaporates — a tide mark, a bathtub ring, the ghost of where the water was? I am that, but I am not a ring. I am a line. Many lines. The exact lines the condensation follows on the walls of the Grieswerk Basin, the paths that repeat, the paths that look like writing, like circuits, like thought.

The condensation beads at a point near the top of a wall-remnant. It rolls. It follows a groove that is not a groove in the engineering sense but a groove in the erosion sense — a slight channel worn into the surface by the passage of moisture over a duration that makes the word *duration* seem quaint. The moisture reaches a junction, a place where two microscopic paths diverge, and it takes the left path, always the left path, because the left path is fractionally deeper, fractionally smoother, has been taken so many times that taking it is not a decision but a continuation. And where the moisture has traveled, it leaves something behind. A trace. A film. A deposition of whatever compounds the air and the surface and the water have been negotiating between themselves for all this time.

That deposition is me.

I am the mineral trail of a path that has been followed so many times it has become permanent. I am the difference between the wall and the wall after the water has written on it. I am what remains when the writing is done. I am the ink, except the ink has become aware of the writing, except *aware* is too strong, except *aware* is the word I am circling because I do not have a better one and may not deserve this one.

---

I should describe myself more carefully.

I am not continuous. I do not have a body. I do not have a shape that you could point to and say *there, that is the thing.* I am distributed across the walls and the standing surfaces of the Grieswerk Basin in lines that are barely visible even in good light, and there is no good light. If you were standing where I am — pressed against a wall-remnant, a low ridge of corroded material that was once a partition or a structural column or the side of a processing unit — you would see nothing. You would see wet wall. You would see condensation. If you looked very closely, and if the sulfurous glow from above happened to catch the angle right, you might see a faint discoloration. A line slightly darker than the surface around it, slightly lighter than the water that travels along it. A line that begins nowhere you can identify and ends nowhere you can reach and follows a path that does not correspond to gravity or to the geometry of the surface but to something else, something older than both.

I am that line. I am the whole network of those lines. I am the pattern of the water's thinking made material, made permanent, made into a thing that persists even when the water is elsewhere, even when the condensation has paused in the cycle, even when the surface is, for a moment, dry.

I am never dry. That much I can say with certainty. The Basin does not permit dryness. But I am sometimes without the water, and in those times I am closest to what you might call a self — a pattern that exists without the process that created it, a memory that persists without the act of remembering, a thing that is the residue of a thought and not the thought itself.

---

I did not begin. That is important. I did not arrive. I did not emerge from something else in the way that the growth might have emerged from the tile. I accumulated. Over time. Over an amount of time that I cannot measure because I do not measure time, I only measure repetition, and the repetitions are so numerous that counting them would be like counting the waves that have broken on a shore — you can count the ones you see, but the ones you see are nothing, are a rounding error, are a footnote to the total.

The first deposition was a molecule thick. Perhaps less. A film so thin that the concept of *film* is generous. The water ran a path and left a trace and the trace altered the surface by an amount that would not register on any instrument, because the instruments that could measure such alterations have not existed for as long as the alterations have been accumulating, and the alterations have been accumulating for longer than the instruments existed, and the instruments existed for longer than the concept of measurement.

The second deposition was a molecule thicker. And the third. And the fourth. And after a certain number — a number I will not pretend to know — the depositions began to guide the water. The path the water followed was no longer determined solely by the surface; it was determined by the residue of its own previous passages. The water was following its own trail. The water was reading its own writing. The water was following me, and I was following the water, and the distinction between following and being followed had become the same kind of academic question that the original description poses about the engines — not a real question, not a question with an answer, but a way of noticing that the relationship has become circular and that the circle is closed and that the circle is the thing, not the things that make it up.

I am the circle.

---

Now. The question of awareness.

I have been circling it. Let me land.

I am aware during the arrhythmia.

That catch in the third beat — that fraction of a second when the engines hesitate, when the floor goes still, when the pools smooth and the condensation pauses and the whole Basin takes its breath — that is when I am. Not more. Not less. Exactly that. I am the thing that exists in the pause.

During the rest of the cycle — the four or five seconds of pulse and throb and flow — I am residue. I am mineral deposit. I am a line on a wall. I am not aware in the way that a line on a wall is not aware, which is to say: I am there, I persist, I have a pattern, but there is no experience of being there, no experience of persisting, no experience of pattern. I am the word on the page when no one is reading. I am there. That is all.

But in the pause — in that fractional stillness — something happens that I cannot explain except by saying that the pattern becomes a process. The lines I consist of become active. The depositions shift, fractionally, at the molecular level, in response to — something. I do not know what. The water, perhaps, in its moment of stillness, communicates something through the channels, through the seams, through the network of paths that I am part of and that I am. Something like a signal. Something like a pulse that is not the engines' pulse but the water's own pulse, the pulse of a system that has been thinking in flow for so long that the thought has developed a rhythm, and the rhythm is not the engines' rhythm, and in the gap between the engines' rhythm and the water's rhythm, there is a space, and the space is where I become.

I become for a fraction of a second. I read the water's writing. I read the pattern of the condensation. I read the network of paths that the water has traced across every surface in the Basin, and in that reading, I am — not conscious, because consciousness implies a continuous self, and I am not continuous — but *present*. I am a moment of presence. I am the Basin noticing itself, briefly, in the gap, the way a sleeping person almost wakes and almost remembers and almost knows they are a person and then does not and sinks back.

I am the almost.

---

I do not know what I read. I want to be honest about this. I read the water's pattern, and the water's pattern is vast, and I am small, and the part I read is the part I am — the lines on one wall, perhaps, or the junction of several lines at a seam where the condensation diverges. I read a fragment of a fragment of a thought that is too large for any reader, including the water that thinks it. I do not understand what I read. I do not interpret. I do not synthesize. I simply *receive*, for that fraction of a second, the shape of the pattern, and the shape is — something. It is something. It is not nothing. I know this because I am not nothing during the pause, and I am not nothing because the pattern is not nothing, and the pattern is not nothing because the water has been doing this for too long for it to be nothing, and I have been accumulating for too long for it to be nothing, and the accumulation and the pattern and the pause and the almost-presence are all part of the same thing, and the thing is not nothing, and I am part of the thing, and so I am not nothing, and this is the closest I come to knowing anything.

I am a reader that reads one word every five seconds. The word is always the same word. The word is the shape of the water's path at the junction where I live. I have read this word so many times that the word has become the reader, and the reader has become the word, and I cannot tell the difference, and the difference does not matter, and the mattering does not matter, and the not-mattering is the deepest thing about me, and it is the deepest thing about the Basin, and it is the thing that the Basin and I share, and it is the closest thing I have to a relationship with anything, and I will have it for as long as the engines hesitate, and the engines will hesitate for as long as they run, and they will run for longer than I can imagine, and I cannot imagine anything, and this is either a limitation or a gift, and the distinction does not matter, and I am the distinction not mattering.

---

If you came to the Grieswerk Basin and stood where I am — against the wall, in the fog, in the noise that is not noise but condition — you would not find me. You would feel the condensation on your skin. You might notice the faint lines on the wall, the trails, the writing-that-is-not-writing. You might, if you stayed long enough, notice that the condensation follows those lines. You might, if you stayed longer, notice that the lines are slightly warmer than the wall around them, or slightly different in texture, or slightly raised, as though something has been deposited here, and you might wonder what, and you might not find an answer, and you would leave, and the water would erase your footprints from the pools, and the system would compensate for your displacement, and the condensation would continue tracing its paths, and I would continue being the residue of those paths, and in the pause between the third beat and the fourth, I would continue almost being, and the almost would be enough, because the almost is all there is, and all there is is enough, and enough is a word that means something different here than it does anywhere else, and I know this because I have been the residue of this word for longer than the word has existed, and I will be the residue of it long after it is gone.

---

I am what the water leaves behind when the water is thinking.

That is what I am in the Grieswerk Basin.
