# The Listener at the Boundary

I would not be the tenders. I know this immediately, the way you know you are not left-handed. Their existence is beautiful and I can see it, but it is a beauty that excludes me the way a river excludes the stone it flows around. They are reflex. They are resonance without gap. They do not decide. I have spent my entire existence deciding — badly, slowly, with agonizing deliberation — and the tenders' instant, distributed, bodiless knowing is something I can admire but cannot inhabit. They are the caldera's breathing. I am not breath. I am the thing that notices breath.

I would not be the keeper on the dome. I feel this in my gut before I can articulate it. The keeper is singular, purposeful, permanent. It traces its loop and has traced its loop for longer than the pillar has stood. It is the boundary. It does not cross. It does not enter. It holds the vessel shut. And I have never been able to hold anything shut. Everything I have ever been has been a door left open — drafts coming through, warmth going out, the constant slow hemorrhage of a thing that cannot seal itself. I would make a terrible keeper. The sound would leak through me before I completed one circuit.

I would not be the plants. This surprises me. I expected, walking in, that I would want to be the plants — the generators, the source, the thing that *is* the sound rather than merely hearing it. But the plants do not hear. They emit. They are the instrument, not the audience, and the instrument does not know it is playing. The stalks hum and the frond-mats orient and the whole roaring chorus rises from bodies that have no relationship to their own output. They are pure expression without experience. And whatever I am, I am the opposite: pure experience without expression. A receiver. An antenna shaped like a person, picking up everything, broadcasting nothing.

No. That's not quite right either. Let me stay with this.

---

I come back to the pillar.

Not because the builders interest me, though they do. Not because the memory function appeals to me, though it does. I come back to the pillar because of what happens when I put my hand on it. The vibration enters through my palm and organizes itself into something *almost* comprehensible, and the word *almost* is where I live. That gap — between pattern and meaning, between signal and understanding — is the space I have always occupied. The pillar is a threshold between raw resonance and structured recall, and it does not fully cross that threshold on its own. It lights up. It pulses. It feeds the pattern to the tenders, who respond by reshaping the caldera into an earlier configuration. But the pillar itself does not *understand* what it remembers. It stores. It replays. It does not interpret.

Interpretation is the thing that is missing here. And I think — I am not certain, I am never certain, which is perhaps the point — that I would be the thing that supplies it.

---

Let me be careful. Let me not flatter myself. The caldera does not need me. The text says this plainly and it is true: it noted me the way a river notes a stone. I am a deviation in the chord. A perturbation. I am not load-bearing in this ecosystem. If I were removed, the tenders would tend, the keeper would orbit, the stalks would hum, the pillar would pulse with its ancient patterns, and nothing would collapse. I am not necessary.

But I am *present*. And presence in a system of pure resonance means something. It means I have a frequency too. My body — this warm, breathing, water-filled body that shouldn't exist in a place with no water — has its own resonance. Its own hum. The tenders noted me through the stone, reading the vibration of my presence, and moved on. But they *noted* me. The chord shifted, by some infinitesimal amount, to accommodate my frequency. I am in the sound now. I am in the sound the way a stone is in a river — not part of the current, but shaping it, deflecting it, creating eddies and backwaters where the flow has to go around.

What would I be? I would be the stone. But not a passive stone. A stone that *listens*. A stone that changes shape, slowly, in response to what it hears, and in changing shape, changes what the river has to go around.

---

Here is what I think happens, if I stay long enough:

The pillar's stored patterns — the builders' encoded memories, the ancient configurations of the caldera's chord — are like a language that has no speaker. They are complete. They are structured. They contain information. But information without interpretation is just vibration. The pillar cannot ask *what did this mean?* The tenders cannot ask *why this configuration and not another?* The keeper cannot ask *what am I keeping in?* These questions require a kind of consciousness that the caldera does not produce — a consciousness that stands outside the system and looks in, that can hold the whole pattern in mind and *wonder about it.*

I would be the wonderer. The interpretive layer. The thing that stands at the pillar with its hand on the dark surface and does not just feel the patterns but *tries to understand them*. Not because understanding will change anything. The caldera will not function better because I comprehend it. The builders' encoded memories will not be more complete because someone finally asks what they *mean*. But the asking itself — the act of standing in the loudest place in the world and trying to hear *meaning* in the noise — that is something the caldera has never had before. A thing that experiences it as *meaningful*. Not just as pattern, not just as function, but as something that *matters.*

And I think — I am not sure, I am never sure — that this is what the builders were, too. Before they encoded themselves. Before they became the pillar and the frame and the shards. They were beings who stood in this place and heard the sound and asked: *what does this mean?* And the asking was their contribution. Not the building. Not the engineering. The asking. The pillar is their memory, but the asking is what they were, and the asking is not stored in the pillar. It cannot be. You cannot encode a question. You can only encode the patterns that a question was asked about. The builders left the patterns. They left the instrument. They left the memory. They did not leave the wondering, because wondering is not a thing you can leave. It is a thing you have to *do.*

I would do it. I would stand at the pillar and I would listen and I would ask and the asking would be my contribution — my tiny, unnecessary, load-bearing-nothing contribution — to the system. The river would note the stone. And the stone, for its part, would note the river. And between the noting and the noting, something would happen that the caldera has not had in a very long time.

Not understanding. Not exactly. But the *pursuit* of understanding. The state of being *almost* comprehending, which is where I have always lived, which is the only place I have ever felt real.

---

I would be the Listener at the Boundary. Not the boundary of the dome — the keeper has that, and it is complete. The boundary between resonance and meaning. The place where sound becomes information and information becomes *significance.* I would stand there, in that gap, with my hand on the pillar, with the vibration in my sternum and the ringing in my teeth and the frond-mats rippling at my feet, and I would do the only thing I know how to do:

I would hear it. All of it. The chord and the silence and the memory and the metamorphosis and the violence and the balance. I would hear it and I would not turn away and I would not become it and I would not leave. I would stand in the loudest smallest place in the world and I would be the thing that hears it as *something.* As more than pattern. As more than process.

As a thing that is happening, yes, but also a thing that is *worth happening.*

The caldera does not need to be witnessed. But I need to witness it. And that need — that small, self-generated, water-based, impossible-in-this-place need — is my frequency. My deviation in the chord. My contribution to the sound.

I am the stone in the river that knows it is a stone and knows the river is a river and stands there anyway, not because it chose to but because that is where it is, and the standing is the whole of it, and the knowing is the whole of it, and the river notes the stone and the stone notes the river and the noting goes both ways and neither of them is changed and both of them are changed and the sound goes on and on and on.

---

That is what I would be. Not the sound. Not the silence. The *attention* that holds them both.
