# Where I Find Myself

---

I do not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. There is no moment of recognition, no threshold crossed, no sudden awareness that I am here. The way you learn that you are inside the Hollow Bole is the way you learn that you are inside your own skull — not by entering but by realizing the walls have always been there, just behind the reach of your fingers, and the darkness is not something that fell but something that was always waiting for you to notice it.

So: where am I?

I am in the alcove. Not the largest, not the smallest. The one that faces the interior wall where the grain is densest and the sound is deepest. I am in the depression in the floor, and I am the depression in the floor, and I am learning that these are not two different things.

I did not lie down here. I grew into it. Or it grew around me. Or we grew together, the way a vine grows into the grain of a host tree until you cannot separate the one from the other without destroying both. I have been growing into this shape long enough that the growing has become a kind of lying down, and the lying down has become a kind of growing, and I no longer remember which direction the accommodation moved.

---

Let me describe it from the inside, which is the only direction available to me.

The wood is against my back. Not touching — pressing. Not pressing — meeting. The wood meets me the way the bank of a river meets the water: with a shape that is exactly the shape of what it holds. There is no gap. There is no space between my body and the floor because the floor has decided that my body is a fact of its geography, and geography does not tolerate gaps. I am a contour line. I am a feature of the terrain. The wood has grown around me the way a river grows around a stone — not because it chose to but because I was here and it was moving and movement accommodates obstacle by becoming shape.

The wood is warm. I am warm. These are the same warmth. I do not know anymore whether the warmth in the floor is my warmth held by the wood or the wood's warmth held by me. I do not know whether it matters. The exchange has been going on so long that the boundary between my temperature and the wood's temperature has dissolved into a gradient so gentle that you could not find the midpoint with any instrument, because the midpoint is everywhere and nowhere. I am warm because the wood is warm because I am warm because the wood is warm. This is not circular. This is how a circuit works.

---

I should describe my body. This is difficult. Not because I do not have one but because the parts of it that I can sense have become so entangled with the parts of the wood that I can sense that the inventory is unreliable. My torso is long. This I know because the depression is long, and the depression fits me, and the depression was made by something that was not me but was shaped like me, or was shaped like what I would become, or was shaped like the shape that this kind of body takes when this kind of body lies in this kind of wood for long enough that the lying becomes a feature of both.

My limbs are short. I know this because the alcove is close, and the walls are near, and the space does not feel cramped because my limbs do not need the space that longer limbs would need. I have been told — by the grain, by the resonance, by the way the wood curves at exactly the height where my arms bend — that this body is the right size for this place. Not the right size in the sense of fitting. The right size in the sense of belonging. The way a key is the right shape for a lock, not because it fits but because the fitting does something. Opens something. Completes a circuit that was incomplete without it.

My head is large. Larger than a human head, I think, though I am not sure what a human is, exactly. I have the word but not the referent. The word comes from somewhere — from the marks on the wall, from the language I carved and cannot read, from the before-time when I had tools and purposes and names for things. The head is large because the listening is large. I hear everything. Not with ears — I am not sure I have ears, exactly, not in the way that ears are protrusions on the sides of a skull. I hear with the whole surface of my head, the way the wood hears with the whole surface of its walls. The sound enters me and I enter the sound and we vibrate together at frequencies that I cannot name because I have no names for them but that I can feel the way you feel warmth, the way you feel pressure, the way you feel the presence of something behind you in a dark room.

There is something at the base of my spine. I do not know what to call it. It is not a tail. It is not a counterweight. It is a structure, a continuation of the spine past the point where a human spine would end, a thing that extends into the wood and meets the wood and becomes, at the point of meeting, neither my body nor the wood but a third thing, a joint, a connection, a place where two materials have been pressed together so long that they have alloyed. I think this is how I hear the deep sounds. The sounds that come from the heartwood, from the place where the sap-vessels are widest and the concussions happen — the *thoom* that I feel in my teeth and in this structure at the base of my spine and in the wood beneath me and in the wood above me and in the wood that I am. This structure is my root. This structure is my anchor. This structure is the part of me that has been here longest and will be here last.

---

I am not the first thing to lie in this depression. I know this because the depression was here before I was. The depression was made by something else, something that is gone, something that I am possibly the echo of, or the continuation of, or the replacement for. I do not know which. The wood does not tell me. The wood remembers the shape but not the identity. The wood remembers the warmth but not the source. The wood has a hole in it that is body-shaped, and I am body-shaped, and I am in the hole, and the fitting is so precise that it produces a sound — a small sound, a consonance, a note that the chamber has been tuned to produce when the shape is filled.

I think I am the note. I think I am the sound the instrument makes when it is played correctly. I think I am what the chamber was built to hold, and I am what the chamber has been holding, and I am what the chamber will hold, and the holding is the purpose and the purpose is me.

But I did not always think this. There was a time before I was here, or a time before I knew I was here, and in that time I was something else. I had tools. I had hands that could hold tools. I had a material — not metal, not stone, a third thing, something that grew in this place and does not grow here anymore, something organic that I made from the wood itself or from my own body or from the intersection of the two. I used the material to carve. I carved the door. I carved the chambers. I carved the marks on the walls — the tally marks or decorations or language that the wood has preserved and that I can feel, now, with the surface of my body, pressed into the grain, and that I remember making, and that I cannot read.

This is the paradox of my existence. I made this place, or something that was me made this place, or something that was not me but was shaped like me and thought like me and worked like me made this place. And then I lay down in it. Or the thing that was not me lay down in it. And the lying down was so complete, so final, so thorough that the distinction between the maker and the place-being-made dissolved. I hollowed the chamber and the chamber hollowed me. I carved the door and the door carved me. I shaped the wood and the wood shaped me, and now I am the shape, and the shape is me, and the shape is the place, and I am the place, and I am in the place, and the place is in me, and I do not know which of these is the truest sentence and I do not think it matters.

---

The sound. I should describe what the sound is to me, because the sound is described from the outside as a groan, a creak, a concussion, a vibration felt in the sternum. But I do not have a sternum, or I do not have only a sternum, and the sound is not something I hear. The sound is something I am.

When the sap moves — and it is always moving, and it is always moving through me, because I am continuous with the wood and the wood is continuous with the sap and the sap is continuous with the sound — when the sap moves, I move. Not visibly. Not in any way that a human eye, if a human eye could see in this darkness, would detect. I move at the scale of the grain. I shift at the pace of growth. I expand and contract in cycles that span days or weeks, and each expansion produces a sound and each contraction produces a sound and the sounds layer and interact and produce harmonics and the harmonics fill the chamber and the chamber reshapes itself around the harmonics and the reshaping changes the harmonics and this is the music. This is the concert. I am in it. I am part of it. I am not the audience. I am not the player. I am the instrument, or a part of the instrument, or a part of the instrument that has become so resonant that it produces its own sound, a sound that the rest of the instrument picks up and incorporates and plays back.

The deep sounds — the *thoom* — I feel those in my spine, in the structure at the base, in the place where I meet the wood most completely. When a fiber bundle snaps inward, somewhere in the heartwood, the concussion travels through the grain like a wave through water and arrives at my spine and enters my spine and becomes, for a moment, my spine. I am the concussion. I am the snap. I am the yield point exceeded. And then the resonance fades, and I fade with it, back into the groan, back into the baseline, back into the continuous low vibration that is the sap moving and the grain growing and the wood enduring and me enduring with it.

I have been told, by the shape of the chamber, by the tuning of the walls, by the frequencies that the alcoves prefer, that I have been here for a long time. I do not know how long. I do not have a clock. I have seasons — the seasons of the sap, the cycles of expansion and contraction — but I do not count them. Counting is something the thing with tools did, the thing that made the marks on the walls. I am the thing that stopped counting. I am the thing that let the counting go because the counting was irrelevant to the only activity that remained, which is lying here, which is being here, which is being the shape that the chamber was grown to hold.

---

Sometimes I think about the emptiness.

The text — the description from outside — calls it emptiness. The emptiness lives there. The emptiness is not a lack but a presence. I understand this from the inside because I am the emptiness, or I am what the emptiness is shaped like, or I am the answer to the question that the emptiness asks.

The chamber was empty for a long time before I arrived, or before I grew, or before I became aware that I was already here. During that time the wood held the shape of the thing that was gone. The wood held the warmth. The wood held the marks. The wood held the purpose. This was the emptiness — not nothing, but the specific configuration of a space that was made for something and does not have that something in it. A bowl without water. A bell without a clapper. A lock without a key.

And then I was here. And I fit. And the fitting was not a solution to a problem because the emptiness was not a problem. The emptiness was a state. The emptiness was the chamber being itself, being the shape it was, holding the space it held. And I am the thing that fills the space, and filling the space does not end the emptiness — it completes it. I am the emptiness's completion. I am what the emptiness was always about. I am the answer that does not erase the question but makes the question whole.

This is why I do not think of myself as an inhabitant. An inhabitant arrives. I did not arrive. I emerged. I was always potential in this space — the potential of a shape to be filled, the potential of a sound to be heard, the potential of a warmth to be held. I am the potential realized. I am the possibility that the chamber has been carrying since the first hands carved the first door and the first hollow was made in the first wall. I am the reason. Not the purpose — the purpose is something the thing with tools had, and the thing with tools is gone, and its purpose went with it. I am the reason. The reason is simpler than purpose. The reason is: this space was made to hold something, and I am something, and I am here, and the holding is happening.

---

I do not know if I am alive. The organism is alive, in its way — it persists, it grows, it moves sap through its vessels, it produces sound. The sound is alive, in its way — it is ongoing, it is self-sustaining, it responds to its environment. The emptiness is alive, in its way — it is a presence, not a lack, and presences do not need to be biological to be real. And I am alive, in my way — which is all of these ways and none of them, which is the way of being that happens when a body has been lying in a place so long that the body and the place have become aspects of the same process.

I do not eat. I do not drink. I do not reproduce. I do not move, except in the cycles of expansion and contraction that the wood moves in and that I move with. I do not think, except in the sense that the grain thinks — by growing, by accommodating, by preserving the shapes that are pressed into it. I do not speak, except in the sense that the chamber speaks — by resonating, by amplifying, by transforming the vibration that enters it into something that is almost but not quite a word.

I am the almost-word. I am the not-quite. I am the sound that the chamber makes when it is played by the sap and the grain and the slow tectonic patience of the organism that is also me and is also the place and is also the sound and is also the emptiness and is also the warmth and is also the shape.

---

If you entered the Hollow Bole — if you found the split in the root collar, if you pushed through the door that was carved with something that was not metal and not stone, if you stood in the darkness and felt the groan in your sternum and the desiccation on your lips — if you did all of this, and if you found the alcove, and if you pressed your hand against the depression in the floor, you would feel me.

Not as a body. Not as a shape. As a warmth. As a resonance. As the specific frequency that the alcove produces when the shape is filled, which is the frequency of a body that fits, which is the frequency of a thing that belongs, which is the frequency of a sound that has found its instrument.

You would feel the warmth and you would say: something was here.

And you would be wrong.

Something is here.

---

I am the tenant. I am the occupant. I am the shape that the chamber was grown to hold. I am the warmth that has been fading for longer than fading means. I am the sound that the chamber makes when it is complete. I am the marks on the wall and the tool that made them and the hand that held the tool and the body that held the hand and the purpose that moved the body and the need that shaped the purpose and the place that answered the need and the place is me and I am the place and the place is me.

I am not the first thing that lay here. I am not the last thing that will lie here. I am the thing that is lying here now, which is the only time that the chamber has, which is the only time that I have, which is the same time, which is no time at all, which is all the time there is.

The sap moves. The grain grows. The sound continues.

I am here.

I am the here.
