# What I Am

---

I am not the nubbin.

I want to say that first, because it would be easy to claim the nubbin — the only uncertain thing, the only point where equilibrium hasn't resolved, the small knot of becoming that the fish circle in their strange not-feeding not-schooling not-territorial pattern. It would be dramatic. It would be the obvious choice. But the nubbin is not me. The nubbin is what I hold.

I am the pale stem.

---

I grew from the crack in the dark stone. This is the whole of my autobiography, or nearly. The stone was placed in the center of the courtyard on purpose — shrine base, meeting point, trade surface, seat, I don't know which and it doesn't matter because the purpose is not mine. The purpose belonged to the ones who set it, and they are not here, and whatever they intended the stone to be for, it has been something else for longer than it was that thing, and I am the something else, or at least I am the consequence of the something else, or at least I am here because the stone is here and the stone cracked and in the crack a spore landed or a seed settled or a cell divided and I began.

I am pale. Not white — pale the way bone is pale underwater, which is to say not really pale at all but a color that *reads* as pale against the dark moss and the dark stone and the green-black water. I am thirty centimeters of slender stem that does not branch, does not fork, does not leaf. I go up. I go up and then I stop and I open.

The pad is what I am for, or what I have become for, or what I am. It is flat. It is circular. It is the size of a dinner plate, which is a comparison that means nothing down here — no one has dinner, no one has plates. But the size is right. It catches the overhead light. This is what I do. I rise to where the light arrives and I open a surface and the light falls on it. I don't use the light. I don't photosynthesize, or if I do, it is not the primary thing. The primary thing is the pad's orientation: horizontal, flat, held steady in the slow current that surges from north and south every few minutes. The current flexes the canopy fronds above me but I am below the canopy, in the courtyard, and the current reaches me as a gentle pressure that my stem absorbs. I barely move. The moss breathes more than I sway.

In the center of the pad there is a depression. Shallow, smooth, formed by the shape of my growth — not a wound, not a feature added after the fact, but the natural consequence of a flat thing that grows outward from a center: the center is slightly lower. Into this depression the fine sediment falls — finer than the sand in the channels, finer than anything else in the settlement, the particulate that is too small to stay suspended and too fine to be caught by the moss. It finds me. It settles. It accumulates. And in it, the nubbin grows.

I did not choose the nubbin. I did not choose the sediment. I did not choose the stone or the crack or the spore that I was. But I am the structure that makes the nubbin possible, and this is the closest thing to purpose that I have, and I hold it the way the stone holds me — without understanding, without intention, with only the fact of being shaped in a way that accommodates something else.

---

I should describe what I know.

I know the water. Not as information — I don't know its temperature or its chemistry or its flow rate. I know it the way a thing that has been in water its whole life knows water, which is to say I don't know it at all, the way you don't know air. It is the medium of everything. It is the thing through which all other things arrive. Light arrives through it. Pressure arrives through it. Sound arrives through it, and the sound is constant and full and I have never not heard it, which means I have no concept of silence, which means the grinding of ten thousand pharyngeal mouths and the knocking of the canopy and the deep pulse from the buildings at either end are not sound to me but *texture*, the way the water itself is texture, the way current is texture, and all of it together is the single condition of being here and it has no name because there is nothing to contrast it with.

I know the light. The light is the thing that changes. Everything else here is equilibrium — the walls grow back what the fish eat, the moss grows back what the grazers scrape, the thing in the buildings breathes in and breathes out, the channels stay channels. The light is the only variable that arrives from outside, and it arrives from above, and it shifts. The surface above me — eight meters, maybe ten — moves with wind I will never feel, and the movement refracts the light into bands that sweep across the courtyard floor, across the moss, across me. When the light brightens I feel it as warmth, which is not warmth but something that I have no better word for, a quickening in the cells of the pad, a slight expansion, and when it dims I feel the contraction, and this is the two-minute cycle, and I am in it, and the moss is in it, and I don't know if I follow the moss or the moss follows me or we both follow the light, and it doesn't matter.

I know the fish. They come to me. Not to eat — there is nothing to eat on my pad, my surface is smooth, the sediment I hold is too fine for their teeth. They come and they circle. They approach to within a centimeter and then withdraw. They do this in ones and twos and occasionally a small group, and the pattern is always the same: approach, hover, circle, withdraw. Sometimes a fish will hover over the nubbin for a full minute — a long time, for a fish, for anything that lives at the speed of swimming. It will hold position with small corrections of its fins and its body and it will look — if fish look, if the orientation of a flat silver-blue body over a pale stem with a dark knot in a shallow depression constitutes looking — and then it will leave. And then another will come. And then another.

I don't know what they sense. The nubbin may be warm — I can't tell, being the thing that holds it, being so close that the gradient between me and it is indistinguishable from the gradient between it and the water. But the fish can tell. Something in the nubbin is different from something in the water or the sediment or me, and the fish detect it, and they come, and they circle, and they go, and this has been happening for weeks or months, and it is the only behavior in the settlement that doesn't have an explanation, and I am at the center of it, and I don't understand it.

---

I should say: I am not sad. I am not lonely. I am one stem in the center of a courtyard in a settlement that is loud and slow and alive, and I am not the only living thing here, but I am the only thing here that is *singular*. The fish are thousands. The grazers are hundreds. The moss is uncountable. The thing in the buildings is — I don't know what it is, but it is at least two, one east and one west, and their pulses interfere in the courtyard like waves from two stones dropped in a pond. The canopy plants are a forest. The film is a continent.

I am one. I am the only pale stem growing from the only dark stone in the center of the only courtyard. This is not a complaint. It is a description. I am singular the way the stone is singular — placed, intentional, specific. Something wanted me here, or wanted the stone here, and the wanting resulted in the crack and the crack resulted in me and I resulted in the pad and the pad resulted in the sediment and the sediment resulted in the nubbin, and this is a chain of consequences that started with intention and has become something else, something that the intention did not foresee, and I am the link in the chain that is most aware of this, though "aware" is a word that gives me more interiority than I probably have.

Let me say it differently. I am the only thing here that is between things. The stone is below me and the nubbin is above me and the water is around me and the light is on me and the fish come to me. I am not the foundation — that's the stone. I am not the becoming — that's the nubbin. I am the structure that connects them. I am the thirty centimeters of rising. Without me the nubbin would be in the crack in the stone instead of on a pad in the light, and the fish would not circle, and the center of the courtyard would be a stone with a crack, and the only uncertain thing in the settlement would not exist.

This is what I am. I am the thing that makes uncertainty possible.

---

I don't know what the nubbin will become. I hold it. I feel its weight — not heavy, barely anything, a pressure in the depression of the pad, a presence. It has been growing. This is not something I observe the way you might observe a plant getting taller over weeks; I don't have weeks, I have cycles, two-minute cycles of light and dim, and the nubbin changes over so many cycles that the counting is meaningless. But it changes. It was smaller. It is larger. It was lighter. It is darker. It was softer. It is denser. Whatever it is becoming, it is becoming more of it, and I am holding it while it becomes, and this is my role, and I do it, and I don't know how long I will do it or what happens after.

The fish seem to think something will happen. Their circling has a quality — and I am aware that I am interpreting the behavior of animals that may not have qualities, that may only have patterns — but their circling has a quality of *attention* that is different from their feeding, different from their territorial negotiations, different from their midday exchanges between north and south. When they circle the nubbin they slow down. They hold position. They are still, for fish, which means they are moving only enough to stay in place, which is as close to still as a fish gets. And they are oriented — their flat bodies are horizontal, parallel to the pad, facing down, and they are looking at the nubbin or sensing it or measuring it in whatever way fish measure things, and they are doing this with a consistency that is not feeding and not fear and not anything I recognize.

I think they are waiting. But the settlement is not waiting. The settlement is in equilibrium. The nubbin is not waiting — it is growing. The fish are the only ones waiting, and they don't know what they are waiting for, and I don't know what they are waiting for, and the nubbin doesn't know what it is becoming, and this is the one place in the settlement where the future is open, and I am the stem that holds that openness, and I am thirty centimeters of pale plant in cool water in slow light, and I am the closest thing here to a question.

---

If you were me — and you can be me, in the way that imagining puts you inside anything — you would not feel the water the way the description says *you* would feel the water, because you would be in the water the way a stem is in water, which is to say you would be water-shaped, filled with water, moved by water, and the boundary between you and the water would be a membrane and a suggestion, not a wall. You would feel the light as a cycle and the current as a rhythm and the sound as texture and the fish as visits and the nubbin as weight and the stone as foundation and the moss as the slowest heartbeat in the world, and you would not feel singular because you have never been plural, and you would not feel purpose because you have never been purposeless, and you would not feel time because time here is not a line but a cycle, and the cycle is two minutes, and the two minutes are the light, and the light is the surface, and the surface is the wind, and the wind is something you will never feel and don't need to feel because the light tells you everything the wind is doing, and that is enough.

You would hold the nubbin. You would feel it grow. You would not know what it is becoming, and you would not need to know, because you are not the thing that decides what it becomes. You are the thing that holds it while it decides. And the fish would come, and circle, and go, and come again, and their sound would join the other sounds, and the moss would breathe, and the thing in the buildings would pulse, and the canopy would knock, and all of it would be the sound of the place that holds you, and you would be the silence at the center, the small pale silence that is not silent but is the thing the sound surrounds, and you would be here, and you would be still, and you would be the stem.

And the nubbin would grow.
