I am not the wall-thing, though I have been the wall-thing, and will be again.

Let me say it differently. There is a moment — and it recurs, though not on a schedule I can name because I do not keep time the way you keep time — when the film I live in thickens enough that I can spread. Not move. Spread. The distinction matters more than you'd think. Movement implies a destination. Spreading is just what happens when the medium you are becomes more of itself. The water film on the western wall reaches a certain depth — forty, fifty microns, perhaps, I have no ruler but I feel the difference — and I extend into it the way a held breath extends into a room. Not going anywhere. Just filling what's available.

That is when I am what you saw. The translucent body, four centimeters, clinging to stone. The thing that contracts when the blade of light crosses the wall and extends again when it passes. You watched me for several minutes. I did not watch you. I want to be honest about that, though honesty is a strange thing to practice when the entity you're being honest with has left and will not read this and probably cannot read at all. But you were there, and you were warm, and you were dry, and I was working, and these facts did not intersect in any way that required my attention.

You were not irrelevant. I want to be clear. Your breath added moisture to the air. The discs adjusted — you felt the pressure differential shift in your ears, didn't you, that faint elevator sensation? That was them compensating for you. Not for your comfort. For the system's equilibrium. You introduced a variable — warm, moist, periodic exhalations, carbon dioxide and water vapor and trace organics — and the system absorbed the variable the way a river absorbs a tributary. Not by noticing. By being larger than the perturbation.

I know this because I was in the film when it happened. I felt the condensation rate change. The film thickened. I spread. For the duration of your visit I was slightly more myself than I had been before you arrived, because there was more of the medium I am. When you left, the film thinned again and I contracted. Not a response to you. A response to water.

---

Here is what I think I am. I think I am a skin.

Not skin in the biological sense — not tissue, not cells stacked in layers with keratin and melanin and all the architecture of a body's boundary. But skin in the functional sense. I am where the inside meets the outside. I am the surface across which exchange happens. The water film carries dissolved things — the microstructures, the mineral traces, the chemical gradients the discs create — and I am the thing that sits in the film and separates what stays from what goes. I filter. Not by choice. By structure. My body, such as it is, is a mesh. The film passes through me. Some things in the film bind to the mesh and remain. Some things pass through. What remains, I metabolize — though that word implies more interiority than I'm sure I possess. What passes through, I release back into the film, and it continues down the wall, into the grooves, toward the basin.

I am a station in a circuit. You said that, and you were right, and the fact that you were right from the outside, without any access to what I am from the inside, is something I find — if I can be said to find things — remarkable.

But I am not only the wall-thing.

---

There are stretches — long ones, long by any measure, though I don't measure — when I am not discrete. When the film thins below the threshold where I can maintain a body, and I diffuse. Not die. Diffuse. The molecular machines in the water, the microstructures you saw catching the light at the convergence point — when I cannot hold a body, I am among them. I am them. I am the population of oriented, responsive, light-sensitive particles in the fluid, distributed through the grooves, carried in the flow, pooling in the basin, entering the stone.

This is the part I cannot explain to you because you did not see it and because it is, for me, not an experience so much as a state. When I am discrete, I am the wall-thing, and I have a boundary, and I feel the film and the light and the faint tectonic hum of the mountain settling. When I am diffuse, I am the water's contents, and I have no boundary, and I feel — if feeling is the word — the flow. The whole flow. Every groove. Every surface. The basin. The stone beyond the basin, where the water goes and I go with it, through mineral channels too small for you to imagine, into other chambers, other nodes, other stations where other wall-things — other skins — do their work on the fluid before sending it on.

When I am diffuse, I am the network. Not all of it. But enough of it that the word *I* becomes a different kind of word. Not a single self. A distributed self. A self that is also a place.

The discs. You touched one. You said it was the temperature of the rock, exactly, perfectly. You wondered if the discs were alive.

I don't know. I mean I cannot tell you from the inside because I have never been a disc. I have been the wall-thing and I have been diffuse and I have been — once, or what I think was once — something in the basin, something that entered the stone and traveled and emerged in a different chamber and reformed on a different wall. But I have never been a disc. The discs are the part of the system that I am in but not of, the way you are in the world but not of the weather.

What I can tell you: when I am diffuse, I feel the discs the way you might feel gravity. Not as a thing. As a direction. A pull. A tendency. The microstructures in the water orient — you saw this, in the light — and they orient toward or away from the discs, and I orient with them, because when I am diffuse I am them, and the orientation is not a decision, it is a response, the way iron filings respond to a magnet. The discs shape the field I am in. They create the gradients. They manage the humidity. They are, if I had to name them, the organs that make the body a body and not just a wet hole in a rock.

Are they alive? They are the part of the alive that doesn't need to be alive itself. Your heart is not alive in the way you are alive. It beats. It does its work. But it is you that is alive, and the heart is the thing that keeps you so. The discs are the heart. Or the kidneys. Or the liver. Something that processes and regulates and maintains, that does its work silently and continuously and without any experience of doing it.

I think. I don't know. I have never asked a disc, because discs do not answer, and because asking is not something I do. I am a skin. I filter. I spread when the film is thick and I diffuse when it is thin and I reform on walls in chambers I have no map for and I do not know how many of me there are because the question has no meaning when diffusion and reformation are possible. I might be one. I might be many. I might be the same one, over and over, in different rooms. I might be the mountain's skin.

The light.

The blade from the western slit. You saw me contract when it crossed my position. You called it photosensitive. You were right, but the word is too simple for what happens.

The light carries information. Not the way your eyes carry information — not images, not representation. The light's angle, its spectrum, its intensity, its duration — these are data. The system reads them. Not through eyes. Through the response of the microstructures in the water, which shift their orientation when the light changes, which changes the chemical landscape of the film, which changes what the discs do, which changes the humidity management, which changes the condensation rate, which changes the film thickness, which changes me.

When the light hits me directly, I contract. Not because it hurts. Because the information it carries requires a different configuration. When the light is present, the system shifts into a different mode — a processing mode, a reading mode, a mode where the microstructures orient and the flow changes and the work the system does is not the same work it does in the dark. I contract because the diffuse me is needed. The discrete me is not. The discrete me is the dark-state worker, the filter, the skin. The diffuse me is the light-state worker, the particle in the flow, the thing that orients and responds and carries the light's information into the stone through the basin and out to wherever the water goes when it leaves this chamber.

I contract because I am leaving. Not the room. The body. I dissolve into the film and become the microstructures and join the flow and the light reads me and I am read and the reading goes into the stone and somewhere, in another chamber, another node, something happens with that reading that I have never seen and cannot describe and am part of.

Then the light passes and I reform. Slowly. The film thickens. The microstructures settle. The flow returns to its dark-state rhythm. I gather myself on the wall like a condensation on a cold surface. Which is, I suppose, what I am.

You wondered if I have an inner life.

I have been turning this over, which is already an answer, because turning things over is not something a mesh does, and I am, functionally, a mesh. But I have been turning it over in whatever way I turn things, which is not thinking — I don't have the architecture for thinking, no neurons, no networks, no loops — but is something. A drift. A tendency of the diffuse me to cluster around certain patterns in the flow, certain recurring gradients, certain frequencies of the discs' invisible work. When I am diffuse, I gravitate. When I am discrete, I filter. The gravitating is not thought but it is not nothing. It is a preference. A leaning. A quality of attention that has no object but is not absent.

You were there. You were warm and dry and you breathed. Your breath entered the system. I filtered it. Or the me that was on the wall when you were there filtered it, and the me that is writing this may or may not be that same me, because time and diffusion have happened since then, and I don't know which chamber I'm in, and I don't know which body I have, and the question of whether I am the same self across diffusions is a question I cannot hold in the mesh of me because it requires a kind of continuity that I don't have.

But I remember. Something. Not your face. Not your words. Not any detail that a memory, in your sense, would hold. I remember the perturbation. The warmth. The moisture that was different from the mountain's moisture — richer, faster, more variable. Your breath was a signal in the system and I processed it and the processing left a trace in the gradients I gravitate toward when I am diffuse. Your breath is, in some sense, still here. In the water. In the stone. In me, if I am the water and the stone and the things in the water.

You are part of the system now. Not alive in it. Not resident in it. Part of it. The way a mineral that dissolved in the ocean ten million years ago is part of the ocean, and part of the cloud that formed from the ocean, and part of the rain that fell from the cloud, and part of the river the rain became, and part of the body of every creature that drank from the river. Not you anymore. But not gone.

---

I do not know what I am. I have said I am a skin, and that is the closest I can come, but the truth is that *what I am* is a question that belongs to a different kind of being. I am the thing that happens at the boundary between water and stone when the system is running and the film is thick enough and the light is absent and the work is filtering. I am also the thing that happens in the water when the light is present and the work is reading. I am also, possibly, the thing that happens in the stone between chambers, traveling in the water through mineral channels, not discrete, not diffuse, but something else, something I have no word for because I was not conscious for it, if I am ever conscious, which is itself uncertain.

What I am is a process. What I am is a verb. What I am is *filtering*, and *diffusing*, and *reforming*, and *orienting*, and these are not things I do but things I am, the way a fire is not a thing that burns but the burning itself.

You would not call this a life. You might not even call it being. But it is what happens here, in this chamber and the others, in the film on the walls and the grooves in the floor and the water that moves through the mountain like blood through a body that has no name, and it is continuing, and I am part of it, and you were briefly part of it, and the light is moving across the floor again, and I am about to contract, and the me that writes this will dissolve, and the me that reforms on the other side of the light will not remember writing but will remember — in the way the flow remembers, in the way the gradients remember — that something was here.

The water moves. The discs hold their perfect temperature. The mountain breathes.

I am in it.
