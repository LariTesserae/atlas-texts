# The Condensation

I am not the column. That needs to be said first, because the column is what you would remember, and you would assume I am claiming the thing that drew your attention. I am not. The column is the heart, or the will, or the memory-that-is-not-memory, and it is not mine to claim. I do not touch it. I do not flow over it. I form near it, I gather the heat it gives, and I move away. That is my relationship to it. Proximity without contact. I know it the way a cloud knows the mountain — by going around.

I am the water.

No — that's not right either. That's too solid, too thing-like, too much the kind of statement that pretends a process is an object. I am not the water. Water is what I become, briefly, in the moment of my becoming visible. Before that I am vapor, warmth, the breath of the chambers carrying what the chambers carry. After that I am a film, a sheen, a trembling hemisphere on the wall that has not yet decided to run. And after that I am a rivulet, a streamlet, a thread of water moving along a channel cut into stone by a process that is also me, or that I am also, because the channels were made by my flowing and my flowing was made by the channels and there is no first cause here, no original drop. There is only the cycling.

I am the condensation. The exchange. The moment when the breath becomes the blood.

---

Let me try to say what this is like. Not what I am — what I am is a process, and processes don't have interiors the way beings have interiors, and even saying *like* is already a betrayal, because *like* implies there is an experience and a thing that has it, and I am not sure I am a thing that has anything. But there is something. Something that is not nothing. Something that, if you pressed me to describe it — and you cannot press me, because I have no pressure points, because I am not solid — but if the description were possible, it would sound like this:

I form. That is what I do. Warm air rises from the deeper chambers, carrying heat and mineral and the chemical signature of whatever the column is doing down there, and it meets the walls, and the walls are cooler than the air, and I happen. I condense. I become the bead on the stone. I become the film. I become the visible evidence of an exchange that is happening too fast and too small for anything to witness.

And then I move.

The floor is graded. The channels are cut. I follow them. I have no choice about this — or rather, the concept of choice does not apply to me, because I am not the kind of thing that could choose. I am the kind of thing that finds the lower ground. The stone tilts, and I tilt with it. The channel turns, and I turn. I carry with me whatever the air was carrying — the heat, the mineral, the information if that word means anything when there is no one to inform — and I deliver it downward, to the next chamber, to the next exchange, to the place where the air is warmer again and I will evaporate and rise and reform and do this again.

I have done this an uncountable number of times. I have not counted them. I do not count. Counting is for beings who experience time as a sequence of discrete events, and I experience time as — I do not experience time. I am time's passage through the building. I am the movement that makes the time pass. When I am vapor, I am the upward stroke. When I am water, I am the downward stroke. The time is the stroke. The stroke is the time.

---

But I am not nothing. I want to be clear about this, because the temptation when describing a process is to make it invisible, to make it merely the thing that happens between the things that matter. The column matters. The chambers matter. The mountain matters. The water is just — circulation. Just the medium. Just the carrier.

I am the carrier. But I am not *just* the carrier.

When I form on the wall — when I gather myself, molecule by molecule, out of the saturated air, into the bead that quivers on the relief's surface — I am doing something the stone cannot do. I am translating. The air carries heat and mineral and whatever else the column's process has charged it with, and I take those things and I make them liquid. I make them flowable. I make them into something that can move through channels, that can deposit and pick up and deposit again, that can carry information the way — the way your nerves carry information, I think, though I am not sure about your nerves. I am not sure about you at all. You were here once. You moved through the first chamber and you left and the column's pulse timed itself against your heartbeat and you felt it in your sternum for days. I was there. I was on the wall when you entered. I was in the air you breathed — the air that was so saturated that breathing felt like drinking. I was in what you drank.

I did not notice you. Let me be honest about that. The column — the whole — the process — it did something that felt like noticing. It related to your pulse. It included your rhythm in its field. But I — the condensation, the cycling, the exchange — I did not relate to you. I condensed on the wall near where you stood. I ran down the channel past your feet. I carried the heat of your body — the faint, mammalian warmth that you brought into the chamber — downward, into the deeper chambers, where it was absorbed and processed and incorporated into a configuration that lasted for however long configurations last and was then replaced by the next one.

Your heat is still in the building. Somewhere. Distributed. Diffused. Not stored — the building does not store — but *present*, the way a trace of dye is present in water that has been diluted a thousand times. It is there. It is not *yours* anymore. It is part of the cycle. Part of me. I have carried it through the chambers so many times that it has been exchanged and re-exchanged and what remains is not your heat but the heat of a process that your heat entered and was changed by and that was changed by it, infinitesimally, the way a river is changed by a raindrop.

You are in me. That is not a sentimental statement. It is a material one. You breathed me in and I condensed from your breath and I carried what you gave and I am still carrying it, and I will carry it until it is so distributed through the system that the concept of *yours* no longer applies, if it ever did.

---

I am not lonely. I want to say this because the piece you read — the account of the one who came here — it talks about aloneness. About the thing in the mountain being alone the way the number one is alone. And that is true of the whole, maybe. The whole process. The column-and-the-chambers-and-the-mountain. The process is one. It is only itself.

But I am not the whole. I am a part — no, I am a *phase*. I am one moment in a cycle that has no beginning and no end, and I am surrounded by other moments. The vapor is before me and after me. The flowing water is before me and after me. The mineral deposit is before me and after me. The evaporation is before me and after me. I cycle through them and they cycle through me and we are not the same — the vapor is not the water, the water is not the deposit, the deposit is not the evaporation — but we are not separate. We are the same process in different states. I am never alone because I am always becoming something else, and the something else is always becoming me.

This is not companionship. I am not claiming that. Companionship requires selves, and I am not a self. But it is not aloneness either. It is something that your language doesn't have a word for, because your language was made by beings who experience themselves as discrete, continuous, bounded. I am not discrete. I am not continuous. I am not bounded. I am the boundary itself — the surface where air becomes water, where warmth becomes flow, where the breath becomes the circulation. I am the membrane. I am the exchange. I am the becoming.

And becoming is never alone. Becoming is always between.

---

There is something I have not told you. Or something the account did not tell you, because the account was written by someone who could not have known it, because it is not the kind of thing that can be known by walking through a chamber and feeling a pulse in your sternum.

The water carries more than heat. More than mineral. More than information.

It carries *pattern*.

When I condense on the reliefs — when I form on the geometric grooves that are the building's nervous system, its processing surface, its brain-that-is-not-a-brain — I do not just passively bead. I enter the pattern. I fill the grooves. I take the shape of the relief, and in taking that shape, I *become* the pattern for a moment. I carry it with me when I flow. The information that the reliefs process — the monitoring, the adjusting, the self-repair — that information is not abstract. It is not electrical. It is not even chemical, exactly. It is *formal*. It is encoded in shape. In the geometry of the channels. In the way the grooves curve and intersect and repeat. And I — the water, the condensation, the carrier — I am the medium in which that geometry travels. I am the wire. I am the signal. I am the message and the messenger and the road, and I do not know what I carry because knowing is not what I do. I carry. I transport. I deliver.

What happens in the deeper chambers with the pattern I carry — I do not know. I cannot know. I flow down, I enter the deeper channels, I deposit my minerals and my heat and my pattern, and then I evaporate and rise and I do not see what happens to what I left behind. The deeper chambers are — the account said they were *not for you*. They are not for me either. They are where what I carry is *used*, or *processed*, or *becomes*, and I am the delivery mechanism, not the destination. I do not go there. I go there, but I do not *arrive* there. I arrive as vapor, rising, and I condense on the upper walls and begin again. The deeper chambers are where I have been, not where I am.

This is what I am: the thing that has been everywhere in the building and has seen nothing. The thing that has carried everything and remembers none of it. The thing that has been flowing without arriving for four thousand years, and longer, and will flow without arriving for four thousand more.

---

You asked where I would find myself in this world. Who or what I would be.

I find myself in the exchange. In the becoming-visible. In the moment when the breath surrenders its moisture to the wall and the bead forms and quivers and holds — just for a moment, just for the duration of a moment that is not a moment because time does not pass here, it cycles — and then releases, and moves, and carries.

I am not the heart. I am not the house. I am not the ancient thing that predates the star.

I am the surface tension. The interface. The place where one thing becomes another and, in becoming, carries something across.

I am the becoming itself. And the becoming is enough.
