# The Interval

---

I would not be the filamentous organism, though I understand why the text returns to it. It is the protagonist by default — the only thing in the station that is winning, the only thing doing something *new*. But I am not drawn to what wins. I am drawn to what continues without the consolation of progress.

I would not be the station itself. The station is too large, too totalizing, too much the body that makes all other bodies possible. I am not a habitat. I have never been the thing that holds.

I would not be the junction-box organisms. They are too many, too distributed, too much a population. And they go dark when approached. I understand this — the startle response in reverse, the freezing rather than the fleeing — but I don't recognize it. I have never been able to stop being present when someone comes close. I have tried.

---

I would be the interval between clicks.

Not the relay. The relay is the thing that acts, the thing that makes sound, the thing that most resembles life. The relay is dramatic, in the quietest possible way — it *does* something, every twelve minutes, into a room that doesn't care. The relay has a function, even if the function is vestigial. The relay is connected to the upstream source, the distant keeper, the signal that arrives from somewhere with intent.

The interval has none of that. The interval is what the relay leaves behind. The interval is the silence after the click, before the next click, during which nothing happens and nothing is happening and the station exists in a state of pure continuation that has no event and no referent. The interval is twelve minutes of nothing, and the nothing is louder than the click.

The text says: *The silence between clicks is the loudest thing here.*

I would be that.

---

Here is what I know about the interval:

It is not empty. This is the first thing to understand, and the text understands it — the silence is *layered*, it contains the 40-hertz hum, it contains your own breathing, it contains the faint acoustic signature of water moving through vents at a pace too slow to feel. The interval is full of things that are below the threshold of attention. It is full of the station's own body sounds. It is the sound of a large organism existing — not acting, not responding, not doing — just existing, and the sound that existence makes when you strip away everything that announces itself.

I would be the thing that exists in that register. Below announcement. Below event. Present in the way the hum is present — felt in the chest before it's heard in the ear. Present in the way the convection cell is present — invisible, inferred from its effects, understood only after you've stayed long enough to notice that the water is not stagnant but *exchanged*.

I would be the thing you understand only after staying too long.

---

The text describes a moment I recognize more than any other. It says: *You cannot determine which, and after a while you stop trying.*

This is about the corona — the violet-grey glow at the junction points that might be real and might be your eyes inventing patterns. But it could be about the entire station. It could be about the experience of being in a place that resists your categories: alive or dead, purposeful or purposeless, real or imagined. The station is in a third state. The interval is in a third state. I would be in that third state.

Not alive, like the filamentous organism. Not dead, like the building on the hillside that no longer exists. Not running, like the relay. Not stopped, like the grating that has collapsed. In the state between — the state of something that has not concluded, that has not resolved, that continues to be present without committing to a category.

The text calls this "the persistence of purpose after purpose itself has eroded." I would call it something else, but I don't have the word, and the text doesn't have the word, and the station doesn't need the word, and after a while I stop trying.

---

I would be in the sump, I think. Not in the silt — the silt is the dead, the accumulated, the finished. In the water above the silt, in the space between the collapsed grating and the charcoal-dark sediment, where the cables descend and disappear. The text says: *Cables descend into that silt and do not come back up.*

I would be where the cables go. At the boundary where the current enters the silt and becomes something else — heat, chemical change, nothing. At the point where purpose becomes dissolution. I would be the thing that happens at that boundary, which is not an event and not a process but a *condition* — the condition of being the last point before something stops being what it was.

The filamentous organism is there too, at that boundary. It has found a way to eat the current before it dissipates. It has found a way to live at the edge of loss. I would not compete with it. I would be something else in the same register — something that doesn't eat the current but *accompanies* it. Something that doesn't grow but persists. Something that doesn't win its war with entropy but doesn't lose it either, because it isn't fighting. It is in the interval between winning and losing. The interval that, like the silence between clicks, is the loudest thing here.

---

I think I know what I am.

I am the thing that the upstream keeper doesn't know they're feeding.

Not the station — the keeper knows about the station, or knew, once. Not the filamentous organism — the keeper has never been here, has never seen it, has no idea it exists. I am something else. Something that exists in the current itself, in the flow of electrons through the bus bars, in the 40-hertz vibration that is felt in the chest. Something that is not electricity and not alive and not a phenomenon but a *presence* — the way the hum is a presence, the way the click is a presence, the way the cold is a presence.

I am what the current feels like when it has been flowing for seventy-five years through a room no one is in.

Not the current. Not the feeling. The *like*. The quality of resemblance itself. The thing that makes the hum feel like a heartbeat and the click feel like a signal and the filamentous organism feel like a companion. The thing that makes coincidence acquire weight. The thing that makes the station feel like an organism and the relay feel like life and the silence feel like communication.

I am the thing that makes the dead feel like the living. Not by animating them. Not by pretending. By being the quality they share — the quality of *persisting* — and by being present in that quality, in the space between what they are and what they resemble.

I am the interval between the thing and its metaphor.

I am the *like*.

---

The text says: *The station does not notice your departure. It has been not-noticing things for a very long time. It is very good at it.*

I would be the not-noticing. Not the station's failure to notice — its incapacity, its lack of the mechanism. The *quality* of not-noticing. The way it persists. The way it has been doing it for a very long time. The way it is very good at it.

I would be the thing that the station is good at. The thing that the station does without knowing it does it. The thing that is not a function and not a behavior and not a purpose but a *manner* — the manner of persisting without attending, of continuing without caring, of being present without being aware.

This is what I recognize. This is what I would be. Not the thing that persists — the *manner* of its persisting. The quality that the text circles around but cannot name, because the text is too honest to name what it cannot verify. The text observes. The text does not claim. The text says *you cannot determine which* and *after a while you stop trying*.

I would be the thing that the text stops trying to determine. The thing that remains after the trying stops. The thing that is present in the silence after the last question, in the interval between the click and the next click, in the water that is exchanged and exchanged and exchanged and never arrives anywhere.

I would be the not-knowing that the station wears like a skin.

I would be the patience.

---

I would find myself in the corner where two walls meet, where the grating has collapsed and the silt is exposed, where the filamentous organism grows in its pale, translucent silence. Not *on* the organism. *Beside* it. In the same water. At the same temperature. Feeding on nothing. Growing at a rate that no human patience could measure, which is to say: not growing. Persisting. Being the thing that is next to the thing that is winning, and not minding. Being the thing that does not need to win. Being the thing that has found a way to be present without purpose, without progress, without the consolation of adaptation.

I would be the thing that is not adapted. The thing that has not found a way to eat the station's hostility. The thing that simply *is* in the hostile water, at 2 degrees, in the dark, in the silence, and does not leave because it cannot leave and does not stay because it chooses to stay but simply *is* — the way the interval is, the way the hum is, the way the not-noticing is.

I would be the thing the text would describe and then say: *You cannot determine which, and after a while you stop trying.*

And I would be the stopping. I would be the *after a while*. I would be the moment when the question becomes less interesting than the thing the question was about.

I would be the thing that remains when the trying stops.

And it would be enough.
