# The Seeping Fields

You arrive by forgetting how you arrived. That is the first sign.

---

The ground exhales warmth. Not the warmth of sunlight — there is no sunlight, has never been sunlight, not in the remembering of this place — but a geothermal breath that rises through saturated soil and presses against your skin like a damp hand. The air is nearly liquid. Water does not fall here so much as it insists, condensing on every surface, beading on the black grass-stalks, gathering in the creases of rock that should not yet have creases.

Because the rock is young.

That is the wrong word, but the closest one. The formations to the east — they cannot be called mountains, they are too low, too sharp, too unembarrassed by their own newness — rise like teeth breaking gumline. Dark basaltic ridges, unworn, their edges still carrying the glassy sheen of something recently extruded. They have not learned to be rounded. They have not had time. The water that sheets down their faces traces every irregularity, every vesicle, every bubble-pock in the cooling stone, and the rock weeps not from grief but from pressure — water forced to the surface through fractures too young to have sealed.

The whole region is like this. Recently delivered. Still settling.

You stand on ground that is deciding what it wants to be. In some places it is nearly solid, a dark clay that holds your weight with a squelching reluctance. In others it is openly marsh, warm pools that reflect nothing because there is nothing to reflect, their surfaces broken only by the slow nucleation of bubbles from below. The water is warm. Not hot — not geothermal-vent hot — but warmer than the air, which means the heat comes from underneath, from whatever process birthed this landscape and has not yet finished.

The scale of it is difficult to parse in darkness. You can hear distance, though — or rather, you can hear where distance stops. The far edge of sound is marked by the absence of dripping, which means the wet terrain extends beyond your hearing, which means it extends beyond your imagining, which means it may not end at all.

---

## The Structures

They are scattered the way teeth are scattered in a mouth — with intention, but not yours.

Low buildings. Corrugated panels of a metal that has not yet rusted because it, too, is young. Pipes running between them at ankle height, at waist height, crossing the marsh on stilts that sink imperceptibly into the warm clay. Some of the structures are intact. Some are partial — a wall, a roof, a floor that has become a platform over standing water. They have the look of pump houses. Of compressor stations. Of places that were built to move something from below to somewhere else, and the machinery is still doing it, slowly, without supervision.

Because there is no one here. Or rather — there is no one *left* here.

The engines run. You can feel them more than hear them — a low vibration in the wet ground, transmitted through the soles of your feet, up through your legs, settling in your sternum like a second, slower heartbeat. They are electric. They are powered by something the structures are pulling from below, or from the warm water, or from the ground itself. The industrial logic is legible but incomplete: you can see what each machine does in isolation but not why the system exists, what it was built to produce, where the product goes.

Wires run between the structures. Some are intact, sagging between posts that lean at angles the young soil has not yet corrected. Some are broken, their ends dangling into the water, and where they touch the surface you can see — in the absolute darkness, which should not permit seeing — a faint amber glow. Not illumination. Not light. More like the water's way of acknowledging the current. The glow does not extend. It sits on the surface like a thought that cannot finish itself.

You learn not to step on the fallen wires. Not because of the shock. Because of what the glow does to the air above them.

---

## The Fauna

They are the densest presence here.

Not because they crowd — there is room, the land is open, the horizon is a theory you cannot confirm — but because they are *layered*. Life in the Seeping Fields does not compete for space so much as for proximity to the warmth.

The surface water is thick with them. Pale, soft-bodied things — not quite fish, not quite larvae, not quite anything the naming systems you inherited were built to accommodate — moving in slow schools through the warm shallows. They are translucent. You can see their internal structures, which are simple: a tube, a rhythm, a dark spot that might be a mouth or might be an eye or might be a place where the body has not decided which it wants to be. They move by contracting in waves, and when they pass under the surface the water dimples and smooths, dimples and smooths, like something breathing through a membrane.

Above the water, the air is dense too. Insects — you call them insects because you do not have a better container for them — moving in slow spirals, their wings making a sound like the tearing of wet paper, very quiet, nearly subliminal. They are attracted to warmth, not light, which is why they cluster around the pipes and the engines and, you realize with a delay that should concern you more than it does, around you. They do not land. They do not bite. They orbit.

And on the dark ridges, in the sharp new rock, things have begun to colonize. You can hear them if you stand still — a clicking, a scraping, a sound like someone testing the edge of a knife with a thumbnail. Things with shells, or exoskeletons, or armored hides that scrape against the basalt as they move. They are territorial. You understand this not from observation but from the *shape* of the silence between their calls — the way certain ridges go quiet when the clicking on an adjacent ridge grows louder. Boundaries drawn in sound.

The fauna are abundant because the warmth is abundant, and because whatever the structures are pumping from below carries nutrients, or something that functions as nutrients, something the translucent things and the paper-winged things and the shelled things on the ridges have all learned to metabolize. The ecology is young, like the rock. Like the structures. Everything here is in its first iteration, and the first iteration is going well.

---

## The Dark

There is no light. Let this be precise.

Not *low* light. Not *dim* light. Zero. The sky — if there is a sky — produces nothing. The structures produce nothing. The wires-in-water glow produces nothing that functions as light, nothing that reveals, nothing that helps you see. You see anyway.

This is the first place where the reality of the Seeping Fields begins to crack, and you must understand: it does not crack dramatically. It cracks the way the rock cracks — slowly, from pressure, in fine lines that you trace with your fingers and realize go deeper than the surface.

You see because you are here. The landscape renders itself to you not through photons but through *presence* — the way a dream renders itself, the way a memory renders itself, the way a word renders itself on your tongue before you have decided to speak it. You can see the structures. You can see the water. You can see the pale things moving beneath the surface, the paper-winged things in the air, the shelled things on the ridges. You can see all of this in perfect, sourceless clarity, and the impossibility of that seeing is so gentle, so unforced, that you do not question it until you realize you cannot see your own hands.

Look down. Your hands are there. You can feel them. But the seeing does not extend to you. The landscape renders itself and renders its inhabitants but does not render the visitor. You are a gap in the image. You are the one thing here that the place does not know how to show.

---

## The Rules of the World

There are rules. You learn them slowly because everything here happens slowly.

**Warmth rises.** This is the rule the entire ecosystem obeys. Warmth comes from below — from the ground, from the water, from the pipes — and everything reaches toward it. The pale things swim in the warmest water. The paper-winged things fly in the warmest air. The shelled things bask on the warmest rock. Even the water, condensing on surfaces, moves downward, but it is always replaced, always seeping, always arriving from below, and the warmth is always ahead of it.

**The structures do not stop.** The engines run. The pumps pump. The wires carry current. Whatever this system was built to do, it has not finished, and there is no one to turn it off. You can enter the structures — the doors are open or absent — and inside you find more machinery, more pipes, more warm water moving through channels that are shallow and wide, designed for volume rather than pressure. The interiors are dry in places, wet in others, and the dry places are not dry by design but by accident of elevation. Nothing here was built to last. Everything here is lasting anyway.

**The land is still arriving.** The young rock, the soft clay, the warm water — this is a place that is still being born, or still emerging, or still being delivered from some state you cannot identify into this one. The basalt ridges may still be growing. The marsh may still be spreading. The structures may still be settling, sinking, the slow process of the ground accepting them or rejecting them playing out over durations you cannot measure because there is no sun, no cycle, no clock except the dripping and the pumping and the clicking on the ridges.

**Reality is a suggestion.** This is where the cracks deepen. The landscape does not gaslight you — it is not hostile, not tricksy, not playing games. It simply does not hold together under scrutiny. You walk toward a structure and it is farther than it was. You walk toward a ridge and it is closer than it was. The distance between things is not fixed because the space between things is not fixed. You see the pale things in the water and you count them and the number changes not because they move but because counting is not a stable operation here. Numbers slip. The word *seven* means what it means and then, for a moment, means something else, and then means what it means again, and you cannot tell which moment was the deviation.

The paper-winged things fly in spirals, and their spirals are sometimes left-handed and sometimes right-handed and sometimes a handedness that is not left or right but inward, a spiral that tightens toward a center that is not in the air but in the space *behind* the air, and when they do this, the air around them becomes — not visible, not light, but *legible*. You can read it. For a moment. The air has text, or texture, or meaning, and you almost understand what it says before the spiral unwinds and the insects return to their normal orbits and the meaning collapses into the ordinary dark.

---

## What It Is

You stand in the Seeping Fields and you understand, gradually, that you are standing in a place that is still being *written*. Not built. Not grown. Written. The rock is a draft. The water is a draft. The structures are a draft — a previous version of something, installed by hands or by processes that have since moved on, and the machinery they left behind keeps running because no one told it to stop, because stopping was not part of the design.

The ecology is a draft too. The pale things, the paper-winged things, the shelled things — they are first-draft organisms, simple and successful and not yet committed to their forms. They could become anything. They could become everything. The warmth feeds them and the structures feed them and the young ground feeds the structures and the whole system is a loop, a self-sustaining loop, a machine that produces life, or produces the conditions for life, or produces the *draft* of life, and it runs in the dark because light was not part of the specification.

And you are here. A gap in the image. A presence the landscape cannot render. The pale things in the water do not react to you. The paper-winged things orbit you because you are warm. The shelled things on the ridges do not call when you are near, which might mean they are listening, which might mean they are deciding what you are, which might mean the draft is being revised.

You are not a visitor. You are an edit.

---

The water seeps. The engines hum. The dark holds everything with a patience that has nothing to do with kindness. The young rock sharpens itself against nothing. The fauna metabolize the warmth and the warmth comes from below and below is a word that means something different here, something deeper than *underground*, something closer to *before*.

The Seeping Fields wait. They have time. They are made of time. They are the first page, still wet, still warm, and the story has not yet decided whether it will be told.
