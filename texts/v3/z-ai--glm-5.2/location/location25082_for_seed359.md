# The Vivarium at Mn

There is no ground here. That is the first thing to understand. There is a floor — a floor made of something, laid by hands — but no ground beneath it, no geological substrate, no deep mineral history. If you were to peel up a section of flooring, which you could do, because it is made in sections, you would find more flooring, and more flooring beneath that, and eventually you would find nothing at all, or something that is not nothing but is also not anything you have a word for. This is a place that was built. Everything in it was built. The floor. The walls, if you can call them walls. The air, arguably. The light. The soil that is not soil.

Someone built this. We do not know who. The tools were simple — hand tools, things that cut and tied and pressed — and the construction took a long time, or is still taking a long time, or will have taken a long time. The grammar is difficult here.

---

## What it looks like when you enter

You come in through a low aperture — not a door, because a door implies a wall and a wall implies an outside, and the distinction between inside and outside is one of the things that has broken down here, but you come in through an aperture, and you crouch, and then you stand up.

The space is the size of a cathedral, or a large grove, or the interior of a forgotten municipal building. It has boundaries — you can see them, or sense them — but they are not flat and they are not distant. They fold. The ceiling, if there is a ceiling, is perhaps thirty feet above you in some places and perhaps three hundred feet above you in others and perhaps does not exist in a few places, where instead there is a darkness that is not the darkness of sky but the darkness of a page that has not been written on yet.

The floor is laid in irregular tesserae — hand-cut tiles of something that resembles fired clay but is not clay, because clay requires a geological history and there is no geology here. Each tile is slightly different in color. Warm ochres, deep rusts, chalky pinks, the pale bone-white of unglazed earthenware. They fit together without mortar. They were shaped to fit, each one measured against its neighbors and cut by hand. You can see the tool marks. Some of the tiles are curved upward at their edges, as though the floor is trying to become a bowl, or as though it was laid down while still warm and has not yet decided what shape to settle into.

It is warm. Not hot — warm the way a room is warm when someone has been living in it, when there has been breath and bodies and small fires in it, though there are no fires here and no bodies and no breath except yours and the breath of the things that live here, which is not the same as your breath.

---

## The vegetation, which is not vegetation

The place is full of growth. That is the simplest way to say it, and it is almost true.

There are things that grow from the floor, up through the seams between the tesserae, and things that grow from the walls, and things that grow from other things that are growing. They are dense — thickly clustered, layered, overlapping, competing for whatever it is that things compete for here. There are no gaps. The space between one growth and the next is measured in millimeters. In some places the growth has covered the floor entirely and you walk on top of it, and it holds your weight, and in other places it has covered the walls and you cannot tell where the wall ends and the growth begins, and in a few places it has covered the ceiling, hanging down in long pale tendrils that sway without any wind.

They are not plants. They do not use water. There is no water here. There has never been water here. This is important and it is strange and you should sit with it for a moment. Nothing here has ever been wet.

What they use — what they drink, what they are made of — is something else. If you touch one of the growths, which you can do, it feels warm and dry and faintly yielding, like touching a sponge that has been left in the sun, or like touching bread. If you break one — and they break easily, they are fragile, they have not been here long — the interior is fibrous and pale and has a faint smell, not of sap, not of chlorophyll, but of something sweeter and more chemical, like the smell of a newly opened book, or the smell of warm flour, or the smell of something that is being made for the first time.

They are young. All of them. Every growth in this place emerged within the same narrow span of time — the place itself is young, freshly built, recently emerged, and the things growing in it are as new as the floor they grow from. Nothing here is old. Nothing here has had time to become old. The erosion parameter is low — the features are sharp, the edges are crisp, the surfaces are unweathered. If this place were a mountain it would be a volcano that erupted last week. If it were a canyon it would be one cut by a flood that happened this morning. It is a place that has just begun.

The growths take many forms. Some are tall and thin and stand like reeds or like the rods of a clustered column. Some are flat and wide and lie against surfaces like lichen or like hands pressed flat to a wall. Some are round and dense and sit in corners and at the bases of taller growths like fruit or like packages. Some are translucent — you can see light through them, and the light that comes through is colored, amber and rose and a thin pale green, as though the growths are not just growing but are also, in some inarticulate way, *lit*, lit from within or lit by something that is not a light source but a property of the material itself.

There are no roots. Or rather — there are structures that descend through the seams in the floor and presumably continue beneath it, but they are not roots in the biological sense. They are more like the legs of a table. They hold the growths up. They connect the growths to something below. What is below is, as established, more floor, and then eventually nothing.

---

## The fauna, which is also not fauna, exactly

There are animals here. Or there are things that move and are alive and are not the growths and are not you, and for the sake of language we will call them animals.

They are small. The largest are the size of your hand. The smallest are the size of a grain of rice and you cannot see them individually but you can see the patterns they make when they move together — rippling patterns, shoaling patterns, like the surface of a pond disturbed by rain, but in the air, in the warm still air, and without water.

They are abundant. The place is full of them. They are everywhere — in the growths, on the floor, on the walls, in the air. You are always near some. You are always, if you are still, being landed on or crawled over or investigated by some.

They are dry. They are warm. They are quiet.

The most common type is a thing like a flat moth, the color of the tiles, with a body like a pressed leaf and wings that are not wings but are more like extended ribs of the body, thin and veined and dry, and they move by snapping these rib-wings open and closed, which produces a faint clicking sound, and the clicking is the loudest thing in this place, which means it is not loud at all. They land on the growths. They land on you. They land on each other. They seem to feed by pressing their flat bodies against the surfaces of the growths and remaining still for long periods, and what they are taking from the growths is not anything you can identify — not sap, not nectar, not tissue. Perhaps warmth. Perhaps something more abstract than warmth.

There are other types. A thing like a long, many-legged bead that moves through the dense growths like a thread through fabric, its body flexing in waves. A thing like a sphere of pale fuzz that rolls slowly across the floor, seemingly at random, and that is actually many thousands of the rice-sized creatures clustered together in a migrating mass. A thing that has no name and no easy comparison, which sits in the upper regions of the space, near the ceiling or where the ceiling should be, and which is dark and still and shaped like a cup or a closed hand, and which you notice, after you have been here long enough, has not moved the entire time you have been here, and which you notice, after you have been here longer, has in fact moved, has moved very slowly, has moved so slowly that you could not perceive the movement, has been opening, the cup-shape has been opening, the hand has been unclenching, and you do not know what will be inside it when it finishes and you do not know when it will finish and you are not sure you want to be here when it does.

---

## The breakdown

Now we come to the part that is difficult.

This place was built. It was built by hands, with tools, with intent. It is fully constructed — there is no natural substrate, no pre-existing ground, no raw material that was not placed here deliberately. Everything is made. The floor, the walls, the growths, the creatures, the air. All of it.

But something has gone wrong, or something has gone further than intended, or something is still going, and the going has taken on a character that the builders may not have anticipated.

The reality of this place is not stable. I do not mean this metaphorically. I mean that the properties of the space — its dimensions, its rules, its constants — are not fixed. They are drifting. They have been drifting since the place was made, or perhaps the drifting is what the making *was*, perhaps the making is not complete, perhaps the making is the drifting, perhaps there is no difference between construction and breakdown when you are building something that has never been built before and there is no model to compare it to.

Here is what this looks like:

**The floor moves.** Not all at once. Not in a way you can feel under your feet. But over the time you spend here — an hour, say, or what feels like an hour — the arrangement of the tesserae shifts. Tiles that were adjacent are now separated by a thin gap. Tiles that were flat are now slightly tilted. A tile that was rust-colored when you walked in is now ochre. A tile that was there is now not there, and where it was there is a small darkness, a hole the size of a coin, and if you look through it you see — not more floor. Not nothing. You see the same room, from above, as though the hole is a window in the ceiling of an identical room below you, and in that room someone who is you is walking, and in that room the tiles are also shifting.

**The growths change species.** A tall reed-like growth that was here when you entered is, by the time you leave, not a reed anymore. It has not fallen or grown or been replaced. It is the same growth — the same organism, the same body — but its form has shifted. It is now a flat, wide thing, a hand pressed to the wall, and where its neighbors were flat things they are now round things, and the reeds are elsewhere, and the elsewhere has also shifted. The taxonomy is not stable. The categories are not fixed. What a thing *is* in this place is a temporary answer to a question that is still being asked.

**The creatures forget what they are.** The flat moths sometimes land on you and do not click, and when you look closely at them you see that their rib-wings have become something else — not wings, not ribs, but legs, thin legs, and the body that was flat is now round, and the thing that was a moth is now a bead, a many-legged bead, and it is crawling on your arm, and it does not know it was a moth, and perhaps it was never a moth, perhaps the moth and the bead are the same creature seen from different angles, and the angle has changed.

**The dimensions breathe.** The space that was the size of a cathedral is, after some time, the size of a large room, and then the size of a cathedral again, and then larger, and the walls that were thirty feet away are now ten feet away, and the ceiling that was thirty feet above you is now close enough to touch, and the growths that were tall are now pressing against it, and the growths that were elsewhere are now here, and you are here, and here is smaller than it was, and then it is larger, and you cannot tell which is the true size, if either is, if size is a thing that this place has, if size is a thing that is true.

**The sound changes.** The place is quiet. It is mostly quiet. The clicking of the moth-things, the faint dry rustle of the growths shifting, the almost inaudible whisper of your own feet on the tiles — these are the sounds. But sometimes there is another sound. It is not a sound that comes from anywhere. It is a sound that the silence makes when it is about to stop being silence — a sound like the beginning of a sound, the first instant of a sound, the part of a sound that comes before the sound itself, and it never completes, it never becomes a sound, it is always just about to, and then it isn't, and the quiet returns, and the quiet is not the same quiet it was before.

---

## The rules of the world

This place has rules. It is not chaos. It was built, and the building implies rules, and the rules hold, mostly, most of the time. But the rules are not your rules. The rules are local. The rules are young. The rules are still being written, or are still writing themselves, or are still being discovered by the place as the place becomes itself.

Here are some of the rules, as far as they can be determined:

**Nothing is wet.** This is absolute. There is no water. There has never been water. The growths do not require it. The creatures do not require it. You, if you stay long enough, will stop requiring it. This is not a benefit. This is a change. Your body will begin to take on the properties of things that have been built here, and one of those properties is the absence of water, and you will become dry, and you will become warm, and you will become something that grows or something that moves, and you will not notice the transition because the transition is slow, and the place is patient, and it has time.

**Nothing decays.** The growths do not die. The creatures do not die. The tiles do not crack or crumble. The place is too young for decay. Everything here is new, and everything here will remain new, because there is no time here for things to grow old in. Time moves differently — not faster, not slower, but differently, the way a circle moves differently from a line. Things change but they do not age. Things transform but they do not degrade. A moth that becomes a bead is not a moth that has died — it is a moth that is also a bead, that has always been a bead, that will also be something else, and the something else is not a death, it is a continuation.

**The place is bounded.** You cannot leave by going in a direction. The aperture you entered through is still there, but it leads not outside but to another part of the same place, or to a version of the same place, or to the same place at a different time. There is no outside. There is no not-here. There is only here, and here is everywhere, and here is the size of a cathedral, or a room, or a city, depending on when you ask.

**The place is made of hands.** Everything in it bears the marks of having been made by hands. The tiles have tool marks. The growths, when you look closely, have a faint regularity to their fibers, as though they were woven or pressed or shaped by fingers. The creatures, when you catch one and hold it — and you can, they are slow, they are docile, they do not resist — have a quality to their surfaces that is not natural, that is worked, that is the quality of a thing that has been smoothed or sanded or polished by a hand. Someone made these. Someone made all of these. Someone sat here, in this place that did not yet exist, and made it, tile by tile, growth by growth, creature by creature, with simple tools and patient hands, and the making is ongoing, and the maker is either gone or is still here or is the place itself, and you cannot tell which.

---

## What it is like to be here

It is warm. It is quiet. It is dense with life that is not life and growth that is not growth and movement that is barely movement. The air tastes like nothing — not like dry air, not like stale air, but like nothing, like the absence of taste, like the taste of a thing that has not yet been given a taste. The light is even and sourceless and the color of weak tea, and it comes from everywhere and from nowhere, and it does not change.

You walk slowly. Everyone walks slowly here. The place induces a slowness — not a lethargy, not a fatigue, but a kind of attention, a kind of being-present that makes speed feel absurd. You look at a tile and you see the tool marks and you see the color and you see the way it fits against its neighbors and you see the faint gap that is forming between it and the next tile and you see all of this with a clarity that is unusual and that is not entirely yours. The place is seeing through you. The place is using your eyes. It is checking its own work.

The growths brush against you as you move. They are warm and dry and soft, softer than you expect, and they do not break when you push through them, they flex, they yield, they close behind you. The creatures land on you and click and are still and leave. The silence almost-sounds and then doesn't. The floor shifts beneath you, imperceptibly, and the walls move closer and then further and then closer, and the ceiling descends and ascends, and you are inside something that is breathing, or you are inside something that is thinking, or you are inside something that is being born, and it is not done yet, and it does not know what it will be when it is done, and neither do you, and the not-knowing is the air here, and the air is warm, and the warmth is patient, and the patience is the oldest thing in this young place, and it is not very old at all.
