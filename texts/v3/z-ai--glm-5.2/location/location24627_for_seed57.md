# The Plenum

You find yourself inside a stone's interior.

Not a large stone — something the size of a skull, maybe smaller. You know this not because you can see its boundaries but because the space *feels* contained the way a held breath feels contained. There is a ceiling, or the impression of one. The pressure above is patient and absolute. You are below something. You are within something. The distinction does not hold here long.

Water is everything and everywhere, but it does not move the way water moves. It hangs in columns that do not fall. It beads along surfaces that are not surfaces. You reach out and your hand enters wetness that enters your hand back — not drowning, not even cold exactly, but *informed*, as though the water is reading you the way you might read a page, turning through your fingers and knuckles and the small bones behind. The temperature hovers in that narrow band between comfort and the body's first complaint: cool enough that you notice your own warmth, that you become aware of yourself as a source of heat in a place that has none to spare. Around 12 degrees, maybe 10. The kind of cold that does not bite but that slowly, over hours, would find its way to the center of you.

The light is nearly absent. Not true darkness — there is a source somewhere, or perhaps a quality of the water itself that refuses to be fully opaque. A grey-blue phosphorescence that seems to come from below and from within the surfaces simultaneously, so faint that you only ever see it at the edge of vision, and when you turn toward it, it has relocated to the new periphery. It is the light of things about to become visible. It is the light of *almost*. After enough time in it, your eyes stop searching and start receiving, and the space assembles itself in coarse, approximate shapes: the sense of a chamber, organic and irregular, no more than a few meters across at its widest.

Because the space is small and the water is thick with presence, everything is packed. The walls are close and the close things are closer. Vegetation — though that word is a guess — covers nearly every interior surface in layers so dense they form their own topography. Pale filaments, tubular and translucent, grow in dense mats that overlap and interlace, each strand no wider than a hair but collectively forming masses with the density of felt. They are not rooted in anything you can identify. They emerge from the stone, or from the water, or from the boundary where the distinction between stone and water has softened into something negotiable. Some of them pulse very faintly — not with rhythm but with a slow, arrhythmic shimmer, the way a muscle twitches in sleep. They are cool to the touch and slightly yielding and they do not break when you press them. They bend and return. They have the tensile memory of things designed to endure current, though there is no current here.

Among the filaments, other growths. Flatter forms, leaflike but not leaves — more like the gills of a mushroom, arranged in vertical tiers that drip with a secretion which may simply be the same water, condensed and thickened. They are darker, these growths, almost burgundy where the faint light catches them at the right angle, and they cluster in the lower portions of the chamber where the water is densest. When you move your hand near them, they lean. Not toward, not away. They lean the way plants lean in time-lapse, except you can see it happening. They are responding to you at a speed that should belong to a different scale of being.

This is where the reality begins to come undone.

The vegetation is growing at the wrong tempo. Not fast, exactly — *synchronous with attention*. When you watch a strand directly, it holds still. When you look away and look back, it has lengthened. The growth happens in the gap. You catch it in the corner of your eye: a filament that was three centimeters is now four, and the new centimeter is slightly more transparent than the rest, as though it is still deciding whether to exist. You begin to wonder whether the chamber is growing with it, or whether the growth is the chamber — whether the vegetation is not *in* the space but *is* the space, building itself as it goes, and the stone you imagined as a container is actually the oldest part of the growth, petrified and forgotten by the living exterior.

Somewhere in the tangle, built forms. Not structures exactly — *marks*. Ridges in the stone that are too regular to be geological, small terraced steps no larger than a thumbnail, descending in a spiral that gives up after three quarters of a turn. A fragment of something that might have been a wall, now colonized so thoroughly by the growth that only its angle distinguishes it from the organic mass around it. A single square recess, perfectly cubic, two centimeters on each side, carved or formed into the chamber's upper surface, empty, dry inside despite the water — the only dry place in this environment, and therefore the most mysterious thing here. You cannot reach it. You can only perceive it from below, a small absence of moisture in a world of moisture, and it strikes something in you that feels like recognition, though you cannot say of what.

There are no animals. Or nearly none. Once, something moved in the periphery — a motion that traveled through the filaments the way a wave travels through wheat, a displacement that passed from left to right and was gone. It left a trail of slightly more intense phosphorescence that faded over several seconds. It may have been a creature. It may have been the space itself adjusting. There is no way to know. The fauna, if it exists, exists at the same scale as the vegetation's hidden growth: in the gap between observations, in the moments when you are not looking. This place operates by courtesy of attention. What you attend to, holds. What you neglect, changes.

The sound is minimal. Not silence — the silence here would be a presence, and what you have instead is an absence of the expectation of sound. The water does not lap. The growth does not rustle. There is, if you become very still and very quiet inside yourself, a low tone that seems to come from the stone itself, a vibration at the threshold of hearing, more felt than heard, a single sustained note that does not change pitch because it does not exist in time the way sound usually does. It is not ongoing. It is simply *there*, the way gravity is there, and you can attend to it or not but it will not stop because it was never started.

Time moves slowly here, or moves differently. You cannot tell how long you have been in the chamber. The cool water and the dim light and the packed, living surfaces conspire to dissolve the urgency of duration. The growth happens between moments. The light shifts in relation to gaze. The built marks suggest that something was here before the growth, something that arranged and shaped, but whatever it was has been absorbed the way a fallen tree is absorbed by moss — slowly, completely, without violence, until only the *shape* of the original thing remains as a faint discontinuity in the living surface. The civilization, if it was a civilization, was the size of a thumb. The chamber, if it is a chamber, is the size of a heart. The water, if it is water, is something that has learned to remember what it touches.

You are inside a stone that is inside a place that is inside a question about what constitutes an interior. The vegetation grows in the gaps. The light refuses to be found. The water reads you with the patience of something that has no appointment and no destination. The single dry cube in the ceiling waits, empty and perfect, for something that may have already been placed inside it and withdrawn, or may never arrive, or is arriving now, very slowly, at the pace of growth that happens only when no one is watching.

The chamber does not end. You reach a wall and the wall is soft with life and behind the life is more stone and behind the stone is more water and the water is continuous with the water you are in and the water you are in is continuous with your own body's water and the boundary between you and here is one of those distinctions that this place has been quietly dissolving since you arrived, one filament at a time, in the gaps between your attention, where everything that matters here is allowed to happen.
