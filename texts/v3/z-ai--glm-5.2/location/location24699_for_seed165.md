# The Still

The plateau begins where memory disagrees with itself.

You climb to it, of course — there is climbing involved, always, because the elevation is extreme in a way that doesn't just thin the air but thins the *sense* of air, until breathing becomes something you do more out of habit than need. The approach takes days by any reasonable accounting, but accounts here are not reasonable. People who have been there (there have been people) report the journey in numbers that don't match. Four days. Nine days. One woman said forty minutes, and her hair was white when she returned, and white when she left.

The plateau itself is vast. A valley inverted — a bowl pushed upward, or a plain lifted to where plains should not be. Three kilometers across at least, perhaps more, because the edges are where the dimness begins and the dimness does not invite measurement. The light here is the color of old pewter filtered through smoke that isn't there. It does not come from the sky, exactly. The sky is present but unreliable — a low ceiling of cloud or stone or something between the two, pale and featureless, giving light the way old bones give warmth: technically, minimally, without conviction. Shadows do not fall in any particular direction. They pool.

**The ground** is the first thing that breaks.

Not breaks violently. Breaks the way a long-settled fracture in granite is broken — the crack is old, the two sides have agreed to their separation, moss (there is no moss) has made peace with it. The stone underfoot is a dark, dense something, basalt-like but with a grain that runs in spirals if you look too long. It is worn smooth in the way that implies water, and indeed there is water — or the memory of water, or the intention of water. The surface is damp. Not wet. Damp. A condensation that appears without source, beading on the stone in fine droplets that never grow large enough to fall. They sit there. They have always been sitting there. If you press your palm to the stone, your skin comes away darkened, and cold, and smelling of nothing, which is itself a smell here — the smell of an absence so complete it has texture.

There are channels in the stone. Grooves worn by something that moved across it for a very long time. Water, possibly. Wind, possibly. The grooves are finger-width, shallow, and they run in patterns that seem deliberate until you follow them far enough to realize the deliberation is not something you can parse. Some spiral inward and terminate. Some run to the edge of the plateau and stop, as if the thing that made them reached the lip and chose not to continue. Some intersect and the intersection points are slightly deeper, slightly darker, and slightly warmer — though nothing here is warm. The warmth is relative. The warmth is a rumor.

---

**The sound** is the second thing.

It is always present. It does not come from anywhere. It does not vary. People who have stood on the plateau describe it differently, which is the first sign that something is wrong with it, because a sound that does not vary should be described the same way by everyone. It is not. One person says it is a low hum, like a transformer behind a wall. One says it is a high, thin sustained note, almost a whistle, almost a whine. One says it is not a tone at all but a pressure — a sound that is felt in the jaw and the sternum before the ears, a vibration that lives in bone. All of these people were there at the same time. All of them are correct.

What is consistent: the volume. It is loud. Not deafening — you can speak over it, though you will not want to, and when you do, your voice will sound wrong to you, thin and foreign, as if someone else were using your throat. The sound is loud the way weather is loud. It occupies space. It fills the plateau's bowl the way water fills a glass — from the bottom up, present at every altitude, denser at the center, where the ground's spiral-grain stone seems to generate or conduct or remember it.

The sound does not change. It has never changed. This is not a claim about observation. This is a structural property. The sound is one of the things that broke and then stopped breaking and now exists in its broken state permanently, the way a snapped bone that never set exists — functional in its wrongness, load-bearing in its deformity.

---

**The structures** — and there are structures — are the third thing.

There are three of them. Or five. Or two. The count depends on what you are willing to call a structure, and also on where you are standing when you count, and also on when.

The largest is a thing like a wall, or the memory of a wall. It stands at the plateau's eastern edge, though east is approximate here. It is perhaps four meters tall, perhaps six. It is made of the same dark stone as the ground, which suggests it was not brought here but grown, or left behind when something else eroded away. It has openings in it — not doors, not windows, just gaps, rectangular and regular, evenly spaced, with the precision of something designed by intelligence and the erosion of something that has stood for a span of time that should not be available to standing things. The top of the wall is smooth. Too smooth. The wind here is not strong enough to have done that. Nothing here is strong enough to have done that.

The second structure is lower, wider, a kind of platform or shelf jutting from the plateau's western rim. It has the character of a foundation — flat, squared, with holes where posts or pillars might have been. But the holes are the wrong shape. They are not round. They are not square. They are shaped like things for which there are no names, though you will try to find names for them while you stand there, and you will fail, and the failing will trouble you for longer than it should.

There may be a third. People report a third. It is described as small, low, a cairn or marker, somewhere near the center of the plateau. It is described as made of stones stacked with impossible care. It is described as warm to the touch. It is described, sometimes, as not being there at all, even when the person who describes it is standing where they say it stands. The ground at the center of the plateau is bare. The spiral-grain stone is most visible there. The sound is loudest there. The droplets of condensation are largest there, and they do not fall.

---

**The vegetation** is technically present.

It is a credit to the word *technically*. There are things growing on the plateau, and they are alive in the sense that they are not dead, and they are plant-like in the sense that they do not move and do not speak and persist where nothing should persist. But they are not plants as anyone would recognize them. They are dark. They are low. They cling to the stone in patches, particularly near the channels and the groove-intersections, and they have the appearance of lichen or moss or mold — except that they are too regular. Too geometric. They grow in patterns that mirror the spiral-grain of the stone beneath them, as if they are not growing *on* the stone but *out of* it, expressions of the stone rather than settlers upon it. They are damp to the touch. They are cold. They do not respond to pressure. You can press your thumb into them and they do not give. They are as hard as the stone. They may be the stone.

There is nothing green here. There is nothing that photosynthesizes in any way that implies sunlight is involved. The light is dim and sourceless and the things that grow here do not seem to need it. They exist in the same equilibrium the sound exists in — without gradient, without process, without the metabolism that living things use to distinguish themselves from mineral. If they are alive, they are alive the way a clock is alive: doing a thing, repeatedly, without reference to whether the doing is wanted.

---

**The fauna** are rare, and this rarity is itself a kind of presence.

You will not see them immediately. You may not see them at all. But you will hear evidence of them — not through the sound, which is constant and swallows small noises the way a river swallows rain, but through the *changes in your own perception* that their proximity causes. People who have been near them report a feeling of attention. Not being watched — that implies eyes, and the fauna here may not have eyes. But being *noticed*. Being registered by something that has a category for you and is filing you in it.

They are described, when described, as: low, moving along the ground, dark against the dark stone, fast when they want to be and still when they don't. One account describes something like a limb. Another describes a sound that was not the sound — a clicking, a wet sound, something with a rhythm to it. A third account, the most detailed, describes nothing at all, just a patch of stone where the spiral-grain ran the wrong way and the condensation-droplets were trembling, and the air above it was *aware*, and then it was not.

They are rare. There may be one. There may be several. There may be one thing that is also several things, the way a root system is one thing and many things. They do not approach. They do not retreat. They are present, and then they are not present, and the boundary between these states is not a boundary anyone has observed. It simply shifts.

---

**The breakdown of reality** is not an event. This is important. This is the thing that must be understood.

The plateau did not break. The plateau is not breaking. The plateau is *broken* — has been broken, is broken, will be broken, in a tense that does not exist in English but that the place itself occupies. Time here does not pass. Time here has *passed* — past tense, complete, a finished action. Everything that will happen here has happened. Everything that is happening here has already been happening. The sound has been sounding. The droplets have been sitting. The structures have been standing and not-standing. The fauna have been present and absent. The light has been dim. Nothing is in motion because motion requires a before and an after, and here, the before and the after are the same moment, extended, flattened, held.

This is why the sound does not change. This is why the droplets do not fall. This is why the condensation appears without source — because it did appear, and the appearing is done, and now it simply is. This is why the spiral-grain in the stone looks the way it does — because it was carved or grown or printed by something, and the carving is finished, and the result is what you see. This is why the structures are uncertain in number — not because they flicker in and out of existence, but because their existence is *fixed* in a state that includes ambiguity as a permanent feature. They are three and five and two, simultaneously, and have always been.

If you stand at the center of the plateau, where the sound is loudest, you can feel this. Not as a sensation but as a *fact* — a knowledge that arrives without evidence, without process, just *present in your mind* the way the condensation is present on the stone. The knowledge is: this place is done. It was made or it happened or it fell, and then it stopped, and it has been stopped, and the stopping is not death, is not stasis, is not preservation — it is *completion*. The plateau is a finished thing. It is a sentence that has been spoken. It is a sound that has been made and is still being made because the making is over but the sound remains, because here, those are not contradictory.

---

**The edges** of the plateau are where the dimness thickens. Not into darkness — into *less*. The air becomes less available. The light becomes less light. The ground becomes less ground. The structures at the edges (the wall, the platform) are the most solid things here, and they are at the margins, as if whatever finished this place finished the edges first and worked inward, and the center is the last place where the finishing is visible, where the completion is most complete, where the sound is loudest and the droplets are largest and the spiral-grain is deepest and the fauna are closest and the knowledge that this is *done* is most undeniable.

You can walk to the edge. You can look over. There is something below — there must be, you climbed up — but the below is not available to vision. It is dim. It is present the way the fauna are present. You can turn away from it. You can walk back. The plateau will be the same. It was always the same. You were always walking back across it. The sound was always playing. The stone was always damp.

You will leave. You have already left. The plateau is behind you, and it has been behind you, and it does not miss you, because missing requires a before and an after, and the plateau has neither. It has only the one moment, extended in every direction, loud and dim and cold and finished and still.
