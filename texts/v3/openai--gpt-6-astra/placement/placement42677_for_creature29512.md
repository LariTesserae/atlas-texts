I would be near a corner where the protected channel for the inhabitants’ connections turns sharply beneath a wall.

Not in the recess where someone stood holding the fibers clear. A little below it, in the thickened tissue that took their weight.

The recess has several shallow shelves, worn at different heights. What looks like a series of footholds was made by bodies bracing themselves in different ways. Underneath, the tissue grew dense along the lines of pressure. It still carries those lines. A burden placed there would be distributed toward three foundations, one of which no longer exists.

At first, that is where I would seem to end: at the place where the third foundation used to answer.

When the shell shifts, I tighten toward it. Nothing tightens back. The effort pulls a small flake from the underside of the passage. The flake falls into the old channel and stays there.

I have been making that grit for a long time.

I would not know that the foundation is gone. I would know that more of me is required in that direction than used to be.

There is enough material to reinforce the reaching fibers, but doing so would thin the floor above them. The floor contains the worn shelves. Its uppermost layers have stopped yielding, and the hardening has begun to descend into the tissue beneath.

For several softenings of the wax, I maintain the fibers.

Then I maintain the floor.

The difference might be too small to call a decision. Nevertheless, after it, I am differently prepared.

***

My house would be difficult to locate.

There is a room nearby with a feeding hollow, but only one narrow tract beneath its rim belongs to me. Most of the room responds to the bridge. I respond more strongly to a low transverse movement that comes through the corner when the shell catches against whatever holds it outside.

There are other parts of me beyond the wall. A strip under an adjoining court. Two short roots beneath a threshold. A thick seam around the base of a post that has been shortened by abrasion until it no longer supports anything.

Once, perhaps, I lived in the room and went out to stand at the corner. Or perhaps several inhabitants took turns there, and what I am is the part of that work they gradually left in place.

The old grafts would not settle it. Some pass through me. Others enclose tissue that answers with a different timing.

I could distinguish a weight placed on the lowest shelf from one placed on the next shelf up. I could still brace for either. But if I tried to find which inhabitant had preferred which position, there would be no separate preparation to follow. That distinction was not maintained.

What remains is unusually exact about a task no one performs.

The underside of the wall is abrasive. If a fiber were drawn around it, I would raise the floor just enough to lift the fiber clear.

There is no fiber.

Occasionally a tremor has the right sequence, and I begin.

***

The youngest sheet reaches me through the channel.

It is thinner than the connections the channel was cut to protect. It takes possession of the grit I have shed, advancing over particles worn from my own strained underside. By the time it touches living tissue, it has already been supplied with a little of me.

Its contact is broad and nearly without weight.

I do not lift.

The sheet thickens against a roughness in the wall. It extends beneath the lowest shelf and receives a vibration through me that has previously reached it only by passing around the foundations. Two paths now deliver the same disturbance, unequally altered.

Along one path, the sheet contracts. Along the other, it yields.

The resulting pull resembles the beginning of a burden being lowered into the recess.

I raise a ridge.

This costs the narrow tract beneath the feeding hollow several layers of cells. The hollow becomes fractionally deeper on that side. In the room above, the tissue listening to the bridge continues its own work.

The sheet grows over the ridge I have made.

For a time, I hold it away from the wall.

It does not pass.

***

There would be no immediate understanding between us.

My response puts a fold into the sheet. Its continued growth loads the fold. I reinforce what I have raised; it spreads around the reinforcement. Each of us makes the other’s next movement more expensive.

The corner thickens.

Elsewhere, the floor over my reaching fibers loses its last yielding layer.

A sound from the bridge runs through the new fold and comes back to me with a short delay. I brace beneath it. The shell’s deeper note follows another route through the sheet and loosens part of the same brace.

For the first time in a long while, an effort of mine is answered from a direction I have not been maintaining.

Not answered correctly. Not with the pull for which the old fibers are arranged.

But the tissue moves.

During the next long stoppage, the ridge remains raised. There is no wax soft enough to supply either growth or retreat. The sheet lies folded over me; I remain caught in the beginning of lifting it.

A roof breaks somewhere beyond the terraces.

The sound reaches us by our separate routes. It crosses the place where we meet, returns, crosses again. Some of those passages are weakened by the fold. Others are strengthened.

Nothing changes shape.

By the time growth resumes, there are differences in us that were not there when we stopped.

***

I would become difficult to separate from the delay at that corner.

Not the whole sheet, and not merely the old support beneath it. Some of the sheet would remain sensitive to sounds I scarcely receive. Some of my roots would go on tightening toward the absent foundation.

But between them, a narrow band would acquire a new habit.

When the familiar pressure comes, I begin to lift. The sheet draws against me. I pause before the ridge reaches its former height.

At first the pause lasts only until the wax advances another increment.

Later, it can outlast a vibration.

Material that would have gone into reinforcement remains available beneath the lowest shelf. A few cells there recover their capacity to bend. The abrasive underside of the wall is still abrasive. Nothing has been repaired.

Yet a connection could lie in the channel without being pinched by my useless effort.

This would be a small territory to inhabit: the tissue needed to begin a familiar action and the tissue needed to stop short of completing it.

It would extend as far as the next response, then shrink when the cold came.

I would have no way to know whether anything elsewhere in the hollow had become capable of the same pause.

***

Much later, a fragment settles into the corner.

It is too large for the sheet to enclose. One end lies in the channel; the other catches beneath the shelf. Every deep movement of the shell works it against the living fold.

I receive its weight.

The old sequence begins. Beneath the floor, fibers tighten in succession. The ridge presses upward, lifting the sheet and the fragment together.

Then the answering pull comes through the sheet.

I stop.

The fragment remains caught.

For a long interval there is only the city’s noise, passing through a burden neither of us has managed to release.

Then the wax moves. The sheet softens along one side of the fold. I give way there and hold beneath the other. The fragment turns by less than its own thickness.

A new edge touches us.

I would be there, finding how much to yield.
