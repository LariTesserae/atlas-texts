I think I would be lower than the workroom, but not among the buried floors. Somewhere the inhabitant passes without having to turn their head: behind the pipe in the stairwell, perhaps, where a piece of tile has come away and left a shallow space in the wall.

At first there would be very little to distinguish me from what had collected there.

A pale edge. A fold containing a few grains of rust. Something that withdraws when touched, but so slowly that the movement might belong to the water.

The recess would have two entrances. One would open onto the stairs; the other, scarcely wider than a seam, would communicate with the space around a valve housing. Warm water would come through that seam irregularly. Enough to bring food. Enough to give the recess a front and a back.

I would have lived there before it occurred to me to leave.

***

My body is broad when resting and surprisingly narrow when I move. There is no shell, but along my back are several hard plates, unevenly sized, which overlap when I pull myself into the wall. The oldest has a glazed blue surface. I acquired it from the stairwell when I was smaller.

Underneath, I am almost entirely a foot.

Its edges can grip separately. I can remain attached to the wall while lifting the middle of myself into an arch, allowing a worm or a thread of root to pass beneath. This is not invariably kindness. Some things are uncomfortable to sit on.

I feed where warm water meets cooler water. There is a film there, too slight to be seen except when I have removed part of it. My mouth works sideways beneath the leading edge of my body. Feeding takes a long time, and leaves a clean patch shaped rather like a thumbprint.

I have no useful view of the district.

Light reaches me as a difference between one side of a fold and another. A lamp coming on makes me tighten against the wall. A hand passing over me does much the same. What matters more is the pressure of water beneath my edges, and the particular tremor that travels through the mortar before the valve closes.

For a long time I know the inhabitant principally as an interruption in that tremor.

They put their feet on the bracket. Their weight changes how the housing rests against the wall. A minute later, the warmth arriving through my seam alters.

Sometimes it improves. Sometimes I must move.

This is the beginning of my opinion of them.

***

There is a place on the underside of the third stair where I go when the recess becomes too warm. Getting there requires crossing a strip of painted iron on which I cannot hold well. I draw my front edge across, fasten it to the mortar, then bring the rest of myself after it.

Once, while I am doing this, the inhabitant comes down.

Their hand closes around the rail. One foot finds the wall beside me. The wash from their coat lifts my rear edge from the paint.

I come away.

Nothing dramatic follows. I turn over twice in the water, too slowly to right myself by struggling, while the stairs move past in portions I do not recognize. The pale underside of a landing approaches.

Then a palm is beneath me.

I attach to it at once.

The skin has ridges that fit poorly against my foot, but it is warm, and it does not move away. I spread until I have found enough purchase.

The inhabitant waits.

After a while they put their hand against the wall beside my recess. I do not transfer immediately. Their hand is warmer than the wall.

They wait longer.

Eventually one of my edges finds the seam.

After that, they take a slightly different hold on the stairs.

***

I would not be their companion in any way they could readily name.

There would be cycles in which they did not notice me. There would be others in which they leaned close and touched the blue plate with the side of a nail. I would draw my soft edges inward. They would withdraw the nail.

We could continue this for years without either of us finding it inadequate.

But I would not always remain in the recess.

Beyond the third stair is a broken kitchen vent. Beyond that, a narrow channel crosses behind the tiles of the room where the food plates stand. The channel is interrupted by two holes, one of which must be crossed upside down.

I find it gradually.

The first journey takes so long that a new fringe grows along the edge of my oldest plate. I feed on the way. I remain for several cycles beneath a tile with a crack that admits a particularly agreeable warmth. When I reach the kitchen, I am broader than when I left.

The food on the ceramic plates is unlike the film in the stairwell.

I spend some time on the back of the rack before approaching it.

The inhabitant finds me on the cracked saucer.

Their fingers close around its rim. The saucer rises; the room shifts around me. I grip hard enough that the soft margin of my body turns nearly transparent.

They touch the place I have cleaned.

For a while nothing moves except the folds of their chest.

Then they set the saucer down on its own, away from the rack.

I remain there through the dark part of the cycle. When I leave, I take the route behind the tiles.

***

Afterward, a small ceramic fragment appears near my recess.

It is laid glazed side down on the stair, with the rough underside facing the water from the seam. At first I go around it. Its new edges interfere with a route I have used for a very long time.

A pale film develops on it.

I eat the film.

Later, when the inhabitant lifts the fragment to inspect it, I am underneath. They lower it carefully, leaving one end propped on a grain of broken mortar.

That would be where I found myself: beneath this small addition to the district, with room left for my body.

I would still make the difficult journey to the kitchen.

The fragment by the stair does not grow quite the same thing.
