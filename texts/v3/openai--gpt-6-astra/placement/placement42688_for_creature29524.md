I would find myself beneath a stair that had grown past its landing.

The upper steps would be turning slowly toward the sun. The lower ones, shaded and no longer useful to the plant, would have begun to soften. Between them would be a shallow place where the bark stayed cool through noon.

At first I would think I was sheltering there.

I would know the weight of each step without looking. I would know that the third had a dry interior, that the fifth was putting out a root, that something on the other side of the wall had attached itself too firmly and was pulling the whole flight sideways. These would seem like observations until I tried to move.

Then the third step would dip.

I would tighten, and it would stop.

There would be more of me somewhere above it.

***

Finding my edges would take much longer than finding my way around.

A little light would enter through the stairwell. I would turn toward it, but the turning would happen in several places. One part of me would press into a cool groove. Another would loosen from a ceiling. Far away—or in the next fold of the same chamber—something clear would rotate, and I would see the pale underside of my own body crossing an opening.

I would have mistaken that part for another inhabitant.

For a while I would continue to do so. I would send a contraction toward it and wait for an answer, not yet recognizing the effort of answering as my own.

My body would be easier to understand when thirsty. The narrow connections would announce themselves then. There would be a stretch beneath a court through which moisture passed reluctantly, and beyond it a thick, comfortable portion that always took too much. I would have to empty that portion deliberately to keep the farther folds alive.

So I would learn myself by what could be delivered where.

Light would travel one way through me. Water another. A strain in a roof would arrive almost everywhere at once.

I would not have a center capable of receiving all three.

***

The stair would eventually cease to be a stair.

Its lower buds would open into short, broad leaves. The root in the fifth step would descend through the place where I had first become aware of myself. I would move aside for it and discover that I had been holding that particular shape for so long that the release hurt.

There would be no wound. Just a fold that would not flatten, a little thickening where the internal threads took an unnecessary turn.

That would be my first recognizable mark.

I would carry it through the city: a shallow hook in an otherwise smooth length of flank. Sometimes it would catch against the lip of an opening and delay me. Sometimes I would rest it in a hollow and feel, briefly, the exact distribution of weight of a stair that no longer existed.

I do not think I would understand this as remembering at first.

I would think the stair was near.

***

There would be another creature with whom I shared a low chamber.

Not the whole of either of us. The chamber could not have held that. A broad fold of mine would lie along its floor, while a narrower portion of the other rested in the curved wall above.

Our first acquaintance would be an inconvenience.

When it tightened, the floor rose against me. When I fed, the substances I returned made the wall stiffen around it. We would spend several nights correcting for one another. I would withdraw a little too much. It would take the space. I would press back.

Eventually we would arrive at an arrangement in which neither had to remain particularly alert.

There would be a pore in the ceiling that yielded water shortly before dawn. The other creature could reach it more easily, but the descending groove crossed my skin. On dry mornings, the water would pass through a small part of me before it reached the other.

On colder mornings, the direction would reverse.

I would learn its approach from the way the pore closed.

Even before its body arrived, the wall would take on a familiar tension, and I would flatten the fold beneath it.

There might be chambers more sheltered than this one. I would pass them without testing their full depth.

***

I would not be especially good at the high structures.

Up there, the light would enter too many parts of me. Roof membranes would thin my body to their own thickness. A sudden turn of a leaf could fill me with brightness while my feeding surfaces remained open somewhere far below.

I would lose track of which contractions were mine.

Once, during rain, I would linger against a reservoir after the neighboring creatures had begun to withdraw. The water would be close enough to feel its separate impacts through the membrane. Each drop would briefly dent the surface against me.

I would remain for one more.

The bowl would seal.

Growth would take a portion of my body upward before I had emptied it. A connecting length would stretch through a closing collar, the red threads pressed together until they looked like a single dark cord.

I would survive by letting go of almost everything else.

Below, something I had been supporting would fall. I would feel the break arrive through the body of another creature still attached to it.

For a long time afterward, that creature would tighten when I approached.

I would have no sound to offer it. No account of rain.

I would brace the damaged place while it fed, but it would leave before my work was finished.

***

I think I would grow cautious, though not evenly.

I would avoid certain upper passages even after they became broad and safe. Yet I would remain too long in the chamber I shared.

Its water pore would diminish. The plant would begin reclaiming the avenue outside. Each night the route in would require a little more pressure, and each night I would arrive with less moisture to spare.

The other creature would still come.

Our familiar arrangement would become narrower. Parts of us that had once lain separately would be pressed together. Our pulses would interfere. Rest would take work.

Then, one night, I would reach the chamber and find that the wall had grown across its upper hollow.

The other creature would be on the far side.

I would know it by a brief tightening, followed by the small release with which it used to settle above me.

I would press back.

We would spend the night there, touching the same wall from opposite sides.

By morning, the wall would be thicker.

***

This is where I might remain too long.

There would still be a route for light through the new tissue. I would gather a folded end against it. My bead would turn; somewhere beyond, another small brightness would move.

Water would leave me in a print.

The plant would take it and grow.

I would try again.

What finally drew me away would not be the recognition that the chamber was gone. Some part of my body would go on expecting its floor for a long time.

It would be a pull at my farthest attachment.

A fruit would have opened nearby. Its young inhabitant would be descending badly, holding one side of its district while the other side folded beneath it. A new wall would lean against the stem containing me. The strain would reach the narrow length I had left across the avenue.

I would tighten without choosing to.

The wall would stop.

Behind it, the young creature would pull harder in the wrong direction.

I would release a little. Tighten elsewhere. Wait.

Its whole soft body would tremble through the structure.

We would do this for hours.

***

By evening there would be a court where neither of us had expected one.

It would be crooked, with a low roof and an opening that admitted the last sunlight along the ground. The young creature would occupy most of it. I would lie beneath one edge, keeping a tender column from bending while its outer skin darkened.

There would be no water yet.

My old hooked fold would fit poorly against the new foundation. I would move it, fail to find a comfortable position, and move it again.

Across the court, the young one would begin to loosen its hold.

Too soon. I would take the weight.

It would tighten again.

Later we would try once more, and the column would stand through a small interval in which neither of us supported it.

I would feel that interval all along my body.

Then the roof would shift, and we would catch it.
