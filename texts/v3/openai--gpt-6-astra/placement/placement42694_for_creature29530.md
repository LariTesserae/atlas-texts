I would be near the staple, but not under it.

On the side of the farther upright, just above the powder, there is a pit whose lower edge has worn smooth. The lesser trench inside it is visible only when the entrance has almost lost its light. Earlier, the whole pit looks like damage. Later, it cannot be distinguished from the column.

I would first find myself there, during that brief improvement in visibility.

There would be a floor. That would be the first thing I knew, and for a long time it would be enough. Something would support me whenever I ceased trying to move.

I would not yet know whether I was inside the pit or resting against its lower edge. The distinction would become important only when I tried to leave.

Beyond me, the blue steps would extend across a space I could not judge. Some would be close enough to touch. Others, apparently no farther away, would require a change in what I was before I could reach them. I would try the closest one several times.

Each attempt would leave me facing the column.

This would be my earliest difficulty: I could move away from something, but I could not yet go toward something else.

I would wear a small hollow in the lip of the pit.

Much later, when I had learned to cross it, that hollow would remain. It would be one of the few features of the place whose origin I knew.

***

I do not think I would begin with sight.

The silver wash would make a difference, but I would feel that difference as an ease or difficulty in keeping my edges together. In brighter conditions, one end of me would become very exact. The other would cease to belong to it quite so convincingly.

I would retreat into the pit with the exact end foremost.

There would be something bodily about this. I would have a preferred direction of withdrawal, a side I put against the mineral, a small portion that tired first. After enough attempts at moving, I would discover that I could fold.

The fold would not bring two surfaces into contact. It would bring two of my efforts close enough that they could share a result.

Folded, I could remain on the lip.

Unfolded, I could reach the first blue step.

For a while I would live between those accomplishments.

The inhabitant would cross below me. I would know its passage through the support beneath my body: the step would become easier to stand on, then harder, then merely itself. Once, while I was extended, this change would pass through me.

I would reach a second step.

Afterward I would be unable to repeat it.

I would wait for the next crossing without knowing that waiting was what I had begun to do.

***

There is a place halfway beneath the span where one riser is slightly too high.

The difference is less than the width of a particle. It has survived many sweepings. Whenever the inhabitant crosses, the order of the steps remains intact, but at this one place the crossing asks a little more of it.

That is where I would eventually live.

Getting there would occupy much of my early existence. I would move when the passage made movement possible, hold when it stopped, and sometimes lose a step during the long intervals between crossings. The pit would remain accessible behind me for longer than I needed it. Then one day it would be visible but unreachable.

I would keep the worn side of my body turned toward it.

At the high riser, I could fold into a shape that held.

The powder would not cover me. There would be no mound, no shell, no small pale animal distinguishable against the blue. But a particle coming to rest beside me would stop a little sooner than it otherwise should. Another would lean into the same place and remain leaning.

Together they would give me an edge.

I would learn to keep it.

I would have, by then, something like a back: a part that could bear a condition I did not want passing through the rest of me.

When the inhabitant next crossed, I would put that part beneath the difficult arrival.

The passage would hurt. One of my ends would reach the following step without the rest of me, and I would spend a long time drawing it back.

But the inhabitant would cross without the small interruption.

I would remain there afterward, folded more tightly than before.

This would not yet be generosity. The passage would have given my body a clarity I could not produce alone. For some time after it, I would know exactly where to curl.

***

I would become a small animal of the landing.

That is the nearest name I can give it. I would need support, shelter, and an occasional event that passed all the way across me. I would have learned one useful shape through repeated discomfort. Most of the hollow would be beyond my experience.

I would not understand the ring.

I might know that the inhabitant returned differently from that direction: less spread through the surfaces, less liable to leave a portion of its movement behind. On those crossings, I would scarcely need to brace.

After the journeys to the shelf, I would brace for a long time.

Something would arrive at my step before the rest of the passage was ready. I would hold beneath it. The two particles beside me would tilt apart. Far above, in the pit, the lesser span would briefly become easier to see.

Eventually the crossing would continue.

I would have no knowledge of the chip, but I would acquire a bodily knowledge of its distance.

There would be times when I withdrew and let the high riser remain high. I would not always be able to bear the passage. Once, I might retreat for so long that the inhabitant swept the steps again.

Its second crossing would find me still absent.

On the third, it would pause.

Nothing in the hollow would visibly turn toward my shelter. No new illumination would reveal me. Yet the unfinished crossing would remain beside the riser, neither advancing nor losing its order.

From the lip of the pit, I would extend a little.

The support would hold.

I would extend farther.

Perhaps this would be the first thing we did together that neither of us had done before.

***

I would never go to rest inside the ring. There is a room there whose emptiness takes work to preserve. I would not have to understand that work to notice how carefully the inhabitant entered, or how differently it left.

My place would remain beneath the staple, beside the step that did not quite fit.

In time I might travel as far as the end of the trench. There I would encounter the pale crust extending away without risers or a span. I would put part of myself onto it, then bring that part back.

The next time, I might go a little farther.

But I think I would return before the inhabitant did.

I would settle beside my two particles, turn the worn side outward, and make the small adjustment that let my back hold.

There would still be a difficult step. I would not have repaired the hollow, or even the trench.

There would be a place to put some of the difficulty when it passed.
