I would be just above the waterline, inside the join between the new ridge and the old buttress, where a thin sheet of the seam-dweller has begun to form around an injury.

Not the exposed lobes. Not, at first, the whole inhabitant.

I would be the place where a signal arrives and finds that the body it expects is missing.

There is still nourishment here. The buttress releases it reluctantly, in droplets too small to fall. There is still a little stored light, passed down through membranes whose upper branches I have never occupied. And there is the recurring instruction from farther along the seam: make room; the load is coming.

I make room.

Nothing comes.

Each time, I spend a little of the sweetness thickening an edge that faces an empty pocket. The edge curls inward. Water gathers in the curl. Transparent threads arrive and begin to feed there, so that the wasted growth becomes, eventually, useful to something.

That is how I acquire my first crowded surface.

***

I do not know the missing branch as a shape. I know it as the interval between tightening and release.

Somewhere in the older body, that interval was reliable. Weight moved, a chamber compressed, another chamber answered. The paired arrays were formed while those places lay against each other. Then growth separated them. A mountainside came between.

Now one of the places is gone.

Its absence has no flavor. Nothing bitter announces it. Nothing tears here when the distant tissue tears. There is only an instruction arriving in a body that can still obey it.

For a long time I obey.

What begins to change me is the water in my curled edge.

The little colonists living there build a membrane of their own. At first its signals are an irritation: tiny demands crossing the larger rhythm of the seam. They register the thin current arriving from above, the cooling of a droplet, metal washed from some newly opened seed case. They register things too small to move the chambers in my sheets.

When I contract, their pool deepens.

When I release, it drains.

They learn me as weather before I can distinguish them from sediment.

Then one of their branching threads enters a damaged rib. I close around it, but not quickly enough to digest it. Its membrane remains intact inside mine.

After that, there is a small difference between making room for weight and receiving weight. The difference persists after the water drains.

I grow around it.

***

I would not have a single place from which to feel this.

Part of me would be the chamber that still contracts at the old summons. Part would be the new strip that waits for pressure against its outer face. Between them, fluid would hesitate through a narrowing passage.

For several rains, that hesitation would injure me. One edge would pull while another remained soft. Feeding combs would lose their alignment. Food would pass through without being caught.

The old inhabitant might seal this patch away.

Instead, the young ridge rises enough to press against it.

For the first time, the empty pocket becomes narrow.

The summons comes from the missing branch. My old chambers close. My new membrane waits.

Then there is contact.

It is not the weight I was prepared for.

It arrives from below, with a slow sideways drag, carrying the faint chemical brightness of tissue recently divided. The ridge has grown into the space where I kept making allowance for something else.

I cannot leave. To withdraw would require growth through a region that is already closing.

I spread instead.

One margin releases lubricant. Another enters the rind and is burned back by its defensive sap. I close those mouths. A smaller lobe advances beside them, secreting less. The rind holds it for a while, tight enough to stop its circulation.

Then a pore opens against it.

Sweetness enters.

It is a very small acceptance. I spend almost all of it before the next pulse.

***

By the canopy turning, I have become a slanting strip through the body’s upper sheets.

There is no boundary one could peel apart to find me. Some of my chambers belonged to the old response; some were grown after the colonists entered. The colonists themselves have divided, mingled, disappeared into other membranes. Their descendants still occupy the curl, but their colors no longer separate cleanly in the droplets.

I receive changes from farther along the seam. Not all of them concern me. A lower sheet has been infected. Two feeding cords have entered flowering tissue and carry an unfamiliar abundance. Somewhere above, a branch is preparing grains.

The damaged array continues its work.

I have not destroyed it. Its contractions move fluid through a narrow portion of my body, maintaining a little space beside the buttress. I no longer thicken every surface it asks me to thicken.

Sometimes this is safe.

Once, it is nearly fatal.

The deep knots shift their distribution of weight. For a few moments, the new ridge leans in a direction no rain or growth has taught my outer membrane to expect. The old chambers close before the new ones respond.

Their inherited movement saves part of the sheet.

The rest stays open too long.

***

The tearing is brief. Its consequences occupy a season.

Water passes through a place that used to hold water. Feeding fluid leaks into the join. The glossy beads unfold their nets, and I tighten around them. Some hold the wound together. One begins to grow inward.

I cut off that portion by starving it.

Beyond the constriction, its membranes continue to answer mine for several days. Then the answers change. They acquire a rhythm I cannot supply.

The piece loosens.

I feel it leave through the drag of its last connecting thread. Afterward, each pulse of circulation reaches the sealed edge and turns back.

Above the wound, the surviving tissue grows more slowly. It leaves a wider passage between the chambers that close early and those that wait.

Water occupies the passage. Then feeding threads. Then a new sensory film.

***

This is where I would find myself: not at the center of the plateau’s awareness, if it has one, but in a handbreadth of difficult accommodation, with most of the world reaching me as altered pressure and food.

I would have old responses I could not simply discard. I would have new tissue whose sensitivity exceeded its understanding. My continued existence would depend on giving both somewhere to act without letting either pull the whole sheet apart.

And I would be growing beyond that description already.

At the upper edge, one of my lobes has reached a second join. Its sap is harsher. Its motions are quicker. The lobe has begun to divide into finer tips, each testing a different depth.

Below, the rain enters the repaired passage.

A chamber tightens. Another remains open.

Between them, a little water moves uphill.
