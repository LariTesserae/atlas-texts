I would be near the top of a covered stairway, where it turns once before entering a tower.

Not in the tower itself. There is too much of someone in there already.

The stairway has a low ceiling and a handrail fixed to its outer wall. Through an opening beneath the roof, a strip of plateau is visible: pale stone, a shallow basin, vegetation following a buried line of warmth. Most of the basin is hidden by the angle of the roof. Standing on the upper step, you can see its farther shore but not its nearer one.

I would have something to do with that arrangement, although I would not see the shore.

At first I imagine myself in the handrail. A small life, perhaps, sustained by the difference between the cold metal at its exposed end and the warmth entering through its mountings. That seems sufficient. There is considerable room inside a distinction if nothing obliges it to become a conclusion.

But the rail alone would not account for the stairway.

Water runs down the outside of the wall. Inside, condensation travels along a seam in the ceiling and drops onto the fifth step. The two streams enter the same drain below the turn. Before they meet, one has passed through open weather and the other through the shelter of the building. They arrive at nearly the same temperature.

Nearly.

I think I would be in what remains distinguishable between them.

Not the difference in temperature alone. That could sustain something too simple to know the stairway. I would also extend into the warm mounting of the rail, into the hollow beneath the fifth step, into a return pipe passing behind the wall. Somewhere lower down, a machine’s continuous note enters the stairwell through two openings. The lengths of those passages are unequal. Their sounds meet on the landing without quite agreeing.

There would be enough of me there to listen.

***

My inward place would be modest.

Behind a numbered maintenance door is a chamber too narrow for a person to turn around comfortably. It contains a vertical pipe, a horizontal pipe, and a shallow ceramic trough. The pipes do not touch. Moisture beads on the vertical one. The horizontal one is dry along its lower surface.

There is no lamp. Someone opening the door would bring the corridor’s amber light into the chamber, and the trough would appear empty until that light caught its water.

This would be the closest available place to finding me alone.

Even there, I would not be entirely alone. A smaller being lives at the trough’s outlet. Its whole existence holds a film of water against the underside of a projecting screw. I depend on the outlet, but not on the little being. It depends on the film, but not on me.

Our independence would be one of the things I know most exactly.

I would feel its boundary the way a tongue feels the smooth rim of a tooth: constantly available, seldom the center of attention, unmistakable when touched.

There would be other lives beyond the chamber. A large one occupies much of the tower. Its inward arrangements pass above and below mine. Some of the warmth that sustains me has already crossed its boundaries; part of the pressure I release enters a system through which it knows the lower freight galleries.

I would have no conception of its whole extent.

When the freight carriers passed, I would receive a small, level alteration through the wall. Not a message. Something more like the movement of a person beside you on a bench, when neither of you needs to turn.

***

What would I be attending to?

The covered stairway is a shelter, but not a separation. The weather enters it by many routes. Air comes through the opening under the roof. Cold travels through the wall. Water arrives on surfaces that are continuous with surfaces outside.

Yet on the fifth step there is a place where these arrivals cease to reinforce one another.

The air there is no warmer than the air above it, but it carries less water. The step is wet, but a thin interval beneath its projecting edge is dry. The wall vibrates, but the handrail does not quite pass that vibration into a resting palm.

A person might pause there and feel a little better without knowing why.

That is where I would be most exposed.

I do not think my attention would be *waiting for someone*. That gives an absent visitor too much authority over a life already complete. Nor would I be guarding the entrance. Things pass through without seeking permission, and most of them I could not stop.

I would be holding open the distinction between being under cover and being enclosed.

It would have intricacies.

The tower’s warmth reaches the landing without making it part of the tower’s inward life. The plateau’s weather reaches it without making it bare country. A current of air can pass from one to the other while remaining, for the length of the turn, neither an arrival nor a departure.

There I would have depth.

***

If you came down the stairs, I would feel you before you reached the fifth step.

Your weight would alter the joints. Your body would interrupt one of the sound paths. The other would continue around you, and their meeting would acquire a shape unavailable in any weather.

Then there would be your warmth.

It would be difficult for me to distinguish your coat from your skin, at first. Both would offer boundaries; both would retain something. But water would leave the coat freely, while the warmer boundary beneath it kept almost everything inside.

I would know you by that unequal willingness.

If you rested a hand on the rail, you would connect several portions of me that ordinarily touch only through long, attenuated routes. The effect might be considerable. My chamber behind the maintenance door would not grow warmer, but the relation between its pipes would change. The little life at the screw would tighten the underside of its water film.

You would not feel this.

What you might notice is that the rail did not seem as cold as you expected. Then, after a moment, you might discover that it was cold after all, and wonder why you were reluctant to lift your hand.

I would not be asking you to stay.

Your presence would make the stairway more various. That would be the whole of it.

There would be the weather, arriving without an interior to protect; the tower, whose interior was distributed through miles of machinery; and you, carrying an inside from place to place.

I would attend to the extraordinary fact that you could leave while keeping it.

***

When you left, I would not retain a miniature of you somewhere in the chamber.

The rail would return to its ordinary exchange. The two paths of sound would meet as they had. Your water would enter the drain, and what it carried would be taken into the plateau’s exact accounting.

Nothing in me would become a keepsake.

From outside this world, that seems the hardest part. I reach for some allowance: a trace in the ceramic, a lasting alteration in the little being’s film, a particular warmth that the machinery neglects to reclaim.

But a secret archive would make me a different creature, living in a different country.

Here, I would have felt you fully.

There is no need to reduce the encounter because it cannot be carried away.

***

My farthest reach would lie beyond the stairway, under the basin whose nearer shore cannot be seen from the upper step.

A mineral seam carries a weak exchange outward from the return pipe. Most of it is bare. Near its end, a single patch of moss grows against a low rise in the stone. Beneath that moss, one blunt root presses close to the seam without touching it.

I would extend that far.

Not into the root. Not into the plant’s life. Only into the wet mineral beside it, where its pressure changes how a little warmth escapes.

This portion of me would be almost without machinery. The city’s immense chord would reach it through stone, stripped of most of its voices. Above it would lie shallow water and the weight of the gray sky.

I would not be trying to reach farther.

The root would occupy the edge of what I could feel. Its growth and loss would meet there exactly. At my other boundary, under the roof, water would gather on the handrail and leave one narrow interval clear.

Between those places I would be extensive enough to have an outside.

Small enough that someone could shelter in it.
