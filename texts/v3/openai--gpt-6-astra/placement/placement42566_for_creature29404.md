At first I would choose the ledge that receives the light. There is something reassuring about a place from which the entrance can still be seen. But the ledge cools at night, and grit falls onto it whenever the wind gets under the flake. I would move farther in.

Not far. The heat would turn me back before the darkness did.

I would find a place beside a splinter, then discover that it was a passage: the beetle would come through, filling almost all of it, scraping dust from the walls with its back. Behind the splinter there would be another opening, too narrow for the beetle. That would seem better until I felt the warmth coming through its floor.

My place would have to be found with a body. From the entrance, none of these differences would be visible.

I would be the spider in the recess, though not quite as the spider looks from outside: an animal fitted neatly into a shape. From within, the fit would be uneven. One leg would reach farther than the others. A point of stone would keep my abdomen from settling lower. There would be room to turn, but only if I gathered the legs on one side first.

I would have arrived when the recess was a little larger.

Across its mouth I would lay very little silk. A line, then another at an angle. There would be no open space for a broad web, and few reliable surfaces. One promising grain would shift when I pulled against it. I would abandon that attachment and go farther along the wall, feeling until I reached stone that held.

The line would tighten.

Immediately the mountain would be in it.

I would not hear a station below the slope. I would have no slope extending that far, no buried casings, no machinery to account for what came through my feet. There would be movements that continued while nothing arrived, movements that changed without anything entering the passage. My body would respond to some of them. Others would continue beneath whatever I was doing.

Once, during a change of load, I would come halfway out.

Nothing.

Returning would take several small adjustments. A hind foot would miss its usual place and find another. I would remain with one leg resting on the silk.

There would be long intervals here that could not be described as waiting for a particular thing. My abdomen would grow smaller. Dust would adhere to a joint. I would draw a leg through my mouthparts and put it back against the wall. Farther in, a crystal would gain material along an edge.

Then something would touch the line and keep touching it differently.

I would move before its shape became clear.

A springtail, perhaps. Pale, caught badly rather than securely, with most of its body still free. Its jump would carry it against the roof. The silk would pull sideways. I would have to leave the protection of the recess to reach it, extending across the passage where my feet had no purchase except on the lines I had put there.

For a moment there would be no spare room at all.

Then I would be back inside, holding something that moved less and less. One strand would lie slack across the floor. I would not repair it immediately.

The meal would last. Afterward, its remains would occupy a corner I had previously used for a foot.

I would live among such consequences. Silk would collect dust. A husk would obstruct a turn. The wall would advance by an amount too small to make me leave, then by another such amount. Each time I settled, one leg would fold a little differently.

Outside, night would empty the ledge. Animals would come inward, passing closer to my recess than they had in the afternoon. Some would touch the web. Most would not. The beetle would put its intact antenna into the passage and withdraw it. For a while its body would block what little cooler air entered there.

I would stay still.

Eventually the beetle would move on. The line would ease. I would reach out and draw it toward myself, testing the attachment.

And one night, backing into the recess, I would find that I could no longer occupy it as before. I would try again, turning slightly. A pale new edge would press against my abdomen.

I would come forward carrying no understanding of how long the place had been closing.

There would still be a little silk attached to my feet. I would release it, touch the wall ahead, and begin looking for somewhere to put my body.
