I think I would be somewhere behind her head.

Not in her thoughts. In the passage behind the rear slab, where the stone keeps a little of the warmth she brings into the shelter. From there I would hear her turn over before I knew what she looked like. The bedding would scrape. Her breath would stop briefly with the effort of moving her knee. Then the long, irregular quiet would return, and I could pull my stem the rest of the way through.

I would be one of the small animals, though not necessarily the one she recognizes. The pale mark above a nose might distinguish us very well in sunlight and not at all beneath the leaves. I might be the animal she thinks has learned something, when the animal that learned it is elsewhere.

My fur would be gray near the skin, warmer at the tips. A seed caught behind my ear would bother me more persistently than her presence. I would have a narrow scar beneath it, hidden until the fur lay wet.

The shelf would not be small to me.

There would be places I seldom visited. The wet depression beside the lip might belong to a distance I crossed only when other animals were still, or when the stems near my own doorway had become too tough. The three green seams would not make an enclosure. They would be separate intricacies, connected by dangerous intervals.

I would know one of those intervals very well: the gravel between the shelter and a stone with a white line through it. There would be a hollow halfway across, no deeper than my body flattened, where I could stop without being entirely above the ground. From the shelter doorway the woman would probably see nothing there.

I would stop there even when there was no shadow overhead.

***

Her arrival would reach me through my feet.

Something heavy testing the approach. A pause. Another weight, put down differently. Before her smell came through the willow stems, before the pack scraped the threshold, I would have withdrawn beneath the rear slab.

I would wait longer than necessary.

Then there would be the sounds of familiar obstruction: the basket raised, the wrapper opened, the little impacts of things being arranged where she could reach them. Once those sounds settled, I would resume using the passage.

She would be tolerable in motion and startling when she spoke. Her voice, so restrained in the open air, would enter the stone behind her as a large, close vibration. If she laughed while I was carrying leaves, I would leave the leaves.

Later I would come back for them.

I do not think I would love her in a way she could confidently recognize. I would take fibers from her things. I would approach the warm place after she moved away. During a storm, when she lay inside all day, I might sleep nearer her than either of us would choose in good weather.

She might hear my teeth working and say something.

I would stop until she finished.

***

There would be a drying place I preferred beneath a thin projecting stone. Air passed through it, but falling water did not. I could spread stems there without taking them entirely out of cover.

For several days I would bring more than the place could hold.

The difficulty would not be obvious on the outward journey. A cut stem dragged easily when its leaves lay backward. Returning, I would discover that I had taken two stems forked around each other, or that one flower head had caught a third. I would pull harder before I tried anything else.

That seems important: I would often pull harder.

The woman might watch and find this funny. Sometimes pulling harder would work.

Under the stone, the cut leaves would change smell as they dried. I would turn them, carry some inside, bring others out again. My days would have tasks that weather could interrupt without making them cease to matter. A hawk could hold me motionless while a stem lay exposed a body-length away. Once the hawk passed, the stem would still be there. I would go and get it.

***

And then there would be a morning when the passage behind her bedding had become narrower.

Only a little. I could enter from one end and get my head through the other, but the stem would catch where it had not caught before.

I would back out.

Try from the other side.

Leave the stem and go through alone.

Perhaps she would notice the brushing behind her head had stopped. Perhaps she would be occupied with the drip beside her face, or with getting her foot into a cold boot. When she made her circuit, the change might be too far inside the recess for her antler to reach.

I would have no way to show her. My several attempts would already have brought grit down into the passage. I would lick it from my forefeet beneath a different stone.

For a while I would continue approaching the old entrance. There would be a turn my body began before I reached it, an expectation of darkness in a particular direction. Each time, the same narrowness.

Eventually I would carry things around the outside.

It would be a worse route. More open. A place where I had to climb over a sharp edge with my mouth full.

***

When she next came, she would find me crossing near the hearth.

She would stand still, her pack not yet off. I would stand still too.

Between us would be the bowl covering the ash, pale in the light. Beneath my chin, a leaf would move in the wind.

I would be waiting for her to put down the large thing on her back. She would be waiting for me to finish crossing.

After a while she would ease the pack to the ground.

I would drop the stem and run.

But not far. Under the white-veined stone I would turn, with room now to turn easily, and wait for a chance to retrieve it.
