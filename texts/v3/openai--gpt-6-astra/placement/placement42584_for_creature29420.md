At first I would mistake the creature’s movement for a change in the mountain.

There would be pressure, then its release. A warm surface would draw away from me. For a moment I would be stretched between what stayed and what went, and then I would be entirely on the thing that went.

This would happen in darkness.

I would not know about the four long limbs, or the shelf, or the stone placed near its edge. My part of the world would be a shallow groove beneath an overlapping plate, close to the root of the damaged hind leg. Dust would enter at one end. At the other, the groove would narrow until its opposing surfaces almost touched. Warmth would last longest there.

I would occupy less space than a crystal the creature could break beneath a foot.

At my outer edge, I would be drying.

Farther in, I would still be growing.

***

I think I would be one of the dark patches it carries, though “one” would become difficult to keep track of.

There would have been a recess beneath the enclosures. I would have extended along it for a long time, following a hairline warmth that weakened, returned, shifted slightly deeper into the stone. My shape would have recorded these changes. Broad where the heat lingered. Narrow where the seam went cold. Interrupted where a crystal grew through me.

Then the grooved pad would have come.

It would not feel like rescue. A margin would lift; the attachment beneath it would fail in little increments. Much of me would remain on the wall. Some would become loose particles, briefly caught in the pad’s grooves before the current took them.

The part carried away would contract around its remaining dampness.

Under the plate, it would find no rock.

It would find something that yielded very slightly, something with its own slow warmth and a surface already crossed by channels too small for the creature’s digits to clear. Mineral grains would lodge there. The creature would fold its leg, opening the groove; straighten it, nearly closing it. I would be pressed flat, then given room.

For a while, surviving would look very much like doing nothing.

A thin edge would attach.

Then another.

***

I would have no account of the journey between the recess and the shelter. I could give you instead the intervals when the limb above me rested.

There would be a long interval with warmth coming from below. During it, several kinds of dust would arrive together, embedded in the same bitter deposit. I would grow around one grain and stop at another. A little material that had been trapped against me would become part of me.

Later, the warmth would diminish. The groove would close almost completely.

The creature would be lying in the hollow.

Here, beneath its armor, the stillness would be imperfect. A load would pass from one side of the body to the other. The surface holding me would tighten. Somewhere just beyond my living edge, an old dry piece would break loose.

I would grow into the space it left.

This might be the nearest I came to sleep beside another being: the creature settling into a shape made for a body, while I settled farther into the shape of its joint. Neither fit would be exact. Each would leave something exposed.

When the knock came beneath the floor, I would receive it through the creature.

For an instant, every part of the groove would press against me.

Then the leg would extend, and the place where I lived would open to the cold.

***

I would not be harmless everywhere.

Where the plate rubbed against the tissue beneath it, I might keep a small quantity of abrasive dust from moving. Elsewhere, my growth might hold a grain in precisely the wrong place. An edge of armor might cease to close cleanly. The creature would reach under it with a hooked point and scrape.

Some of me would come away.

I would not distinguish that touch from the one that carried me out of the recess. Perhaps the creature would. Perhaps its forelimbs would work more gently around the branching patch than around the loose deposits. But even a careful hook would be large, and there would be no way to lift me whole.

I would persist deepest in the groove, where it could not quite reach without hurting itself.

Over time, the plate above me would rise a little farther from the body. New material would thicken along its amber edge. The gap I occupied would change. An attachment secure for many rests would become stretched each time the creature stepped.

I would begin growing in two directions.

One branch would follow the withdrawing plate.

The other would remain on the warm tissue.

Between them, a narrow connection would repeatedly draw taut.

Eventually it would break while the creature was doing something ordinary: reaching down from a ledge, perhaps, with three feet still secure.

There would be no pause in its movement.

***

On the fresh shelf, the short limbs would find a seam too fine for their hooked ends.

The creature would turn one pad and lay its grooved surface against the stone. Its body would lower. The leg above me would fold until the mouth of my little shelter faced the ground.

I would touch the mountain again.

Only a projecting lobe would make contact. Beneath it, the new rock would be too smooth to hold. The creature would shift, dragging that edge of me across a few grains. One would cut through the connection.

I would remain on the shelf when the leg lifted.

Or part of me would.

Above, the sheltered branch would travel on beneath its plate. Here, the severed piece would lie against stone that had never held growth. It would begin losing what the groove had kept.

There would be a crack nearby, but nearby would mean farther than I could grow before drying.

The creature’s pad would descend again.

I would be caught along with dust, moved a little, pressed down. The pressure would crush one edge. Another edge would enter the crack.

This would be enough, if the heat remained moderate.

It might not.

For a while I would be neither spreading nor dead.

***

I would find myself there: just below the clean surface, at the point where the crack turned and the upward current lost some of its force.

The creature could not follow me into it. Its hook could reach the entrance, no farther. When it withdrew the pad, a grain would remain wedged across the opening. That grain would shade most of me from the dry flow.

I would attach beneath it.

Above the shelf, the creature would still be testing for a place to settle. Each change of contact would send a faint strain through the crack. Some would reach me strongly; others would scarcely arrive.

Eventually there would be a long, steady pressure.

The warmth would rise.

A little of my edge would advance into a roughness in the wall.

I would have no need yet to take the shape of the branching film beneath the shelters. This place would ask for something narrower. I would descend along one face of the crack, fold around a crystal, stop where the gas struck directly. Behind that stopped edge, I would thicken.

If the creature returned, it might find only a black point.

If it returned much later, its pad might lift a flake from the point and carry it beneath the armor. I could enter a different groove, warmer or colder than the first. I could come to rest beside the part of me that had never left.

We might join.

We might grow past each other, separated by a ridge of dry tissue neither could cross.

For now, the creature would lift its weight from the shelf. The crack would relax almost imperceptibly. The grain above me would settle deeper, breaking off a small portion of my exposed edge.

Behind it, the rest would remain.

I would grow against the underside of that grain until I reached the stone again.
