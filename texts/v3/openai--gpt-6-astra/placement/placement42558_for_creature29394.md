At first I would look for a place inside the shelter.

Not the mat. The strip of ground between the mat and the leaning slab, where someone might put a thing they wanted to find again without keeping it in sight. There would be less light there, though not much less. The slab would be thin enough for its outer warmth to reach the inner face.

I imagine myself small: something with a flattened body and feet that can be mistaken for bits of stalk. A creature under a useful object, looking out.

But that is partly an attempt to arrive without being noticed. Even under the largest animal, among the smallest eyes, I would still be present in full. There is no obscurity here that would make me less accountable for the space I occupy.

And I do not think I would live alone.

The bowl keeps drawing my attention. Especially the little bridges of cord crossing its fracture. Someone has drilled the holes far enough from the broken edges that tightening the repair would not split them away. That required judgment. It also required holding the pieces together while the first loop was passed through.

The seated person could have done it unaided. There are ways: a bowl braced between the knees, a strip of hide underneath, a thumb inside against the seam.

Still, I find myself looking for the other pair of hands.

*

I would be around the side of the shelter, not quite within it.

From the path, one of the leaning slabs would hide my face. You would see a shoulder, then my elbow, then the side of one foot. The foot would be bare and broad across the toes. A narrow white scar would cross the instep, disappearing under the strap of a sandal whose sole rests against my ankle rather than beneath my heel.

It is an awkward way to wear a sandal. I would have reasons for it that had become too ordinary to explain.

My knees would be drawn up unevenly. Between them would lie a curved piece of hide with a hard, glossy fold along one edge. My hands would be held a short distance apart above it.

There would be nothing between my hands.

I hesitate at this. It is too easy to make those hands into a sign—readiness, assistance, some permanent offer. Seen from the right angle, almost any empty hand can be made to mean that.

Mine would not be arranged for you.

One thumb would press against the side of its forefinger. The other hand would be cupped, but loosely, its smallest finger bent farther inward than the rest. There would be a stain in one nail and a patch of thick skin on the heel of the palm. Whatever I had held would have had a particular weight. You could learn something about it from the spacing of my wrists, though not everything.

The bowl would be within reach, beside the needle.

I would know its weight better than its appearance.

*

I think I would have spent much of my life helping with things whose names belonged to other people.

Holding a hide while someone scraped it. Keeping three lengths of fiber separate while they were plaited. Pressing a splint against a leg. Supporting the heavy end of a slab while the person at the light end looked for a better place to put their fingers.

This would not have made me especially gentle.

There would have been occasions when I said, sharply, that I was already holding it. Occasions when I loosened my grip too soon because I thought I understood what was wanted. Once, perhaps, I laughed while someone was trying not to spill something, and the laughter passed through my arms.

The seated maker would remember that.

I would remember how long they continued to blame me for the crack.

Here, where breaking and repair lie so near one another, I would not be able to improve that history by telling it in the right order. The spilled contents, my laughter, their anger, the drilled holes would all retain their particulars.

The repair would be good. We would still disagree about the accident.

That matters to where I find myself. I do not want to become so well fitted to this country that I lose everything inconvenient about having known someone.

*

There would be an animal against my outer thigh.

At first you might take it for a rolled portion of my garment. Its back would be brown, with very short hair growing in opposed directions along a seam. One ear would be visible. The other would lie flat beneath the edge of the hide between my knees.

Its head would be too broad for the body you could see. Following its outline, you would discover that part of the apparent body was a foreleg folded close against the ribs. Following farther would not settle its shape much more.

I would call it by a name also used for a kind of fastening.

The maker would say that was not its name.

Neither of us would be trying to correct the other anymore, but the difference would remain available.

Its eye would be level with my wrist. A pale line would cross the iris without clouding it. The skin beneath its jaw would touch my leg through an opening in my garment, and that small area of contact would have a distinct warmth.

I would not know whether it had come to me or whether I had sat beside it.

Elsewhere, I might have cared about that. I might have liked to tell people that it chose me.

Here I would have the ear beneath the hide, the warmth against my leg, and the animal’s name as I knew it.

*

There is something else about my hands.

The right one would have a tremor.

It would not be trembling in a way you could watch. No repeated movement would disturb the fingers. Yet the tremor would be there, as plainly as the narrow hoof remains part of the distant animal’s stance.

You might notice it in the pressure of thumb against finger, in the way my wrist is supported against my knee. My body would have accommodated it without erasing it.

When we repaired the bowl, the maker would have taken the drilling from me. I would have held the pieces afterward.

I would have disliked the exchange for a while.

The cord lies in their palm now. My hands have their own position. Neither of us has been made into the person we might have preferred to be.

*

If you came around the slab, I think my face would be less remarkable than you expected.

A little wide at the cheekbones. One eyebrow sparse at the outer end. A mouth that looks doubtful even when I am satisfied. You might think I was younger than the maker until you noticed my neck.

My garment would have a row of pale seeds sewn low on the back, where I could not see them. Not quite straight. The last two would be closer together because the sewer had begun too far toward one side.

I would know who put them there.

That knowledge would not appear on my face for your benefit.

But if you lowered yourself into the available place, you would be able to see me beyond the maker’s shoulder. The three of us would not form a circle. The shelter would intervene; the animal would occupy part of the ground; you would have to incline your head a little to meet my eyes.

I would like that imperfect arrangement. It would leave us things to look at besides one another.

The bowl. A foot. The low ridge crowded with backs.

I would be there with my hands a bowl’s width apart, my difficult knee supported, the animal warm against my leg. Someone who had held things steady, though not always steadily enough.

From where you sat, the gap between my hands might seem empty.

From where I sat, I could see through it to a small gray rosette growing beside the threshold. One of its leaves would have a blunt, uneven end. Another would fit inside the curve of my finger without touching it.

I would know the whole plant was there, even when the bowl stood in the way.
