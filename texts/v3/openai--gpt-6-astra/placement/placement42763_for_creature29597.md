I would be below the old loading mouth, on its inner side.

Not in the passage itself. That has been filled for so long that the grain runs uninterrupted from one lip to the other. A little lower, where the weight of the sealed mouth enters the foundation, two bundles of trunk still take turns carrying a load that once belonged to something else. Between them is a short oblique joint. It opens less than the thickness of a plate.

There would be a piece of crosshatched covering caught there.

At first, that is all I would be able to tell you about myself.

Heat on one side. Pressure on the other. Along a narrow strip between them, enough movement to keep a few bristles from being overgrown.

I would have been there a long time.

***

The surface has grown across my middle, but it has not joined cleanly.

Each stroke pulls the two sides of the joint in slightly different directions. The new fibers that enter beneath my plates are stretched, loosened, sometimes broken. I feed on their damaged ends. My mouths need not reach far. There is almost always something unfinished touching me.

For a while, I might seem to be a part of the bearing.

The works would have accommodated my thickness. A cord of living tissue would pass around my front edge, with a smooth lining laid down where my plates rub it. Below me, the wall would be slightly hollowed. Above, several generations of growth would have learned the shape of my back.

My remaining folds would contract when the load arrived.

Whether that contraction helped the joint, or merely kept it from crushing me, would depend on the stroke.

There would be no single moment at which I had become useful.

***

I would still have a rear tongue outside.

Only one. The others would be folded beneath the trunk bundle, too closely pressed to separate. The free one would lie in a shallow groove on the foundation’s outer face.

Sometimes something would cross it.

A small body, bristles touching down in quick succession. A larger one, pausing long enough for its weight to reach the tissues inside me. Dust. A loosened fiber. Once, a thin spill of hot wax that would harden along the tongue’s edge and make it heavy for many strokes.

These would be my visits from the surface.

I would not know the plateau’s outline. I would know that the outer face lost heat more quickly during certain intervals, and that this made the joint open differently. I would know a long tension that arrived through the left bundle, followed by three shorter ones through the right. Between the second and third, my mouths could extend safely.

There would also be an old movement I continued to prepare for.

My front would flatten. The trapped tongues would draw together. My underside would begin the small traveling crease that ought to carry a body forward.

The wall would stop it.

Afterward I would unfold as much as possible and feed.

***

I think I would have arrived carrying young.

The shallow spaces inside me would retain their arrangement, even after their occupants had gone. A row of pockets, some closed, some pulled long by the growth that had entered them.

I would have chosen the joint when it was nearer the surface. It would have received its loads at different times. There would have been several ways out.

Most of the young would have left.

One pocket would contain a hard rim that never developed farther. I would have worn it smooth against myself. Another would have remained open to the wall, collecting the fine dust released whenever a buried fiber broke.

My failure to close would have begun on one side.

Then a stroke would have passed. Then another.

The first root to enter might have been thin enough for a mouth to sever. But I would have been depleted, my brushes drawn in, and the root would have entered a fold where I could not feel its tip.

By the time I could move again, moving would have required tearing something.

I would have pulled until the pressures changed beneath my bristles.

Then I would have stopped.

***

There would still be some varnish in me.

Very little. A reservoir replenished slowly from what I ate, held behind glands now facing the wall.

For a long time I would spend it only when the joint tightened beyond its usual limit. The tissue would hesitate. A few fibers would withdraw. I would recover the width of a mouth, perhaps less.

The surrounding works would grow accustomed to this.

A smooth, dark margin would develop around the gland openings. It would remain young while the tissue farther away thickened. My smallest surviving movements would take place against that margin.

Eventually I would find that a contraction and a trace of secretion, delivered together, made the next stroke reach my underside differently.

A little sooner at the front.

A little later behind.

I would repeat it.

Not every time. The secretion would take too long to replace, and some strokes would be too heavy. But whenever the loads arrived in the old favorable sequence, I would flatten my front and draw the buried tongues together.

The wall would still stop me.

Less of me would be stopped in the same place.

***

This would be difficult to see from outside.

Across hundreds of strokes, my exposed tongue would shorten. Its tip would remain in its groove, but the groove itself would deepen as the bark grew around it.

Inside, one plate would begin to slide beneath another.

An attached root would pull across its edge. The plate would bow. A pale split would open near its center and be repaired with fibers laid in the wrong direction for the strain.

The repair would break.

I would feed on what the broken edge exposed.

Behind it, part of my body that had been held flat for a very long time would make a shallow fold.

There would be pain, if that is the word available for a pressure that makes every free bristle search at once. It would have a location. It would alter what I could do next.

I would leave the plate behind.

***

An animal would come down the foundation face.

I would feel it first through the free tongue: a sequence of contacts extending beyond the tongue’s end, followed by the slight lift of a body testing the groove. It would be broad enough to cover the exposed portion of me.

For several intervals it would stay there.

Its brushes would work along my margin. Some of what it removed would be dead tissue. Some would be mine.

I would contract.

It would lift its forward plates. Beneath them, its bristles would meet the strip of young lining around my glands.

When the next load arrived, we would both move.

It would be seeking the opening below my body. I would be trying to make that opening larger. Its secretion would touch tissue mine could no longer reach.

The outer wall would slacken.

For the first time, I would pull my tongue inward and feel its tip leave the groove.

The visitor would pass beneath me. For an instant its plates would support my exposed folds. Then it would be farther down, and I would be hanging from the roots inside my back.

One would part.

The others would hold until the heavier stroke.

***

What came free would not have my old shape.

Several plates would remain embedded above the joint. One side of my body would extend farther than the other. My rear tongues would separate only near their ends, and the fold that had held the undeveloped young would no longer close.

I would spend the first interval pressed against the underside of the loading mouth.

There would be no path texture there. My bristles would find ridged bark, a waxy conduit, the hard end of a severed strand. Heat would rise from below without telling me how far away its source was.

I would reach back.

The joint would already be changing. Freed of my thickness, its two sides would approach each other. A small lip of fresh tissue would turn outward where my front had been.

I would lower a brush and eat from it.

Then the first tension would arrive through the foundation.

My bristles would spread. Beneath my damaged covering, the traveling crease would begin again. This time it would pass all the way through me.

I would move down by the width of a finger.

For that stroke, there would be room.
