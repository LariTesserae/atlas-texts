I would find myself lower than the drinking animal, in one of the passages that appears to end in leaves.

There would be a little space beneath the lowest leaf. From standing height it would be invisible. From where I was, it would contain a strip of stone, three fallen seeds, and the legs of whatever crossed beyond it. The warmth would have weight there. Damp earth would touch my back.

At first I would know very little about my own shape. I would know that the passage fitted me, and that turning around required going farther in.

Outside, something would put a foot on the leaf.

I would feel that before I saw it.

Then I would discover how completely I could stop.

*

I think I would be one of the land crabs, though not the large one she has learned to leave alone. My shell would still be small enough to pass beneath the terrace step. One claw would be heavier than the other. There would be a worn place on it, pale against the rest, from pressing against the same stone when I backed into my burrow.

My eyes would rise into the space above my body. My mouth would continue its small work even when there seemed to be nothing in it.

I would live below the fitted masonry, where the builders had put a small stone beneath a larger one to make it sit level. The small stone would have shifted. Behind it, earth would yield to a passage that bent twice and descended.

I would have made some of that passage. Water would have made some. A root would have determined the bend.

At the bottom there would be room enough to lower myself onto the earth without touching the walls on both sides. It would be cooler there, but not cold. The floor would remain damp after the mouth of the burrow had dried.

This would be the place I returned to.

I would not know there was a hillside over it.

*

My journeys would be short and liable to end early.

A leaf fallen close to the entrance might be sufficient for a night. I would pull it by an edge, stop when it caught, pull again. Sometimes the leaf would turn and come easily. Sometimes I would continue pulling long after another grip would have served me better.

There would be things I could move that I could not eat, and things I could eat that I could not move. My claws would discover the difference.

I would carry fragments below. Some would soften before I ate them. Some would remain in a corner, becoming part of the smell of the chamber.

When I dug, I would bring earth upward in small loads. The loose crumbs outside my door would be conspicuous to anyone who knew to look for them. I would sometimes push a leaf across the opening afterward, leaving the fresh earth uncovered beside it.

The hollow would be full of evidence I had made without intending to announce myself.

*

I would encounter the female at the bare stone.

Her arrival would come through her feet: a rapid sequence, then nothing. Under the leaf, I would hold a piece of fruit against my mouth.

For a while we would remain close enough that she could smell what I was eating.

Then her nose would appear.

I would lift the larger claw. The fruit would fall.

She would draw back, but not far. I would stay raised beyond the moment when raising myself was useful. Eventually she would move around me, quick across the stone, and I would lower myself over the fruit.

There would be no agreement between us. On another night she might reach the fruit first. On another night I might be too far from my hole to hold my place.

Her young would grow behind the wall above my passage. Their movement would sometimes reach me through the earth. I would stop feeding until it ceased.

*

Rain would alter what counted as shelter.

The first water might arrive along the root, well before the ceiling began to drip. I would meet it below, in the chamber I had chosen for its dampness.

At first I would press into a higher corner.

When water covered the floor, I would climb.

This would be awkward. A burrow easy to descend backward would offer poor holds to an animal trying to leave in haste. My legs would push clay from the walls. At the upper bend, I would have to settle back once before finding purchase beneath the small foundation stone.

Outside, the passage would smell newly opened.

I would spend that interval under the terrace step, close to the female’s route. Each time she passed, I would fold myself farther into the angle. Once her tail would touch my shell. She would turn sharply, and I would raise my claw against the underside of the stone.

Neither of us would have much room.

By the next night the water might have gone. I would enter my hole slowly, finding its floor higher with silt, a stored leaf pressed against the bend.

I would begin carrying earth out again.

*

There would come a time when I stopped carrying anything.

For several nights I would feed less. I would linger at the entrance without emerging, then retreat before the air outside had cooled. Finally I would push soil into the mouth of the passage from within.

Below, my body would loosen inside its shell.

The old casing would open. Drawing free of it would occupy the whole chamber and all the strength I had. My legs would come out soft. The large claw would no longer be something I could depend on.

I would remain beside the shape I had left.

Above me, the female might cross the bare stone. A seed might fall through a roof opening and be taken before morning. Water might gather at the seam, reflecting the sky in drops I had never seen.

Nothing would pause while I was unable to defend myself.

For a while even the wall of my chamber would be hard enough to injure me. I would keep still against the damp floor. Gradually the joints would bear weight again.

Later I would eat what I could of the old shell.

Later still, I would open the passage.

At the upper bend, the familiar stone would catch against my back. I would withdraw, turn a little, and try again. Then I would set my smaller claw into the earth beside it and begin to widen the way out.
