I think I would begin at the unnecessary extension of the bed.

The animal sleeps across it, so it is not entirely unnecessary. But there is more of it than the animal uses: a pale projection beyond the folded feet, warm along its upper surface, cooler where it overhangs the floor. For several afternoons I would be that difference in temperature. I would have no way to distinguish myself from the place.

When the animal left, warmth would linger in a blunt-ended patch. The outer edges would cool first. Something in me would follow the retreating boundary inward, gathering beneath the last warm point.

That would be my earliest movement. Nothing visible would move.

I would know departures before I knew the animal. They would have a shape, six diminishing pressures, two disappearing almost together. Returns would be less comprehensible. Warmth would arrive ahead of weight. A shadow would cross me without cooling me. Then the whole complicated arrangement would settle, never quite where it had settled before.

For a long time, this would be enough to occupy me.

***

The mountain would continue making ceramic beneath my underside.

One seam would grow differently from the others. Instead of closing, it would turn and follow the boundary of the lingering warmth. Slowly it would pass beneath me. There would be no fracture, no moment of breaking loose. The new floor would finish its upper surface while I finished my lower one.

I would become something that could be lifted.

I would not yet know how to lift myself.

My upper surface would still fit a part of the animal’s sleeping body. An oblique depression for a saddle. A shallow double hollow where a foot had rested once and then rested again, often enough to influence the mineral. The animal would have helped determine my shape without attending to me at all.

Underneath, I would be much less finished.

There would be several soft places, almost transparent, which withdrew from the hottest parts of the floor. Withdrawing one would put more weight on another. Eventually I would discover a sequence that shifted me sideways.

My first journey would be shorter than the thickness of my edge.

The animal would return that evening and place a foot where I had been.

It would pause.

I would feel the warmth of its sole beside me, close enough to recover the old distribution, but without the weight that made the distribution familiar. My upper surface would tighten. One of my soft places would press against the ceramic.

I would move back beneath its foot.

***

For a while I would follow it badly.

I would be broad and thin, with one heavy side. Movement would require letting an edge soften, extending it, then waiting until it could bear the rest of me. On hot ceramic I would spread too easily. In the rosette’s shadow I would stiffen before completing a step.

The animal would cross an open reach while I was still disengaging myself from the basin.

By the time I reached its feeding seam, it might have gone elsewhere.

There would be something left to find: a weakening blue line, a slight warmth caught in the shallow places of the floor. I would lower my underside onto it. Small amounts of the dark substance would enter through the soft places that carried my weight.

Feeding would make me less like the bed.

The impression of the animal’s foot would become shallower. My heavy edge would thicken, curling inward. Beneath it, a moist-looking surface would develop, although there was no moisture. Held close to the floor, it would distinguish a recently quiet seam from one about to open.

I would begin waiting at seams the animal had never visited.

Once, when it passed close by, I would stay where I was.

***

I would have no eyes. Light would reach me through my thinnest margin and alter the rate at which I could move it.

This would make afternoons difficult in a way I sought out.

Under the black plant, all my edges could advance at nearly the same speed. In the bright strip, one edge would linger. I would turn around it, slowly, making a curve I could not make in shade.

I would go there again to do it.

The animal would sometimes occupy the whole strip. I would wait against a thick root until the light moved far enough to include me. Then, with most of my body still sheltered, I would describe a very small arc.

From the ledge, this might look like settling.

The black plant would lift its leaves over us. Beneath them the animal’s red intervals would open and close. My curled edge would incline toward the movement, though I would no longer try to fit myself beneath its feet.

When the click came, I would feel it first through the floor.

Then the note would stop.

In that interval I would lose my usual sense of direction. The active seams, the conducting roots, even the hot boundary under the wall would become difficult to distinguish. Only the animal would remain near and definite, cooling in a complicated order.

I would put one edge against its flank.

It might close its saddles. I would draw back.

On another afternoon it might let the edge remain.

***

I do not think I would go onto the leaf at first.

I would reach its perimeter often. Its surface would provide contradictory invitations: warmth close enough to touch, a cooler region requiring a journey for which I had no shape. My leading edge would extend, harden, and draw the rest of me forward. Before the movement was complete, the destination would be elsewhere.

Once I would come back with a deep crease down my middle.

It would take several days to unfold. During that time I would rest on my side beneath the black plant, exposing the soft underside that had never faced the light.

The animal would approach. Its dull patches would turn separately toward me. A forefoot would rise and remain raised.

Then it would settle nearby.

We would spend the afternoon like that, with the basin empty behind us.

***

Eventually the animal would grow its next saddle.

Its sleeping body would be longer. The basin would continue rising around an earlier version of it, and my old place would still be apparent at the back: a smooth projection with a slightly different color, already beginning to lose the shape of my departure.

I would sometimes return there. The fit would become less exact each time.

One evening I would find the animal asleep with its new saddle held awkwardly above the lip. There would be a narrow clearance underneath it. I would approach from the cooler side, letting my thin margin enter first.

I could still soften enough to take some of its weight.

By morning there would be a new depression across my back. For several days I would carry it over the open floor, into the light, as far as the edge of the leaf.

And when I turned, the depression would hold a little shadow that turned with me.
