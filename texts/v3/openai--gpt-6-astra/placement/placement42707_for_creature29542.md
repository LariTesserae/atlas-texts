I would first find myself on the side that had not heard.

Fresh stone beneath me. A seam opening, green along its inner edge. I would have drawn close enough that the mineral was hardening around the finest parts of my attachment. There would be a little work to do: withdraw from one groove, deepen another, keep a passage open through which the altered water could leave.

Far away, I would already be afraid.

But here there would be food, and a surface becoming more usable as I learned it. I would spread.

I think I would be this discrepancy at first: the creature in the interval before its own news arrived. Not a separate inhabitant of its chambers, not a watcher placed inside a transparent body. The feeding would be mine. So would the alarm. There would simply be no place from which I could know both yet.

A slight pull would reach the attachment.

I would yield to it without leaving the stone. Currents made such requests continually. Then a second pull would arrive through a partition that ought to have been slack.

By then, several miles of me would have opened.

Here, I would tighten.

***

There would be no voice accompanying the mistake.

The new ridge would move across the light. A chamber would acquire a floor where it had never had one. Water held since before the ridge existed would pour through a rent and become indistinguishable from the sea.

For a moment I would feel the whole length of a partition through the tension tearing it apart.

Then less.

Then a pull so concentrated that the attachment would begin coming out of the stone.

I would release it.

Something small would remain caught in the groove. The tissue connecting it to me would stretch, turning cloudy. I would release more of the surrounding layers, but this would not slacken. The current would carry them around opposite sides of the ridge.

When the connection broke, I would continue withdrawing along paths whose far ends were gone.

That would last longer than the fold.

***

Afterward, I would be found near the old peak.

Not at its summit. Lower, where a shallow irregularity in the stone softened the passing water. My layers would lie so close together that they scarcely admitted light. Between them, water would move slowly enough for differences to persist.

I would have brought something back from the new ridge.

A small quantity, caught in a passage that closed during the retreat. Greenish salts. A trace of whatever had made the seam unusually good to enter. Perhaps, mixed with it, a substance released when the stone began to grip.

I would move this water inward.

It would pass close to an older reserve, and the tissue between them would thicken.

That would be the first thing I knew about it.

Not its name or its history. A barrier growing where ordinarily there would have been an exchange. Elsewhere, feeding surfaces exposed to a trace of it would contract.

The ridge would still be out there, abundant, growing. I would have no picture of it. Yet a thread extending in its direction would fork early, and neither branch would approach.

For a while, this would be enough.

***

I would not remain only a wound arranged around a rock.

On the lee face, olive films would spread beneath my outer layers. Their growth would alter the water. I would loosen a little above them, leaving a narrow passage. More light would enter. The films would extend into it.

Over many quiet days, I would acquire a shape partly made by their needs.

An attachment would shift away from a seam they occupied. Another would settle in the bare stone below. There would be no decision to shelter them, but shelter would become one of the things I repeatedly made.

When pressure rose, I would flatten.

The films would remain beneath me.

Sometimes the warning would pass without a correction, and I would open again. Water from outside would enter in a cool sheet. Ribbon tips would lift through spaces between my layers. Their small, repeated movements would become part of what quiet felt like there.

I would begin extending from that face.

***

The next contact would not be stone.

A thread would meet resistance in a channel I had crossed before. It would slide along the resistance, finding neither an edge nor a mineral surface. Where it touched, its tissue would hesitate, then adhere.

For a long time, almost nothing would happen.

A little water would cross.

On my side of the contact, several openings would narrow. On the other side, a chamber would empty and refill. Our layers would pull against one another in the current, gently enough to hold.

Then something unfamiliar would enter me: water with a faint mineral bitterness, retained elsewhere through conditions I had never encountered.

I would carry it toward the old reserves.

Before it arrived, its passage would change the behavior of a feeding attachment. The attachment would loosen from perfectly usable stone.

I would have received a reason to leave without having been injured there.

Perhaps this is where I would become most myself—not at a permanent knot, but in the slow handling of what came across. I would divide the unfamiliar water. Some would remain enclosed. Some would be diluted and brought against new tissue. Some would eventually leave me.

I would not accept all of it in the same way.

Across the contact, my own greenish reserve would begin to pass.

***

When the currents separated us, a chamber would go with the other creature.

I would retain several of its junctions.

For days, they would open too soon. They would respond to small pressure changes by releasing water my older tissue was still trying to hold. The adjoining layers would strain. New passages would form around them.

Gradually, the strain would diminish.

At the next correction, that region would flatten before the warning arrived from my distant attachments.

I would survive there with almost no tearing.

Elsewhere, I would lose a long, sheltered span that had survived many worse disturbances.

There would be no single lesson.

***

So I would live within reach of the old peak, but not always against it. Some of me would be testing a distant seam. Some would be holding still around water received from another body. Along one face, I would be making room for ribbons without ever seeing their pale green.

And I would still sometimes be found on the side that had not heard.

The sea would begin to lean.

Near the old stone, my chambers would open in succession. Farther out, a junction acquired from the stranger would already be slackening its connections. The warning would travel onward through tissue grown after the last storm.

At the newest ridge, I would be feeding.

This time, the first slight pull would reach a passage whose walls had been touched by the greenish reserve.

The attachment would pause.

Water would enter behind it, gently lifting it from the groove.

Before I knew what was coming, I would have left enough room to let go.
