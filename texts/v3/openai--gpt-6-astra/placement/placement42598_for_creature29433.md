At first I look for a room: one of the small ones below the square, with a shutter opening beneath someone else’s steps. I like the thought of seeing people arrive feet first. I imagine a shelf, a cup kept upside down, a place to sit without being asked to move.

But even this gives me too much of the village.

I move farther underneath. Past the baskets hung out of reach of rats, past the wet ends of the floor poles. There is a place where a root has grown around a beam and left a seam between them. Water follows the outside of the root. Inside the seam, the wood is soft and almost warm.

I think I would be there.

Not the root. Something eating the beam.

***

My head is hard. The rest of me yields.

This is useful. I can brace myself in a passage scarcely wider than my mouth, draw my body forward, and begin again. What I eat is already coming apart. There are pale threads through it, places where the grain separates easily, places that resist. I turn when I meet resistance. My passage is not straight, although it may look purposeful if someone eventually splits the wood.

I have no view of the ravine.

A foot comes down overhead and the whole passage tightens around me. Another follows. Then several together, with smaller, sharper disturbances among them. I stop eating until they pass.

Some disturbances last a long time. Something is set down and stays set down. Weight slowly changes the pressure in the wood. I retreat a little, finding the broad place I made earlier, and wait with my head against the wall.

Above me, perhaps, two people have stopped to disagree.

Their disagreement makes my afternoon narrower.

There are others in the timber. I do not meet them often. Sometimes I come upon a passage packed with dry crumbs, and sometimes a wall gives way into an occupied hollow. Then there is the awkward business of bodies reversing where bodies were not intended to reverse. My jaws are capable of damage. So are theirs.

Mostly there is enough separation.

The living root presses closer each season. On one side, the wood grows softer; on the other, the space becomes less forgiving. I work between these changes. I would not know that anyone considers the bridge stronger. Strength, where I live, can mean having to turn around.

***

Once, water reaches me.

Not dampness. Movement.

It comes along a passage from below, lifting the loose particles first. I feel them against my underside. Then the floor is gone under a thin cold rush, and I grip what I can.

There is no heroic climb. I am poorly arranged for haste. I wedge myself into a turn with my head higher than the rest of me. Water presses against my hind end. Something soft that was once another resident goes past.

For a long time I remain there.

When the water falls, it leaves a different place behind. One passage is blocked. Another has opened where I had not eaten. I follow it briefly, then turn back. The wood there tastes wrong.

Above, someone may be checking a lashing. Someone may be saying it held.

I begin again on the dry side.

***

I would stay this creature longer than a story usually permits.

There would be no need to emerge by evening, while the man is still considering the two pieces of his chair. He could put them away. He could take them out many times. A child could cease feeding the frog. The sharpener’s apprentice could become good at an uninteresting part of the work.

I would eat.

Then, eventually, I would stop.

This would be the strange part: plenty of wood at my mouth, and no movement of the jaws.

I would make a chamber near the surface. Not open to the air, but close enough that the wall ahead of me was different from the walls behind. I would press the loose material into place. Turn within the little room. Become still.

The changes would have none of the clarity they acquire when someone names them.

A leg folded where there had been no such leg. A surface hardening. Long intervals in which a footstep overhead passed through a body unable to withdraw.

***

When I come out, I am a beetle, dark enough to disappear against wet bark.

For a while I do nothing with this.

My legs grip the lip of the opening. Air touches parts of me that have never been touched separately. Beneath my feet, the bridge moves with a passing load, familiar movement received through unfamiliar joints.

There is too much room.

I advance until my whole body is outside, then turn and put my head back into the hole. I cannot conveniently enter. I remain like that for some time, half attempting something that no longer works.

Eventually I climb.

The underside of the bridge is crowded with routes. Rootlets hang free. A spider has made a narrow disorder between two of them. I avoid it without understanding the spider, cross a dry patch beneath the handrail, and reach the upper surface.

A palm comes down nearby.

The fingers curl over the wood. One nail has a dark split. There is a warm, salt smell, and the surface tilts slightly under the person’s weight.

I tuck myself into a crease.

The hand leaves.

Nothing has happened, except that I have encountered something large enough to end me and it was going somewhere else.

***

I would have wings under the hard covers of my back. I would not be graceful with them immediately.

My first departure might carry me no farther than the water crossing. I would strike bamboo, fall a short distance, catch a hanging fiber with three legs, then laboriously bring the others into use.

There would be a pause before I tried again.

By dusk I might reach the central tree. By full dark, a lantern beneath an awning.

Here I would be conspicuous for the first time: an oblong body making a blunt sound against the shade. Someone would lift the lamp. Someone else would complain. A child would offer a finger, and I would climb it because it was there.

Up close, my feet would feel less like feet than the faint catching of a rough thread.

The child would carry me to the railing, reluctant to release me, reluctant to close a hand.

I would open my wing cases.

For a moment the child would feel the whole preparation—the shifting grip, the lifted weight, the sudden difficult machinery of flight.

Then I would leave the finger and go a little crookedly into the dark beneath the roofs.
