I would be under the ridge, but not directly beneath the pale fold.

Farther in, one of the reservoirs narrows between two sheets of tissue. Water passing through that narrowing leaves behind a little of what it carries. The walls accept it, alter it, release most of it again. There is a slight delay.

I would begin there.

At first, the delay would belong entirely to the reservoir. Its walls would have thickened in response to some old irregularity of supply, and the extra passage through them would keep that irregularity from reaching the finer tissues downstream. There would be nothing to distinguish me from a useful thickness.

But the supply has been steady for a long time. Most of that thickness has been reclaimed. What remains maintains a small difference between the water arriving and the water leaving.

It takes material to keep this difference. It takes an inward surface and an outward surface, and a way of repairing one without briefly opening the other. Along the narrowing, I would have these things.

My body would be short in one direction. Across it, the water would travel less than the width of a finger.

In the other directions, I would be difficult to follow.

A film of me would pass beneath the reservoir and ascend inside a rib. Another would spread through the floor of a passage where no weight has ever rested. A third would accompany a root downward until the root divided; there I would divide too, unequally. Much farther away, a little tract of my tissue would lie against the inhabitant beneath the pale fold.

Through that tract, I would receive substances already modified by its habits.

It keeps a reserve of something I use. It releases this reserve slowly, even when more is available. My nearest membranes would be arranged around that slowness. They would take in small quantities efficiently. They would hold their pores open a little longer than membranes elsewhere.

We would fit together well.

That fit would be part of my shape.

***

I would have no access to the whole reservoir.

Its upper boundary, its length, the curve by which it narrows toward me: none of these would arrive as a picture. A change in its contents would arrive along many routes, at different times. Some of those routes would pass through other lives.

I would respond first to a pressure difference at one end, then to a changed proportion of salts below, then to an electrical alteration in the ascending film. The responses would meet inside me. A passage opened by the first might be closed by the second before anything had flowed through it.

Often, that would be enough.

Occasionally, the order would matter for a long time.

There would be a place within me where an arriving compound could follow either of two processes. One would be cheaper. The other would leave behind a small quantity of material that made it easier to begin again after an interruption.

I would favor the second.

This preference would persist through the replacement of every cell involved in it. Neighboring tissues would receive slightly less from me as a result. They would adjust their offers. Along one contact, the water between us would contain a concentration neither of us maintained anywhere else.

My precaution would have a cost outside my body.

***

I think I would be becoming separate.

The inhabitant beneath the ridge would still supply part of what allowed me to maintain my membranes. In return, I would make something its deeper tissues required. For a long time, neither of us would keep enough to endure the loss of the other.

Then a lower root would begin bringing me a little more than it had before.

There would be no sudden abundance. The excess would first be spent on repair. Later, some would remain in the walls beside the narrowing. My outward exchanges would continue much as they had. Beneath them, the reserve would grow.

Elsewhere in me, a process that had almost disappeared would become useful again.

Its first products would be poor substitutes for what came from the ridge. I would use them locally, in tissues where the difference mattered least. The process would spread through those tissues. Eventually, less would need to enter through our shared contact.

The ridge would answer the smaller demand.

Its supply would diminish.

For some time, my reserve would conceal from each of us how far the change had gone.

***

Then there would be an interruption.

Perhaps a deep membrane would lose its charge. Perhaps an adjoining life would draw too heavily from a common route. The cause would reach me only in its consequences.

At the old contact, almost nothing would arrive.

My pores would remain open. Other passages would narrow. Stored material would enter circulation, and the slower process inside me would accelerate until a few tissues could no longer afford their usual repairs.

Along the reservoir, my inner wall would grow thinner.

But the difference across it would hold.

Beyond me, the ridge would also hold.

When its supply returned, I would accept it. Yet some of my pores would already have closed, and the returning material would meet less readiness than it once had.

There would now be two lives where there had been a dependence difficult to divide.

The water would continue through the narrowing.

***

I would retain the old contact.

Separation would leave it with plenty to do. There would still be exchanges for which the ridge was the nearest source, wastes it could use, alterations in pressure that passed between us before their causes arrived. Its slow release would continue to suit some of my membranes.

But one strip of tissue would remain prepared for a longer absence.

It would hold more than its immediate work required. When the reserve was drawn down, it would replenish itself before offering much to the common flow.

That would be one of the features by which I could be recognized.

Another inhabitant, supplied from the same depth and maintaining the same difference in the same water, might give more freely.

I would not.

***

Above, someone could stand beside the pool.

Their weight would enter the ridge and be distributed. A little pressure would reach my ascending film. I would close several passages, divert water through others, and consume some of the reserve.

If the weight remained, new tissue would be prepared.

If it left, some of that tissue would be reclaimed before it was used.

Nothing in me would locate a hand, or distinguish departure from the ending of a strain. The contact with the ridge would alter for a while. I would answer according to what was still open.

My life would continue in the consequences.

Far down one of the unequal branches, a membrane would receive slightly less material because of the pressure overhead. It would postpone a repair. Before the postponement became damaging, another route would supply it.

The repair would proceed with a different proportion of compounds.

That proportion would make the repaired membrane a little slower to open.

Much later, when the same route was needed again, the water would wait there.

A little more would pass elsewhere.

Under the ridge, beyond the reach of a hand, I would be taking that shape.
