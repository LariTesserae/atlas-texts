At first I would put myself beneath the flashing, in the packed folds that stay wet. There is room there for almost nothing to happen. Water is held against water; the steel prevents an edge from lifting. I would take the dull pressure overhead for shelter.

But that is where I would withdraw to, not where I would chiefly be.

I think I would be spread between that protected thickness and the newest crescent on the floor. Some of me would already have committed to the exposed concrete. Some would remain folded where the water lasts. I would not be able to bring those parts into agreement except by passing fluid between them.

In the recess, there would still be enough.

At the margin, enough would be ending.

That difference would keep me moving.

The court would not arrive as a place surrounding me. I would have no view of its four slopes, no sense of the duct rising on one side or the water tank on the other. The nearest thing to a wall would be the place where I could spread no farther. The nearest thing to distance would be how much of myself I had to thin to reach a damp patch.

The mountain would have no part in this. Nor would the roof.

Yet the little curl of metal would be very much present. I would have grown around it before it began to rust. Now it would prevent a fold from closing completely. A small volume would stay open there when the rest of me tightened, and the water in that volume would acquire something from the metal. I would accommodate both the stiffness and the taste without separating them from the shape of my body.

If the curl loosened one day and washed out, I would retain its shape for a while.

That might be one of the ways I could be old.

***

The valve begins before the water does.

I would tighten at the first roughness in the floor. Not everywhere equally: the lobe nearest the discharge would draw in sharply; the newest growth would respond later, transmitting a weaker pull. Beneath the flashing, the folds would fill.

Then the flood would cover what remained outside.

There would be no need to know whether the contraction had worked. The surviving attachment would be the result. A little grit shifted. One margin turned under. A thin portion sheared away so cleanly that the water passing over it scarcely changed direction.

Afterward, I would extend along the remaining edge.

I would not be the same width as before.

I would not have an account of the loss, but the next contraction would pass through a different body. At one point it would travel faster. At another it would stop, having reached a place where there was no longer anything to tighten.

This is where I find myself more clearly: not quite in the secret, intact animal waiting under the steel, but in the uneven thing that has come through several floods. A body whose responses still contain the dimensions of parts it no longer has.

Some of those responses would be useful again. Others would gradually diminish.

***

For a long time, the blue film would make one direction difficult.

Its tapping would run down the fastening and through the cuff. I would loosen there and spend more of myself on the floor. During still hours I would return to the screw. There would be food along that passage, and water arriving even when the broad concrete began to dry.

I would grow and retreat through many such hours.

Then a worker might pull the strip free.

The event itself would be enormous: weight on the court, torque in the screw, a short violent disturbance in the steel. I would retreat as far as I could. Perhaps the worker would wipe a hand along the panel. Perhaps water would be pushed into my recess.

After the footfalls ended, the fastening would remain quiet.

I would not immediately occupy it.

Mist would come. My cuff would thicken, then loosen at a vibration from elsewhere. Water would pass through the gap without much of me touching it. The next cycle might be different. Eventually I would close farther around the screw than I had before.

By then the strip would be somewhere below, caught in a bin or plastered to another roof. My body would still be changing because it was gone.

***

I would also be out on the floor where I appeared not to be.

The clear trace would remain after the exposed tissue withdrew. It would hold a little dampness, catch a little matter, alter the next drop’s edge. There would be a period when I was absent from a route that nevertheless continued to do something I needed.

That is the part I would hesitate over, if I had the means to hesitate.

A worker could destroy it without touching my body. A dry season could erase it. But while it lasted, water would cross the concrete differently. My later growth would follow a condition left by my earlier growth.

I would be neither entirely under the flashing nor entirely at the dark crescent. There would be these intervals between: nearly dry, apparently empty, still making my return possible.

And I would sometimes extend too far.

A run of heavy mist would let me spread beyond the reach of an ordinary cycle. The surface would seem reliable because it had been reliable several times. Then the fans would take a different load. Air would scour the corner. My outermost edge would lose its gloss while the protected folds remained full.

Fluid would move outward, then inward.

A thin line would stay behind.

For several cycles, water would find that line before I reached it. I might follow eventually. I might never follow again.

There would still be the screw to hold, the pressure to answer, the crowded film passing inward through my softness. In one recess, a fold would open. Along one exposed edge, contact would improve.

I would live there with no assurance that the court was becoming more habitable. Only this corner, during this return of mist, would receive water a little sooner because I had been in it.

I would spread into the extra time.
