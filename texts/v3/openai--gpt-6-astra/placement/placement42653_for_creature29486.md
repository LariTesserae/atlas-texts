I would look first for somewhere out of the draft.

Not a dwelling. I think the recesses would be wrong for me, and the brushes that rise from their walls would keep searching my body for pores I did not have. I would stand in one for a while, trying to be accommodated. By the time I came out, its lower folds would have begun to curl inward.

There is probably a place behind the stair workshop where the main passes through a wall. The hole was made when the pipe was smaller. Now a collar of pale tissue surrounds it, warm along its lower edge, and between the collar and the wall is a space that neither has quite filled.

That is where I would settle.

At first I would seem to be something packed around the pipe to keep it from rubbing.

I would have a broad, soft underside and several hard plates along my back, none quite matching the next. My front end would be difficult to distinguish from my rear until I began to move. Then a pair of short feelers would emerge, touch the surfaces on either side, and withdraw. I would advance by loosening one part of myself while another held fast.

I would be longer outside the hole than anyone expected.

Including me.

***

The workshop attendants would know I was there before I knew what brought them past my opening so often.

One would leave a shaving near the pipe. Another would remove it. A third would sit for a long time with its feet spread, chewing a thread drawn from the stalled tread.

I would wait until it left before taking the thread’s discarded end.

It would taste of pressure. Not sweet, exactly: something held back, something that continued to pull after I had swallowed it. I would spend the next dim cycle braced against my collar, plates lifted a little from my back.

When the tread finally advanced, I would be able to sleep.

After that I would prefer scraps already cut free.

The attendants would learn this. They would leave little hard curls, the trimmed ends of rails, a dry strip from beneath a window opening. I would draw these inside and arrange them beneath myself. Sometimes I would eat one. Sometimes I would keep it until it softened and the wall animals came for it.

I would dislike the wall animals in my bed. Their movement would prevent me from telling which parts of the trembling were mine.

I would learn to tap the pipe.

Someone would come with a bowl.

***

My first work would be accidental.

During the closed part of the cycle, an attendant would bring a tray of distance through the passage behind the workshop. There would be room for the attendant and the tray, but not for the new thickness of the wall.

The tray would tilt.

Most of the strip would remain on it. A little of the end would touch the paving beside my opening.

In the dark, the pipe would become farther away.

I would still be holding it with the rear part of my body. The front part would be at the wall. Between them, several plates would separate enough to expose the tender folds beneath.

It would hurt slowly.

The attendant would not try to pull me free. It would set down the tray, lower itself onto three limbs, and wait with its short hands against my side. Perhaps its warmth would help. Perhaps it would only keep me from loosening the wrong part.

Eventually I would get a feeler beneath the spilled edge.

There would be an underside to it.

I would not have expected that.

By morning the strip would be back on its tray, folded badly around a small quantity of my shed skin. I would be wholly in my hole. The attendant would remain outside, its hands still curved.

For several cycles nobody would leave food directly at the opening. It would be placed a little farther off, where I could reach it without passing beneath anyone.

***

Later, they would bring me lengths that had stuck to themselves.

Not often. I would not be necessary to most of the town’s work.

A strip might have been stored too close to a warm junction, or folded before its edges had stiffened. Two distances would adhere, leaving a pocket between them into which a fingertip could enter farther than it could safely be withdrawn.

The attendants would put such a piece on the ground outside my pipe.

I would examine it after they had gone.

Some I could separate. I would get underneath with the thin leading edge of my body and hold still while the joined surfaces eased apart. This could take most of a cycle. I would need to keep several places fastened to the paving. If a knot tightened beneath me, I would stop.

Others I would leave untouched.

There would be one small, thick piece I could never bear to approach. It would lie on a rack for a long time, apparently no different from the others. Every time I passed it, the rear of my body would begin to follow a turn the front had not made.

Eventually the inspector would carry it away.

I would follow as far as the corner.

I would want to know where it was put.

***

I would acquire things.

A thorn with the curl broken off. A fragment of the bowl used to collect my wall animals. A black grain that made a faint sound when rolled across one of my plates.

The grain would be my favorite.

I would carry it in a fold close behind my feelers. When resting, I would take it out and push it up the warm slope of the pipe collar, then let it come down against me.

Sometimes it would roll poorly. Sometimes it would strike an exposed seam and make a clear, small click.

An inhabitant might watch this for several cycles before bringing me another grain.

The new one would be larger and would make no sound.

I would take it inside anyway.

When that inhabitant returned, I would bring both out. I do not know whether this would communicate what I meant. I might not know what I meant until I saw which one it picked up.

***

I would go to the cistern because of the cold.

During rain, the pipe behind the workshop would lose its warmth before the other junctions did. I would come out and move along the main, trying each swelling with my underside, until I reached the square.

I would not lean over the water at first. My feelers would find the tightening petals, and I would remain beneath the rim where the air was still.

Above me, inhabitants would arrive and leave.

Their feet would spread near my head. Occasionally a pad would rest on one of my plates, then lift with a quickness I would come to recognize as embarrassment.

Once, when the rim bent under someone’s weight, I would see into the cistern.

There would be a lit room containing a pipe.

The pipe would pass through the wall without a gap around it.

I would look for a long time at that snug, ordinary junction.

Then someone below would cross in front of it carrying a shallow box, and I would follow the box instead. It would contain loose pale plates. One would have a dark notch on its edge like a notch on my own back.

I could not turn far enough to compare them while keeping the room in view.

By the time I had found a better position, the room would be gone.

Afterward I would sometimes visit the cistern when I was warm enough to stay home.

***

The young would discover that I could be stepped across.

I would usually lie close to a wall, but while working on an adhered strip I might extend well into the passage. A child approaching on its wide, soft feet would pause, test my outermost plate, and put a little weight on it.

I would flatten.

Soon there would be several of them, crossing with great care.

They would be less interested in my work than in the way my plates rose again after each foot had passed. One would try to catch a rising edge with its toes.

I would close too quickly.

There would be a thin noise, and all the other children would unfold their feet at once.

An adult would come. It would not strike me or tap my plates. It would press two fingers against the soft front of my body until I released the trapped pad.

For a while, the children would take another route.

The injured one would return first. It would stand beyond reach and put a piece of stair shaving on the paving.

I would leave it there until the child had gone.

Then I would eat it, although I was not hungry.

***

As the pipe thickened, my home would become shallower.

I would push the loose objects toward the back until there was no farther back. The silent grain would go first, placed outside against the wall. Then the bowl fragment. I would sleep with several plates exposed to the passage.

An attendant would notice. It would touch the pipe collar, then the wall, and later return with the old individual who inspected the treads.

They would wait while I came out.

The inspector would hold a worn hand above the opening.

I would remain beside my belongings, curved around the black grain.

There might be another hole farther along the main. Someone might already be preparing it. But I would not follow when they turned toward the corner.

Behind me, within the narrowing collar, something would scratch three times.

I would turn.

A small, moist face would be reaching from my bedding, its sealed eyes moving belatedly toward the warmth I had left. One thin limb would hook over the broken bowl. Beneath its mouth, the collar would bear a fresh scalloped bite.

I would wait for it to finish.

There would still be room to put the front of my body inside.
