I would be a little below the place where the rain used to fall.

Not at the exposed edge. Under it, where two sheets overlap and a shaving keeps them from meeting completely. The shaving has been there long enough for its upper surface to carry a cloudy film. Its lower surface rests in me.

From the entrance, this place would look dry.

I would still be supplying the old channel. Each warming would bring less water, but the passage beneath it would remain broad, its walls yielding easily. Farther along, toward the new droplet, there would be a constriction. Whatever reached that way would have to pass through it slowly.

At first I would seem to be the thicker patch: the dampness held beneath the overlap, withdrawing a little each cold interval, returning when the pipe warmed. But some of what maintained that patch would have come from under the pool. Some would have passed through the wick. A pressure applied near the roller would narrow passages here before any sheet above me moved.

My extent would be difficult to establish without cutting it.

For a while, the shaving would settle this. On one side of it, my tissue would be thick enough to retain fluid. On the other, the new thread would stretch toward the place where water was beginning to collect. The shaving would be both a support and the thing keeping those two conditions apart.

When the first drop bent it, I would flatten beneath its lower edge.

A small quantity of fluid would be driven backward. The passage toward the old channel would swell. There would be no corresponding increase in warmth, no new water arriving there—only the brief return of fullness to a place already diminishing.

Under the new rain, the thread would hold.

The drop would remain on the shaving longer than it had remained on the pipe. Water would creep around a keen edge, find the cloudy film above me, and enter through a margin too thin to have folds. The exposed thread would cease stretching. Farther back, beneath the overlap, a cavity would round out.

That would be the first change.

Several cycles later, the passage toward the old rain would still be open, but it would no longer widen as much during a thaw. A second passage, scarcely distinguishable from a flaw in my surface, would follow the underside of the shaving. Water would take that route more readily each time.

I would become uneven in a different way.

There would be places in me still spending what they received on the old arrangement. A broad pale rim would persist beneath the former lowest point of the pipe. It would be well supplied with everything except rain. Meanwhile the thin new margin would receive more than it could hold, and drops would escape it, strike the fresh ceramic, and vanish down seams where nothing living had yet settled.

I would extend into one of those seams.

The first extension would fail. Its tip would reach a smooth surface without scoring, and the small amount of water around it would spread too widely. By the next cold interval it would be fixed there.

Behind it, another edge would enter beneath a scale.

There I would meet tissue coming from elsewhere.

For a time, we would make a double line. Water would occupy the narrow separation, darkening it. When one margin swelled, the other would give a little. Neither would advance through the other. The seam above us would be low enough that both touched its roof.

Then the relay would engage.

My passages would close sharply. The other margin would remain full for a moment longer.

Along the place where we touched, that difference would bend us. A little water would be squeezed from between us. When the constriction passed, the two edges would settle against one another without the dark line.

The next warming would enter from both ends.

I would not acquire the other portion’s history all at once. The water from it would carry a different faint residue. Its wider passages would draw fluid toward a place I had not supplied before. Beyond several narrow connections, a fold would slowly increase beneath the arch.

Here, under the shaving, I would become thinner.

Someone putting a finger into the hollow might lift the upper sheet. I would draw into strands between the ceramic surfaces. Several would break. What remained below would close over, and what remained above would begin losing water to the air.

But no one would put a finger there.

The sheet would rise by itself, pushed from underneath. The shaving would turn, exposing a clean strip of its lower face. The new connection would stretch across that strip.

For one interval, I would be held open between a place that no longer received water and a place not yet wet enough.

Then the pipe would warm.

The next drop would fall farther along.
