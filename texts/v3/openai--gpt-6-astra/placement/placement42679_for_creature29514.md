At first I would mistake my place for a hollow in the ground.

There would be a pale ceiling close above me, deeply creased, with little beads of water gathered along the creases. Below, the mineral would be equally pale but without a pattern. A root would cross the opening. Beyond it I could see part of a leaf and the legs of something standing on the water.

I would have enough room to spread my hands.

Then the ceiling would tighten. Its creases would draw together, and the whole hollow would rise, carrying me with it.

I would be underneath a foot.

Not trapped. Attached, with my belly against the sole and all six limbs spread into its softness. The animal would hold its foot above the water for a while. Light would enter around me, showing a snail beside my head, its horns extended against the skin. Then the foot would settle, and warm water would come up over my eyes.

I think I would be small enough to live there, but not so small that the foot’s movements were my only movements. When it rested I could loosen one hand, reach to the edge, and put my face out.

My nostrils would be two short slits. I would close them against the water without attending to it. My mouth would open beneath my head, where I could not see what I held.

This would matter more to me than the view.

A surface would enter my knowledge by touching the pleats of my mouth. Leaf would draw differently from root. Fruit would give almost before I began to pull. The pale ground would resist for a long time, and then there would be something in the resistance that my throat could take.

I would remain there after feeding, mouth resting against the shelf. Along my lower lip, tiny feet would collect in the clear fluid.

One of them would occasionally step inside.

I would lift my jaw until it found its way out.

*

The large animal above me would not be a landscape to itself. It would have its root held in the curve of one forelimb, its particular folded leaf within reach, a pressure along its other flank where a neighbor leaned. I would know some of these things through its foot.

When it fed, the sole would become very slightly firmer.

Before it called, there would be a drawing-in that reached even the places between its digits.

I would feel that and release my mouth from the ground.

My own throat would answer, although perhaps not every time. Something farther away could hold my attention: a lower note arriving through the shelf, or the soft belly of another small animal passing across the opening. I might remain silent while the body above me sounded.

The call would be enormous from underneath. There would be no single direction to it. The water against my eyes would move; the skin over my back would move; the mineral under my hands would seem to become briefly less solid.

But it would not hurt.

I would open the little chambers in my neck, and my sound would pass into those surfaces. Out beyond the root it might scarcely be audible. Here it would make the snail withdraw one horn.

That would be enough to notice.

*

I would not stay beneath the same foot.

There would be a route upward, though from outside it would not look like a route. First the ankle, where I could fit between two loose scales. Then the fold of the middle limb, with its faint animal smell stronger than the flowers. Then a stretch of flank so broad that I would have to cross it with my belly flattened and my tail searching behind me.

At each breath, the ground beneath me would slope.

Small shells would interrupt the seams. I would put a hand on one and feel it continue, slowly, under my weight.

Near the underside of the body there would be openings beneath soft flaps. I would know them before I could see them. Water leaving one would touch my face, carrying the taste of the chamber inside.

I might have come from there.

There would be no memory of emergence, no remembered first opening of my eyes. But I would turn toward one chamber rather than the next. My forelimbs would find a way beneath its flap without searching, and the membrane inside would lie against my cheek with a pressure I already knew.

Another body would occupy most of it.

I would feel a folded tail, then a foot pressed outward. The digits would spread against my mouth. I would wait through a breath, my head inside and my hind limbs still gripping the flank.

Then I would back out.

Not driven away. There would simply be more room elsewhere.

Water would follow me from the opening. A fine root hanging across it would adhere to my back, travel a little way with me, and slip free.

*

Perhaps the place I would return to most often would be behind the jaw.

There, the scales would diminish into skin. A ridge would shelter my tail, and I could hook my forward hands over the edge of a larger scale. When the head lowered, I would be submerged. When it rose, my face would come just clear of the water.

The adult’s eye would be above and behind me. I would seldom see it.

I would see what fell.

A piece of fruit, worked loose by fingers overhead, would strike the snout and slide toward the water. I could catch it against the skin with my mouth. Sometimes the great tongue would emerge below me and gather it first. Sometimes I would already be holding it, and the tongue would touch my forelimb and withdraw.

I would feed until the fruit’s covering became a loose transparent bag.

There would be other small ones along the jaw. One would habitually lie across the place where I put my tail. I would press against it until its limbs adjusted. Later, when I loosened my grip to call, it might put its head beneath my chest.

We would be watchful without looking very far.

A toe opening. A membrane drawing inward. The underside of a leaf turning within reach.

The basin would extend beyond all this, but I would encounter its breadth chiefly in the calls. Some would reach us through the water with their roughness softened. Others would arrive so close that I could feel the separate contractions making them. I would hold my mouth away from the skin and let my own neck swell.

I would not know which animal would take up my note.

While I was still sounding, a deeper vibration would enter the jaw beneath me. I would spread my hands, settling more firmly into the fold. The head would begin to lower toward its leaf, and I would go down with it, my call continuing a little while under the water.
