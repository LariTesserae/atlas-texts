At first I would put myself in the service recess, standing very still, trying to hear what moves behind the wall.

But standing still would not be enough. My warmth would go on entering the metal. Even if I held my breath, something on the other side would have to respond to me.

I think I would be on that other side.

Not the adult, which already knows so many ways through the room. I would be the young one that has not yet begun to feed on the leaf.

The other has moved a short distance away. I know this because a place that used to resist my legs no longer resists them. When I extend sideways, I find a loose fiber. It bends under me. I bring the leg back.

There is still enough damp where I am.

***

My body has little weight, but the surface can fail beneath it.

A flake turns over. A fiber pulls out of the packing. What felt firm while I rested becomes loose when I try to move. I have to discover this separately with each foot. Sometimes I hold so tightly to one strand that I cannot advance until another movement, somewhere behind my head, releases it.

I do not yet move as one animal. Parts of me arrive, and the rest must be brought along.

Above me is the adult’s underside.

I know its approach first through the fibers. They settle under a weight that does not crush them. Then a leg passes close enough for mine to catch it, and withdraws gently, and the low space above me fills.

I reach upward.

There are folds I can fit my mouth against. Along one of them, moisture gathers. I stay there while the fold slowly loses its fullness. The other young is beside me now. Its feet work against my side, then grow still.

For a while neither of us has to find anything.

***

When the adult leaves, the space becomes larger in several directions.

There is no sudden parting. One surface draws away, another remains. I follow the remaining surface until it, too, lifts beyond my reach. My front legs hold nothing. I lower them into the packing.

The fragment lies nearby.

Its torn edge is darker than its middle. The other young has found something there and keeps its head pressed down. I touch the edge with a pad. It gives under pressure, then returns slowly. I put my mouth where the pad was.

Nothing happens that I recognize.

I move back beneath the place where the adult settles.

Later I return.

***

The wall warms.

It begins beneath several feet at once. I lift one, set it down farther away, lift another. The difference is small and diminishing. Places that were cooler are becoming like the place I left.

The adult is here. Its body rises above us. For a moment there is more room, enough that I begin to move beneath it.

Then a limb comes around my side.

I catch it. It draws me backward through the fibers, farther under the curled metal. One foot remains hooked behind me and my body stretches until the foot comes free.

Here the warmth reaches me more slowly.

The adult touches my mouth, then presses down against the packing. I find a damp strand beneath its head and hold to that.

We remain crowded at the back of the curl.

Long after the wall has cooled, a leg still lies across the way out.

***

I would be this young one partly because I would want to know what happens when it returns to the leaf.

Not a revelation. The edge has softened a little more. Something comes away beneath the mouthparts this time.

I stop, holding it.

Then the small movements continue.

There is nourishment here, but it takes a long time to obtain. My feet shift while my head stays down. The other young works along the opposite edge. Between us, the leaf fragment rocks almost imperceptibly in its bed of fibers.

When the adult returns, I am still feeding.

Its underside hangs above the place I used to wait. I turn toward it. Moisture touches a plate near my head, travels along the overlap, and reaches the softer skin below. I lift myself into contact.

Afterward I go back to the torn edge.

***

Beyond the packing is a strip of exposed metal.

I reach it by accident, following a fiber that runs farther than the others. The end slips from beneath me and my front feet meet something hard and cool.

They hold.

I place another foot beside them. It slides. I draw back, but the first two remain fast, leaving my body stretched between the fiber and the wall.

For a while I stay like that.

A rear foot finds a fold in the packing. With that purchase I can pull myself back. Instead, after resting, I extend the slipping foot again. A little lower this time.

There is a scratch in the metal.

The tip catches.

I move forward by less than the length of my head. Behind me, my body uncurls enough to expose a clear joint between two plates. The moving air reaches it. I pause, then withdraw into the damp.

That is as far as I go.

The scratch remains just beyond the fiber. I return to it after feeding, and again after the adult has gone. Each time I find it with a different foot.

From the chamber, there would still be nothing to see: the cool wall, the machinery obscuring the stair, a basin slowly receiving water.

Inside the wall, I would be learning the first place where I could turn around.
