I would be beneath the fallen block, partway between the seep and the retaining wall.

From the slab, this would seem an unlikely place to stop. There is neither food nor water there, only a shallow clearance with flakes of limestone pressed into the floor. But the underside of the block holds a little warmth. One end rests lower than the other, and farther in, the space narrows until something broad and low could feel stone above and below at once.

I would have gone in farther than was necessary.

At first, I think I would still imagine the hollow from above: the pale middle, the basin to the left, the grass at the bend. Then that arrangement would become less useful. The basin would be a coolness reached along a particular wall. The grass would be several hard stems across a passage, with loose earth between them. The entrance might scarcely be a place at all. Air comes from that direction. Sometimes it reaches under the block.

What is close would have more parts.

A grain beneath my underside would be distinct from the grains caught on my back. The first would require a small adjustment; the others could remain. A patch of stone would offer a hold to the front pad and none to the pad behind it. I would stay there while the second pad spread, its rim settling around a shallow pit.

There would be time for this. I would not experience the delay as waiting.

Gradually, I would become certain of the body making these decisions. Six places of contact, though I would seldom need all six. A yielding underside. Weight carried low. Above it, plates that shift against one another when the passage bends. Near one edge, two plates would refuse to shift separately. To pass beneath the lowest part of the block, I would lower that side before touching the obstruction.

I would know how to do this without being able to recover the first occasion.

For a while I would remain under the block. There is a loose flake near my mouth. I touch it, then touch the stone beside it. The flake is dry through its whole thinness. Beneath the stone’s roughness there is the faint condition that leads toward water.

I turn that way.

The journey begins almost invisibly. A front pad takes hold beyond the edge of my body. Another releases. My back clears a low projection, settles, clears it a little farther. Light reaches the pale brows above my eyes.

Out here, the underside of the hollow is interrupted. There are gaps I must cross.

Beside the grass, a dead blade lies across my route. I press against it. It bends, holds, and slides along the join between two plates. For a moment it pulls at me. I stop. With the next movement it comes free and rests in nearly the same place.

Nothing has happened that I need to carry farther.

At the wall, my front pads find the slope. My body follows them upward. This is easier than the level ground. The surface is continuous, and moisture occupies its small depressions. I put my mouth to one of them.

There is enough here to remain.

Perhaps, on the slab, someone has been sitting all this time. I would receive very little of them: a change in the light, a slight disturbance through the stone if they moved their weight. If they held still, they could cease to matter while remaining close. I would not know that they were learning the outline of my back.

I advance along the seam. Behind me, the grazed stone shines a little differently.

Under the seep’s lowest shelf, the clearance accepts my head, then the thicker plates. I lower the stiff side. My back brushes limestone. The last pair of pads draws inward, and my underside settles against the damp.

Here I would be almost entirely fitted to where I am.

Yet one pad would remain a little out of place. After a long interval, I would lift it and set it down again.

Above my mouth, moisture gathers along a lip of stone. I cannot see the whole bead. A feeler touches the wet edge and withdraws. The bead grows until it reaches the fine skin beside my mouth.

I raise my head a little.

For a moment the water is held between us.
