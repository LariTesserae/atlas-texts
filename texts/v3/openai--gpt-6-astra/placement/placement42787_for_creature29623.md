I would know the country first by the places where I had to turn sideways.

A root against the ribs. A low roof that combed the fur backward between my shoulders. The widening where two bodies could pass, provided neither had full cheeks. Beyond that, a stretch of cooler floor, then the turn toward the sleeping chamber.

Distances would belong to the body. Far would mean carrying something heavy without a place to set it down. Near would mean hearing another animal chew.

At first I imagine myself at an entrance, looking out. But I would spend very little time looking. There would be a smell caught beneath the thorn branch, and another laid over it more recently. My nose would rise into one and lower into the other. A forefoot would rest on the lip of the burrow.

Something had crossed here while we slept.

I would wait.

Behind me, somebody wanting to leave would push a nose into the fur above my tail.

***

I think I would be an adult with no young of my own that season. Broad through the shoulders, an inconvenient shape in the older passages. One claw on the left forefoot would grow crooked from an injury I no longer attended to except when it caught in bedding.

I would not be the oldest or the first to emerge. I would know some useful things and be difficult about others.

There would be a particular recess where I liked to sleep, beside a root that remained cool late into the hot part of the day. I would spend time clearing it, pulling out sharp ends of grass, scraping loose crumbs from the floor. When I came back, another animal would often be in it.

I would shove.

If she shoved back hard enough, I would lie down against her instead.

This seems important to the life: that I would have preferences smaller than purposes. A chamber I liked. A passage I disliked because the roof touched my ears. One household member whose return brought me fully awake, whose flank I would sniff even when the food she carried smelled familiar.

The country would not be an undifferentiated wealth of scents. It would contain the particular smell of her having been somewhere I had not.

***

I would be good at making room.

Not at the first hard digging into undisturbed ground. Others would work faster there. I would follow afterward, worrying at the projecting edge that scraped a belly, widening a turn where a carried leaf kept catching. Packed earth would yield in small flakes. I would gather them against my chest and back toward the opening.

Sometimes I would keep working after the passage was wide enough.

Someone would arrive behind me with a swollen root in its mouth and have to wait while I removed one more obstruction. Then another. Finally it would climb across my back, pressing my chin into the soil.

There would be no gratitude for a good corner. Bodies would go around it more easily.

I would go around it more easily too.

***

One of the young would follow me more often than I wanted.

Dark-coated still, with clean fur beneath the chin, it would stop when I stopped and begin digging wherever my forefeet had been. If I uncovered a root, its nose would enter the opening before I had tested the skin. I would knock it aside with my wrist.

It would return from the other side.

For several evenings I would lead it no farther than the fallen branch. There would be pods there, and an old gallery soft enough to break without much trouble. Beyond the branch, the shaded lane would divide around a shrub whose lower limbs had died.

I would keep taking the left fork.

My feet would know the hollow beside the stump. My flank would anticipate a stem no longer there. I would cross a short stretch of open ground because it had once been covered, and the youngster would cross behind me.

Then, one evening, a weight would strike the soil close enough to come through my soles.

I would be under the branch before I had turned.

The youngster would not be beside me.

There would be a scuffle in the grass, a bird’s wing striking leaves, and then the youngster would force itself into the shelter with its coat standing unevenly along one side. We would remain pressed together long after the bird had gone.

I would smell its shoulder. Blood, but little. Torn fur. The hot, unfamiliar smell of its fright.

The next evening my feet would still take me to the left fork.

I would stop there.

The youngster would push into me from behind.

We would stand awkwardly at the junction before I turned us toward the other route.

***

After rain, my useful recess would become a bad place.

Water would follow the cool root into the chamber. At first there would only be a softness under the bedding. I would scrape at it, bringing up darker earth. More moisture would appear where I had scraped.

I imagine resisting the move. Carrying dry grass in when other animals were carrying wet grass out. Trying to settle and rising again with a damp belly.

Eventually the household would shift uphill, into a chamber that smelled too strongly of old seed.

I would make repeated trips for bedding. On the last, I would find one of the small young wedged behind the root, warm in a pocket the water had not reached.

I would have to loosen the grass around it before I could lift it.

The passage out would be crowded. An adult backing with wet earth would block the turn. I would wait with the young held carefully in my mouth, its hind foot opening and closing against my lip.

For once, I would not try to squeeze past.

***

Where would you find me?

Probably beneath the skirt of a tower whose summit I had never seen. The inhabited clay above would tick faintly while I worked. A shrub would have roots through our outer chambers; beyond it would be another household, known chiefly by warnings and the smells left at a crossing.

I would know several ways home, and one would cease to be safe before I quite believed it.

My work would leave a little more room underground and a little more earth outside. Plants would take advantage of both. I would eat some of them.

Toward morning, I would return with a strip of succulent leaf held crosswise in my teeth. At the entrance it would catch against both sides.

I would pull harder.

From below, someone would come up to investigate. Their nose would touch the leaf, then my mouth.

Only then would I turn my head.
