The first thing I would know is where I am held.

There is pressure underneath me, and along one side, and a little above. Between these contacts, parts of me are loose. They can thicken or draw inward. If the warmth changes, I can bring more of myself against the stone.

I would not know the shape of the hollow. There would be no overhead view, no entrance framed by shrubs. Somewhere beyond my contacts is a direction from which light sometimes comes. It reaches the dark areas beside my mouth as a change without an edge.

The place where I am held is narrow. A few of my feet have entered a continuation of it that the rest of me has not reached. They rest there, spread against the irregular surfaces. Behind them, other feet remain attached.

I could stay like this.

But one of the forward contacts is warmer than another.

A little more of me goes toward it.

***

I would be the smaller one, though smallness would not yet be something I knew. The seam admits me. That is what I have instead of a measure.

My folds would still have places where their surfaces did not quite resemble the wall. Dust would catch unevenly. Some of it would leave with me when I moved; some would remain in a faint, broken line along the lower lip of the crack.

One foot closes on a grain that is not attached to anything.

The grain falls.

Through the feet still holding, the disturbance is slight. For a moment there is also something in the air. I gather against the stone and do not continue.

Nothing follows.

The warmth has scarcely changed when I begin again. The foot that lost its purchase opens against a broader surface. Another advances past it. My body draws after them, slowly enough that one fold can ease out of the seam while the next still rests inside.

There is no single moment when I leave.

***

I would have places I returned to before I had travelled enough to make a route between them.

An unevenness in the seam supports my underside without requiring all my feet to hold. Farther along, a small pocket stays warm after the surface outside it has begun to cool. There is also a constriction where I must lengthen myself. Beyond it the rock offers more room, but I do not always go through.

Sometimes I remain with my forward part beyond the constriction and the rest behind it.

The two sides cool at different rates.

My body fits that difference for a while.

***

Once, the surface ahead yields.

Several feet have already spread upon it. Under their pressure, it draws inward. The movement passes beneath me from one contact to another.

I stop.

This surface is warm, finely uneven, dusted with grains like those on the wall. But it gives where the wall does not. One foot lifts and settles farther forward. Beneath it, the surface holds.

I travel onto the larger animal’s edge.

I would not know it as larger by looking. I would know how long this yielding ground continues, and how far I must stretch before my leading feet find stone again. There would be a shallow movement under my underside, then stillness. Enough stillness to cross.

For a little while, my weight rests partly on the rock and partly on another body.

Then the last feet release.

I go on toward a place too narrow for it to follow. Behind me, after an interval, its folds begin to move.

***

The dead branch would still be beyond most of my experience.

Changes from that direction would reach me: cooler air, a trace of moisture, vibrations carried through the rooted shrub into the fracture. On some nights I would turn toward them. Perhaps I would descend farther than before, until the stone beneath my feet met something with a different grain.

I would touch it for a long time.

There would be no need for the first journey to reach the food. I might turn back at the living stem, or stop beneath a lifted strip of bark. The hollow has distances that can occupy a whole night without carrying me out of shelter.

For now, I would be farther back.

The seam presses gently along my upper fold. My forward feet are released; the others hold where they happened to settle. A small part of my underside rests over an irregularity that will, after many returns, become smoother.

Outside, the ground is cooling.

Here, the change has not yet arrived.
