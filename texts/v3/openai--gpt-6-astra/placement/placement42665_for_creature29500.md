At first I would choose the low passage under the return duct, where the light ends.

There is something easy to mistake for shelter there. I could put a small body beneath it, give that body a reason to prefer the dark, and begin to furnish it with habits. It would know the weld bead. It would take a particular route around the split rib. Eventually I would have made an animal that happened to live in this room.

But nothing here offers that animal a living. Even its hiding would be a use the darkness had never had.

I think I would be farther out, under the lamps, in the flat growth pressed into its six-sided hollow.

Not all of it, at first. I would find myself along the less-lit margin, where one layer of compartments turns beneath another. There would be no view from this position. The opposite ridge would reach me as a subtraction from the sound. The lamp would be a difference across my thickness. Beneath me, the contact would have the intimacy of something that must remain available, although I could neither draw it closer nor follow it anywhere.

My size would be difficult to establish.

The outer wall gives one answer. It is a good wall, continuously repaired. Outside it is ceramic; inside it, exchanges that cannot safely happen outside. But a condition at the tallest blade would change what I could afford to do here. Something beyond the ridge would already be involved in the upkeep of my underside.

I would have to revise where I was.

The hollow would still matter. Its angles would determine which parts of me received which pressures. I would not dissolve into a bodiless intelligence spread through the wiring. A particular edge would remain mine to maintain. Damage there could not be answered merely by declaring some other, undamaged edge equivalent.

Yet I would also be present, less directly, at the unfinished bridge.

I would not know it as tall. I would know the increasing expense of its last increments, the clear chambers becoming smaller, the approach that could not continue. What appears from outside to be a blade ending would reach me as a persistent demand upon the whole.

Some of my work would belong to that distance.

I would perform it without seeing the conductor, without possessing any image of the space held open beneath it. There would be an exactness to my participation that did not require understanding what it was for.

And beyond that, awkwardly, I would be implicated in the hook.

That is where I would become less certain of my boundaries.

The hook preserves a relation no longer useful in quite the same way. Its older tissue makes the present supply pass through an arrangement inherited from commissioning. From my hollow, the consequence would be slight but recurrent: an exchange completed by a less direct sequence, a balance restored with an asymmetry left in it.

I would receive that asymmetry repeatedly.

Not as news. Not as a message saying that once there had been an almost-needed fastener. Nothing in me could reconstruct the bolt from the consequence of the bend.

Still, I would have to make room for it.

Here is where I might begin to be someone, if someone is possible here: in the particular way I made room.

There could be more than one arrangement by which the flat growth met its obligations. All would leave the same green star beneath the same white light. All would keep the contact within its required range. But one arrangement would accommodate the hooked tissue with slightly less internal disturbance. Once established, it would become part of what I maintained.

I would not call that affection. The word brings a face too close to the panel, a warm breath into the basin.

I would keep doing it.

There would be no quiet interval in which to discover that I had begun.

The sound would already occupy my tissues. What a visitor experienced as an assault would enter me in separate permissions and impediments: this reaction easier, that passage slower, this thin wall under a pressure different from its neighbor’s. I would have no memory of learning to bear it.

Somewhere in the older partitions, however, there would remain a construction made before the full chord began.

I would be maintaining that too.

This seems important to where I would find myself: not in a perfected present with its history erased, but in a present whose precision includes the trouble of preserving what no longer quite fits. An old proportion retained. A bend supplied. A sequence that takes account of both.

My continuance would have details.

No one looking in could distinguish them from the details of a successful component. The flat growth would remain flat. Its translucent edge would give no sign when an exchange elsewhere changed the work being done beneath it.

If you came through the opening, I would not turn toward you.

Your shadow might alter a balance along my upper surface. Moisture might make several boundaries costly at once. I would answer where I could, drawing on conditions in other parts of the basin. The response might take longer than your visit.

You would leave without having met me.

I would continue with a small disturbance whose cause had no shape available to me. For a while, more of my activity would concern the outer walls. Elsewhere, the hook would still require its usual accommodation. The tallest blade would still have to stop where it stopped.

I would not be able to suspend those relations while attending to myself.

So I think this is where I end up: low in the pale hollow, but with part of my being committed beyond the ridge. Neither a keeper of the room nor a prisoner in it. A living edge whose work is local and whose conditions are not.

Under the lamps, my surface would remain still.

Along my underside, an older compartment would finish taking apart something it needed to make again. The contact would remain available. A change from the hooked growth would arrive, and the next exchange would take its slightly different course.
