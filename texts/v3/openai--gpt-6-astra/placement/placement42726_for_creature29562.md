I would be beneath a root that curves away from the light, where its pale grain is visible on one side and lost in green on the other. Not in the deepest water. An opening in the floor would be nearby, but the root’s thickness would hide it from most of my body.

From above, I would look like a loose fold of skin along the wood.

There would be more of me than that view allowed: a heavy torso tucked into the clearance, a head held sideways, limbs distributed across surfaces too far apart to see together. One hand would lie in full daylight. My back would be in shadow. The light on the hand would not be a report arriving in the dark.

I would be one of the animals, though perhaps a less evenly extended one than the others. Five of my limbs would reach the plant. The sixth would be short and broad, held clear of everything.

Short by the proportions of this place. A person could lie along one of its fingers.

There would be no wound at its end, no sign that it had failed to reach a destination. Its joints would have the same shallow, unforced angles as the joints of the longer limbs. Along its palm, little sensory cups would open into unobstructed water. Between two fingers, a silver fish would occupy the blue.

That hand would be the easiest part of me to misunderstand. It would look as though I were offering something, or asking to receive it.

There would be nothing in it.

*

My face would be broad on one side and slightly flattened on the other, where the cheek lay close to a root. Not pressed against it. A narrow wedge of water would separate us, widening toward my mouth.

The mouth would have an ungainly softness. Small teeth, a thick lower lip, one corner more open than the other. There would be no expression I could make with it beyond the expression it had. Nevertheless, another of my kind would have me present as this particular animal, and not as a quantity of sensitive tissue.

My forward eyes would not share a view.

One would face the underside of my own short hand. It would contain the palm’s pale creases, the dark centres of the cups, the little fish beyond the fingers. The other would look along the root into a distance occupied by no animal large enough to distinguish.

I would have both the nearness and the distance. Neither would explain the other.

Under the skin of my throat, the great white cords would lie in uneven bundles. A transparent patch would expose the edge of an organ, brown and finely furrowed. Beside it, hidden by ordinary opaque skin, would be another part no less fully mine.

I do not think I would be transparent enough to understand myself.

The clear windows would show anatomy. That would be all.

*

The roar would be most particular at a place along my longest arm where the wood changed grain.

There, the root would contain a broad, dark band, almost invisible beneath its green covering. My arm would lie across it. The sensory seam on the inside of the joint would fit the surface closely, and the sound would have a dense, flattened character there, different from its depth in the adjoining wood.

To an ear in the open water, the difference might be negligible.

For me it would occupy as much of the world as a whole escarpment occupies for an eye. I would have the band’s width, the oblique way it lay beneath my limb, the finer presences at its edges. There would be no need to inspect it more closely. No closer place from which to inspect it.

Yet I would not know what the band did.

The plant’s labour would be intimate without being legible. Nothing in my contact with it would make me its keeper, its interpreter, or the small intelligence installed to comprehend its enormous work.

It would hold up the sea.

My arm would lie there.

*

Farther along that arm, where it divided, another animal’s fingers would rest across one branch.

Only three fingers. They would be thicker than mine, their skin dark and finely creased. One nail would face outward into the water. The contact would occupy a patch of my skin smaller than the clear window on my throat.

I would have no better access to the other creature through this contact than through the interruption its torso made in the basin’s sound. Its blood would be its own. Its distant limbs would extend into places absent from my senses.

Still, those three fingers would be there with a specificity that the whole surrounding root could not replace.

There would be a small exposed crescent of my skin between two of them. Cold water in that crescent; a different condition beneath the fingers. The distinction would have no urgency. It would belong to the shape of being next to this one.

*

The opening below the roots would be visible to an eye set high on the less flattened side of my head.

Not the whole opening. A slender part of it, interrupted by the edge of my shoulder.

Through it I would have white cloud and a piece of mountain. The mountain would be dark, almost without detail. A very narrow line of brightness would lie along one face.

Its smallness in my eye would coexist with the immense extent of my hand on the terrace. I would not need to correct either size.

Perhaps that is where I would find myself most exactly: with my body spread through the workings of the sea, and this dry, inaccessible stone included in what was present.

But not only there.

Also in the soft crease beneath my jaw. In the pressure of three fingers. In the palm held clear of the rootwork, where there was no contact to account for its shape.

I would have a body too particular to become merely a way of attending. Some of it would look awkward. One toe would lie sideways against the wood. A fold along my belly would shelter a crustacean whose transparent legs touched me at six minute points.

The great sound would be present throughout.

Between the fingers of my empty hand, the fish’s silver flank would have a narrow dark blemish near the gill. My eye would face it. Beyond the fish, blue water would extend past the reach of every limb I had.
