At first I would have difficulty locating my own edge.

There is sediment on my back and sediment on the shelf above it. Between the two lies a little water, scarcely moving. A snail reaches from the ceramic and puts its mouth against me. For a while this does not seem to require a distinction.

Then its weight passes over a place where the skin is thin.

I draw one leg closer. The snail lifts away into the dark.

So there is a body here, and some of it is mine to move.

I would be beneath one of the middle trays, neither near the good inlet nor in the deep shelter of the fallen partition. My place would have two entrances. One admits my head easily but requires a long, sideways adjustment of the shoulders. The other is wider, with a loose piece of ceramic at its threshold. I dislike touching that piece. When it tips, the disturbance comes through my jaw and belly together, too close to tell which direction it has come from.

I have lived here long enough to go around it.

Not long enough to be large.

The largest animal sometimes passes outside. Before any part of it arrives, water presses softly into my recess. My chin lifts. The shrimp beneath the adjoining tray close themselves into the available corners, and I draw back until the wall stops me.

A foot appears. Then the pale underside of a throat.

I keep my mouth shut.

Afterward there is a long interval during which small things come out again. I wait through most of it. Hunger is not yet a reason to leave.

*

I think my knowledge would lie unevenly through the room.

A few surfaces would be known with extraordinary precision: the underside of a rim, a joint with a hollow deep enough for two fingers, the slope where my belly begins to slide unless I turn. Between these would be stretches I cross without learning much about them. Beyond those, water in which something might be moving.

I would not know the six pillars as six pillars.

One would be the broad surface that receives my tail when I reverse out of shelter. Another would be encountered on the way to air. Its crust gives under the fingers at first, then holds. Higher up, there is a smooth band where I must spread both forefeet and push with the tail.

The pillar beyond the broken screen might scarcely exist for me. Its presence would arrive as a change in what the water carries.

Nor would I know that I am high above the country.

There would be no height in the floor beneath my belly. It holds. Under the silt, it holds everywhere I have tried it.

Only at the slit would support end.

I would approach that place more often than hunger could explain, though not often. There is less warmth against the face there. Something changes beyond the eyes without making a disturbance the jaw can read.

I put a foot forward.

The fingers find the outer edge of a joint, follow it, open.

Nothing.

For a moment I keep them open.

Then I draw them back and remain with my chin against the stone. The light weakens, strengthens. No animal passes between me and it.

I leave before I need to.

*

I would still have a shallow fold behind each side of my head, the last thickened remnants of the gills. Small grazers sometimes trouble them. I rub against the underside of an arch, first one side, then the other.

There are holes in the broken screen I can no longer enter.

Occasionally I try one.

My head passes through; my shoulders do not. I turn slightly, place a forefoot farther in, pull. The screen holds me behind the jaw. Water moves through the holes beside my face.

Backing out takes longer than entering.

Afterward I rest close by, with my chin in the silt. Tiny shrimp continue to pass through the opening. I feel them going where I had intended to go.

My body would be changing the room before I understood that it had changed itself.

*

The route to air begins behind my tray.

I follow the partition upward to a broken pipe, brace inside its mouth, and reach for the pillar. At the top I wait beneath the bowl. Sometimes another belly is there, pale and close, its feet loosely spread above my face.

I can wait.

When it descends, I flatten against the stone and let it pass. Its tail brushes my mouth. Then I climb the last short distance.

The surface touches my nostrils.

Here the room becomes briefly different. There is a sound I cannot feel through the floor: the soft slipping beyond the wall. My throat opens. Air enters. For those few movements I have an inside that the surrounding water does not share.

I lower my head.

On the way down, my left forefoot finds a familiar joint. Two fingers enter; the others spread against its edge.

This time the edge comes away.

I release it at once. The fragment strikes the pipe below. Silt lifts around my face, and I push backward so abruptly that my tail hits the pillar.

For a long time I hold there.

The new opening moves water against the underside of my wrist.

I descend by another route.

For several visits I avoid that joint. Then, approaching from below, I put my chin near it. The water coming through is faintly richer than the water outside. Something small withdraws within.

I touch the opening with one finger.

Farther in, there is another surface.

That would be where I find myself: partway between an old foothold and a passage not yet used, one hand inside the wall, my body still braced in the room.

I withdraw the hand.

Wait.

Put it in again, farther.
