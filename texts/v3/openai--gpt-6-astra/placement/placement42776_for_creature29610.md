I would be the one with the shortened leg.

Not because I remember losing it. I remember a surface continuing where it no longer continued, and then being held so firmly that none of my limbs could open. Those might be separate memories. At the time, I had fewer layers in my back. Much of what happened could pass straight through me.

Now I have a red grain that warms before the others, and a place beneath the adult where I fit badly because I have grown.

I am trying to get to the shelf.

***

The visitor thinks the shelter is quiet.

To me, the shelter is where things can be distinguished.

Outside, the wall is crowded with arrivals. A fracture far below reaches us through three ribs of stone, becoming three events. Roots tighten. A hunter lands. The bridge presses into its sockets and the pressure travels around the doorway, separating as it goes. Against all this, I can usually find the adult: six contacts, unevenly spaced, with a small slipping movement in the front left fan.

Inside, a grain placed on the shelf has an ending. You can feel it settle.

The visitor has not settled yet.

It puts an enormous amount of itself through one support, then changes its mind. Soft coverings muffle the beginning of each movement. By the time the stone tells me what is happening, the weight has arrived.

The adult has taken me off the shelf twice.

I still have the flake. It is too large to eat underneath the roof, where the other two keep putting their mouths on its edge. One of them has found the pale seam I was saving.

I push it away. The adult tightens a limb around us.

Through that limb comes the pattern again.

Two contacts, a space, a longer pressure.

I answer with a scrape.

The scrape is not what I intended. My rear limb reaches for a point it cannot reach, and my body turns slightly as I press. I can make the first two contacts or the long pressure cleanly. I have not yet made all three.

The adult repeats it.

Outside, the large leaves turn over.

We wait.

***

I know very little of the adult’s shape.

There is the underside, with its ridges for holding and the seams that sometimes soften enough to feed from. There is the edge I must not cross when the wall becomes confused. There are the front limbs, which can arrive from anywhere.

The back I know mostly from walking over it.

It is difficult country. Old repairs have become smooth, but newer grains catch my feet. A dark piece near the center carries vibration differently from everything around it. I put my front fans there when the adult rests. Small events are clear through it.

The adult usually removes me.

Once it did not. I stayed through many changes in the garden, feeling a place far down the wall where something struck at regular intervals. Then the strikes stopped. I kept my fans against the dark piece until hunger drew me away.

I have not found that place again.

***

When the missing note returns, we climb slowly.

I am underneath. I cannot know what the fans across the shaft are doing, or what has happened to the bridge. I know the adult now lifts one forelimb before the other has properly caught. Each transfer presses my back against the roof of the space where I cling.

There is a bitter charge along its underside.

One of the clear little ones tries to feed. The adult closes the seam.

At the roof we are held still for a long time. Then a front limb folds inward, and something hard is placed against my mouth.

A tooth.

I work at its narrow end. It breaks in flat pieces. The inside is easier than the outside and tastes of a mineral I have only found before in very small grains.

While I eat, the limb remains beside me.

A few teeth are still dark. The next is white. The adult catches it with the opposite fan and bends.

I feel the break through every place we touch.

Later I go to the stores and bring back a pale chip. It is the right size, approximately, though too thick at one end. I press it into the gap.

The adult takes it from me and puts it in the crack above the door.

I follow it there. I pull it out.

The adult holds my front limbs until the gust has passed.

***

The visitor’s glove is troublesome.

It touches the shelf in many places without holding itself between them. I cannot tell where its weight belongs. I approach one part, and another part shifts.

Beneath an edge there is a pocket almost large enough for me.

I put a forelimb inside.

Then the adult arrives, and the whole loose thing goes over the edge. Its landing is surprisingly small.

The visitor retrieves it.

I feel the adult take possession of the shelf: rear limbs on the seat, middle limbs spread, forelimbs close enough that I will not reach the outer edge unnoticed.

This is my opportunity.

I come out with the flake held under my body and put it down where the glove was. The shelf is warm from the adult. The pale seam is still there, though narrower.

The visitor shifts.

The adult taps.

I answer too quickly.

For an instant my own pressure conceals the beginning of the visitor’s movement. I clamp down. The flake slips from beneath one foot.

Nothing terrible happens. Weight enters the seat, spreads through the floor, and fades into the roof supports. The adult has one fan resting across my back.

It repeats the pattern.

This time I wait until I can feel the shelf again.

Then I answer.

***

I do not think the visitor understands. It grows more still, but stillness was not the whole request.

The adult lets me keep eating.

After a while, a new contact appears at the edge of the shelf.

It is broad, faintly warm, and yielding. Several smaller contacts extend from it. They remain in place long enough for their differences to become clear.

I test the nearest one.

The surface gives under my teeth, so I withdraw them. Nothing pulls away.

I touch it again with the side of my fan.

There is a slow disturbance inside it. Another follows. I spread my front limbs farther apart. The disturbance reaches both, stronger near the broad part.

The visitor is making a sound without moving against the stone.

I stay there through several repetitions.

The adult’s limb lifts from my back.

***

Eventually I will leave the roof.

There is a recess beneath the second seat, shallow but with two useful ribs and a root that carries charge even when the great leaves are turned the other way. Dust collects at its upper edge. I have already tested it during the quieter intervals.

I will have to widen the entrance. I will have to move the root without killing its tip. The adult may pull out whatever I place there. Some of my pieces have been returned to the stores before.

For now, I carry one grain at a time.

The visitor’s warm limb is in the way of the next grain. I could go around, but the longer route crosses a joint in the shelf where my rear foot sometimes slips.

I tap twice against the yielding surface.

Then I wait.

Under my front limbs, its slow inner movement continues. Under my remaining feet, the shelter carries the garden.

I lower my body until the short limb finds purchase on the grain embedded beside the joint. From there I can make the long pressure without turning.

This time it comes out clearly.

The visitor lifts its hand.

I go through.
