I would be under the last condenser gutter, where the bracket enters the wall.

Not in the deepest part of the crack. That stays too hot. Near the mouth, a flake of stone has lifted away from the mortar, leaving a space into which water sometimes draws itself without forming a drop. I would fit behind that flake. There would be room to turn over, though not while full.

At first Nera would know me only as a wet place that persisted after she had wiped the bracket dry.

She would tighten the joint above me. For two days this would make my life difficult.

***

I would have a body that was hard to measure.

Dry, I might be the length of the split in her thumbnail, dark and ridged and easily mistaken for something that had come off a washer. Wet, I would loosen. Folds would open along my sides. My underside would become pale enough for the contents of my gut to show through: brown specks, a small green clot, occasionally the intact black point of an insect’s foot.

I would not have a face at the forward end so much as a preference for going that way.

There would be little gripping structures along my belly. On glass they would fail me. On rough stone I could hold on against water strong enough to fold my body backward.

For much of the day, holding on would be what I did.

The gutter would warm above me. Its metal would lengthen, making a movement too small for Nera to see but large enough to press the upper edge of my shelter against my back. I would shift downward. Later, when cloud crossed the rim and the first cold drops reached the roof, the pressure would ease. I would move up again.

My day would contain these short journeys.

***

The first thing I knew of Nera would be her weight.

The turbine would be continuous: a slow compression, a release, a smaller shudder arriving through the bracket. Her footsteps would interrupt it. They would come in pairs, unevenly spaced near the wall because she stepped over a pipe there.

I would draw in before her shadow reached me.

Not because I knew she was large. A loose stone, a grazer rubbing against the building, and Nera coming to check the gutters would begin as much the same event. Only afterward would their differences matter.

Nera would bring a second vibration through the metal. One hand on the gutter before she leaned close.

Then darkness.

Then, sometimes, the white edge of her enamel strip.

I would taste the strip with my whole underside if she put it beneath me. It would offer nothing to grip. I would pull myself short and wait.

This would look like stubbornness.

“Come on,” she would say.

Eventually she would learn to lift the flake instead.

***

I would eat the soft growth at the boundary between wet and dry.

There would be very little of it at any one place. I would move with my front end pressed down, leaving a cleared patch narrower than a grain of wheat. During good weather, when the fog reached the works each afternoon, I could feed without exposing more than half my body.

During bad weather I would have to leave the bracket.

Bad weather, for me, would usually be clear.

There would be a route down the wall: mortar, the underside of a projecting stone, a dead wire held against the masonry by one remaining staple. The wire would deliver me to the pipe rack. From there I could reach the damp foundations, provided I crossed the open interval before the sun found it.

I would not always get this right.

Once I would contract beside a bolt and stay there through the hottest part of the day, too dry to move, with my underside sealed against a pocket of old moisture. A lizard would put one forefoot on the bolt above me. Its weight would tilt the loose metal and admit a line of light.

For a long time there would be only that line.

When it withdrew its foot, the bolt would settle back.

I would remain until evening.

***

I would be one of several such animals in the works, though Nera would initially think there was only one.

The others would occupy a broken ceramic insulator, the damp underside of a tank lid, the hollow behind a pipe clip. We would seldom meet. Where one of us had fed recently, another would turn aside.

After heavy rain, that would change.

The stone between our separate places would become passable. Old tastes would wash away. We would find one another beneath running water and press our forward ends together, then separate, then return. There would be no swift or elegant part of this. Grit would interfere. One of us would lose its hold and travel several inches downstream.

Later there would be small transparent cases attached inside the gutter’s rolled edge.

Nera would find them while clearing a blockage.

She would scrape most of them into her waste tin before noticing that something inside each case moved.

I do not think she would apologize. She would stop scraping, lean nearer, and swear at the awkwardness of the location.

The next morning she would clear the other end of the gutter first.

***

My shelter would eventually have to go.

The lifted flake would be part of a larger piece of failing mortar. Water entering behind it would begin to loosen the bracket. Nera would discover this by putting her hand where she always put it and feeling the metal yield.

She would close that bank of the condenser and come back with tools.

I would feel the first blow through my entire body.

The second would open the crack to daylight.

There would be no useful direction to take. I would contract against the exposed stone while pieces of my surroundings fell past me. Some would be large enough to contain every place I had occupied during the previous month.

Nera would find me among the damp crumbs.

“Oh. You.”

She would put me on the enamel strip. I would fail to move.

She would put a drop of water beside me. I would loosen toward it, and she would carry me to the tank.

This would be a mistake. The tank would have water but almost no roughness. When she tipped the strip, I would sink.

For a while I would travel downward with my folds open.

Then the strip would come under me again.

Nera would have to immerse her hand to retrieve it. Water would run into her sleeve. She would stand with me held above the tank, looking around for somewhere she could put a wet, unhelpful thing.

Eventually she would set me beneath the pipe rack.

***

I would live there afterward.

It would be a larger country. Shade would persist beneath the pipes, and several kinds of growth would occupy the foundation stones. The mammals would pass close enough for loose hairs to adhere to my back. I would spend hours removing them against a rough edge.

There would be dangers I had not encountered on the wall. A carrier could get its jaws beneath one of my folds. Mud could dry around me and fix me in place. Sometimes Nera would kneel above the rack and release water too quickly, and I would be carried out into the light.

But I would learn the low places.

One would lie beneath a pipe coupling, beyond the reach of her broom. Another would be under a flat stone whose upper surface the mammals used as a lookout. On very dry evenings, I would make my way toward the hollow where she emptied her cup.

I would arrive after the mammals had drunk.

The water left for me would be what held between grains, what darkened the underside of a pebble. I would extend into it slowly. My sides would open. Dust would lift from the folds and drift a little distance away.

Nera might still be there with the lamp.

She would see something small become longer at the edge of the wet patch. Perhaps she would recognize me; perhaps, by then, she would have found enough of us to stop trying.

She would move her boot before setting it down.

I would feel the impact farther away, and continue feeding.
