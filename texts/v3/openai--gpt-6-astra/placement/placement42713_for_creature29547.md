At first I would be in one of the quiet alcoves, close to the entrance, where the downward scales end. Farther inside, the floor is almost still. A little way outside, there are too many movements to distinguish. Here a narrow part of the basin’s noise comes through clearly.

I would have one foot beyond the threshold.

For a while that is all I can make out about myself: the sheltered body, the foot in the open, the need to stay between two conditions. Rain is coming. There is no smell of it yet, and the light has not changed, but something beneath the entrance draws tight, holds, then eases.

I lift the foot.

The tightening continues through the others. It is coming from farther under me than I thought.

I put the foot down in a different place.

***

My body is high above the floor. There is a substantial interval between noticing somewhere and arriving there with my underside. I must arrange myself around the descent.

Two legs open toward the entrance. Another finds the thicker collar where the wall begins. The remaining pair take my weight while I lower, and then my weight changes; there is less of it in the legs, more in the folds that have begun to part beneath me.

The floor here is not suitable.

I stand again.

Nothing has hurt. There is only a refusal somewhere in the meeting surfaces, a slight roughness that would become worse if I stayed. Outside, rain begins to fall in the cups.

I leave the alcove.

My feet are broad enough to keep me on top of the wet nap. Each releases at its own pace. One of my rear feet releases more slowly than the others, so that my body has already begun its next arrangement when a last piece of me is still attached to the previous one.

This gives my walk a small hesitation.

I am accustomed to it. On a long crossing I turn that leg a little outward. When the floor is especially wet, I have to stop and gather myself over it.

The rain fills the shallow films beside my head. Things grow broad and uncertain. I tip the water out, and the legs of an animal ahead of me become separate again.

It waits while I free my foot.

***

I would live mostly along a channel that passes east of the central pools. From across the basin, there would be nothing to distinguish my stretch of bank. Up close there are three places where the cuff folds under itself, and a small overhang beneath which water moves uphill for the breadth of a foot before disappearing into a pore.

I often stand there.

The movement is easy to feel. It reaches my feet unevenly: a short pull, another, then a longer interval in which the tissue swells. Sometimes I move a few steps along the channel and find the same sequence arriving in a different order.

There are days when I follow it all the way to the pools.

There are days when I remain beside the pore until evening.

Another animal comes to this bank. It has a dark branching mark beneath its skin, though that is not how I recognize it first. When its foot meets mine, it settles a little too much weight into the contact and then withdraws some. It has always done this. I brace before it happens.

We stand together with our heads turned apart.

Along the touching edges of our feet, a pressure begins, ceases, begins again. My slow foot rests elsewhere. Beneath it, the channel continues its short pulls.

After the other animal leaves, I go down to the second fold of the bank and lower myself.

The fringes open. Coolness enters them despite the warmth of the ground. For a time I am occupied by the many separate points of contact; then the separations become difficult to locate.

Water pushes out around me.

The light shifts over the pool. Somewhere under its surface, a contraction starts and travels past.

***

There is an old place on the bank where I fit badly now.

It lies beneath an overhang, close enough to the wall that one side of my body must remain raised. I can press part of my underside against it, but not all. When I stand, a crescent stays dry among the damp marks.

I return there anyway.

The wall has a low swelling. Above it, a pale vein divides into three branches, one so fine that it disappears when cloud passes over the sun. I used to climb to that division. My feet would find purchase on either side, and I would remain there until an adult placed a leg against the wall.

The descent was difficult. I remember the long reach to the leg, then the warm yielding surface moving slightly beneath me.

Now, when I put my head under the overhang, my back touches its lower edge.

Once there is a small animal on the wall.

It moves sideways as I approach, fast enough that two of its feet lose contact together. The others hold. Its body hangs crookedly for a moment before it draws itself close again.

I stand beside the swelling.

After a while I place a leg against the wall.

The small animal stays where it is. I wait until the light has withdrawn from the finest vein. Then it comes down in a rush, catches itself above my joint, and stops.

Its feet make five little areas of pressure, painfully distinct.

I keep the leg still.

***

I would not be especially bold.

At the southern rail I remain farther inland than some of the others. The floor there carries a wide, slow motion that troubles my descent. Whenever I begin to lower myself, the motion seems to come up to meet me, and I stand again.

But I go there after heavy rain.

The canopy drops strike far apart. Between them, the seam beneath the rail creaks steadily. I can feel that sound through a foot placed near the upturned edge.

Once, a visitor sits nearby with a hand resting on the rail.

The hand presses. A pale place appears.

I turn toward it.

The visitor presses again, watches the color return, and then lays the whole hand flat.

I approach from the side. The visitor’s foot does not open under its weight. It is enclosed in something that carries very little of the floor’s movement, and when I touch it, the visitor draws away.

I wait.

Eventually the foot returns.

I set one folded lobe against its side and begin the small pressure I use with the dark-lined animal. There is no answer I recognize. But after several repetitions, the visitor shifts, and a brief uneven force comes back through the covering.

I remain until the canopy has shed most of its water.

When I leave, the visitor follows for a short distance. Their steps arrive heavily through the floor, then stop.

***

The dry depression is farther from my bank than it looks.

My slow foot makes the last part of the journey tiring. I pause above the shallow side before going down. Others are already there, distributed around a space that I cannot judge properly from the slope.

One moves aside.

I descend into the place it has made available.

The bridge hangs above us. Water has gathered beneath its rounded end, although no channel crosses it. A drop lengthens and falls between two bodies.

I turn my head inward with the others.

For a while I keep all my feet beneath me. Then I extend one into the open space and touch the floor with the lobe tips.

There is the fine nap, the warmth, a faint movement from a channel well below. I hold the contact. Beside me, another animal makes a small adjustment, and I adjust too.

We stay until the shadows of our legs have moved across one another.

I do not go directly home afterward. I stop in an alcove, with one foot outside.

***

Near morning, beside my channel, I am often the last to rise.

The fringes close slowly. My legs have taken my weight, but a narrow part of the underside remains joined. If I lift too soon, it pulls sharply along my flank.

The dark-lined animal waits a little way off.

I settle a fraction lower.

The remaining contact softens and draws out. Through it comes one last small thickening, enough to make me still.

Then the strand breaks.

For a moment I hold my slow foot above the damp oval. The bank is slightly raised there. Water gathers along its lower edge and spills into the channel.

I touch the place once before we go.
