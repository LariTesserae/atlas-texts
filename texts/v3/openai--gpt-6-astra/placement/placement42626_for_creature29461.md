I would be low down, where the bridge interrupts the view of the gardens.

From there its three bones would not look slender. They would be a pale roof with narrow seams, and the curve of the handrail would be hidden above them. The maker’s face would lie beyond the supports, enormous and faint. Through their cheek I could see the salt. Their eyes would obscure two portions of the room.

At first I think I would belong to the crack beneath the bridge. There is so little darkness elsewhere that has not been given an interior: the chambers of the stems, the coils inside the animals, the pierced center of the thing in the pocket. The crack offers darkness without an enclosing body. I could be something broad and shallow there, pressed between its sides, listening with my whole length.

But I would want the underside of the bridge within reach.

And there would be feet.

Very small feet, folded close to a body that at first resembles a rough place on the bone. Four near the head, two farther back. Between the pairs, a soft abdomen held clear of the surface. I would have a shell, though only over part of me: two translucent plates that fail to meet along the back. Through their interval, a dark organ would be visible. It would have the shape of a thread laid down in a hurry.

I would be tucked beside the farther support.

The crossed turn of fiber would be directly overhead.

That is where I find myself most clearly: beneath the faulty joint, with one foot resting on the short end of the lashing.

My foot would divide into several pads, too soft to grip an edge by force. Each pad would accommodate what it touched. Against bone, it would be almost flat. Against the fiber, it would have a deep crease. There would be no claws. A creature like me could move over a leaf without marking it, though here the distinction between having a foot and taking a step would be very large.

One of my rear legs would be extended.

I would not know whether it had been about to follow the others or withdraw from them. Its posture would contain both possibilities without discomfort.

The maker’s hand would be close enough to cover the entire bridge.

From the entrance that hand looks delicate, its divided thumb made for exact work. From underneath, I would see the breadth of it, the squared nails, the dark deposits in the creases. One fingertip would have a loose husk fiber caught against its side. The grain of the palm would be as elaborate as the gardens.

I would have no reason to be afraid of it. Nor would I possess a history of being safe.

There would simply be the hand, with its underside turned partly toward me, and my exposed back beneath it.

My mouth would be a short seam behind the foremost legs. It would hold a sound too low for so small a body: a soft, coarse note, like something being drawn across the end of a hollow stick. The bridge would give it a particular breadth. Heard from the basin, it might seem to come from inside the bone.

I would hear the maker through that same bone.

Their pleasure in the bridge would reach me with the roughness of their dissatisfaction still in it. I would not separate these into feelings. They would be features of the place where I lived, as distinct and inseparable as the polished top of the rail and its unpolished underside.

There is something I would have that the maker could not see.

Beneath my shell, against the soft portion of my back, there would be a little hollow. It would contain a salt grain. A single grain, clear except for one milk-white corner.

It would not be food or an egg. I would have no use for it that could be inferred from my legs or mouth. Perhaps my body had closed around it. Perhaps I had pushed beneath it when the grain was supported between two others, and it had remained with me.

The shell would fit less well because of it.

I would be aware of that pressure. It would occupy a small, exact place in the enormous chord: my own hardness, carried inside the parts of me that yielded.

I do not think I would want it removed.

The imperfect lashing would make a ledge. My forward foot would rest there, its pads fitted around the crossing of the fibers. The maker could not correct the joint without changing the surface beneath me.

This would not make the mistake fortunate. The raised turn would still catch a hand passing along the rail. The maker would still be right to notice it.

But under the bridge it would be part of a room.

There would be a roof, a pale column, an opening toward the black basin. There would be a place for my feet. Farther away, the low animal would hold its young, and the comb would keep its measured intervals among the leaves.

I would not be the creature tied to the maker’s garment. No braid would lead from me to anyone.

Still, one end of the fiber beneath my foot would pass over the rail and return through the knot toward their waiting finger. I could follow it with several eyes without moving my head.

I would live there, on the underside of something carefully made.

Above me, almost touching it, would be the hand that wanted to make it better.
