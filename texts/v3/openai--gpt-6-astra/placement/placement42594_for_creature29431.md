I would be beneath the lowest terrace, between the bark facing and the old skin. Not inside the skin at first. There is a little space under one of its lifted edges, where a leg once joined the body. Silt has collected there in a direction different from the silt outside.

I would fit without disturbing either deposit.

For some time, that would be all I could tell about myself: where I fitted, and what did not have to move.

The white animal would pass close enough for its feet to pull at the fibers above me. I would feel each pull separately. The fourth pair would make a different pressure, softer and more prolonged. After it had gone, a blue particle would remain caught against the skin’s edge.

I would reach toward it.

What reached would be almost clear, with a bend not quite in the middle. The end would flatten against the clay particle and stay there. I would have no reason to bring it closer. Touching it would occupy me for a long time.

By the time I let go, there would be a shallow crease across my end.

That crease would stay.

***

I think I would have difficulty finding out which movements were mine.

A leaf would draw taut overhead, and I would feel stretched. Somewhere beyond the terrace, a soft joint would loosen, and something near me would become easier. But I could wait for these things to happen again and they would not. The next tightening of the leaf would be only a leaf tightening.

Once, while trying to reach the blue particle a second time, I would move a grain of silt behind myself.

I would practice that.

Not because it was useful. The grain would travel less than its own width, then return when I rested. But it would move every time.

Eventually there would be a clean place underneath it.

I would keep this small place clear, even after I could move farther.

***

There is food beneath the terrace. A soft rim on the old skin, a little darkening where two plates overlap. I would find it by resting against it. Afterward I would be less transparent, although there would be no light with which to discover this.

My first hunger would arrive after my first meal.

I would go back to the same place and find a firmer surface. For a while I would continue to feed there anyway. Then I would learn to follow the softened edge around the bend.

The old skin is larger inside than it looks from where I began. There are places where I can spread without touching both sides. I would not like those places immediately. I would cross them with one end of myself still holding the margin, until the distance became too great.

Then I would have to let go.

Nothing much would happen. I would arrive against another surface.

Still, afterward, I would rest.

***

When the louse came beneath the terrace, I would withdraw into a fold near the empty head.

It would enter slowly, testing the old joints with its good antenna. Its body would exclude the little water that had lain loosely around me. For the first time I would feel a movement that passed over my whole surface.

I would contract.

The louse would stop.

For a long interval we would remain like that, with the old skin between us in some places and nothing between us in others.

Then it would browse along a seam.

I would feel the seam becoming thinner. Not pain, but something close enough that I would move before I knew where to go. My creased end would come down against one of its plates.

The louse would continue feeding.

I would keep touching it.

When it left, I would follow as far as the terrace edge. Beyond it, a filament would be stretched across the opening. I could pass beneath the filament, but not without losing contact with the skin.

I would stay there until the louse was gone.

***

I would not be another white animal waiting to harden. At least, the hardening would not come.

Instead, one of my bends would become reliable. I could rest my weight on it. Another part would grow fine enough to enter the bark, where there are passages that do not reach either face.

I would learn the underside of the terrace this way.

There is a place where the bark is coming loose. I would press against it when knocks came through the clay. Sometimes I would feel another pad pressing farther along. Sometimes I would have to hold alone.

Between disturbances, I would leave.

I would go back to the blue particle. I would feed. I would explore a passage and turn around before its end. Once I would carry a shred of softened skin almost to the entrance, losing it twice and finding it by touch.

The white louse would be cleaning its antennae beneath the curl of bark.

Beside it there would be room.

I would enter with the shred held against the crease in my end. The water there would be colder than beneath the terrace, and faintly troubled by movements outside. I would take some time finding a position in which I could rest.

The louse would shift one leg.

I would draw closer, then stop.

Behind us, beneath the lowest terrace, the grain of silt would slide back into the place I had kept clear.
