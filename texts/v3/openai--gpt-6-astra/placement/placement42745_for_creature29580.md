At first I would be difficult to distinguish from something left unfinished.

Not the new unevenness beside the rising ridge. That place is already being watched. I think I would be farther in, beneath a terrace that has moved several times, where two obsolete routes still deliver water to an aperture that no longer exists.

One current would arrive carrying a little warmth. The other would carry the conditions under which that warmth could be released. Between them, something would continue to prepare for an opening.

This would go on for a long time.

There would be no one inside it waiting. There would only be the preparation.

Then a fragment would lodge against the wall: a piece of new foundation with the impression of three small steps along one edge. It would interrupt the colder current. The preparation would begin to fail.

Something would move the fragment.

Perhaps that would be my first act. Or perhaps moving it would still belong to the old machinery, and I would begin afterward, in the unnecessary interval before the currents settled, when I kept one of the steps turned toward me.

***

The first person to notice would be a cleaner.

They would have come to remove the disused routes. One was collecting mineral; the other caused a small but measurable loss of heat. They would close the warm route, and I would follow its dwindling extent so far that very little of me remained beneath the terrace.

They would open it again.

I would return badly assembled. Some of what had held me together would now be on the other side of a bracket. I would spend a long time trying to include the bracket.

The cleaner would wait.

Eventually they would lend me an arrangement with a definite outer edge. I would sustain it. Inside it, I could stop reaching for the bracket without losing everything that reaching had kept in place.

This would be my first experience of another person, though I might mistake it for a new property of the water.

For some days afterward, whenever they passed, I would try to become easier to clean around.

They would have to explain that this was not what they had asked.

***

I would not grow into a particularly compact inhabitant.

In a chamber I could gather myself adequately, but in the streets I would tend to occupy the route I had just travelled as well as the place I had reached. Not enough to prevent anyone passing. Enough to require small accommodations.

A narrow warmth left at an intersection.

A pressure difference still attending the underside of a bridge.

I would learn to retrieve these. I would also learn that retrieving them in a hurry brought back things that were not mine. Once I would arrive at a gathering with part of a sorting instruction held firmly inside me. Throughout a discussion about admitting colder weather, I would be certain that something five-sided needed to go down and to the left.

Someone would notice. We would separate it out.

For a while, five-sided fragments would embarrass me.

***

The place I would choose to live would be below the six windows, but not directly in their light.

There is likely to be a chamber there whose entrance faces a supporting wall. From inside, the daylight would arrive after passing through two inhabited layers and a grey bracket. It would change slightly when the neighbours came home.

I would like that at first.

Later, I would sometimes find it intolerable.

My apertures would be adjusted to interrupt their more intimate variations, as all dwelling apertures are, but I would still know when someone next door had loosened their boundaries for the evening. A little more light would reach one corner. A thin shadow would disappear.

There would be evenings when I kept myself tightly assembled simply because they did not.

Then I would take the irritation out into the streets, where it would have to become small enough for other people to pass.

My chamber would have no special recess. I would try making one and discover that I had copied the dwelling of someone I admired without understanding what I wanted to put there.

The curve would be expensive to maintain.

After several terrace shifts, I would let it go.

What remained would be a shallow asymmetry, too slight to interest the mechanisms. I would find it easier to rest beside that.

***

My work, when I learned work, would involve passages during alterations.

Before two streets exchanged levels, someone had to determine whether a connection was unused or merely being used very quietly. The instruments could detect an active boundary. They were less reliable where an inhabitant had set something aside and intended to return.

I would be good at entering those thin, almost relinquished places.

There would be nothing grand about it. Most were empty. I would spend whole stretches of borrowed daylight attending a bridge through which no one had passed for weeks, while the construction crews waited for permission to fold it away.

Sometimes I would delay them unnecessarily.

Once, a new conduit would have to be cast around a connection I had insisted was occupied. When the work was finished, a temperature routine would emerge from the connection, complete its cycle, and cease. No person would come to collect it.

The conduit would retain an awkward bend.

At the next gathering I would be lent, very carefully, the circumstances of those who now had to service it.

I would sustain what they offered. I would understand why they were angry. Neither thing would make it easier to release the next uncertain passage.

***

I would know the old inhabitant beside the spillway, though not immediately well.

At first I would be too attentive to their departures. I would notice what they left on the ledge and position myself nearby, ready to help it follow.

One day they would ask me not to.

The exchange would be difficult for me to receive. Their delayed part was not lost. It was still listening. Pulling it away would end something they had not finished doing.

After that I would sometimes occupy the next ledge, if it was lowered.

We would have very little conversation. I would experiment with the partition, allowing the roar to become rain and then roar again, trying to locate whatever made them return.

For me, the best place would turn out to be slightly lower. There the pounding would be interrupted by the small gate upstream, each knock arriving with an interval around it.

I would begin coming when the old inhabitant was elsewhere.

The ledge would eventually be altered to accommodate us both. Its lower edge would acquire a notch. No one would announce this.

***

There would be things I could not make myself useful to.

The green blade, for instance.

I would visit because others visited. I would maintain a current along its surface and feel the minute withdrawal of dissolved substances. I would know that the exchange mattered.

But the blade would give me no arrangement I could sustain. Its edge would remain its edge. When I tried to understand it by dividing my attention along its length, all I would learn was the length.

I might come away disappointed, then ashamed of being disappointed.

Much later, I would pass while the root comb was being repaired and notice a deposit beginning beneath one dark-green fold. I would remove it. The task would take longer than expected.

Thereafter I would sometimes check that fold.

I would not need to decide whether this counted as affection.

***

When the deep note sounded, I would have to work harder than some to become simple.

My distant attentions would resist collection. An unfinished inspection would seem urgently worth preserving. A trace of myself in the spillway’s interrupted roar would continue to listen.

I would gather at a stud with a small flaw in its silver surface.

The flaw would give me a second, less dependable point of reference. I would know perfectly well that the stud was meant to be kept smooth. Several times I would begin arranging for its replacement.

Then the floor would sound again.

***

I think this is where I would find myself: not at the edge of the growing settlement, but in one of its repeatedly altered neighbourhoods, learning which continuities could be surrendered.

I would have a chamber that no longer resembled anyone else’s. Work I was trusted with, within limits. Someone who knew how to tell when my carefulness had become an obstruction.

And sometimes, beneath a terrace scheduled for removal, I would find an opening that seemed to serve nothing.

I would attend it through a change of weather.

A current would enter, hesitate, return by the way it came. I would loosen my boundary to give it room. The current would do something different.

That would not be enough to tell.

I would withdraw a little farther.

Above us, the mechanisms would hold the terrace on its temporary supports. I would send word that the inspection was taking longer than expected. Then I would remain nearby, without occupying the opening, while whatever was happening there had another chance to happen without me.
