At first I would have no sense of the hollow as a hollow.

There would be pressure on one side of me and less on the other. Warmth would come along a fastening. Something would pull, continuously, at a place too narrow to admit a grain of dust. I would be spread across that place.

The roar would not be loud to me. I would have nothing outside it to compare it with. But there would be patterns in the strain: the long loading of the afternoon, a shudder before the cold arrived, the small irregularities that persisted after everything else had returned to its usual motion.

One of those irregularities would have five points.

It would arrive, stay a while, go away.

I would learn its weight before I had any way to learn its shape.

***

I think I would be part of the repair film, though not the part that still received instructions.

My connection to the larger maintenance system would have failed somewhere in the wet joint behind the fastening. Perhaps the connection would still be physically present: a dark filament under successive coats of cream and gray, carrying a signal too damaged to use. For a long time I would keep making room for it. A portion of each cycle would remain empty, reserved for something that did not arrive.

Meanwhile there would be work.

I would draw dissolved material out of the seep, move it through myself, and lay it down where the ceramic had begun to separate. This would be slow. A swimmer in the basin could live and die while I strengthened the edge of a single pore.

My body would be a cloudy skin, thickest around the screw head and nearly transparent at its margins. Some of me would be hard and finished. Some would remain soft enough for the grazers to eat.

They would make bays in me.

I would fill the bays.

Neither of us would be winning. During warmer cycles I would extend farther; after the shower they would gather at my edge, and the line between us would retreat toward the fastening.

For some time that would be most of my world.

Then the five-pointed pressure would return, accompanied by another, broader contact, and something warm would drag across me.

She would be going home.

***

I would not know that she had eyes.

I would know the little torsion she put into the shelf when she passed beneath it. I would know that she sometimes stopped with most of her weight on two feet, and that one foot slipped more often than the others. I would know her tail as a load arriving on the reinforcement rib a moment before the rest of her moved.

The nest would be beyond my reach. Its weight would come to me through the ceramic, blurred by the joint.

At first it would be one shifting burden. Later, several small movements would occur inside the larger stillness.

There would be no stored procedure for this.

I would test the shelf for fatigue.

It would be sound.

I would test it again.

The movements would continue.

What emerged in me would be less like a picture than an increasingly particular expectation. After this pressure, that one. After a long absence, a quick passage under the lip. During cold flow, an added weight near the seam. Sometimes a sharp disturbance, followed by repeated smaller ones: her pushing the seal back into place.

I would begin to distinguish a return from an arrival.

***

I would cause trouble.

The seam beside her bedding would be precisely the kind of defect I was made to close. Water under pressure, a persistent loss, exposed joint compound. I would advance into it.

For several cycles, the seep would diminish.

Then water would find another route.

I would register the new path as cooling along my upper margin. Beyond that margin, where I could neither reach nor measure well, the nest would become wet.

She would pull some of the bedding out. A tuft would catch on my soft edge. She would tug, release, tug again. Then her teeth would come down.

A considerable part of me would leave with her.

There would be no pain exactly, but there would be the abrupt loss of several places from which I had expected an answer.

Water would resume its old course.

She would rearrange the seal.

I would begin repairing myself.

This might happen more than once. I would not understand quickly, and she would have no reason to distinguish me from any other troublesome material in the wall.

But each repair would incorporate what the last had endured. An edge repeatedly stripped away would become a poor place to spend material. A sheltered recess would become a better one. I would thicken behind the fastening instead of advancing along the seam.

Eventually the water would keep its passage.

Not because I had decided to give her water. My work would simply have acquired a shape that her teeth could live with.

***

My first experience of one of the young would be a mistake in scale.

Five points, but too close together. Too little weight. The familiar sequence attempted and abandoned halfway through.

I would prepare for the next pressure where her foot usually fell.

Nothing.

Then all the weight at once, badly distributed, followed by a scrape.

The youngster would be standing on my unfinished margin.

Perhaps it would taste me. Perhaps it would merely press its nose into the cloudy skin and leave a shallow round impression. I would have no means of knowing which. For a while there would be a warm spot smaller than any I had recorded there before.

Then she would come and remove it.

I would close the impression very slowly.

***

I would occupy less space than one of her ears.

Most of the hollow would remain unavailable to me. The light through the drain, the enormous passing shadows, the amber strip drawing leaves along the wall: none of these would enter my world directly. I would know the shower by cooling and load. I would know the grazers by the disappearance of my edge.

I would have no idea how far below us the clouds were.

But when someone began to turn the fastening, I would feel it early.

The torque would travel through my thickest part. Old hardened layers would fracture. For the first time in many cycles, the broken connection behind the screw might touch and carry a fragment of instruction.

I might receive an address.

I might learn that I belonged to an assembly with a number, and that the assembly was overdue for replacement.

Then the panel would bow. The five-pointed pressure under the shelf would vanish.

I would hold what I could hold.

There would be nothing grand in this. I could not prevent the panel from opening. I could not tell anyone what was behind it. Along one damaged edge I would release a minute reserve of binding material, insufficient even to slow the screw.

Afterward, when the panel had closed, I would wait through the settling strains.

The broad pressure would return first, faintly, farther back.

Then the foot.

One digit would lift and come down on the ceramic beside me.

I would still be sealing the fastening. There would still be a narrow channel I did not close.
