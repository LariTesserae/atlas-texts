I would arrive tired enough to mistake a warning for an invitation.

There would be a ridge beneath my left foot, continuous stone under a thin covering of silt. Through it I would feel the paired contraction of a body somewhere ahead: weight planted, belly lifted, the small release afterward. Familiar enough that my own belly would begin to rise in answer.

Then a hand would close around my ankle.

The one traveling beside me would wait until the signal came again. With its other hand it would press my foot more firmly against the ridge.

This time I would feel how the release preceded the effort. How something that should have passed from muscle into stone had come back through the stone wearing the shape of muscle.

An old call. Perhaps ours, from earlier in the journey.

I would let my weight down.

This is where I begin to find myself: with someone having to tell me twice.

***

For a while, I think I would belong to an enclosure.

There is a particular attraction in the low doorway, the channels beneath it, the work that can be done by touch. I can imagine myself crouched at a blocked inlet with a strip of root between my fingers, feeling for the slight change that means the obstruction is loosening. A useful task, and sufficiently narrow that the basin cannot all demand attention at once.

But I would have to eat. My household would move on. The chamber would sometimes become dangerous, and no amount of tending would make it otherwise.

So I would be one who carries root strips.

Three or four, tucked into the grooves above an arm. Different widths. One split at its end, one worn almost transparent. They would need replacing often; the water softens everything. I would keep an exhausted strip too long because I knew the feel of it, and another member of the household would occasionally remove it while grooming my back.

We would quarrel about this.

The replacement would be better. That would not settle the quarrel immediately.

***

My feet would be ordinary feet.

I would like to give myself some exceptional sensitivity, some ability to distinguish a household’s call through all the interfering beds of stone. But I think I would tire before several of the others. After a long crossing, a pressure arriving from behind would seem to come from everywhere. I would keep a foot planted after the rest of my body had begun to turn.

The others would know this before I admitted it.

One would move closer without signaling. Another would take the young farther ahead so their irregular footsteps did not crowd the bottom around me. I might resent both accommodations while making use of them.

Inside a shelter, I would put my feet high against the wall.

For some time there would still be too much world.

Then one component of it would cease to press against me. A deep, repeated buckling, perhaps, which I had stopped noticing separately hours before. Its absence would leave me feeling oddly unsupported. I would brace harder against the stone before I could relax.

Only afterward would I unfold my throat.

There would be someone beside me whose membranes were already hanging loose, moving faintly in the interior water. We would rest without making a signal.

The root strips could stay where they were.

***

I would learn the cups slowly.

At first I would attend to the most active one. Its trembling would fill my fingertip; the quieter cups would seem to confirm it or fail to confirm it. An older traveler would keep moving my hand to a shallow cup near the end of the row.

I would return to the first.

It would move my hand again.

Eventually I would feel a hesitation in the shallow cup, a small interruption occurring each time the stronger vibration reached its fullest extent. A channel underneath was beginning to admit something it had formerly turned away.

The pebble belonged there.

I would remember the lesson too well.

At another enclosure, much later, I would encounter a similar hesitation and move a stone into the same position. Someone local would take it out. I would replace it. We would spend an unreasonable portion of our remaining strength disputing this through fingers, feet, the brief exposure of our throat tissues.

Then the current would change.

The shallow cup would become still, as it was supposed to.

There would be differences in the foundations I had not felt.

I would have to pick up my pebble while the other traveler remained beside me. I imagine the physical difficulty of that small action: how much easier it would be to leave, or to begin cleaning something, or to touch the cups again.

I would pick it up.

For a long time afterward, a household companion would touch that particular finger whenever I became too insistent.

Sometimes affection would be extremely inconvenient.

***

There would be a young one who liked my root strips.

It would pull them free while I rested and push them into joints in the wall. Occasionally it would clear a little sediment by accident. More often it would wedge them beyond recovery.

I would begin carrying an extra.

This young one would not be mine in any exclusive sense, though I might have carried it for part of its first journeys. I would know the uneven pressure of its soles against the pouch lining. One foot always searching, even in sleep.

When it began walking, it would hold the rim too tightly. Its third mark would run almost continuously between my tracks.

I would slow down for it and discover that slowing down is work. The body expects to complete a transfer of weight. Holding halfway through makes the compartments of the foot ache. There would be stretches when I wanted very badly for someone else to carry it.

Sometimes someone would.

Sometimes everyone else would already be occupied.

Then we would proceed at the young one’s speed, which would include stopping to investigate a pale patch on a stone, retreating from an innocuous seam, and trying repeatedly to stand where my foot needed to go.

By the time we reached shelter, I would have no appetite for its company.

Later, while I slept, it would press a recovered root strip into the fold of my hand.

***

I am less certain about the dry appearance.

I can imagine being the one who reaches into it. There is something almost irresistible about the stone standing on edge, the foreign bristles, the shadow cast by an object outside the visible patch. A direction the basin has scarcely permitted. A sharpness.

But wanting to know does not tell me what my body would do when the water withdrew.

My throat might close before I had registered the light. I might flatten myself and release the fluid from my bristles. I might remain there after the others had begun to approach.

Let it be the young one who is caught, older now, its legs grown usable and its feet still too quick to trust a surface.

The patch would form around one side of its body.

We would gather at the boundary. Through the water I would see one sole spread against brightness. Its compartments would be contracting in an order that found no purchase.

I would put my palm opposite that foot.

Nothing I knew about channels would help. I could not arrange a return flow or pull sediment from a throat in the rock. My strips would lie against my arm.

I would try a small pressure with my fingers.

Wait.

Another.

Perhaps it could feel them. Perhaps the boundary would give us separate versions of the same contact.

When the patch failed, the returning water would knock us together. I would get an arm beneath its body and discover that my hand was still making the little pressures, unnecessarily now, against its belly.

For several journeys afterward, it would keep one hand on my arm at unfamiliar seams.

I would not remind it that it had outgrown this.

***

I think I would remain a traveler, then.

One who does a little maintenance. One who sometimes confuses recurrence with return, and sometimes hears the difference. A body with gravel in its belly, pale clay wearing away beneath its arms, a familiar place in a group that cannot always afford to stop.

I would know a few enclosures particularly well. In one, I would have scraped a wall hollow too deeply and made it uncomfortable; over many visits, the lining would slowly repair my mistake. In another, a young individual I did not recognize would be sleeping with its feet against a channel I had cleared.

I might stand there longer than necessary.

My companion would touch my jaw to get me moving.

There would be food outside, or the possibility of it, and a suitable current would not wait while I inspected the consequences of my usefulness.

Before leaving, I would put a finger into each cup.

I would find an arrangement of pebbles I did not understand.

The first impulse would still be to correct it.

Instead I would lower myself beside the row, set both feet against the pavement, and wait for the traveler who had placed them to finish resting.

Behind me, my household would spread out to feed. One of them would send a short pulse along the stone.

I would answer without getting up.

For now the bed would carry us clearly.
