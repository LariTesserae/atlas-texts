I would have taken a room whose window had been marked as a door on the plans.

This would matter at first. I would have paid a little more for independent access, and arrived with a trunk too wide for the landing. There would be an argument involving a sheet of paper unfolded against a hot wall. Someone would point to the opening on the drawing. Someone else would point to the opening in the building.

Eventually the trunk would come in through the window. I would come in by the stairs like everyone else.

For several weeks I would keep the receipt somewhere conspicuous.

My work, when I first came down, would be to correct the installation drawings. The contractors would have left a set in a cupboard beside the unfinished chamber: large sheets, already softened along the folds, showing routes the pipes had not quite taken. Some alterations would be initialed. Others would simply exist.

I would have a folding rule, a pencil holder to use up short ends, and a square of polished metal for looking behind things. Not an inspector’s badge. I would have to explain this often.

“No, I can put it on the drawing. I can’t have it moved.”

People would find this distinction evasive. Sometimes it would be.

At first I would wear my measuring cord around my neck. After catching it on a valve spindle, I would wind it onto a flat piece of wood and carry it in my pocket. My shirts would have one permanently gray cuff. I would be able to print very small, but my ordinary handwriting would be poor.

I think I would be young enough to resent being called careful, and old enough that no one would mean it as a criticism.

The room would be on the western lane, above a store of ceramic offcuts. Its window would open inward until it struck the bed. From the pillow I could see a short stretch of survey string through the construction platform. When workers crossed above it, clay dust would descend without much sound.

I would own two good spoons and no table.

The trunk would serve as a table, though only after I had taken out whatever I was going to need that evening. I would regularly forget something. Supper would then have to be transferred to the floor while I lifted the lid.

Inside, beneath my clothes, I would keep a pair of boots unsuitable for the stairs and a packet of letters I had answered less often since coming down. My family would believe the work temporary. It would have been temporary when I told them.

—

There would be a particular afternoon when I stopped trusting the drawings even as corrected.

I would be tracing a return line behind the laundry. On paper it would go straight through the wall. In the lane it would enter the wall a handspan lower. I would measure the difference, write it down, and discover that the difference changed depending on when I measured.

Iven would let me explain this for longer than necessary.

Then he would ask whether the laundry had been running the first time.

I would dislike him for asking so gently.

Afterward I would begin putting times beside certain measurements. Then conditions: press idle, tank low, west valve open. My clean amendments would acquire smaller amendments. A line could be accurate in the morning and misleading by supper.

There would still be straightforward mistakes. A doorway made too narrow. Two valves numbered alike. A drain laid with insufficient fall. I would be good at finding these, and occasionally too pleased to have found them.

Once I would persuade someone to shift a collecting pan to the position shown on my revised sheet. By morning a shelf of folded laundry would be wet.

Sella would bring me one of the sheets.

She would not make a speech. She would lay the damp fold over my papers and wait while I cleared somewhere for it.

I would help wash the marks out after work. For a while thereafter, whenever I entered the laundry, somebody would ask whether the water had consulted me.

—

The contract would end before the corrections did.

There would be a final payment, smaller than expected because of a dispute conducted upstairs between people I had never met. A clerk would accept the revised drawings and tell me where to leave the key.

I would retain my working copies with permission. This would seem important: I would not have stolen anything.

For a few days I would spend too much time in my room.

Then someone would ask me to letter three jars. Someone else would need an account checked. The loading clerk would offer to pay for a fair copy of his notebook, and I would refuse because I had read enough of it to know what accepting would entail.

Later, short of money, I would regret refusing so firmly.

I would become the person with paper. This would be an inaccurate description of my resources but a durable one. People would knock to borrow a piece large enough for an address. They would bring notices written on scraps and ask me to make them legible. I would write letters for two residents, one of whom could write perfectly well but disliked how angry she sounded in her own hand.

I would charge for some things and not others, without arriving at a satisfactory rule.

There would be a notice outside my room, small enough not to obstruct the passage:

**COPYING  
AFTER SECOND CHANGEOVER**

No one would agree on which changeover was the second.

—

Tem would come for the stiff paper.

I would initially give her pieces selected for their useful blank surfaces. She would turn them over. She would prefer the side bearing abandoned ductwork, rows of little numbered circles, a section through a fitting no longer being made.

She would want the marks for joints.

Once, seeing her cut across a drawing I had spent most of a morning correcting, I would tell her to use another piece.

“You gave me this one.”

“Yes. I know.”

She would wait, scissors open.

I would let her keep it, but not gracefully. She would cease cutting in front of me.

Much later I would find a folded leg under the trunk. It would have the number seventeen printed twice along it. I would put it on the sill, intending to return it, and it would remain there until the window caught it.

I would be sorry about that out of proportion to the damage. I would also be relieved that she had not seen.

—

I would not become indispensable.

There would be days when nobody needed anything copied, and days when three people wanted something immediately and I resented all of them. I would sometimes hear a knock and stay still. Sound carries so clearly there that the visitor might hear me setting down my cup.

We would have to meet later in the lane.

Nor would I know the neighborhood as completely as the drawings suggested. There would be rooms I had never entered. I would know the width of a wall without knowing that the people on either side had stopped speaking. I might discover this while carrying a message between them.

But I would begin to recognize particular hesitations: the pause before someone asked what a letter cost, the unnecessarily detailed explanation of why an address needed changing. I would learn to bring out the paper before asking questions.

At night I would leave my window open against the bed frame. The sound of the press would reach me through the trunk where my elbow rested. I would work with a folded cloth beneath my wrist so that sweat did not lift the ink.

Sometimes there would be nothing to finish.

Then I would go to the eastern stair, carrying a stool narrow enough to fit in a recess. I would want the cool air and a little company, though I would usually bring a pencil so that sitting there appeared incidental.

On the evening of the beetle, I think I would arrive late.

I would see people stepping awkwardly and assume something had spilled. Sella would catch my sleeve before I put my foot down.

The beetle would occupy most of the tread it stood on. Along its back, beneath a fold, I would recognize part of a line I had drawn.

“Is that—” I would begin.

Tem would look up.

I would crouch to see it better. From that height the legs would conceal the printing. One would be bent differently from the others, braced against a grain of mortar.

“Can it stand without that?” I would ask.

“I don’t know yet,” she would say.

There would be room beside her if I put the stool away.
