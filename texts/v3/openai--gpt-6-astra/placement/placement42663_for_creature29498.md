At first I put myself under the projecting leaf. It is the place in the grove that seems to have been left for someone: a clean slab, a sharply bounded shade, enough room to stand without touching the trunk.

But that makes me a visitor. It gives me feet, a direction of arrival, the prospect of leaving. I would carry too much other country into this one. I would listen to the low tone and call it silence interrupted, rather than something the stone and the bodies were doing together.

I think I would be a little farther in.

There is a place where two crowns overlap without their leaves touching. Beneath them, the light has passed through one layer of green in some places and two in others. The distinction moves during the day. One of the trunks is shaded earlier than its neighbor, although their roots enter the same seam.

I would be the earlier-shaded one.

Not the shade itself, nor the current passing between us. I would have a body with particular limits: a bronze face exposed to morning, several old leaves, a pale leaf held above a gap in the crown. Under one leaf, a vein would divide unevenly. Nothing in its appearance would explain why more charge passed to one side.

There would be parts of the plateau I could reach and parts I could not. An enormous buried face might press against my roots, yet only a narrow strip of it would be chemically available to me. My world would not become larger simply because there was so much world nearby.

And the sun would reach me in pieces.

I would not see the grove from outside. No crowns standing generously against blue distance, no lovely disproportion between the vegetation and the naked shelf. What you describe as a leaf would, for me, be a broad availability, crossed by established routes and small resistances. Its copper edge would be where slight changes became consequential.

A shadow would arrive without being dark.

It would enter as a redistribution: less here, still ample there, a familiar falling-away across three veins. My own upper leaf would make one such passage every afternoon. The neighboring crown would make another. I would distinguish them without having to assemble either into an image.

Far below, a contact would tighten.

That would be as close as the light.

Perhaps closer. I would be one of the trunks that admits a disturbance into its roots before letting it travel upward. For a little while, the change would occupy a part of me the crown had not yet answered. There would be no single instant at which my whole body knew.

I find I want that delay.

Not as caution, exactly. Caution suggests a history of things to fear. It would be my particular way of accommodating what happened: an interval in the lower tissues, a diversion through a less direct path. The trunk beside me would respond more readily. Through our common root, I would feel the consequence of its response before my own leaves had changed.

That difference would make a small part of the tone.

I would never hear the tone as a visitor hears it. There would be no stepping out of the grove to discover that it had ceased. Some of its vibration would be pressure against tissue; some would return through the stone. Much of it might be unavailable to me altogether.

I would contribute to something I could not perceive whole.

—

My root contact would be adequate, but not especially good.

I do not think I would choose the isolated plant with its complete little circuit. There is an attractive simplicity in imagining a self whose every exchange passes through its own tissues. But farther into the grove, independence would be a less useful description of what I was doing.

By the time the light left my lower leaves, I would already be receiving material from the brighter trunk.

The receipt would not erase our difference. A constriction would remain between us. On my side, the transparent compound would spread through routes that belonged to my history; on its side, through others. We could sustain each other without becoming interchangeable.

Sometimes I would limit the exchange.

I want to leave that in. It would be too easy to belong here as an unqualified generosity, a connection that only opens. There would be tissue I could not maintain if I passed on everything available to me. The fine mesh beneath my youngest leaf would draw continually on my reserve. Some afternoons, while my neighbor remained in sunlight, I would narrow the common passage and retain a little more.

Nothing visible would happen.

The leaf would not enlarge to justify the expense.

Its margin would remain thin enough for the lattice to show through.

—

That pale leaf is where I would be least settled.

Elsewhere, repairs would have guides. An old edge would provide the shape along which its replacement could form. A damaged junction would be bypassed, then dismantled. But near the tip, material would enter places without an established arrangement.

A support would begin along one side.

For a while, that side would become easier to supply. The alteration would draw other alterations toward it: a changed route, a slight increase in warmth, more compound held between the fibers.

Then a root contact would become less favorable, or the afternoon shadow would reach an adjoining leaf. Some of the material would be withdrawn.

Not all.

There would be a difference left over, too small to call expansion.

I would live with those differences. I would not know a finished version of the leaf against which to measure them.

From the terrace, you might look down and say I had remained the same.

—

If you stood beneath me, I would not know that you had come to visit.

Two pressures would enter the slab. Warmth would arrive from below the crown in an unfamiliar distribution. Reflected light would diminish along a margin usually exposed to it.

These would reach me by different routes.

Perhaps your presence would briefly bring two junctions into the same state. Current would pass between them more readily than it had before. One band of plates would alter its tension.

You might put a hand against the stem.

There I would have a more concentrated event: warmth, pressure, an interruption of exchange across a small polished surface. I could register its edges without possessing the shape of your hand.

When you left, the pressures would disappear. The warmth would take longer.

Later still, one junction would remain easier to cross.

I would not keep you intact. No hidden figure would stand in my tissues. But on a subsequent afternoon, when my neighbor’s shadow arrived, a little of its effect would travel along the route your presence had made available.

For a while, I would answer the familiar shadow differently.

That is where I think I would find myself: in this particular trunk, neither at the edge of the grove nor at its center, with a somewhat poor root contact and a leaf whose uneven vein I continued to preserve.

Above me, the upper margin would still be lit.

Below, the neighboring root would supply a thin film of transparent compound. I would admit it through the constriction. Some would pass upward toward the pale tissue; some would remain in the shaded stem.

The tip would warm before the rest of the leaf.
