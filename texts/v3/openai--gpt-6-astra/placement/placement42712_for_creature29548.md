I would look for a place where the sound changed without becoming quieter.

Not the deepest hollow, or the opening with the sharpest whine. Somewhere between two of the stone bosses, perhaps, where a small movement of the head brought a thin note out of the larger roar. I would stand there longer than was useful. Then I would lower myself onto the stone, and the note would disappear from my hearing while remaining faintly present in my teeth.

That would be my first mistake about where I belonged: choosing a place by what could be heard above it.

Under that place, the distinctions would be much finer.

A shallow chamber. A roof with one long depression in it, no deeper than the mark left by a thumb in dough. Water holding against the roof without any air between. The underside of the stone would be dark, though not a different color from the upper side; there would simply be no light with which to see its color.

I think I would be close to that roof.

At first I imagine myself spread along it, receiving the plain through many small attachments. A weight overhead would reach me unevenly. Near one edge, an increased pull; farther inward, a slight easing. I would have no need to collect these sensations into the picture of a foot. They would already specify what had changed and where I needed to yield.

But that is too large a body to claim so quickly.

The animal extends into passages I cannot imagine from inside. Some of its work must be as remote from other parts as the chambers are from the sky. I would not be the awareness of all of it. I would not know the whole river by touching one slab.

The place that feels nearer is enclosed within a fold.

There, the roar would reach me through stone, water, attachment, flesh, and another small thickness of water. Much of it would arrive as a pressure almost evenly distributed over my surface. The differences would be minute. I would have the means to detect them, but not yet the distances across which those means were useful.

My cups would be turned inward.

Their rims would hold against something soft. When one released, the surface beneath it would follow a little before letting go. The hard center would tilt. I would move the cup and fasten it again.

The surface would be my own.

So perhaps I would be one of the compact bodies in the rounded chambers: already furnished with the parts for a wider life, but folded so that most of what I could touch was myself.

Not an egg. There would be no clean shell, no single line to break. Water would enter by one narrow passage and leave by another. Between them, a living wall would keep a space open for me. A channel in that wall would supply something richer than the water. Its opening would tighten and ease independently of anything I did.

I could not count on its rhythm because it would have none that I could distinguish. I would know its opening by a change along one side of my body. Several folds would become more active there. Fluid would pass inward.

Enough would arrive.

That would be a fact of the place, like the nearness of the wall.

***

I would not experience myself as small.

There would be a considerable distance between the cup tucked near my feeding fold and the cups crowded along my outer curve. An alteration at one end would take time to affect the other. Some contractions would travel successfully through me; others would spend themselves in the fold where they began.

I would have a difficult place.

Every body should be allowed one, even here.

A narrow turn where two mineral plates approached at the wrong angle. Not an injury. Not quite a fault. When pressure increased outside, one plate would slide readily and the other would hesitate. A little of the load would pass into the soft tissue between them.

I would loosen a cup on that side.

The pressure would redistribute. The plate would settle.

There would be no reason to remember this as an event, but the cup would become easier to release. The tissue beside the plates would become slightly thicker. Even without growing larger, I would acquire a particular way of fitting inside myself.

That is where I would become distinguishable from another folded young animal in another chamber. Not by a private name or a different appetite. By the place where I yielded first.

***

The larger body would move around me.

I would not necessarily know it as a body. Some of its changes would be indistinguishable from changes in the stone. Others would have a softness to their arrival: a tightening followed by fluid displaced, then a new pressure maintained.

Occasionally my chamber would lengthen.

A fraction of one fold would come free of the part beside it. Water would enter a space that had held only a thin film. My released skin would register the difference across its whole surface.

This would not make me want the river. I would have no river to want.

But I might extend into the available space.

Not far. Enough to straighten the narrow turn. Enough for the two troublesome plates to lie without pressing together. One attachment cup, finding nothing immediately beneath it, would remain open in the water.

Its rim would receive very little useful information.

Then the chamber would shorten again. I would fold, the cup would find purchase, and the familiar pressures would return in a slightly altered arrangement.

Something could happen to me without becoming a beginning.

***

If you stood on the slab overhead, I would receive only what the larger animal failed to absorb.

Perhaps it would be almost nothing. A small oblique compression, reaching my chamber after several membranes had already accommodated your weight. I would release the easy cup. My difficult plates would slide.

You might remain for a long time.

During that time, one side of my enclosure would hold a little closer. The supplying channel would continue to open. Water would continue through the narrow passages. I would make the adjustment necessary to occupy this version of the chamber.

I would have no exposed margin for you to find. No pale patch visible in the bank. Even if you lay beside the cavity and looked into it, the living fold nearest your face would not be mine.

Still, your presence would reach me.

When you left, the wall would ease outward. The cup I had loosened might not fasten in exactly its former place. A small area of skin would remain uncovered that had been covered before.

I think this is where I would be: inside the substantial animal under the shallow water, folded around my own sensitive surfaces, receiving a diminished but sufficient part of what happened above.

I might eventually leave. The chamber might open along a seam, and water might bring a face of stone within reach of an outward-turning cup. There is nothing in the plain that promises this. Nothing in my body would make it impossible.

For now, one cup would hold against my flank. Another would release.

Between them, a narrow fold would ease enough for water to pass.
