I would begin beside the closed pipe socket, where the wall has worn into a hollow. Not quite in the hollow: a little below it, where water passing through the room has to divide around whatever rests there.

From the hatch, I might mistake this for the least interesting place. The plants have the light; the exposed floor has the clear water. Here there is only a change in curvature, a patch of masonry that cannot quite be distinguished from its repair.

But I would stay long enough to notice the passage along one side. Even with something occupying the hollow, water gets through. There is room to settle without making a seal.

At first I would still be arranging myself as a visitor. I would imagine a shoulder against the wall, knees drawn up, some way of resting without holding my head bent. None of these arrangements would be comfortable for long. My attention would keep returning to the low space beneath the moss, where comfort seems to require a different body.

I would want to stop holding so much of myself above the water.

That is where the change would begin: with the underside. A broad contact, interrupted by the shallow seams between blocks. Not weight pressing on a few points, but a yielding surface that can rest across an unevenness and still admit water beneath it.

The room would lose its far wall. Or rather, distance would become less useful than the order of things touching me. Ceramic under the mouth. A seam under the middle of the body. Along the left flank, water moving freely; along the right, a narrow place that needs opening.

I would lift that side slightly.

Fresh water would enter. I would settle.

For a while I might seem to be part of the floor even to myself—not because I believed I was masonry, but because there would be so little reason to gather into a separate shape. A limb could stay folded. The edge of the tail could lie loose. Something in the water would arrive before I needed to reach for it.

Then the mouth would find a place already grazed, and the distinction would matter. The floor would remain there. I would move.

I think I would be the pale animal, including the damaged foot.

Not a new, unmarked one hidden farther under the ledge. I would have that particular difficulty obtaining purchase on a smooth surface. The middle foot would spread, meet less resistance than expected, and shift toward a joint. Another limb would bear more of the pull. I would not need to examine the healed edge to make this adjustment.

At the glazed crescent, three fingers would be enough.

There would be things I could distinguish very well. The softening of a root before it broke free. The difference between film that yielded to the mouth and a surface that merely felt coated. A loose grain caught beneath the body. A passing disturbance in the foundation, abrupt enough to draw the limbs inward before any other part of me had changed position.

Much of the pumping house would never become distinct.

Its work would reach me through all the surfaces I used. I would not locate the motors or imagine the water falling beyond the wall. Their operation would be present in the hold of a foot, in the edge of a skin fold, in the water against my mouth. If one pitch altered, I might stop feeding. Whether I moved afterward would depend on what followed.

The hand would be more difficult.

It would change several things at once: current, warmth, light, the small movements conducted through the floor. I would approach along one of its diversions of water. A fingertip would offer an edge, but not a dependable one. Under my front foot it would give slightly. Then it would move again.

I would withdraw beneath the frond, bringing the thin hind limbs after me.

There I would remain with my head still facing the hand. No conclusion would be necessary. The water around it could continue changing while I stayed where the contact was better.

Later, in the hollow, a grain would lodge under a fold along my side. I would open the fold. The grain would remain. I would draw one foot nearer the wall and raise myself a little farther, enough for the current to take it.

Then I would lower myself, though not into exactly the same position.

The mouth would meet a strip of surface it had not reached before.
