At first I would look for somewhere with a view: an inspection bridge, perhaps, where the handrail trembles and the roofs separate enough to show how water passes from one district to the next. I would want to stand there until I understood which movements belonged to the machinery and which to the animals.

But the bridge would be an arrival place. I don’t think I would live there.

I would go down among the low housings, where a passage that looks empty from above is full at ankle height. Then lower, beside one of the broad animals, to the place where its skin leaves the water. There are several kinds of boundary here, packed into a few inches. Wet becomes damp. Damp becomes dry enough for mites. The skin moves, but the ceramic under it does not. Each breath alters the available space.

Somewhere along that slope there is a shallow pocket with its opening turned away from the light.

I think I would be the small amber creature inside.

***

From outside, most of me would be a head.

It would be broad, blunt, slightly translucent at the edges. Folded into the pocket, I would resemble an inclusion in the host’s skin, something firm around which the softer material had grown. My forelegs would lie together beneath my face. The rest of my body would be narrow and comparatively unimportant to look at.

When I came out, this arrangement would change. The forelegs would unfold farther than expected, reaching over the lip before my abdomen had left the bottom. Their weight would keep me secure while I worked.

The pocket would not be a tidy little room. Its floor would swell toward me as the animal breathed. One wall would be warmer than the other. A clear secretion would gather in a groove too shallow to see, and occasionally bring a loose fragment of skin against my feet.

I would have several ways of holding things.

The outer edges of my forelegs would push. Their inner surfaces would carry close-set bristles that could draw a soft particle along without losing it. Near each joint would be a short, harder projection for material caught in a fold.

With these I would keep the pocket clear.

It would take much of my time.

***

I would not know the large animal as large.

Its size would be present chiefly as delay. A movement begun somewhere beyond my reach would travel toward me through the skin. First the floor of the pocket would become firmer. Then the opening would tilt. Then warmth would gather along one side, and I would brace.

Sometimes nothing else would happen.

Sometimes the whole entrance would darken beneath a pipe, or open onto light so broad that I would pull back before any shadow crossed me.

Walking would have a different sequence from settling. So would reaching into an adjacent basin. I might distinguish these long before I could have encountered more than a small portion of the body carrying me.

There would be one movement I particularly knew: the host leaning my pocket against another animal.

Pressure would close around the entrance without crushing it. Moisture would come in carrying unfamiliar traces. My host’s breathing would continue beneath a second, less distinct rhythm.

I would stay near the lip then.

Something might need removing afterward.

***

Before I lived there, I would have crossed the ceramic.

That would be the exposed portion of my life: a pale surface beneath my feet, too much light, water deeper than it had looked from the skirt of the animal I had left. A grazer would pass close enough for its mouth fringe to brush my foreleg. Each filament would pull free in turn.

I would stop repeatedly.

Not everything that stopped me would be dangerous. A shallow channel might carry more force than I could cross directly. A stud might interrupt the chemical trace I was following. Beneath a bridge, I might encounter a patch of cool enamel and remain there while my body recovered its usual shape.

Eventually a basin animal would move past, leaving a broad wet interval behind it.

I would follow until I reached skin.

There would be empty pockets I did not enter. One would face too far downward. Another would contain a secretion whose sharpness made me withdraw my forelegs. At a third, I would climb halfway in, turn, and come out again.

The pocket I kept would require almost no adjustment. I would back into it and find that my folded legs rested against the walls before my softer body did. My head would fit across the entrance. Water would continue moving beneath me through the narrow groove.

For some time I would remain closed.

Then I would put out one foreleg.

***

The host would learn me through the work.

A slight dragging at the lower edge. Two touches near the opening. The prolonged pressure of my body while I freed something caught beneath a fold.

It might never distinguish me from the condition of having a comfortable pocket. Yet there would be differences I introduced. I would begin on the warmer wall. I would make two attempts at a stubborn fragment before changing my grip. When the animal settled, I would wait through the first full breath before coming out.

Another occupant might behave otherwise.

The host would accommodate me without needing to identify these habits. Its skin would relax beneath a familiar pressure. A small muscle would cease pulling the pocket away from my feet.

If I took too long, it might lean.

I would feel the lean beginning and work farther into the troublesome corner.

Sometimes the trouble would already be gone. The fold would still hold the irritation of it, and I would keep touching a clean surface until the tension eased.

***

I would know a little of the neighbors.

The worms would arrive as thin wet contacts along the upper lip. Usually I would let them pass. The mites would be lighter and more numerous, their feet scarcely distinguishable from secretion drying on the skin.

One mite might repeatedly come too far down.

I would push it away with the broad side of my foreleg. It would climb upward, browse for a while, and return. There would be no larger dispute. On some breaths the damp boundary would rise enough to turn it back without my help.

A flier landing nearby would be a considerable event. The skin would dip under its feet. Its folded wing might cover the entrance, bringing the smell of runoff from somewhere my host had never been.

I would close my head against the opening and wait.

After it left, one fine scale could remain.

I would take hold of that extraordinary object and move it outside.

***

There would be intervals with nothing to clear.

During those I would rest with my forelegs just beyond the entrance, laid against the host. Light passing through their amber edges would make two faint marks on the skin.

The machinery would reach me indirectly. A change in water pressure would alter the host’s posture; its posture would alter my floor. Far below, a valve would open. Here, a wall would become a little warmer.

I would move toward it.

I would not be maintaining the district, or judging whether its abundance was kind. There would be predators in the channels and bodies passing under recovery screens. Whatever held the whole plain in balance would continue beyond my senses.

But I would have some latitude in this pocket.

I could withdraw when the entrance was clean. I could remain at the lip. I could keep a foreleg against a fold after the fold had stopped tightening.

Perhaps I would often do that.

Above me, the broad animal would rest beside another, their skirts touching in the water. When one moved away, the skin around my pocket would stretch as my host extended a limb after it.

I would brace, expecting a journey.

No journey would follow.

After a while, I would loosen my grip. The entrance would still be clear. The water would still pass beneath me.

I would leave one foreleg out, resting against the warm wall.
