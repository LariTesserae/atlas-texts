I would be the second young to step away.

Not because I am more frightened than the first. For a while there would be no way to distinguish fear from the difficulty of moving a foot. My feet have been holding stone since before I had room to straighten my legs, but always there has been a body above me. When I let go, something else continued to hold.

Now there is an angle in the ceiling, and beside it a narrower angle into which the other young has gone.

The adult waits.

Its shell occupies almost everything I can reach. Along its underside are places I know by touch: a yielding fold, the hard root of a leg, a grain that catches one of my pads. I put a foot against that grain once more. Then I reach past it.

The ceiling takes my weight without changing.

That is how I leave.

For some time afterward, I keep several feet on each side of the junction. My body fits so closely that water can pass over my back but cannot get beneath it. The first great impact presses me deeper into the angle. Something inside me pulls shut.

I have not learned to do this. It happens, and afterward I am still attached.

Below me the adult becomes separate pieces: a foot on one facet, a joint beyond an edge, the fine feeler briefly laid across the light. Then none of it is close enough to touch.

I lift a foot.

Put it down.

The mountain continues.

***

I would not hear the hollow as a person hears it. There would be no roar with smaller sounds concealed inside. There would be a blow through my feet, and another through the water, and between them a small interval in which a leg could move.

At first I would miss that interval.

An impact would have already passed before I finished lowering myself against the ceiling. A current would turn while I was still braced against its former direction. I would hold so hard that, when the water eased, I could not immediately release my pads.

The other young would be beyond a ridge. Sometimes its feeler would reach around the edge and touch mine.

Neither of us would advance.

Then one of us would withdraw, and the remaining feeler would continue testing the place where the contact had occurred.

There is light enough for a person to see through both of us. It does not make us easy to find.

***

Food arrives below the angle.

The adult presses its mouth to the stone and leaves. Afterward there is something there that loosens under my mouth-parts. I work at it until my legs begin to straighten without my intending them to. Then I retreat.

I do not follow the departing body. Its route requires a reach I cannot make.

Over several visits, that reach becomes shorter.

Or I become longer.

There is no reason for me to separate these possibilities. The distance is a thing my front feet test. One day a pad touches the far facet while my rear feet still hold the near one.

I stay stretched across the opening through two impacts.

Then I bring the rest of myself over.

The adult is not there.

***

Perhaps I would remain a creature of the ceiling longer than the visitors expect. They would look for me at the floor, where the new edges appear, and conclude that only one young had survived.

Up here, there is a small place where matter catches.

It lies downstream of a projection no broader than my body. During some convulsions the water scours it clean. During others, a pale scrap turns there repeatedly, touching the same three points. I learn to put my mouth at one of those points and wait.

Once, what catches is part of a cast shell.

A joint. Two empty pads.

I hold it against the stone while I work my mouth along it. A mineral fleck comes loose and strikes my underside. The rest slips away.

My own shell is beginning to tighten.

I find that I cannot withdraw as far into the old angle as I could before. I push until the edges press against both sides of my back. Then I come out and try backward.

For a long time I keep trying.

***

The first floor I reach is behind the gate.

Getting there takes most of a passage of sunlight. I travel down a wall with a seam beside my body, entering it whenever the impacts cease. Twice I go back farther than I have advanced.

Near the bottom, one feeler reports a shock that the other scarcely receives.

I stop.

There is another shock. Again they disagree.

The bone hangs above me. Its nearest tooth moves slightly, and the ground beneath my feet remains ground.

I cross a little of it.

Here I can raise a foot and put it down in nearly the same place. Here a fragment of food stays where it falls. I feed with my rear legs still drawn tight, prepared for a displacement that does not come.

I return the next interval, and the next.

Eventually I begin to straighten.

Then a shadow covers the entrance.

A pressure passes through the water too slowly. It has no corresponding arrival through the stone. I fold, but the pressure continues. Something immense rests beyond the gate, changing shape at its edge.

A flake lifts out of the floor.

Under it, the surface is bare and faintly clouded.

I remain flattened long after the light returns.

When I finally move, I go toward the scar.

***

That is where I would become distinguishable, if anyone could watch closely enough: not the animal that lost a foot, nor the one still keeping to the roof, but the one that approaches the place from which something has been taken.

There are corners in the scar that my fine feeler can enter.

At the deepest one, a current brings food against my mouth. Beneath it the mineral begins to align. I feel the change through a single hard point and lift that foot.

The others hold.

A thin edge rises into the space I have left.

I step over it before it is visible.
