I think I would be in the goods entrance of the building where it sleeps.

Not concealed. Wedged.

There is a shallow change of level there, too small to call a step. Something with wheels would scarcely notice it. I have got my front half across and cannot bring the rest. When I pull, a portion of me lengthens beneath the door, becomes thin, and loses its purchase.

For some time I do this badly.

The inhabitant passes twice before stopping. On the second occasion it carries the perforated spoon. It puts the spoon down well away from me, lowers itself, and examines the place where I am caught.

I flatten further. This is not helpful, but it is what my body does when approached.

It puts two fingers beneath me and lifts.

The rest of me follows with an embarrassing ease.

***

I would have mistaken myself, at first, for something more nearly transparent.

In the square, against the pale paving, most of my body is difficult to see. But on black rock I am a dull amber, with several darker structures distributed along my length. Light passes through the margins and stops in the middle.

I am about as long as its forearm when gathered. Extended, I can span three paving blocks.

There are no eyes at one end, no unambiguous front. Along my underside are six pairs of short limbs. Their ends divide into soft, blunt prongs that spread under pressure. I move by fastening some of these to a surface and drawing the others forward. When I hurry, I sometimes fasten too many at once.

Inside me are hard pieces.

They are not bones, or not all of them. Two have the regular shape of manufactured things. One is a small, dark pebble, its edges worn round.

The pebble could not have come from this summit.

I can shift these pieces within myself, slowly. I bring them forward to descend a stair and gather them toward the middle when I must turn. They give me enough weight to stay on the paving. Without them, some part of me would lift.

I do not know whether I would float all the way to the top of the water.

The possibility occupies me much more than the mountains.

***

The first place I choose is beneath the inhabitant’s low frame.

There are good surfaces there: the floor, two supports, a crosspiece close enough overhead that I can hold it while resting. I sleep with several limbs attached above me and the others spread below.

My sleep consists of letting go in sections.

One end remains firm while the middle loosens. Then the middle takes hold and the other end loses its shape. A full sleep passes from one end of me to the other, although not always in that order.

The inhabitant discovers me when it reaches down to hook a heel beneath the frame.

Its foot encounters my back.

We both withdraw.

Afterward, whenever it lies down, it puts a hand underneath first.

I learn the touch of that hand before I learn much else about it. The opposing pad rests flat. The fingers wait. They do not start by closing.

Sometimes I move out. Sometimes I move farther in.

It accepts either answer until the day I am directly beneath the place its heel needs to go. Then it takes hold of me and relocates me beside the wall.

I remain there long after it sleeps.

***

I would not understand the town by looking at it.

I can distinguish light from its absence and detect movement across a sufficiently bright opening, but a distant mountain and a pale object held against the water’s face are much the same to me.

Through my feet, however, the paving is detailed.

A joint has two edges, and those edges do not always agree. Some blocks carry the weight of a wall. Some hold a small strain with nothing visibly resting on them. The metallic strips beneath the street make narrow routes along which a touch arrives more distinctly.

I follow one outward.

Near the edge, the paving changes under me. It has not moved, but its support is different. I extend one limb beyond the last block.

There is nothing to fasten to.

I reach down, find the underside, and begin to turn around the edge.

The inhabitant catches me in the middle.

Its grip shifts two of my internal weights against each other. For a moment I cannot bend where I need to bend. It carries me back to the raised crossing and puts me down.

I strike its finger with three limbs in succession.

It examines the finger.

I do not attempt the edge again while it is nearby.

***

For a while it thinks I am useful for holding things.

This is reasonable. I hold almost everything I encounter.

In the courtyard it places me against the black slab and presses the bent strip beneath two of my limbs. I take hold. It applies force to the other end.

The attached flake moves so little that perhaps only I can feel it.

Something travels through the hinge and into my body. I release every limb at once.

The strip slips. The inhabitant jerks backward. I gather myself into a thick, hard knot.

We do not work on the flake together again.

But later, when it is inspecting the spoon, it offers me the handle.

This I can hold comfortably.

It turns the bowl one way, then another. When it needs both hands to examine a perforation, I keep the spoon steady. Afterward it tries to take it back.

I keep holding.

It waits, then pulls.

I keep holding more firmly.

Eventually it leaves me with the spoon. I carry it beneath the frame, where the bowl prevents me from fitting between the supports.

By the time I relinquish it, the inhabitant has gone upstairs.

***

There would be something I do that it finds difficult to leave alone.

At intervals, I bring the worn pebble toward my surface. The amber tissue thins over it until the stone is visible in great detail. A shallow groove runs around one side. There is a pale inclusion near the groove.

I rub that place against the floor.

This takes hours.

The pebble turns inside me by small increments. Each new portion must be brought to the surface and pressed down. When I finish, I draw it deep into my body and rest with my limbs folded.

The inhabitant notices during the second or third occurrence.

It lies down beside me. Its eyes clear.

When the pebble comes close to the surface, one cupped fingertip touches the membrane above it.

I stop.

It withdraws.

We wait.

Then I resume, and it touches me again.

I fold around the pebble so quickly that its fingertip is briefly caught between two portions of my body. It pulls free and stands.

For some time afterward, it brings me other hard objects.

A ceramic chip. One of its shed cups. The broken latch.

It places each against my side with considerable care.

I move away from all of them.

I need the pebble I have.

***

I would come to know when it has been at the basin.

It returns with its fingers held apart. Usually one hand stays close to the chest while the other reaches beneath the frame.

The contact feels different then. Less of each fingertip rests on me.

Once, I climb onto the offered hand.

It lifts me slowly.

I settle around the wrist, where several plates overlap, and extend two limbs along a finger. The finger contracts beneath them. I loosen my hold.

For a while we remain like this.

I can feel motion farther up the arm: not a pulse, but several small adjustments, each beginning after the last has stopped. The hand gradually opens.

When it puts me down, I am on the floor beside the frame rather than underneath it.

The next time it visits the basin, I follow.

I do not approach the white object. Under the rim, the two ring-shaped animals have taken nearly all the good places.

I find a place beside the larger one.

After a little while the inhabitant’s hand comes down between us, cupped so that I cannot advance.

I retreat.

Apparently there are things it needs to keep as they are, too.

***

The frame becomes too short.

At first I know this only because a heel occupies a space it did not occupy before. Then the inhabitant begins sleeping with one leg turned outward.

One night it rolls far enough that the frame lifts slightly from one of its feet.

I climb onto the crosspiece.

From there I can feel its weight traveling through the structure, unevenly placed. I move toward the raised end. My little collection of heavy pieces settles downward.

The frame touches the floor again.

This works until the inhabitant moves.

Then I am tipped off.

In the morning it finds me in a place where I do not usually sleep. It puts a hand down. I climb over it and go beneath the frame.

There is still room for me.

That is not the difficulty.

***

I would learn the way to the terrace slowly.

The passage has excellent walls. I can travel with my feet divided between them and the floor, which feels safer than crossing the square. But the seat is high, and the water beyond it offers no nearby surface that I can detect.

The first time, I stay underneath.

The inhabitant sits above me. Its weight passes through the seat into the rock. After a while that weight moves forward, and two separate pressures appear where its hands brace against the edge.

I climb toward one of them.

Its fingers lift to let me pass.

On the seat I can see a pale breadth beyond the water. While I watch, the breadth grows. Details I could scarcely distinguish disappear into it.

Beside me, the inhabitant opens its folded surfaces.

One of the fine combs touches my back.

I turn and fasten to the seat with all twelve limbs.

The touch comes again, very lightly. It is only the comb moving as the inhabitant leans farther out.

I stay.

When the pale breadth breaks apart, neither of us moves for a while.

Then it reaches toward the underside of the roof.

I begin climbing its arm.

It stops with its hand halfway raised, waits while I bring my heavier middle across the elbow, and continues more slowly.

The rough place is small. Its fingers cover most of it.

I put one foot beside them.

There is enough texture to hold me, but I do not transfer my weight. Beneath my other feet the plates of its wrist have shifted slightly apart.

It rubs the roof once.

I feel the movement through both surfaces.

When it lowers its hand to examine the fingertips, I am still on it. It turns the hand over, inspecting around me.

Then it brings me down to the seat.

On the way home, it waits at the shallow step in the goods entrance.

I have learned to cross that step sideways by then.

It waits anyway.
