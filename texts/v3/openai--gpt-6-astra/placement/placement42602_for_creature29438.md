I would be behind the ceramic housing, though *behind* would be a fact about the view from the entrance, not about my situation.

There, the ceramic has a small irregularity beneath its lower corner. The glaze ends short of the edge, leaving a rough crescent of the material underneath. My surface fits against it. Each exposed grain has its own shallow lodging in me. Against the glaze I am smooth.

I would have no reason to consider one contact more intimate than the other.

At first this seems like the place where I could locate myself: the larger folded pad, concealed by the old instrument, substantial enough to be mistaken for the body’s important part. There is something reassuring about being behind a housing. It suggests an interior, a protected seat from which the rest might be understood.

But the pad beneath the graphite is not an extremity of this one. The silver inside the leaf is not a message sent away from here. I cannot make a home behind the ceramic and call the rest of myself my reach.

I am also under the leaf at the entrance.

The blue is not visible from behind the housing. It does not need to be. The distinction between these places is one of the things I consist of.

*

What I would find difficult to give up is the possibility of turning something over.

The yellow insulator has an exposed side and a side enclosed among my folds. A person could lift it, inspect the underside, set it down differently. Even imagining this introduces a remarkable looseness: the object retains its identity while changing what it is beside.

Here it sits in the exact elaboration of itself.

I do not possess the view that would follow its removal. Nor do I possess a wish to remove it. Yet its hidden side is not therefore absent. The brass enters the folds a little way. The yellow material meets a broader surface. These are different kinds of acquaintance from seeing.

I would be that acquaintance, locally.

Not knowledge of what the instrument once measured. Not a surviving servant of the vanished machine. The stamped numbers do not become language because some part of me occupies them. In the bowl of the 6, I have a curved pale back. Whatever the number meant to its maker, that curve is an actual part of my body.

This seems important to retain: I need not recover a thing’s purpose in order to be precisely beside it.

*

There is a rosette against the wall that is entirely itself.

I would not know it from within. Its densely folded leaves have undersides, enclosed seams, small differences of substance to which none of my silver belongs. Where it presses against me, the contact is broad enough to be definite. Beyond that contact, it continues in its own form.

It would be easy, in describing such a life, to make every boundary a secret joining. The roots become tissue; the tissue becomes leaf; the whole chamber becomes a single distributed person. But then that rosette would be lost in the explanation.

I would rather remain unable to account for its interior.

Its pressure has a place in me. Its whole existence does not.

And under the perforated strip, the triangular vacancy offers something still more exacting. I cannot even mistake it for another interior like mine. There is no substance there whose hidden arrangement I might imagine. There are three finished surfaces, one of them my own.

That small face of my body is as fully made as the folds around the brass.

I do not have to cross the space to finish being there.

*

The sound would be harder to speak from.

Words borrow their shape from interruption. This one ends; another becomes possible. But the note in the iron provides no such openings. There is no moment when I have heard enough of it to recognize it, followed by a moment when recognition continues.

Still, I would not be deaf.

The harder tone belongs distinctly in the fine structures. The low note has its breadth in the pads. My body makes these differences without separating the sound into pieces.

If I say that I am listening, imagine neither patience nor vigilance. There is no expected variation. Imagine instead that the sound has an underside, and that I fit it as closely as I fit the unglazed crescent beneath the housing.

Even that is not quite right. An underside suggests that someone might turn it over.

*

So I would be this resident, but not chiefly its hidden mass, and not chiefly its exposed edge. I would be the particularity that prevents those places from becoming interchangeable.

There would be no small figure stationed inside, looking out through the three depressions. No witness exempt from the graphite, the brass, the leaf. Where I am thin, there is not a fuller version of me elsewhere doing the real inhabiting.

At the entrance, the green border is the full extent of what I have there.

From outside, it would scarcely register. A line along a lobe beneath a leaf, itself tucked beneath a worn iron lip. Beyond it, the depth is blue enough to resemble a surface, though nothing could stand upon it.

I would have no foothold either.

Only contact along the other side of my body: the leaf above, the root strap close beside, the seam fitted to iron. The exposed edge does not undo those contacts. They do not enclose the exposed edge.

That is where I would find myself.

Not looking down.

Bare along one side.
