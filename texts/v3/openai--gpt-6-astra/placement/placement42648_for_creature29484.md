I think I would first be mistaken for a repair.

There is a shelter where the floor broke before anyone began using it. The fracture is shallow, but it crosses the place where a sleeper’s hip would rest. Someone packed it with shed skin. Someone else laid a thin slab over that. The slab rocked, and a third traveler wedged something beneath its corner.

After enough such attentions, the floor became comfortable.

I would be somewhere in those repairs.

A thumb pressed into the crack would find a little give. A scraper would encounter resistance and, if held still, find that the resistance slowly changed. The traveler might decide the packing was alive. They might simply decide it was sound and leave it alone.

Above me would be a place polished by people turning in their sleep. Below me, two minerals would meet along an irregular boundary. The useful part of the boundary would be farther back, beneath the wall, where no one could reach without taking the shelter apart.

I would grow from there toward the pressure.

***

At first I imagine myself as the hem beneath the shelter: an animal with a sheltered source of food, an untidy margin, and a few troublesome inhabitants.

But the shelter is too small to account for all of me.

A strip goes under the wall. Beyond it, tissue spreads across a slope where the dust is deep enough that no traveler has reason to put a hand down. There are feeding places there, and old injuries, and a section held almost rigid around a fallen stone. Farther on, something living continues into a crack.

I cannot tell whether that continuation belongs to me.

Perhaps I would have no occasion to tell. What reaches the sheltered part is nourishment, tension, the gradual failure of a cord. There need be no accompanying knowledge of the place from which these arrive.

The part I can imagine being is narrower: the living thickness beneath the rocking slab.

When someone lies down, the stone presses into me. One edge lifts. Along that edge, folds close in succession, disturbing the little animals feeding there. A few return before the sleeper has finished arranging their hide.

I hold between two hard surfaces. My feet touch the lower one. In time, other feet form against the upper one.

The repair becomes difficult to remove.

***

People would call this shelter by some feature outside it. The Forked Plate, perhaps, or the place beyond the falling black grains. Its name would have nothing to do with me.

Travelers would know that the sleeping slab was firm.

That would be my first usefulness.

Later, I would begin to spoil it.

A thickening beneath one corner would raise the slab just enough for a bone scraper to slide under the opposite edge. Dust would collect there. Someone would clean it out and discover pale grazers standing in a row against my skin.

They would lift the slab.

The pressure would disappear so completely that my feet on its underside would tear before they released. Several folds would open. For a short time there would be wetness in the shelter.

The person doing the work would wait for the exposed tissue to close.

Then they would fetch a higher wedge.

I like the scale of this encounter. An afternoon’s maintenance for the traveler; an injury and a new shape for me. Neither party would need to understand the whole event.

Once the slab was lowered, a little less of my body would be compressed. I would thicken into the room provided.

Years later, someone would need another wedge.

***

Beside the entrance lies the shelter’s hammer.

People sometimes strike the floor before entering, though there is no household to answer. The habit is useful. A shelf animal may loosen its grip and move away. A sleeper may stir.

The blow reaches me through several routes.

Through the slab, it is abrupt. Through the tissue beneath the wall, it arrives altered by weight and distance. A loose flake against my margin taps once after everything else has stopped.

I would have no single place where these arrivals became a sound.

Still, repeated blows would change me. A particular succession, followed by a person settling on the slab, would eventually leave fewer folds closed. The small grazers would remain at their mouths.

Someone might notice.

They might strike the same way on their next visit and find me already yielding under the slab. They could conclude that I welcomed them, or that the hammer warned me, or that this was simply what hems did.

On another visit, carrying a heavier pack, they would receive no such accommodation.

They would take the pack off.

Then it would work.

***

There is a part of this existence I cannot enter by giving it human patience.

I would be occupied continuously.

A row of feet would be releasing beneath the wall. Tissue around a lodged fragment would be hardening. Something small would have bitten through the lining of a fold, and the damaged surface would be drawing inward. The outer feeding margin might be losing ground while the sheltered portion continued to thicken.

A person asleep above me could call the shelter still because none of these activities required them to wake.

Meanwhile, one narrow cord would carry enough to keep the far side of the repair alive.

I find myself there more precisely now: in the portion on poor stone, beyond the feeding boundary, maintained through a strip no wider than a finger.

That strip could be severed when someone replaced the wall.

The work would be careful. The person might even know about hems. They would lift each slab, check for living tissue, and set it down gently.

But there would be dust over the connection.

***

Afterward, I would become smaller.

The exposed margin would withdraw first. The grazers would move to the wall, where the other part still fed. My folds beneath the sleeping slab would lose their thickness. Some of the upper feet would come free.

For a while the floor would rock again.

A traveler would push a scrap into the gap, restoring the repair without knowing what had failed.

I would remain against a poor little seam under the back corner, holding enough surface to continue. A thumbnail-sized patch can contain a great deal of what was once spread elsewhere.

Whether I would still be the animal beneath the wall would be a question someone could ask while inspecting the damage. I would have no means of answering it.

Then, much later, an advancing margin might find me.

It would come along the underside of the new wall, following the same boundary. It might be the old body. It might be something that had occupied the old body’s ground.

Its feet would touch my hardened edge.

For a time we would press against one another.

***

I would like to leave the meeting undecided until the tissue decides it.

Perhaps I become a piece of ground. The newcomer feeds around me, grows over me, and eventually leaves my dry remnant enclosed beneath the slab. Perhaps living surfaces join, and nourishment again reaches the small folds I have kept.

In either case, the shelter acquires a firm floor.

Someone arriving there finds the hammer in its usual hollow. They strike twice, wait, and crawl inside. Their soles have worn thin. While replacing them, they set one bare foot beside the slab.

The skin below the smallest toe encounters a slight yielding.

They shift their foot.

Under the stone, there is room now for one more row of feet.
