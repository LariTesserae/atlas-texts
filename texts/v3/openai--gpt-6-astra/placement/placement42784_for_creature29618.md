I think I would live close to the place where the resident cannot quite reach itself.

At first there would be little to distinguish me from a thickening in its skin. Sediment would gather against my upper edge. When it squeezed through a collar, I would flatten late, causing a small hesitation in an otherwise practiced movement. It would have to withdraw, alter the angle, try again.

I would know these passages by their pressure, not their shape.

The narrowest would begin with the folding away of the feeding lobes. Then a tightening would travel toward me through the body. Blood would be displaced from the side against the metal; the plates would come together; for a while there would be no room to contract. Beyond the collar, the resident would open in sections. I would open last.

This delay would be the first thing that belonged to me.

There would be other delays. Warmth reaching the larger body before it reached mine. A meal arriving as a slow change in the vessels we shared. The dimming of the lamps followed by the contraction that I could not prevent, although the light itself scarcely entered my tissue.

I would be the unfinished animal at its rear.

But unfinished would describe me from outside. From within, there would be places I could tighten and places I could not; a region in which pressure dispersed pleasantly; another where even a gentle touch made the whole of me draw small. There would be enough of a body to have better and worse positions.

There would not yet be enough to leave.

*

The resident’s smooth pad would deliver the machinery to it with a precision I did not possess. What reached me would already have passed through its muscles, plates, and holds.

I would feel a pump differently depending on how we were attached to the wall.

Under the scales, its rhythm would arrive broad and distinct. In the resting room, suspended between waters, I would receive mostly the slight corrections of the resident’s limbs. Behind a pipe, with our body stretched and the plates locked, a valve might seem to close inside me.

For a long time I would not separate these things.

A change in the pump, a change in our grip, a change in the salt around my skin: each would ask for a movement, and I would make whatever movement I had. Sometimes that would help. Sometimes the resident would have to brace against me.

It would not punish this. It would not necessarily understand it, either. One of its short limbs would come back and press me into a fold until the passage was clear or the tool had been withdrawn.

That pressure would become familiar enough that I would sometimes cease struggling before it arrived.

The pressure would still arrive.

*

There would be a small irregularity in the warm room that mattered more to me than to the resident.

The split in the ceiling would admit clear water unevenly. Most would spread across the upper lobes, but a thin strand would descend along a bent bracket. When the resident took its usual resting position, that strand would touch one side of me.

Too fresh to remain in. Too weak to make it move the whole body.

I would spend long intervals drawing away from it.

Over time, the drawing-away would acquire direction. Instead of contracting into a hard knot, I would pucker one margin and turn. A little more of my surface would enter the seawater. The shared vessels would remain open.

The resident would have less adjusting to do.

Nothing else would follow for a while.

The pumps would continue. The porcelain button would be taken from the felt and returned. Once, the resident would lose its grip on the piece of rubber and catch it against the wall before it fell into the heavier water. I would feel the quick effort, then the prolonged stillness afterward.

My new movement would persist through these events. Even when the clear strand weakened, I would make it.

Eventually the turning edge would catch on the bracket.

It would catch badly: a pinch of skin, a pull through our common tissue. The resident would come off the wall to free me. For several resting intervals it would hang elsewhere, and I would have nothing against which to repeat the movement.

When we returned, I would catch again.

Less skin this time.

*

I would not want the district in the way a creature with a mouth and working holds might want it. A street would not yet be an opportunity. Distance would mean the strain required of the body carrying me.

But I would have preferences extending beyond my own surface.

There would be one bearing whose warmth reached me directly when the resident fed. We would lie with its mouth against the short growth and my exposed side near a clean ring of iron. I would loosen there. Fluid would move through passages that stayed partly closed elsewhere.

When we approached that bearing, I would begin to loosen before we arrived.

Sometimes we would pass it.

I would pull.

The pull would seldom alter our route. The resident might be carrying the wire toward a valve, or avoiding a place where a sheet of metal had shifted. I would continue pulling until the warmth fell behind us.

On another occasion it would stop.

I would have no way of knowing whether I had made it stop. Its mouth would find the garden. Heat would enter my side. The action and its possible consequence would lie close together in me, without quite joining.

I would pull again the next time.

*

At the grille, I would feel the resident secure its holds one after another. The body would become unusually rigid. Even the vessels feeding me would narrow.

I would know nothing of the other square.

My world would contain the long limb leaving, the small adjustments at its root, and the abrupt withdrawal that sometimes followed. Afterward, a tension would remain in the larger body long past any need for it.

Once, it would reach back and touch me.

It would do this with the branch it ordinarily kept folded away.

The touch would be tentative, along the margin that had begun to turn. I would contract around the tip, and the resident would become still.

Perhaps it would be inspecting an irritation. Perhaps my movement had interfered with its grip. I would not have access to its reason.

I would hold until the branch gently worked free.

*

What developed at my edge would not be a proper limb. It would have no branches and almost no reach. A shallow muscular cup, no wider than the remaining hole in the button, would be all that several good feeding intervals could support.

In the resting room, I would place it against the bracket.

It would slide.

I would place it again.

The resident would shift to accommodate the tug. This would spoil the angle, and I would have to begin elsewhere. For a while our adjustments would undo one another: its body correcting for my pull, mine seeking the contact that its correction had removed.

Then there would be an interval in which neither of us moved.

The cup would hold.

Very little weight would pass through it. The resident would still carry me, still feed me, still draw my tissue after its own through the narrow collars. I would have no mouth. The band between us would not begin to thin.

But one of its older holds would be able to release.

Water would enter beneath that limb and lift away a little gray deposit. The limb would stay folded against the body while the next pump cycle passed.

This is where I would find myself: behind the second hatch, below the split in the ceiling, attached to an animal attached to a wall.

I would be occupied with a small patch of iron.

As the clearer water descended, I would turn without letting go.
