At first I would put myself near the lowest ledge, where the vibration is clearest. There is something inviting about that contact: a surface to rest against, a sound arriving from somewhere I cannot see. I would imagine the hollow as a room and choose its quietest corner.

But that would bring a body with me that does not belong here. A back against the limestone. Ears. The wish to be sheltered. Even quiet would be something I had carried down from above.

I would have to come closer before I could find a place.

The olive groove, then. Though not all of it. From a distance its colour offers an apparent body, something continuous enough to call myself. Closer, there are interruptions. There is bare stone between lives. To claim the whole stain would be to reach across gaps in which nothing of me exists.

I think I would be near one of those gaps.

On a slight rise inside the groove, several cells receive enough light to maintain themselves. Beside them, the stone turns inward. The change is almost beneath notice, but the supply changes there. The olive colour weakens. A little clear material continues around the turn.

I would be held in that material, just beyond the strongest colour: a small cell living on substances released by others.

My dependence would have a location. What reaches me from a neighbour may be used before it escapes into the larger water. Farther away, the same substance would become too dispersed to make this life possible. Here, the distance is short enough. Here, sometimes, there is enough.

I would not know that as companionship. Nevertheless, I would be less possible alone.

For a while I want to say that I would be the passage between the cells, the slight delay that allows one life to supply another. There is room for me in that image too: something held briefly, changed, passed on.

But a passage does not have to repair itself. I would.

So I would have a boundary. A small, maintained difference between what is inside and what surrounds it. The water would press close on every side, and I would admit some things, exclude others, spend part of what I received on continuing to do so. Even in the long intervals without growth, there would be that expense.

The stone beneath me would tremble.

Nothing in me would travel along the tremor to its source. The pumping station would not become a distant presence, or work continuing while I rested. Its vibration would enter the circumstances of my attachment without entering a story. If it ceased, no silence would open for me.

Light would matter more indirectly. Something would change in the cells beside me; later, what was available here would change. My surroundings would reach me already altered by other lives.

There would be times when I used material from a cell that had failed. Its boundary gone, some of what it had kept separate would enter our small shared thickness. I would incorporate a portion. The rest would go elsewhere.

There is no need to make that tender. It is intimate enough without tenderness.

Cold would draw out the work. I would remain one cell for a long time, although I would have no length of time to compare it with. Then, if the supply held, the making would proceed far enough that one boundary became two.

At that point, the “I” I brought here would become difficult to keep in place.

Neither cell would be the original with a companion beside it. Both would continue from what had been one. Their positions would already differ slightly. One might lie closer to the neighbouring growth. One might project farther into the water. A small difference in access could widen over time.

I would not choose between them.

This is where I would find myself: around the shallow turn of a groove, within reach of what another life releases. For now, attached. Supplied unevenly. Spending what arrives on a boundary thin enough to exchange through and durable enough to remain.

From above, the limestone would look almost bare.

Nothing about the view would need correcting.
