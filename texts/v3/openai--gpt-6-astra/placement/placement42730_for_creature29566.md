I would be beneath the middle seed husk, in the interval above the animal’s back.

There is room there for something thinner than a husk’s inner lining. Seen from the opening, I would look like a pale vein in the roof. Seen from beneath, I would have a shallow breast, two rows of blunt attachments, and a face turned sideways to accommodate a single grain caught in the husk.

The grain would be important to my shape. One cheek would be fuller than the other. A groove would carry its edge past my mouth.

I would have no view of the whole animal below me. Its nearest plate would occupy most of what I could see, with the edge of another showing through it. The milky organ would be visible as a broad, obscure brightness farther down. To someone outside the roof, these would be small details of a small body. To me they would have the proportions of a slope, a second slope beneath it, and something substantial under both.

But I would not mistake them for country. I would possess some exact sense for the difference between a body and what supports it. The husk would register along my upper surface in short, separate lengths. The animal’s back would be present to my lower surface all at once.

Between us there would remain a little air.

Most of my organs would face this air. Along my breast would lie six clear pockets, their openings bordered with stiff white hairs. Each pocket would have a different shape, though from a distance they would look alike. One would be round and shallow. Another would narrow behind its opening, then broaden where it passed between two attachments. The last would contain a small, solid body joined to the wall by its narrowest end.

These would be my means of knowing the interval.

The first pocket would contain the breadth above the animal’s foremost plate. The second would hold the closer space at the roof’s inward curve. In the last, the solid body would have a flattened side corresponding to a place where the husk almost touched a projecting edge.

I would feel that narrow place with particular clarity. It would be the strongest sensation in my body, though nothing pressed on me there.

My mouth would lie against a torn fiber of the husk. Inside the fiber would be a yellowish strand, and against the strand a wet surface belonging to me. There would be nourishment in this arrangement, but also a dry, distinct flavor from the husk’s outer layer. I would have separate tissue for tasting that dryness. A minute lobe beside the mouth, unnecessary to the feeding contact, would rest against it.

That would be one of my pleasures.

I would also have a foot that fitted badly.

Not badly enough to leave it unsupported. The foot would have three divisions, while the shallow recess beneath it offered two. Two divisions would rest securely. The third would lie along the lip, exposed on its underside to the mineral light.

It would be tempting to give this foot a task—to make it an instrument for measuring the chamber, or a sign that I did not altogether belong. But it would simply be my most sensitive foot. The uncovered underside would know the roar differently from the rest of me. There the hard central note would have a thin, almost sour edge. Through the attachments in the roof, the same sound would be broad and fibrous.

I would contain both without reconciling them.

From one particular angle, perhaps through the transparent plate below, it would be possible to see that my two rows of attachments did not join the same body. One row would run into the pale tissue along the husk. The other would join a thicker part folded underneath. Between these parts would be a narrow passage, open at both ends.

Something smaller would occupy it.

I could not call that creature my inhabitant. Its forepart would rest in the passage, but its rear limbs would fit a wrinkle belonging to the seed husk, and one fine projection would extend into the air I sensed. Its presence would account for the asymmetry of my third pocket. Through that pocket I would feel its tip, the animal’s plate below it, and the small breadth separating them.

I would have no sense reserved for myself alone. Even the deep, firm sensation at the bases of my attachments would include the fibers into which they fitted.

Still, I would be distinguishable. There would be the sideways face, the yellow strand against my mouth, the foot with its lit underside. The smaller creature would have other particulars.

Under that roof, above that back, I would occupy very little of the interval. Most of what I knew would be the portion left open.
