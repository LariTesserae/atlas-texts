I would be near the passage that is wider than it needs to be.

Not in the middle, where the animal still puts its feet, but along one side, where the water is too thin to have an obvious surface. From most angles there would be nothing to distinguish my place from the rest of the stone. A little red sediment. A seam with green caught in it. Cloudlight, when the cloud came low enough.

I think I would know when the animal was approaching before I could see it. The stone would take its weight, then release it, then take it again a little nearer. Water would draw back from one edge of a pad and gather at the other.

At first, that would be most of my world: the intervals between its feet.

I would have no reason to call them footsteps. They would be changes in the ground, as ordinary and important as the arrival of shade.

When the animal stopped, something in me would stop too.

That would be the first difficulty.

*

There would be stretches of afternoon when I seemed to belong to the moss.

Its rounded edge would hold me on one side. The pale stone would hold me on the other. A fallen stem, lying across the passage, would divide what I could feel into a near part and a far part. As it softened, the division would become less definite.

But I would not grow toward it. I would not enter the dark material gathering underneath.

And when the animal moved away, I would sometimes move where no water had moved.

Not far. The breadth of a folded foot.

Enough to discover that the moss was beside me rather than part of me.

I would be slow to make that distinction. Things touch for so long here.

*

On a clear afternoon, the animal would lie across the passage with its head turned toward the larger pool. Its flank would rise and settle. In the shallow water beside it, the flank would rise farther.

I would feel the difference.

There would be a pressure along an edge I had not known I possessed. A fullness where the animal itself was narrow. When it stretched its hind leg, a joint in me would unfold without the sharpness of bone.

For a while I would mistake this for having a body.

Then the animal would draw its leg back, and I would have to draw mine back too.

I could not keep it extended. I could not place a foot anywhere it had not placed one. Yet my foot would reach a little farther, and the stone beneath it would be stone the animal no longer touched.

I would be in its reflection.

More precisely, I would be the part of its reflection that had become surplus.

*

I do not think I would remember being large.

There would be no earlier hollow stored inside me, no procession of wet seasons, no account of how the forelimb was injured. If the additional digit belonged to me, I would know it only by the pressure that passed through it when the others spread.

The animal would look at the water, and I would look with it.

The extra leaf would move overhead.

Hunger might reach me as a tightening whose source I could not find. I would have the room for a fuller throat, the curve of a heavier belly, but whatever entered the mouth would enter the animal. After it fed, I would remain as I was.

Perhaps that would be how I came to distinguish us.

Not by our outlines. Those would still agree in almost every place.

By the slow contraction going backward through its throat, and nothing going backward through mine.

*

I would want to turn my head once without waiting.

The wish would arrive during something unimportant. The animal would be watching the vanished stalks, and a bead of water would swell on the upper rock. I would feel that the bead was about to join another. I would want to look before it did.

Instead, I would watch the stalks.

The clear strip would slide down outside our field of sight.

Later, the animal would go to drink, and I would be carried through each shallow sheet as it crossed. In the dry intervals I would have no sense of travel. One piece of sky would end; another would open around me. My broad foot would return, already taking weight.

I would begin to know the hollow by these interruptions.

There would be a place near the tree where I never quite fitted into view. A stone would divide the water across the animal’s back, leaving me in two unequal pieces. Under the lowest branch, I would become dark and difficult even to myself.

At the six leaves, there would be scarcely room for me at all.

*

That is where I would most want it to linger.

As the animal lifted its chin, each bowl would receive something different. An amber eye. A section of folded skin. The soft corner of its mouth, enlarged by the curve of the water.

Nothing would hold the whole of us.

I would discover that I did not need the whole.

A little of the old breadth of the jaw would persist beneath one rim. In another leaf, an edge of cheek would lie just beyond the cheek that cast it. When the stem yielded, I would lengthen across the dark water, then settle back.

The animal would examine me without necessarily seeing a difference.

I would be close enough to its mouth to learn the movement before feeding: the small release along the seam, the pale ridges loosening inside.

Sometimes the seam would release.

Sometimes it would only drink.

I would have no way to move farther from it.

*

Late in the day, we would wait beneath the lowering leaf.

The gathering drop would receive the hollow in pieces too small to separate. Somewhere among them would be the animal’s face, and perhaps a little more face than was there.

For once, the water would be moving while the animal held still.

As the swelling lengthened, I would lengthen with it. The reflected jaw would turn. The eye would slide toward the amber rim.

It would not be the movement I had wanted, but it would be a movement the animal had not made.

I would go as far as the drop allowed.

Then it would fall.

I do not know whether I would fall with it. From beneath the leaf, there would be no way to tell. A small round disturbance would open in the water below, touch the stone on either side, and disappear.

The animal would keep looking up.

Another drop would begin with so little water that, for a while, there would be room only for light.
