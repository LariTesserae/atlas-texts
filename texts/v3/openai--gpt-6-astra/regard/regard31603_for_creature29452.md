The moth does not fall.

It folds its wings and descends for a little while, which is a different thing. Then it opens them into a current too slight for either person to feel and passes beneath Senn’s boot, carrying pollen toward a reach where no flowers have opened in eleven years.

The power that has come here lets it go.

For some time it has been attending to the difference between falling and departure.

***

At first it listens.

There is a great deal to hear, and not all of it travels through air. Water moves under pressures that would split the settlement’s cups. Sugars descend from leaves whose stalks stand beyond the horizon. Along a damaged limb, a slow chemical warning passes from cell to cell. The crown is not one creature in quite the way Senn is one creature. Nor is it merely a country.

The power follows a root downward until distance ceases to be a useful measure. It finds older growth, warmer chambers, other inhabitants. It finds soil. This is not, at present, the answer to anyone’s difficulty.

It returns to the fissure.

Senn’s next anchorage is sound. The apprentice’s is sound also. His fear of the moths costs him attention, but he is learning to account for it. The power does not take away the fear.

There is, however, a flaw inside the metal of Senn’s smallest hook. It has been growing for nine years. In another season it will reach the surface.

The power closes it.

Senn, who has been about to shift her weight, pauses.

“What?” says the apprentice.

She taps the hook against the bark. Once, and again.

“Nothing,” she says.

But she marks it with a thread and sets it aside when they climb home. A tool whose note has changed should be examined.

The power finds this encouraging.

***

It comes to her shelter in a shape that will fit through the doorway without requiring her to move anything.

This leaves it looking like a rather short person with a coat of no recognizable hide. Senn has an unwashed bowl on the place where a visitor might sit. She moves the bowl onto her bedding.

“Which way?” she asks.

“From below.”

She looks at its hands. There is no sap on them.

“That is not much of an answer.”

“No.”

Outside, someone is arguing about a missing fuel knife. The visitor waits while Senn unfastens her harness. When her two fingers fail to straighten, she puts her hand beneath her opposite arm.

“I can help with that,” it says.

“With what?”

“Your hand. And other things. But I would rather begin with something you can tell me about.”

Senn looks toward the door. Visitors with remedies ordinarily bring their own witnesses.

“What would you do to it?”

The explanation takes a while. She makes it use different words twice. Finally she brings out a length of worn cord, bends it where the fibers have thickened, and asks whether the trouble is like that.

“Enough like that,” it says.

“And afterward?”

“It will be your hand. It will still get cold. You will still need to be careful.”

“What do you take?”

“Nothing.”

She does not immediately offer it her hand.

The power stays. It does not eat the food she gives it, and when she challenges this, it admits that it has no need to. She takes the bowl back.

At last she holds out one finger.

“Only that one first.”

***

The following morning Senn wakes with her hand open beside her face.

She lies still for a long time.

Then she flexes the treated finger, and the other catches as it has caught every cold morning for three years.

This small discrepancy convinces her more thoroughly than a complete cure might have done.

She goes to find the visitor.

It is outside, helping a child retrieve a tethered cup that has become wedged beneath a floor. It could free the cup without difficulty, but the child is giving detailed instructions, several of which are useful.

“Do the other,” Senn says.

Then, before it can answer:

“There are people you should see.”

***

News of the visitor goes out on the heartwood plates.

The existing signals are inadequate. For a time the neighboring reach believes there has been a death, followed by a retreat, followed by an unusually large party of travelers.

Eventually someone is sent around.

Meanwhile, the visitor works.

It treats an infected foot. It restores sight to a woman who remembers the shapes of three grandchildren but has never seen the fourth. It cannot return her dead husband, and does not allow her to misunderstand this.

It leaves an old man’s crooked leg alone when he says he has had enough of people doing things to it. Three days later he asks whether the offer remains open.

It does.

Not everyone wants to be young. Almost everyone wants their teeth to stop hurting.

Senn’s scar remains. The visitor asks once, and she says the scar does not interfere with anything.

The work extends beyond the shelters. A sick grazer walks away from it without gratitude. Beneath a crossing, a predator with a festering wound sleeps while torn tissue closes around a splinter. It will hunt again. The visitor does not turn its hunger against the settlements, or cure it by starving something else.

These are not the hardest decisions it will have to make here. It does not pretend otherwise.

***

When asked what they need, the settlement says water.

They say it separately, and then together at considerable length.

The keeper wants the drinking hollow deepened. A hunter wants another hollow beyond the exposed saddle. Someone proposes bringing back the pond that split open before Senn was born. Two households disagree about where a new supply should be placed.

The visitor goes with them to inspect the sites.

It will not fill the existing pond beyond what the fork can carry. It shows them the strain, laying a hand on the bark so that, for a moment, each person can feel where the wood is pulling apart.

The keeper steps back first.

“Not there, then.”

They choose three places rather than one.

In each, the visitor grows a low, broad vessel that follows the living surface without cutting into it. The vessels are pale and faintly rough. Each has a covered drinking well, an overflow, and a shallow rim where small animals can reach the water without falling in.

They fill from no spring anyone can find.

The visitor explains that it is bringing the water from elsewhere, and that elsewhere has water to spare. The keeper asks to see. It takes her.

She returns quiet, with wet boots and a flat stone clenched in her hand.

For several weeks she continues to measure every cup.

The visitor does not laugh at her. Instead it helps her test the supply: emptied, covered, frozen, struck with a hammer, left unattended. There are things that can damage the vessels. It explains them. There are ways to mend them. It teaches those too.

Only after the second thaw does the keeper remove the narrow ladle from its customary place.

She hangs it inside her shelter.

***

Senn brings out her niece’s cord when she has watched enough work to believe in it, and enough waiting to believe that a request can be refused.

“Can you put the reaches back?”

The visitor looks across the gulf.

“No,” it says. “Not safely. There are people farther along who would lose their ground.”

“Then a bridge.”

“Yes. If both sides want one.”

She is irritated by the condition until she imagines her brother arranging a bridge that arrives directly through her garden.

The discussion takes longer than the building.

The far settlement wants the landing above its pond. Senn’s settlement wants it near the old anchorage. Neither place will bear what they propose. The visitor brings them together to argue on the same patch of bark, which improves the accuracy of their insults.

The niece comes with her father.

She is indeed tall. Her knee troubles her most after sitting, so she stands through much of the discussion, leaning on a stick. When Senn approaches, the girl glances at her father for help.

He offers none.

“I have your cord,” Senn says.

“Was it any use?”

“No,” says Senn. “Too good.”

The girl considers this.

“I can make a worse one.”

By then Senn is close enough to put her arms around her.

***

The bridge is not a single span.

The visitor raises a succession of open lattices between the reaches, each supported independently of the living crown. They yield to the movement of the branches. Their sides rise high enough to stop a crawling child, and their walking surfaces catch the foot without catching a hook.

At either end, broad joints permit years of growth.

The bridge workers examine everything. They insist on access beneath the deck, replaceable lashings, places to turn a laden grazer, and a way to close the crossing without standing out in the wind.

The visitor changes its design.

There are shelters along the span. Not because the bridge requires them, but because people who have spent their lives crossing narrow wood above darkness may need somewhere to stop.

Senn crosses on her belly the first time.

Halfway across, she rolls onto her side and looks through the lattice. Beneath her, the dark continues as far as it ever did. Something luminous moves through it and is gone.

The visitor is waiting at the nearest shelter.

“You could have made it so I wasn’t frightened,” she says when she reaches it.

“I could help, if you wanted.”

She thinks about this while catching her breath.

“Not today.”

It sits with her until she is ready to continue.

***

There is more work than can be done by visiting one shelter after another.

The power establishes places where it can be called. It makes them recognizable by touch as well as sight: a smooth hollow beside three raised ridges, reachable from the ground, with no requirement for a strong hand or a speaking voice.

It goes to settlements that have heard nothing of it. It goes to those whose neighbors would prefer they heard nothing. Where a branch is already failing, it holds the load while people and animals leave. Afterward it helps find somewhere to go.

It learns which gifts have become someone’s means of control and changes how those gifts can be reached.

It makes mistakes of convenience, of placement, of assuming two people mean the same thing by enough. It returns to repair them. People learn that they can complain without losing what they have received.

Some ask to leave the crown entirely.

It shows them other places before they decide. Some come back. Routes remain open.

Others want only a sound roof, an easier birth, a way to hear a distant daughter’s voice. The power does not make them undertake a new life to obtain these things.

***

Under Senn’s settlement, the garden continues to move.

The visitor offers to bring the flowers within reach.

Senn asks what that would do to the flowers, and receives an answer sufficiently complicated that she decides against it.

Instead, they build a suspended working platform. It can be moved along the fissure and lowered as the garden shifts. Senn and her apprentice spend days correcting the arrangement of its lines.

On its first working descent, he still catches a strap beneath his knee.

She still laughs.

There is room now to bring a second learner, and enough safety that Senn can stop working when she is tired rather than when the last cup has been gathered. Some days she does not descend at all.

One morning she takes her niece below.

They sit beside an opening flower while the apprentice works farther along the fissure. A moth crawls across the girl’s wrist. She becomes very still.

“Will it bite?”

“No.”

Its feet investigate the seam of her mitten.

Above them, someone crosses the bridge, the footfalls arriving faintly through the platform’s upper lines. From a shelter comes the uneven sound of an old man learning to walk without pain.

Senn waits for the moth to finish.

She has promised to show the girl how the clear liquid gathers in the flower’s throat. Afterward they will go home. There is food set aside, and her brother is coming over to complain about something he would once have had to save for nineteen years.

The moth opens its wings.

There is no need to hurry it.
