The power arrives during the low water and waits on the third step.

For a long time, it does not make itself any larger than a visitor. Rain darkens the shoulders of its coat. Its shoes are ordinary shoes, with grit in the welts.

It watches the brass washer.

When the water rises, a pale extension emerges beneath the control booth, travels against the current, and touches the washer where it rests on the sloping brick. Nothing is moved. The extension withdraws.

The power had thought the washer misplaced. Now it leaves it alone.

***

The first thing it gives the district is time in which nothing gets worse.

This is a small exercise of its strength, though a difficult one to perform precisely.

The failing bearing continues to turn, but loses no more of its surface. The roof bolt remains loose without loosening further. Behind the wall, the switch takes seven attempts to catch, as it did yesterday, and on the seventh it catches.

Leaves still fall. Roots grow. The water carries sediment through the old channels. In the settling tank, a new flower opens.

The power has not stopped the district. It is holding back the accumulation of faults while it learns which motions belong here.

At the first deep impact, it feels the pads lift all across the court.

Some are behind the shutters. Some are beneath the flooded brick. One is pressed against the old plates in the chamber behind the pump house.

The power feels the disturbance travel upward through the aquifer and the foundations. It could silence the sound. Instead, it follows the force that makes it.

It goes down without making a hole.

Beneath the hot water are layers folded under an immense, uneven load. There is movement there, too slow to see except at the moments when it catches and breaks free. The aquifer takes the blow. The masonry receives what the water passes on.

Farther down, there is more than the power can learn by listening once.

It returns to the third step.

When the next impact comes, the district receives it less sharply. The long pressure that lifts the water remains. The sudden force spreads beneath the foundations over a wider area, passing into stone that can bear it.

The pads rise again. The bodies hold against the piers.

The power waits with them. It does not expect one gentler shock to undo what the others have taught.

***

It begins its repairs with the loose belt.

There is no grand quieting of the pump house. The power sits on the service platform with its sleeves rolled up and watches the folded copper do its work. During the next rise, the inhabitant comes to the casing.

Its pads settle beside the power’s hand.

The power lays a new guide on the platform. The guide is brass, smooth at its edges, with room behind it for a pale fold to pass. It is neither attached nor offered directly.

The inhabitant examines it through most of a high-water interval. Its dark ridges hold one end. Fine extensions enter the mounting holes.

When the lever drops, it leaves the guide beside the casing.

The power does not install it.

At the following rise, the guide has been turned over.

At the next, it is held against the machine, slightly lower than the power had intended.

From there, the difference is apparent: a channel behind the mounting plate must remain open. Something thin passes through it on its way to the switch housing.

The power makes the adjustment.

Together they secure the guide. The inhabitant works one bolt, slowly. The power works the other at the same speed.

The chatter ceases.

For several seconds the court sounds wrong.

Pale pads touch the casing, then the guide, then the casing again. Under the loading platform, a broad fold opens and closes. The clocks continue toward their next sectors.

Eventually the inhabitant carries the green strip of copper away.

The power sees it later beneath the control booth, beside the glass, where it will remain available.

***

There are repairs that can be made without changing the inhabitant’s work, and repairs that change it profoundly.

The power learns to distinguish them.

It shields the electrical conduits from condensation, but leaves the warm stone bare. It clears a dangerous obstruction in the culvert, then restores the small eddy that used to collect fallen leaves against the west wall. It replaces a cracked pressure vessel without increasing the pumps’ appetite.

One dark pane in the roof is cleaned.

Only one.

Over the following days, leaves beneath it turn toward the brighter patch. The water there loses a little more heat. A feeding fold visits, withdraws, then returns along the shaded edge.

The power clouds half the pane again.

It had imagined light would be an uncomplicated gift.

It keeps watching.

***

The most difficult work is making it possible for work to go undone.

The district has survived through attention so constant that neglect and catastrophe have become close neighbors. A bolt left for another cycle. A root mat thickening against an intake. A switch failing on its eighth attempt.

The power builds reserves.

Behind the pumping court, within an unused foundation, it makes a chamber that can accept excess pressure and return it slowly. The chamber has broad passages, no cutting impellers, and surfaces that give a clear vibration when touched. Its inspection covers can be opened from either side.

It adds a second bearing where one has carried too much. It makes the shutters safe to halt midway. Beneath the platform, it places a dry catch for any part of the body that loses its grip when the water falls.

The catch is shallow and warm. Water drains out through slots too fine to admit a folded edge.

For days, nothing uses it.

Then a narrow body settles there after a rise. It stays through the low interval, loosely gathered, with no pads against a machine.

The power is elsewhere when this happens.

There is no reason it needs to be present.

***

In the old chamber, it sits beside the disconnected wheel.

The honey-colored plates are warm. Mineral deposits have formed along their lower edges, and one plate carries a fine crack that opens slightly whenever the cord extends.

The power could soften them. It could supply new tissue, quicken the slow contractions, make the cord strong enough to reach the court.

It does none of this at first.

It supports the cracked plate so that the movement no longer pulls it apart. It frees the iron brace from a little of the building’s weight without withdrawing it from the smooth opening that has grown around it.

Then it waits.

The cord turns the wheel partway.

Pauses.

Turns it back.

The power puts no hidden mechanism behind the movement. It does not make a lamp glow to reward it.

During one visit from the mobile portions, a fine extension reaches across the old plates and touches the supported crack. It remains there a long time. Later, the old cord extends farther than the power has yet seen, passing the wheel and coming to rest against the doorframe.

On its next visit, the power leaves a length of broad, warm rail between the chamber and the nearest bridge.

The rail is removable. There is shelter along its underside.

For eleven cycles it is untouched.

On the twelfth, the old cord curls around its nearest end.

***

Outside, the city has begun to notice that the district no longer threatens to fail.

This brings a different danger.

There are proposals for draining it, now that access appears feasible. Someone measures the court for storage. Someone else suggests that the raised water might be displayed at fixed hours, under clearer glass.

The power attends these conversations.

Here it allows itself to seem considerably less ordinary.

The people responsible for the district receive an accurate account of what occupies it, including the limits of that account. Maintenance funds acquire conditions. Ownership ceases to confer permission to destroy the habitat. The bowl, its water source, and its passages become a place whose continued life must be considered in every plan made around it.

There will still be visitors. There can be study. Repairs will be possible.

But nobody will have to demonstrate a human kind of mind to remain in their own home.

***

Late one afternoon, the power returns to the steps.

The belt runs true. The high electrical note is gone, though the pump house still grinds and the discharge still strikes its plate with a force felt through the paving.

Near the west wall, the cleaned portion of the roof lets a narrow patch of rain-bright light into the water.

The needles approach blue.

As the power descends, a loose tread steadies beneath its shoe.

It had overlooked that tread.

A pale grip holds it from below while it crosses. The power pauses long enough to feel the small adjustments, then steps onto sound brick.

When the water lifts, the inhabitant spreads beneath it. One portion goes toward the pumps. Another gathers yellow powder in the furrows of its upper surface. Under the loading platform, a third remains in the warm catch, its folds moving faintly with the current.

The old cord is visible along the rail outside its chamber.

The power sits beside the court. For this cycle, there is nothing it needs to repair.

A fine extension approaches its shoe, removes a leaf from the welt, and carries it toward the roots.
