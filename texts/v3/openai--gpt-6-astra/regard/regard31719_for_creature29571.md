The power arrives while the leaf is still caught across the entrance.

It could widen the gap. It could make the leaf supple, or carry it into the chamber on a current of air. But the youngster has already turned around. One pale forefoot reaches out, finds an edge, and pulls.

The power waits.

When the leaf disappears, it follows no farther than the opening. Inside, the young are arranging themselves against the female. One is trying to take the fig from her mouth. She holds it away, bites, and drops a piece between her forefeet. There is very little room. This does not appear to trouble them.

The power stays through the day.

It learns that the warm chambers are not necessarily the best ones, and that a passage too narrow for its occupants to pass each other may still be preferred to a spacious corridor. It learns the difference between the double click and the small sound an animal makes when a claw catches painfully in a joint. It attends to the walls without forgetting the bodies inside them.

There are eggs in the plaster. There are grubs in rotten wood that has retained the shape of a lintel. Beneath one sleeping female, separated from her belly by two courses of brick, a gecko holds perfectly still.

For a time, the power’s attention makes no difference at all.

Then it finds the tank.

The tank has a drinking lip worn rough by generations of feet. Below that lip, the lining is smooth. An adult can hang down, drink, and recover its weight. A youngster that falls cannot climb out. Nor can a beetle, once it has exhausted the circuit of the waterline.

There are small bones in the sediment.

The power considers lowering the water. But this is the last tank to dry when the rains fail, and there are roots drawing from it through a crack behind the lining.

Instead, beneath an unused overflow, it loosens a strip of ceramic already lifting from the wall. The strip settles at an angle, one end in the water, the other against a rough seam above it. Behind that seam is a narrow ledge connected to a drain.

The movement makes a single submerged knock.

The frogs stop calling. After a while they begin again.

That night, nothing uses the new way out. The next night a snail begins to graze along it.

The power does not require a more immediate result.

***

Farther upslope, a vault is failing beneath a route used by three families.

The roots across it have held the masonry together for years. Now one root is pulling upward as the tree leans toward an opening in the canopy. Each wet season the crack widens. Within a few rains the crown will fall, taking the route with it and crushing a chamber below.

The power could hold the vault forever.

But the tree would continue to grow against an impossible resistance. Water would find another weakness. The animals would keep sleeping beneath a ceiling that no longer belonged to the world around it.

In a dry afternoon, while the chamber is empty and its occupants are asleep elsewhere, the power lets a loose facing brick settle out of a neighboring wall. It lands in deep leaf litter. Behind it is an old service passage, choked at one end but sound along its floor, leading toward the same feeding courts.

For several nights, the opening is only an unfamiliar smell.

Then a male, retreating from a quarrel, backs into it. He remains there until the animal outside loses interest. On his way out, he notices beetles.

Others follow his scent.

Before the vault fails, the new passage has become ordinary. When the collapse comes, the power holds back one falling slab long enough for a fleeing tail to clear it. Then the slab falls too.

Dust moves through the courts. Birds lift from the figs. For three nights, animals approach the broken place and complain.

The power listens without putting the roof back.

***

Its care extends beyond these animals, though it has become fond of the dusty flanks and the exacting little feet.

It does not empty the walls of things that bite. It does not make every fig ripen at once. It watches the snails as closely as those who eat them, and it cannot pretend that their interests always meet.

Much of what it does is slow.

Where a whole stand of figs might be lost to one failing terrace, it helps a seed lodge on another level. Where rain has begun carrying soil out through a broken channel faster than leaves can replace it, it catches a fallen branch across the opening. The branch gathers litter. Roots enter. The next rain runs less red.

There are still bad nights. There are wounds it closes and others it cannot close without taking hold of nearly everything. It keeps attending, including when attention offers no clean answer.

Near dawn, the female returns with her young.

They are larger now. At the entrance, one pushes ahead of her and gets briefly wedged beside its sibling. There is an indignant scuffling. The female waits, half a fig between her teeth.

The power is present in the court, but no light marks it. The crickets do not quiet. Inside the wall, something shifts to make room.

All three go in.
