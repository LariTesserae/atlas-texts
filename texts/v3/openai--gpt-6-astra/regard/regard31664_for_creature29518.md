The power waits until the animal begins to comb again.

It has noticed the pause. It does not yet know what the pause means.

For a while it attends through the six points of contact: the hooks engaged in the root’s skin, the pads receiving the approach of pressure. What fills a human ear as noise is not noise here. There are distances in it. There is the weight of water beyond the left wall, the different tension of the island’s underside, the small interruption made by another animal walking.

The power does not quiet the chamber.

Nor does it mend the valve. The new tissue has seated properly. Behind it, cells are already thickening along the line of greatest strain. To make that margin rigid and everlasting would move the wear elsewhere. The plant is not waiting for a better engineer.

The power listens farther out.

It follows the old body beneath the plain, through junctions with different inhabitants, through roots thick enough to contain darkness untouched by daylight. It finds stores being replenished, salt being carried outward, a damaged shoot sealed below its injury. The hollow is neither the center nor an expendable corner. Its water has come through other places. Other places will receive it.

Then the power returns its attention to the animal in the light.

The scrap turns. A clear window appears in the brown velvet.

There is something caught beneath the shell.

It is not the thread removed during the last wet sigh. This is shorter, folded twice between the bases of two gills. Each movement of the gills draws it a little tighter. The animal has reached for it several times since leaving its notch. Its cleaning limb passes beside it.

The power could take the fiber away.

First it learns where the living surfaces end.

This takes almost no time by the knocking of the root, but it gives the task its whole attention: the delicate attachment of each gill, the current that must continue through them, the shell still firming after the molt. Nothing here is too small to be injured by a careless kindness.

When the next flushing sigh begins, the animal braces and tilts.

The power loosens one bend in the fiber.

The cleaning limb catches it.

For a moment the animal holds the little obstruction beneath its body. Then it draws the limb outward, and the fiber trails into the water. The current takes it toward the seam.

The animal remains tilted a fraction longer than usual. Water passes freely between the gills.

It lowers itself.

There is no new sound, no brightness at the entrance. Its feet report the root’s familiar tightening. It crouches before the knock.

The power stays.

It would be easy to mistake this for a sufficient answer: one fiber removed, one creature eased, the chamber left picturesque and intact. But the power is not attending to a picture. Beneath the next island, young bodies press against the plates of a pouch. The old animal’s inward-bending antenna explores a surface it has crossed many times. The frog is hungry.

It does not punish the frog for that.

It does not promise the crustacean that nothing will ever reach it.

Instead it keeps learning what help would cost, and to whom.

When the brood begins to disperse, there will be passages whose pressures it must understand before touching them. When the plant can no longer repair a valve, there will be more to do than preserve the sound of a working valve. When an animal is hurt, its hurt will matter even if the hollow continues unchanged.

For now, the root has water enough. The frog turns toward a movement at the entrance. The mother lifts her underside into the current.

The red-jointed animal finishes its scrap.

It lets go, raises the foreleg against which the fragment catches, and waits. The fragment slips free.

Then it crosses the short distance to the island’s edge and folds beneath it. Its back settles into the notch. Two legs lie forward, four behind.

The gills move without catching.

Above them the root knocks on, neither blessed into stillness nor abandoned to its working.
