The power notices the softened wall.

It notices, too, that the wall stiffens again after the neighbor leaves. Not entirely. A little of the accommodation remains, enough to make sitting there easier next time, not enough to determine who must sit there.

This seems worth understanding.

For a while, it does nothing.

It watches the corrector bring the seat back after taking it home to mend a split support. It watches a cleaning growth approach the threshold and turn aside because the room has no classification. It watches the neighbor arrive with food for two, having recovered enough to become critical of the meals previously brought to them.

The power could examine all the place’s records at once. Instead, it attends to an argument about whether the room needs another seat.

The corrector thinks it does.

The neighbor finds the floor comfortable and objects to being made into a reason for furniture.

They settle nothing. Both return.

***

Benevolence has practical difficulties here.

An abundance of water would be a catastrophe. Unregulated warmth would loosen the salts in load-bearing tissue. A sudden flowering, besides being biologically obscure, would block the sorting lanes.

Even repairing the corrector’s hand is not a simple matter.

The coldness comes from a narrowed passage beneath an old graft. Widening the passage would restore circulation, but the hand has spent many cycles accommodating its reduced charge. Its smallest movements now depend on those accommodations. The corrector uses it for delicate scraping. A restoration performed without preparation might leave them warm-fingered and unable to work.

The power does not mistake the absence of a complaint for consent.

Nor does it decide that, because help is complicated, the present arrangement must be best.

It begins with the cleaning growth.

On its next approach, a fine root finds a permissible route through the classification system. The room is entered as occupied space awaiting designation. Such spaces may be cleaned. They may not be reclaimed while occupied.

The designation requires periodic review.

The review interval is long enough to be useful and short enough that a mistake need not become a custom.

Dust withdraws from the threshold in narrow, pale lines.

The corrector notices immediately.

They spend much of the next cycle finding out who has entered the room.

***

The answer is unsatisfactory.

There is a record, but no originating department. There is an authorization, but it does not identify an authorizing body.

The corrector brings a reader, two obsolete readers, and the colleague who suggested sealing the doorway.

“Now will you report it?” the colleague asks.

“I am reporting it.”

“To whom?”

They consider this.

Eventually the corrector cuts a small panel of sign tissue and sets it against the inner wall.

WHO IS RESPONSIBLE FOR THIS SPACE?

The letters darken over the next few hours.

Beneath them, much more slowly, another line grows.

I HAVE BEEN HELPING TO KEEP IT OPEN.

The colleague goes outside.

The corrector remains by the doorway.

“That was not the question,” they say.

The panel admits this by shedding its second line. It grows a longer answer.

***

What follows is not a revelation that everyone understands.

The power explains what it can. The corrector asks questions that would not have occurred to a theologian and several that would.

Can it prevent the room from being crushed by new construction?

Yes.

Has it examined the lower decking?

Some of it.

Is the room a mouth?

No.

Does it require attendance?

No.

Can it read a person’s internal salts?

It could examine them, but will not do so without permission.

The corrector thinks of the cold hand and puts it in their coat.

“Do not.”

The lettering changes.

I WILL NOT.

There are further questions about freight, ownership, and whether something can be held responsible if it has no location at which a notice can be delivered.

At the power’s suggestion, they use the panel.

At the corrector’s insistence, the panel keeps copies.

***

The first request is not for the hand.

A gate attendant in an adjoining district has been unable to take a full cycle away from their gate for longer than the corrector has lived in their present home.

The attendant does not want a miraculous replacement. The last attempt to provide one made the gate admit every third consignment into a wall.

They want the dependency removed properly.

They want their pay to continue during the work.

They want the young replacement worker, who was blamed for the wall incident, present at the inspection.

The power can make the first of these happen. The other two require people.

It provides a description of the gate’s dependencies precise enough that the maintenance crews are initially offended. Then they find an error.

The error concerns a trace material on the attendant’s coat. It is not part of the coat. It comes from a particular roasted bud eaten during the middle of the cycle.

The power corrects the description.

This improves the crews’ opinion of it considerably.

The adjoining lines are supported while the grafts are changed. For once, a shutdown has room in it for mistakes. Nobody has to put a hand into moving tissue.

The attendant’s first leave is disappointing.

They go to a cultivation exhibition and dislike most of the entries. They return early, but do not open the gate.

On their second leave, they go somewhere else.

***

More requests arrive.

Some concern narrowed passages, failing rooms, dangerous seams. Others concern promotions, household disputes, and a conviction that the district across the western boundary is receiving better food.

The power does not treat all of these as the same kind of problem.

It will not make the corrector like their neighbor. It will help establish whether the neighbor’s convalescent tissue is healing badly. It will not decide which household should possess a disputed memorial strip. It can show them how to move the strip without injuring it, if they choose to move it.

Some decisions remain slow.

The power finds this painful without finding it intolerable.

It does act without waiting when a nursery support begins to fail. It holds the slings level, sounds the local warning, and keeps holding until the children have been carried out. Afterward it gives an account of what it changed.

People disagree about that account. Several think it should have warned them sooner.

They are right.

***

The room becomes busy enough to be unpleasant.

People wait on the platform with wrapped meals and evidence. Someone proposes receiving desks. Someone else starts growing a partition to separate urgent requests from ordinary ones.

The corrector arrives one evening and finds their seat behind a counter.

They stand looking at it.

The power has been attending to a damaged transit seam. It returns its attention to the room.

“I brought that here to sit,” the corrector says.

The counter stops growing.

It takes considerably more work to remove than it took to begin.

After that, the power maintains other places where questions can be brought. Existing service rooms are adapted by agreement. Traveling crews carry contact panels. People confined to their homes can reach it through roots they already use.

The unused platform becomes quiet again.

For several cycles the corrector does not visit.

When they return, the seat is where they left it.

***

The power has not forgotten the deck.

Deep below the maintained districts, tissues carry pressure beyond their original specifications. Some have had no dormant interval since before the current ceiling grew. There are growth chambers that continue issuing material to buildings long reclaimed, and old warning signals passing through roots whose receiving organs no longer exist.

The power does not wake everything.

Some systems are merely old. Some should be allowed to finish.

It begins restoring the possibility of rest to the ones still carrying weight. Loads are transferred by degrees. New supports grow beside the old, rather than through them. When a buried chamber finally releases its charge, the surface above it settles by less than the thickness of a delivery label.

Three surveyors notice.

So do the gray animals.

Their crossings change. Small groups appear in districts where nobody remembers seeing them. The power protects their routes but does not lure them into the regulated enclosures.

Asked what the creatures are for, it gives the best account it has of their feeding, nesting, and movements.

The administrator asks again.

The second answer is shorter.

THE CROSSINGS SHOULD CONTINUE WHETHER OR NOT WE FIND AN ADDITIONAL USE FOR THEM.

The old delay code remains.

***

Much later, the corrector asks about the hand.

They want an examination first. They want to know how much sensation might return, how long retraining would take, and whether treatment can be stopped between stages.

There are limits to what can be promised.

They accept the first stage.

During recovery, they sit in the room with a hinge balanced on their knee. The neighbor occupies the softer place along the wall and offers advice without relevant expertise.

Outside, the decking carries its enormous freight. A warning leaf opens. Somewhere beyond the doorway, a carrier comes gently to rest.

The corrector presses two fingers against the neighbor’s wrist.

It is an ordinary contact: stop talking for a moment.

The neighbor stops.

For the first time in many cycles, the corrector can feel the answering pressure clearly.

They sit that way until the leaf closes.

Neither owes the room anything.
