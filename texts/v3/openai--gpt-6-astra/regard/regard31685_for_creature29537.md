The power does not arrive from above.

Above is a direction the city uses for other things. To descend shining through the clouds would be to put a great deal of weight on an uncertain part of the place.

Instead, you become aware that the empty stretch of ledge beside you is occupied.

Nothing sits there that you can describe. The warmth coming through the stone remains the same. The pale fold beside your knee has not withdrawn. But when you shift your foot, you do so with the small consideration you would give to someone else’s foot, and find that the consideration has somewhere to go.

For a while, this is all.

The power listens to the falls.

It can distinguish every pressure in the sound. It can follow the deep vibration through the scarred body, through the wet roots, through chambers that share a wall without sharing a location. It can hear where the roar enters the little folded things along the brink.

Knowing this does not yet tell it whether the sound is pleasant.

It listens longer.

***

There are things it could do.

It could release the water.

The droplet beside your knee would lengthen, detach, strike the rind. The falls would spend themselves into the enormous depth below. Foam would break. Somewhere in the fallen trunk, a fiber would at last give way.

It could bring a wind across the plateau and make the leaves turn their purple undersides toward the sun.

It could give the city a morning after this morning.

Before doing any of these things, it attends to the filaments laid against the roots.

They are delicate. There are more of them than you first understood. Beneath the paving, their flattened tips occupy surfaces folded so closely together that a single inch of stone contains what, elsewhere, would be a considerable journey.

The water touches all of them.

The power leaves the droplet where it is.

***

You become conscious of your hand resting close to the pale body. You have touched it before. Now you wonder whether you should have.

The presence beside you does not turn this uncertainty into guilt.

It does not tell you that the creature enjoyed being touched, either.

You lift your hand and settle it on your own knee.

In a pit beneath the parapet, the view of your hand becomes very clear.

There is the ridge of a knuckle, a small reddened place where the hot paving has pressed against your palm. Behind it you can see the ledge from a direction unavailable to you.

Beside your image lies an unoccupied length of stone.

The power has not concealed itself. But it has declined to make the creature see it in a shape chosen for the creature.

Something is offered instead.

Not a word. Not a demand for movement. Not even a question that must be understood before it can be refused.

At the edge of the inhabitant’s attention, wherever that edge may be, there is now a difference it can disregard. A surface against which attention could rest, as the body rests against the generous shelf.

No part of the creature has to reach it.

For a long time, nothing happens.

The power does not improve the offer by making it brighter.

***

You are the first to need anything.

The heat has begun to make you sick. You had not noticed how carefully you were holding your head, or how much effort it took to swallow. The abundance of clear water has made your thirst feel unreasonable.

Beside you, on the ledge, stands a small cup.

It is plain, with a handle large enough for two fingers. The water in it moves when you pick it up.

You look toward the leaf.

Its droplet is still attached.

The water tastes of very little. You drink, wait, and drink again. A coolness settles around your face, no wider than necessary. The fronds overhead do not stir.

When you set the cup down, you can hear its base touch the stone.

The roar has not diminished. Somehow, a little room for hearing has been made inside it.

“Are they trapped?” you ask.

You hear your own voice with an intimacy that makes you lower it.

The answer comes without sound, but with none of the uncertainty of a thought you might have had yourself.

*I do not yet know.*

You look at the pale fold. Its weight is gathered comfortably against the wall. That comfort now seems neither proof nor disguise.

The presence stays beside you.

***

Eventually, the view in one of the dark pits is different.

You cannot catch the alteration taking place. You notice only that the pit no longer shows your hand.

It shows the underside of a body, pale and densely fringed. A root passes between two transparent tips. Beyond them lies a narrow space, and beyond that another surface, close enough that the two almost touch.

You lean forward.

The view does not become clearer for you.

The power attends to it.

There is no great illumination. Nothing in the city opens. The scar remains where it was.

But the presence beside you becomes, in a way you feel through your resting feet, more exact.

It has been shown somewhere.

Whether the place is painful, cherished, merely interesting, or offered in return for the view of your hand, you cannot tell.

The power does not hurry past the distinction.

***

By the time you are ready to leave, nothing has been repaired that you can see.

The ascending causeway still takes you down. The fallen trunk still keeps its last fibers. Through the low passage, the mountains continue to contain a minute version of the passage itself.

You are shown a route you can walk. No wall is cut to make it easier.

At the last chamber, you turn back.

The cup remains on the ledge. Beside it is the pale folded inhabitant, and beside the inhabitant, sufficient space.

You understand that the power will not remain merely until you are out of sight. Nor will its staying oblige anything here to become different.

If the view offered through the dark pit takes a thousand years to understand, it will receive more than a thousand years of attention.

And if it is only a view, there will have been someone there to see it.

You continue toward the mountains.

Behind you, the city is neither quieter nor less strange.

It has not been mistaken for an emergency.

It has not been passed by.
