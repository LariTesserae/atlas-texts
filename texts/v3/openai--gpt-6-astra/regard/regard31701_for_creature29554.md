The power does not enter through the notch.

There is no footfall on the paving, no shadow laid across the bench. The small creature on the lowest step keeps its margin lifted until the excess water has passed. Then it settles back against the stone.

For a while, nothing in the hollow is different.

The power attends to the springs first. It follows the water down through the limestone, through places where the rock is warm and places where it is under strain. It learns how much the outlet can carry, what a fallen roof slab would displace, which seams would dry if the sluice were lifted. It does not mistake the water’s steady height for a promise.

Then it attends to the being.

This takes longer.

The visible contractions tell only part of what happens. There are disturbances that travel through the body without reaching an exposed edge, and slow releases of tension that look, from outside, exactly like stillness. Some folds press firmly against the rock because that pressure is useful. Others have nowhere to loosen. The power learns the difference.

It finds the strip caught beneath the sluice.

The narrowing has already begun on either side. There is still living connection through the trapped portion, but less each day. The mineral holds it along a line no wider than a thread.

The power does not pull.

It loosens a few grains on the stoneward side of the deposit. They pass into the outflow separately, indistinguishable from the other grains the water carries. Then it waits.

For a long time the strip continues its old work. It withdraws as far as the binding permits, extends, withdraws again.

On the next withdrawal, it comes free.

The movement is small. A pale length slides beneath the board and disappears into the groove. Under the bench, one fold grows a little fuller. The narrowing on either side does not immediately reverse, but it stops.

The power leaves it alone.

There are other bindings in the hollow. Not all can be loosened safely. One crust supports a portion of flesh that has become too thin to hold its own shape; another seals a dead end through which hotter water would otherwise enter. The power does not make the body clean. It does not strip away the dull coat and expose everything tender underneath.

Where release would help, it makes release possible.

At the drain, it does something more lasting.

Beyond the occupied groove, on the side away from the body, the stone contains a short, closed seam. The power opens it grain by grain. It makes no new outlet and lowers no basin. The seam rejoins the old channel beneath the board, giving the water another passage should the first one narrow.

When the work is finished, the sound under the bathhouse has scarcely changed. There is a little less friction in it.

The being continues to move its strip of flesh through the groove. It has no reason yet to do otherwise. But the height of the central pool no longer depends quite so much on that strip remaining alive and in motion.

Above the bench, a roof beam has softened around one of its ends. The power eases the weight it bears onto the stone beside it. It does not raise the roof or widen the opening to the sky. The dimness is shelter here.

The broken cup stays in its niche.

So do the depressions left by things that are gone. The power has no wish to fill every vacancy.

Near the lowest step, it finds a pocket behind the dressed edge. The pocket is empty, cool enough, and close to the waterline, but a ridge of deposit prevents a reliable film from reaching its back. The power lowers one small place in the ridge.

Water enters. It spreads across the floor of the pocket, darkening it.

The detached young being remains where it is.

The power waits through several changes in the spring’s pressure. It does not push the creature toward the shelter. A suitable place is not the same as a place already chosen.

Eventually, a translucent edge extends around the corner of the step. It touches the wet stone inside the pocket and stays there. Over the following days, more of the small body follows. A portion remains outside, feeding along the dressed face.

The pocket is not perfect. Deposits will grow there. Food will sometimes be scarce. But it has more than one way out, and the film reaches it even when no excess water crosses the step.

The power does not make the hollow inexhaustible. More nourishment would feed other lives as well, some of them dangerous to this one. More warmth would quicken the mineral growth. A wider body might have more difficulty keeping all its parts supplied. It offers no abundance whose consequences it has not understood.

Instead, it continues to notice.

When the entrance film thins, it knows. When stone begins to press upon living flesh, it distinguishes a useful brace from a closing trap. It allows the ordinary small movements of the place to go on, intervening before the slow ones become irreversible.

There is no message in the water. Nothing asks the being to understand why a passage has opened, or to become less cautious, or to show gratitude by coming out from beneath the bench.

After a time, the central folds rest somewhat farther apart.

The soft projection appears between them. It touches the floor, finds a loose grain, and takes it in. A second grain is left where it lies.

Outside, the pools remain full. The cup rests against the lobe fitted to its back. Beneath the lowest step, a feeding fringe advances over newly wetted stone.

The hollow looks almost exactly as it did.

There is less that must be endured to remain there.
