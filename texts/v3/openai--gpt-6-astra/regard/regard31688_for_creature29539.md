The next pressure does not reach the small one.

It approaches through the new ground, flattening the tool marks as it comes. Water lifts along its leading edge. The three larger bodies begin to draw together.

Then the pressure stops.

There is no sound of resistance. Nothing bright appears above the plain. A narrow fold rises in the wet earth, taking the force along its length and carrying it around the occupied patch. On the far side, the work resumes. A ridge grows toward the brown vacancy.

The small one continues getting up.

It has gathered the unfamiliar chip too far to one side. Each time it raises an edge, the weight turns it slightly. At last it lets itself settle and moves the chip back through its body.

Whatever has interrupted the pressure waits through this.

It could hold the creature upright. It could fit the fragment into a more useful position. Instead, it keeps the ground steady beneath the next attempt.

—

For a time, the new attention is difficult to distinguish from good fortune.

A basin does not empty while two bodies are drinking from it. A hollow beneath a ledge remains damp through an interval of increasing heat. Where a creature has been trapped by its own bracing, the bank acquires another support.

This last change is made slowly.

The creature has spent so long beneath the bank that its upper surface bears the bank’s weight even when that weight is briefly lifted. Its folds do not open. The pale lines along its sides have become almost straight.

Beside it, clay gathers into a short, thick column.

The column is imperfect. Its first broadening presses against a sensitive edge, and the creature contracts.

The clay withdraws.

A second attempt leaves more room.

Over the following hours, the weight passes from the body into the column. The creature remains where it is. Water is allowed to bead within reach, but is not poured over it. The space beyond the bank is kept clear.

Eventually one edge loosens.

It reaches out, tests the ground, and retreats.

Much later it comes out carrying three heavy flakes and a tiny piece of blue glaze. There is scarcely any blue elsewhere on the plain.

It moves only a short distance before stopping. Its underside still holds the shape of the burden.

Another creature comes to it. Wet clay is placed between them. Pressure is applied, then eased.

The attention remains nearby. It keeps the bank from settling while they work.

—

It learns that an empty-looking wall may be occupied.

That the shallow dishes beneath certain ledges are not stores of repair material.

That a body pressed against a failing seam may be unable to leave, rather than unwilling.

It learns something else at the edge of the soaking fields.

There, it makes a crossing firm.

The distance settles. The far bank ceases its slow recession. Heat rises evenly, and the pale surface will bear weight all the way across.

The approaching group does not use it.

They gather at its beginning, touch it, and turn aside. One lays a broad underside against the old, unstable lip. Another reaches toward a half-submerged plate.

The new crossing has been made too smooth. There is nowhere to release a contact gradually, nowhere for a fold to catch while the next part takes its weight.

Small ridges are added.

The group waits.

One ridge is lowered. A shallow depression is made behind it, holding a little warm water.

This time a creature ventures onto the crossing. It travels partway, returns, and presses against the others. They cross joined together, though the distance no longer requires it.

The attention does not separate them.

—

When the sky next begins to sag, something reaches upward from the plain.

It is not a tower. It has no summit. The force that has been keeping small places steady follows the approaching pressure to wherever pressure begins.

For several minutes, every basin holds its water at a slant.

Then, high in the dim plaster, a fresh impression appears.

A broad hollow.

Beside it, another.

The shapes resemble those on the ground, but they are being made from the other side.

Whatever works beyond the sky does not immediately stop. A scoring point advances across the soaking fields. Beneath it, plates divide and slide apart.

The new attention holds the point above a cluster of occupied blisters.

The point shifts.

Again it is held.

There is a long stillness. Moisture gathers on the abandoned paddle in the old district and runs along its split end.

Then the point is lifted.

The creatures beneath the blisters remain concealed. They have felt only the beginning of a storm that has not yet arrived.

Across the underside of the sky, a small pattern forms: closely spaced ridges, branching and turning back upon themselves. The same pattern lies beneath the creatures’ bodies, where they touch the ground to discover whether it will hold them.

The pattern remains for a long time.

When work resumes, the first tool to touch the plain is not the scoring point.

It is something broad and light. It rests against the surface without changing it.

It waits.

—

The world does not become finished.

Water still appears along seams. The soaking fields still receive their pale, unwieldy pieces. Beyond the last shore, lengths of new ground are joined onto the old.

But before a district moves, warmth passes through its uppermost layer.

At first the creatures hide from it.

The warmth passes again, and afterward the adjustments begin elsewhere.

Over many repetitions, some come to distinguish this new feeling from the heat that drives them out of shelter. It is shallow and brief. It leaves the water where it was.

Once it has passed, there is time.

Time to detach from a cross-wall. Time to gather a young body that has spilled itself in haste. Time to go back beneath a ledge for a fragment left in a depression.

If they do not leave, the pressure waits.

Sometimes it finds another route.

There are places where no warning can be made sufficient: an injured creature cannot unfold, a pocket between two adults cannot withstand a crossing, an old body has become too extensive to move without tearing.

Around these, the work is altered.

The great plain begins to acquire persistent irregularities.

An isolated shelf survives where a trough would otherwise run straight. A low bank curves around a shallow deposit of useless shards. In one district, a sheet of new land has a broad opening through which the old surface remains exposed.

The joins around these places are carefully made.

—

Not all injuries can be eased by removing their cause.

Some bodies retain hollows through which water continues to escape. Some draw back from sound ground long after the ground has become dependable. One lies beneath a ledge through many quiet intervals, holding its fragments so tightly that they cannot slide.

The attention does not pull it into the open.

It keeps several routes open from the ledge. One leads to a basin, one to a place where others gather, and one to an unoccupied hollow. The heat along all three remains bearable.

A visitor begins leaving small quantities of fine clay beside it.

For a long while, the clay is untouched.

Then there are marks in it.

The marks are neither repairs nor attempts to move. They are short grooves, pressed close together, crossed by a longer line. The next visitor puts a fold against them.

Later it makes a groove of its own.

No implement passes over that patch.

—

The small one grows around the chip it was given.

The piece is awkward, thick at one end and thin at the other. It learns arrangements that accommodate this. Eventually it acquires two more fragments, but keeps the first near a particular fold.

It does not become fearless.

At every unfamiliar join it still lowers its sensitive surface and waits. Sometimes it crosses. Sometimes it chooses another way.

One day it reaches a cross-wall where a bead of water has begun to swell.

It stops.

Fine clay lies nearby, damp enough to work. The creature takes some into its side and kneads it against the thick end of its chip. Then it presses the clay into the crease.

The leak closes.

For a while the creature remains against the wall, feeling the water held on the other side.

There is another small fault farther along. It approaches, touches it, then turns away toward a sheltered dish where several others are resting.

The fault remains.

After a time, a little clay rises from beneath it and seals the opening.

Beyond the dish, the plain is being extended. The work proceeds with its slight sounds: a scrape, a release, the soft settling of something large.

Under the creatures, the ground stays still.
