The power first noticed the folded scrap of packing cloth.

It was not an offering. The man had carried it because the table rocked, and because remembering a small inconvenience at the right time was one of the ways he cared for his household.

The power watched him fit it under the leg. Then it attended to the water beneath the floor, the old chambers, the fine living surfaces that kept those chambers open. It followed the cable out across the bank.

By morning it knew a good deal about the town. It did not yet know what the people would agree to change.

***

Its first visitor was the wall-tender.

She had gone below the square to investigate a change in the drainage note. In a chamber where she ordinarily had to crouch, she found someone standing upright.

The chamber had not grown taller. She checked that before anything else.

“I can come up,” the stranger said.

“Then do. You’re making it difficult to judge the clearance.”

They sat on the bench beside the weighing platform. The stranger wore a coat of no particular distinction. Water from the morning’s condensation lay thinly in the square.

The wall-tender unrolled her instruments.

“What have you done?”

“Nothing here yet.”

“What are you proposing?”

“To help.”

She waited.

“I can provide warmth without drying the walls. I can keep the pumps working if the cable fails. There are illnesses I can mend. There are loads your oldest foundations should no longer be carrying.”

“And the price?”

“There isn’t one.”

She closed the leather roll, then opened it again to retrieve her pencil.

“That will take some explaining.”

***

The meeting lasted well into the evening.

At first people asked questions intended to expose a fraud. Then they asked questions intended to establish the limits of the offer. These took longer.

Could warmth be supplied to one room and not the next? Could it be stopped? Would it accelerate growth? Would it kill the roof beetles? Did accepting it place the town under any obligation? Who would control it? What happened when the stranger left?

The power answered. Where an answer needed demonstration, it demonstrated on a basin of water, a scrap of living rootwood, and eventually the damp corner of the meeting room.

The corner remained damp. The people sitting beside it became comfortably warm.

Someone took off a coat.

The foreman asked whether the workshops could run without the motor.

“Yes.”

The operator looked at the table.

“But you needn’t decide that tonight,” the power said.

The woman from the northern row wanted to know whether her upstairs rooms could be made safe.

“Yes.”

“Without moving me?”

“Yes.”

“People have said that before.”

“Then choose someone you trust to inspect them before you go upstairs.”

She asked about the wardrobe next.

***

The power did not begin by making the town vigorous.

There were places where vigor would have been disastrous: a pipe held in a careful truce with its socket, a drying chamber whose walls already pressed against the racks, the empty corner beside a woman’s stove.

For several days it accompanied the wall-tender. It listened to the reservoir keeper, the drain workers, and the man who had fitted ordinary shutters. It read the angry letters.

It also attended to the town in a manner for which no resident had an instrument.

What it found was neither a sleeping human mind nor an empty mechanism. There were distinctions held across the root mass: injury remembered in diverted growth, dry years preserved in the arrangement of storage tissues, an inclination toward light checked by generations of cutting. Some responses belonged to a single wall. Others passed slowly beneath whole streets.

The power did not assemble these into a voice and announce that the town had spoken.

It learned where warmth would distress it. It learned where its trained shape required a continuous effort, and where a small alteration would give relief. On one northern terrace, it discovered that a thin strip of apparently sound flooring was keeping a great buried section from settling.

The terrace was closed that afternoon.

Under supervision, children came to watch the repair. They were allowed to ask questions and forbidden to keep the warning pegs.

***

The first lasting gift was fitted beside the power-house motor.

It was a dark, smooth object with two ordinary terminals and a handwheel. The operator had insisted on the handwheel. Turned one way, it supplied power; turned the other, it stopped. There was no concealed reserve that might wake unexpectedly while someone worked on the shaft.

The power allowed it to be tested under every load the town could produce.

The drawing machine no longer slowed when the pumps began.

After a week the operator stopped keeping his hand near the old supply switch. After a month he began rebuilding a bearing he had never been able to take out of service for long enough.

The motor remained where it was. The cat’s folded coat remained above it. A small warm plate was installed beneath the coat, with no entry made in the maintenance accounts.

Warmth came to the houses separately.

Some households wanted every room comfortable. Others wanted warm beds, a warm kitchen, and the rest as it had always been. The woman who liked her empty corner accepted a heated footrest and refused everything that would have required moving her chair.

The wall-tender took measurements through the first cold spell. Sap movement remained steady. Condensation did not gather inside the fibers. Under the streets, the creatures of the permitted rot continued sorting their pale legs through the dark.

People began leaving their coats on hooks.

***

There were less visible changes.

Medicine arrived when it was needed, even when the roads were closed. Anyone who wished could meet the power privately. Some came about pain they had ceased mentioning. Some came with someone else and did most of the talking until gently asked to wait outside.

Not every grief could be treated as an illness. The power stayed for those conversations too.

It made copies of the town’s records, with permission, and put them somewhere fire and moisture could not reach. The originals remained in their cupboards. Marginal corrections were preserved, including those whose wording the authors later regretted.

In the northern house, sound fibers took the burden of the upper rooms without forcing the lower ones outward. The wardrobe was reassembled upstairs.

The woman stood before it for some time.

Then she opened a drawer that had always stuck.

“That’s new,” she said.

“Would you prefer it as it was?”

“No.”

***

The workshops could now have produced much more.

For a while they did. There were orders overdue, roofs to repair, debts that had narrowed people’s choices for years.

Later, a meeting was held about reducing the hours.

It was a contentious meeting. The new warmth did not improve anyone’s arithmetic or make them agree about money. But the pumps no longer competed with the southern yard, and nobody had to argue that cold floors were unavoidable. There were fewer necessities to shelter behind.

Some people chose more work. Others chose less. A young resident left to study metal casting, with enough money to return if she wished and no promise that she would.

Her house was kept for her because she asked, not because the town assumed.

***

The power stayed available. It did not require a shrine, a festival, or a name under the clock.

Eventually its presence became one of the facts residents explained to visitors, along with the drainage rules and the reason for the awkward arch behind the houses. Explanations differed. The power corrected only the ones likely to cause trouble.

One evening the late-shift worker came home with warm hands.

His daughter seized his wrist before he could reach her neck. Discovering that he had lost his usual advantage, she accused him of cheating.

There was soup on the stove. The drainage notice on the table concerned work already completed.

When they sat down, the table rocked.

He reached into his pocket, found nothing, and went to fetch a scrap from the kitchen drawer.

The floor was safe. The house was well. Outside, the pumps were moving water through the long dark without drawing heat from anyone’s supper.

He folded the cloth twice, tried it beneath the leg, and sat down again while the soup was still hot.
