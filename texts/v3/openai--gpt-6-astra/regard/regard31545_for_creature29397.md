The power’s attention reached the hollow without arriving at its entrance.

No weight crossed the slabs. The shrubs cast no additional shadow. Along the back wall, the larger animal continued the shallow movement near its mouth, and the smaller kept its feet spread against the inside of the seam.

At first, the power did nothing.

It followed the warmth through the stone. It learned how long the back wall held the day, and how the cooling of the plain eventually drew that warmth outward. It attended to the air beneath the dead branch, where a change too slight to wet the wood could nevertheless make feeding possible.

It waited through several such changes.

The larger animal’s stillness was not always the same. Sometimes its body rested broadly against the rock, almost all its feet released. Sometimes it held itself just clear of the surface, supported at many small points. On a few nights it began the journey toward the entrance and turned back before reaching the shrub.

The power learned the difference. It did not take the animal’s slowness for a difficulty that needed correcting.

There were other things to learn. The smaller one sometimes lost its purchase because it reached too far, and sometimes because the stone itself gave way. After a fall of two grains, it might remain motionless long after there was anything left to fear. But eventually a foot would extend again. Its soft end would settle over a different irregularity. The rest of the body would follow.

The power left those irregularities in place.

Farther within the shrub, where the pale branch joined the living stem, a fracture had begun. It was hidden under a last strip of bark. Each day’s heating opened it a little; each night’s cooling closed it without restoring what had separated. Before many more seasons, the branch would fall with its feeding surface against the earth.

The power held the fracture for a while.

It did not turn the dead wood living. It did not prevent the films upon it from changing, or keep the shrub from releasing what it could no longer support. It gave the branch time enough for another, lower length of wood to weather smooth, within reach of the same route.

Neither animal noticed.

Later came a succession of dry nights.

The larger one went to the branch twice and found little. The smaller reached the entrance for the first time, sheltered beneath a fold of bark, and returned with dust adhering to the surface it used to feed.

The hollow remained warm. Its dust stayed loose. Nothing in its appearance asked for help.

The power had now watched long enough to recognize the cost of the waiting.

Beyond the slight rise on the plain, cool air was collecting in a shallow run of ground. There was moisture in it, though not enough to form mist. The power allowed a little of that air to take a different course.

It entered the hollow without stirring the grass.

Under the pale branch, the thin living film softened. Along the lower wood, smaller patches became active. There was no drop large enough to drown a foot, no wetness running down into the powder, no sudden richness that would draw a crowd of other mouths.

That night the larger animal fed.

Before morning, the smaller found the lower branch.

The power did not make every night like that. It continued to attend: to what the animals could endure, to what they sought, to the small replenishments by which endurance became something other than a gradual loss.

Much of the time, there was nothing to do.

The warm bend remained warm. The narrow seam remained narrow, though the smaller animal would someday leave it for another. Dust accumulated on the ledges and was disturbed along the old routes. Beneath the ceiling, a thick rear fold hung exposed while the rest of the larger body rested in its preferred indentation.

No sign marked the hollow as protected.

On a night when the wood was suitable, the larger animal emerged from its groove. Its leading feet entered the familiar fracture and spread. Behind them, the body gathered itself, fold by fold.

It went toward the branch because it wanted to go there.

The way was still open.
