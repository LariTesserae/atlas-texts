The first thing the power did was wait for the beetle to be taken indoors.

It could have held the descending air in the stairwell all night. It could have made the folded legs walk. But Tem was watching them closely, and the movement was hers: a consequence of the thin paper, the particular fold, the care she had taken with a joint. It left that discovery alone.

When she picked the beetle up, the air had already stopped.

That night the power went through the neighborhood by the paths heat took.

It passed through Sella’s floorboards, through the wall behind the shoe cabinet, through a bolt whose head had become too hot to touch. It followed the conduit out beneath the upper streets. The district above was asleep in rooms where windows could be opened on two sides. Farther away, in a municipal yard, a valve stood half closed because a man who had died four years earlier had once been told to leave it that way.

There were reasons for some of the heat. There were reasons for less of it than anyone supposed.

The power traced the water next. It did not mistake the four green pumps for the source of the difficulty, nor the disputed spout for its whole extent. Beyond the meters and pressure governors it found a reservoir deep enough that its lower water remained cold through summer. The cut had been entered in the supply tables as a group of industrial premises with incidental accommodation.

There was a number beside this description.

The power remained with that number for some time.

By morning, nothing visible had changed.

Sella woke on the floor. Iven examined the float. The woman with the brown growth waited for two men to finish talking, then ceased waiting and lifted it in front of them.

At the eastern stair, a person sat with a roll of drawings across their knees.

They had no badge. This was an inconvenience. The loading clerk asked who had authorized them to occupy the stair.

“No one,” they said, and moved to let him pass.

They did not ask the neighborhood what it needed as though nobody had already said. There were complaints in municipal drawers, letters returned for insufficient particulars, requests copied carefully by people whose hands were tired. The visitor brought these down in a box.

Some were stained. One had been folded around a smaller, angrier letter.

They set the box beside the shoe cabinet and asked which requests were still accurate.

This took three days.

People corrected one another. A woman who had asked for a washing place no longer wanted it where she had first proposed: the sound would carry into her room. The clerk’s suggested handrail would narrow a landing too much for the laundry trays. Iven objected to replacing the pumps.

“Which part of them?” the visitor asked.

He began explaining. They stayed.

After a while he stopped explaining what a pump was and told them what these pumps did.

The visitor wrote down the trembling strips of tin.

There were also things nobody put in the box. Sella did not submit a request for somewhere to wear her hat. The young men did not ask for a place to sit that would not immediately become everybody’s place to sit. The press worker said nothing about the brown growth, except that work in the court must not throw dust into her corner.

The power took care not to decide what these silences meant.

It made the water safe first.

Not abundant at once. The tank had seams that would not bear a sudden full head, and two of the upper outlets would have burst before the lower ones received their share. For six nights, while the court was least busy, work proceeded beneath its paving. Temporary drinking taps were fitted on both stairs. No one had to plead for a key.

The new supply came through a branch from the upper main, properly supported, with its own shutoff and a meter anyone could read. The drinking water no longer traveled through beds used by the workshops. The old circulation remained for the laundry and pulp vats, with alterations Iven inspected suspiciously.

He found a handle badly placed.

It was moved.

When the tank began to fill, people gathered over the glass. The copper float rose past marks that had never meant anything except a better or worse morning.

Someone cheered too soon. A vent released a long, vulgar noise.

For several days this was the best joke in the lanes.

The owner of the private catchment still said the rain belonged to him. His neighbors still disagreed. There was now enough water to conduct the argument without anyone’s supper depending on its outcome.

The heat was harder.

The workshops needed some of it. People needed the wages from the workshops. Brickwork that had been heated for weeks did not become cool because a drawing had been approved.

The great elbows were enclosed in insulation that could be opened for inspection. Waste warmth was led to a new drying room at the western end, away from the bedrooms. A shaft was opened through an unused upper building, and the power secured the building as well as the shaft, so that no future owner could charge the lanes for the passage of their air.

The excavation stopped twice: once for unstable clay, once because the children had nowhere safe to go while the landing was closed.

During the second stoppage, the workers were still paid.

This fact traveled farther than any account of the shaft.

There was no dedication when it opened. At midday, a piece of thread tied near the pump court leaned westward and stayed there.

Sella stood in her doorway.

She went back inside, unfolded her mattress, and left it flat.

That evening she complained that the new current lifted the corners of her patterns. Someone brought her four smooth weights. She accepted three, having a suitable object of her own for the fourth.

The power had rooms made available above, too.

They were not rewards for patience or places reserved for those judged unable to cope. A household could look at one, refuse it, look again. Moving did not cancel a debt someone owed you or deprive you of work below. Staying did not mean consenting to the old conditions.

The clerk took a room without stairs. He continued to visit with his notebook.

A family left so quickly that for a week everyone was offended. Then a letter arrived describing the mother’s first uninterrupted night of sleep, and the offense became more difficult to maintain.

Others stayed. One liked being near her sister. One distrusted the upper landlord, despite the guarantees. Another had only just finished fitting his shelves.

Iven went to his daughter’s for three nights after the waterworks were complete.

He left instructions longer than the emergency instructions attached to the pumps. A second attendant learned them, then asked which could be shortened. Their ensuing dispute was sufficiently absorbing that Iven almost missed his departure.

When he returned, he brought oranges.

The unfinished chamber was not turned into another workshop. Its floor was raised, its sharp corners dressed, and daylight was admitted by a broad, sloping opening. Along one side there were benches. Along the other there was space left without a stated purpose.

The children distrusted this space at first. They expected to be moved.

The boy who tended the herbs carried a pot there, then carried it back because he was not sure about the light. Someone helped him establish a place where water could drain without being lost. He continued to find the plants unsatisfactory, but one of them eventually flowered, which he had not been told it could do.

The brown growth remained in its corner. The press worker tried it elsewhere and brought it back. No one required her to demonstrate that it improved the air.

The power did not fill the cut with birds. It made a way toward the living ground above: steps ending in an open piece of soil, shaded at one edge, with room for roots that would not be severed next season. What came there would come by routes of its own.

Tem found an insect under a damp stone some months later. It was smaller and less impressive than any in her book.

She watched it for a long time before replacing the stone.

By then the visitor had stopped sitting beside the shoe cabinet. There were names attached to the work, wages for maintaining it, copies of the plans in three places, and a means of reaching the power that did not depend on a clerk believing an unlikely story. The visitor had explained this means more than once. People tested it with a blocked drain before trusting it with anything worse.

Life did not become agreeable in every particular.

The bag reappeared on the forbidden bracket. The courting men quarreled on a bench instead of standing up. Sella charged the full price for an elaborate cuff and was told it was too much.

But the evening air now reached the pump court. Beyond it, the thread by the western opening stirred even after sunset.

On a hot night, people still brought stools into the lane. Some came for company. Some came out of habit. Some sat awhile, discovered they were tired, and went indoors to sleep.

Tem brought her beetle out again, repaired along one fold with a narrow strip of paper.

She set it on the step.

In the steadier current, it tipped onto its side.

Iven made a movement to right it, then saw that she was examining the angle of its legs. He waited while she altered them.
