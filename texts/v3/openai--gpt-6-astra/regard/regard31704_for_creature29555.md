Before making itself known, the power follows the buttress down.

It passes the last tufts in the service joints, the inspection rails, a platform whose access hatch has not opened in ninety-three years. Cloud closes over it. Below the cloud are other inhabited districts, dark galleries, a nursery lit by lamps, a hall in which dismantled engines stand beneath sheets. Farther down, water runs inside the structure with a sound no one at the summit has heard.

The power examines the things that hold.

There is no single fatal flaw. There is fatigue, corrosion, competent repair, repair made under pressure, and one ingenious redistribution of weight whose author died without recording it. There are instruments that have been reporting their own faults rather than the condition of the structure. There are good materials in unexpected places.

Near the summit, behind a wall of glazed tiles, a cracked fitting is being held shut by the pressure that will eventually open it.

The power steadies it.

For the present, this is enough.

***

Eda notices the stranger because they are standing inside her barriers.

She has just carried the barriers away. Nevertheless there they are, around a patch of paving near the cold-water main, and inside them stands a person in a loose gray coat.

“Out,” she says.

The stranger steps out.

Eda sets down the barriers she is carrying. She looks from them to the identical set around the paving.

“That is unnecessary.”

“Yes,” says the stranger.

The second set disappears.

Eda keeps her hand on the first.

Behind the wall, something gives a quiet metallic click.

“What was that?”

“A fitting that needs replacement. It is safe until it can be reached.”

“Who says?”

“I do.”

She looks at the stranger for a long time.

“Then you can stay until somebody qualified gets here.”

The power stays.

***

By the time the pipe crew arrives, Eda has made the stranger explain several things. By the time the wall is open, she has made them explain several more.

The crew leader asks questions Eda would not have thought to ask. The power answers those too. It does not pretend that its temporary support is a repair.

Inside the wall, the crew finds the fitting exactly as described.

They also find enough space to work comfortably, although the drawings show considerably less.

The crew leader measures the recess twice.

“Is this permanent?”

“It can be.”

“Leave it, then.”

There are people to notify. There is a revised drawing to make. Someone fetches a lamp. Eda buys her rolls late, and both are cold by the time she passes the elevator door.

The scar-nosed cupboard pig takes a piece through the seal.

“You wouldn’t believe the afternoon I’ve had,” Eda tells her.

The animal withdraws without committing itself.

***

The power does not ask Eda what the district needs and mistake her answer for everybody’s.

It asks her about exposed metalwork.

She shows it the western screens, the underside brackets, the anchor points that require a second line to reach the first. She shows it three repairs she dislikes, including one of her own.

“Why did you do it that way?”

“The proper collar was six weeks away.”

“What would you have done with the proper collar?”

She draws it.

Elsewhere, the power speaks with people who wash bedding every other day because of the damp. It sits through a meeting about water allocation. It listens to the night workers, who are difficult to gather at an hour convenient to anybody else. It watches a woman carry a sleeping child up nine flights while an empty private lift passes within the wall.

Some requests contradict others. Some people lie. Several ask for things that would improve their own lives by making a neighbor’s worse.

The power has time to find out.

Meanwhile, the woman carrying the child discovers that the public lift works. So do the other public lifts. Their worn parts have been replaced, their brakes tested, and their maintenance crews have the drawings and spares.

The private lifts continue to work. Their owners find this less reassuring than they might have expected.

***

For a while, Eda suspects that everything is about to become strange and seamless.

She imagines walking her section with nothing to inspect, no evidence of wear, no tool capable of making a mark. She does not like the thought. Then she becomes annoyed with herself. People could stop falling from failed anchors; she could endure a change of occupation.

But the new metal takes paint.

She checks.

The power has left samples at the workshops, along with instructions that do not require the power to be present. The foundry people object to two of the instructions, try them, and withdraw one objection. The other produces a long discussion and a better method.

In the storehouse, essential parts are no longer scarce.

This takes some getting used to. For months, people hide small reserves behind cupboards. The power leaves them there.

Work does not disappear. Neither do wages when dangerous jobs disappear. This arrangement requires more than a replenished storehouse, and produces much louder arguments.

Eda attends one of them. A man with excellent windows explains that the district cannot afford to make life comfortable for everybody.

“It appears we can,” she says.

He begins to explain what he means by afford.

She has heard it before.

***

The power asks before touching her shoulder.

They are sitting on the boiler seat. Her friend has gone to fetch drinks, having asked the power whether it can make the receiver stop turning certain consonants into the sound of tearing foil.

It can. He wants to try that first, before discussing anything else.

Eda holds her right elbow.

“What would you change?”

The power explains the old injury, the inflamed tendon, the ways she has learned to move around pain. It can repair the damage. Her body will take a little time to become accustomed to the change.

“Will I still know when I’ve done too much?”

“Yes.”

“And the rest of me?”

“Only what you ask.”

She considers this.

“All right.”

There is no light. Her shoulder becomes warm, then feels as though it belongs to somebody who has been sitting more comfortably.

She raises her arm.

Halfway up, she stops from habit.

Then she raises it the rest of the way.

Her friend returns carrying three cups. She reaches for the tray with her right hand, and he nearly drops it.

For the next several days she keeps finding things to take down from high shelves.

***

The lower rooms are not emptied in the name of improvement.

Some people want to leave. They are given good places to go, and the choice of returning. Others want dry walls, clean air, reliable lifts, and to remain near the people who knock on their doors.

Eda’s daughter wants her quiet rooms.

She also wants a window through which something other than a service wall can be seen.

This presents a structural problem. The power brings it to the engineers, who are by now beginning to enjoy structural problems again.

A new light court opens gradually through a series of disused chambers. Its walls are pale without being glaring. It has landings, seats, and a way for warm air to rise without carrying kitchen smoke into the bedrooms. Water condenses along its upper ribs and runs to a covered cistern.

Eda’s daughter chooses a shade for her window.

“You’ve finally got some sun,” Eda says.

“Which I can exclude by choice.”

“Yes.”

They look at each other.

The cuttings remain long and pale. Her daughter turns out not to like those plants very much.

Eda takes them home.

***

There are changes for which no meeting can provide all the voices.

The power follows the cupboard pigs through their cavities. It learns where they bear their young, where the old elevator shaft becomes a trap, which warm nesting spaces will be lost when the machinery is made safe.

Before that work begins, there are other spaces.

They are dry, sheltered, and difficult for larger animals to enter. They do not open into anyone’s food cupboard. The animals investigate them with considerable suspicion.

The scar-nosed female moves last, carrying strips of laundry lint in her mouth.

Eda still insists she does not feed them.

The new sensor casings resist teeth.

***

One evening, when the immediate work has become ordinary enough that people complain about its scheduling, the power asks Eda whether she still wants to see the foot of the structure.

She is cleaning her brush.

“How long?”

“For the journey you would enjoy, eleven days.”

“My section?”

“Ask whom you would trust.”

“There’s the cost.”

“No cost.”

She works the bristles against the bottom of the pot.

“Can someone come?”

“Yes. If they want to.”

Her friend wants to. He objects to eleven days until he learns that she objects to his objection. They spend a week arranging it.

Before leaving, Eda shows her replacement the bolt at the outer lookout.

“After the next wet cycle,” she says. “Not before. Take the whole plug if you can. There’s a box by the western stair with the same exposure.”

Her replacement writes it down.

***

The journey downward is not a fall through light.

There are lifts, broad slow ones, and stops where people get on carrying parcels. There is a night in a guest room beside a great horizontal brace. There are districts where the food tastes unfamiliar and the plants have enormous dark leaves.

The power goes with them for part of the way. For the rest, there are guides who live there.

At the bottom, the structure enters a plateau of weathered stone.

Eda had imagined a point of contact: a line, perhaps, where the made thing ended and the earth began. Instead there are foundations spread across a valley, drainage works, old quarries filled with water. Moss covers both stone and concrete. Trees have grown against the retaining walls.

She stands for a long time.

Her friend stamps one foot experimentally.

“Well,” he says.

They walk beyond the foundations. The ground rises without stairs. In a shallow hollow, rainwater has collected among reeds, unpumped and unmeasured.

Eda crouches and puts her hand against the soil.

When she straightens, her shoulder does not hurt.

***

On her return, the fern is beside the western stair.

Its old mortar plug sits in a pocket of grit. One frond has browned at the tip, but the newest has opened.

Eda examines it, then goes to look at the bolt.

The collar has been replaced. The paint is sound. In the maintenance book, the work is recorded in another person’s handwriting.

She checks the date, runs a thumb around the edge, and closes the book.

There is still time to buy bread before the bakery shuts.
