The power noticed the call, but did not answer in the maintenance worker’s place.

It listened while Eren gave her address, described the warmth, and admitted that she had not recorded the inspection strip’s readings during the preceding eleven days.

“No sweet smell?”

“No.”

“Any tightening around the door?”

Eren crossed the room and tried it.

“A little at the upper corner.”

“Leave it open. Someone will come.”

She wedged the door with a folded cuff. Tavi wanted to know whether they should take Cutter to his grandmother’s.

“Not yet,” she said.

The power, which had arrived without a body and could have remained that way, considered the open door. Then it made itself a body that would fit through it.

***

The maintenance worker arrived first.

Her name was Ves. She had a case of instruments, a sore shoulder, and another six addresses waiting on her wrist terminal. She put a probe against the wall, moved it twice, and asked Eren to lift the calendar.

Eren did.

Behind it, the inspection strip had turned the dull violet of a plum.

Ves exhaled through her nose.

“The crown is feeding through the wrong return.”

“Is that dangerous?”

“It’s repairable.”

“That wasn’t what I asked.”

“I know. I’m measuring.”

There was a knock at the open door.

The person outside stood in the entrance recess while the fan ran. Nothing came off them. They looked down at the clean filter with the mild interest of someone learning a custom.

“I can help with the wall,” they said. “If you would like.”

Ves glanced at the empty hands.

“Are you from the nursery?”

“No.”

“Then please keep the doorway clear.”

The visitor stepped back.

Ves watched the probe. Its fine indicator was moving toward the end of its scale.

“What help?” she asked.

“I can keep this from becoming worse while you decide what to do.”

“Without stopping the return?”

“Yes.”

“Where would the pressure go?”

“I would hold it.”

Ves looked at the visitor properly then.

Outside the window, an amber lamp illuminated the soft crown of the tower. One of its braces gave a short, hard creak.

“Do that,” Ves said.

The indicator stopped.

***

Nothing else stopped.

A glider crossed the window. Cutter worked its mouthparts along the base of the chair. Somewhere in the wall, a carrier scratched past with its burden.

Ves removed the probe and tested it against her wrist. She inserted a second instrument beside the first.

The visitor waited.

“How long can you maintain this?”

“As long as you need.”

Ves called her supervisor.

Eren put the kettle on the heater, then took it off again. She was not sure this was an occasion for offering anything, and there was no room on the table for four cups.

Tavi rolled his marble toward the visitor’s foot.

“Are you holding the whole tower?”

“Only the strain that has nowhere safe to go.”

“Does it hurt?”

“No.”

“Could you hold a tram?”

“Yes.”

Eren said his name.

“I wanted to know.”

“So did I,” said Ves.

***

The power had come upon the city as one might come upon a light under a door.

It had seen the black plain, then the bowl opening grain by grain beneath it. It had followed the conducting fibers and the slow, clear circulation. It had noticed the animals in the walls, the masked passengers, the hinges flexing in their beds.

There was much here that it had no wish to interrupt.

It did not mistake expansion for illness. Nor did it mistake the absence of ruins for the absence of loss.

Now, holding a small portion of the tower’s effort, it listened to three people argue about a return conduit.

The supervisor wanted to withdraw the upper feed. Ves said this would load the adjoining tower unless the square’s relief bed was opened first. Eren asked why the letting clerk’s certificate did not include the walls.

“That’s a separate certificate,” the supervisor said.

There was a silence.

Tavi retrieved his marble.

***

By the second hour, two nursery workers had arrived. They brought folded sleeves, a ceramic manifold, and a pot of compound whose sharp smell drove Cutter beneath the cupboard.

They opened the wall.

Its contents drew away from each cut. Pale layers parted with a series of small adhesive sounds, revealing a dark cord as thick as Eren’s wrist, flattened almost shut beneath a newer growth.

One of the nursery workers put a hand beside it.

“Here.”

Ves nodded.

The visitor looked closely. It had already perceived the constriction, but the worker’s hand showed something more: where a person could reach it, how much room a tool required, which part could be repaired again without opening the entire wall.

They worked until the ceiling had dimmed across the neighboring districts.

When the new route was ready, Ves asked the visitor to release a little strain.

The instruments rose, settled.

“A little more.”

The living sleeve filled.

“All of it.”

The wall cooled beneath Eren’s palm.

For several minutes, nobody spoke. Then the nursery worker stretched his back, looked at the table, and said, “You can probably keep that there now.”

“Probably,” Eren repeated.

He gave her an apologetic look.

The visitor did not improve his answer.

***

Before leaving, the power asked Eren what she wanted.

She was tired enough to answer plainly.

“I want to come home and not do another inspection.”

The visitor looked toward the exposed strip.

“And I don’t want him having to do it either.”

Tavi was asleep in the chair, one hand closed around his marble.

“Not a house that never changes,” Eren added. “I know where I live.”

The power stayed beside the doorway for a moment.

“May I come back when you’ve slept?”

“After my shift.”

***

It returned. It also went elsewhere.

At the bridge-joint works, it waited through a resistance test before asking its questions. At a frontier shelter, it was told to move because it was standing where a hauler liked to put its head. At the oldest district’s tenancy office, it listened to a clerk explain how a household could officially occupy a room that had not finished growing.

It visited the crews who held prosperous dining rooms still.

Their work was intricate. Some of it was necessary. Some consisted of going back every week to correct what the previous week’s correction had displaced.

The power asked to see the neighboring service channels.

Then it asked to see the channels beyond those.

***

Its first public offer was modest in wording.

It would provide places for excess growth to go, without taking anyone’s room or pushing the burden into another district. It would support the city while those places were made. Nothing would be installed in an occupied building without its residents’ agreement.

The offer produced six meetings, a petition, several enthusiastic designs, and a rumor that the ceiling was about to be replaced.

The power attended the meetings.

People asked whether its work would consume the mineral feed, whether it would interfere with withdrawals, whether the new structures could be repaired with ordinary equipment, and who would own them.

A woman from the western galleries asked what would happen when it lost interest.

This was the question that altered the design most.

***

The first reserve chamber opened beneath an existing growth lot.

It was not beautiful from outside. There were three access doors, broad inspection panels, and a low shelter where workers could set down their cases out of the dust.

Inside, the vegetation had room to unfold through a network of durable ceramic ribs. The ribs could be uncoupled. The conduits could be isolated. Every part had space around it for a hand and a tool.

The power supplied what the city could not yet make. It also supplied the means to make more.

The nursery workers changed its initial arrangement of the valves.

Ves insisted on a second warning strip at the access door.

A handler asked for a lower threshold so frightened haulers would not have to be dragged across it.

All three changes were made.

Before the chamber began operating, the power withdrew its support for a supervised trial. The city carried its own weight. The instruments remained within their markings.

Then a worker deliberately closed the wrong valve.

A warning sounded. A second route opened.

They tried several worse mistakes.

***

There was other work.

The power did not wait for the housing committees to finish before easing a hauler’s infected foot. It asked the handler to remain beside the animal and stopped when the sensitive mouth-fringe tightened.

It made protective balm freely available without deciding that everyone ought to prefer it unscented.

It gave the retrieval crews instruments that could distinguish ceramic, metal, and living tissue in the collection channels.

Eren objected to this last improvement on the grounds that it would encourage Tavi.

“It would have saved you half a day’s wages,” Ves said.

“That is exactly what he’ll say.”

***

Sove declined to have her room held to its present dimensions.

“The kitchen needs widening.”

The power asked whether she wanted anything preserved.

She showed it the photograph between the glass sheets.

It could have removed the scratches, sharpened the background, supplied detail no lens had caught. Instead, it asked.

“A copy,” Sove said. “For Eren. Leave the supervisor in.”

When it was finished, Sove examined the copy beside the original.

“Good,” she said. “Now she can stop borrowing mine.”

The old keys remained on their separate rings.

***

Not every building became still.

Some residents liked rooms that lengthened with a household. Some workshops needed their walls to thicken during heavy runs. The frontier continued advancing beneath its leaning survey posts.

But a tenancy certificate came to include limits a tenant could understand. If a room exceeded them, there was somewhere to stay, and the cost did not come out of the tenant’s wages.

Maintenance calls were answered sooner. There were more people to answer them.

The power remained available, though increasingly it was summoned for things no one had thought to ask at first. It was careful about promises. When it made one, people could plan around it.

***

Several months later, Eren came home, stood in the entrance recess, and shook the dust from her cuffs.

Tavi was on the floor beside the table. He had arranged three ceramic cups as obstacles for his marble. Cutter occupied the middle one.

Eren passed between the kitchen and the chair without striking her shin.

She set down her bag.

On the wall, the uncovered inspection strip showed a quiet, even color. She glanced at it as she might glance at a clock. Then she went to see what they had for supper.

The chair still wore its red cord.

By then it meant where Cutter slept.
