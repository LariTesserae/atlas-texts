The power notices the hand that has not been released.

It notices the elbow, too: the wax pressed into the split, the small quantity of yellow material already lost, the way the keeper has placed their arm so that its own weight holds the wound closed. It could restore the missing fingertip. It could quiet the whole mountain.

For a while, it does neither.

The small one’s vibration is passing into the bench. The keeper feels it through the bones of the wrist. To change the bench now, even to make it softer or warmer, would interrupt something whose work is not finished.

Outside, the fallen limb settles another fraction. Calls break out beneath it.

The keeper starts to rise.

At the doorway, a loose wooden ring rolls uphill.

It comes to rest against the sill.

The keeper goes still. The small one opens its fingers.

After a moment the ring rolls back, slowly, and lies flat on the stone.

There is no current that could have done this. The keeper’s facial filaments point in several directions at once.

The ring rises onto its edge again.

The keeper reaches for the mallet.

The ring stops.

***

This is how the power begins to make itself understood: by stopping when stopped.

It does not enter the shelter. It does not reach inside the keeper’s thoughts for a language that would spare it the inconvenience of being strange. When the mallet lifts, the ring lowers. When the keeper makes a sharp scraping sound with the wrist spur, it withdraws beyond the sill.

The keeper watches it for a long time.

Then they set down the mallet and pick up the hooked pole.

With the small one held against their uninjured side, they go out to inspect the fallen limb. The ring stays where it is.

The limb has come down across a low fan of growth. Beneath it, something knocks three times, pauses, and knocks again.

The keeper puts the small one inside the garment, tightens a cord around both their bodies, and tries to lever the limb upward.

The injured elbow opens.

A little dust escapes.

The ring makes a single sound against the sill.

The keeper turns.

Beside the fallen limb, a flake of stone rises from the ground. It slips beneath the wood and lifts it by the width of a finger.

The keeper draws back sharply.

The stone lowers.

Nothing is crushed. The power has attended to that.

The keeper crouches, looking from the flake to the narrow dark space underneath the branch. They touch the wood with the end of the pole. Then they lift the handle.

The flake rises with it.

Together they raise the branch far enough for a blunt-headed animal to scramble out. It carries a dark kernel between its gripping feet. Two more follow, one dragging a torn nest of fibers.

The keeper taps the ground.

The branch descends.

For the first time that night, the keeper makes a sound unrelated to pain or warning: a brief, dry rattle deep behind the jaw.

The power remembers it.

***

It would be easy to clear the garden of danger.

The currents could be moderated. The fissures could be sealed. Every seed could land in a prepared seam, every flower open without trapping anything inside.

Within a season, much of the garden would be dead.

The power understands this before it acts. There are other things it must learn by watching.

The keeper allows a failing trunk to fall because its hollow will make three shelters. They save a nearly leafless creeper because its roots hold open a mineral seam. They cut healthy growth from one place and leave damaged growth in another. Some decisions follow needs the power can trace through the mountain. Others belong to the keeper: the striped callers, the dark wood, the chamber with the worn entrance.

When the power moves toward that chamber, the keeper places the pole across its opening.

It does not approach again.

***

Near dawn, the keeper sets a stone wedge on the bench.

They point to the wedge, then to the doorway.

The wedge moves outside.

The keeper brings it back.

They point again, this time holding one hand above it.

The wedge rises until it touches their palm.

Over the following days, this becomes a useful vocabulary.

Lift. Lower. Hold. Leave.

The keeper has a fifth instruction, made by turning both pairs of fingers inward. The power first takes it to mean *stop*. Later it learns the difference.

It means *I will do this part*.

***

The elbow heals sooner than it otherwise would.

This, too, requires an understanding.

The power begins by drawing the scattered meal from the keeper’s sleeve into a small heap. Beside it, it places a curl of clean wax. The keeper examines both and pushes the meal away; dust from a wound is not food.

The power leaves it.

Next it warms the wax until it has the consistency the keeper has been trying to achieve between their teeth.

The keeper touches it with the wrist spur.

Then they hold out the injured arm.

The power closes nothing at first. It supports the arm and keeps the wax warm while the keeper cleans the split. When the edges are ready, it holds them together.

The keeper sleeps sitting up.

During that sleep, the power does a little more. It helps the torn inner passages meet without a hard ridge between them. It leaves the older scar alone. It leaves the missing fingertip alone.

In the morning the keeper bends the elbow experimentally.

They examine the seam for a long time, then extend the arm once more.

This time, they point beneath it, toward the old binding scar.

***

The small one dislikes the ring.

It wants the ring to remain where it puts it.

Once this becomes clear, the power stops using it.

A plain stone serves instead. The small one soon incorporates that into its arrangements as well, pushing it into the basket and placing wax crumbs on top.

The power waits until it has been taken out before moving it again.

There are interruptions of this kind.

There is also work.

Under the keeper’s direction, a shallow recess is made beside the shelter door, below the worst of the rising air. Its lip is rounded so that small gripping feet can find purchase. The keeper tests it with husks, then with an empty garment, then by lying in it through an exhalation of the mountain.

Only afterward do they put the small one there.

The recess is no prison. The small one can climb out. For a while, climbing out is its principal interest.

The keeper teaches it to feel the first orange grains lifting beneath its palms.

The power could hold it safely through every gust. Instead, it remains close while the lesson proceeds. Once, when a grip fails, a stone rises no higher than necessary.

The keeper sees.

They tap it once with the wrist spur and continue the lesson.

***

Eventually the pale curved scrap comes down from the ledge.

The keeper places it beside the power’s stone.

Then they carry both to the notch.

For several changes of wind, nothing happens.

The keeper lies flat, face beyond the rim. The power attends to the closed hand, the scrap inside it, the country hidden below.

It could clear the atmosphere. It could bring every distant place into view at once.

The keeper lifts the scrap and points outward.

So the power follows its history.

Far below, where the mountain’s shoulder divides, there is another hollow. The scrap broke from the casing of a tall growth there, long ago. That growth is dead. Others occupy its seam.

There is a shelter.

Someone has fitted its doorway with overlapping plates that knock softly in the wind.

The power does not carry the keeper there.

First it goes to the doorway and waits.

***

When it returns, it brings a length of dark cord tied in an unfamiliar knot.

It lays the cord at the notch.

The keeper touches it, withdraws their hand, then touches it again. They bring it close to the pits above their eyes.

For a long time they remain flat against the warm rock.

Later, inside the shelter, they take a striped caller’s shed throat casing from among the pieces of dark wood. They wrap it in leaf skin and bind it with a short cord.

The knot they choose is one the power has seen only once before, around the entrance of the empty chamber.

They place the little bundle beside the stone.

The power carries it down.

***

The mountain continues to exhale.

Seeds are lost. Branches tear. The chalk-colored animals remain ill-tempered, and the keeper still drives one of them away from the shelter more often than the others.

There are messages now, though not every day.

There is a route below the rim that the keeper is beginning to ask about.

And there are evenings when the bench is warm beneath both their hands, the small one is occupied with its stones, and a crash comes from the garden.

The keeper listens.

At the doorway, the plain stone lifts a little.

The keeper considers, then points toward the sound.

They stay on the bench while something else goes to look.
