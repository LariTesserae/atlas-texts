The first thing the power did was keep the water from getting colder.

It did not make it warm. The woman had put the glass down and gone back to sleep, and her husband preferred cold water. It remained at the temperature at which she had left it.

This required some attention. The glass belonged to the bedside table, the table belonged to a room, and the room had begun, very slowly, to belong to a different northern corner. Heat was being lost in the transfer.

The power held the room where it was until morning.

Then it continued holding it.

***

There were several things it could have done immediately which it did not do.

It could have made the plain old. There would have been soil, river channels, softened ridges, perhaps a forest near enough for the older children to find disappointing. But beneath that landscape would have lain a history which had not happened, and the consequences of that history would have entered the foundations from below. Wells would have acquired former owners. Someone’s kitchen might have become the site of a battle.

It could have made the district entirely consistent. This would have required deciding which of several incompatible homes had the stronger claim to existence.

It could have moved everyone elsewhere.

Instead, it examined the spaces in which people would have been lost if someone had stopped remembering them.

There were more of these than the fire service knew.

For a while, nothing visible changed.

A tired parent forgot to call a child back into the kitchen. The child stayed in her bedroom, drawing a horse with an excessive number of knees. When supper was ready, the door still opened onto the same corridor.

A man away caring for his sister returned after nine weeks. His flat was cold, but it was all there.

Near the laundry, the iron guard around the shrub worked loose from its sockets. The baker found it lying sideways one morning. Beneath it, the crack and the roots had remained.

He fetched a hammer.

***

The power first spoke at the municipal office, through the grille used for enquiries about drains.

This was not a disguise. There was no sufficiently ordinary body for it to use without spending a good deal of everyone’s time on the body.

The clerk asked it to speak up.

It did.

The clerk listened, fetched the senior surveyor, and stayed to hear the rest.

What the power offered was initially entered in the minutes as *assistance with subsidence*. The surveyor crossed this out.

They met again with the heating engineer, representatives from the tenants’ association, the fire service, and a woman who had arrived to dispute a bill and refused to leave merely because the meeting had become important.

“Are you going to change the addresses?” she asked.

“No.”

“Because they did that before.”

“I know.”

“You don’t know what it was like getting the pension sent.”

The power listened while she explained.

By the end of the afternoon they had agreed on three trials: one unoccupied room, one troublesome length of water main, and a cupboard whose owner was willing to risk its becoming a different size provided the contents were first removed.

The owner counted the shelves.

The surveyors measured everything on paper.

***

Warmth proved easier to retain once a room no longer had to demonstrate that it was being used.

The power did not install little summers in the walls. It gave the heat fewer ways to cease belonging to the place where it had been put.

In the trial room, a heater ran for an hour and was switched off. The engineer sat beside it with three thermometers and a packed lunch.

At noon she removed her coat.

At two she opened the door to make sure the corridor was still there.

By evening the room was cool in an ordinary fashion.

They repeated the test with nobody inside.

After that, there was a waiting list.

Some residents refused. They wanted to see what happened over a winter. The power maintained the underlying support of their homes, but left their heating arrangements alone.

Others asked for every room to be done at once. They had been waiting years to sleep without a curtain touching their faces.

The two brothers accepted the work and continued eating from the same pan. With the rest of their apartment habitable, they now had considerably more space in which not to speak.

***

The northern wall took longer.

The power found that several locked doorways were not empty beyond the locks. There were unfinished rooms, rooms that had been abandoned before anyone entered them, and one room containing a drying rack with a wet shirt on it.

The shirt was warm.

It left that door shut and sought the person who had hung it.

This led it to the district beneath the pool, though *beneath* was useful only for the first eleven meters.

The caretaker was asked to stop scraping for one morning.

He refused.

“There are children here by eight.”

“Then before eight.”

He arrived at five with his scraper and found the pool covered by a plain wooden lid. The lid had a handle on each side. It looked sufficiently municipal that he inspected the hinges before touching it.

The power did not ask him about the cinema.

It asked what the ice did if allowed to remain.

He told it things he had never included in a maintenance report. Once he began, he was angry. The power kept the lid in place and listened.

There were people in the other district. They had their own difficulties with water, and a courtyard in which something like a reflection sometimes appeared overhead. They were not waiting to be replaced by a better version of themselves.

The work became a matter of boundaries, drainage, and eventually introductions.

The caretaker did not attend the first meeting. He asked to be told whether the swimming bath was there.

It was.

He sat in the disposal shed for a long time after hearing this.

When he went home, he told his daughter. She asked whether they had surveyors.

***

No one was required to remain in order to keep a street in existence.

This change was harder to establish than the heating.

For years, departures had involved arrangements: a neighbor to open a door, a cousin to sleep in a bed, someone to keep buying from the shop whose owner might otherwise find it facing away from the town. People did not stop making these arrangements merely because a notice appeared.

The first resident to test the new guarantee was the woman who had thrown away her measuring strings.

She went somewhere warm again.

The second was a young man who took an apprenticeship beyond the plain. His mother left his cup on its hook. Three months later she moved it to a shelf because it was in the way.

Nothing happened to his room.

The regional council was less readily persuaded to recognize the local qualifications. The power could hold a stair in place without difficulty. Getting the council to accept that repairing a junction with an extra direction of access constituted skilled work required examples, examination standards, and an inspector.

The electrician passed.

She stayed.

Several of her colleagues did not.

***

The gray animal avoided the first alterations to the service passages.

It stood at a junction with its forefeet on the new, steady floor and the rest of itself farther back than the available passage seemed to permit.

The power stopped work there.

It had been making the passages safe according to the requirements of creatures which arrived all at once.

For three nights it watched without approaching. The electrician came with the tin. The animal ate, retreated, slept, and used a way through the junction that had no room for a human body but was nevertheless a way.

The revised plans preserved it.

They also preserved the nest, including the tooth.

Later, a shallow drinking dish appeared beside the electrician’s locker. Its front and back edges did not quite share a moment. Water stayed in it.

The animal used it after first pushing the brass washer underneath.

***

The power could not settle every question by looking more closely.

When it examined the warmth left by the dead, it found several different things. Some were habits of rooms. Some were consequences of heat taking a long route home. Some did not yield an answer it was prepared to give a grieving person as fact.

The priest asked whether it could leave those alone unless help was wanted.

It could.

She also asked for a cupboard in which the hot-water bottles would dry properly.

That was finished by Thursday.

***

Over the next year, the town became less remarkable in a number of useful ways.

Doors could still open unexpectedly, but no occupied room could be cut off without another exit becoming available. The surveyors retained their paper plans. Now an old line remained an old line rather than slowly acquiring the authority of a mistake.

Children still had to learn the difference between a shortcut and a passage that had become shorter. When they sat down to wait, however, someone could always reach them.

The power held such passages open without straightening them. A courier might arrive by a route that offended the map, carrying a blanket and looking cross.

The elderly man’s hip ceased to be an essential component of the warning system. On learning this, he slept late for four consecutive mornings.

People complained about the works. Dust entered a bakery through a wall that had not previously communicated with anything. A new access hatch occupied the best place for a coat hook. The first safe road across the plain was too bright in afternoon sunlight, and had to be given a rougher surface.

The power accepted these complaints at the drains grille until the clerk insisted on separate opening hours.

***

It kept no count of gratitude.

It did keep records of its alterations, in forms the surveyors could read, and it left room for corrections.

Much of its attention remained where nobody would notice it: beneath a sleeping household, beside a root, along the thin boundary that allowed two different streets to have the same childhood without consuming one another.

The public warming cabinet stayed in service. People liked standing there together. Before the afternoon inside reached evening, the power arranged with its keeper for it to open onto another willing patch of summer.

The old afternoon was allowed to finish.

Somewhere there was the smell of cut grass cooling after sunset.

***

Near the northern boundary, the woman woke thirsty again.

This time her own glass was beside her. There was room for a small table on her side of the bed now, though buying it had taken several months and an argument about the expense.

She drank, listened briefly, and lay down.

Through the wall came the ticking of the foundations. The stone was still forming. Rain was still feeling for a course across the plain.

The power had not hurried it.

Outside, beneath the bakery wall, a grain loosened from the seam and moved a few centimeters downhill.

It left a very small space behind.

Nothing needed to hold its breath.
