The power did not first touch the animal.

It watched the water enter and leave. It followed the covered drain beyond the notch, through a bend where broken mortar had collected, to a cistern from which there was no way to climb. It watched the brush. It watched the animal raise its injured toe over the joint.

When the animal lay on the blue tile, the power left it there.

There were things it could not learn merely by being powerful. A surface that looked smooth might be unpleasant against the belly. A current too slight to stir the grit might be the reason for staying in a particular place. It could have made the basin flawless in a moment. Instead, it attended to the small refusals by which the animal distinguished one part of its world from another.

Then it came to the courtyard in the shape of someone who knew about water.

The person carrying the brush found it kneeling beside the opening.

“There is something living under your stair,” it said.

“I know.”

“You leave that corner.”

“It struck itself when I touched it.”

The power nodded. For a while they looked down together. The animal’s foot rested beyond the lowest step. Nothing else was visible.

“Will it foul the water?” the person asked.

They needed the basin. The power had followed the pipes and knew this too.

It answered that question carefully. They spoke about where the water went, what was washed there, which surfaces needed scrubbing and which did not. By the time the bowl was set down, there was an agreement more useful than a general instruction to be kind.

The shaded joints could remain green. The inlet would still be cleaned, but not all of it on the same day. The brush would not enter an occupied refuge.

“And when it gets bigger?”

The elbow had appeared beside the foot.

“Yes,” said the power. “That too.”

***

The first alteration was at the overflow.

It was not a grating laid across the mouth. A grating would catch fibers, gather slime, and eventually raise the water against the rim. Instead, the power set a low hood of pale fired clay over the approach to the notch. Water could pass through broad, shallow openings on three sides. Its pull was spread over a larger space, and the animal could no longer put its whole body into the outgoing sheet.

The outer surface was rough enough for its toes.

For two evenings it would not go near this addition. On the third, it attached one foot to the hood and stretched sideways toward the algae. The threads still bent in the water. Small things still gathered among them.

The power did not count the first touch as approval. It stayed until it had seen the animal feed there, withdraw easily, and return.

At the unfinished joint it closed only the narrow cleft that had caught the toe. The grooves beside it remained.

For several days the animal continued to lift its foot.

***

Making more room was harder.

The lowest step carried weight. Beyond its rear wall lay brick, not a waiting cavern. There could be no careless hollowing beneath the people who used the stair.

So the power made another shelter beside the first: a shallow arch, open at both ends, with a broad floor and enough space beneath it for the animal to turn when it was larger. A little of the cooler water was led through it. Nothing was done to the old recess.

The new shelter stood empty.

Grit gathered at its entrance. One of the dark threads near the overflow lengthened. The animal slept where it had always slept, with an elbow in the light.

The person with the brush began to say that the work had been wasted, then stopped. Under the new arch, barely visible from above, four pale toes were feeling the floor.

It took another week before the animal rested there.

Even afterward it sometimes squeezed beneath the step.

***

The exposed supply channel received a cover against the fiercest midday sun. Not its whole length: enough to keep the sheltered water from becoming as warm as the courtyard stones.

Beside the main inlet, the power fitted a smaller passage that could remain open when the wooden plug was used. Its narrow flow entered below the surface. During an ordinary cleaning, water would still move over the resting foot.

The first time the plug went in afterward, the animal lifted its head.

It waited.

The loud stream had stopped, but the skin between its toes continued to bend.

After a while its head came down.

***

There were limits to what the power arranged.

It did not put another animal into the basin. It did not know whether company would be welcome, or whether the newcomer would be a rival confined within reach of the same few feeding places.

It did not make the animal larger, cleverer, or grateful.

And it left the blue tile exactly where it was.

Before going, it showed the keeper how to clear the small passage, how to lift the overflow hood without reaching blindly beneath it, and how to recognize when the new shelter too had become cramped.

“Will you come back?” the keeper asked.

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Before this is too small.”

That evening, the animal crossed the floor by its usual indirect route. At the swollen lip of the white tile it paused, forefoot suspended. Then it went around.

It settled on the blue square.

The water had cooled a little. Near the overflow, a new surface interrupted the light. Beneath the stair there were now two darknesses, one familiar and one beginning to be.

Its throat moved. The folds between its eyes opened.

Above it, the keeper rinsed the brush and laid it beside the bowl.
