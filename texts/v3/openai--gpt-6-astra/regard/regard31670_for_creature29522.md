The power first noticed the hand reaching back from the entrance.

It could have followed the touch through every person in the room: the ankle turning, the knee drawing up, the child lifted from a sleeping pad before the adult had fully woken. It could have followed the warning down through the floor, into the throat taking water beneath the southern shelf.

Instead it remained with the hand.

The skin had split beside the thumbnail. Each time the person spread their palm against the rock, the split opened a little. They had learned to put the weight elsewhere. By morning they would have forgotten that they were doing it.

The power did not mistake this forgetting for an absence of pain.

***

There were many things it might have done badly out of kindness.

It might have stilled the chambers beneath the country. The roofs would have stopped trembling. People would have heard one another across a room.

Along the warm fissures, the feeding water would have slowed. The colonies beneath the shelves would have consumed what remained within reach. The little transparent animals would have gone clear and empty.

It might have raised the land.

Water would have found a direction then. It would have carried away the nursery pools, the brine pits, the animals grown into the undersides of ledges. Generations of knowledge would have become, in one merciful afternoon, instructions for a country that no longer existed.

It might have made the people untroubled by any of it.

It did not.

For a while, it attended.

It attended to the person with the split thumb, who was not especially patient, who sometimes pretended not to see a request from the other end of the shelter, and who had once gone out in a rising basin to retrieve a foot wrapping that belonged to someone dead.

It attended to the child beside them, who disliked the sweet flesh near the shell’s attachment scars and secretly traded it for the tougher parts.

It attended to the fixed animal under the entrance ledge. The animal had been there before the shelter. Its exposed lip was kept clear by three generations of hands, and its chambers were beginning to fail.

The people knew this last thing. They did not know where another could be found that would survive being moved.

***

When the power made itself perceptible, it did not speak from beneath the ground.

Too much depended on the meanings of those sounds.

It came instead as a visitor over the eastern crossing, carrying nothing. At the waiting stone it stopped.

The person with the split thumb saw it from the doorway and signed that it should remain where it was.

It remained.

After the pressure turned, they came to fetch it.

Inside, it put a palm on the bare oval of floor. It was clumsy with the household signs. The child corrected it twice.

During the lull, it said, “I can help with some things. I would like you to show me which.”

This was not a question anyone answered immediately.

They asked where it had come from, whether it had eaten, and what had happened to its foot coverings. Someone wanted to know whether the eastern crossing still had all its markers.

Only later did the person hold out their thumb.

“Start with that.”

The power asked before touching it.

The split closed. The thickened skin remained thickened. The nail kept its crooked edge.

The person pressed the thumb hard against a scraper handle, then harder.

“Does it open again?”

“Not from that injury.”

They considered this.

“My sister has a knee.”

***

Requests came unevenly.

Some people brought old hurts. Some brought tools, assuming the distinction would not matter much. One person wanted a lost carrying hook returned and was annoyed to learn that it had already been ground into fragments inside a western passage.

The power offered to make another.

“That one fitted my hand.”

So they spent part of a day getting the new one right.

It did not require gratitude before taking the next request. When two households disagreed over a basin, it declined to make either household’s memory infallible. When someone asked whether a pale swimmer had killed their brother, it found out, and asked whether they wished to hear the answer with someone beside them.

Its presence did not make every conversation easy.

But people began making room near their entrances.

***

The first work that belonged to more than one household concerned the failing sentinel.

The power showed them what was wrong by making a shallow pool transparent all the way through the stone beneath it. The children crouched so close that adults pulled them back by their waist folds.

They could see the animal’s chambers. One had narrowed. Another no longer emptied properly.

“Can you mend it?” someone asked.

“Yes.”

“Will it still close before the surge?”

“Yes. But you should test that.”

They did.

For several turns, no one trusted the repaired animal alone. They went to other shelters, compared the floors, watched the feeding kinds move. The power waited through their checking without asking what further proof they needed.

Afterward, it helped them establish young sentinels at houses that had none. The animals chose their own places beneath the ledges. Some settled inconveniently. A doorstep had to be moved.

***

The largest undertaking began with a complaint from the child.

They were tired of songs that stopped.

Their parent said there were more urgent things.

“There are,” said the power. “We need not do only one.”

It made no silence over the country.

Instead, on a broad rise where nothing nested and no warm run surfaced, it helped build a room.

The builders chose its entrances. They argued over the pitch of the roof. One elderly person insisted on a second way out, although the power assured them that no tide would reach it.

“Then it won’t trouble you to put one in.”

It did not.

The room’s walls took the violence out of the air. Rain could still be heard against the shell roof. So could footsteps, a dropped wedge, someone breathing through a blocked nose.

At the center, an uncovered stone carried the familiar tremors up from below. Along the sleeping ledges, a gentler motion repeated the warnings the households agreed upon. Nothing dangerous was made inaudible without being made perceptible another way.

The first people inside kept looking toward the doors.

Someone spoke too loudly and laughed at themselves.

A person whose hearing had diminished sat with a grandchild close against their good ear. The power offered to restore what had been lost. They said they would think about it. For that afternoon, they wanted everyone to stop asking them questions.

Outside, the shell-bearers continued calling.

The room did not steal their voices to make space for human ones.

***

Other work was less remarkable to look at.

A dangerous shelf acquired a stable crossing, set clear of the swimmers’ passage. The person who had checked the old route for thirty years walked across it, returned, and sat down abruptly.

No one hurried them.

Food pits ceased leaking into nursery water. Several shelters acquired sleeping places that stayed warm without drawing heat from the living seams. A household that had spent nearly all its waking time gathering enough to eat found itself with an afternoon unclaimed.

They quarreled through most of it.

The power came back the next day.

It learned that relief could leave people tired before it left them happy.

Beyond the houses, it eased a hunter’s broken crushing plate. It freed a swimmer trapped in a hollow too deep for anyone to have noticed. When shells were opened for food, it offered a way for the animal to become insensible first, without spoiling the flesh or endangering the hands around it.

Some adopted this at once. Others watched before trusting it.

The power kept showing them.

***

It did not complete the country.

Passages still changed. People still had to learn which water they were looking at. Children still fell, although fewer falls became funerals. The old still disagreed, sometimes correctly.

There were absences no repaired knee or safe crossing could replace.

The power did not explain those absences as necessary.

When asked why it had not come earlier, it gave no beautiful answer to make the question smaller.

It stayed to hear what followed.

***

One evening, the person whose thumb had healed brought the child to the quiet room.

They had finished the work that could not wait. Other work remained, but someone else had taken it.

The child lay with their head against the parent’s side.

Under the central stone, the southern throat began to open. A soft warning passed along the ledge. The parent paused, checked it with a palm, and settled back.

Then they sang.

Beyond the walls, chambers filled. The fixed shells opened their wet lips. Across miles of shallow water the living chorus rose to meet the stone.

Inside, the song reached a verse the child had never heard.

“What happens after that?” the child asked.

The parent had to think. It had been a long time since anyone had needed that part.

The power sat near the second entrance, where it would not be in the way.

It waited while they remembered.
