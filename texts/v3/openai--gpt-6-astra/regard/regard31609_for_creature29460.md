For several evenings, nothing changes.

The old animal feeds at the root beneath its bank. The tree empties its chambers. Ice forms where the water is shallow and remains absent where a slow current crosses the stones.

The attention resting on this place does not at first resemble an arrival. There is no light in the clouds, no interruption in the weather. It follows water into the roots and waits through the long filling of a chamber. It attends to the pale colonies on the partitions, to a beetle sealed inside a knot, to the animal holding its mouth against the dark.

It could understand these things quickly. It chooses to understand them together.

There is an old injury beneath the animal’s fourth left limb. Years ago, a shelf gave way under its weight. The limb healed turned slightly inward. Now, whenever the animal climbs from the pool, the pad fastens well enough, but releasing it draws a hard ridge of scar through softer tissue.

The animal has learned to release that foot last.

This makes certain routes difficult. It has stopped visiting a tree beyond the rise, although vibrations from that tree still reach its bank. On some nights it begins the journey, travels as far as the first sloping stone, and returns.

The power notices this. It also notices that the pool is a good pool, and that remaining in it is often precisely what the animal wants.

On the fourth evening, a smooth place appears beneath the injured limb.

It is no warmer than the water. It has no scent. The animal lifts its foot from it and gathers the limb close against its body.

The smoothness does not follow.

For most of the night the animal keeps away. Toward dawn, while turning to feed, it touches the place again. A little of its weight comes off the scar.

Its breathing tubes rise.

The power waits.

Over the following nights, the support is there whenever the animal places its foot upon it. It does nothing when the foot is withdrawn. The animal tests it with another limb, with its mouth plates, with the sensitive pads of its belly. Once it presses hard enough to push its own back above the surface.

There is no edge beneath which a finger can reach.

Eventually it sleeps against it.

During that sleep, the scar begins to soften.

The change takes place through the animal’s own living processes, though they have not managed such a repair for many years. Small vessels enter tissue that had become nearly bloodless. Fibres settle along the directions of strain. The curved attachment of the limb alters by less than the thickness of a leaf each night.

When the animal shifts away, the work pauses.

There is some unfamiliar itching. It rubs its flank against a submerged stone. There is no wound to clean, nothing to pull out with the narrow appendages beneath its mouth.

By the next heavy rain, the foot releases without pain.

The animal does not immediately leave the pool.

For two days it lies under the bank, feeding intermittently, with all four eyes closed. Water passes through its open filaments. Once, a small furred creature ventures down a root and stands on its back to drink. The animal contracts its skin, and the creature jumps away.

On the third evening, it climbs.

***

Beyond the rise, the failing tree has developed a vibration too fine to hear in air.

A mineral cup deep inside the trunk has worn unevenly. The spindle catches at one point in each rotation. Around the bearing, living tissue has thickened in response to the repeated strain, narrowing the passage and making the fault worse.

The old animal reaches the tree after midnight.

It presses its belly against the lower chamber and remains there while rain gathers on its ridged back. Then it climbs to an opening it has used before.

Its throat appendages enter the trunk.

The power attends without moving the animal’s limbs for it.

There is work here the animal knows. It loosens a strip of swollen tissue, withdraws it, eats part, discards the rest. It clears silt from the approach to the bearing. Near morning it encounters the worn cup.

It works at this for a long time.

The curved edge of its appendage cannot reach the underside. Twice it withdraws and changes position. The third time, it slips a little on the bark, holds, and rests.

Inside the tree, where the animal has cleared access, the rough mineral surface becomes smooth.

The spindle completes a turn.

The animal goes still.

It probes again. The next turn passes through its mouth plates and into the bones beneath its eyes: an unfamiliar steadiness at a place where it has felt a catch for years.

It waits for many cycles before withdrawing.

There is no message in the motion, and no claim upon it. By daylight it has returned to its pool.

It sleeps there through the following night.

***

The power does not make the old animal responsible for the forest.

There are other worn bearings beyond its routes. There are animals with infected wounds in basins it will never enter. Under a fallen trunk, a juvenile has been caught for two days with water just beyond the reach of its filaments.

The trunk settles a little differently. The juvenile pulls free.

In a grove above the eastern escarpment, a pressure line begins to fail beneath a stand carrying eggs. A second root reaches it before the first gives way. Water continues through the chambers.

These changes do not spread as a new command through the conductive fibres. The groves retain their differences. Incompatible roots still form insulating knots. Rain falls unevenly, and the animals still learn the country one journey at a time.

The power keeps attending. Where it does not yet understand the consequences of an intervention, it stays long enough to learn more. Where suffering is plain and relief is simple, it does not make a mystery of withholding it.

***

Late in the season, another adult comes along the bank.

The old animal meets it in the narrow water beneath the roots. Their jaw plates touch. Each holds the other’s flank, and dissolved substances pass between them.

The visitor pauses at the healed limb.

For a while neither moves. Then the old animal flattens against the bank, and the visitor passes over it toward the next pool.

What has been communicated is uncertain.

That evening, the old animal travels a route it has not used for seven winters. The vegetation has grown high across one sheltered stretch. It presses through, leaving the stems bent and shining behind it.

At the ridge it stops.

Through the stone come the working movements of several groves. Water is returning to the ground. Somewhere below, a valve seats with a soft knock.

The animal rests its belly more fully against the rock.

It has enough strength to continue. There is no pain requiring it to turn back. For a long while it stays where it is, receiving the country through its skin.

The power remains with it.

After dark, the animal chooses a direction.
