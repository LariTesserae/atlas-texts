The first thing the power did was wait somewhere other than above it.

Above was where the animal looked for leaves, for weather, for the slow completion of a drop. There was no need to put a stranger there.

It waited beside the spillway, where the hollow’s water passed out of sight. Nothing took shape. The silt remained unpressed. When the animal came that way, it found no new scent and no obstruction.

For a long time, nothing in the hollow changed except what was changing already.

The power watched a meal fail. It watched another succeed. It followed the contraction through the throat, the long rest afterward. When the animal slept, it attended to the body beneath the folds: not merely its shape, but what the sleep replenished and what it could not.

There was hunger.

There were other things the power did not name so quickly.

The watching beneath the six leaves, for instance.

*

Far down the mountain, trees grew beside water that did not keep their fallen leaves.

The power went there too.

It found a branch brought down by its own weight. Most of its leaves were still green. Beneath it, in a quiet bend of the stream, its reflection had already become exact: broken wood above, broken wood below.

The power did not mend the branch. Nor did it reach backward and prevent the breaking.

But it found what had gone from the water when the branch fell.

This was delicate work, even for such a power. A thing’s absence was not the thing itself. There were ways of carrying it that would have brought only a picture, and ways that would have brought the breaking with it.

It brought neither.

In a little hollow of stone beside the mountain’s upper spring, it laid the water’s relinquished green.

Then it waited again.

The animal found the place after drinking. It passed its mouth across the wet rock, stopped, and turned back.

For some time its clear lids moved over its eyes.

The power could not make this an answer merely by wishing it to be one.

The animal opened its mouth.

One pale ridge rose. Something green drew thin against the stone.

Afterward, the animal remained at the spring until cloud entered the hollow. When it stood, it did not immediately go down to the largest pool. It crossed to the stalks and rested beside them, in a place it rarely used.

The power watched through the following days.

This meal stayed with it.

*

There were forests enough below, and storms enough, and leaves that fell without anything needing to be persuaded to let them go.

The power made no great store of them.

It learned how little the upper hollow could receive before its water began to look unlike itself. It learned that the animal would not feed beneath certain reflected leaves, though they appeared no different from others. Those it took away again.

Sometimes the shallow place beside the spring was empty.

Sometimes a leaf appeared there from a tree that did not grow on the mountain. It moved in a breeze that could not be felt. By evening it might be gone.

The animal began to include the place in its rounds.

It still visited the old clump of stalks in the pool. It still passed over the stone reflection without interest. It still examined all six bowls after rain, carefully lifting the uneven corner of its mouth over the amber rims.

Nothing had been made to depend on its understanding why.

*

The low tree took longer.

Its roots were sound. Its leaves opened generously. The branch across the animal’s back was alive all the way to its smallest twig.

The power could have lifted it.

Instead, during the tree’s next season of growth, a little more light reached the leaves on its outward side. Not enough to dry the moss below them. Not enough to change the hollow’s afternoons.

The new wood followed that light.

Slowly, the branch began to carry more of its own weight.

For many days the animal slept as it always had, with one rear foot tucked into the space between the roots. Then, one noon, it shifted. Its back no longer met the branch where it expected to.

It woke.

The dull lids rose. Its forefeet pressed against the ground.

The power did nothing.

After a while the animal lowered itself again. It put its rear foot somewhere else.

By the end of that season there were three ways it could lie beneath the tree.

*

The power left the wider passages wide.

It did not fill them with another animal made from a guess.

It did not change the reflection that sometimes lay around the living body like a larger, unoccupied skin. Whether that image belonged to growth, loss, or something for which there was no useful word, the animal approached it without alarm.

The short scar remained. So did the soft corner of the mouth.

But beneath the folds, the distance between bone and skin increased.

Crossing the hollow no longer required quite so many pauses. Once, encountering a loose flake, the animal lifted its foot and set it beyond the stone rather than taking the longer way around.

The power was there for that small movement.

It did not ask for another.

*

Late in the day, cloud came level with the rim.

The animal lay beneath the bowl-shaped leaves. One had grown broad enough to touch the pale stone on the far side of the groove. Water weighed it downward.

At its edge a drop gathered.

The power could have held the drop indefinitely. It could have made the small inverted face clear, made the whole hollow visible within it, kept the leaf from ever emptying.

The animal watched.

The drop lengthened and fell onto the soft skin above its eye.

Its clear lid closed.

For a moment it stayed perfectly still. Then it moved its head a little to one side, settled its chin against the warm stone, and looked up again.

Along the upper face of the basin, fresh water entered through the rock.
