The first thing the power did was keep a stone from falling.

It was not a large stone. It lay in the underside of the bridge, above the fracture through which daylight entered, and had been moving outward by less than the width of a root each year. When it fell, it would take two others with it. The opening would admit more light, briefly, before the canal bed settled into it.

The power held the stone where it was.

Below, the inhabitant returned the spanner to the bench. They brushed sediment from the folds of their chest, pulled a thread of root from the split in their nail, and fitted themselves beside the warm pipe.

Nothing woke them.

For several cycles, nothing new entered the district.

The power attended to the water coming through the masonry, to the load carried by the washhouse tree, to the food on the ceramic plates. It followed the pipes below the floor that had disappeared. There were chambers beneath that floor, and beneath those a bed of gravel through which the returning water had worn three narrow channels. Most of the gravel no longer did any filtering.

The late movement in the mechanism was not caused by the nut the inhabitant had adjusted. That adjustment had compensated for wear elsewhere. It was skillful, and it would suffice for a while.

The power could have restored the machinery in an instant.

It waited long enough to understand why the largest spanner had been moved away from the edge of the bench.

During one part of the cycle, the bench trembled. A tool left there would eventually fall behind the cabinet, into the place where the inhabitant slept.

There were many such arrangements.

***

When the power made itself perceptible, it did so in the central square.

It took a small shape, neither blocking the passage nor occupying the fountain. It had limbs with which to hold the cornice and a face that could turn aside. Its presence displaced water. The threads along the inhabitant’s jaw spread toward it.

The inhabitant stopped.

The power stayed where it was.

After a long interval, the inhabitant withdrew into a doorway. The power did not follow.

At the next cycle it was there again, farther from the doorway.

On the fourth meeting, the inhabitant approached close enough to touch its wrist.

There was no pulse there, but there was warmth, and something responsive beneath the surface. When their fingers tightened, the power turned its hand without closing it around theirs.

The inhabitant examined the palm, then the joints. They looked behind the power, toward the narrow way by which it seemed to have arrived.

The power let them look.

Learning to speak together took longer than repairing the district would have taken.

Some of it was done against a railing, through taps felt in the fingers. Some required objects. The inhabitant brought the worn brass label, then took it away again without having communicated what they intended. Once they became angry and pushed the power’s hand from a pipe.

The power had been making the pipe silent.

The inhabitant needed its vibration.

It restored the vibration immediately.

After that, they made better progress.

***

The first help the inhabitant accepted was not a repair.

It was someone holding a light.

There was a seam behind the largest pump housing that they had never been able to examine while keeping both feet anchored and one hand on the pressure lever. The power held a small amber light close to the seam.

The inhabitant scraped away the deposit with the edge of a broken ceramic plate.

Beneath it, the metal was split.

They touched the crack along its length. Then they took the power’s wrist and brought the light closer, as though sufficient brightness might reveal that the damage was something else.

The power did not dim it.

For a while they remained together beside the housing.

The inhabitant’s breathing folds moved rapidly. Their free foot searched behind them until it found a bracket.

The power asked permission to show them something.

On the cleared stair tread, it made a small likeness of the pump and its passages. Water moved through it, pale with mineral grains. Beside the failing housing it placed another channel.

The inhabitant watched. They put a fingertip across the new channel.

The little fountain stopped.

They lifted their finger. It resumed.

They touched the join where the new passage would meet the old one, then pointed upward, toward the rooms full of roots.

The power showed that part again.

Nothing would need to be drained.

The inhabitant examined the likeness through the rest of the cycle. When the padded knock came, they reached automatically for the spanner.

Then they set it down and touched the place where the new channel began.

***

The work took place slowly because the inhabitant wanted to inspect it.

A passage opened through buried masonry without disturbing the floors above. Its inner surface was rough enough for useful growth to establish itself, but there were removable sections, handles that could be reached, and no fastening that required a body to be in two inaccessible places at once.

The power offered a mechanism that would never wear.

The inhabitant asked how they would know what it was doing.

So it gave the mechanism a wheel, a pressure plate, and a faint movement that could be felt through the housing. It made a model that could be taken apart on the bench.

The old system remained beside the new one.

When the flow was finally divided between them, the fountain’s plume scarcely shifted. In the lamp bowl, the shrimp continued to work at a particle caught between its forelegs.

The inhabitant stayed at the pump for three complete cycles.

On the fourth, they went to the upper kitchen.

On the fifth, they slept through the moment when they would ordinarily have gone down to make an adjustment.

The power kept watch, not over them, but over the work it had done.

***

Other things were asked for.

A plate on which the cream-colored food grew more readily. A patch for the coat that did not catch fine roots. Something to ease the soreness beneath one of the breathing folds.

That last request came reluctantly. The inhabitant first presented it as a difficulty caused by the coat.

The power treated the sore place and left the shape of their body alone.

There were offers they declined.

They did not want the workroom made taller. They did not want the washhouse cleared. When the power asked about the objects pressed into the mortar, they covered the tooth with one hand.

It asked no more about the tooth.

At the locked cage, the inhabitant indicated that the power should go ahead without them.

It went a little way, then waited at the next turning.

The cage stayed locked.

But afterward, by agreement, another passage was made between the tanks. It was wide enough to use without folding painfully at the hips. No roots had to be cut for it.

***

Eventually the power showed them that the district was not the whole of the water.

There were deeper places, colder places, broad flooded halls where other beings lived. There were warm shallows with food they might be able to eat. There were people who could learn the rail-tapping language.

The inhabitant wanted to know whether the machinery would continue in their absence.

The power put their hand on the new housing.

They wanted to know whether they could return.

The power showed them the route both ways.

They did not leave that cycle, or the next.

Instead they moved the four white stones into a different arrangement. They cleaned the cracked saucer. They visited the dark fish’s passage and waited until it appeared.

When at last they went beyond the district, they carried the middle spanner inside their coat.

The power accompanied them as far as they wished.

Behind them, beneath the bridge, the repaired circulation carried warmth through the kitchen rack and around the roots of the washhouse tree. The amber lamps kept their old intervals. The workbench remained clear.

On their return, the inhabitant paused in the square before going to inspect anything.

They held the cornice with one hand.

With the other, they brought from their pocket a smooth red stone, unlike any stone in the district, and turned it slowly in the fountain’s plume.
