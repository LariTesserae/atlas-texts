The power did not open the plate.

On the other side were air and distance, neither of them an answer to a creature whose lungs were gills. Even rescue could arrive as a pressure failure.

It attended first to the largest animal.

Not to its expression. The half-lowered eyelid might have meant exhaustion to someone who had eyelids and knew exhaustion; the open mouth might have meant agony to someone who cried. The power went nearer than resemblance. It attended to what, if anything, it was like to have that mouth, that flank, that small folded hand.

There was sensation.

There was no memory against which to measure it. The animal did not know that it had been waiting. It did not know a word for the steel against its back, or possess an image of somewhere else. Yet the pressure had a character. So did the cold. Within the uncompleted work of its nerves were distinctions that mattered: this could lessen; this could cease; this must not be torn.

The power heard those distinctions without making them into speech.

Then it attended to the others.

It did not pass over the red animals because they were small, or the cloudy crescent because only one eye was clear. Nor did it assume that every attachment was an injury. The worm against the copper received something through its branching vessels. The creature in the ceiling drop was not asking for a floor. Several of the closely fitted bodies needed contact; separation, too sudden, would have been another kind of violence.

The chamber required something more careful than an open door.

For a while—though there was not yet a while inside it—the power studied the machinery.

It learned what the compressor maintained besides noise. It followed the current through the water, the salts through their arrested diffusion, the loads through the ceramic. It found which pressures held bodies together and which merely held them captive. It traced every consequence of permitting the hanging white neck to descend.

Simply starting everything would have been easy.

The first falling drop would have crushed the animals below it. The first completed contraction would have driven one hooked leg through a neighbor’s shell. The foremost animal might have taken its step, but behind it there would have been a small collapse, and beneath that collapse, others.

So the power made room before it made haste.

Between the wall of the largest recess and the animal’s back, it placed a little more distance.

Not much. Enough that the next movement could belong to the animal rather than to the bolt.

The gills retained their water. The skin retained its support. Nothing was pulled from anything it needed. Along the channels, the ceramic widened by such slight degrees that the interlocked crustaceans could loosen without being scattered. Beneath the worms, water gained depth. Around each red comb there came a current too gentle to bend it, carrying what the body could use and taking away what it could not.

The ceiling drops remained attached. But their narrow crowns opened into wet passages through which fresh water could arrive, and through which an inhabitant might eventually leave.

The power did not move them out.

It gave them somewhere to move to.

Behind the unfinished shelf, the cavity acquired another recess. Beyond that lay a shallow basin, then a branching succession of ledges, pools, damp hollows, and dark surfaces. Their conditions differed. Some were cold; some less cold. Some held firm against a searching foot, and some yielded. There were places for bodies that needed neighbors and places where a body could withdraw.

From outside, the inspection plate remained square.

Inside, distance no longer ran out at the back wall.

Only then did the power loosen the chamber’s hold upon the present.

It began beside the largest animal’s eye.

The lid descended.

As it moved, the power kept the delicate surface beneath it moist. The lid met its lower edge. For the first time, the animal did not have to receive the green light.

Its little hand opened under its belly.

The cry changed.

It did not become a song. It broke unevenly as the throat completed its effort, and for a moment there was a rough, unfamiliar sound. The power supported the exchange across the gills. Water moved between their red leaves.

The animal closed its mouth.

At the corner, two antennae slipped free.

The crustacean remained where it was for a moment, clasping the imperfection beneath the ceramic. Then its bead-shaped heart widened. The dark filament advanced around the bend in its intestine. One leg released its hold and found another.

Throughout the cavity, changes began to have consequences.

The power stayed close enough to keep those consequences from becoming a catastrophe. Where a mouth had been held against an egg, nourishment became available without the breaking of a living body. Where a root pressed against a capsule, the gel softened beside it. The larva drew one more segment through its opened casing, rested, and drew another.

The machinery grew quieter by degrees. Its necessary work continued without making every wet surface carry the force of it.

Small sounds became audible.

A shell touched ceramic.

Water entered a metal cup.

One of the two animals in the narrow recess shifted its face away from the other’s brow. The other followed the contact briefly, then settled with its cheek against a smooth, newly accessible wall.

The largest animal opened its eye again.

The plate was still there. Its body was no longer fitted exactly to the steel. It tested this discovery with a heel, then with the hand beneath its belly. There was resistance, but now resistance allowed movement.

The power offered no explanation.

It remained available in the water, in the steadiness of the temperature, in the paths that did not close behind those who entered them. It would learn from what the animals did next. The basins could be altered. More room could be made. Nothing required them to understand the extent of the help before using it.

At the edge of the ridge, the foremost shelled animal lowered its feelers.

They touched the glossy white material.

Behind it, the heavy forelimb came down onto a newly exposed strip of ceramic. The row loosened. Under the foremost animal’s pleated body, the bead of water lengthened, detached, and fell into a pool that now had room to receive it.

The animal lifted one front foot.

It placed it beyond the bare patch.

For a moment it stood there, with its weight divided between where it had been and where it had chosen to go.

Then the rear feet followed.
