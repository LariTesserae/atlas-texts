The power first attends to the pause.

A plate of stone contracts in the evening cold. Beneath the garden, feeding folds close in several places, remain closed, then open at different intervals. Farther down, a chamber holding young stays sealed a little longer.

It would be easy to quiet the stone.

Before doing so, the power waits.

Through many evenings it follows the sharp arrivals through the animal’s body. Most carry no consequence. A few accompany the settling of a root, the collapse of an old passage, a grazer forcing its way beneath a plate. The same sensitivity that interrupts feeding allows the animal to withdraw before it is crushed.

The power leaves the clicks.

It attends more closely to what follows them.

***

There are kinds of comfort here that would be difficult to recognize from above.

A feeding surface opens against a leaf whose resistant layers have already softened. A dense knot passes through a constriction without catching. Cold enters an upper slit while deeper chambers hold their temperature. Along a broad underside, pressure is distributed so evenly that no part must keep adjusting.

The power learns these things without making the animal answer questions.

It does not gather the dispersed senses into a single point. It does not add a view of the plateau, or an understanding of the distances beyond the canopy. The animal has never needed to imagine the white curvature on the horizon in order to find a good place beneath a root.

For a long time, the power’s attention changes nothing that an observer could measure.

Then, near the oldest knot, it finds a plate that has ceased to loosen.

A root grew across it many seasons ago. The root is dead now, but its hard core holds the plate against an underlying rib of stone. Each time the animal spreads toward that side of the garden, the buried edge presses into renewing tissue. The animal has narrowed around it. Several chambers have been abandoned.

The power follows the pressure outward through root, soil, and living cords. There are branching organisms in the dead wood. Two young occupy a hollow on its far side.

During the next cooling of the ground, the root separates along an existing weakness.

The movement is less than the thickness of a fallen leaf.

Nothing drops into the hollow. The pale growths retain their passage through the wood. The old plate lifts.

Beneath it, the animal remains contracted for most of the night.

Toward morning, a margin opens into a space it has avoided for years.

***

Beyond the garden, a traveler has stopped in the lee of a rounded block.

It has chosen badly, though nothing available to it could have disclosed the mistake. The bitter trace it followed came from foliage high on an escarpment. Cold air carried the scent down a bare drainage and out across the plain. Following it farther would lead to stone too steep to climb.

Its chambers still contain food. Its filaments are alive. But sunlight reaches the underside of its shelter earlier with each passing day.

The power could place it beneath mature foliage.

Instead, it first examines the traveler: the seals between its plates, the small repeated tests of the ground, the withdrawal that begins wherever brightness warms the stone. Moving the whole animal without contact or warning would spare the crossing, but leave it abruptly surrounded by unfamiliar pressures.

There is no need to do that yet.

A little farther along the drainage, a slab has been undercut by centuries of grit. At the next contraction, it settles onto two low ribs. Beneath it is a shallow corridor, dark even at midday.

The coming mineral front flows through that corridor. For several hours, its current joins a colder sheet descending from a lower garden to the east.

The traveler opens a slit.

It waits.

It opens another.

When it moves, the power does not move it. Its folded underside finds each purchase for itself. Twice it stops beside a hollow that offers no lasting shelter. Twice it resumes.

By the time the weather clears, it is beneath the slab.

The power keeps watch through the bright interval.

***

This cannot be done carelessly across a continent.

Some empty gardens are empty because their litter contains a compound the animal cannot tolerate. Others hold residents whose margins already fill the useful soil. A traveler delivered there would meet neither abundance nor room.

The power follows the buried seams, the flowering seasons, the slow accumulation of dead foliage. It learns which shelters are merely cool and which remain cool long enough. It learns where a passage would drain a garden’s warmth, where moving one stone would bury an entire colony of colorless feeders.

There are places where it leaves everything alone.

Elsewhere, a loose block comes to rest with a little space beneath it. A narrow shelter survives a season longer than it would have. A traveler whose reserves have failed is carried, still closed around its familiar soil, to the nearest place where opening will be safe.

The help varies with the need.

No animal is required to survive a lesson.

***

In the lower garden, the traveler remains beneath a cushion for eleven days.

It takes in fragments from a single fallen leaf. The filaments carried across the plain begin to branch through fresh material. One feeding fold extends beyond the edge of the largest plate and stays there through daylight.

Nearby, a grazer scrapes the silver from living foliage.

The newcomer withdraws from its route.

There is room.

The power attends to that fact with particular care.

***

Seasons later, beneath the first garden, the resident has reoccupied the chambers beside its oldest knot.

Its habits have not become exemplary. It still carries mineral waste to three distant margins when a fourth would serve. It still closes a passage used by grazers, though they reopen it every year. When the stone clicks, it pauses.

The released plate lies tilted in the soil. Fine roots have begun to cross its upper face.

Nothing in the garden marks the place as favored. There is no new light, no lasting warmth, no substance that the roots must learn to use. The flowers open beneath the leaves for their few scented hours. Predators remain between overlapping ribbons. Young sometimes prosper and sometimes fail.

But the pressure beneath the old root does not return.

And far out on the pale ground, during the long intervals between weather, there are a few more places where a closed animal can wait without spending the last of itself.

At dusk, cold gathers beneath those stones.

The next white bank climbs toward the plateau.
