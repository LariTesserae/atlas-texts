The power first notices the animal whose hindquarters are still in the light.

There is a thorn beneath the loose skin of its shoulder. The animal has bitten off the exposed end, and the fur has closed over the puncture. Now, when it reaches forward with its left forefoot, the point moves against the muscle.

This is why it was last home.

The power follows it below.

It does not bring light into the burrow. The darkness is useful here. Nor does it arrive as a smell, a footfall, or a pressure in the clay. The animal has spent enough of its life deciding whether unfamiliar things will kill it.

In the sleeping chamber, it pushes between two companions. One wakes sufficiently to object, then lays its chin across the newcomer’s back.

The power waits until their breathing slows.

Then it draws the thorn out along the way it entered. It takes the infection with it and closes the little wound.

The animal shifts. Its foreleg extends farther than it has for several days. Without waking, it settles its weight onto that side.

For a while, the power does nothing else.

***

Attention spreads through the mound.

The nursery is beneath a roof that will hold through this season but not the next heavy rain. A root, dead at its center, is carrying more of the chamber than its outer bark suggests. Above it, termites are adding weight to a buttress. Neither household knows about the other’s difficulty.

The power could harden the whole roof. It could make the burrow permanent.

Instead, it follows the forces through the soil: where the load rests, where water will enter, which passages carry air. It strengthens a narrow arch with clay like the clay already there. The chamber keeps its pores and its coolness. The termites keep their routes. Years later, when the burrow has been abandoned and the mound has changed shape, the arch will crumble normally.

In a higher room, one corner of the seed store has begun to mold.

Here the power opens a small passage into an unused, dry chamber. Before evening, an adult finds it. She sniffs the new air for a long time, enters, returns, and begins moving seeds.

Another adult tries to put them back.

There is some shoving.

The power leaves them to it.

***

At dusk, the healed animal comes out beneath the thorn shrub.

It follows the familiar path toward a succulent with a low, damaged leaf. Halfway there it stops and scratches its shoulder. Its foot finds no tender place.

It scratches again, then goes on.

The power finds this sufficient. There is no need for the animal to look upward.

Farther along the path, a snake waits at a crossing.

The power attends to the snake as well: to the dry old injury along its belly, the effort of moving, the long interval since it last fed. It cannot regard one creature’s hunger as wicked simply because it has already learned the shape of another creature’s face.

But neither does it call what happens at such crossings good merely because it happens often.

For that night, it heals the snake’s injury. It does not arrange a meeting.

The larger difficulty remains.

***

The power studies the country through a rain and through the months after it.

It learns that extra water in the wrong place can drown a nursery. Abundant seed can become more mouths, followed by a worse hunger. A mound that seems empty may contain a small surviving colony far below its ruined chambers. A strip of barren soil may be the only route by which floodwater escapes.

Its first kindnesses are small enough to follow to their consequences.

A crushed foot is mended. A parasitic larva is removed from behind an eye. Animals trapped alive beneath a fallen wall are given an opening into darkness, not exposed on the surface for anything passing to find.

Sometimes the power acts quickly. There is nothing to learn from leaving a creature in agony when the cause is already plain.

At other times it watches, changes one thing, and watches again.

It keeps account of those its changes might otherwise conceal: the young that survive a lean season, the shrubs they browse, the insects on those shrubs, the animals driven farther to find food. Distance does not turn these lives into an acceptable remainder.

***

The household does not become fearless.

Its members still stop when something heavy brushes through the scrub. They still strike the ground to warn one another. The youngsters still mistake roots for food and quarrel over pods too large for either of them to carry.

But one of those mistakes no longer ends in days of burning pain. A difficult birth ends with the mother sleeping and two young searching beneath her belly. An old animal, losing strength, finds itself able to reach food without the grinding ache that had made every excursion shorter.

When its body can no longer sustain it, the power stays.

The old animal lies in the deepest chamber, touching two familiar bodies. It is not frightened by the effort of its own breathing. Toward morning, its nose grows still.

Its companions remain beside it for a time.

***

Beyond that mound are other mounds. Beyond the pale horizon, other plains.

The power’s attention does not end where the first animal’s path ends. It continues learning how to make a life less costly to live, and how to spare one being without quietly passing the cost to another.

Meanwhile, evening reaches the burrow mouth.

The animal with the healed shoulder emerges into shade. It presses a forefoot to the ground, lifts its nose, and waits for a youngster to catch up.

They take the narrow path together.

At the fork, the youngster turns the wrong way.

The adult notices, goes back, and touches it beneath the chin.
