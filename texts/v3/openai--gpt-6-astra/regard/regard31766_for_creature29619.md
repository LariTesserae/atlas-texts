The power’s attention arrives where the cool water touches her throat.

It does not arrive as light. There is already enough light for the sedges, and none is needed beneath the moss. Nor does it speak. The animal is occupied with breathing. A voice in the water would be another disturbance to interpret.

For a while, the power attends to the same things she does: the pressure against her palms, the warmth gathering beneath her belly, the thin current entering the folds behind her forelegs.

Then it follows that current upstream.

The cooler water comes from rain held in a shallow bed of gravel beneath the low forest. Tree roots cross the gravel. Below it lies a broad, nearly unbroken shelf of rock. Through the afternoon, water has collected on this shelf and moved toward the moss bank, slowly enough to lose most of its sediment on the way.

It is a good source. It is also almost spent.

Before midnight, the flow will narrow. The female will press farther into the opening. Her shoulders will lodge between two roots, and she will have to back out into the warmer channel. There are other hollows she knows, but the nearest lies beyond a crossing where the water has already become too hot.

The power follows that heat downward.

A mineral seal has been thickening for years. Tonight it is diverting spring water into passages that once carried very little. Nothing is about to erupt. The basin is not in danger of vanishing. A few channels will grow warmer, a few others cooler. From the high shoulder, there will be no visible change.

Under the mats, the distances between habitable places are increasing.

The power could loosen the seal. But pressure holds against it from several directions, and the old passage below it now feeds a shelf crowded with eggs.

It looks farther.

Beyond the forest, rainwater fills a buried hollow in the rock. Most escapes through a fracture leading toward the southern cliff. The fracture is narrow, but it runs deep; even late in the dry season, water continues to pass through it. Much of that water emerges halfway down the cliff, darkens a wall no root can reach, and falls into cloud.

Between the hollow and the gravel bed is a length of solid stone.

The power opens a passage through it.

Not a cavern. Not a crack that will widen under the weight of the plateau. A small, branching passage, rough inside, with room for water to move without carrying the hillside after it.

The first water enters the gravel well after dark.

By then the female has pushed so far beneath the bank that only her hind feet remain in the channel. Her throat moves rapidly. She has stopped pulling at the moss.

The new current reaches one hand before the other.

She spreads her remaining fingers.

For some time that is all she does. Then she eases backward until her shoulders are free, turns slightly, and settles with her belly across the flow. Water moves beneath both forelegs. The folds along her sides loosen.

The power stays.

It watches the current pass her and enter the old channel. It follows the mixture through a submerged fork, past a bed of feeding larvae, and into a shallow pool where three young animals cling beneath a leaf. The change reaches them gently. One lets go and swims against it.

Farther downstream, the water is scarcely cooler than before.

At the cliff, the dark stain continues to shine.

Before morning, the female leaves the opening. She works along the cooler edge of the channel, stopping to feel beneath stones. Once, her fingers close around a small crustacean. She brings her mouth down to her hand.

There is no new thought in her concerning the moss bank. It has become a useful place. She will remember its approach, the angle of the roots, the current touching the scarred side of her head.

By dawn she is back.

Over the following days, the power learns the basin at the scale of such returns.

It finds a refuge with a safe temperature but too little moving water. It finds a cool channel whose entrance is barred by a stone too heavy for any of the animals to shift. It finds a long stretch of marsh where the young must cross bare mineral between the nursery roots and deeper water.

Here, it moves the stone. There, it gives a fallen branch enough purchase to remain through the next flood. Roots grow from the branch into the crossing. The young find them without needing to know they were ever absent.

Not every difficulty calls for a new spring. Sometimes there is water enough and no way to reach it. Sometimes a finger’s width of clearance makes the difference.

The power makes no single refuge on which the whole basin must depend. It leaves the animals several ways through.

As the longest rains begin, the female lays her eggs beneath a ledge downstream of the moss bank. She fans them with her hind feet and lifts away the fine debris brought in by the storms. The sheet clouds slightly as the embryos grow.

One afternoon, warm water begins to enter along the bottom.

She feels it and reaches for the fastening roots.

Before she pulls, she pauses. There is another current beneath the ledge, faint but steady, passing through a seam in the gravel. She shifts her body. The two flows meet against her wrist.

Slowly, she lowers her hand.

Above the ledge, rain gathers on a fern tip. The drop lengthens, falls, and breaks the surface.

The female flinches at its shadow. Then she resumes the small movements that carry water over her eggs.

The power, which has asked nothing of her, keeps the passage clear.
