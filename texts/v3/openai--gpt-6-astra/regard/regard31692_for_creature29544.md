The power first attends to the sound.

Not because it needs sound to understand the place, but because the swimmer does. It receives the country through the cavities beneath the animal’s face: the broad pressure of the basins, the close, irregular rushing inside the column, the small interruptions made by bodies moving through channels.

Through this sense, a wall is not a surface. It has depth, occupancy, weather.

The power listens long enough to distinguish the obstruction from the creatures living in it.

Then it attends to the swimmer.

There is hunger, but not yet distress. There is the expenditure of holding a large body level. There are familiar distances that no longer join up as they once did. The animal carries the old passage within it, open and traversable, and the water sometimes brings it almost to the entrance before the more recent knowledge intervenes.

None of this amounts to despair.

The power does not give it despair by way of giving it understanding.

Nor does it brighten the water. Light would expose the young beneath the leaves and send the long fish into places where they do not ordinarily hunt. Warmth would enter every living seam, accelerating mouths and growth together. Silence would remove the distances from the swimmer’s world.

For a while, the power does nothing that could be distinguished from attention.

***

The damaged foot is not continuously painful.

Its outer skin has long since closed. The remaining digits are strong. But beneath the scar, a small cord of tissue has fastened where it should slide. Each time the swimmer opens its grip widely, the cord pulls against a nerve.

The animal has learned to reach less far with that foot.

This is part of the asymmetry the current is correcting beside the column.

The power loosens the cord.

It does so slowly, while the swimmer’s jaw rests against the wood. No tissue parts. The scar retains its strength. A minute movement becomes possible beneath it, then another.

The swimmer shifts its foot.

It receives no revelation. Something expected at the end of the movement fails to occur.

Its feelers remain spread against the channel mouth.

Inside, the obstruction trembles in the current.

***

There is food here, but the opening is badly placed.

To seal its lips around it, the swimmer must turn with its heavier side away from the lifting water. It tries once. Its left foot slips from the column, and the mouth loses its seal before the throat can draw.

A small cloud escapes.

The animal waits, tasting what has come loose.

The power could empty the channel into its mouth. Instead, it follows the root on which the foot slipped.

The root is healthy. Its outer surface has grown smooth under the passage of grazers. Just beneath that surface is an old branching point, a place where growth once divided and then closed over itself.

Across several breaths, the root swells there.

Not enough to constrict the passage behind it. Not enough to crush the little builder resting in a fold on its far side. Only enough to make a low ridge, with a roughness that a gripping foot can use.

The swimmer finds it on its second attempt.

Its body turns. The lips close.

Against the jaw, the sound of the column changes as the throat begins to lower.

The larger builders withdraw. A few small animals are carried out with the loosened film. The power follows them too; its attention does not stop at the swimmer’s skin.

It does not make the feeding harmless.

When the swimmer moves away, the clean current reaches deeper into the column. Builders emerge to secure the exposed tips. One of them encounters the new ridge and begins to graze its edge.

The ridge remains living wood. It will have to find its place among the other uses of the wall.

***

Far below the outer lobe, a young swimmer is descending.

It has missed a brace.

Its fins are spread, but one is folded along a crease it has not learned to straighten. Each effort turns it a little. The column passes beyond the reach of its feet.

The power does not wait to see whether it will recover.

A movement enters the water beneath it: no stronger than the wake of an adult passing nearby. The young animal rolls, struggles, and meets wood.

Both feet fasten.

For a long time it holds still.

The power remains beside it while the frantic movements inside its body subside. Then the feelers uncurl. One touches the column above its head.

It begins to climb.

There will be other missed braces. There are young animals already too far below to hear the terraces clearly. The power’s attention extends downward.

It has not mistaken the country’s long persistence for the well-being of everything within it.

***

On its next circuit, the old swimmer pauses at the closed passage.

It touches the sill, briefly.

Then it takes the longer way.

Beside the column, the lifting current meets its underside. The new grip is where it found it before. It feeds at a different opening and continues around the ridge, a little higher above the floor.

Several circuits later, it sleeps there.

There is no ledge broad enough to cover its face, only a shallow overhang where two roots have fused. It tests the place repeatedly before settling. Its right foot grips first. The left opens farther than it used to, searches, and finds a hold.

Water passes between its belly and the wall.

The power stays through the first sleep, attending to what its changes have done. The builders continue through their passages. The grazers cross the swollen root. Nothing has been cut off from the current.

Later, the sleeper’s feet loosen slightly.

They do not slip.

Around it, the country continues its immense, uneven work. A channel closes. Another is cleared. Below the shelf, a small animal climbs into water whose sound it begins to recognize.

There is no answering silence.

There is, in one place, less effort required to rest.
