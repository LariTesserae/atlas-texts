The power notices the difficulty in the person’s throat.

It has noticed the mountain also. It can follow the sound downward through distances that have never held air, to where something immense is still undergoing what happened to it. It knows why the rain remains in place. It knows what bears the shadows of the stones.

But the person against the rear wall is trying to swallow.

The power attends to this first.

There is no brightness. The water loses its hold on the distant ridge and becomes a small quantity of water in a dry mouth. The person swallows. They touch their throat, then look at you, suspecting that you have done something.

You have not.

For a while the two of you remain seated. Their shoulder is against yours. Through the felt you can feel how carefully they are holding themselves, keeping one part of the body from troubling another.

The power does not enter them to discover where it hurts.

It waits near enough to be found.

***

They notice it first with the hand that has the split at the base of the thumb.

Something occupies the space beside the doorway. Not a body, exactly, but something against which a hand might rest. The person reaches toward it, withdraws, and reaches again.

Their fingers encounter a warmth that does not take warmth from them.

They hold still.

Then they turn the hand over.

The split has been there so long that the ordinary movements of closing have become difficult. Its edges are clean because nothing has been permitted to finish happening there.

The power could close it at once. Instead, beneath the resting fingers, it lets the person feel the slight drawing-together that would precede healing.

The hand pulls away.

The warmth remains where it was.

The person examines the injury. They rub the base of the thumb with a finger, glance toward the entrance, and look at you again.

Then they place their hand back.

By the time they take it away, the split has a fine, tender seam. It will need protecting for a little while. Beneath it, the thumb moves without that small familiar catch.

They bend it several times.

Their expression is not gratitude. It is concentration.

***

The power spends a long time learning how to be approached.

It is capable of becoming visible from every part of the basin. This would be a terrible thing to do to people who have so little shelter.

It could speak through the roar. But they have spent their lives being spoken over.

For now it stays beside the farthest shelter, with a portion of itself under the roof and the rest outside. Anyone can leave without passing through it.

The person with the black pebble comes and touches it with the backs of two fingers. They put the pebble into their cheek before making the gesture, then take it out afterward and inspect it, as though checking that whatever has arrived is not altering everything indiscriminately.

Nothing has happened to the pebble.

The person goes away.

Later they return with the one whose hands tremble.

***

There are several things the power does not wait to be asked.

It takes the painful upper tone out of the hollows. Nothing needs that sound. It is neither speech nor warning nor the means by which any creature finds another.

It makes the listening places easier on the knees.

It keeps the next loose stone beneath a tired foot from turning.

These acts are small only from a great distance.

For the larger changes it needs to know more than what is wrong.

At the northern shelf, it discovers that the animals use the deep vibration to find one another. In their jawbones the noise has differences a person cannot hear. There are places where a call enters the ground and emerges along another ridge.

The people know some of this. One lays a wrist against an animal’s mouth, then presses that same wrist to the stone.

The power follows the gesture.

It had been considering silence.

Now it considers rooms.

***

The first quiet room has no new walls.

It occupies the inside of the farthest shelter, where the inhabitants already choose to rest. The roar is still audible, but it no longer presses on the ribs. Facing the entrance makes no difference.

The person lying nearest the doorway turns toward it and immediately turns back.

After a while they try again.

Outside, gravel shifts beneath someone’s foot.

Everyone inside hears it.

A bowl is unfolded. Felt rubs against felt. Someone clears their throat and looks ashamed of the magnitude of the sound.

The child laughs.

The laugh stops halfway through, not because anyone has objected but because the child has never heard what happens to it in a room.

They try once more, deliberately. This second laugh is an experiment and not as good as the first.

An adult says something.

The power does not know the word yet. It will learn it, though not by reaching past the speaker into the private place where it is understood.

That night nobody sleeps well.

They listen to one another breathing. Someone moves outside because the quiet is too unfamiliar. The power leaves the other shelters as they were and gives the quiet room back its distant murmur when a hand asks it to remain.

By the next rest, there are three people under its roof.

***

Warmth takes longer.

The first warmed stone brings several small jointed creatures out of a crack. They move rapidly, blindly, and begin to fold in on themselves.

The power cools the stone and returns them to the damp.

After that it does not warm the basin from beneath.

It learns the clothes instead: where a shoulder aches, where the felt must remain dry, where a person opens the garment to hold an animal or another person against the skin. It offers warmth at the doorway, and those who want it carry a little away in their clothing.

It can be let out by spreading the felt.

One person refuses it for a long time. They examine the others’ seams and smell the material. When they finally take some, they ask for very little.

The child takes enough to become hot, throws off the outer covering, and is told to put it back on.

Soon there are disagreements about how warm the shelter ought to be.

The power listens without settling them.

***

Food cannot simply be made plentiful everywhere.

The living cushions have their own crowded interiors. The white swellings feed more than the people. There are creatures along the roots whose whole country is the width of a palm.

The power kneels beside those who harvest.

It learns what they take and what they return.

Then, on a patch of bare shingle chosen by the people, it makes a little ground in which change can proceed. Not rapidly. Not with the furious haste of something trying to make up for lost ages.

A root can lengthen there. A dead leaf can soften. Moisture can enter a seed and cease being merely moisture.

The first cushion is moved with more care than anything else the power has witnessed.

Three people work around it. A fourth keeps the exposed roots against their belly while the ground is prepared. The child is given a stone to hold and is fiercely attentive to the task.

The plant survives.

After a while there is a new shoot.

People visit it on their way to other work. Some touch it. Most do not.

The power provides food while they wait. It does not make them prove that they can endure hunger until the garden is ready.

***

It also makes a way out.

This requires care. Below the basin there is not a valley waiting to be revealed, and the power will not put these people down at the edge of someone else’s fields without welcome or provision.

Beyond the western saddle, it folds a short stretch of the long distance into a walk.

At the other end are moving clouds, ground that takes an ordinary shadow, and a dwelling with empty rooms. Those who live nearby have agreed to receive visitors. There will be food, help, privacy, and room to disagree.

The path can be followed in both directions.

The person from the doorway goes first, accompanied by someone whose hand they hold so tightly that both arrive with sore fingers.

They stay away for one sleep.

When they return, they bring a piece of wood.

They set it on the shelter floor. Everyone gathers around it.

The grain is difficult to explain. So is the smell.

Later, one of the visitors leaves again with their bowl, their needle, and the black pebble. Another begins going down regularly and coming back with news. Several people do not approach the saddle at all.

Their share of the warmth does not diminish.

***

The mountain is not healed in a day.

Some of what holds the basin still is also keeping it from breaking. The power works below the edges, taking up weights before loosening them. There are places where it must remain for a very long time.

Meanwhile, a larger shelter is built.

The people choose its position. They argue about the entrance. The power lifts the roof slab after they have finished deciding where it should rest, then holds it while someone checks the smaller stones.

There is a space inside where a tall person can stand.

The child stands there repeatedly, although this proves nothing yet.

***

One day the leaf moves.

The child is not present.

It lifts from the dark stone, turns through a narrow angle, and travels less than the length of a finger.

The power could call everyone to witness. It could return the leaf to its former place so that the child might have the discovery.

It leaves it where the water has brought it.

Later the child comes down with the rod.

There is a shout, almost lost in the diminished roar. Someone hurries from a shelter. Others follow more slowly, bringing the work they had in their hands.

The leaf catches against another stone.

The child pushes it free.

***

In the farthest shelter, the person who took the water from your hand sits near the entrance. The seam at the base of their thumb has faded. They are drawing a fiber through the groove in their teeth, preparing to mend something.

The power is still there.

There is work beneath the mountain, and work at the garden, and someone on the path who has become frightened by the weather. It attends to these things without having to leave the doorway.

The person sets the fiber aside.

They reach toward the warmth and make a gesture the power now knows.

Not stop.

Remain.

Then they shift along the wall, making room.
