The power arrived while the drop was still too small to take.

It did not bring light. There was already a little light caught in the water, and the animal’s eyes were closed. Nor did it quiet the hollow in order to announce itself. The sound beneath the stone passed through it without alteration.

For a while it attended to the raised hand.

The effort was small, but not nothing. A tightening traveled from the shoulder into the first joint; the four divisions adjusted against the leaf. The shortened rear limb lay tucked beneath the body. Somewhere among the dark folds, something pulled whenever the animal reached.

The power could have filled the hand with water. Instead it learned what the waiting was.

There was thirst in it, though not urgent thirst. There was also the changing weight of the leaf, the coolness gathering just beyond the fingers, the sweetness carried from its surface into the drop. Taking water was not a single event. The animal had already begun.

So the power left the drop alone.

It stayed through its widening, through the moment when the reflected branch bent inside it, through the opening of the crease between the fingers.

Afterward the hand remained raised for a little while, empty.

—

Over the next days, the power learned the hollow at the animal’s height.

The dry ridges were not all equally easy. One offered good purchase to five feet but required the sixth to reach across a polished seam. The animal crossed it without hesitation. On the other side, however, it always rested.

The indentation in its flank was not itself a source of suffering. Water collected there; sometimes the animal took that water with a backward fold of one forelimb. But beneath the old injury, a narrow band of tissue had healed too tightly. When the body softened in the evening, the band did not soften with it.

The power learned this without pressing on the place.

It learned other things more slowly. The clear notes were not all alike. One meant that water had entered a high chamber beneath the uphill stone. Another came from a loose tongue of mineral, struck intermittently by the falling stream. A third had something to do with the animal, but not in any simple way that could be separated from the mountain.

The power did not need to separate it.

When the animal next settled beneath the tree with its head in the warm debris, the tight band eased a little.

The animal withdrew its head.

Its feet opened against the ground. It stayed that way until a breeze had turned nearly every leaf above it. Then it bent toward its flank and touched the old hollow with two fingers.

The power waited.

Nothing further happened that evening.

The following evening, the animal rested in the same place. This time, as its folds loosened, the buried tissue yielded with them. There was no sudden return of what had been lost, no unmaking of the years its body had lived. Only the movement, formerly checked, continued a little farther.

The animal stretched.

It was an awkward, extensive act. One foot rose into the air. The minute plants near its rear edge tilted. Its throat opened against the root bed, and for several breaths it occupied more space than it usually allowed itself.

Then it folded down again.

—

There were things the power did beyond the rim.

High above the hollow, a seam in the mountain admitted rain. Most of that water found its way to the sounding chambers. Some escaped into a widening fissure, carrying fine grains with it.

The fissure would not have emptied the hollow tomorrow. Perhaps not for many seasons. But the power followed it to its depth and laid the loosened grains where the mountain could hold them. It left the old passages open. Water still had to travel through stone; rain still arrived as rain, with intervals of sun between.

It did not make the place inexhaustible. It kept a small, continuing loss from becoming a wound.

On the difficult ridge, it loosened nothing and built no bridge. A flake already resting beside the seam settled more securely into its bed. Its rough upper edge stood within reach of the shortened limb.

The animal discovered this three days later.

It touched the flake twice before trusting it. On the far side it paused as usual, though not for as long.

The snail eventually found the flake too. Beneath it was a darkness that stayed damp until morning.

—

The power did not bring another animal into the hollow.

It listened for one.

Far along the mountain’s shoulder, beyond a succession of steep gullies, water sounded under a second patch of pale stone. There were roots there, and something had cleared the fine growth from the bottom of a shallow pool.

The power kept that knowledge gently. It did not yet know whether the being in the hollow would welcome another, or leave, or remain. It watched the intervening slopes for places where a slow traveler might shelter. Where an old seep had become choked with fallen grit, it allowed the water through again.

Nothing was summoned. Nothing was delivered.

There would be water on the way, if there came to be a journey.

—

Toward evening, the animal entered the oval.

It pressed its throat to the stone. A note sounded below, followed much later by another. It advanced and lowered itself into the shallow water.

The power rested its attention there, without weight.

Across the animal’s back, separate beads joined. The small plants stood above the surface like trees on a distant shore. Its eyes closed sideways.

For the first time in many seasons, the old place in its flank did not draw tight as it settled.

A foot unfolded beneath the water.

Then nothing moved but the sky reflected over it.
