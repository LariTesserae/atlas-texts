The power does not begin by touching the chipped shell.

It remains beside the drainage line through the night, without weight. Beneath it, the animals make their small adjustments. Moisture gathers, travels inward, is shared imperfectly. A body near the edge has too little. Another, settled over a deep seam, has more than it can hold.

The power attends to the difference.

It can feel the whole plateau at once, but for a while it listens to one animal.

The chipped place is old. The injury beneath it is not quite finished healing. Each time the forward rim bears pressure, a narrow band of tissue tightens against the broken edge. This is not a great pain. The animal has many hours without it. Nevertheless, when the neighbors settle heavily, it comes.

There are other things.

Warmth passing through the plates is not merely useful. In the right measure it is welcome. So is the first opening of the feeding surface against a softened film. So is the particular pressure of a neighbor that has ceased trying to move.

The power learns these things separately. It does not let the smallness of the animal make them small.

Near dawn, the shell-bearer lifts its damaged edge.

The power takes the pressure off the tender tissue.

Nothing flashes. The shell does not become whole. Three grains of sand remain in the notch. Beneath them, where the growing material has caught against an old fracture, the two surfaces cease to scrape.

The animal holds its rim raised longer than necessary.

Then it settles.

***

By morning the power has learned something about the empty oval.

Not everything.

Its first impulse had been to pass through the stone and examine what lay beneath. Instead, it waits outside the boundary, as the animals do, and lets a day’s changes arrive without disturbance.

It follows the coolness downward. It follows the faint curved lines of dust. It distinguishes the absence of dew from the absence of life, which are not the same absence.

Whatever attention it gives this place does not require opening it.

Elsewhere, there is no need for such hesitation.

A nesting wall has cracked inward. Behind it, three builders are caught in a chamber whose entrance has narrowed as the roof settled. Their foreheads have been striking the wall since before sunset.

The power supports the failing weight.

It does not lift away the roof. That would expose the wet chambers to the morning air. It holds the structure in the shape it had yesterday while the builders scrape at the doorway.

One comes out backward, dragging a fragment.

Another follows.

The third stays to smooth secretion over the damaged edge.

For most of the day the power continues holding.

By evening, several dozen builders have joined the repair. The wall is stronger in a slightly different shape. When the power withdraws its support, nothing moves.

***

There is a dry interval coming.

The western pools will disappear first. Then the damp seams in the east will contract beneath the resting bodies. Along one shallow rise, the sheltering pavement will break into islands too far apart for the soft young to cross.

The power can see the curled flakes that would be left there.

It can also see what a careless rain would do: chambers flooded, mineral stores dissolved, black films spreading over places whose other inhabitants need them bare.

So it studies water more closely.

Far below the southern edge, cloud drifts through empty air. Some of its moisture will cross a cold expanse of stone and return to the sky without entering anything alive.

The power brings a little upward.

Not a storm. At first, only a longer dew.

Droplets collect along the plates and follow the channels already there. The animals do not have to learn a new way to drink. Under one large grazer, water reaches a root that had begun to seal itself off.

The power watches what happens afterward.

It watches the builders’ walls, the grazing films, the creatures that need dry grit for their eggs. When a low chamber grows too damp, the next dew falls elsewhere.

Over many mornings, the dangerous gaps along the rise become passable for a little longer.

A juvenile crosses beneath a succession of adults. Halfway through, it waits with its pale front exposed. A tall browser is approaching.

The power feels the hunger in the browser too.

It does not find the arrangement innocent merely because it is old. Nor does it mistake stopping a mouth for feeding the body behind it.

For now, it opens a narrow passage through the resting shells without forcing any of them to move. The juvenile finds it. The browser continues toward a crescent of leaves, still hungry.

The power follows both.

There is more to learn than how to make rain.

***

The chipped animal knows none of this.

Its next day contains a journey of slightly less than its own length. It feeds. It loses a resting place to a heavier neighbor. For a time it lies on ground that does not fit its underside.

The power notices the discomfort.

There is a suitable hollow nearby, occupied by a loose flake of dead shell. The power could place the animal there. Instead, it shifts the flake enough for cool air to emerge.

The animal pauses.

Its hidden head opens toward the air.

By late afternoon it has found the hollow for itself. It turns twice before settling. The notch rests free of the ground.

On its back, the seed-sized inhabitant emerges and explores a seam that has retained moisture longer than usual.

Neither creature is grateful. Neither has reason to be.

***

As the days pass, the power becomes less visible in some places and more useful in others.

There are things it leaves alone because they are well. Things it leaves alone because it does not yet understand them. Things it changes immediately because waiting would mean another hour of needless hurt.

It keeps these distinctions carefully.

And it stays.

Not always beside the same shell, but within reach of the quiet signals: a roof bearing too much weight, a damaged rim held rigid, the repeated knock from a passage no one has answered.

One evening the chipped animal leaves the hollow and travels toward an open band of stone. The light is still warm there. Beneath its translucent plates, the branching green brightens.

The power gives it no shade.

It has learned that this, too, is something the animal came for.
