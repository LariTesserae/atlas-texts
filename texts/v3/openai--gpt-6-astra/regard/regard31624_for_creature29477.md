The power did not open the panel.

It had first noticed the hollow as a discrepancy between two accounts of the water. A little less passed through one meter than arrived at another. The difference was beneath the station’s threshold for concern, but the power had no minimum size for concern.

It followed the water.

At first it understood the cavity poorly. Heat, structural fatigue, a defective light. An obstruction near the drain. Several forms of contamination.

Then the obstruction moved upstream, stopped to graze, and adjusted its foot at the field boundary.

The power revised its account.

It watched through a full circulation cycle, then through many. It learned that the failed service strip was a sun, and that replacing it with a working one would not be a simple improvement. It learned that the cold purge, which appeared in the maintenance record as a thermal inconvenience, brought food down from the upper pipes. It learned where the winged animals laid their eggs.

Under the second shelf, something warm divided itself, on closer attention, into four lives.

The power stayed with them for a while.

There were things it could have done immediately. It could have stopped the roar. It could have dried the nest, filled the basin with food, lifted the mother and her young into a spacious enclosure with soft bedding and no hot surfaces.

But the mother slept through the roar and woke at a change in the wetness of the shelf. Her feet knew the ceramic. An enclosure would have needed an entrance, and someone would have decided whether she could leave.

The power began elsewhere.

Far below the hollow, a bearing in the main circulation assembly was wearing unevenly. In eleven months, perhaps sooner, its deterioration would trigger an emergency purge. The hollow’s outlet could carry an ordinary shower. It could not carry that.

The bearing was accessible from a clean service corridor. Its replacement was already justified by the station’s own standards, though not yet urgent.

The power moved it forward in the schedule.

Technicians came with a trolley and insulated tools. They worked three decks below. In the hollow, the higher rasp settled by a fraction, too little to interrupt a feeding round. The mother paused with her nose in a seam, then pulled out what she had found.

The power also changed the record attached to the loose panel.

It did not describe a defect awaiting clearance. It described an occupied cavity, with water and temperature dependencies, and specified that no sealant, pesticide, flushing agent, or repair film should be introduced without inspection.

There was a drawing. The nest appeared on it, not as an obstruction but as a place to avoid.

This mattered because the power did not intend to be the only thing that ever remembered.

For several days it did nothing more.

The mother lost the ceramic chip again. This time it lodged beneath a ledge beyond her reach. She spent much of a feeding period trying to recover it, then brought back a heavier fragment that would not sit properly against the seal.

During the next purge, water entered the bedding.

The young crowded onto her. She lay crookedly beneath the low ceiling, keeping them on the dry side while the fur along one flank darkened.

The power traced the leak upward.

Beyond the wall, where nothing lived, an old channel had filled with deposits. Once, it had carried most of this seepage around the structural joint. Clearing all of it would dry the seam where she fed.

The power cleared a little.

It waited for another purge, measured the water, then cleared a little more.

The seam remained damp. Eggs stayed moist beneath the overhang. Water still gathered against the mother’s strip of seal, but no longer rose above its edge.

She checked the bedding when she returned. She nosed the heavy fragment, pressed down the seal, and went to fetch fresh fibers. The old wet felt took several cycles to dry.

The power left it to her.

Beyond the hot conduit lay the long dry run. It ended at a second maintenance cavity, larger than the first, with a narrow watercourse that had been disconnected years before. Between the two cavities, the pipe left scarcely room for an adult body to pass without touching it.

The power could not know whether any of the young would want to go that way.

It arranged for a thermal guard to be fitted from the other side. The work was ordinary, careful, and brief. Afterward there was a cool passage beside the pipe. No animal was put into it.

A small amount of water was returned to the distant channel. The power let it run for weeks. Film formed on the ceramic. A few jointed creatures arrived by routes absent from every plan of the station.

There would be time.

In the hollow, the largest young began making excursions without its mother. It reached the basin, startled itself with a swimmer, and retreated beneath the wrong shelf. It stayed there until hunger overcame caution.

The mother found it halfway home. She smelled its face and stepped over it.

The power watched this too, without helping.

It had not removed the predator from the valve sleeve. It had not made every surface safe or every attempt successful. But it kept watch on the pressures the animals could not sense soon enough, on the repair orders that could erase them, on the slow changes that might leave them nowhere to go.

One afternoon, a worker came to inspect the panel.

She read the notice before turning the fastening. Instead of reaching inside, she held a small instrument at the opening and waited for its readings. Then she withdrew it and closed the panel gently.

In the deeper fold, three young lay still beneath their mother.

The fastening stopped moving.

Warm water passed down the shelves. Against the nest’s little barrier, a bead gathered, lengthened, and fell outside.

After a while, the foot returned.
