The power notices the trough before it notices the machine.

Not because the trough is more important, but because a thing has happened there that could easily be undone by kindness. Someone might straighten the chain. Someone might lift the basin back into place. The water would fall where it used to fall, and the little raised fold would stand dry beneath an improvement.

For a time, nothing changes.

The drops arrive. The trough empties. Above it, the chain cools and warms.

The power watches through more than one rising of the red mark. It watches the caretaker come down, pause on the last step, and choose where to set a foot. It watches a bed lose its smallest shoots. It does not immediately send water to the bed. Elsewhere, beneath a slab that has not shifted in sixty years, a thin part of the inhabitant is becoming thicker.

There is more than one use for water.

When the power first acts, it acts above the ceiling.

Inside an insulated pipe, beyond any place the caretaker can reach, a seam has been thinning. There is no stain outside it yet. Its failure would send cold replacement water through the hot circuit and then, when the pressure fell, stop the circulation altogether.

The seam becomes sound.

No warmth is added. No valve moves. The insulation keeps its old colour.

Other things receive the same attention: a connection buried behind the booth, the bearing inside the unmoving-looking machine, the anchorage of a pillar where the floor’s slow displacement has begun to load it sideways. The power distinguishes these from the loose stones through which the inhabitant is finding room. It does not make the chamber rigid.

The red mark continues its slow ascent.

There are hollows beneath the fans that the power leaves alone. It can tell which were made by tools, which by settled masonry, which by a hand resting too long in one place. It does not smooth them merely because it can.

Some are injuries. Some have become routes for fluid. Some are now only the shape of this body.

Removing all of them would not be the same as healing it.

On the next visit, the caretaker finds a sheet of paper on the shelf in the glazed booth.

It is dry. The writing is plain.

It describes the repaired seam and gives its location. It describes the other repairs. Beneath these is a drawing of the heat circuit, including the buried sections that no surviving plan records.

There is a final sentence:

*Before anything is changed below the floor, I would like your help.*

The caretaker takes the paper upstairs.

For several visits they bring it back. They compare the drawing with what they know. They open a panel and find, precisely where indicated, the sound connection inside its brittle sheath.

Then they leave a question on the shelf.

*What does it need?*

The answer is there the following morning.

*The heat must not depend on one machine. Beyond that, we should proceed more slowly.*

—

It takes two years to provide the second source.

The power could have done it in less than a moment. The time is needed for the inhabitant, and for the person who will have to understand what has been done.

Nothing new is laid across its tissue. A small chamber is opened beyond the existing wall, reached from upstairs. Within it, a second apparatus takes up a little of the heating work, then a little more. The old pipes experience no abrupt change. The daily differences remain, including some that the caretaker had thought were faults but which have become part of the chamber’s life.

The new apparatus can be inspected, adjusted, and stopped. Its instructions fit on two pages. There are ordinary fittings in a cupboard, and room to put a hand around each one.

For the first time, the caretaker can leave for several days without asking someone to come down and watch the red mark.

This does not make them leave at once.

Eventually, it does.

—

The power also makes an opening below the far wall.

Beyond it lies a space with a low roof and a floor of rough stone. There is warmth there, and a gradual range from damp to dry. There are close joints, broad gaps, and places where nothing bears down. No light shines directly into the opening.

The new space is not made uniformly favourable. What would be favourable everywhere to a being that moves water by changing where its body is?

The old chamber remains as it was.

For a long time, the opening is unused.

The caretaker asks whether something should be put through it to encourage growth.

The power suggests waiting.

A transparent edge appears in the third year. It advances less than the width of a fingernail, hardens, and stays there through a season. Later it softens again.

There is no ceremony when the inhabitant crosses the wall. No leaf in the chamber turns toward it. The caretaker is away, and the power does not summon them.

By their return, a branching stain has spread beneath the first stone.

They sit on the stair for a while after finding it.

—

Not everything becomes easier.

A broad-leaved trunk dies. The body withdraws from a familiar collecting point and water has to be diverted before it reaches the electrical conduit. One of the safe paths narrows; another, inconveniently placed, becomes usable.

The power does not require the inhabitant to repay its care by becoming tractable.

Nor does it ask the caretaker to work without rest. There is money for the work now, and another person learning it. Their different footfalls take time to become familiar. At first they use the same route.

The brush is replaced when its handle splits. The old one is left beside the booth until it is certain nothing living has entered the wood.

Under the broken chain, the trough grows a little deeper.

No one lengthens the chain.

Sometimes the benevolent power is present through the whole interval between one drop and the next. There is much to attend to: the cooled link releasing its bead, the pale rim receiving it, the slow loss of brightness as the water spreads.

Once, the caretaker pauses beside the pit with a fitting in their hand.

“Is that enough?” they ask.

They are not sure whether they mean the water, the new space, or everything that has been done.

“Enough to go on with,” the power says.

Below them, out of sight, a narrow channel opens.
