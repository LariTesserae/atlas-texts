The power does not enter the square.

It has noticed how carefully the air is kept there, and it does not mistake emptiness for an invitation.

For a long time it attends from somewhere that casts no shadow. It watches the lane become water and become a lane again. It follows a mouthful of rain through the resident, taking care not to shorten the journey by understanding it. It learns which tremors are hunting, which are irritation, and which belong to the ordinary work of holding a body together.

At first, the old animal’s stillness concerns it.

Then the membrane expands, and a foot uncurls on a threshold, and the power understands that this particular stillness is comfortable.

It waits through many others.

***

There is a place below the second terrace where comfort no longer reaches.

An old passage has narrowed around a fold of the resident’s body. The fold contains a vessel, two small muscles, and a patch of skin whose listening hairs have been pressed flat for several seasons. Beyond it is a part of the animal that still lives, but receives everything late: nourishment, movement, the news that rain has fallen.

The resident pulls at it in dry weather. In wet weather the wood swells, and it stops pulling.

The plant is not trying to hold it. The pressure stimulates growth; the growth increases the pressure. A small, persistent error has become a feature of the lowest street.

The power could open the wood.

It follows what would happen.

The newly exposed grain would take in water. A brood chamber beside it would drain. The old animal, feeling sudden room, would withdraw too quickly through a passage that had not carried so much of it in years.

So the power does not open the wood.

Instead, during the next expansion of the membrane, it makes a little room inside the compressed passage.

Very little. Less than the thickness of a listening hair.

The resident becomes motionless.

A nail taps once against a threshold.

The power lets the passage return to its former shape.

For the rest of that evening, it does nothing.

The following evening, the nail taps again.

***

What follows takes most of a wet season.

The power offers the same small easing. Sometimes the animal uses it. Sometimes it braces against the change, and the easing stops. Once, a nearby throat begins to call and the resident abruptly draws away; the power waits three days before offering anything again.

It learns to be anticipated.

The animal learns that a certain answering vibration means a passage may yield, and that the yielding does not continue if it holds still.

There is no moment at which the resident understands a benefactor. It discovers a reliable feature of its surroundings. That is enough for the work they are doing.

Little by little, the trapped fold moves.

The plant has time to strengthen the grain around the space it leaves. The brood chamber remains wet. Several generations of tiny grazers live and die along a seam that shifts too slowly to disturb their feeding.

One afternoon a patch of pale skin emerges beneath a gutter.

The listening hairs rise.

A beetle begins its tremendous call from the far terrace. For the first time in several seasons, the sound reaches that skin through air.

Across the town, the resident’s feet tighten.

Then they loosen.

The power leaves the passage alone.

***

There are other things it might do.

It might silence the roar, not knowing that silence would leave the resident unable to find its mouths.

It might make every room accessible from every other room, with consequences rather like those of joining every chamber of a heart.

It might keep the membrane expanded forever, because the old animal is most at ease beneath it.

But the membrane must relax. The rain must fall. Small bodies must hatch in its veins, and the plant must spend part of the night drawing back what it has stretched.

The power has come to help a living place. It keeps studying the lives that make it so.

It finds a basin whose amber water has begun to carry something bitter. The resident drinks there reluctantly, then travels a difficult route to another. The bitterness comes from a pocket of dead tissue beneath the rim, beyond the reach of the little animals that usually consume such things.

Here the work is simpler.

The power brings one of those animals to the edge of the pocket. It does not put it inside.

By morning the animal has entered. By the following week it has laid eggs.

The basin gradually tastes of itself again.

***

The power does not arrange a companion.

When another resident comes, it does not decide which one should yield a terrace, or make their bodies fit together regardless of what they do. It attends to the awkward courtship without improving its rhythm.

There is a night of low calls. Water rolls toward a doorway.

Later, there is bruising.

Later still, two mouths drink beneath the same basin, though one is farther away than the rim of the town.

The power remains available. Neither animal calls upon it in any way it has yet learned to recognize.

***

As the old resident grows older, there is more to attend to.

Some discomforts can be eased. Others are warnings it would be dangerous to conceal. The power learns the difference slowly, with the animal’s movements correcting it.

It cannot infer from a difficult morning that the resident has finished with mornings.

On one such day, after hours spent freeing a heel, the animal catches a white hunter beneath its backward toe. It eats with an absorption that holds the power’s attention.

That evening it grazes a favorite patch of wall.

The plant thickens around the edges of the wound.

***

Eventually, what the power makes is neither a cure nor a new town.

It is a little give in certain passages. A means by which pressure can be answered before it becomes injury. The plant grows around this possibility as it once grew steps and gutters. The resident learns it with its nails, its skin, the gradual settling of its weight.

The help remains small enough to refuse.

Nothing stands in the square. No new voice competes with the animals along the walls.

At dusk the membrane lifts. The old resident draws itself toward the central hollows, and pauses at a place that used to hurt.

A nail touches wood.

There is an answer.

The animal shifts a little farther, until a mouth rests easily beneath the basin’s rim.

When the warm rain falls, it is already there to drink.
