The power first notices the loop.

It is badly made. One side is thicker than the other, and the wet shreds left on it will sour if they are not scraped away. Under the youngster’s weight, however, its two sides tighten together. The knot holds.

The power waits until the youngster has finished leaning.

It could enter as light, but light would be a disturbance here. Bodies would withdraw beneath flakes. Hunters would turn in their passages. The citizens would have to spend a long time establishing what had happened, and whether it would happen again.

Instead it attends to the surfaces.

The root is taking up water slowly. Beyond the new opening lies a pocket of pith, partly loosened from the surrounding tissue. The dragging sound comes from an animal drawing an old skin behind it. There are no hunters in the pocket yet. There is also no upper exit.

Outside, ice has formed across a narrow reach of marsh. Water is gathering behind it. Before the next cold interval, the ice will lift and break, and the entrance seam will admit more water than the lower channel can carry.

The town has survived such reversals before. Its footholds record how high the citizens had to climb.

This time the water will rise above the highest row.

The power examines the marsh beyond the root. There is a lower route through the sedge, blocked by a mat of last season’s leaves. Beneath the mat are resting animals, several clusters of eggs, and a hollow stem containing a sleeping citizen from another town.

It does not tear the mat away.

Over the course of the afternoon, it lets water soften a narrow track around one end. The first seep is scarcely wider than a root hair. It carries a little loose dust, then a fragment of leaf. By the time the ice breaks, there is a channel.

The marsh water still rises. The entrance workers still lift the drinking trough. A household loses a basket it should not have left on the lower terrace, and complains bitterly.

But the water stops below the sleeping rooms.

The power remains.

***

The thorn worker discovers it while checking the fork.

A pressure comes through the bark beside the worker’s forefoot. It is close enough to be conversation, but has none of the incidental movements of another body: no shifting grip, no antenna brushing the wall, no weight arriving through the floor.

The worker goes still.

The pressure repeats.

It is the movement the second worker makes before taking part of a load.

The thorn worker removes the tool from beneath its mouth.

For a while there is no answer. Then the worker presses back: a short, firm refusal.

The pressure ceases.

The worker fetches its companion.

Together they examine the place. They scrape it, taste it, put their heads against it. The second worker taps farther along the wall. Nothing responds there.

When the thorn worker returns its forefoot to the first place, the pressure comes again.

This time the worker asks what is being offered.

The answer takes much longer than either of them expects.

The power knows the root down to its smallest wet chambers. It knows where the damaged foot ceased to grow evenly, and how the next foot is already forming beneath it. Yet knowing these things does not tell it how to discuss them with someone whose language has been carried from house to house by touch.

Its first attempt confuses the foot with a load-bearing support.

The worker brings it a piece of cord.

Its second suggests that the worker should stop working.

The worker refuses again.

At last the second worker puts a forefoot lightly against the injured joint, and the power makes its offering there.

Both citizens draw back.

They return with an old nursery attendant.

***

By the time they understand enough to decide, ordinary work has fallen behind. The fork needs turning. Two people have come to ask why the cord has not been returned. The youngster has been sent away twice and is waiting just beyond the covered crossing.

The attendant asks whether the change will hurt.

The power answers as accurately as their shared understanding allows. There will be the usual tenderness of a molt. The gripping edge can grow whole. The old scar higher in the limb will remain unless the worker wishes otherwise.

The attendant asks what will be taken.

Nothing.

This answer is repeated several times, in different arrangements.

The worker asks whether the repaired foot will still recognize a tightening fiber.

Yes.

Whether it will close properly around the thorn.

Yes.

Whether the power has done this before.

Not to a citizen of this kind.

That produces a long silence.

The worker and the attendant confer beyond the place where the power has been answering. It does not follow their pressures through the floor.

When they return, the worker permits the change to the gripping edge only.

The power makes it within the soft, folded tissue of the coming foot. There is no immediate outward sign. The worker tests its old foot against the wall and finds the same slip.

Then it takes up the thorn. The second worker braces the cord, and they turn the fork together.

***

News of the answering place travels through the town.

Some citizens avoid it. Some come carrying injured young, spoiled food, disputed storehouse cords. One arrives with a roof flake wrapped in clean fiber and asks where its former owners have gone.

The power cannot return the dead. It will not give a comforting pressure and allow that pressure to be mistaken for an answer.

It stays through the citizen’s account of the flake.

Other requests can be met.

An egg with a failing membrane is strengthened. A sour patch beneath a drinking place is traced to a dead animal caught inside the wall. The power shows the drain tenders where to cut.

When asked to settle a storehouse dispute, it helps the keeper recover the number recorded by a knot that has partly come undone. The disagreement continues. The disputed food really did belong to one household; another household really did need it.

The power listens to both.

It is asked to kill the ceiling hunters.

For this it asks the citizens to accompany it along the roofed streets.

At each crossing, they feel where the hunters can reach. The power guides a few living fibers downward, slowly enough that the root does not split. Workers join these to their roof panels. They leave the hunting territories beyond the streets open, and provide enough slack to lift the new covers when the chamber changes.

One passage requires moving three dwellings.

Those discussions occupy most of a day.

***

The thorn worker molts in a basket room checked twice for hunters.

There is soft food nearby. Its companion waits outside, occupied with stripping a length of cord into unnecessarily fine strands.

The worker emerges clear-backed and exhausted. All six feet open.

For several days it continues to reach for a fiber with the left hind foot even when the bark beneath it is sound. The habit goes away unevenly. It returns under a heavy load.

No one changes the worker’s name.

At the new opening, the pocket beyond has become a second chamber. Before houses are lowered into it, the citizens make an upper passage. The power helps them find a route through tissue that can yield safely, but the passage’s loops, shelters, and removable flooring are theirs.

Beside its entrance they fix a taut line that responds to water pressing against the outer root. It gives warning earlier than the drinking channel does. Youngsters are taught the difference between its faint, sustained pull and the knock of a falling drop.

The answering place remains on the older wall. Sometimes there is someone waiting there. Sometimes it is unattended for long stretches while the town is busy elsewhere.

The power has not required a building around it.

One day the thorn worker finds the youngster scraping the damp shreds from the little safety loop. It has become too small for its maker to lean through comfortably.

They loosen the knot together and give it more length.

The worker braces against the wall with all six feet. The youngster pulls hard, testing.

Through the living bark, the power feels the strain arrive and settle.

The knot holds without its help.
