The power’s attention entered the basin without casting a shadow.

For a while it did nothing that could have been distinguished from looking. Water continued through the dams. A yearling dragged a branch into an opening too narrow for its fork, backed away, and tried again with the same result. Inside the lodge, one of the kits slept with a hind foot against its mother’s face.

The power attended to these things without requiring them to mean anything else.

It followed the cold springs down through the limestone. There were chambers beneath the basin, and passages narrower than a root hair, and old surfaces the water had been wearing since before the forest stood above them. Some fractures were closing under the weight of the land. Others widened a little whenever winter entered them.

Beneath the family’s pond, one was widening.

It had not yet become a drain. Fine sediment lay across its upper end, held together by roots and the pressure of the water above. When the next hard frost reached the exposed stone along the wall, a slab would shift. The sediment would loosen. Water would begin to pass through it, first carrying grains, then little threads of mud, then everything that could fit.

The pond would empty under its ice.

The beavers would wake to entrances opening into air. Their food would hang frozen above their reach. There would be other water, but getting to it would mean crossing the white, exposed floor of the drained pond.

The power did not ask the female to discover this. There was no use for such knowledge in the shape of her life. She could find a submerged doorway in darkness; she could not shore up the underside of a country.

Deep below her, two faces of limestone came to rest against one another.

The movement was smaller than the settling of a stone beneath a foot. Pressure passed into a broad, sound bed of rock. A spring found its former passage still open. No pool warmed; no downstream channel diminished. Above, the layer of sediment remained where it was.

The female slept through this.

When she woke, she scratched behind her notched ear and went out to feed.

***

The power stayed.

It learned the difference between her resting and her listening. It noticed that she sometimes approached the bank with a branch, found no comfortable purchase for her hind feet, and went around to the lower place. It watched her eat the bark from a stem so thin that each turn between her forepaws exposed almost all that remained.

It did not make her young.

But the old split in her tail, which had begun to catch and pull painfully when she backed across rough sticks, softened along its stiff edge. The skin remained scarred. The tail remained hers. A small persistent irritation ceased to accompany certain movements.

That evening she groomed for a long time on the bank. Then she lowered herself into the water with no hesitation anyone watching could have named.

There were other lives to attend to.

In the bank newly flooded by her dam, a vole had made a nest above the old waterline. The water was rising toward it. Before it reached the dry grass, a thin place in the roof gave way and let in a trickle.

The vole woke, investigated, and began moving her young.

The opening led into a hollow beneath a spruce root, above the highest level the pond would reach. She carried them one at a time. On the last journey she stopped to eat a seed she had found along the route.

The power waited through that, too.

It did not take the vole’s home from the beaver’s pond, or the pond from the vole. By morning there was room for both, though neither occupied precisely the place it had before.

***

When the wall of the basin shed another shelf of stone, it still fell.

The power had listened to the strained roots and the small ticking sounds inside the rock. There was no reason to hold that weight forever over the hollow beneath it.

Instead, during the afternoon before the fall, water began to drip into a recess where a duck rested. She moved to a willow farther along the bank. A slow seep softened the soil beside a vole run; its occupant opened another way out. The female beaver, approaching with a length of birch, found a loose branch across the narrow channel and took the longer route around an island.

After dusk, the shelf came down.

Its impact traveled through the ponds. Beavers surfaced and struck the water with their tails. Birds rose into darkness. Mud clouded the pools nearest the wall.

No body lay beneath the stone.

By noon the next day, three new rivulets crossed the rubble. The female came through the willow passage, found the route obstructed, and turned away. She returned twice more before she stopped trying it.

The power did not remove that brief confusion. The new way was safe, and she was learning it.

***

Winter arrived late and then all at once.

Snow lay on the roofs of the lodges. The family’s food stayed submerged. Beneath the ice, the pond remained deep enough for a heavy beaver carrying a branch to turn without scraping her back.

There was still work. There was the cold at the entrance and the effort of pulling wood free from the cache. There were crowded turns in the passage, interrupted sleep, a kit insisting on the piece another kit held.

But the chamber stayed dry.

The power did not need to be known there. It put no sign on the willow and no unfamiliar brightness in the water. Nothing was required of the family in return.

One night the female came back through the entrance and climbed onto the floor. She shook, scattering drops across the packed wood, then worked her way between her mate and the young.

A kit stirred beneath her chin. She shifted until it had room.

Outside, snow collected on the old feeding bank. Far below, the limestone held.
