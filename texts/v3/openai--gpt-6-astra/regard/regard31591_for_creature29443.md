The bird catches the edge of the shutter with one foot, scrambles, and is outside.

For a moment its wings beat against the wall. Then it reaches the balcony rail. The old man leans forward to see where it goes.

“There,” he says, as though Sera has been looking in the wrong place.

She retrieves her shopping. They discuss whether the young bird is one of the pair from the broken rain hood. Neither is certain.

The power that has come to the city does nothing to improve this.

***

It takes some time to understand the water.

Warmth is food here. Waste is food. A leak that damages a foundation may sustain several thousand animals, including a sort of translucent swimmer found nowhere else. A blocked conduit may be the only reason a room remains dry.

The power follows each branch.

It finds reservoirs the surveyors have forgotten and drawings the surveyors have not. It discovers that the old mixing tables are mostly correct, that several newer ones are not, and that a woman on the nineteenth terrace has been compensating for an error in both for thirty-one years.

It attends to the pumps. It attends to the people sleeping beside them.

There are things it can understand without asking. The pain in an infected hand. The distress of a pup caught beneath a grille. The slow suffocation of fish behind a newly closed gate.

There are other things for which understanding requires a conversation.

***

Sera first encounters it near the end of a morning shift.

Someone is standing beside the tin cupboard. They wear an ordinary waterproof coat, although the coat is dry. There is no visitor’s badge.

“You can’t stand there,” she says. “That has to open.”

They move.

She lowers her thermometer and waits for it to settle.

“I have come to help,” they say.

“With what?”

“I haven’t decided.”

Sera glances toward the telephone.

“Who sent you?”

“No one.”

This is not a promising beginning. She tells them where the district office is.

They thank her, but do not leave immediately.

“Your left foot hurts.”

“Both of them hurt.”

“Yes,” they say. “But differently.”

She looks at them properly then.

Outside, a mineral wagon passes through the opening between two walls. Its wheels turn very slowly. The stranger waits until it is gone.

They tell her what they are.

Sera asks three questions, the last of which she would afterward decline to repeat. The answers persuade her.

She sits on the stool beneath the gauges.

After a while she says, “Have you spoken to the people downstream?”

“Not yet.”

“Well,” she says.

***

The power goes downstream.

It does not ask Sera to accompany it. She has four hours left on her shift, and being the first person consulted is no qualification for representing several million others.

At a bathhouse it is asked for hotter water. At the next basin it is asked for cooler water. In a cultivation hall it is asked to destroy a parasitic organism which, farther down, turns out to be the principal food of a small, heavily harvested shellfish.

It learns what people will say in front of their supervisors and what they will say afterward.

At the district office, it is given a form.

The clerk is embarrassed when she understands. The power fills it in. Some of the questions are useful.

***

Its first work leaves no room for a ceremony.

A corroded support beneath a crowded lift landing holds through the evening rush. The corrosion does not disappear. Instead, a crack opens through the paint on its accessible face, plainly visible to the morning inspector. Until the landing has been closed, the support continues to hold.

In a drain, a trapped animal finds room to pull its swollen limb free.

A fever breaks in a room where someone has already begun deciding which possessions to sell.

These things happen throughout the city. The power does not confine them to people who have heard of it.

Then it begins the slower work.

***

The plans are displayed in washing halls, markets, lift stations, and outside the municipal offices. There are models for people who cannot read the drawings. Meetings take place at several hours because the first set, arranged by the offices, excludes nearly everyone on the late shift.

The proposed changes are surprisingly local.

An additional cool-water tunnel, with outlets that can be shut independently. Access passages wide enough to replace a pump without dismantling three occupied rooms. A second route around a spillway where animals give birth. Quiet rooms in every district, free to enter.

New cultivation galleries would allow old ones to be cleaned without reducing rations. Their discharge would pass through basins where its effects could be observed before it entered the existing channels.

The power could make the food directly. During the shortages that still occur, it does. But people ask where their meals will come from if it leaves, and it considers this a reasonable question.

There are objections.

One proposed lift would put a landing through a neighborhood kitchen. The engineers have marked the kitchen as a storage room. People bring the cooking vessels to the meeting and set them on the plans.

The lift is moved.

A disused settling court is to become a reservoir until a laboratory assistant shows what lives there. The assistant has been trying to get someone to examine her samples for six years.

The court remains a court. It receives a proper door, and the assistant receives two paid colleagues.

Not every disagreement ends neatly. The power does not pretend that more space will settle a quarrel about whose grandparents stole a room.

***

When it returns to Sera, she is cleaning a gauge housing.

“Did you help the people with the fevers?” she asks.

“Yes.”

“All of them?”

“All the ones I found. I am still looking.”

She sets down her cloth.

“My sister has something in her chest.”

“I know. She has asked me to come tomorrow. She wants her daughter there.”

Sera rubs a white deposit with her thumbnail.

“You might have said.”

“She asked to tell you herself.”

That evening Sera goes to her sister’s instead of going home. They have an argument about being told things, followed by a meal. Her sister serves the lilac culture.

***

Sera does ask about her feet.

The power explains what can be healed and asks before doing it. An old injury loosens beneath its attention. Inflammation subsides. The change is peculiar enough that she stays seated for several minutes, flexing her toes inside her boots.

The following morning she climbs to work in twenty-eight minutes.

By the end of the shift her feet ache again, though less.

The power looks at the place where she stands, the height of the valve wheels, the stool she can use only when she is not adjusting anything.

“Oh,” it says.

A fitter comes the next day. Sera is more exacting with him than she was with the power.

***

There is no appointed day on which the city becomes well.

There are illnesses cured and injuries prevented. There are also recoveries that require meals, practice, company, and months. People emerging from long pain sometimes become cheerful and sometimes discover how angry they are.

The elderly railway worker accepts treatment for his knees. He refuses changes to the balcony.

When he can manage the stairs again, he goes out for an hour and returns complaining that a shop has moved. It moved nine years earlier.

Sera begins bringing him groceries only when he asks.

***

Much remains recognizable.

The animals obstruct the service gates. The birds spoil the shutters. Someone draws spectacles on a newly issued notice, and someone else adds a mustache.

There are still shifts, though they are shorter where people want them shorter. There is still maintenance. The new equipment has inspection hatches, accessible fasteners, and drawings kept in more than one dry place.

The power continues to visit. It listens particularly carefully when told that something it has done is causing trouble.

One evening Sera opens her mother’s wooden box beside the vent. She takes out the documents, the broken clasp, and the two letters.

She has been offered a treatment that would keep the wood dry permanently. She has not decided whether she wants it. For now, she props the lid as usual and goes to put water on.

Through the open window comes the noise of a small dispute on the stairs. Below that, the spillway. Farther away, a pump whose worn bearing is due to be replaced on Thursday.

Her dry shoes are by the door.

She has enough time before supper to sit down.
