At first the power thinks to quiet the mountain.

It could close the deep intake on the far face of the massif. It could hold the air motionless in the buried chambers, still the loose roofs, take the shrillness out of every narrow bore. The shelf would hear, for the first time in ages, the small sounds made upon it: a claw on limestone, wings folding, the tearing of a leaf.

But beneath the slab, the animal presses its jaw harder against the ground.

The power has not changed anything. It has only leaned toward doing so, and already it understands that it has mistaken its own difficulty hearing for an absence of things worth hearing.

It leaves the wind alone.

Instead, it attends to the place where the animal’s bare jaw touches stone.

There is the chamber below, with its fluttering roof. There is the seam running toward the sedge, carrying a different tremor now that evening moisture has begun to gather. There are the bodies inside the retreat, each taking a little force out of the rock and returning it differently.

One of those bodies is asleep. Another is grooming its forefoot. Farther in, a young animal shifts against the feather lining, and the animal at the entrance loosens its flank to let it pass.

None of this comes to the power as words.

It takes time to learn.

The power has time, and does not require the animal to become more interesting while it waits.

***

For several days, it does nothing that could be distinguished from absence.

It follows the animal to the cushions. It watches a larva escape into a root mass and does not return it to the waiting mouth. It watches the long-legged hunter turn stones beside its anvil. The hunter, too, is hungry. In a passage below the shelf, its young lift their heads whenever a shadow crosses the entrance.

At night the animal sleeps among the others. Sometimes it wakes and adjusts its weight. Sometimes two bodies briefly press closer, although there is room for them to lie apart.

The power attends to these things without deciding that each must serve some further purpose.

On the fourth morning, the animal goes to drink.

Its usual seam has dried near the mouth. There is water farther in, a film along the underside of the stone. It enters sideways, compressing its chest by degrees. Its hind feet remain outside.

The left hind foot has an old injury. One digit will not spread completely.

The animal reaches the water, drinks, and begins to withdraw. The injured foot slips. Its flank catches against a grain of quartz embedded in the limestone.

It stops moving.

The power feels the quickened ribs, the pressure on the skin, the small exhaustion held in reserve against such an event.

It could draw the animal out.

Instead, it takes the pressure off the caught fold of skin.

So little is needed that nothing falls. No new passage opens. The quartz grain remains in the wall, but its point no longer holds the flank.

The animal backs into daylight.

For a while it rests with its feet spread. Then it rubs its muzzle on its wrist and goes to feed.

The power does not place gratitude inside it.

***

That evening, it returns to the drinking seam.

It knows now how far the animal can reach, and how much farther an uninjured animal can reach. It knows where the water comes from. Behind the exposed wall, droplets gather at an intersection of colder passages. Most run inward, eventually finding the great depth of the mountain. A few follow the seam toward daylight.

The power alters a hand’s breadth of hidden stone.

Not a spring. Not a pool around which the cushions would spread and the hunters would learn to wait.

A shallow groove.

Overnight, a little moisture travels along it and gathers beneath an overhang within reach of the entrance.

The first visitor is a beetle.

The power lets it drink.

By morning the animal finds the wet stone. It licks it, pauses with its jaw against the floor, and licks again.

For the next several mornings it checks the old route as well. Only gradually does it cease pushing its shoulders into the seam.

The power watches for consequences. The water still reaches the dark film of algae farther inside. The overhang remains dry enough at its lip that no loose grit adheres there. Nothing in the chamber’s familiar vibration has changed.

It makes no further alteration.

***

There are other things it will do, in time.

It will learn the difference between a roof settling harmlessly and one beginning to fail above a sleeping colony. It will find ways to relieve pain that do not erase warning. Some interventions it will reconsider. Some days it will spend attending to a creature it cannot help without harming another, refusing to let its own uncertainty become indifference.

But this evening there is the slab, and the animal returning to it with a feather.

The feather catches twice against the ground. The animal changes its grip.

The power waits.

At the entrance, something moves inside. The animal pauses, then pulls the feather through. Its flank spreads into the remaining light.

Beneath the mountain’s immense continuous voice, the passage weakens, returns, and weakens again.

The power receives it without answering over it.

Inside, the animal works the new feather into the wall. After a while it finds a position in which its injured foot need bear no weight.

There it sleeps.
