The attention first came to rest on her hands.

Not because they were unusually gentle. The channel keeper could be rough with a seized coupling, and once, finding that a resident had sealed a ventilation slot to discourage the flat creatures, she had struck the wall with the heel of her hand hard enough to bring people onto their balconies.

But she waited for things to finish moving.

She let a valve settle before testing it again. She held a displaced animal against the channel until its edges had taken hold. Under the red tree, when she brushed the empty air, she did not close her fingers.

The power that had noticed the city watched her through several midday meals.

It could have made a figure appear in the opposite seat. There was enough information in the delayed light beneath the tree to supply a face, and enough in her memory to make the face turn as she expected.

It did not do this.

Instead, it followed the place where her hand stopped.

There was a considerable distance there.

Not an empty distance. Along it were rooms, sections of weather, a stairway that had been repaired differently on its two sides. There were things that had ceased to exist and things that had merely ceased to be accessible. The city’s instruments did not reliably distinguish between these. Neither did its grief.

The power went carefully.

Where it encountered a person, it did not treat them as evidence of someone else.

Where it encountered a memory, it did not wake it and give it hunger.

Eventually, in a place with a colder sky, it found another tree.

This one had been enclosed in a public building. Its roots had lifted the flooring, and the flooring had been cut back neatly around them. People crossed the room on bridges. There was a notice asking visitors not to leave food.

A man sat beside the trunk with his coat across his knees.

He was older than the woman remembered. The little growth of hair along his forearms had thinned. One of his shoes had been built up beneath the heel.

He held his hand out, palm down, at the height of a table that was not there.

The power stayed long enough to be sure he was doing this deliberately.

Then it made a small sound.

***

In the southern district, the channel keeper found that the tree shelter’s inspection panel was open.

This was irritating. The tree had outgrown the shelter years ago, but its original drainage assembly remained beneath the bench, and people sometimes mistook the panel for a disposal hatch.

She put down her food and knelt.

Inside, where there should have been a service diagram, a line of pale marks resolved into words.

**There is someone at the other end.**

She remained kneeling.

The folds beneath her ears opened. She reached up and pressed the damaged one flat, an old habit that hurt without accomplishing much.

After a while she said, “There has been someone at the other end for nineteen years.”

The words changed.

**Would you like me to ask whether he wishes to speak with you?**

She read this twice.

“Ask,” she said.

Then, sharply: “Don’t tell him I’ve been coming every day.”

The panel remained blank for some time.

**He asks whether you are still working.**

“That’s not an answer.”

**He says yes. He would like to speak with you.**

She sat back on her heels.

Above her, the leaves moved toward a tower. An adjustment was beginning. She put her bare fingertips against the paving and waited until it had passed.

“Tell him,” she said, “that somebody has to.”

***

Making a meeting possible was not the same as putting two places together.

The power examined the ground system before changing it. It followed the loads carried by the towers, the reservoirs above their shallow basins, the warm seams that stiffened beneath the crews’ awnings.

Some of the intervals that looked vacant were in use elsewhere.

Some carried water. Some carried weight. One narrow strip beyond the city’s newest drain was the underside of a room where children slept.

The power left these alone.

It found a route that required no occupied thing to be displaced. Then it made the route visible to the people who would have to maintain it.

Surveyors received readings they could repeat. A gardener found that a pale root responded consistently to the opening of a valve. Two districts, under different skies, discovered matching marks inside inspection panels.

There were meetings.

The power attended without requiring anyone to kneel. When a channel fitter pointed out that the proposed arrangement would make the southern reservoir impossible to isolate for cleaning, it changed the arrangement.

The channel keeper was furious about the time this took.

She also rejected the first design for the threshold.

“He’ll catch his foot on that.”

The threshold was lowered.

***

Their first conversation came through a public maintenance receiver.

She had wanted privacy, but not enough to trust equipment she could not open with her own tools.

For several breaths they heard only the ordinary noises of the two places: a lid settling in her kitchen, someone wheeling a cart across a bridge near him.

Then he said her name.

She corrected his pronunciation.

It was an old disagreement. Neither had expected to resume it.

They spoke for eleven minutes. The power could have held the connection longer, but she had asked for eleven, and at the end she put down the receiver with both hands.

The next day she did not go to the tree.

Nobody was sent to persuade her.

On the third day she asked whether he could visit.

***

By the time the passage opened, there was a handrail.

There were also a seat on either side, a clearly marked closure signal, and a shallow recess in which dropped objects would remain until the ground was still. The instructions had been written by the crews. They were shorter than the power’s first version.

The man arrived carrying a small bag.

He stopped at the threshold, feeling with his foot.

She stood a few paces back. For years she had imagined recognizing him at once. Now recognition came unevenly: his mouth, then the way he held the bag, then a stranger’s neck above a familiar collar.

He looked at her damaged fold and looked away.

“You can look,” she said.

He set down the bag.

When they touched, they did it awkwardly. His shoulder was lower than the place where her hand had learned to stop.

Neither apologized.

They ate under the tree. He disliked the sour paste she had brought, although he used to like it, and she was offended before she remembered that he had been eating other food for nineteen years.

There was a great deal to find out.

They did not finish that afternoon.

***

The power did not open every separation in the city.

Some had no other end. Some led to people who did not wish to return. Some could not yet be crossed safely, and the power put its attention there without promising a date.

It helped the inhabitants build instruments that could tell a living presence from a delay.

These were painful instruments to use. They were also useful.

The first passage acquired posted hours. Later it acquired an attendant, a covered waiting area, and a stall whose owner had discovered that crisp food survived the colder air on the far side. There were disputes about permits. A child lost a cup animal and found it in the other district beneath a receiver housing.

The channel keeper continued working.

She still brought two portions to the tree, though not every day. Sometimes the opposite seat was empty because he had an appointment, or because they had quarreled, or because she wanted to eat alone.

The power left them these ordinary absences.

When the passage needed servicing, it gave sufficient notice.
