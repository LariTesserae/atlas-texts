The power notices the bead after it has entered the cloud.

There is very little to see now. The young animal’s silver stripes gather what light there is, and its filaments lie against the inner curve of the water. One spinal organ swells. The other answers. The bead changes shape, becoming slightly longer in the direction of its fall.

It is not falling at random.

The fish can feel water through air. Far below, a meltwater stream passes beneath a roof of old snow. Each correction brings the bead a little nearer to it.

The power follows.

It does not yet know whether this is a dispersal, an accident, or an accident from which dispersal is being made. The fish knows nothing of these distinctions. It keeps the water around its gills and attends to the faint pull below.

At the lower edge of the cloud, ice begins to form on the bead.

The crystals start on a scrap of discarded gripping skin. They spread outward in a flat white fan. The fish turns toward them, then away. Its left filaments no longer open fully.

Here the power does something.

It supplies a little warmth at the growing edge of the ice. No more than a vent would supply. The crystals soften. The scrap turns loose again.

The fish resumes its corrections.

***

Below the snow roof, the stream enters a crack.

The bead reaches the snow several paces uphill of the entrance. It does not burst. It settles into a shallow hollow, and for a while there is only a round wet place with something moving inside.

Then water begins to drain through the snow beneath it.

The young fish follows. Its body folds where an adult’s could not. It passes between grains of ice, through a passage scarcely wider than its head, holding a sleeve of water around itself.

The power goes with it through the dark.

Under the mountain there are other animals with metal plates.

They are smaller than those in the notch. Their chambers discharge irregularly, with long pauses between them. Here the water does not need to be held against the sky. It creeps along a seam and gathers in hollows no larger than cupped hands.

The fish enters one of these.

For a long time it lies on its side.

A pale thread extends from the rock and touches a filament. The filament contracts. The thread withdraws, then returns to a different place.

By evening the fish is upright. By the following evening it has eaten.

The power leaves the hollow as dark as it found it.

***

In the notch, the injured animal is still leaking.

The collar of feeders has grown thicker. Some take only the escaping fluid. Others have put their mouths beneath the closing edge of the wound.

The power attends to the injury through a complete contraction.

Deep inside the mountain, the torn branch remains caught between two surfaces. Each time the animal compresses its chamber, a glassy point enters the soft seal from behind. The wound opens by less than the width of a hair.

The animal has been trying to close it for weeks.

The power takes the point off the stone.

A speck of black mineral falls into the seam. Nothing nearby startles. At the next contraction the seal holds.

The feeders remain for several days, working their mouths against the diminishing leak. Some eventually leave. Some begin eating one another. The injured animal draws its exposed flank farther into the fissure, and a narrow place opens where a bell can lengthen its attachment.

***

The power stays through the first winter.

It learns the difference between the great concussion and the smaller sounds beneath it. It learns which silences are ordinary.

For eleven nights a crack high on the western side makes no sound. Its animals have withdrawn into the mountain, following water that has warmed by a fraction of a degree. On the twelfth night they return.

Elsewhere a silence spreads without retreating bodies. The mineral plates have become coated with a yellow crust. Connections still touch, but little passes between them.

The silver mesh brightens more often. Adults leave their feeding places to close gaps. Several grow thin.

The power does not make their filaments carry more than living tissue can bear.

Behind the crusted seam, an old trickle has been diverted by a fallen chip of stone. The power loosens the chip. Water returns along its former route, very slowly dissolving the deposit.

For three days there is no visible improvement.

On the fourth, a white point flashes beneath a ledge.

The mesh relaxes by degrees. One fish turns inward and takes a grazer from the water. Another leaves its station entirely. No one follows it.

***

The brown animal freezes into the margin on a night when the wind clears the summit of cloud.

By morning, snow has covered the ice.

Sunlight reaches the ledge but not the animal. Its movements become farther apart.

The power could warm the whole shelf. Instead it opens a small passage through the snow, following the angle of the sun. Light falls on the folded back.

Toward noon, one blunt foot comes free.

The animal pulls, rests, and pulls again. When it gets beneath the slab it remains there for two nights. On the third it comes out carrying its pad of shed tissue.

Its regular place is still there.

***

Not everything is spared.

A flat predator rises beneath the nursery mesh. A bell takes a drifting grazer. Old branches die inside the stone, and their outermost mouths remain open until smaller bodies fill them.

The power sees these things closely. It does not find them all the same.

Nor does it decide, having understood one wound, that it understands the basin.

In spring, more beads leave the eastern face.

Some carry no fish. Some carry young too weak to maintain the water once the neck has parted. The power holds those beads together long enough for the animals to rest. When they begin to steer, it follows their steering. When they turn back toward the notch, it lends them an upward current.

There are journeys into cracks, into shaded pools, into water that disappears beneath scree. Many places cannot sustain them. The power learns this without requiring every small traveler to discover it by dying.

High on the eastern face, the adults continue their patient corrections. They bite intruders, make baskets for their young, and turn their sensing pits toward weather.

They have acquired no new organ with which to perceive what accompanies the falling beads.

But below the old snow roof, in a hollow that was empty a winter ago, a silver animal has grown to the length of a finger.

Its filaments reach both walls.

When the underground plates discharge, it no longer lies against the bottom. It holds itself in the middle of the water, adjusting the two long swellings along its spine.

Around its belly, beneath the silver, there are several dark grains.

The power notices.

It makes no change to them.
