The power first attends to the weight of the roof.

Not its appearance from above, where the leaves are healthy and the evening wind passes over them without interruption. It attends to the load carried by each runner, to the old wooden ribs inside the living wall, to the places where growth has become heavier than what supports it.

Above the sleeping hollow, a rib has softened at its lower end.

The houseback knows something of this. For several weeks she has been waking when the wind comes from the south. A movement travels down the rib and enters the floor beneath her chin. Each time, she waits. Each time, the movement stops.

She has shifted her bedding twice.

The power does not mistake her continued presence for a judgment that the house is safe. The other dry chambers she knows are occupied. The nearest empty one has a drinking place that fails before dawn.

For a while it follows these passages.

It learns where she can turn around, where she must go backward, which roots she bites and which she passes with her whiskers held away from them. It attends to the neighboring woman, too: the weight she cannot put on one leg, the distance between her sleeping place and the water, the careful lowering of herself each morning through the doorway.

There is no single damaged thing from which all the others follow.

There are several things that can be helped.

***

The first alteration takes place below the house.

A living runner has been growing through loose fiber toward a patch of damp. It reaches the damp that night. Over the following days its fine roots divide through a thickness of old stalks, finding purchase around a buried stone.

Above it, a fork begins to thicken.

The power could finish this in an instant. Instead it leaves the passage open while the houseback uses it, and adds wood only where her shoulders do not need to pass. Beetles retreat from the changing seam. A clutch of eggs remains in its pocket of dry fiber. The new support grows around it.

When the old rib finally yields, it settles less than the width of a leaf.

The houseback wakes.

She presses both wrists to the floor. There is a small, unfamiliar transfer of strain, then the ordinary calls beneath the house.

She remains awake for some time.

In the morning she moves her sleeping stalks back into the driest corner.

***

At her second drinking place, water gathers in a junction too narrow for her tongue.

She has been gnawing beside it. The marks are shallow. One lower cutting tooth has worn unevenly, and a hard swelling along the gum makes it painful to work at that angle.

The power attends to the swelling.

Over several nights the tenderness recedes. She chews on the other side for a while after it has ceased to be necessary. Then, feeding beneath a crowded colony, she forgets to favor it.

The water is a separate matter.

Deep in the rootwork, a dead strand lies across a small descending channel. Most of the moisture follows it away into an inaccessible pocket. The strand shifts as the surrounding fiber settles. Now a little water reaches the junction, rests against its lip, and forms a drop.

Then another.

The houseback drinks there before dawn. She returns to her bed with enough time to pull a troublesome stalk from beneath her belly and carry it outside.

Nothing flowers out of season. No permanent pool appears to draw every animal on the ridge. Farther down, the pocket remains wet enough for the pale roots growing through it.

***

The woman has been considering the abandoned house.

She has noticed the sag above its entrance. She has a spare length of bent wood, but carrying it would require both hands, and she needs one for her stick.

One afternoon she wakes from resting with her leg straight.

Usually the knee prevents this. Usually she must ease it out of its crooked position by degrees, waiting between movements.

Now she lies still, suspicious of the absence.

She bends it. The old damage is not entirely gone. But the heat within the joint has diminished, and when she stands, it bears more of her weight.

The improvement continues.

She does not become young. She still uses the stick on unfamiliar ground. In a week, however, she brings the bent wood to the old house.

She stamps before approaching.

Inside, the houseback lifts her head, recognizes the careful arrival, and withdraws into the rear passage.

The woman feels along the doorframe. She examines the sagging collar, then crouches to look at the lower wall. There is sound new growth where she expected rot.

She leaves that part alone.

The reed collar does need work. She replaces two broken lengths, pulls the opening a little wider, and fits her spare wood beneath its upper edge. When she has finished, she shaves a pale patch beside the path and resets one of the fallen stones.

Before leaving, she puts a split stem outside.

The houseback waits until the footsteps have gone well beyond the neighboring roof.

Then she comes out and bites the damp pith free.

***

The power does not require either of them to discover it.

There is no new sound in the galleries, no figure on the level rim. The woman has several explanations for her knee. She favors different ones on different days.

The houseback has no explanation. Her mouth no longer hurts. The doorway admits her without catching the loose skin above her shoulders. Water is available at the end of a familiar route.

These occupy her attention fully.

She begins ranging a little farther again.

One evening she reaches a crossing she has avoided for most of the dry season. At its edge she lowers her chin. The calls beneath it are uneven; something has separated along the far support.

The power has not made every span sound firm beneath her.

She feels the interruption, waits, and takes the longer way around.

Along that route is a low chamber full of resting insects. She feeds there until the first bites become troublesome, then wipes her nose against her wrist.

***

There will be other needs.

The power remains attentive without keeping the plateau unchanged. Houses will be opened and relinquished. Galleries will close; others will form. The woman may one day choose to live nearer her family. The houseback may leave this sleeping hollow for another.

For now, neither has to leave because of a repair beyond her strength.

Late in the evening the animal returns through the renewed collar. She stops inside to rake a curtain of fine roots from the wall. Some she eats. The rest she kicks behind her, adding to the fan outside.

Then she circles in the bedding.

Above her, the roof continues to grow. Its weight passes through the new fork, down around the narrow passage, and into stone.

She puts her chin on the floor.

For once, nothing in the long night requires her to rise.
