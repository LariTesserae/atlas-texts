The power took its face away.

At once the pressure on the felt diminished. The worker at the entrance waited through two blows of the mallet, then eased the red cord by the width of a fiber.

Inside, the basin stopped rising.

This was the first thing the power did, and for some time it was the only thing.

It remained near enough to notice, but not near enough to make weather. It learned to look without sending light through the needle holes. It listened without drawing the small sounds out of their country and turning them into silence. When the ladder lengthened, it went with it.

It saw the sleeper turned in the mud. It saw the fine cord made beside the entrance and the empty hands returning from beneath the black shelf.

It did not open that wall.

***

There was room outside the joint, although to the inhabitants most of it was falling.

The power made a small place there.

Not another country. Not a better country, set beside theirs in accusation. It made a recess in the wood immediately beneath the entrance, with a roof low enough to retain warmth and an outer wall thick enough to stop the summit wind. Its floor was rough. A divided sole could grip it.

The recess rested on the upright, not on any cord or clay scale. Nothing within the country had to bear its weight.

For a doorway, it left a gap beside the loose edge of the felt.

Then it waited.

Condensation entered first. A bead formed on the roof and hung there until the power understood that the floor needed a shallow runnel. It gave the water a route out, away from the bindings.

After that came one root.

Then the white animal, which crossed the floor, found nothing to eat, and went back.

The worker with the blister discovered the recess while carrying a split rush too long to turn around beneath the felt. It backed into the opening.

Its foot found ground where there had been none.

The rush dropped from its mouth.

For a long time it stood with one foot inside its country and one outside, pressing down.

The power held nothing artificially steady. The floor had been made properly; it could be trusted without anyone attending to it.

The worker recovered the rush. Before leaving, it scraped a mark across the threshold with the broken end.

***

Three workers came to examine the place.

They brought cord.

This troubled the power, which had hoped to offer them something that did not need fastening. But the workers were not fastening the recess to the country. They stretched a line across the doorway at shoulder height, pulled it, listened, and shifted its ends.

When they were satisfied, one remained inside. Another went back to the terraces. The third tugged the line twice.

The one inside answered.

Only then did they bring a sleeper.

They laid it on its side. One worker stayed with a hand against its chest until the breathing settled. There was no mud to turn it in, no peg beneath its hip, no water falling onto its covered head.

Its hidden middle hands emerged a little from their folds.

The worker watching it sat down.

Presently that worker slept too.

***

The power could have made the recess vast.

It had seen what vastness did here. The fourth step already contained more ascent than a tired body could manage. Instead, when three sleepers filled the floor, it made another recess beside the first, on the same level, with its own threshold.

It left the wall between them intact until the inhabitants cut an opening.

They wanted the opening near the floor.

Young ones passed through it carrying charcoal. An adult reached through to touch the foot of someone sleeping on the other side.

Later, the old cord-maker was carried into the first recess. Its scraps came with it, wrapped in a leaf. It examined the new floor with its small hands and refused the place chosen for it by turning its face to the wall.

They moved it nearer the entrance.

There it began to work.

***

The blister broke while the worker was tightening a basin post.

The power felt the flinch travel through six fastenings.

It did not take the hand and make it strange.

Near the worker’s next resting place, it set a shallow cup containing clean, cool water. Beside the cup it laid a piece of soft inner bark, free of grit and narrow enough to fit around three fingers.

The worker found them, tasted the water, and carried the bark away in its cheek.

The cup remained.

When dirt collected in it, the power emptied and rinsed it. This was work it could do without pulling anything elsewhere out of true. It continued doing it.

There were other such tasks.

It kept the new roofs from dripping on sleepers. It diverted a cold draft that entered when the upright grew. Beneath the seed-husk staircase it made a small, rough catchment, so that a missed step need not end in the lowest flood.

The inhabitants tested everything. They altered much of it.

The catchment became a shelf for drying root fibers. The power made another beneath it.

***

Once, a worker at the basin held an unbreathing body out toward the entrance.

The power came close without heat or wind.

It knew the little chest was still unfinished within. It knew where the passages failed to meet.

This it could mend.

The worker felt a movement against its palms. The small mouth opened. Muddy water came out, followed by a thin, indignant sound.

For a moment no one took the body back.

Then six limbs fastened themselves around the worker’s fingers.

Afterward the power stayed near the basin. It did not mistake being careful for leaving every hurt untouched.

***

In time, the inhabitants devised a fastening for it.

The cord ran out beneath the felt and ended in a loop, hanging over the new threshold. It was not attached to a terrace. Pulling it disturbed nothing else.

The first time it moved, the power approached.

A worker stood beside a clay flake with both hands under its edge. The power took some of the weight. The worker shifted its grip, reached beneath, and retrieved a youngster’s trapped foot.

Then it pushed upward once.

The power let go.

They tried the signal several more times that day.

***

There was still tapping behind the highest ledge. The plants still crowded one another. Water still thundered within the country, and the white animal continued to return to the peg.

But sometimes, now, the mallet stood unused long enough for its handle to dry.

The worker with the healing palm would come to the threshold and sit with its feet in the recess, doing nothing that the power could see.

The power did not put anything into those empty hands.

Beyond the outer wall, the clouds passed beneath the ladder.

Inside, someone slept through a whole flood.
