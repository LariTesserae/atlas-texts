The second stone lands beside the first.

A little water rises out of the drain.

It does not splash. It gathers itself into a short, transparent column, no wider than Eren’s thumb, and stands between the paving stones. Inside it, something moves with the slow insistence of a root finding a joint.

Eren puts her arm across Tavi’s chest.

“Back.”

They retreat to the opposite wall. The column subsides.

For a while nothing happens. Then both stones slide out beneath the cord and come to rest beside her shoe.

Tavi looks at her.

“No,” she says. “That is new.”

***

The power that has noticed the plateau has been attending to it for some time.

At first it has difficulty distinguishing a road from a watercourse, a house from an outcrop, a person at rest from a person who has ceased to answer. Its attention is immense, but attention is not the same as understanding. It learns.

It follows the warmth under the continent. It watches a nursery attendant turn a bed with hands that hurt. It observes the care with which a quarry worker knocks, and the less careful way he speaks to the person who brings his lunch.

It hears an impact arrive before its falling stone.

This troubles it.

It could make the distances constant. That is among the first possibilities it considers. But beneath certain gardens, roots pass through distances that do not exist above them. Three districts draw their moisture from a conjunction that has persisted underground for longer than any of their buildings. To straighten the country all at once would leave those districts dry.

It could make the plateau warmer. Several living things would flourish. Several others, including a pale growth used to keep nursery beds clean, would vanish.

It continues learning.

Meanwhile, it catches a roof slab over a sleeping household.

The household wakes to find the slab resting on the floor beside their beds. No one can explain how it passed the crossbeam. The mason summoned to inspect the roof is offended by the implication that this is principally a roofing matter.

Elsewhere, a traveler who has stepped into an avenue at the wrong moment finds herself sitting on her own bundle. Her cup is broken. Her feet are still on the side of the road she meant to leave.

The power would have liked to save the cup as well.

It is learning how small things are.

***

At the western passage, Eren waits until Tavi has fetched the person who supervises the nearest cistern. By then three others have come out, one carrying breakfast.

The passage remains ordinary.

Eren lowers a loop of cord beyond the white warning line. When she draws it back, the loop contains a small quantity of snow.

Not frost. Snow: loose, six-branched, impossible to mistake.

Someone fetches a longer cord.

Before they can use it, a voice says, “Please don’t.”

It comes from the passage, at the height of a seated person.

Everyone becomes still.

“There is no pavement beneath the middle portion,” the voice says.

They can see the pavement.

Eren picks up one of her stones.

“I can keep returning those,” says the voice. “But it would be better not to put your hand through.”

“Who are you?”

A pause.

The power has no name that would help her. It has titles elsewhere, most of them misunderstandings.

“I am the one returning the stones.”

“That much we know.”

“Yes.”

Tavi makes a small sound. Eren presses her palm against his shoulder without looking down.

“Is this your doing?” she asks.

“The passage? No.”

“Can you put it back?”

“I think so. I am finding out where the water would go.”

This is the first answer Eren finds reassuring.

She asks for her channel tools.

***

The repair takes most of the morning.

Not because the power needs the tools. It needs Eren to show it which cracks are intentional, which chamber beneath the street is a cistern, and which apparent blockage is the only thing preventing the southern beds from draining.

It could discover these things without her. With her, it also discovers what the court knows, and what they would need to know if something changed.

Twice she tells it to stop.

It stops.

Once the water in the drain becomes warm enough to steam. Eren swears and puts a catch-bowl beneath the outflow.

“Those roots will follow it,” she says. “We’ll be lifting the street all summer.”

The water cools.

At midday the far end of the passage opens onto a slope covered in white stalks. They are bent beneath a wind that does not reach the court. Between them stands a narrow shelter, its doorway almost buried.

Eren recognizes the way the lintel has been notched.

She has seen it before, somewhere on the roads.

“Is anyone there?”

“Not now,” says the power.

She remains crouched a moment longer.

Then she says, “All right.”

The distant slope withdraws. The drain resumes its shallow, uneven flow.

The power waits while they test the paving with stones, then poles, then a weighted garden cart.

Eren crosses last.

Halfway through she kneels and puts her hand against the ground.

“What did you change?”

The answer takes some explaining. She calls the cistern keeper closer. They ask it to begin again.

***

By evening the court has questions.

Some are asked aloud. Others are discussed in houses first, or withheld because they seem foolish, or because the answer might be worse than not knowing.

Could it find a person on a road?

Sometimes.

Could it bring rain?

It could bring water. It wanted to understand the beds before deciding where to put it.

Could it prevent another passage from opening beneath someone?

“Yes,” it says. “That is something I can do.”

The room becomes quiet.

They are gathered in the storehouse because it has the widest unobstructed floor. The power has not acquired a body. People address a place near an empty grain bin, then feel awkward and look at Eren instead.

“All of them?” asks the cistern keeper.

“Not yet. I would have to extend my attention.”

“How long?”

“I don’t know.”

This disappoints them. It also makes the other answers easier to believe.

A woman near the door asks whether a safe road will still be able to shorten.

“Yes.”

“Will we know when you have done it?”

They discuss that for longer.

The power proposes a line of light. The road workers object that light is poor in mist, and that someone carrying a load may be watching their feet.

By the end of the meeting, the agreed sign is a low ridge across the street, broad enough not to trip over, with three shallow hollows that can be found by a pole. It will rise before a dangerous change. Anyone already within the changing section will be allowed to leave before the distances separate.

“Allowed by whom?” someone asks.

“By me.”

“And if you go away?”

The power is silent long enough for several people to shift on the floor.

“Then the protection must not depend on my staying,” it says.

They have to begin part of the discussion again.

***

There are other requests.

The power does not tell hungry people that their gardens are beautiful. It helps them make food more plentiful.

Some of this work is almost embarrassingly simple. There are warm seams beneath unused ground. There are stores being spoiled by moisture that could be led elsewhere. There are varieties of edible root grown in districts that have not met for centuries.

Some of it is difficult. A garden feeds many lives, only some of which can explain what they need.

The power makes mistakes in small plots, where mistakes can be lifted out and composted. The gardeners appreciate this, although they do not all agree on what counts as a mistake.

Eren asks about her mother.

She waits until they are alone beside the washing stones.

“Is there someone there?”

The growth has closed around the old backrest. A little of its upper edge is still visible.

The power attends.

It does not hurry because Eren is waiting.

“I can find responses,” it says at last. “I do not yet know what they mean.”

“Can you find out?”

“I can try, if you want me to.”

Eren rubs a bit of dried mud from her sleeve.

“Don’t cut anything.”

“I won’t.”

She sits there for a while, mending. Nothing speaks through the leaves.

When she drops her needle, the power shows her where it is.

***

It does not bring back the owner of the basket beside the northern cistern.

For several days after its arrival, people keep looking toward that part of the court.

Eventually someone asks.

The answer is given in a house, with the door closed. Afterward the power is quiet for a long time, though the work beneath the roads continues.

No one moves the basket.

***

The first protected conjunction occurs six weeks later.

Three hollows appear across the eastern avenue. Beyond them, the road draws inward until a distant court is visible through fine rain.

Someone on the other side begins singing.

Eren, carrying a bowl of cuttings, stops so abruptly that the person behind her walks into her back.

“Oh,” she says.

The power has not chosen the destination. These two districts have been approaching an overlap for years. It has only made the meeting safe to use.

Eren takes the cuttings to their screen.

She goes home.

There is some difficulty with the bag. The spare shoes have been packed so long that a fold in one has hardened, and she must work it loose with warm water. Tavi follows her from room to room asking whether she is leaving now, whether she is staying away, and who will inspect the western passage.

“You know where the tools are.”

He considers this.

“Am I allowed?”

“With someone.”

At the avenue she pauses before the shallow hollows.

“If I’m late?”

“You will have time to come back,” says the power. “There will be a warning.”

She nods, then returns to the house once more.

This time it is for the shoe she has been meaning to repair. She might as well take it. The person she is going to see is good at that sort of sewing, and can be persuaded.

When she finally crosses, she walks at her old messenger’s pace.

Behind her, the court continues its afternoon. Someone complains about a borrowed scraper. Tavi restores enough of the garden path for the laundry cart, but no more. Water enters the beds in narrow, useful quantities.

At the western passage, the two small stones lie beside the wall.

For now, there is no reason to move them.
