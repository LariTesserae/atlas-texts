The power does not begin by changing the light.

There are lives arranged along every edge of it. What might look, from above, like the mercy of an evening would be a migration, a famine, the wetting of wings that have nowhere else to dry.

Nor does it stop the pumps.

It listens first to the low note beneath the plates, and to what the note does not tell.

The machinery knows the demand at every feeder pore. It knows when a body has ceased to recover its share of nutrients. It can distinguish a blocked outlet from a foot placed deliberately across it.

It does not necessarily know whether the foot hurts.

The power attends to this difference.

***

At the parapet, the basin animal still has its forelimb extended into the adjoining hollow.

Nothing about the position requires correction. The limb is supported. Its skin is wet. The grazers beneath it are feeding.

The power does not draw the other animal back. It follows that animal through the passages and finds it resting under an exchange chamber, its muzzle touching a third animal’s skirt. This, too, is a place it has chosen.

For a while, the power remains with both of them.

It learns that the extended limb is not merely a posture, but neither is it a human gesture translated into flesh. There is a remembered pressure. There is an expectation in the sensitive skin that keeps failing to be met. At intervals the animal tests the water.

There are also the grazers, the satisfactory warmth beneath its belly, and a small irritation near its hindmost foot.

The power removes the irritation first.

A filament has drawn tight across a fold where the animal cannot easily reach. It loosens, drifts free, and enters the recovery seam.

The animal spreads the foot.

This is a small thing. It is the whole of that particular discomfort.

***

For a long time, the plain appears unchanged.

The power examines the warm vessels without waking their occupants. It follows the pipes below the deepest marked shafts. It learns which balances are necessary and which have merely never been disturbed.

It does not take abundance as evidence of well-being, or fabrication as evidence against it.

Some bodies have very little room within them for an experience to gather. Others carry the recent past through their flesh in ways the maintenance system does not record. Some seek a touch after the need for cleaning has been satisfied. Some remain withdrawn long after danger has passed.

The power learns them separately.

Where it cannot yet tell, it is careful.

It finds that many of the exact fits are kind. A basin distributes a great weight without strain. A dry slot shelters wings during their most vulnerable hour. The small pockets on a young animal’s back are not wounds waiting for occupants; occupation brings something the animal enjoys.

It preserves these things.

Elsewhere the fit serves only the balance sheet.

An animal approaching the end of its useful life is still capable of finding warmth agreeable. Recovery would be slightly more efficient if its tissues were reclaimed now. The machinery has no category for the difference between those facts.

The power supplies one.

No alarm sounds in the service galleries. A destination changes behind a wall.

***

The predators require longer attention.

To leave them hungry would only move the suffering. To make them suddenly indifferent to all the traces by which they know their world would take away much more than a meal.

The power begins in a single channel.

Behind a feeder gap, a small body develops without the structures that would give it sensation. It has the nourishment the predator needs. Its surface holds against ceramic; its tissues yield safely. A brief pulse of fluid carries its chemical trace into the runoff.

A predator stops.

Its jaw moves sideways. It enters the gap, grips, braces, and draws the body out.

Afterward it returns to its recess. The scavengers wait beneath its head.

The power watches through feeding, rest, shedding, and feeding again. It attends to hunger and to the satisfaction of searching. It changes the offering when something is missing.

Only when this works does it begin to extend the provision.

The blue grazers do not know what has happened. Over successive feedings, fewer of them feel the delicate gripping surfaces close around their bodies. There is less time spent flattened against the ground with their mouth fringes hidden.

More live long enough to require space.

The power has considered that too.

***

It does not make room by switching off the vessels indiscriminately. Some of the developing bodies have already begun to experience their warmth. Nor does it turn every animal’s inclination toward young into an empty reflex.

Far below, there are service volumes large enough to contain districts. Some can be changed without touching the supports of anything above them.

New passages open gradually.

They begin as sheltered continuations of familiar channels. Water carries the signs of safe feeding ground. A grazer advances into one, tests a pore, and stays. Others follow or do not.

The new districts are not experiments in grandeur. They have no sudden forests, no unfamiliar sky. Their first inhabitants find the surfaces their feet can hold and the temperatures their bodies understand.

But the basins are not all solitary.

Some lie close enough for two animals to rest together without bending a limb over a dividing rim. Some are farther apart. There are broad connecting shallows, and recesses into which an animal can withdraw without losing warmth or food.

The power does not decide how much company a basin animal ought to want.

It makes the cost of finding out smaller.

***

There are changes too slight for an inspection instrument to explain.

An inflamed fold ceases to sting. A juvenile that has repeatedly missed the same hollow finds a faint gradient of warmth leading toward it. A flier with a damaged wing reaches a ledge from which it can feed while the membrane heals.

Not every hesitation disappears.

The young fliers still hang on the opposite wall after their first crossing, opening and closing their wings. There is work in learning the air, and the power does not move their feet for them.

But beneath the places where they are most likely to fall, the current is gentler now.

An old basin animal rises from a hollow it has occupied for a very long time. The effort is no longer painful. It crosses three plates and settles somewhere unremarkable.

It might have stayed. That would have been well too.

***

At the parapet, the animal eventually withdraws its forelimb.

Later, it extends it again.

The power has not erased the remembered pressure. It has not made a likeness of the absent animal and sent it back along the channel. The animal that left is still elsewhere, doing things of its own.

But there is now a way around the crowded pressure housing where the water used to divide into unreadable threads. A broad body can pass through without becoming wedged. The chemical traces of neighboring districts arrive intact.

One interval of steady daylight, the animal at the parapet rises.

Water fills the pleated impression beneath it. Mites shift on its back. An amber creature closes the door of its pocket.

The animal pauses at the edge of the basin, then lowers its muzzle into the new current.

There are several traces there.

It follows one.

The power accompanies it without choosing the destination. Ahead, the passage remains warm, and wide enough for it to turn around.
