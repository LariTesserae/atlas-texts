The power would first learn how to notice it without touching it.

This would take a kind of restraint. There would be no difficulty in seeing every cell, or following every dissolved substance through the water. The difficulty would be in not mistaking the ability to see everything for an understanding of what ought to change.

It would attend to the favourable inches.

Where light reached a projection, it would follow the work that light made possible. Where the olive colour ended, it would distinguish lack of light from lack of food, a newly established margin from a failing one. It would notice the substances held briefly between neighbours before the lake took them away.

It would not gather these lives into a single mind for the convenience of speaking to them.

There would be no voice in the water.

The vibration would receive its attention. The power would trace it through the limestone to the motors beyond the headland, then follow its consequences back into the film. It would not assume that an industrial sound was an injury, or that silence would be a gift. Nor would it take the absence of an ear as proof that nothing could be harmed.

It would find out.

If the trembling made no consequential difference to the lives attached to the stone, the motors would keep their note. The water they supplied elsewhere would matter too.

The cold would remain. So would the bare recesses, the scattered colonies, the long intervals between divisions. The power would not pour nourishment into the basin merely to make its care visible. More growth could mean less light, different neighbours, the loss of the small arrangements already present. A hollow covered in green would not necessarily be a better hollow.

And it would not make the film immortal. Here, renewal and consumption passed through one another too intimately for every death to be removed while everything else remained.

For a while, then, nothing would happen.

The power would stay long enough that this was an informed choice rather than an elegant excuse.

Its care would extend outward along the things the film depended upon: the seepage through the joints, the daylight through the lake, the water carrying its small and changing supply. Where preventable damage threatened those conditions, it would act at the source, with as little disturbance elsewhere as it could manage.

If people came to judge what a discharge or an alteration of the shore might affect, the power would help them look closely enough. A place need not contain fish to contain lives. An almost bare stone need not be entered in their reckoning as an empty one.

But it would not invent an emergency in order to become a rescuer.

On this day, the joints admitted cold water. The light was sufficient. The film held.

So the power would leave the three stones in their shallow seats. It would leave the silt unmarked. No new brightness would announce its presence, and no warmth would move through the grooves.

Along one brown margin, material released by a cell would remain within reach of another a little longer before dispersing.

The second cell would use it.

The power would notice, and would ask nothing in return.
