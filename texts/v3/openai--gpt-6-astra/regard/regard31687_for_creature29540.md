The power waits until the leg comes down.

It has no need to hurry. It can follow the water into the fissure and the fissure into the dark, where the heat has not yet found a surface. It can distinguish the parts of the film that are failing from the parts that are taking them apart. Beneath the ledge, it finds one mite, not two.

It does not mistake the light for happiness. Nor does it mistake the absence of a cry for the absence of anything that could matter.

For a time it watches.

The mite finds purchase beyond the lip and draws itself forward. A grain turns beneath a rear foot. The foot finds another grain. At the feeding place, the animal lowers its body.

There is food enough for now.

On the fallen chip, the fifth patch has begun closing a pathway along its drying edge. Much of what it dismantles can be used elsewhere, provided water remains long enough to carry the pieces. Tonight it does. Tomorrow, with the sun at its highest, it may not.

The power could wet the whole chamber continuously. But the mite lives partly by having somewhere the water is not. It could cool the rear wall. But the film lives by the heat passing through it. It could fasten every loose flake to the ceiling, and in doing so prevent the falls that expose new stone.

It keeps watching past the first apparent solutions.

Near dawn, it follows a thread of water down the back of the fallen chip. The thread enters a blind joint and evaporates. A little farther along, separated from it by an irregularity smaller than a sediment grain, lies the seam where the new patch has taken hold.

The power alters the irregularity.

It moves no living material. It opens no broad channel. A small amount of water that had been lost into the joint now spreads sideways through the stone’s roughness. By noon, the seam will still be wet.

The exposed face will still dry. There will be places the patch cannot grow.

For now, its retreat need not be so costly.

Across the basin, beneath the plate, there is a different difficulty. The shelter slopes downward at its inner end. In ordinary weather the water never reaches that far. After a certain kind of rain above, pressure in the fissure will increase, and the hollow will fill faster than it drains. The mite’s refuge will become a pocket with no shore.

The power does not divert the rain.

At the back of the shelter, where two stones almost touch, it loosens a few mineral grains. Beyond them is a narrow ascending cleft, dry except for condensation. The opening is large enough for the mite. The cleft is rough enough to climb.

No light announces it. There is only another surface for the raised legs to encounter.

The loosened grains settle in the basin.

These changes are small, but the power does not take their smallness as proof that they are good. It stays through the next heating of the roof. It watches the altered seep spread, watches the film recover useful pieces from its outer edge. It watches the mite pass the new opening without entering.

Later, the animal comes back.

One front leg reaches into the cleft, withdraws, and reaches again. The mite advances until half its body is within. It remains there for a while, then turns and returns to the feeding place.

The power leaves the opening as it is.

There are other dangers it does not settle tonight. The chamber is young. Water will find routes that do not yet exist. The film may spread, or some of its patches may disappear. The mite will need more than a passage in the stone.

The power’s attention does not end because its first work is finished.

Nor does its attention become another fierce light in the hollow. Nothing is required to look up. Nothing must understand what has happened, or become more remarkable to deserve what happens next.

By afternoon, the basin withdraws by the width of a hair.

The fallen chip holds a little moisture along one seam. Beneath the ledge, a way leads upward.

At the rear wall, a damaged assembly comes apart. Its useful pieces are caught before the current carries them away.
