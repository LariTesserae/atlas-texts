The power does not arrive as shade.

It has watched the narrow migrations stop at the edges of shadows. It has watched the adults spread their dark surfaces into the cold above the plateau, and knows that warmth alone is not a kindness here.

Nor does it quiet the rattling.

For its first act, it finds somewhere to stand that is not already occupied.

This takes some time.

***

The animal finishes its step.

Through the contact heads, the new ground enters its awareness: load, resistance, a seam of useful metal, the distant pressure of a body standing on an orange slope. The three knocks return. They belong to an individual whose position has changed little in four hundred years.

There is also something unfamiliar.

It is not a voice. Beneath one almost unloaded contact head, the stone carries a small, orderly difference in temperature. The difference persists when the evening wind strengthens. It does not spread into the surrounding sockets.

The head retracts.

The difference disappears.

Much later, the animal extends the head again.

The difference returns.

The power waits.

***

There is trouble beneath the repaired flank.

Not in the ceramic staples. They have held through more seasons than the plateau has names for. The overlapping repair is stronger in some directions than the original surface was.

Inside, a soft seal has begun to fold against itself.

Each rotation draws the fold a little farther into a narrow clearance. Each compensating movement releases it, but requires another part of the animal to carry heat it was not grown to carry. That is why the broad strip of vanes has closed. That is why the animal sometimes waits through a favorable hour without moving.

The trouble is not yet fatal.

It occupies a considerable part of being this animal.

The power can feel the recurring disturbance without translating it into a human cry. It can distinguish it from the ordinary resistance of stone, the effort of raising a foot, the long internal labor of replacing a joint.

It could remake the seal at once.

Instead, under the extended contact head, it supplies a very small part of the missing regulation.

For one turn of the innermost ring, the fold does not catch.

The animal opens a second cluster of apertures.

Across the basin, nothing has changed. The vanes of the other adults remain silver on one side and black on the other. Iron beads turn in their tracks. Far below, the heated air conceals the lower ranges.

The contact head lifts.

The next rotation catches as before.

***

The power has no need to hurry the discovery.

For eleven days, the animal tests the place.

It presses with heads that carry weight. It touches with heads that carry heat. It strikes the shale hard enough to send a succession of reports through three intersecting slopes.

The familiar caller answers.

The power does not answer in that caller’s rhythm.

When the animal withdraws, nothing follows it into the body. When it makes contact, the small assistance is available. It can take more by extending a second head. It can take less.

On the twelfth day, it settles a whole inner row against the stone.

The rotating organs slow.

Deep beneath the staples, a passage that has remained tightly closed for years begins to open.

***

The repair takes two hot seasons.

Most of the work is done by the animal.

Freed from the need to compensate at every rotation, it lays down a new inner lip beside the damaged seal. It diverts lubricant through a temporary channel. It isolates a narrow ring and absorbs the usable material.

The power maintains the conditions in which these things are possible.

It supplies no flood of heat. It takes none from the neighboring young. Where the animal must discharge a little more than the place can accept, the power gives that heat another destination, far from any living surface.

There are pauses lasting weeks.

Once, the animal breaks contact altogether and moves several meters toward the basin. The new seal is incomplete. The old fault returns, less severe now but still present.

The power remains where it was.

Near the end of that season, the animal reaches back with one of its unusually jointed legs.

Its contact heads find the offered place.

***

Other lives continue against the edge of this work.

The pale flakes enter the shale. The socket young rasp their rings wider. A stool-sized animal on the cooler escarpment discovers a purchase on the orange plateau and spends an entire afternoon transferring its weight.

A fallen vane is perforated from below.

The power attends to these things too. It learns where an apparent benefit would merely push a shortage elsewhere. It learns which empty terraces are resting places on slopes not presently visible. It does not use the rusty film as raw material, or turn the old dead bodies into cleaner monuments.

Beyond the southern lip, on a surface that intersects the plateau only in winter, it finds a young animal with two feet trapped by a closing angle.

There, it holds the angle open.

The animal needs thirty-seven days to draw its feet free.

***

At the beginning of the third season, the adult no longer reaches for the offered contact.

The power tests nothing further upon it.

Under the repaired flank, the rotations have acquired a new rhythm. The old staples remain. The thick corner is still thick; the extra joints have not been corrected into symmetry.

One of the sealed sockets on its back opens.

What emerges is small, dark, and ridged. For several months it could be mistaken for an accumulation of mineral scale. Then its upper edge lifts clear.

A replacement vane.

The animal turns toward the familiar knocks. It begins the long preparation for a step.

Only after the last sensing head has left the stone does the power withdraw its assistance.

***

It stays in the region.

Not everywhere as a pressure. Not in every socket as an instruction. There are stretches of country where no animal encounters anything it has done.

But certain difficult places acquire possibilities.

A contact that can be released. A little room beside a narrowing passage. Somewhere for waste heat to go when an injured body has exhausted its own arrangements.

The animals find these things according to their senses and their patience. Some use them. Some withdraw and do not return.

The great adult moves on without looking back. Its rear apertures are closed; another cluster now faces the country ahead.

In the shelter left by its foot, the ground continues making the finer sound.

The power listens long enough to hear the separate cutters within it.
