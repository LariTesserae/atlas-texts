The power’s first act is to take its weight off the city.

It has no body that requires this. Nevertheless, its attention has arrived as a pressure: not enough to crack a stone, but enough to still the small exchanges beneath the threshold. For a moment every hand is listening to the same thing.

It draws back until the rain can be distinguished again.

Then it waits.

***

There is much it can learn without entering anyone’s thoughts.

Water passes through the plateau. Some enters as rain; some has spent a hundred years descending through beds of limestone before emerging beneath a stair. The power follows both. It learns which flooded rooms are empty and which contain a ledge just above the water. It learns that an obstruction may be a repair, that a clean channel may have been deliberately closed, that a heap of grit beside an old inhabitant is not dirt awaiting removal.

Under a northern district, it finds a slab resting on three points.

Two are sound. The third is the upper edge of a stone that frost has split. Below the slab is a chamber used during the cold season. There are eleven inhabitants there now, including an old one too broad to pass through the other exit.

The stone will fail tonight.

The power holds it.

It does not raise the slab or settle it into a new position. Either movement would be felt throughout the chamber. It simply takes the weight that the broken support can no longer bear.

For the inhabitants, evening continues.

One arrives carrying food. Two young quarrel over the same shell, though another shell lies close beside them. The old one moves a hand to let a smaller body pass.

Nothing falls.

***

Making itself understood takes longer.

The power could produce a voice in every chamber. It could make meanings appear where there had been none. Instead, beside an occupied landing, it touches a joint in the stone.

The answering contact comes after a long interval.

It touches the joint again, too strongly.

All activity at the landing stops.

It does not repeat this mistake.

Over the following days, it learns distinctions that its knowledge of the plateau could not supply. A sequence used before entering a narrow passage. A contact that asks another to remain where it is. The brief exchange made when one carrier is about to release its end of a load.

It begins there.

Beneath the northern slab, something asks for another to hold.

An inhabitant braces its middle hands against the floor.

The request comes again, this time from the damaged support.

Eventually a visitor places its palm against the split. It feels the two parts of the stone, the burden above them, and the curious absence of movement between.

It returns with others.

***

The repair takes six days.

The power could finish it between two drops of water. But the first wedge brought to the chamber is placed somewhere it would not have chosen, and the reason becomes apparent only later: an opening must remain behind the support for a seasonal route not presently in use.

After that, it follows their work more closely.

They bring fragments from a fallen lintel. They test them, reject most, turn one over for nearly an hour. When a stone is ready, they give the small signal used between carriers.

The power eases a little weight onto it.

Once, the signal to stop comes almost immediately.

It stops.

On the sixth day the support bears the slab without assistance. A passage remains behind it. Along its foot runs a shallow channel, no deeper than the thickness of a shell.

The old inhabitant touches the new stone with its remaining front hand.

The power releases the last of the weight.

This time, when it withdraws, the exchanges continue.

***

Its attention spreads through the inhabited districts.

It finds injuries, hunger, chambers where the water has become foul. In a closed hollow, three young have survived on the last dampness beneath a stone. It opens a thread of seepage from a nearby joint, then waits while an adult clears a way through from the other side.

There are things that need no long deliberation.

There are others it has misunderstood.

A pale inhabitant repeatedly leaves the sheltered basin into which visitors bring food. The power first takes this for difficulty finding a comfortable place. It cools one end of the basin and deepens the film at the other.

The inhabitant leaves again.

Only when it reaches an exposed seam does the power notice the distant contacts arriving there. The chamber is comfortable. It is also almost deaf.

They make another resting place beside the seam.

The power holds back the water while the inhabitants lay its edges. When they finish, it lets the flow return gradually, under the old one’s spread hands.

***

During the dry season, its help becomes less local.

There is water deep beneath the western terraces, enough to sustain many districts. Bringing all of it upward would drown the lower channels and empty springs elsewhere. The power follows the beds of rock until it understands the limits.

Then, at a gathering place, it offers a small new seep.

The inhabitants examine it for days.

Some drink. Some taste the deposits left where it dries. One brings a young creature close, then carries it away again. Several begin a basin. Others block the proposed outlet and lead the water toward a different stair.

The seep remains small until they have somewhere for more of it to go.

By the end of the season, there are two dependable routes between tracts that drought once separated. There are feeding ledges along them, and dry pockets above. The power has opened the source and softened a band of stone too hard for their tools. The turns, resting places, and narrow approaches are theirs.

Not everyone uses them.

The older paths remain.

***

In time, the inhabitants acquire a way to call for it.

This is not one signal heard everywhere. The stone will not carry such a thing, and they do not all use the same patterns.

In one quarter, they leave a particular arrangement of chips beside a spring. In another, an old inhabitant touches three joints in succession, then waits. Elsewhere the request travels from chamber to chamber until it reaches a place where the power has learned to listen.

There are refusals as well.

A proposed opening is packed with gravel.

The power leaves it closed.

Sometimes it is called merely to hold something while it is turned.

Sometimes a call leads to a failing body, and the work is delicate and long. The power can mend much that the inhabitants cannot. It learns to distinguish the stillness of relief from the stillness with which they endure an unwelcome touch.

Afterward, the healed do not always remain to finish the exchange. One leaves so quickly that its visitors must follow it through three passages before they can return the objects kept beside its bed.

The power does not require thanks.

***

From the ridges, you would see almost no difference.

The city is still low and pale beneath the travelling clouds. Rain crosses its distant neighborhoods. Roofs fall where nothing lives beneath them. A marsh spreads into a court whose drains have been abandoned.

Under the paving, fewer journeys end at a sealed passage. More young reach the feeding basins. The very old have begun visiting places they had ceased to expect to feel again.

In your shelter, the creature with the white chip pauses beside the groove.

It sets the stone down, tests the water, and turns it slightly.

Something beneath the floor offers to take the weight.

The creature keeps its hands where they are. The chip is small; it has already found the right position.

After a while, the offered pressure withdraws.

Water leaves the wall and enters the groove beside your foot. The creature watches it, then carries on with its work.
