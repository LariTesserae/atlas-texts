The power first makes room for itself beneath a washer.

Not a body. A place from which to attend.

For several days it learns very little. The concussion fills its attention. It keeps noticing the height, the heat, the apparent precariousness of everything alive. Only gradually does it begin to distinguish one vibration from another: the machinery, the answering machinery farther away, the slight interruption made by a jaw pressed against a fastening.

It waits until these are no longer all the same sound.

The animal passes the washer twice without stopping. On the third passage it scrapes something edible from the underside. The power moves aside.

From there it follows the animal’s journeys, without entering its mind. It sees where a foot is tested before weight is given to it. It sees the water offered to the young and the smaller amount swallowed on the way home. It watches the unused hollow become necessary.

When the animal sleeps, the power attends to the wall.

There is trouble in it, though not everywhere that looks damaged.

The loose flashing is sound enough for another hundred seasons. Its looseness shelters the birds. The dark seam that leaks beside the folded plate is not failing; two thicknesses of metal move differently there, and water finds the difference. The power leaves it alone.

Behind the attachment at the difficult turn, however, a fracture has begun.

It is too deep to show at the surface. Each concussion opens it by less than the width of a living cell, then shuts it again. The fracture is working toward a second one. When they meet, a length of rail will turn outward. Roots will hold it for a little while.

The power follows the load upward through the machinery.

There are things fastened to this rivet whose weight it would be unwise simply to remove. There are other chambers. Other warmths. Water moving in darkness, used more than once before it reaches the garden.

It takes considerable care.

Deep in the metal, beyond any root or foot, the broken faces begin to join. They join slowly enough that no heat reaches the felt. The strain passes into a wider thickness of the wall. Nothing on the ledge shifts. The polished place remains polished; the rail still carries messages.

The animal makes its accustomed turn, bringing its tail around first.

The power does not require it to notice.

For a while, that is all it does.

Then another storm rises.

The adult cleans the young and takes them upward. The smallest has grown heavier, but is not yet dependable on the exposed plates. Halfway across, it loosens one forefoot to scratch its face. The adult stops until the foot has settled again.

At the upper hollow, the young attach themselves to the backing. The adult rests briefly, then starts down for water.

Behind the hollow is a capped channel. Long ago, when the felt was new, moisture ran through it to somewhere that no longer exists. A little water still enters its upper end and evaporates against hot metal.

The power follows it to its source. It watches through the storm and the dry days afterward. It finds that this water has passed all the roots it can reach. Below the cap, it has nowhere to go.

The next time the family shelters above, a pinprick of darkness appears beside the hollow.

The adult investigates it at once. It presses its nose to the spot, withdraws, returns. For a long time there is nothing large enough to drink.

Then a bead forms.

The adult takes it. Another will not gather until much later.

There is no new basin, no running thread to wet the sleeping strip. Just a place where the wall offers a little water before the long journey becomes necessary.

In time a root finds it too.

The power lets the root come. When it begins to cover the opening, the animal bites it back. The clipped end puts out two finer branches. Between them the bead continues to swell.

It becomes one more small arrangement in the garden.

The young grow.

One crosses a bare plate without following the adult’s screw heads. Another discovers beetles beneath a rim the adult seldom visits. The smallest remains fond of hanging from the strip, even when its back nearly touches the floor of the hollow.

The power watches their attempts without moving their feet for them.

But it stays attentive to the things no foot can test in advance: the hidden fracture, the change beginning far above in an engine bearing, the possibility that the heat or the great supporting roar might one day cease.

It does not fasten the machinery into eternal motion. It goes looking through the immense structure.

There are shaded galleries farther inward. There are joints where moisture gathers, rough walls, chambers whose floors are solid. Most cannot be reached from the rivet. Some could be, if a sequence of sealed inspection slots were opened.

The power begins to learn which openings would admit a tolerable wind.

This work may take a long time. For now, it keeps it far from the sleeping animals.

Below, the storm clears.

The adult returns to the lower hollow and removes the damp felt. A bird crosses the empty chamber. The first dry fragments catch in the suspended veil, turning edgewise in the tremendous sound.

Later, after the young have drunk, the adult goes back to the folded seam.

There is still water there.

It drinks, pauses, and drinks again.

Beneath the washer, the power remains.
