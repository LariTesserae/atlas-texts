The power’s attention rested first on the woman’s shoe.

It did not mistake the cut leather for a custom, or the patience with which she stood for an absence of pain. It could see the care taken with the new hem, the good light at the doorway, the usefulness of the bowl’s broad rim. These things were real. So was the joint that hurt before she put her foot on the floor each morning.

For a while it watched her retrieve the beans.

The boy had delivered them to the correct house, but had put them into a vessel containing something else. This required a visit, an explanation, and some sorting. The woman came back with fewer beans than she had sent and a small piece of cheese. She was not entirely dissatisfied.

When she sat down again, the power made itself perceptible in the empty place beside the door.

She looked at it, then at the basket occupying half that place.

“Will that be in your way?”

“No.”

“What do you want?”

“To know whether you would like help with your foot.”

She drew it beneath her chair.

“What sort of help?”

The power explained. It could ease the inflammation and mend what had been damaged. It would not take anything from her, or from someone else. There would be no service afterward.

She listened with the expression she used when someone described an unfamiliar method of preserving food.

“Will I be able to walk on it?”

“Yes.”

“Today?”

“Yes.”

She held out her foot.

There was no light. The leather did not repair itself. After a moment she moved her toes, then put her sole against the paving. She leaned forward, rose a little, and sat back.

The power waited.

She stood fully. Her body still anticipated the pain; her shoulder lifted to spare a weight that no longer needed sparing. She took three steps into the court and three back.

At the doorway she began to cry, which annoyed her.

The power remained where it was until she had finished.

Then she said, “There are others.”

“Yes.”

“Do you know already?”

“Some things. I would rather they told me the rest.”

***

Word went through the town faster than an animal could have been led from gate to gate.

The first people came with troubles they could show. A finger that would not straighten. A cough that returned each winter. An eye through which the square had become a pale interruption of light.

Others waited until there was less company.

The power found a place where conversation could not be overheard. It asked before touching. It explained what it proposed to do. When a man arrived with his wife and answered every question addressed to her, it found something useful for him to fetch.

Not everyone wanted help. One old woman said she had been examined enough in her life. The power told her where it would be if she changed her mind, and did not send anyone to persuade her.

It also attended to the animals. The mule favoring its foot had a deeper injury than its owner knew. The old dog’s briskness beyond the wooden shovel had concealed considerable pain.

By the second evening, the woman with the beans had taken to sitting nearby, not as a guardian or interpreter, but because people wanted to speak to someone who had gone first.

She had sewn her shoe closed. The seam was clumsy. She intended to do it properly later.

***

The cistern keeper brought the power beneath the stone hood.

He talked at length about the windlass. The power listened to the whole account, including the difficulties of obtaining a suitable length of timber, before asking what happened when the water grew low.

He stopped talking about the windlass.

Together they followed the water beyond what his eyes could follow: through the dark joints, into the mountain, down to the narrow places where it gathered. The power showed him enough to understand the supply without making him pretend to understand everything.

There was a way to keep the cistern filled without emptying a spring farther down the slope. They went to that spring as well, and spoke with the people who used it.

The work took less time than the discussions.

Afterward, water entered beneath the cistern’s surface at a steady rate. The power left a means of reducing or closing the flow, and the keeper practiced with it until his hands knew what to do. The drains remained drains. The fern kept its shaded seam. No meadow appeared on the roofs.

The keeper complained that everyone would now think his work easy.

“Will it be easier?” the woman asked.

“Yes.”

“Then complain about something else.”

***

The girl at the grinding stone came with a question about her brother.

With his permission, the power brought them into conversation. He was sitting at a wooden table when she saw him, older about the mouth than she remembered. Behind him someone was frying onions.

For several moments she forgot every question she had prepared.

Later she asked about travel. She wanted to leave, but not to vanish from the lives of people who might need her. The power helped her arrange a journey with companions she could trust, a place to stay, work that would pay what was promised, and a way to return.

Her mother objected to some of these arrangements and improved two of them.

The girl chose to go after the next market.

***

There were requests the power could not grant together.

The sisters each wanted sole possession of the storage vault. The loud man wanted his wife to agree that he had never raised his voice without cause. Someone asked that a son lose his desire to leave.

The power did not settle these matters by reaching into anyone’s mind.

It could make another vault, if another vault was wanted. It could provide a quiet room in which an answer would be heard. It could help a frightened parent remain in touch with a child. Sometimes its offers were accepted. Sometimes the person asking went away angry.

It stayed available afterward.

Nor did it make the town safe by closing the mule path or taking the children’s climbing places away. It secured an unseen fracture beneath the outward landing. It strengthened a failing roof timber after showing the household where the rot had entered. It left the small risks people could see and choose, and relieved them of several large ones they had never had a fair chance to judge.

***

Before long, there were afternoons when no one needed its attention urgently.

The power did not invent an occupation for itself.

It sat with the woman in the doorway. She had gone down to the lower fields that morning for the first time in several years, and was describing a boundary wall that had been badly repaired. Her legs were tired. She appeared to find this worth mentioning.

The boy approached while she spoke.

Without looking around, she lifted the bowl.

“You can’t still do that,” he said.

“Why not?”

He had no clear account of why her recovery ought to have made her easier to steal from.

She gave him a handful of beans.

“This time,” she said, “wait until someone takes them.”

He went up the stair. At the turn he called back to ask whether he might stay and play afterward.

“Yes,” she said.

Then, remembering that she could, she rose and carried the empty pods to the oven herself.
