The power does not begin by entering.

For a long time its attention rests on the lake outside, where the alder’s roots divide the clay. It follows the weight of the water, the loosening wood, the slow descent of silt. It learns which pressures the hollow bears and which it uses. A knock that tightens all the scattered pads is not necessarily an injury. Darkness is not necessarily a lack.

When the animal moves nearer the entrance, the power does not put anything into the space it has made.

It waits.

Under the bark, the unoccupied place remains unoccupied for several days. Then a transparent pad turns there, presenting its soft underside to the clay. The white animal draws its fourth pair of legs closer to its body.

The power’s attention moves away.

Not far. Only as far as the outer face of the root.

Inside, the pad settles.

This is the first thing the power learns that it could not have learned by looking more closely.

***

In late winter a branch breaks from the drowned alder.

It falls slowly through the lake, turning once. Small flakes leave its surface. Its heavier end comes down above the lowest root, where the blue clay has grown thin.

The power lets the branch fall.

But there is a stone beneath it, buried almost entirely in silt. Before the wood arrives, the stone turns by less than the width of its own weathered edge.

The branch rests across it.

There is still a knock. The animal grips its fibers; the farther joints draw tight. One flake of bark slips on the upper terrace and remains tilted. These are movements the hollow can bear.

The root does not split.

In the weeks afterward, sediment gathers behind the fallen branch rather than against the entrance. The slit remains narrow. Water passes it. The free end of the algae moves, and the still end stays still.

Nothing inside has been made stronger than it was.

***

The brown film returns to the fresh clay.

The white animal approaches, flattens itself, and backs away. Farther in, a pad releases its hold.

The power watches from outside.

It could remove the film. It could make the clay incapable of bearing it. But it has not yet learned what else would cease to grow on such clay.

A clear filament advances across the brown surface. Where it touches, the film gathers into minute beads. Some of these darken. Some become transparent. The animal waits beneath its bark.

The power leaves this work to the filament.

When the louse returns, it feeds beside the cleared place, not upon it.

***

There is one intervention within the hollow.

It happens while the hollow is empty.

Above the lake, thaw water has entered a crack in the bank. A little shelf of earth gives way. The disturbance travels downward through roots and water, too slight to trouble most of what lives there.

At the slit in the blue clay, the algae thread snaps taut.

Inside, the white shape has no definite edge. All the farther pads are loose. The amber speck is long and fine within its pocket.

The power receives the disturbance at the mouth.

For an instant there is pressure against something that has no surface. The moss leans outward. A bead of clay separates from a filament and hangs beside it.

Then the first pair of legs becomes distinct.

Behind the bark, a new pad finds its wall.

Only when it has taken hold does the power allow the remaining pressure through, slowly, as a change no greater than the thickening of a root.

The clay bead settles.

The animal spends the next interval cleaning its antennae.

***

Afterward the power leaves something where it can be refused.

Not food. The hollow already has its ways of feeding.

Not light. A brightness placed in the wrong fold might take away a darkness that has work to do.

It leaves a grain on the moving end of the algae.

The grain is dull and rounded, with no dark inclusion. It has come from the lake bed nearby. There is nothing remarkable about it except that, if pressed from within, it yields a little without breaking.

For many days it remains outside.

Then it is beneath the splinter.

Pale structures turn it by fractions. Once it is brought back to the entrance. The power does not return it.

Later it disappears.

Whether it joins the row of accepted grains is not seen.

***

The alder continues to soften.

The power does not preserve every branch, nor keep every leaf from thinning. In time, one of the three terraces loosens. The bark facing falls inward and becomes a roof over something smaller. The splinter darkens at one end.

But the outer roots have room to settle without closing the slit. Where the bank bears heavily, the weight finds stone. Where fresh water is needed, a passage remains among the moss.

There is no wall around the hollow.

There is, instead, a little care taken with what reaches it.

For long intervals the power attends elsewhere. No light marks its departure. Nothing is required of the animal in its absence.

Once, on returning to the lake, it finds a sharp grain balanced on the still end of the algae. The grain has a dark inclusion.

It carries it away.

Beneath the bark, the white animal shifts. A second small depression has appeared beside the pits where its antennae rest. It may be the beginning of another socket, or merely a place where the clay has yielded.

The power does not lean nearer to determine which.

Inside, something is making room.
