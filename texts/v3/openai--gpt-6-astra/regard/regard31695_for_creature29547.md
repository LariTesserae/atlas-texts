The power came first as a weight on the floor.

Not a great weight. About as much as one of the clear beads gathered at the end of a filament. It rested near the southern rail, where the wet creak of making continued through the night.

For a long time it did not increase.

Below the country, clouds passed among the hanging roots. Water traveled down a pale rib, divided at a collar, and entered two channels whose sounds reached the surface at different times. The power followed neither channel to its end. It was capable of entering every chamber at once, but there were membranes here that closed when touched, and it had not yet learned what counted as touching.

At dawn, the animal with the short rear leg came along the rim.

It passed the place, stopped, and corrected the broad curve of its body. One foot rose.

The power removed its weight.

The animal waited.

When the weight returned, the animal set down its foot a little nearer.

That was all they did the first morning.

***

It would have been easy to finish the arches.

The power could see how little material was required, where the vessels would join, which hollow columns could bear the additional load. It could have opened the great bud without tearing a single fold. It could have given the bridge its other half.

But the channels within the unfinished arches carried something besides water. Their narrow ends approached and withdrew by distances too small for a visitor to see. Substances crossed the gap in the damp air. Joining the lips would have stopped this.

Within the great bud, folded surfaces moved against one another. Some movements took a day. Others had not yet completed themselves in the time since the power arrived.

And the bridge ended in living skin, entirely healed.

The power let its imagined continuations go.

***

By the time the rains changed their hour, it had learned to make a pressure that did not resemble a footfall.

A footfall spread through the country in a particular way. It announced weight arriving, tissue compressing, water displaced. The power’s pressure came from several directions at once and moved nothing out of its way.

The animals could distinguish it.

Some approached. Some lifted a foot and turned elsewhere. The one with the dark branching line often answered from a distance, without interrupting whatever else it was doing.

The short-legged animal was the first to remain.

It set a foot over the place where the pressure gathered. Inside the translucent lobes, the fine green threads shifted. A faint impulse traveled downward.

The power repeated it.

The animal pulled its foot away.

For six days it did not return.

On the seventh, when it rested its foot there again, the power did not repeat anything. It waited through a long series of changes, some so slight that they were scarcely pressures at all.

Then the animal put a second foot down beside the first.

The power began to learn the difference between an answer and an echo.

***

There were things it learned slowly because speed would have destroyed the distinction.

The ground beneath a settled animal was not simply giving nourishment. Nor was the animal simply giving something back. At times, the exchange traveled through structures that belonged wholly to neither of them while they were joined.

The power could not have separated the contributions without separating the surfaces.

It did not try.

There were other things it learned and kept to itself.

There were things it never learned. The animals’ visits to the dry depression continued. Once, the short-legged one paused on its way down and touched the place where the power rested.

The power accompanied it to the edge.

Below the bridge, the animals arranged themselves around their unoccupied space. The power stayed outside the arrangement. When a foot extended inward, it did not meet that foot with a hidden pressure.

Afterward, the short-legged animal stood beside it for a while.

Nothing was explained.

***

The first help it gave was small enough to be taken away.

Under an eastern overhang lived a young animal whose feet released the ground at different speeds. This was not unusual in the very small. Usually the difference diminished as their legs lengthened.

In this one it persisted.

It could cross a level surface readily enough. On a wall, however, the last lobe of one foot sometimes remained attached after the others had begun a new movement. The body would turn around the lingering contact. An adult would put up a leg, and the little animal would descend along it.

It climbed again.

The power watched this happen many times before doing anything.

Then, on a low stretch of wall, it offered a small support beneath the young animal’s body: no gripping, no lifting, merely a surface against which weight could be set.

The animal recoiled. All five feet flattened against the membrane.

The support disappeared.

For several days the power stayed away from the overhang.

When it returned, it placed the support near the floor, where the young animal could reach it without climbing. The thing was visible only because the nap bent around its edge. It was narrow, warm, and firm enough to press against.

The young animal tested it with a folded foot.

An adult tested it too.

The power let the support yield under a sideways push. Under a downward push, it held.

That afternoon the young animal stood on it with three feet and attached the other two to the wall.

Nothing more happened.

The following day it used the support to turn itself around.

***

The support did not become a stairway.

There were mornings when the young animal climbed without it, and mornings when it pushed it aside. Once it carried a clear thread from the bank and spent a long time trying to lay the thread across the place where the support ought to have had an edge.

The power remained available.

Eventually the adult discovered that a particular touch against the wall could bring the support nearer. A different touch made it withdraw.

This was not the signal the power would have chosen. The distinction depended partly on a tremor transmitted through the adult’s other feet. It learned.

Other animals came to the overhang. Most lost interest. One used the support to rest a leg while its underside remained joined to the bank. The animal with the dark line pressed against it for nearly an hour, then walked to the western vault and did not return for several weeks.

The country itself responded beneath it, thickening a little where pressures had become unfamiliar.

The power eased the load.

Over time, a shallow fold developed in the wall. The young animal used that instead.

The power withdrew the support. No hollow was left behind.

***

It did not make the country silent.

There were passages in the great rushing sound that the animals attended to with their whole bodies. A chamber filling under the western flats could be felt at the southern rail. The dry booming of a ridge coincided with changes far below the hanging roots.

But within one quiet alcove, a loose scale had begun to strike its neighbor. The sound was faint, irregular, and close to the moist clicks exchanged there.

An adult interrupted its sequence whenever the scale struck.

The power held the scale still.

The adult stopped.

The power released it.

Later, a foot touched the wall below the scale, then touched it again.

The power steadied it for the remainder of that gathering.

When the animals left before the rain, it let go.

This arrangement continued until the living wall drew the scale into a new position. No one needed to ask again.

***

Years passed without the bud opening.

The power did not count those years against it.

It learned where visitors could stand without troubling a feeding place. When someone approached the tender rim too quickly, it sometimes made a hollow warning beneath the next step. It never made the rail look stronger than it was.

It acquired no hall. There was no image of it on the flats.

Sometimes it was absent from the surface for a season. Sometimes an animal would come a long way to the southern rail and find its small, unmistakable weight waiting there.

Their contacts could last a few breaths.

One morning the young animal, now taller, approached on five long legs. It no longer needed the fold under the eastern overhang, though it still went there. It placed a foot beside the power and made a sequence the power had not felt before.

The power waited.

The animal made it again, with a difference.

Far below, a cloud loosened from the roots. Sunlight entered the open spaces beneath the country. The rail continued its wet creaking.

The power answered carefully.

The animal kept its foot where it was.
