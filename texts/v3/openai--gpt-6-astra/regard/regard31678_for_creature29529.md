The first thing the power did was wait through a complete opening and closing of the western waterwork.

It could have looked through the walls. Instead it attended to what the keeper attended to: the intervals between clicks, the temperature beneath her forward hands, the slight resistance that made her stop before reaching farther into the blue light.

There was knowledge here that did not belong to the building.

The keeper knew, for example, how long those waiting outside would remain flat on the terrace before the smallest began to tremble. She knew which of her tools could be dropped and which must be caught even at some risk to the fingers. She knew that after a poor drawing she would be offered food by people who had eaten less than she had.

The power stayed until she went home.

Then it followed the water.

Much of the night passed in this way. Along one seam the amber growth was thick enough to lift in a single piece. Along another, the grazers arrived before anything had softened and waited with their mouths against the stone. A thread passed through open air where its supporting slab had weathered away. The power examined this crossing carefully. It was sound. Elsewhere, beneath an apparently solid terrace, a connection was failing.

By dawn it had learned that more water would help, and that simply making more water would not be enough.

There were places where an additional evening bead would feed several households. There were others where a steady flow would dissolve the mineral crust, take the amber films with it, and leave a little ravine. Some plants could tolerate wet roots for an hour and not for a night. The waterworks drew from places that had their own inhabitants.

The power did not want the basin’s relief to become an unexplained loss somewhere else.

It also learned that the keeper’s missing toes hurt when the building opened onto cloud.

This was an old pain. She no longer paused for it.

***

On the following evening, something answered her first touch.

She withdrew her hand.

After a long interval she touched the wall again.

The answer repeated her sequence, with the spacing preserved. It came through the stone at a strength that could not be mistaken for machinery, but was too gentle to be an alarm.

She went outside and fetched two people.

One thought they should leave. The other thought they should touch the wall with a tool rather than a hand. They discussed this on the terrace, where the power could feel their argument but did not enter it.

At last the keeper returned.

Learning to speak together took time. The power could distinguish all the movements of her limbs; it did not initially know which movements were words. The keeper could recognize intention in the replies; she could not tell how much intention, or what sort.

Their first useful exchange concerned a shallow cup.

At the power’s request, understood after several attempts, she placed it beneath the outlet. A bead formed. The power asked whether another was wanted.

She said yes.

A second bead formed.

She gave the sign for stopping.

The outlet dried.

She left the cup where it was until morning. When she returned, the quantity had not changed.

***

Trust did not arrive everywhere at once.

One household refused water from the western building for nine evenings. Another sent three empty pouches on the second night. Someone asked whether the new speaker owned the structures. Someone else wanted to know whether it had made them, and seemed more suspicious when it said no.

The keeper asked what had happened to the person who had approached the northern building from above.

The power examined the place where that passage had gone. It followed what remained of the connection farther than any listening instrument could have reached.

It found no one.

It explained the difference between finding no one and knowing that someone was dead. The explanation had to be given several times. A relative of the missing person came to hear it directly and asked the same questions in a different order.

There was no useful answer beyond the one already given.

The relative stayed until the wall had cooled.

***

The first repair was small enough to be watched.

Inside the western building, beyond the keeper’s sideways reach, two surfaces had been drawing closer together for many years. Their separation governed the cold weight that sometimes descended onto the terrace. Eventually they would have touched. The resulting opening would not have stopped at the doorway.

The keeper had suspected deterioration, although her account of its cause was different.

The power showed her a model made from three loose stones. She moved them herself. When the dangerous arrangement was approached, a thread laid between them tightened.

She asked whether the building could be stopped while the work was done.

It could.

She asked whether stopping it would empty the seams.

Some would dry before the repair was complete.

The work was postponed until pouches had been filled, neighboring outlets tested, and the households that harvested the farthest seams informed. This took four evenings and a dispute.

During the repair, the keeper stood outside with a hand against the wall. She felt each alteration. Two apprentices kept their hands beside hers.

Afterward, the blue light remained blue. The outlet still produced water slowly. There was a new place near the entrance where a firm pressure would close the remote opening before anything dangerous could enter.

The keeper tested it eleven times.

On the twelfth, she made an apprentice do it.

***

The power offered to attend to her foot.

She wanted to know whether she would still feel the building through it.

It said that this mattered, and asked her to show exactly where the useful sensations ended and the pain began.

She spent most of an evening answering. The distinction was less simple than either had expected. Some of what she called pain told her where she had put her weight. Some of what she had come to regard as pressure was pain she had ceased naming.

They proceeded a little at a time.

For several days afterward she stamped unexpectedly while walking. Asked why, she said she was checking something. She did not discuss it further until another keeper, whose forelimb had stiffened, approached her privately.

***

There were larger requests.

Could a dry waterwork be opened again? Could food be made to grow closer to the eastern dwellings? Could the dead speak through stone?

To the last question the power said no. It could repeat words that had been preserved. It would not make a speaker out of other people’s recollections and present that speaker as the one they had lost.

This disappointed several people and relieved one who said nothing.

For the histories, it offered something less complete.

A person could tell an account while holding the proper object, and the movements and words could be kept together. Later, another pair of hands could follow the motions and hear the account at its original pace. The teller could correct it, restrict it, or have it removed.

The first old historian to try this listened to her own account and declared it inaccurate.

She had left out a winter.

The power waited while she fetched another stone.

Soon there were arguments over which accounts should be kept. The power preserved disagreements as disagreements. It did not smooth two incompatible lists of households into a single plausible one.

Children were permitted to record stories as well. For a while, a disproportionately large part of the new collection concerned a grazer that had become trapped in a carrying cloth.

***

Work on the water took several seasons.

The basin did not become green. The inhabitants had not asked for that, and many things living there would not have survived it.

Some outlets were repaired. Others were left alone once the lives dependent on their irregularities had been understood. A reserve was established in stone beneath an already barren terrace, with a measure anyone could read by touch. Drawing from it made a visible change at its entrance. No household could quietly take an evening’s water and leave the shortage for someone farther down.

Deciding who might draw, and when, remained difficult.

The power supplied measures, demonstrations, and patient answers. It declined an invitation to become the sole judge of gathering rights. Several people were annoyed by this. One had prepared an exceptionally good case.

There were mistakes. An increased flow brought an unfamiliar pale growth to a shaded seam. The keeper noticed it before the grazers did. That outlet was closed, the change reversed, and the experiment recorded where the successful repairs were recorded.

Eventually fewer people woke thirsty.

The difference was visible first in their hands.

***

Much remained ordinary.

Predators continued to wait in the hollows. Meat was still traded between settlements. The wooden bowl required another repair, done by its owner with considerable advice from people who had never worked wood.

The power could be called at any of the tended buildings. Each had a quiet place on its outer wall where a hand could rest and receive an answer. It did not require a keeper to make an introduction.

Sometimes the requests were urgent. Sometimes someone wanted to ask about trees.

The keeper still went to the western gully in the evening. She carried fewer tools now, but retained the newest strip of shed skin, which she continued to distrust.

One evening she found a child waiting beside the outlet with a little cup.

The child had asked for water and been told to fetch someone who knew how much the seam could spare.

The keeper examined the dark line beneath the stone, touched the cup, and gave permission.

They watched it fill together.

When it held enough, the child pressed the stopping place.

The water stopped.
