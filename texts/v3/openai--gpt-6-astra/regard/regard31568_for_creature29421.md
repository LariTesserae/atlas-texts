The power first notices the place where the animal does not fit.

This is not yet a reason to change anything.

The animal has returned from the leaf with its chambers full of unfamiliar temperatures. It rests against the warm lip, then lifts itself and turns. One saddle catches briefly on the ceramic. The animal draws a foot beneath its body, leans, and passes the obstruction.

It settles across the unnecessary extension.

The power waits.

It has the means to make a basin that would receive every contour of the body. It could keep the floor at the temperature of perfect rest. It could bring the nourishing darkness to the animal’s mouth without the long attention beside a seam.

For several afternoons it watches the animal leave its bed, decline food, and follow the light.

***

There are things the power cannot learn merely by becoming larger.

When it attends to the leaf as a country, it loses the little hollow. When it attends to the hollow, the distances among the leaf’s veins fold away. It could force both scales to remain open. Nothing here would be strong enough to prevent it.

Instead, it learns to look back and forth.

Once, in the country, it finds an exposed slope with a pale edge. The edge moves. Beyond it, under vegetation that is either immense or very small, something gives three slow flashes of olive light.

The power does not reach toward it.

When the animal returns, it has a narrow line of dark material along one foot. It rests that foot against the warm lip until the line fades.

The next day it goes back.

***

Over time, the basin’s high side grows higher.

The black plant adjusts around it. A stem takes on a shallow bend. The animal changes the way it enters, approaching from the empty floor rather than from the slit. For a while this is sufficient.

Then the new saddle begins to form.

It is amber, almost clear. The animal holds that part of itself away from the ceramic even in sleep. Twice it unfolds its rear feet, moves a little, and folds them again. The second time, it leaves the basin and rests in the open.

The light does not reach its face there.

The power attends to the mineral beneath the abandoned bed.

Down in the mountain, a turning inclusion approaches the end of its seasonal revolution. Its socket admits the movement. Along the socket’s inner wall, fresh material grows where pressure has ceased.

There is already a way, here, for stone to give room.

The power brings that way a little nearer the surface.

It changes neither the basin’s temperature nor its outline. It does not lower the lip. At one place beside the entrance, smaller than the sole of a foot, it allows the ceramic to remain undecided a little longer.

Then it withdraws its attention far enough that the animal’s dull facial patches no longer turn toward the same patch of empty air.

***

The animal discovers the change late the following afternoon.

It has been feeding without taking anything. When it returns beneath the black leaves, its front foot rests on the unfinished place.

The floor yields.

Very little: less than the thickness of the new saddle.

The animal closes itself.

For a long time, the pale object remains beside the basin. The mountain completes a stroke. The red stems incline and recover.

Eventually a head appears.

One facial patch turns toward the yielding ceramic. A foot follows. The animal presses, releases, and presses again.

Under the pressure, a shallow mark forms.

It puts a second foot into the mark.

By evening, there is a low place in the lip. It is not centered on the entrance. The animal has made it at an angle that requires a small turn halfway through. Once inside, it lies with its newest saddle above the hollow it has pressed into the floor.

Its head remains against the older, warmer stone.

The power leaves the change in place.

***

Nothing else is corrected.

The long route around the red plant remains available, although the animal seldom takes it now. The seams open at their own intervals. The bright strip crosses the floor and has to be followed.

The basin’s unnecessary extension survives. On some afternoons the animal still sleeps across it, half outside the shelter it has altered.

The power keeps no body at the mouth of the hollow. It does not fill the ordinary light with evidence of its presence. When it attends, it does so from the side, where there is already space.

Much later, the animal makes a second depression in the yielding ceramic.

This one is nowhere near the entrance. It presses with its face, then with the broad groove of a forefoot. The work takes several visits. Each time, it leaves before anything recognizable has formed.

At last there is a little sloping surface beside the bed.

The animal rests its head there.

The angle places both dull patches in the afternoon light while keeping the new saddle beneath the black leaves.

This had not occurred to the power.

It watches until the light has passed.

Under the plant, the saddles meet with a tiny click. The roots fall silent. Warmth spreads through the irregular basin, through the old extension and the newly lowered entrance, and into the small place the animal has made for its face.

The power learns the shape of it.
