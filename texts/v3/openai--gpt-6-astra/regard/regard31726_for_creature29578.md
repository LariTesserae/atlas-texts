The power first attends to the foot that is farthest away.

It would be easy to draw it nearer. The body in the crack would become a body of ordinary length, and the distances within the mountain would agree with those outside.

But the foot is resting somewhere.

Around its claws, in a warmth that has never reached the outer hollow, small animals are feeding. Their young occupy the pits in an old mineral grain. To draw the foot nearer would be to move their weather.

The power leaves the distances alone.

For several pulses it does nothing that can be distinguished from watching.

It follows a flake from the mouth of a nursery worker into a blocked channel, then downstream, then into the fold of a grazer where it lodges against a stalk. It attends to the stalk as closely as to the grazer. When the fan opens, it follows what enters.

There is no smallest life at which its attention gives out.

Nor does familiarity make the hollow simpler. An animal that crowds a newcomer away from the nursery later abandons a favorable current to clear water over a capsule. The capsule contains neither its own kind nor anything that will remain harmless to it. A predator closes over a grazer, and under the predator’s lifting rim a population escapes that would otherwise have gone inward on the grazer’s back.

The power does not mistake this for an arrangement in which nothing is lost.

It stays with what is caught, too.

***

On the fourth morning, the outer flake begins to loosen.

The change is too small to interrupt the sunlight. A white grain parts inside the stone. The weight above it settles onto a narrower attachment.

Beneath the nursery, a channel closes.

The workers discover this before the water stops. Three arrive with flakes. One braces its mouth against the ledge and pushes so hard that the translucent plates along its body cease moving.

The obstruction is not in the channel. It is the channel.

Farther inward, the creature spanning the crack draws another foot toward itself. Water gathers under its belly. Along the summit’s exposed face, frost left by the night melts into a seam that has not previously admitted water.

By the next morning the flake will fall.

The power follows that falling.

Some inhabitants would return through the hottest beads. Some would pass into the distances opening in the broken stone. Many would not. The nursery ledge would strike an outward ridge before reaching the clouds.

Holding the flake forever would be easy.

But the mountain is still moving. The hot seam is moving with it. Held in precisely this shape, the hollow would eventually become a dry pocket above water it could no longer reach.

The power looks for what can be supported without being kept still.

***

Behind the nursery there is a blind depression.

A worker sometimes probes it when the neighboring channels clog. Its splinter enters a short way, turns, and comes out carrying a few grains of pale salt.

This time, one more grain comes out.

Water darkens the depression.

The worker touches it with its mouth. Its plates spread, close, spread again. It leaves and returns with a thinner tool.

Beyond the depression, the power has brought two surfaces almost together. Between them is room for water, then for a mouth, then—where the stone widens—for an animal carrying a flake crosswise.

The passage leads behind the failing attachment, into stone that will remain.

It is not uniformly warm. Near its entrance, a shallow groove takes cooler water from the outer lip. Farther in, the bronze discoloration begins to form. There are roofs low enough to exclude a crowded grazer and shelves broad enough to receive one.

The worker does not go through.

It clears the entrance, drops its flake, and returns to the capsules.

For a long time no animal uses the passage at all.

The power waits.

***

The first to enter is a fan-bearing creature without a host.

It has come loose in the disturbance around the clogged channel. Turning slowly, it catches its stalk in the fresh groove.

Its fan opens.

A grazer follows the new wet edge. At the narrowest place its passengers catch against the roof, and it retreats. Later it approaches from the side and finds the broader shelf.

The workers begin carrying loose capsules. Attached capsules remain where they are.

The power takes their weight from the stone without taking away their attachment. A thin scale lifts beneath each one. The workers can get their tools under these scales. They tilt them into the cooler flow, and the current carries them a little way.

Some lodge badly. Some require moving twice.

One worker stands over an empty capsule for most of a pulse before taking up its flake again.

***

When the outer stone finally gives, it does not fall all at once.

Its upper edge tilts into the wind. The remaining attachment stretches beyond what this kind of rock can bear, allowing the lower edge to rest briefly against the new entrance.

For that interval, the old floor and the new floor touch.

Bodies cross. Passengers cross between bodies. A flat predator spends nearly the whole interval lifting and replacing its hooks before drawing itself onto the underside of the remaining stone.

The creature in the crack does not cross.

Its distant feet hold. As the flake turns away, its body lengthens into the widening space. For a moment, morning is visible between all its joints.

Then the stone falls, and the creature goes somewhere the fall does not.

The power follows it long enough to know it has arrived.

***

The new opening receives less sunlight.

What enters reaches the nursery late, passing through a hanging drop and laying a trembling amber mark across the grit.

The grazers find the fresh pasture. Tools accumulate briefly in a bend, then disperse. Beneath a shelf, the predator settles around an unfamiliar ridge.

There are losses. There are empty stalks.

The power remains through the first cold night and the next hot pulse. Where the new roof would shed water into the wind, it leaves a roughness no larger than a grain. A bead catches there, swells, and bends inward.

Below it, an animal raises its front limbs.

The water reaches them.
