The change is not the beginning of a fracture.

The old carrier keeps her wrist against the rock. She has two fingers on that hand. Between their stumps, an old bite has drawn the skin into a hard seam.

Below her wrist, something gives three slow knocks.

She lifts her arm.

The others move at once, misunderstanding. A youngster scrambles onto the root-holder’s back. Two adults gather their hammers. The old carrier strikes the ground sharply with hers.

Nothing follows.

She waits until they have settled, then touches the rock again.

Three knocks.

Far below the roots, the power that has come to this country becomes still.

It had meant to announce itself. Already it has learned something.

***

At first it attends to the old carrier because she is the one touching it.

There is swelling inside her cheek. She has been eating with the other side of her mouth for several days. Her companions have tried to help. One still smells of her blood; another avoids the reach of her good hand.

The power could remove the swelling, the parasite, the memory of pain. These would be different acts. It considers them separately.

The parasite is anchored by a ring of muscular hooks. It has no wish beyond holding, feeding, and eventually releasing its young into water. The carrier’s cheek is the only country it has known.

The power loosens the hooks.

The old carrier jerks her head. Her tongue reaches something it could not reach before. She coughs, works her jaw, and spits a red button onto a leaf.

Immediately she tries to eat it.

The power moves it beneath the leaf.

She searches for some time, annoyed.

By then the parasite is in a shallow warm pocket of mineral water, where a membrane supplies what it formerly took from her. Whether it can live there, whether its young can live otherwise, will require attention. The power gives that attention.

In the forest, the old carrier touches her cheek with the back of her wrist.

Then she takes the nodule away from the root-holder and begins to eat.

***

The power does not mistake abundance for ease.

It follows water through the country. Water climbs inside roots, waits behind rind, passes through a browser’s enormous dark gut. It lies in the cups of leaves above animals that are losing strength beneath them. It spills during quarrels and vanishes into six-legged bowls.

A great rain would fill the cracks. In some of those cracks it would meet stone hot enough to turn it violently into steam.

Cooling the whole country would kill things whose lives depend upon the warmth. Holding the ground motionless would store its pressures elsewhere.

These are reasons to look more closely.

Beneath a ridge, the power finds a seam through which clean vapor rises and is lost into the canopy. It lengthens the seam sideways. It cools a handspan of stone.

At the coolest hour, beads gather there.

The first visitor is a beetle. The second is an animal so small that a bead becomes a wall against its face. The power roughens the stone.

By the time the carriers find it, there is a narrow wet ledge with many approaches. No pool waits beneath it. Excess water travels through fine grooves into roots downslope.

The old carrier tastes one bead, retreats, and returns.

For three mornings she drinks alone. On the fourth, someone follows her.

There is a fight.

The power had expected this possibility. Expecting it does not make the bitten youngster’s pain less instructive.

Over the following nights, other cool surfaces form along the ridge, far enough apart that one animal cannot defend them all. Some are beneath overhangs too low for a hunter to reach under comfortably. Some lie beside branches that can bear a feeding group.

The carriers still quarrel. But a defeated animal can go somewhere else.

The power watches who cannot.

***

It also listens beneath the rock.

The carriers already know much that it did not know on arrival. They distinguish the settling of a shelf from the first strain of a deep fracture. They hear through the bare centers of their wrists. They test a tree through its bark. Their knowledge is exact, local, and sometimes fatally overtaken by events.

The power follows each tremor outward and backward, learning what they feel before they run.

When a sleeping tree’s deepest roots begin to cook, it brings that familiar warning into the trunk.

Not a new language. Not three mysterious knocks.

The old carrier wakes with her wrist against the fork.

She presses harder. Beside her, a youngster grinds its teeth in protest as she climbs over its belly.

At the foot of the tree, she smells the roots. Nothing. She climbs again, touches the trunk, descends.

The others resist leaving. The sleeping forks are good. A heavy adult bares his mouth at her.

Then he feels it too.

They spend the remaining dark crowded into an inferior tree. Several times, the heavy adult starts back toward the old one.

At the warmest hour, its roots release.

The crown falls slowly at first, leaning into neighboring crowns. Then the branches break.

From their cramped fork, the carriers watch a volume of frightened light open through the forest.

The power does not require them to know whom to thank.

It continues learning how much warning is useful. Too little, and the young cannot climb. Too much, and the animals return before the danger comes.

***

There are failures.

A safe ledge becomes a hunting place. The power alters the approaches, and the hunter moves to another route. Hunger moves with it.

A new source of water encourages growth that closes an escape gap. The power notices late. It catches the falling carrier, but the animal’s shoulder is injured. For weeks it cannot brace a root.

The power stays through those weeks.

It does not offer the rescued body as proof that its work was good. It follows the consequences into the neighboring basin, into nests, mouths, and the narrow chambers where eggs wait for wetness.

Some changes it undoes.

Others it makes more carefully.

Where prey can escape more often, it attends also to the hunters. Where it prevents a death, it watches the lives that would have fed upon the body. There is no single adjustment after which the country can be forgotten.

Fortunately, the power has not come to finish quickly.

***

The old carrier has been carrying a second hammer.

Its owner died before the power arrived. The stone is shorter than her own, with a thumb hollow that does not fit her thumb. At each feeding place she sets it beside her foot. When the group leaves, she gathers it against her chest.

The power can see the difficulty this causes.

It does not make her let go.

One day a youngster takes the stone while she is feeding. She seizes his arm so fiercely that he drops it. Later, when he is asleep, she puts it down beside him.

At the next departure she takes it back.

This continues for some time.

There is no intervention.

***

What the power makes, over the long passage of upheavals, is more room.

Room to lose a feeding place without losing the day’s water. Room to abandon a familiar tree and find another before exhaustion. A few more routes across each ridge. A few more places where an injured animal can rest without having to defend its body every moment.

It tends the warning paths under the stone. It learns the diseases of crowded sleepers. It keeps working on the small red creatures in their warm mineral hollows.

Above it, the forest remains loud.

The carriers strike their roots and steal from one another. They learn new drinking places. They forget some old ones. A youngster discovers that shell held at an angle can guide a leaking thread of fluid into another mouth, and for several days everyone nearby tries to take his shell.

The old carrier lives long enough to become bad at climbing.

By then there is a sleeping fork she can reach by a low, rough branch. She goes there early. The others arrive smelling of bruised roots, pressing around her, shoving her toward the middle.

Her own hammer rests against her belly.

The shorter one is elsewhere. She sometimes reaches for it on waking, then puts her hand down.

Beneath the tree, the young ground shifts.

A wrist touches bark. Another joins it.

There is time to wake the sleepers.
