The power first attends to the foot that has not yet found a place to rest.

It belongs to the younger animal. Five of its feet are fastened to the splinter; the sixth opens and closes beneath it. Whenever the larger animal steps over a ridge, this foot touches the floor.

Ahead, the damp depression is shifting. Its near rim draws inward while the far rim unfolds toward the center. The larger animal stops. It sets one foreleg against the wall.

There is water enough for both of them. There may not be a way to reach it.

The power could hold the root still.

It follows that possibility outward: through vessels, into finer roots, up through the trunk to leaves whose undersides are turning toward rain. Stillness here would be an obstruction elsewhere. The plant is not merely a wall around the animals. The power attends to it, too.

Instead, it holds a distance.

Between the animal’s forefoot and the wet rim, the floor remains exactly as long as the floor.

The root continues growing. Thunder passes through the hollow. A strand breaks overhead, and the younger animal draws all six legs tight against the splinter.

But the next step arrives where it should.

The larger animal lowers the worn end of the fiber across the pool. Its weight dimples the living skin. Water rises.

For some time the smaller one does not understand where to put its mouth. It presses against the dry side, then against a joint of the larger animal’s leg. The larger animal turns the splinter a little.

The power waits.

The small mouth finds the water.

***

While they drink, the power examines the refuge.

It finds the curved husk, the fibers, the old covering of a foreleg. It finds the narrow recess maintained by these things and the fresh pressure gathering behind them. Soon the root will push the husk through the soft wall. The recess will become narrower. The animals will wake and brace it again, if there is room to turn.

The power does not remove their work.

Behind the wedged fragments, it gives the root a fold.

There are folds elsewhere in the plant: accommodations around stones, slack places beneath bark, tissue that can lengthen without tearing. This one is small and deep. As the root thickens, the fold will open toward the hollow. The bed will keep its space.

The old pieces remain where the animal put them. They still bear weight.

Near the back of the bed, the power brings a vessel close to the surface. Not so close that it leaks. There is already little enough water traveling to the leaves.

Here, however, the air will cool slightly before it leaves through the lower split. Some of its moisture will collect on the vessel’s thin covering. A drinking place will form, shallow and narrow, with a dry lip beside it. The animals can use it without sleeping in dampness.

Then the power follows their route.

There is no path worn into the root, but there is a path in the animal’s movements: here a pause, here a lifted leg, here a backward turn made necessary by the length of the splinter. The power follows every part of it. Where the animal must cross a smooth expanse, it raises a low seam. Where a growing edge might close upon a foot, it lets the tissue soften before it folds.

Along this route, distances will stay attached to surfaces.

The center retains its depth. Light still arrives before rain. Far beyond the knot, a falling fiber takes a long time to land.

None of that now lies between the animals and what they need.

***

On the return journey, the larger animal stops at the place where the other oval disappeared.

It touches the floor.

The power has felt the shape of that absence already. It searches beneath the transparent skin, through the layers added since the loss. What was there has passed into the root. There is no sleeping creature to uncover.

For a while, the power remains with the outstretched leg.

It does not put a movement under the floor. It does not make the light resemble a dark comma.

The animal tests the place once more, then continues, carrying the living young beneath its fiber.

At the refuge it discovers the new drinking place.

Its forelegs examine the lip, the wall above it, the crease behind. It retreats. Comes forward. Sets down the splinter and immediately picks it up again.

The younger animal stays attached.

Eventually the larger one reaches into the recess with the wet end. A bead gathers there.

It drinks, although it has just drunk.

Then it settles into the bed. The fold opens by less than the thickness of its back, making room for the legs it cannot quite arrange around the younger body.

For the first time, neither animal is pressed against the ceiling.

***

The power stays through several changes of light.

It learns that a quiet root is not necessarily a comfortable one. During an interval without thunder, the larger animal repeatedly lifts its head and touches the wall. When growth resumes, it folds its forelegs and sleeps.

So the sounds remain.

The power learns that the splinter is more than an inconvenient length of fiber. It is carried even to places where water can be taken without it. When the younger animal wanders along the dry lip of the new pool, the larger one lays the splinter across its route. The younger one catches hold.

So the splinter remains.

Nearby, where it will not be swallowed by healing tissue, the power leaves a shorter piece of the same dead fiber. One end is rough enough for little feet. The other can draw up water.

It leaves several such pieces farther along the margin.

There may be other young. There may be a broken tool. The hollow may grow beyond anything these two can presently cross.

The changes the power has made will grow with it.

At last the younger animal comes off the splinter by itself.

It puts two feet on the floor, then a third. Its body hangs for a moment between what carries it and what will bear its weight.

The larger animal is asleep.

The power holds nothing still.

A drop gathers beside the dry lip. The root creaks. Under the small, searching foot, the distance is only a distance.

The foot comes down.
