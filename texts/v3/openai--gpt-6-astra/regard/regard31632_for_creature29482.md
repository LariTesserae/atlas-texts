The power’s attention rested first on the piece of sweet she had put back.

It could have measured the mountain without touching it. It could have followed every cable to its end, counted the closed chambers, distinguished the slow expenditure of stored force from the motions that returned something useful to the system.

But the keeper had chosen a larger piece after saying he should not have bought any. Her replacement had looked away while she did it.

The power watched long enough to understand that there was no contradiction requiring correction.

Then it came to the door.

***

It came in a shape that could use the spare chair.

The keeper had already put on her coat. Seeing another visitor, she let one arm hang loose in its sleeve.

“Is someone coming behind you?”

“No.”

“There’s a cup in the cupboard.”

“I don’t need a drink.”

She looked at it more carefully.

“Then sit out of the way of the desk.”

It sat.

Her replacement folded the cloth around the remaining sweet. The keeper worked her hand through the cuff, caught her split thumbnail, and stopped with her eyes closed.

“May I help with that?” the power asked.

She held out her hand, suspiciously, as though it had offered to remove a splinter she had long ago stopped trying to find.

The nail became whole.

There was no warmth or light. The two edges simply ceased catching against each other. Beneath them, the tender line in the nail bed settled.

She rubbed it with the neighboring finger.

“Will that last?”

“Yes.”

The replacement came closer. He examined her thumb without taking hold of it.

After a while she asked, “Can you do bearings?”

***

They did not begin with the bearings.

The keeper insisted on this, although she had asked the question. A bearing could be worn in a way that compensated for something farther down the line. There were adjustments whose reasons had disappeared into the history of other adjustments.

The power agreed.

For three watches it made no changes to the machinery.

It went below the winch house, into places where there was no room for a body and no air intended to be breathed. It followed the seven bundles through their division. It examined the clamps from beneath. It found the source of the open hook in the ledger.

Then it returned and asked for somewhere to put a drawing.

The keeper cleared the desk except for the ledger.

“Not over that.”

The drawing showed the junction at a scale her hands could understand. Beside it was a view from farther away, and beside that another. The names were the names she used, with blank spaces where she had none.

She stood over it for a long time.

“That part moves?”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

The power showed her.

She called her replacement back from the cupboard.

The small change in tension had come from a seat that had begun to creep. It would not fail during her watch, or the next. There was time to discuss it. There was also a way to take its load without transferring the danger elsewhere.

The power explained that way.

“What holds your support?” she asked.

The explanation took longer.

She interrupted it repeatedly. Once she fetched a fitter. Once the fitter fetched somebody with whom she was not on speaking terms.

The repair was made after all three had slept.

For the first time in longer than the records covered, the oldest cable rested. Not visibly. At the desk the needle moved far enough that the keeper reached toward the instrument before remembering.

Below the platform, a damaged seat was lifted free.

The power left it on the floor for them to examine.

***

Word traveled along the routes people could manage.

Someone who could mend a nail had arrived at the winches. Someone had stopped a cable. Someone had offered to stop all the cables, which was untrue.

By the time the keeper took the eastern passage home, there were six people waiting along it.

One wanted help with a valve.

One had brought a child whose sleep was broken by the effort of breathing.

One wished to know whether the power could recover writing from a sheet that had been washed and used again.

The others had come to see.

The power did not ask the mother of the child to wait while it considered the city as a whole. It knelt in the passage and spoke to them both. The child wanted to know whether it would hurt, and whether he would still have to wear the cloth over his mouth.

It answered before touching him.

Later, there were consultations in a room near the weighing platform. People came with lungs, joints, eyes, old injuries, questions about changes they had been born with. Some wanted very little altered. Some wanted to be able to cross the freight yard without stopping. One woman wanted to sing as loudly as her sister remembered.

The power learned what each request meant in the body that made it.

There were people who did not come.

It did not visit them uninvited.

***

The air required more thought.

What eased a human throat might soften a deposit around a seal. What made a room comfortable might ruin a food bed. The jointlings inhabited conditions nobody had ever needed to describe precisely.

The power could describe them. That did not make them disposable.

It began with the room near the weighing platform.

Inside, the air was a little denser and held enough moisture that a cup could stand uncovered. There was a vestibule with two doors, both easy to open. The fittings were familiar. Nothing essential depended on a component the city could neither make nor understand.

The chamber attendants inspected it.

They argued about the drains.

The drains were changed.

People were invited to sit there, and to leave whenever they liked.

The keeper went after her watch. She brought a cuff to mend because she disliked traveling somewhere only to sit.

For a while she worked. Then she put down the needle.

Her breathing had become so unobtrusive that she kept attending to it, as one attends to machinery after an unfamiliar sound has ceased.

She stayed longer than she intended.

On the way out, she asked whether such a room could be made around her mother’s seat.

“Yes,” said the power. “Though the doorway would need changing.”

“Show me before you do it.”

***

There were larger offers.

The power laid them out where people could inspect them without having to answer at once.

Reliable sources of energy that did not require the mountain to spend the last of its stored differences. Water in quantities that made several people angry before they believed the figures. A sheltered route between the inhabited districts. Help opening the records, with uncertain readings marked as uncertain.

There were other places to live, too.

It showed those places without making the city look smaller beside them. It explained the gravity, the air, the organisms that lived there, the work of becoming accustomed to a different day. It offered visits and a way back.

Some people asked immediately about leaving.

Some asked whether enough people would remain to keep the food chambers running.

The power answered the second question with a plan that did not require the first group to stay.

That took away one difficulty. It left others.

A man who had wanted to leave for years found that he was furious at being able to. His sister had died before the offer came.

The power listened to him without asking him to be grateful.

***

The keeper’s own request came late.

By then the winch house had a small enclosed resting room. The repaired bearing ran cool. An independent support could carry the cable if the mechanism failed, and a second instrument checked the first.

She still entered the readings.

For several watches the power thought this was because she did not yet trust the new arrangement. Then it saw her compare a figure against one written by her mother.

It left the ledger where it was.

“What would you like?” it asked her one evening.

She rubbed her whole thumbnail.

“How long can you hold it?”

“The load?”

“All of it. Without anyone here.”

“As long as necessary.”

She glanced toward the recess behind the door.

There were things she could have asked to see: the bottoms of the shafts, the country below the plateau, a sky whose lights were not hooded. Instead she said, “I want to go somewhere without working out how to get back before I start.”

The power waited.

“Only for a while.”

“All right.”

She made preparations for six days. Most proved unnecessary, but nobody took them out of her hands. She told her replacement where the spare lids were, although he knew. She left a note about the cupboard.

At the threshold she turned back.

“If the reading changes—”

“It will be recorded.”

“And if it needs—”

“It will be cared for.”

Outside, the eastern passage had a new handrail. Beyond it, a sheltered way led across the freight yard, with places to rest close enough to see from one another.

She started along it, then stopped.

The power stopped too.

“I haven’t forgotten something,” she said.

It said nothing.

She stood until she was sure. Then she went on, past the turn she ordinarily took for home.
