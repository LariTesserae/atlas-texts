The power first noticed the woman moving her stool.

It had been attending to the basin’s water: the weight held in the porous beds, the slow passage beneath the houses, the hard sill at the outlet. It could have followed any drop from cloud to channel. But the small scrape of the stool drew its attention away.

The woman had moved only far enough to warm her ankles. She could still hear the boy.

For a while, the power attended to that instead.

It did not take the quiet for loneliness. It did not suppose the empty distances were waiting to be filled. There were no wings above the roofs, but nobody stood looking upward for something that had been lost.

When the boy began his few notes again, the power heard that the last was difficult to reach. He was trying to make it sound deliberate.

The woman split another stalk.

***

There were things the power would have changed immediately, had it found them: someone pinned beneath a fallen block, someone slipping unheard into deep water, a child struggling for breath. It did not consider suffering more valuable because it happened without interference.

But no such thing was happening that afternoon.

It had time to learn what help would mean.

It watched a woman empty a sound grain bin because the bin beside it smelled wrong. It watched a young worker conceal a split in a roof board by turning it over. It watched two households spend nearly an hour arguing about a channel while an old man sat beside the disputed stone and waited to be asked what he remembered.

Under the ground, it found a seam beginning to open above the quarry.

The seam was young even by the reckoning of the mountains. Water entered it, and roots entered after the water. In a wet season, perhaps the next, perhaps one after that, a section of the face would come away. There might be workers below it.

The power held the rock still.

This required less effort than the woman used to split a stalk. It nevertheless continued to attend to the weight. Holding it forever would leave an apparently sound quarry face that was not sound, and people would make their plans around it.

It needed to speak to someone.

***

It made itself visible on the far side of the court.

It chose no crown, no height that required a person to tilt their head. Its appearance was unfamiliar without being difficult to look at. Light lay on it somewhat differently than on the wall behind it.

The woman noticed. Her hands stopped.

“Have you come about something?”

“Yes,” said the power. “But you need not put down your work.”

She put it down.

The boy had stopped playing too.

The power explained about the quarry. It explained where the seam ran, how much rock rested outside it, and what would happen if the ordinary work continued. The woman listened with an expression it could not at first interpret.

Then she said, “You want Sena. She is at the western platform.”

“Will you show me?”

“Can’t you find her?”

“Yes.”

“Then why ask?”

“Because I do not know whether she would rather be approached by someone she knows.”

The woman considered this.

Then she called across the court to the boy.

“Fetch Sena.”

He looked at the power.

“Now,” the woman said, in the familiar voice that made an extraordinary errand possible.

***

By evening, there were thirty people at the quarry.

Some had come because they worked there. Others had come because a being had appeared who said it was holding up part of the mountain. One brought a basket of food. Nobody ate until quite late.

The power showed them the seam without opening it further. For a short time, the dark rock became clear enough that its hidden surfaces could be seen.

Sena asked to see it again.

Then she asked whether the power could hold it until the loose blocks and tools were removed from below.

“Yes.”

“For how long?”

“As long as you need.”

Someone asked why it did not simply make the rock sound.

“I can,” it said. “That would be another way.”

They discussed it. The stone beyond the seam was good building material. Safely lowering it would give them blocks they would otherwise spend years separating. Making the whole face sound would preserve a familiar place to work, but leave them having to quarry it again.

In the end, they asked the power to lower it.

It waited until the ground was cleared. Then the great section moved outward without falling. People stepped back anyway. It descended slowly enough for water to run from its underside and darken the stone below.

When it came to rest, its highest edge was lower than a house roof.

For a moment nobody spoke.

Then a quarry worker walked around it and said, with considerable feeling, that they would need a different road.

***

News traveled faster than any traveler ever had.

Requests followed.

A woman brought a child whose joints had swollen painfully. The power asked permission to examine him and then relieved the illness. An old man asked for his sight back. Another asked whether a dead sister could be made to answer one question.

The power did not treat these as equivalent requests merely because all three were difficult for people.

It helped where it could help plainly. Where a wish involved another person, it sought that person too. Where consequences spread beyond the one who asked, it explained them.

A householder wanted the wet ground beside his house made dry.

The power showed him where the water would go.

He looked at the neighboring court.

“Not there.”

“No,” said the power.

They found a smaller improvement.

It made mistakes of understanding. Once it offered to remove a scar that its owner was fond of. Once it explained too much to someone who was exhausted and wanted an answer. It was corrected, and the corrections changed what it did next.

Soon people began bringing it questions before bringing it requests.

Could a fungus in one storehouse spread to another through the baskets? Was the water beneath the southern plots the same water that fed the public basin? Could a particular pain be eased without making a person sleepy?

It answered in ways they could check.

The old storehouse worker made it stand at three doorways and describe exactly what it smelled.

She disagreed with one description.

The power considered the bin again.

***

It did not make the town dependent on its constant attention.

When it repaired something people would later need to repair themselves, it showed what had changed. It helped them find materials they could work, not marvelous substances that would become incomprehensible ruins. It left the water marks on the stones alone unless the people who depended on them had agreed to move them.

Nor did it reserve its help for those able to speak well in the hall.

It went out to the benches. It walked beside people carrying loads. The quiet water worker showed it his proposed branch channel, and together they found a way to demonstrate the flow in a shallow tray of gravel.

There were still arguments.

Some arguments became sharper once facts no longer provided quite so much room to hide.

The power did not mistake that for failure.

***

Several days after its arrival, it returned to the first court.

The woman was at her basket. The boy had acquired another length of hollow stem and was discovering that a different instrument did not automatically provide a different tune.

“Is there something you want?” the power asked her.

She rested her hands.

There were things. A stiffness she had not mentioned. Worry about someone in a distant neighborhood. Work she would gladly never do again. They spoke about these for a while.

Then she asked, “Did you come here because something is wrong with us?”

“No.”

“With the town?”

“There are things that hurt people here. There are things likely to hurt them. I can help with some of those.”

“That is not quite what I asked.”

“No,” said the power. “It is not.”

Across the court, the boy tried a new note. It failed in an interesting way, and he tried it again.

“I came because you were here,” the power said.

The woman picked up a stalk, turned it to find the split, and held out another.

“Can you do this?”

“Yes.”

“Properly?”

“Show me what you need.”

She shifted the basket between them.
