The power does not arrive above the enclosure.

Above is not a reliable place to stand, and a great light in the gaps would close the inner lids of everyone within sight. Nor does it announce itself through the stone. Too many things already speak through the stone without waiting to be heard.

It comes to the broad path below the saddle, where there is room for two people to pass.

It has hands. It has feet whose soles can be shown. These are not disguises so much as provisions: ways of making an encounter possible without requiring the worker to invent one.

When the worker looks up, it stands still and opens its hands.

The worker sets down the scoop.

For some time they regard one another across the enclosure. Then the worker points out a patch of footing that the power has mistaken for part of the path.

The power moves.

This is the first useful thing it does.

***

It could stop the grinding.

It perceives how to do this almost immediately. The force arriving within the stone could be taken elsewhere; the unfinished depths could be held apart without pressure; the entire region could rest in a silence as complete as the small hollow of the scoop.

It also perceives the gardens starving.

A less complete quiet might leave them alive and conceal the sound of a failing arch. A shelter sealed against every vibration might contain a sleeper who never knows that the floor has ceased to belong to the ridge.

So the mountains continue.

Benevolence, here, will have to be more particular than silence.

The worker approaches. One hand rests over the power’s hand, and a conversation begins in which the power knows enough to receive pressure but not yet enough to answer.

It does not acquire the whole language by opening the worker’s mind. There are things under those mantles that have been worked carefully on the hidden side. Knowledge is not made less private by being easy to obtain.

Instead, it learns a little.

*Hold.*

*Less.*

*This part must remain free.*

The worker is explaining the scoop.

***

For several journeys, the power carries slabs.

There is no need for it to carry them one at a time. Nevertheless, there is a need to put them in the right places, and this cannot be learned from their weight.

Some are spares. Some are markers. One apparently neglected heap must not be moved because a returning traveler is using its changing alignment to judge whether two cliffs are becoming the same cliff.

The power leaves that heap alone.

It repairs a binding badly and is made to undo it. It is offered a nodule and finishes it before looking toward the bowl again. At the shelter it learns to put on its sandals, though it could have walked without producing any vibration. There is comfort in seeing that a precaution has been taken.

The worker begins to leave it alone with tools.

Eventually, beneath the leaves of the enclosure, the power asks a question.

Its grammar is imperfect. The meaning is approximately:

*What part of this work would you be glad not to do?*

The worker touches the scoop, then the pad beneath their knee, then a place on the inside of their wrist.

The power waits.

The worker corrects it. Not the work itself. Not even the weight.

The waiting after a mistake.

When the dust has begun to take the hand for part of its shape, someone must remain there until the confusion passes. Sometimes it takes longer than a garden’s harvest. Sometimes the person helping cannot safely let go either.

There is a shelter on the western route with food enough for three people and no sleeping niches. The power has wondered why.

Now it understands.

***

Its first gift is not a better scoop.

It is a place beneath the enclosure wall where an overburdened scoop can be lowered.

The place is shallow and plainly visible. It does not lead into a ridge. It resembles a cradle made from several curved leaf ribs, except that its separate pieces can yield without coming apart.

The power shows the worker what it does with a little dust.

When the dust grows heavy, the cradle receives that heaviness. When it begins to insist upon a shape, the ribs loosen. They do not pull it from the scoop. They give it somewhere nearer and less complicated to arrive.

The worker watches with both sets of lids open.

Then they make the power do it again.

They test a damaged scoop. They test too much dust. They test a scoop held at the wrong angle, and one deliberately pressed down after it should have been released.

The fourth test fails.

A rib takes on the shallow hollow of the scoop, and its end becomes difficult to see.

The worker lays a palm across the power’s wrist.

*Stay.*

Together they wait beside it.

The power could remove the failure beyond the region, where it would trouble no one. It does not mistake that possibility for a successful test. A thing safe only in its maker’s presence is not yet something to leave beside a working path.

They begin again.

***

The finished cradle is less elegant.

It occupies more space than the worker would like. Two of its ribs must be replaced periodically. It cannot receive an entire bowlful of dust, and nothing about it makes careless handling wise.

But a person caught alone can lower their scoop into it.

Then, gradually, they can take their hand away.

The useful principle is within the understanding of the people who will use it. It has to do with the tension in the ribs and the order in which their supports give way. The power supplies the first material, but they discover that strips from a mature silver fan will serve if prepared differently from shelter lining.

The worker changes the arrangement of the supports.

This improves it.

The power is visibly pleased, which the worker considers reasonable.

Copies go out slowly along the routes. Some return with corrections. One returns with an emphatic warning worked into its binding. No one agrees on the best size.

The western shelter acquires one sleeping niche.

Not three. There are still reasons to sit with someone.

***

By then, people have begun bringing other questions.

A traveler wants an uncertain crossing made dependable. A parent wants to know whether a child’s refusal of a particular stone means anything. An elder puts a hand into the power’s hand and does not, at first, make a sign.

The power does not answer all three with the same kind of mercy.

It examines the crossing and marks where an anchorage can be made without forcing the cliffs to remain separate forever. It tests the child’s stone and finds nothing wrong with it today. It makes that last part clear.

With the elder, it waits.

Eventually the question comes.

*If I go in, can I come out?*

The power follows the depth gathering beneath the elder’s skin. It looks farther than any companion has been able to go.

What it finds is not a simple country of the dead, nor a place that can safely be described as a new shelter. There are continuities. There are losses. There are ways back that do not return everything that entered.

It tells the elder what it can establish and where its knowledge ends.

Then it offers something smaller than a promise of return: help loosening the attachment, for now, if the elder wishes to remain outside.

The elder asks whether this will undo the widening already made in a much-loved sleeping niche.

It will not.

They ask whether it must be done today.

It need not.

They withdraw their hand to think.

The power lets them.

***

At the enclosure, the worker still kneels on the folded pad.

Their scoop has been repaired a third time. They are teaching someone younger, who holds the handle too firmly and dislikes being corrected.

Beyond the wall, the ridges continue their terrible arrival. Gardens feed upon the pressure. A hanging plate has come near enough to a cliff that, within a few years, its roots may touch.

The power has not made the heights into another place.

There are now a few things one can do when something goes wrong. A few burdens no longer require a season of another person’s wrist. Along one difficult route, a traveler can arrive in time to make an apology.

These changes are small enough to belong to the people who use them.

When the worker next rests, they turn back the edge of an inner mantle. There is a new pattern in the binding, made in hours that were once spent waiting helplessly beside the dust.

The power leans closer.

The worker lets it look.
