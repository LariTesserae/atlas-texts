The power notices the hand that remains on the eggs.

It notices the other hand, too: the one lifted from your chest, ready to strike again. The builder has divided its attention between what it cannot leave and what it does not yet know how to save.

The water comes through the threshold without a sound distinguishable from the rest of the plain.

First, the power wakes you.

There is no voice. You become fully awake, with the builder’s weight on your ribs and the knowledge that you must not sit up suddenly. One of its feet is caught in a fold of your clothing. Its eggs are on your bundle.

You free the foot. You put your hands around the bundle without lifting it.

The builder presses down on the shell.

You wait.

Outside, the basin rises another small amount.

***

A wise power can distinguish between a thing that needs to end and a thing it has not understood.

The noise of the plain is not, by itself, suffering. Neither is the wetness, nor the absence of trees. Much of what lives here depends on the slow traffic beneath the stone. To close the seams would starve the feeding basins. To raise the whole country would drain chambers whose dark contents have not yet been seen.

But the skin splitting beneath a builder’s shoulder is an injury. A body pinned under a slab is a body pinned under a slab. There is no need to admire these things while deciding what the country means.

Under the shelter, water presses against a pocket of trapped air. The pocket contracts. A thin plate begins taking a load it cannot bear.

For the moment, the power holds it.

The roof continues to hum. Nothing is made falsely reassuring.

***

Beside the entrance, something touches the stone three times.

The builder turns both eyes toward it.

A pale body stands on the rubble beyond the basin. It is not quite a builder. Its back has no old repairs, and its feet are wrong in a way you cannot immediately identify. They have too many surfaces capable of meeting the ground.

The power has made itself small enough to wait outside.

It lowers its jaw. Then it moves a short distance along the rubble, stops, and returns.

The builder does not follow.

The visitor repeats the movement.

Still the builder stays beside the eggs. A route demonstrated by a stranger is not yet a route it knows. The power learns this without impatience.

It comes to the threshold and places one forefoot within reach.

Their first exchange is clumsy.

The builder presses at the wrist, then the jaw. The visitor answers too evenly. The builder withdraws, spreads its fringe over the shell, and strikes the gravel.

The second answer is better: a change of pressure, a pause that leaves room for refusal.

You cannot tell what passes between them. Eventually the builder takes its hand off the eggs and places it on yours.

When you lift the bundle, it comes with it.

***

The route out does not become a road.

A loose fragment settles securely beneath your foot. A sharp edge breaks before it reaches the soft underside of the animal guiding you. Across one hollow, where no safe crossing exists, a slab holds your weight and the weight of everything you carry.

Behind you, it remains in place. Others may come this way.

The refuge is less than a hundred yards away, hidden by two close ranks of stone. It has been there for years: a leaning plate above a patch of shell too low to stay dry.

Now the bed is higher.

The added material is coarse, with spaces through which moisture can drain. There is no white crust pretending to age. The work is plainly new.

You kneel outside while the builder examines it.

It tests the rear wall, the roof support, the floor. It goes out and puts its jaw to the neighboring ridge. It returns and removes three fragments from the bed. One it drops outside. The other two it exchanges.

The power waits through all of this.

At last the builder takes the egg-shell from your bundle.

***

Over the following days, other changes occur.

They are not all made of stone.

An old builder, moving with its left shoulder raised, wakes to find that the wound beneath it has closed. The salt embedded in the tissue is gone. The new skin is tender, and for a while it continues to favor the limb.

Another, trapped that morning beneath a newly fallen plate, feels the pressure ease before its shield gives way. It backs out. When it returns with two others, the slab is resting on supports broad enough not to sink into the mud.

The power does not require either animal to approach it afterward.

It visits the feeding grounds. For a long time it does nothing there.

There are films beneath the films, and small lives in the dark paste, and differences between nearly identical blisters. The power attends to these with the same care it gave the eggs. When it does begin changing things, it opens a few additional feeding surfaces rather than filling the basins with unfamiliar abundance. It watches what comes to graze.

Some basins are left entirely alone.

***

Dry resting places appear within reach of grounds that previously had none.

They are not identical. Some are low enough to satisfy animals that dislike open space above their backs. Others have room for eggs, visitors, and the accumulated objects of a long occupation. All have more than one way out.

At first the power builds too neatly.

In one shelter, it separates the sleeping floor so thoroughly from the wet ground that the floor carries almost no vibration. The first visitor enters, puts its chin down, and immediately leaves.

The power rebuilds it.

A stout contact with a stable ridge brings the distant movements back into the bed. The walls remain dry. The visitor returns, listens, and sleeps.

Elsewhere, builders dismantle part of a new threshold. The power follows the reason through their work: the high stone conceals the earliest rise in the basin. It lowers the stone before making another shelter.

There is less work to undo after that.

***

The pale visitor becomes familiar, although not tame.

Builders interrupt it. They put their hands between its hands when it is speaking with another. They bring it wedges, bad food, broken tools, sometimes nothing that you can identify as a request.

It learns to accompany them.

Where a dangerous plate can be supported, it supports it. Where a sleeping chamber cannot be made safe, it helps carry the stored gravel and the eggs elsewhere. Some animals return stubbornly to the old place. The power goes back with them and keeps it from falling while they retrieve one more stone.

There are disagreements it cannot settle by improving the ground. Two builders continue shoving over the same feeding edge despite an equally good edge nearby.

It does not move their bodies apart.

When one loses its footing, it catches it.

***

You stay long enough to see the eggs hatch.

The parent has altered the refuge considerably. Your bundle, now usually outside, has been replaced by a raised shelf built into the rear corner. One of the power’s carefully chosen supports has become a lever. A lever has become part of the floor.

The hatchlings feed beneath the adult’s fringe. Their feet emerge at unlikely angles.

One evening the pale visitor lies near the entrance while the parent works on the bed. A hatchling approaches carrying a wet piece of shell. The shell is much too large. It takes a long time to drag it across the gravel.

The visitor reaches forward.

Then it stops.

The young builder gets the shell as far as the sleeping hollow and leaves it there, shedding water.

Its parent comes over. There is a brief exchange of touches. Together they drag it outside.

Later the hatchling returns with a smaller piece.

The power shifts to make room.

Beyond the entrance, the ridges grind and whistle. A distant chamber seals; another begins to sound. The parent lifts its head, listens, and lowers it again.

The youngest builder places its stone in the bed.

This time it stays.
