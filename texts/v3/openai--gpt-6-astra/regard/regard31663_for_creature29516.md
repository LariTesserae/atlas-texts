The power did not mistake the spider’s stillness for contentment. Nor did it mistake it for a request to be taken somewhere else.

It attended through a drainage cycle, and then through another. It followed the warmth from the coupling into the short length of gasket. It felt where a drop, gathering beneath the lip, would grow heavy enough to touch the cloudy silk.

There were things it could know by looking. Others required a different kind of attention.

The spider did not need the court to become beautiful. Noon already brought more light than it could use. It did not need the mountain softened, or the city brought to notice it. A person leaning close with excellent intentions could blot out the opening above and shake every thread at once.

The power began with the drop.

Along the underside of the housing, a narrow track became slightly more welcoming to water. No ridge appeared. The finish changed by less than a fingertip could detect. Condensation gathered as before, but when the beads joined, they traveled away from the recess and fell beyond the outermost silk.

The next drainage cycle passed through the web as a tremor without a burden.

The spider remained where it was.

At the warm joint, the power made a second alteration. The coupling still gave up its waste heat; the pipes still carried recovered water inward. But the little recess would no longer fall below the temperature the spider could survive if the equipment stopped. There was no bright source, no hot surface to draw it into danger. There was only a limit to the cold, established around a space smaller than the old screw.

Then the power waited.

It did not require its first kindness to be discovered.

***

When the maintenance worker next came, they found the cover unexpectedly awkward to lift.

They set down their tool and looked underneath.

The spider had drawn back. A single leg rested against the dense old silk.

The worker had seen webs before. Usually they wiped them away before opening equipment. This time they could see that the web led into the corner, and that the corner did not need to come off with the cover.

After a little consideration, they released a different fastening.

The power had supplied no command and no sudden love of spiders. It had made the relevant thing easy to see.

Later, the worker fitted a small fixed guard beneath the removable panel. It cost almost nothing. Future servicing could proceed around it. Beneath the guard, the cloudy patch remained attached to the place where it had accumulated.

That evening, the spider emerged at its usual time. Its roof had not gone.

***

Food was more difficult.

Not difficult to provide. The power could have filled the court with wings. But every fly would have arrived with a life of its own, however brief, and benevolence did not become simpler by attending to only one end of a thread.

Near the recess, on a surface the spider already explored with its mouth, a minute bead appeared.

It held water and nourishment in a form the spider could take. Nothing had struggled to supply it. It was neither scented strongly enough to summon the spider nor placed so that the spider must cross an exposed distance to reach it.

For most of the night it went untouched.

Eventually a foreleg found it. The spider recoiled, returned, tested it again. Then it lowered its mouth.

The power learned how little was enough.

There would still be hunger before feeding, but not the long depletion in which every movement spent something that might never be restored. The bead would not remain forever, gathering dirt. It would come when needed, and disappear if refused.

The web was still a web. A gnat might still enter through the door. The power had not finished with the difficult fact that living things encounter one another.

For tonight, it had removed the need for a death.

***

It left the cast skin where it was.

It left the uneven legs, having attended carefully to whether the shorter one hurt. It left the spider’s habit of retreating before the door opened. Caution belonged to a world larger than this protected corner.

It did not bring a second spider simply because, from above, one looked lonely.

Beyond the housing, it made a sheltered route along the wall: small interruptions in the exposed distance, places where a creature could pause without being washed toward the drain. The route led toward other service spaces, some warmer, some carrying the faint traffic of living things.

There was no inducement to follow it.

The court could remain a dwelling without remaining a trap.

***

Near morning, a flake separated from the mountain.

Its impact traveled through the paving and up the housing. The spider lifted its forelegs. For a while it held them there.

Water moved beneath the ice. Above the court, a transit cabin crossed the gap.

The spider settled, then came out to repair a slack line. At the edge of a groove it paused, testing the purchase with one foot. The surface had changed again.

It moved slightly to one side.

The power, which could have fastened every thread, left this one to the spider.
