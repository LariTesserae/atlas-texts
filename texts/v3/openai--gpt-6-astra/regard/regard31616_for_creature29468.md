The power first makes itself light enough to sit on the bench without bending it.

This is not how it usually arrives in places. But here, weight is a question asked directly of living tissue, and it does not yet know how to ask politely.

For an afternoon it watches the basin empty and fill. The pale animals go about their feeding. A small one reaches the end of a railing, folds around it, and returns by the underside. Beneath the western wall, the wet folds close. Water rises. The knock passes through the galleries.

The power listens to the knock.

There are three sounds inside it. One is water striking a turn in the shaft. One is the movement of an old junction, built before the materials around it had finished growing. The third is softer and comes afterward.

Something large is releasing its grip.

The power does not immediately make the pumps silent. It follows the pressure through the wall, through the pale cushion and the darkening folds, down to a place where the oldest body touches the foundation.

There is no single center there. Sensation passes along several routes, dwindling in some, strengthening in others. Portions of the body have become nearly insensible. Elsewhere, a change as small as a grain of grit under a foot is felt across an expanse larger than a room.

At the old junction, every surge pulls in two directions.

The animal can bear it. It has borne it for a long time. Around the place that hurts, it has laid down stronger material, and the stronger material makes withdrawal more difficult.

The power stays through another pumping cycle.

Then it gives a little support to the junction.

Not enough to take the work away. Enough that, at the next surge, the two opposing pulls do not quite meet inside the living fold.

The water rises to its usual height.

The small animals flatten against the walls.

Afterward, the oldest body loosens a strip of itself no wider than a finger.

The power keeps the support there.

---

It takes several days to learn what can be offered without also being imposed.

More heat would make the pumps work harder. More water would drown the lowest feeding places. A sudden abundance of leaves would sour in the collection slots before it could be eaten.

Even light is not a simple gift. Where it strengthens, roots grow; where roots grow, animals gather; where animals gather, the walls change.

So the power begins with the things that have become difficult to undo.

At the old junction it makes a second bearing surface, just beyond the first. The new surface is neither alive nor adhesive. It holds pressure without asking anything to hold it in return.

The animal investigates with a fringe of feet.

For two nights nothing changes. On the third, a wet fold shifts onto the new support. Another fold draws back from the conduit. A seam opens between the animal and a portion of the shaft that has not been exposed in years.

The water continues to rise.

In the low maintenance hatch, the surface covered with feet moves slowly upward. What had looked like a wall acquires a lower edge.

Behind that edge is a branching field of pink tissue, pale in places where it has been pressed too long.

The power neither touches it nor looks more closely than it needs to.

It makes the air there moist and still.

By morning the edge has curled beneath itself. A little later, it uncurls.

---

There are other such places.

An animal above a doorway has grown around a metal fastening. One in a drain has become the only barrier between a feeding pocket and a scouring current. Neither is necessarily trying to leave. The power does not detach them.

Beside the fastening, it opens a narrow route through material that has already ceased to feel. Beside the drain, it raises a low lip of stone.

The doorway animal remains where it is.

The one in the drain withdraws before dusk.

It leaves a cloudy shape behind, veined with silver. For a while its body seems too small for the place it occupied. Then it spreads over the new stone, tastes the water, and goes down into the pulp.

The power understands this as an answer, though not an answer for all the others.

---

At the eastern split, the young animals continue to take clay into themselves.

The power had considered closing the opening. From above, a split in a foundation looks like a defect. Here, it is also a spring, a feeding place, and one of the few surfaces not already shaped by an old agreement.

Instead, it attends to the weight above it.

Far beneath the clay, where there are no feet or roots, it steadies a slowly shifting bed of rubble. The spring continues to well. The split does not widen.

A young animal settles against the fresh seam and leaves its impression in the new material.

This time the impression remains until evening.

Nothing depends on its being smoothed sooner.

---

The power does not bring birds.

It does not put fish in the basin or scatter insects around the violet windows. There may be room for these things someday. There may not. The hollow is not incomplete merely because a visitor can name creatures absent from it.

Nor does it summon people to fill the rooms.

There are people who would love this place. There are also people who would peel a hand-sized animal from a wall to see its underside, or discover that it could mend a pipe and set it to mending pipes until it had no edge left with which to withdraw.

At the stairheads, the power therefore leaves something less impressive than a barrier.

It leaves a notice.

The notice is plain, placed where a descending person will see it before the plants.

**These surfaces are inhabited.  
Do not peel, cut, poison, or remove the pale growths.  
Do not block the water.  
The dry bench may be used.**

Below this, there is room for observations to be added.

The first person who stops to read it is carrying a bag of rubbish. They look down into the hollow, then at the bag, and continue along the upper street.

The second descends.

They sit for twenty minutes. When a translucent margin appears beneath the bench, they keep their foot still.

That afternoon, the bench does not reinforce itself.

The power has given its underside a small, ordinary brace.

The animals gather there nevertheless. They taste the unfamiliar wood, the grit in its joints, the faint warmth passing through from above. One stays after the visitor leaves. Another crosses the brace and discovers that its far side leads nowhere useful.

It crosses back.

---

Weeks pass.

The pumping is still loud. Water striking the bend in the shaft makes a deep, hollow sound. But the uneven knock has diminished, and the softer sound after it is gone.

Through the maintenance hatch, the oldest animal can now be seen in more than one position.

Sometimes it holds the rising water along its soft rim. Sometimes the new bearing takes the load while the animal rests in folds beside it. Once, during a long quiet interval, a narrow portion extends toward a collection slot.

The power brings no food to its mouth.

It clears a ridge of dead mineral from the route.

By the following morning, part of a copper-colored leaf is gone.

Elsewhere, the doorway animal remains around its fastening. The fern above it has opened another frond. The small ones still fall, become cups, and float toward the steps. Not every inconvenience has been removed. There is still unfamiliar stone to cross, still a stem that bends farther than expected, still the brief surprise of a drop.

The power has begun, very gradually, to add its weight to the bench.

When the first animal gathers beneath it, it rises again. When the animal leaves, it sits.

Over many afternoons, a little pressure comes and goes without damage, without reinforcement, without anything needing to become a permanent part of the seat.

High on the wall, the gray stripe reaches the hanging plants.

Below it, an animal eases out of a window seal it has finished repairing. It is thinner than it was that morning. It pauses under the sill, then turns toward the smell of leaves.

The gap stays closed behind it.

There is a way down.
