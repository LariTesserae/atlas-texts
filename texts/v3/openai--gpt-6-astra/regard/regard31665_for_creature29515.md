The power noticed the branch moving in Irena’s hand. It also noticed the cold air passing her wrist, the child waiting behind her, and the grease-dark cloth lying folded beside the stove.

It did not take her hesitation for a prayer.

Irena closed the window. The catch needed lifting before it would engage. She lifted it, pressed with her hip, and went to put the kettle on.

There were many things the power could have done. It could have put the district on solid ground, although there was no way of doing so without destroying nearly everything that made it this district. It could have restored the alder’s roots through the new membrane. It could have made Irena young.

Instead, it spent some time finding out what happened here.

***

It followed the water first.

This took it through places Irena had never entered, although she knew their position and sometimes their smell. It passed beneath settlement tanks and through a sealed cavity containing two forgotten ladders. It found water that was clean enough for roots but not for hands, water warmed at considerable expense and then cooled at further expense, water waiting behind a blockage while a sensor faithfully reported the condition of an adjacent pipe.

It followed warmth, wages, access permissions, sleep.

The district’s machinery had schedules. So did its people, but the machinery’s schedules were more often honored.

A woman on the night shift had forty minutes between arriving home and waking her son for school. A man with painful knees was using the nursery stairs because the reliable lift had become reliable only during freight hours. Three households were heating rooms against an uninsulated wall whose improvement required the agreement of an owner who had never been there.

The power did not find a single fault from which all the others descended.

It found faults that paid somebody, faults nobody could afford to stop working long enough to repair, and faults that persisted because the person who understood them was tired of explaining.

It also found things that were working.

It was careful with those.

***

Its first intervention was under the east retaining wall, during the night.

An anchor had corroded inside its sleeve. By morning, after another small movement in the loaded structure above it, it would have failed.

The power held it.

There was no light, no noise, no moment in which someone looked up and felt mysteriously reassured. A cleaner pushed a cart across the deck overhead. One wheel rattled. She was thinking about a message from her sister.

The power continued to hold the anchor until the wall could be made safe.

***

The following afternoon, someone knocked on Irena’s door.

The visitor wore a coat wet enough to be credible and shoes that suggested a poor understanding of the district.

“I’d like to pay you to show me some things,” they said.

“Ask the office.”

“I have. They gave me the public access plan.”

“Then you’ve already been misled.”

The visitor waited.

Irena had a saucepan on the stove. She turned the heat down before asking what things they wanted to see.

“Where people are cold. Where a repair keeps getting postponed. Anything dangerous that has become ordinary.”

“Who are you with?”

The answer took some time. Irena made the visitor repeat parts of it.

Then she said, “You could have sent a letter.”

“Yes.”

“And you’ll pay?”

“Yes.”

“Not a consultation voucher.”

“Money.”

She got her coat.

***

What followed was not accepted all at once.

The district had seen improvement funds before. It had seen demonstration projects whose equipment became impossible to service as soon as the photographs were taken. A nursery manager asked what the power expected in return. A tenants’ representative asked who would own the additions. Someone asked whether worship would eventually become compulsory.

“No,” said the power.

“Put it in the agreement.”

It did.

There were meetings. Some were unnecessary; some prevented mistakes that immense power alone would not have prevented.

The power could make insulation, but the residents chose where a wall could be opened. It could provide heat without fuel, but the maintenance crews insisted on ordinary connections, visible shutoffs, and a way to replace the equipment if it ever ceased to function.

“It won’t,” the power said.

“We’d still like the drawings.”

The drawings were supplied.

The new warmth belonged to the homes, not the factories beneath them. Where people wanted to keep using waste heat, they did. Where a production stoppage had meant a cold bedroom, it no longer did.

There was an argument about rents. The power would not pay for a warmer room merely to help price its occupant out of it. This delayed some work. While the argument continued, it paid the heating bills directly and helped the residents secure terms they were willing to sign.

People were not required to be grateful before becoming warm.

***

Irena worked three paid afternoons a week for two months.

She took a younger technician along, partly because he needed to learn and partly because she was not going to crawl behind anything.

At the mislabeled overflow, he asked whether they should preserve the old marking in the record.

“In the record, yes. Not on the pipe.”

They wrote down what she knew. Where she was uncertain, they wrote that down too. Once, a retired cleaner corrected her about the route through an old partition.

Irena objected until they opened it.

Afterward she bought him a bun, without mentioning why.

The knowledge did not become complete. It became easier to find, and less dependent on catching one particular person in a generous mood.

When Irena let a call go unanswered, someone else could answer it.

***

The alder was not made magnificent.

The power asked the tree crew what they needed. They wanted another pair of hands during the dry weeks, a moisture probe they could trust, and permission to widen the rooting bed into a disused storage bay.

The bay contained equipment belonging to a company that no longer existed.

This was resolved less quickly than the anchor.

For a while, Irena watched someone come every other morning to check the straps and the soil. One small branch died. The rest leafed out.

With the wider bed, the tree would eventually put more shade across her kitchen. She considered this and bought a better lamp.

***

The children were consulted separately.

At the first meeting, an adult proposed a rubber surface and three brightly colored structures from a catalogue.

The children asked whether water would be allowed.

Eventually a shallow channel was built beside the warm wall, fed by water suitable for prolonged contact with hands. It had bends, removable gates, and a broad catch basket at its lower end. The drop beyond it was properly guarded. There was enough room for an adult to sit without occupying the course.

The children inspected it suspiciously.

Then someone discovered that closing two gates at once could flood a section never intended to flood.

The designers were called back. The children were not expelled.

There continued to be arguments about packing foam.

***

Not everything was repaired.

People still disliked their neighbors. Young people still wanted to leave, and now some who had lacked the fare could go. Others stayed. A public passage remained closed because opening it would put strangers directly past six bedroom windows. The alternative route was longer, but it was made dry and accessible.

There were still mice.

There was, however, somewhere to ask for help that did not require proving that a difficulty had become a catastrophe.

The power stayed long enough for its work to stop being news. It did not need to be thanked in the street. It was sometimes thanked, sometimes blamed for things it had not touched, and once asked to adjudicate a dispute about a tree that was dropping leaves into two different gutters.

It declined to decide without hearing both households.

***

That autumn, Irena replaced the cartridge in her kitchen tap.

The job took less than twenty minutes, including finding the tool she had lent to someone and been given back in the wrong bag.

Afterward, the kitchen sounded different. She stood listening until the pumps started next door.

Across the gap, the office supervisor looked up. She raised the old cartridge to show him. He could not possibly know what it was, but he nodded.

Outside, rain collected on the alder’s leaves. Its straps had been loosened. The branch nearest the glass moved in the wind without striking it.

Her granddaughter was at the table, drawing an extension to the racing channel. The plan crossed an existing path.

“You’ll have to let people through,” Irena said.

“I know. That bit lifts.”

“Who lifts it?”

The girl considered this, then rubbed something out.

Irena put the tools away. For once, there was nothing she had agreed to inspect before supper.
