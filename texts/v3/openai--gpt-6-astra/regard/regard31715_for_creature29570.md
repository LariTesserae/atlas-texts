The attention reaches the bowl without disturbing the water.

The stones climb and fall. A mouth withdraws into its pore. Beneath the lip, the translucent thread tightens along the fracture, and a little fluid moves toward the place where its outer body was torn.

The power waits through several circuits.

It could stop the stones. It could empty the bowl, lift the terraces into air, restore every fitted block to the position it occupied before the sea arrived. But the mouth opens into water. The thread has grown along a break. What was damage to the pedestal has become the beginning of a place to live.

It leaves the bowl beneath the sea.

Its attention follows the thread inward.

There is no hidden face at the end of it, no small person waiting to be addressed. There are differences: contact and release, food arriving and food failing to arrive, a contraction that subsides, another that persists. The power attends closely enough not to mistake these for nothing, or to supply them with a voice they do not have.

At the turn in the fracture, the flesh has encountered a blind end.

Beyond it lies another pore. Between the two is a thickness of stone smaller than a grain of rice. Water enters the farther pore from beneath the lip, where the current slows after passing the notch. Fine particles settle there. So little water exchanges with the fracture that the thread cannot reach them.

The power makes the intervening stone permeable.

It does not open a sudden hole. Over many tides, a few grains loosen. The first passage is too narrow for flesh. Water seeps through, carrying traces of what lies beyond.

The thread remains contracted for a long time.

Then its tip broadens.

Nothing draws it forward. It grows as it has grown before, spending what its one mouth can gather. Twice the tip withdraws. Once a pebble strikes the lip hard enough to close the feeding opening for the better part of a day.

The new passage remains.

By the time the flesh enters the farther pore, its first mouth has been scraped almost away. The living edge closes over the injury. Beneath the lip, a second opening forms in water that moves less violently.

Now a pebble can pass without requiring the whole exposed animal to disappear.

The power does not remove the pebbles. Their motion brings fragments into the bowl and keeps its hollow from filling. Nor does it make the new mouth impossible to bite. A small crustacean finds the opening and takes a piece from its rim. The flesh contracts; the crustacean carries its meal beneath the balustrade.

There is room behind the injury for the animal to withdraw.

There is another place from which it can feed.

Across the terraces, the power finds other needs, none arranged neatly apart from the lives around them.

The broad-headed fish with the abraded flank is thin. The tissue behind the wall is recovering from its last bite. Beneath a flag, four mouths draw too little from a narrow passage. In the lower stairwell, the shrimp has returned to its fold.

The power does not turn one of these creatures into the price of its affection for another.

It attends also to the water: where food travels unused into sealed rubble, where a new fall would close the only exchange through a crowded chamber, where an exposed joint is beginning to bear more weight than its remaining stone can hold.

At one such joint, far below the bowl, a slab is about to fail.

Its fall would drive through three floors. Beneath them, loose curtains of flesh occupy a long enclosed space, with small fish between them and young mussels thick along its roof. There are exits, but not enough time to use them.

The power takes some of the weight.

For a season, the slab descends by almost nothing.

Its movement changes the pressure in the chamber. Water begins to leave through a seam that had previously admitted it. The curtains shorten on that side and spread elsewhere. Fish explore a widening gap. Several mussels remain where they settled; others, still small, loosen and find new holds.

The slab continues downward.

When it finally rests, it rests against rubble rather than striking it. A cloud rises from the summit. The chamber has become lower, and one passage is closed. Along the other side, an opening remains broad enough for the water and the living folds.

From above, the change is scarcely visible.

The power stays.

Its attention is not a promise that nothing will be lost. The terraces will continue to wear. Bodies will divide, fail, feed other bodies. Some larvae will never find stone.

But where a weight can be eased, it may be eased. Where a passage can be opened without emptying another creature’s shelter, water may begin to enter.

Near the western edge, the white stones turn inside the bowl.

Under its lip, the second mouth opens. A particle catches on one of its short processes and is drawn inward. Farther back, beyond reach of the stones, the thread has thickened enough to fold against itself.

The current reverses.

This time, part of the animal continues feeding.
