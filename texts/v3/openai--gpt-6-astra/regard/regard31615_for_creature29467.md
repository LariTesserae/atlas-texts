The attention arrived without weight.

Nothing on the shelf was crushed. The dust remained as it had fallen, and the little swimmers kept their bubbles beneath the water. At the upper seam, a grain of rain passed through a place where something had begun to listen.

For a long time, listening was all it did.

The mountain gave back its sounds after their usual delay. The power followed them into the stone: through crystals, through wet bands thinner than roots, through places where a pulse divided and arrived at the same living surface from several directions. There were distances here that could not be measured by the height of the summit. A small injury near the pool was felt far below the clouds. Another, only a little farther along the wall, was felt nowhere beyond itself.

The oldest grazer came to its opening and left a little fluid there.

The power attended to the cost of that fluid. It attended also to what received it.

It did not mistake the grazer for a servant of the mountain, or the mountain for a house built for the grazer. There were many lives here. Their dependence on one another did not make their hungers identical.

Some of the long bodies within the crystals were trying to cross the split. Others were withdrawing from it. The flat creatures waiting with their heads inside the cracks were not all waiting for the same thing.

The power stayed through several unfoldings of the silver leaves.

***

The first thing it changed was the course of a drop.

A fracture had opened beneath an overhang in the middle chamber. Within it, a wet band was exposed to air. The nearest flat creatures had already begun to gather, but the stone above them shed water to either side. Their backs tightened over the opening. Beneath them, the band was drying.

Farther up, a drop hung from an edge.

The power drew it along the underside of the stone. It moved slowly enough to carry no grit. At the fracture it spread, lost its rounded shape, and entered beneath the overlapping plates.

The inward contraction stopped.

Nothing else was asked of the injured thing.

When the next drop came, it followed the wet track left by the first.

***

The power could have held the mountain together.

It examined what this would mean.

Below the chambers, pressure was gathering along a plane that had not yet opened. Farther down were warm passages, blind mineral surfaces, lives whose nourishment arrived in the movement of stone. To arrest the summit would transfer its burden elsewhere. To close the split would crush the colonies and put the grazers’ ledges back inside the mountain.

Even a wound could become a place to live without ceasing to be a wound.

So the power did not close it.

Instead, where a living strand crossed from one wall to the other, it loosened a minute length of crystal around the strand’s root.

There was room there now for the connection to yield.

The change was less than the width of a hoof. It allowed the next small movement of the mountain to draw out a curve instead of pulling the strand apart.

Along the curve, a dark bead continued its journey.

The power waited to see what followed.

Some strands used the slack. Some thickened where they had always thickened, accepting none of it. One withdrew into the wall, leaving a clear sheath behind.

It left those choices undisturbed.

***

The oldest grazer’s difficulty was not in its feet alone.

Beneath the polished ridge on its back, a joint had become inflamed. Passing under the low projection pressed upon it. The grazer had changed the way it folded its legs, and this had made one of the familiar footholds difficult to reach.

Each journey to the sounding wall now required a movement it anticipated long before making it.

The power could have changed the animal’s body while it slept. Instead, it remained nearby while the grazer woke and tested its extended hoof against the crystal.

A little warmth lay beside its resting hollow.

The grazer touched the place with its bristles. It withdrew. Later it touched it again.

The warmth did not follow.

On the third visit, the animal lowered one flank against it.

The power eased the inflamed tissue slowly. When the grazer lifted itself, the easing stopped. When it settled again, it continued.

There was no sudden strength. Its worn hooves remained worn. Its deliberate manner of walking belonged partly to knowledge, and the power had no wish to remove that.

Afterward, the grazer slept with three legs folded beneath itself and the fourth against the crystal.

Several flat creatures climbed into the fur at its shoulder.

This time their weight did not wake it.

***

The next great opening began with warmth behind the sounding wall.

The stone arrangements rattled in their accustomed order. One grazer rose, then another. The young were nudged from beside the pool. Across the chambers, bodies compressed and passed, taking their old routes away from the changing slab.

But a hanging colony had fastened itself to a crystal that would fall with the stone.

The power heard the strain before it entered the grazers’ little arrangements.

It gave the threatened crystal a small movement.

The colony tightened.

The power stopped.

After a while, it placed a second hold within reach: a roughness on the neighboring wall, warm as the crystal had been warm, no farther away than an outstretched wrist.

One hand found it.

For many breaths nothing happened. Then another animal reached across. The cluster stretched between its old support and the new place. Breath passed through it unevenly.

The power supported neither chest nor limb. It held the loosening crystal steady while the animals found their own way off it.

The last one remained for a long time, arms spread around the familiar strand.

Only when its hand opened did the power let the crystal take the weight of the mountain again.

The slab fell.

Its impact returned through the chambers with the force of distant weather. Dust covered the pools. The grazers stood along their narrow ledges, listening through their feet.

Beneath the broken stone there was a new surface, dark and wet.

Soon the flat creatures began to descend.

***

The power remained attentive, though its attention spread beyond the split.

It followed the rain upward into cloud and the living bands downward into heat. There were injuries elsewhere. There were also movements that looked like injury until their long course could be seen.

Within the chambers, it kept a few small possibilities open: a route for water, a little yielding space around a strand, a hold that could be reached before another failed.

The bare shelf remained bare.

Sometimes the oldest grazer came to it and lowered its head. Nothing required it to stay. Nothing entered its body to make it understand.

Once, it brought a pebble.

It set the pebble near the edge, pressed its bristles against it, and waited.

There was no answer except the breathing of the colonies and, after a while, the deep returning sound.

The grazer removed the pebble.

The power let it.

Above the pool, a silver leaf had begun to unfold. A drop gathered at its tip, and one of the young stood beneath it with its mouth lifted.

This drop needed no help.

It fell.
