The attention that reached the plateau cast no shadow.

At first it rested on the ordinary things: the cord in the maker’s hand, the uneven hoof, the six leaves of the tree. It did not pass more quickly over the beetles than over the people. Where a tail entered the stone, it followed without pulling.

It found nothing that could be understood merely by calling the place still.

There were lives here for which stillness was exact. The bird’s lifted foot lacked nothing. The creature within the shell was not confined. No thirst inhabited the dry mouths of the grazers. To bring rain would have been to answer a question the ground had not asked.

The power could have made rain.

It attended more closely.

***

Beside the shelter, you remain standing.

Something about the available stone has begun to trouble you. The accommodation is so precise that it is difficult to tell whether you have been offered a seat or discovered your position.

You bend one knee, then straighten it.

The seated person’s eyes remain on yours. The brown wedge in one iris has an irregular edge, like a little piece of land seen from above.

The power does not sit you down.

Nor does it draw you away.

Instead, it attends to the difference between room made for someone and a place from which that someone cannot be absent.

It is a small difference to look at.

The plateau has room for it.

***

At the edge, beyond the post, the descent remains enormous. No lower country comes obligingly into view. There is no green valley, no smoke from a house that would explain where everyone ought to go.

But one of the trails has a turning place.

It is hardly more than a widening. A walker reaching it could turn without leaning over the drop. Someone carrying a child could change the child from one hip to the other. An animal with branching horns could bring its head around.

The stones there are the same gray stones. Their surfaces show no new fracture.

The power has not disguised the change as something ancient. Anyone who remembers the narrow trail can remember it truly.

There was not room before.

There is room now.

***

This alone would not have been enough.

An open gate is of little use to a creature whose inability to approach it has been mistaken for contentment.

So the power’s attention went where even the narrow corridors did not go. It reached the particular life behind each clear eye.

It did not ask for a gesture. It did not require speech, or the imagining of a world beyond the plateau. A beetle was not made to understand a journey. The child was not asked to decide what should become of the tall animal.

Where there was contentment, it remained contentment, unexamined by its owner and undisturbed.

Where there was pain, the power did not call it history and leave it there.

A missing part did not have to be replaced for an old hurt to cease. A scar could remain without keeping its wound alive. And where an injury was not merely remembered but still unwanted, the power held the possibility of repair as patiently as a good craftsperson holds two broken edges together before choosing how to join them.

Nothing was restored to a standard shape.

Nothing had to be grateful.

Most difficult were those lives in which wanting had no room to form. Here the power did not supply a desire of its own. It sheltered the faint distinction between one thing and another: this pressure and its easing, this proximity and a little distance, this light and shade.

It could wait without making waiting another demand.

***

The child’s hand is still spread against the animal’s leg.

Then one finger lifts.

The movement is so slight that you might have attributed it to your own changing angle. But a narrow shadow appears beneath the fingertip, and the shadow was not there before.

The child studies it.

The tall animal does not topple into hunger or age. The plateau does not crack. None of the small creatures beneath its belly is crushed by the arrival of a next thing.

The child lowers the finger.

For a while, that is all.

***

The power had been careful about this.

It had not released movement and left its consequences for the inhabitants to bear. Those who moved would not thereby begin to starve. Those who remained would not become obstacles. A body could change its arrangement without stealing another body’s necessary space.

Nor would departure, if anyone chose it, be a fall.

Beyond the turning place there was now a continuation. From the post you could see only its first few yards, a descending passage with a broad inner margin. Farther country would become visible by traveling, not by promise.

There would be help there.

There would also be a way back.

***

Beside the shelter, the seated person looks at your cuff.

You have the distinct, mildly embarrassing sensation of having been right about this all along.

Their thumb presses the short cord against the palm. The other fingers close enough to lift it.

They do not reach for you.

You turn your wrist. The loose stitch hangs where you can both see it.

After a moment you sit down—not quite where the stone seemed to suggest. Your troublesome knee extends farther than you intended, and you adjust your heel twice.

The maker waits for you to settle.

The needle is inside the shelter. There will be the small business of fetching it, of deciding whether the cord is suitable, of discovering that a repair to cloth is not quite like a repair to a bowl.

Perhaps you will do the sewing yourself.

Across the basin, innumerable animals remain in their particular ease. The six dusty leaves remain on the tree. At the edge, an old creature with a fastidious face stands beside a plant with one bitten stem.

The power does not withdraw its care when nothing spectacular follows.

The maker holds out the cord.

This time, the space between the two hands is something that can be crossed.
