The power does not begin by stopping the fall.

Below the edge, where the heat currents turn between the foundation columns, a carrier trunk is passing through the dark. Its outer bark is scored with shallow grooves. The animal lands across three of them, folds under the lip of the fourth, and is carried downward.

It has made this crossing before.

The power follows.

***

There are inhabited places beneath the inhabited places.

Animals lie along the undersides of galleries, their bristles turned upward into the loads. They pass through the walls of old compression beds. Some have coverings so thick with repairs that they can no longer enter the clearances in which they grew. These remain near exhaust junctions, living on what the current deposits.

Farther down, the strokes arrive separately. What seems at the surface to be a single immense contraction begins here as many movements, corrected and gathered together.

The crescent-backed animal leaves the carrier trunk where two of these movements nearly meet. It slips into a bearing gallery and begins to feed.

The power watches it empty a stranded bud.

Nothing in the act asks to be changed.

Elsewhere, an animal has entered a seam whose two sides are receiving different instructions. One wall is withdrawing. The other has begun to grow.

Its rear tongues find the error first. They flatten and pull. Its front half is already beyond a ridge of hardening tissue.

Varnish gathers along its edge.

The power holds one small part of the wall still.

There is no light, no new sound. For the animal, the passage merely fails to finish closing. It draws itself backward, leaving a mouth filament caught in the grain, and remains thickly folded on the open surface.

The power keeps the clearance until it moves away.

Then, before releasing the wall, it follows both instructions to their sources.

***

The works have ways of protecting themselves.

A bearing can ask a neighboring organ to delay its growth. A conduit can announce an approaching load. Injured tissue can divert pressure around a split before the split becomes visible.

These messages are old, exact, and local.

The animals live by borrowing them.

Their varnish is almost a word in the language of the machinery. Almost, because it asks without saying how long; almost, because the oldest walls have ceased to listen for that particular word. Under heavy load, a request for clearance is treated as an error to be overcome.

The power learns the language all the way down.

It does not make every wall soft. The columns are carrying miles of country. A careless mercy in one joint would arrive elsewhere as a crushing weight.

Instead, it gives the request somewhere to go.

Deep beside the foundations is an ancient return chamber, packed solid by growth that has been pressing against itself for longer than any living animal has existed. Its contents are still supplied, still trimmed, still repaired. They perform no task except to resist the next layer.

The power takes their load.

Over many strokes it unthreads the chamber, preserving each useful connection and carrying the displaced material away from the crowded works. Nothing is forced upward. No distant seam has to close to pay for the opening.

For the first time in a very long while, an addition is made without sending its cost through every neighboring wall.

What the power adds is an allowance.

The chamber becomes a place into which pressure can briefly retreat. Narrow living lines connect it to the machinery’s existing exchanges. When a clearance must remain open, the load that would have closed it can pass elsewhere and return later.

The first use is scarcely perceptible.

An animal crosses a junction.

The junction waits.

***

The power does not assume that waiting is enough.

It watches the hot oils, the electrical gradients, the microscopic stores of water. It watches which buds the animals empty and which they pass over. It learns the difference between a body resting and a body that can no longer move.

The coverings conceal much. The bristles conceal almost nothing.

Pain has its own disruptions: contacts repeated without useful information, contractions that spend the last reserves without changing the animal’s position. There are individuals living for days in such patterns, fastened into the bark by one injured fold.

Where it can release them, it does.

A repair takes place beneath a covering, in darkness familiar to the body. Torn tissue receives oil and the materials from which it would have rebuilt itself, had there been time and room. The power supplies both.

Some animals leave immediately.

Others remain where they are, testing the repaired part through several strokes before trusting it.

One old individual, freed from a root that has grown through its underside, returns to the same hollow. The power does not move it again. The hollow is warm, well supplied, and known.

It keeps the root from entering a second time.

***

The changes spread through routes already used for maintenance.

A closing seam acquires a way to feel contact before it crushes what is inside. An animal’s secretion becomes a request the oldest tissue can recognize. Where postponement is impossible, the strain announces itself earlier, giving the bristles time to find another course.

Not every touch holds a district motionless. The works still turn. Loads are handed onward. Bearings renew themselves under immense pressure.

But a small body no longer has to purchase every delay from its own dwindling glands.

The power remains through the consequences.

There are more animals surviving the crossings. There are more mouths at the unfinished margins. At several junctions, useful linings begin to fail.

The power does not answer this by taking back the clearances.

It opens a few of the fused loading mouths.

Beyond them, supported independently of the old columns, it grows exchange beds from material it brings itself. They are hot, dark, close-grained places, threaded with moving seams. Their buds are nourished without being needed by a pump or bearing. Feeding there leaves no socket unable to wake.

The first animals to find them turn back.

The loads are unfamiliar.

For a while the beds remain almost empty.

Then a small individual enters, withdraws, and enters again. It feeds near the threshold. Several strokes later, another settles beside it.

The power makes no attempt to hurry them.

***

At a junction near the surface, a parent unfolds to release its young.

The last leaves late. One of its rear tongues remains caught against the pocket wall, and the parent holds itself open while the next stroke begins beneath them.

Previously, this would have been a choice made by tissue rather than intention: close and trap the young, or remain spread until the growth underneath took hold.

Now the ground receives the pressure and passes it down.

The junction stays loose.

The young animal frees itself. It waits under the edge of the parent’s covering, touching a dozen bristles to the familiar underside.

Then it goes.

For a long time the parent does not move.

When at last its folds begin to gather, there is room for all of them.

***

The crescent-backed animal returns to the upper plateau by a route you never see.

You find it near the old loading mouths, where a passage has opened onto a broad shelf in the darkness.

There is no green there. No cool rain falls. The power has not mistaken the needs of this place for those of another.

Heat moves through closely fitted walls. Under the shelf, a deep chamber takes a load and releases it slowly. Its rhythm is related to the plateau’s, but less crowded.

The animal tests the threshold.

It spreads its rear tongues across the old bark while the front advances onto the new. For several strokes it remains between them.

Then it brings the rest of its body forward.

It does not stay forever. Later it returns to the worn load lines, feeds beside a tower, waits at a junction. Its routes have become longer. Sometimes it passes beyond the loading mouth; sometimes it does not.

Behind it, the opening remains.

***

The power could leave a machine to maintain what it has done.

It does leave such machinery: independent supports, reserves that do not have to be stolen from living chambers, ways for the works to register that a refuge is occupied before reclaiming it.

But it also stays.

There are depths it has not yet understood. There are old signals whose meanings changed as the country grew over them. There may be needs that have never reached a surface.

At intervals, the plateau still becomes completely quiet.

The animals spread themselves along the paths.

A pulse arrives from below, and thousands of bodies move through the permissions of the world.

One crosses late.

The wall beside it pauses, carrying its pressure elsewhere.

Its last tongue clears the seam.

Only then does the passage close.
