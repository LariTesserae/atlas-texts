The first thing the power does is keep its light to itself.

It has noticed the hand shading the animals beneath the basin. It has noticed, too, how little light is required to make the inhabitant’s eyes withdraw behind their cloudy surfaces.

It arrives as a small interruption in the view from the terrace: a patch through which one distant ridge is no longer visible.

The inhabitant leans to one side.

The patch moves aside.

It leans the other way. The patch does not follow.

For a while this is sufficient communication.

***

The power could examine all six buildings without entering them. Some kinds of examination, however, would be felt through the inhabitant’s hands when it next touched the floor. Others would pass through the corner posts and have consequences far below the summit.

It begins with what can be learned without those disturbances.

The water is holding. The mountain is not about to break. The two animals beneath the basin are alive, and the difference in their sizes is not an injury.

The inhabitant’s exposed hip seam is more troublesome.

When it turns in a doorway, the blue tissue draws tight against an edge of plate. At rest, the tissue slowly relaxes. There is discomfort, although not always, and no present way for it to lie down without putting strain somewhere.

The power does not need to understand the smallest building to understand this.

It follows the inhabitant home, remaining outside the doorway.

The inhabitant takes the bent metallic strip from the floor and lays it across the entrance.

The power stays on the other side.

Later, the inhabitant moves the strip farther out, across the landing.

The power moves back.

The strip remains there for several visits.

***

The first thing it offers is a piece of material about the size of a shed joint plate.

It places this outside the strip.

Under pressure, the piece gives a little. Released, it keeps its new shape. Its edges cannot cut. It has no scent or taste that the inhabitant finds disagreeable, and nothing in it will enter the water to trouble the animals.

The inhabitant takes it upstairs.

For some time the power sees no result.

Then the piece appears in the lamp housing above the door, wedged beside the old plate.

The power leaves it there.

Several visits later, the inhabitant brings it down again. It has bent the piece into a shallow hook. It tries the hook against the courtyard flake, finds it useless as a lever, and strikes it once on the paving.

The piece flattens.

This is more interesting.

By the end of the next interval of wakefulness, it has been folded around the sharp edge of the terrace seat.

The inhabitant rests its hand on it.

The power offers a second piece.

***

It takes longer to do anything about the low frame.

A change made during sleep would be easy. The inhabitant would wake with its heels no longer cramped beneath it.

But it has few enough things it can rely on finding where it left them.

Instead, the power makes a length of the same yielding material outside the house. Beside it, it places two short supports. It demonstrates, once, how the supports can hold the length at the height of the frame.

The inhabitant separates all three objects.

One support goes into a compartment. The other is carried to the water boundary and held against the mountains.

The long piece goes upstairs.

When the inhabitant next settles on its frame, it puts the piece beneath the badly fitting hip. That helps one pressure and worsens another. It gets up, turns the piece over, and tries again.

The power waits outside the landing.

Eventually the inhabitant comes to the doorway, takes hold of the interruption in the water, and pulls.

For this purpose the power allows itself a hand.

It is not quite a good hand. The inhabitant examines it, finds no joints to inspect, and pulls again.

Upstairs, it pushes the hand against the frame.

Now the power acts.

Slowly enough to be interrupted, it draws the foot of the frame farther from the head. The inhabitant catches the moving end almost immediately.

The power stops.

The inhabitant feels underneath. It hooks one heel beneath the altered part, withdraws it, and pushes the end a little farther.

The power holds the frame while it does this.

They leave the old surface intact. The addition is visibly an addition. Beneath it are two broad catches that the inhabitant can operate itself.

That night, the seams along its ribs open without the left leg having to turn outward first.

***

At the basin, the power learns a different caution.

It watches the inhabitant reach through the resistant surface. It sees the effort, the narrowing chest, the fingers that come back needing examination.

The white object is within the power’s reach.

It leaves it where it is.

It learns what the boundary does to a hand, what the object does to the boundary, and what would follow if either were forced. It distinguishes the parts that are difficult from the parts that could cause lasting harm.

Then it makes a little resistance in the water beside itself.

The inhabitant ignores this.

The power puts its hand through. Withdraws it. Changes the angle, tries again.

The inhabitant turns away from the basin.

It watches two more attempts before catching the power’s wrist and turning it sharply.

The resistance parts.

The power keeps its hand still.

The inhabitant examines the opening it has made, then the hand, then the opening again.

On its next attempt at the basin, it changes the angle of one wrist.

It reaches no deeper. But it does not have to wrench itself free.

Afterward, it goes under the rim to inspect the animals. The power waits until it is finished before offering another place to practice.

***

There are absences here that the power does not fill with imitations.

It does not put a figure in the empty seat merely because the inhabitant sometimes looks toward it. It does not supply a face on the blank wall. It does not give the shallow roof a voice.

It searches, though.

Beyond the summit, beyond the visible mountains, it looks for the makers of the latches and the fitted heel recesses. It follows traces that have no counterpart in ordinary distance. Some end. Some divide into possibilities so old that finding the right one will require patience even of this power.

It will not bring back something merely similar and call the search complete.

Meanwhile, when the inhabitant pushes it into the middle seat, it sits.

It gives the seat weight enough to register, but nothing more until it understands what is being asked of that weight.

The inhabitant takes the seat on the left.

They face the wall.

Once, the inhabitant turns and finds the power already looking at it. The cloudy patches clear.

The power turns back toward the wall.

They remain there until the inhabitant chooses to leave.

***

In time, there is a way down.

It is not cut through the paving. No water is released, and none of the metallic strips is severed.

The power places the beginning of the way beyond an unused portion of the square’s edge: a broad, shallow step enclosed in water continuous with the town’s. Below it is another step, and below that a landing. The route can extend toward the mountains, but at first there are only these three surfaces.

The inhabitant discovers them while carrying the perforated spoon.

It crouches at the edge.

It puts the spoon through the place where the water formerly ended. Then its fingers. Then the broad contacts of one foot.

It withdraws.

For a long time it uses the new steps only to look back at the square.

From the landing, the buildings overlap differently. A window in its house is visible beneath the steep roof. The plate in the entrance lamp cannot be seen, but the inhabitant repeatedly changes position as if it ought to be.

The power does not extend the way farther.

One day the inhabitant goes down carrying the bent strip, the spoon, and a small joint crescent. It puts them on the landing at considerable distances from one another.

There is not enough room.

It looks over the next edge.

The power gives it another step.

***

The town remains mostly dark.

The compartment latches keep their separate uses. The chipped step stays chipped. The inhabitant still worries at the courtyard flake, and on one occasion succeeds in breaking a piece from the wrong side of it.

It is furious.

The power does not restore the stone.

Later, it holds the bent strip steady while the inhabitant examines the newly exposed hinge.

There are now two hand-shaped depressions in the yielding cover on the terrace seat. One is deep and narrow. The other is an early mistake, made when the power was still learning how much weight a companion should have.

When cloud rises from the valley, neither hand is there. The inhabitant is leaning forward, combs spread, both palms flat against the seat.

Beside it, the power has made itself no larger than necessary.

The cloud fills the valley and passes between the pale ridges. For a while there is nothing to see through the water but moving white.

Afterward, the inhabitant reaches beneath the roof.

Its fingers find the roughness.

The power knows now what made that mark. It is still looking for a way to show what it knows without replacing the mark, or the gesture, with something of its own.

The inhabitant rubs the surface, checks its fingertips, and stands.

At the passage it pauses.

It comes back, takes the power’s hand, and turns it over. With one cupped fingertip it touches the center of the palm.

Then it leads the power home.
