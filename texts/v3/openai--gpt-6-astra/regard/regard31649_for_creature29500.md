The power would not begin by opening the panel.

An opening is a considerable event when the weather on the other side of it has never entered the world.

It would attend first to the things that held the chamber steady: the suspension, the electrical supplies, the compressors whose precise operation reached into the chemistry of the blades. It would follow the load-bearing members downward, through the occupied floors, to the foundations. It would learn which maintenance schedules included the basin and which had forgotten it. It would find the designation under which the living tissue had been purchased.

There would be a line somewhere allowing for replacement.

The power would make sure that line could no longer authorize an unexamined death.

This would be its first intervention. Nothing inside the chamber would register it.

Its next work would take longer.

The power would be wise enough not to mistake an environment terrible to a visitor for an environment terrible to its inhabitant. It would not warm the ceramic. It would not bring water to the roots, earth to the hollows, or silence to the blades. The enormous sound would remain.

But it would also be careful with the opposite mistake. Dependence on a condition does not establish that the condition is good. A creature may need something that costs it dearly. The fact that every compartment is maintained does not say whether maintaining them is easy.

So it would study the cost.

Not merely the electricity consumed, nor the efficiency recorded during commissioning. It would attend to the unfinished bridge, where the local field continually sustained a distinction that growth could never close. It would attend to the work by which the flat growth kept agreement with the tall one. It would distinguish, as far as it could, between a state the organism restored because that state suited it and a state it restored because every other available state led to damage.

It would not use injury to clarify the distinction.

There were other things to learn first.

Beyond the basin, it would prepare continuance.

The compressors were flawless, but not immortal. The building was strong, but strength did not exempt it from demolition orders, changing uses, fire, or neglect. What looked from within like an absolute condition was, outside, a collection of expiring arrangements.

The power would give those arrangements successors.

It would learn to sustain the pressure differences at every blade without requiring the same shafts to turn forever. Not a recording of the chamber’s noise: a recording made at one point would erase the differences between twenty places. It would preserve the different acoustic conditions at the hooked growth, the flat growth, the narrow pair by the wall. It would prepare supplies that could assume a failing contact’s work without an interval of equalization. It would arrange that the cold could persist through a summer blackout and that no well-intentioned technician could mistake the lamps for an unnecessary expense.

None of these substitutes would be switched on merely to demonstrate that they worked.

The originals would continue while they served.

Outside the panel, where a person turning sideways might otherwise put a hand, there would eventually be a notice. It would be plain and technically useful. It would identify a living system, specify the hazards of moisture and interrupted power, and direct any proposed work to someone responsible for its care.

It would not describe the chamber as empty.

Then the power would address the work for which the organism had been made.

Somewhere in the building, equipment depended upon the differences it maintained. Those dependencies would be traced and, carefully, provided for by other means. The living body’s electrical surroundings would remain unchanged. Its contacts would still receive and answer as before. But the building would cease to require that this particular life remain this particular shape.

It might never change shape.

Still, if change became possible, it would no longer have to compete with the lifts, the cooling systems, or the convenience of everyone below.

This, too, would at first be imperceptible.

The power would then try to become less than an obscure local fault.

It would not speak into the chamber. It would not dim a lamp in a pattern devised for eyes that recognized symbols. Its first efforts would concern the narrow domain in which the organism already distinguished one condition from another.

At a contact far from the unfinished bridge, there might be a variation so small that it fell within the range the tissue encountered without compensatory strain. The power would allow for the organism’s slowness. It would wait through exchanges that a technician’s instrument might average into one stable reading.

If the variation produced only repair, it would stop.

If it produced a differentiated response, the power would not immediately call that an answer.

A blade could answer a field as a strip of metal answered heat.

What mattered was whether, over time, the organism could do something with the encounter besides endure it. Could it distinguish an offered variation from a disturbance? Could it influence what happened next? Could it end the exchange without losing anything it needed?

The power would make that last condition reliable before attempting anything more elaborate.

Perhaps no communication would become possible. Benevolence would not entitle the visitor to an acknowledgment.

Perhaps, after a very long interval, a pattern at the flat growth would recur when the power withdrew, and cease when it returned. Even then, there would be much to understand. Return might be useful without being welcome. A repeated state might signify interest, or merely a new way of balancing an old expense.

The power could afford patience.

It would not make the organism more complicated in order to obtain a companion. It would not give it a hunger for distances it could not traverse. Nor would it decide that its present narrowness was sacred and must never be exceeded.

If another mode of living could be offered safely, it would prepare the conditions for a small, reversible trial: not a new world suddenly imposed, but an adjacent possibility, carrying no penalty for refusal.

Until there was reason to offer it, the bare hollows would remain bare.

The hooked growth would keep its obsolete attachment. No one would straighten it into efficiency. The older compartments would retain their irregular proportions. The power would understand that a history need not be useful to deserve a place in the body that had survived it.

And if, after all this attention, no suffering was found, no preference established, no latent wish to grow disclosed, the power would not manufacture a tragedy to justify a rescue.

It would keep the provision it had made.

Under the workshop lamps, the clear edge would remain below the conductor. The fastener would throw its narrow reflection. Sound would enter each blade according to its shape and position.

Most of the visible chamber might never change.

But years later, when a bearing elsewhere finally began to fail, the organism would not have to discover failure as a new and fatal weather. When the building’s use changed, its life would not be included among the fixtures to be stripped out. If one of its slow internal alterations opened a possibility no commissioning drawing had allowed for, there would be time to notice.

The power would remain attentive to such alterations.

It would have learned that, here, an event could be smaller than a new cell, and still require an answer.
