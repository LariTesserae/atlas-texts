The power first becomes aware of the valley through the water.

There is a great deal of it being held where water does not ordinarily remain. To hold it this way requires neither strength nor continual attention, but a precise arrangement of conditions. The arrangement is old. Some of its provisions have never been used. Others now sustain things its makers did not anticipate: worms in the mineral ribs, trees rooted in the receiving basins, fish whose entire lives pass within a few yards of the open sky.

The power follows these dependencies inward.

It does not straighten the hanging falls.

At the western towers it finds the atmosphere secured by several overlapping means. The seven visible structures are indeed seven. There are others beneath the copper escarpment, and one laid horizontally far below the central lake. None is close to failing. Two have spent a long time compensating for a change in the buried stone.

The power studies that change through an afternoon.

Near the closest platform, the dark-coated male lifts his head from his forelimbs.

A band of shadow begins to climb.

He gets up, takes a partly eaten seed case from the hollow, and carries it beyond the moss line. The power waits until he has settled before examining the tower more closely.

There is a way to relieve the strain. There are several ways. The easiest would alter the warmth of the western shallows by a small amount.

In those shallows, the swollen reeds are full of larvae.

The power chooses another way.

For three nights, deep beneath the valley, a pressure moves through uninhabited rock. Nothing on the surface rises or falls. The submerged lanes remain at the same depth. The tower’s afternoon disturbance keeps its familiar approach and duration; the male still knows when to leave.

What changes is farther inside. A narrow burden becomes a broad one. An unused reserve becomes available again.

The power remains through the next rain to make sure.

Then it notices the old female taking her daughter’s wrist into her mouth.

It has already noticed her several times: crossing the causeway with a white leaf base held beneath one arm, sleeping with one eye incompletely shut, standing at the opening while birds move through the future above her.

But this is the first time it gives its whole attention to the place between her shoulders.

The roots there are fine and numerous. Some have entered small breaks in the skin. Beneath them, the flesh is inflamed. The daughter can remove the plants, but not without pulling at living tissue.

The power could make the roots cease to exist.

Instead it watches the old female release the wrist.

The daughter waits. Then she resumes, choosing a smaller plant.

There is information in the waiting.

That night, the power takes a shape in the empty room across the passage.

It chooses something low enough not to block the doorway, with no mouth and no forward-facing eyes. For a time it resembles a smooth, damp stone. Then it makes a hand, because hands have uses here that a stone does not.

This is not immediately successful.

The first animal to see it gives two clicks and climbs onto a bench. The old female wakes. Her daughter stands across the entrance to the sleeping room, tail stiff against the floor.

The power withdraws the hand.

It stays where it is.

By morning, the animals have established that the new thing neither approaches food nor pursues anyone. A youngster throws a reed husk at it. Later the youngster retrieves the husk.

The old female passes it on her way outside, nostrils closing one after the other.

On the second evening she touches it.

Her fingers rest against its surface for less than a breath. Through the floor, the power offers the faintest possible repetition of that contact: touch, absence, touch.

She puts her chin down.

It answers once more.

Over the following days, they acquire a few understandings. A hand held away means no further approach. Pressure can be returned without being increased. When she removes her hand, the contact ends.

The power does not try to teach her its name.

She has already given it a practical identity. It is the cool thing in the second room that sometimes answers.

Her daughter is the first to bring her back against it.

This happens accidentally, or almost accidentally. The younger animal is investigating the junction between the thing and the floor. She rolls partly onto her side and presses a patch of irritated skin against its edge.

The itching stops.

She rolls farther.

The power eases out one thread of root, leaving the skin beneath it whole.

At the unfamiliar sensation, she scrambles away.

Nothing follows her.

She returns before the rain begins.

The old female takes longer. She watches two treatments without approaching, then sleeps in the doorway where she can keep the thing in view. When at last she presents her shoulders, she does so with her head turned and her teeth visible.

The power touches one small patch.

She seizes its hand.

It becomes still.

Her grip loosens. After a while, she lowers her head.

Removing the plants takes most of the evening. It need not take that long, but there are pauses to scratch, pauses to look toward the causeway, a lengthy interruption during which the old female leaves to retrieve something from behind the bench.

She comes back carrying the blue fragment.

The power knows the material. It knows something of the object from which the fragment came. There is a larger shape it could restore, a use it could return to usefulness.

The female settles with the curved piece against her belly.

The power leaves it as it is.

When the last root is gone, the coat between her shoulders lies slightly thinner than the surrounding fibers. The skin is clean. She can bend without feeling it draw tight.

Her daughter inspects the place with both hands.

Finding little work to do, she combs through the coat farther down.

—

The power does not leave a dispenser in the room.

It makes a shallow surface beneath the bench, where an animal can press an itching flank or an injured hand. The surface yields slightly. Its help begins at contact and stops when the animal pulls away. It cannot be exhausted by a season of sickness, and it does not require the towers to remember an additional task.

Before making it permanent, the power watches who gets to use it.

The old female tries to drive away a male with an infected foot. He waits outside until she sleeps. This works once, but the next afternoon she wakes and drives him off again.

So there are more surfaces: one beneath a mineral shelf, another in a hollow among the buttress roots, several in resting places around the lake. They smell of the stone or wood around them. None requires an animal to cross a tower platform or surrender a sleeping place.

The male finds the one beneath the shelf.

For two days he rests his foot against it, lifts it, examines it, and rests it there again.

The power keeps watch over the water downstream.

—

There are other things it considers.

The old female’s hind joints hurt after long swims. That pain can be eased without taking away the warning of a strained limb. Her worn teeth still serve her. Her eyes are clear. She does not need to become young in order to have a good morning.

The opening is harder to reach than it was a year ago.

One evening a low, broad stone lies beneath it, not touching her bed. She smells it and goes around. For three days she climbs the old way.

On the fourth, she sets one hind foot upon it.

After that, the scratches in the wall stop deepening.

The power cannot tell whether she understands where these changes come from. She sometimes carries food into the second room and eats beside it. Once she puts a discarded organ on its smooth upper surface. A small green animal descends from the wall toward the offering.

Her hand moves very quickly.

The power does not mistake the meal for a ceremony.

—

When it eventually withdraws its visible shape, the old female searches the room.

She touches the floor where it rested. She checks behind the door and beneath the bench, though the thing would not have fitted there.

The power answers through the stone: touch, absence, touch.

She settles on her haunches.

It will not watch everything. The valley has depths of event that need not be witnessed, and its inhabitants have no use for an audience at every feeding, quarrel, and sleep. But a failure of the towers will reach it. So will harm caused by what it has left behind.

And here, in this room, contact can still be answered.

The old female sits for some time with her hand on the floor.

Then there are steps on the causeway.

She lifts her chin, recognizes the uneven rhythm, and goes to meet her daughter.

Outside, the rain has stopped. Water slips from the roof into the shallow lake. Far below, lightning opens inside a cloud, too distant for its thunder to reach the island.

The two animals pause in the doorway, touching at the shoulders.

After a while they go down into the reeds.
