The pot is the first thing the power notices.

Not because it is important in the usual ways. The district contains larger vessels, some holding enough pressure to alter a season if they fail. There are doors opening onto distances that have not been measured, and beneath the central hollow something turns against a bearing worn almost through.

But the pot has been placed on a landing that did not exist the day before. Someone has carried it out, found a position where it will not obstruct a foot, and turned its chipped side toward the wall.

The power attends to this for a while.

Then it attends to the people who put it there.

It does not at first make itself visible. Visibility here is a complicated matter, and arriving as light would be particularly careless. In the cupboards, children are sleeping.

Instead it learns the district’s small distinctions: which water can be drunk, which silence follows a question, which cups are left for someone expected and which for someone who is no longer expected but must still have a cup.

This last distinction takes time.

The power has time. What it must learn is when the residents do not.

*

Three evenings later, the woman finds a dark thread hanging in her own doorway.

She stops with one foot raised.

The thread is not hers. It is tied in a knot she does not recognize, to a hook she has been meaning to replace. Beyond it, the smaller resident sits at the table sorting the contents of a tin into several categories, none of them apparently useful.

She tells him to stay where he is.

Then she fetches her straightedge.

The sill is sound. The blue cord passes through without shortening. Going around by the staircase takes the expected number of breaths, and when she returns, the room contains the same afternoon.

She takes out the bell.

On each side of the door it makes a small, ordinary sound.

At last she touches the thread.

It comes loose at once.

Behind it, impressed very shallowly in the enamel, are the marks she uses for an opening whose far side has not yet been examined.

Below them is a question mark.

It is not quite the right question mark. It resembles one copied by someone who knows what questions are but has never needed to write one.

The woman sits on the threshold.

After a while she says, “Which side?”

The voice that answers is quiet enough not to wake anyone in the cupboards.

“Yours.”

*

She does not invite it in that day.

She asks it to hold the other end of a cord.

The power does this.

For six mornings, she gives it work that can be checked. It holds a straightedge, waits beside an opening, carries a basket around by a route she has already measured. It learns that when she says wait, she sometimes means several hours.

On the seventh morning she takes it to the seam where she leaves the cup.

The cup stands upright. It has been washed recently.

“Can you tell?” she asks.

The power examines the wall without opening it.

Behind the outer ceramic is an old room, folded along three incompatible directions. Its remaining air is sufficient. There is water, though reaching it has become difficult. A resident lies awake on a bed whose foot has entered the ceiling.

The power could pull the room open.

It could restore the vanished corridor, or draw the occupant through the wall as easily as the grazing animals withdraw their heads.

Instead it says, “Someone is there.”

“I know that.”

“Yes,” says the power.

She keeps her fingertips against the seam.

“Can they hear?”

“Not from here.”

“Then make that possible.”

A small place beneath her fingers becomes warm.

She bends toward it. For a long time she says nothing. The power waits beside the cup.

At last she says a name.

Something shifts within the wall.

*

The first request from inside is not to be rescued.

It is that the woman stop leaving the cup on the higher shelf.

The shelf tilts during the press stroke. Reaching it has become troublesome, and two cups have already been lost.

The woman closes her eyes.

They argue quietly for nearly an hour.

The power hears enough to understand that the room is not the whole reason the resident stayed. It stops listening to the words after that, keeping only the passage open.

By afternoon, an arrangement has been reached.

The room will remain where it is. Its occupant will have a door that opens safely onto the orchard service terrace, provided the door can be closed from inside. No one is to come through without knocking. The old window, although it looks onto nothing accessible, is not to be replaced.

“Can you do that?” the woman asks.

“Yes.”

“And the wall?”

“It will have to grow around the room.”

“Will that put pressure somewhere else?”

The power looks again.

This is the first question she has asked that makes it alter its plan.

*

News travels at the pace of visits.

Within a week, people bring requests to the orchard terrace. Some concern rooms. Many concern sleep.

A warm conduit has begun giving off a sensation like a word almost remembered. A freight lift takes one resident reliably to work but will not bring him home. Someone wants a patch of flooring retrieved from a wall that has been solid for forty years.

Others come only to look.

The shadow workers arrive carrying their broad blades wrapped in felt. They ask the power not to improve the illumination.

It agrees.

They ask what it has already done.

It shows them.

They spend a considerable time examining the little speaking-place at the seam. One runs a blade around its edge.

“This leaks,” she says.

Not air. Not light.

The private darkness of the room is thinning slightly each time a voice passes through.

The power repairs it under their supervision.

Afterward, the workers leave it a small piece of shadow in a covered dish, so it can learn the feel of a healthy one.

*

It would be easy to stop the factories.

The power considers this when it finds the worn bearing beneath the basin. A single failure there would send a change through every threshold on the lower terraces.

But the warm pipes feed households. New channels carry water to nests that did not exist last season. The district’s slow manufacture is entangled with almost everything that lives in it.

So the bearing is repaired.

During the work, the great machine is supported without being lifted. Not a cup trembles. The families on the polished pipes cross their couplings as usual.

The worn component is brought to the surface and left beside the maintenance house.

Residents gather around it.

There are tools in the district for such a repair, but no route by which they could have reached the bearing. The power makes a service passage. Two workers insist on walking its entire length before accepting it.

One asks whether the passage will remain.

“Yes.”

He presses his own mark into the soft plate beside the entrance.

*

At the next evening stroke, the beads of water stand up on the rails.

A woman on the lower terraces has her hand against a cupboard back. A child is asleep on the other side.

The wall begins to move.

Then it stops.

Not the whole district. The press completes its work. New paving advances at the eastern mouths. Far away, an empty warehouse loses another narrow portion of its floor.

Only this wall waits.

In the morning there is a shallow recess beside the household’s door. Inside are three ceramic pieces that can be moved by hand.

The threshold woman comes to explain them. Then she stays to hear why the arrangement is no good.

The pieces are too high for one of the occupants. Their marks can be mistaken for storage tallies. And waiting until the wall begins to move is no way to give notice.

The second version is better.

Before installing a third, the power asks several households to try it.

Eventually, rooms approaching withdrawal acquire a notice residents can read, a means to ask for help, and a way to make the wall wait. People still move. Some want larger windows, or a warmer floor, or to be nearer someone whose stairs have become difficult.

Their worn patches of flooring go with them.

People who stay can still be visited.

*

There are things the power cannot return.

At certain seams it finds only ceramic. Elsewhere it finds a room, but no one alive within it.

It does not wash those cups.

When asked, it tells what it has found, carefully and without making the answer larger than its knowledge. Sometimes it carries out a wrapped object. Sometimes it accompanies a resident through an opening and waits while they choose what to bring back.

One cup remains on its shelf long after the shelf has ceased to serve any practical purpose.

The wall grows around it.

*

The smaller resident is disappointed that the power has not dealt with the cart carrying the upright door.

It still stops at intersections.

After several complaints, the power examines it and discovers that the cart is waiting for a passenger. The destination on its plate no longer exists. No instruction has told it what to do when both these things are true.

“Can you tell it?” the smaller resident asks.

“I can.”

“Then tell it.”

The cart is given somewhere safe to wait, off the crossing. Its door remains upright. A bench is placed nearby, and soon someone uses the glass shelter of its load to start seedlings.

This is not the solution the smaller resident had imagined.

He comes to like it when the first leaves appear.

*

Above the orchard, rain continues to fall from the next landing every afternoon.

The pot catches a little of it.

One evening the woman returns early enough to sit outside. The smaller resident is beside her, trying to attach a handle to a cup made from pipe. He has drilled the holes unevenly.

The power holds the cup while he works.

Across the basin, the press descends the last small distance.

There is a click.

The woman pauses, listening through her fingertips to the railing. Then she reaches for the loose handle before it rolls into the drain.

For the first time in many evenings, she has forgotten to check the wall beneath the bed.

She remembers later.

It is still worth checking. The district is large, and the work is not finished.

But she finishes her drink first.
