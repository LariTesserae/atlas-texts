The first thing the power did was keep a sleeve from entering the sliding joint.

It did not pull the animal backward. The sleeve was attached in six places, and pulling would have torn three of them before the others yielded. Instead it held the two faces of the joint apart by the thickness of a fingernail until Mara came outside.

She noticed the obstruction because the cradle would not quite seat.

She checked the track, the hydraulic line, the return sensor. Then she saw the clear tissue stretched between the faces.

For a moment she stood with her cutting tool in her hand.

The sleeve had passed through the joint and attached on the far side. Clouded fluid moved along it in both directions. Beyond it, sheltered beneath a flange, several new feeding cups were open.

Mara put the tool away. She unbolted the smaller guide plate and worked the sleeve free.

It took most of an hour. The animal whitened slowly around her gloves.

Throughout that hour, the power held the cradle still.

***

It had come prepared to do something immense.

From beyond the world, the mountain province had seemed a simple wound: cold stone made to ring without respite, animals pressed into the shapes of disturbance, people sleeping inside engines.

There were things it could have done before Mara finished loosening the first bolt.

It could have stopped every turbine. It could have raised the ridges through the surface. It could have given the water a different way to carry sound.

Instead, while she worked, it followed the cables.

Some ended in machinery no one needed anymore. Some supplied other stations, and those supplied others. Far from the plateau, beneath water too deep for sunlight, a hospital took its power from the province. So did a nursery, a set of pumps, a room where someone was keeping the last viable seeds of a plant that no longer grew anywhere.

The power followed the water too.

At one loud crossing, a band of feeding animals hung in a standing wave. Farther down, a white swimmer was moving toward them. The swimmer had followed that route for years. Within a fault, old branching tissue stretched through a passage the engineers had never found.

Stopping the machines would change the pressure there.

The power withdrew its intention to stop them all at once.

This was not the same as deciding to leave them running.

***

It made itself visible beside the station’s clear bunk.

The cook found it first.

He had a folded sheet under one arm. He looked at the stranger, then at the dry floor beneath its feet, then at the hatch indicator.

The power raised a hand.

It knew the official maintenance signals. It knew the language on the displays. It did not yet know what the cook meant by holding his sheet a little farther from his body.

He set it down and signed, very completely:

*Who admitted you?*

The power answered on the green display beside the door.

*No one. I should have asked.*

The cook considered this.

Then he went to fetch Mara.

***

They did not ask for silence first.

They asked whether the pressure hull was sound. Whether something had happened elsewhere. Whether the power had come for the station, the turbines, or one of them.

When these questions had been answered, the crawler technician asked whether it could repair the damage inside his left wrist.

It could.

He made it explain how before letting it begin.

Mara watched him move his fingers afterward. He flexed them once, then again, with the irritated expression of someone looking for a fault that ought to be there.

The power offered to ease the noise.

The cook pointed down.

*That tells me when the circulation pump is wrong.*

An older resident touched the wall beside her.

*That tells me where I am when the lights fail.*

Mara signed:

*Can you take away what hurts and leave what we use?*

The power did not answer immediately.

This improved her opinion of it.

***

They began with the spare bunk.

Not the whole compartment. Just the space inside its straps, from pillow to foot rail.

The power prevented the panels from passing their shaking into that small volume. It left the air at the same pressure and temperature. Beside the pillow it fitted a plate that could carry the station’s familiar tremor into a resting hand. The plate had a control large enough to find without looking.

It added a light and a gentle pull at the straps for alarms.

Then it stepped away.

No one used the bunk that night.

The next afternoon the crawler technician lay down in it for eleven minutes. When he sat up, he was pale.

He wrote on his screen, erased it, and wrote again.

*I could hear myself swallowing.*

He did not say whether this was good.

The cook tried it after his watch. He slept for four hours and woke furious that nobody had called him for a meal he was supposed to prepare.

Mara’s first attempt lasted less than a minute.

With the station absent from her bones, she had the sensation that the entire structure had broken loose and was descending.

She put her palm on the plate. The floor returned to her, small enough to fit beneath a hand.

The power waited nearby, without watching her face.

On her fourth visit, she slept.

***

Outside, its work was less certain.

It could follow the whitening through the branching animal. It could distinguish a feeding response from an injury response. It could perceive the altered chemistry where a sleeve was being stretched too hard.

None of this told it what being the animal was like.

It did not invent an answer.

For several weeks it attended to the individual beneath the cradle. Food passed through the cups. Tissue thickened along a sheltered brace. An old passage narrowed while a newer one took its flow. Fish entered and left.

The power saw that the attachment nearest the sliding joint was not especially rich in food. It was simply the next available place to hold.

With Mara’s agreement, it fitted a curved bar beneath the frame.

The bar was rough along its lower surface and clear of every moving part. It led away from the intake toward a sheltered opening through which food already drifted. Mara checked the cradle through its full travel before approving it.

The animal did not use the bar.

For nineteen days, it grew in three other directions.

On the twentieth, a transparent edge touched the roughened surface, withdrew, and touched it again.

Mara was asleep.

The power did not wake her.

By the time she next went outside, the attachment was firm.

***

There was no single quieting of the mountains.

There were trials, and mistakes small enough to undo.

At one station, a change in the turbine housing reduced the grinding note without altering the flow. At another, the same change made a distant cavern louder. The power restored the second housing and tried something else.

It gave the hospital a source of power that did not depend on water moving through a fault. It found the people responsible for the seed room and did the same there. It did not require them to understand the mountain province before helping it.

Stations ceased supplying abandoned equipment.

Governors learned speeds they had not been built to hold.

The work took time, though less time than it would have taken hands alone. Where waiting served nothing, the power did not wait. It repaired damaged nerves when people wanted them repaired. It relieved pain. It made failing seals sound before anyone had to stand a watch beside them.

Where creatures had built their feeding grounds around a standing wave, it proceeded differently.

Some bands remained. Small, local instruments held them in place while the immense machinery behind them changed. In other places, food could be carried just as reliably by a gentler current. The power watched whole breeding cycles before removing the old support.

It kept the deep warm seams open.

It did not plant gardens on the summits. There was no need to make the pale rock resemble a country it had known elsewhere.

***

People continued to sign.

In the first compartment quiet enough for conversation, three residents discovered that they disliked one another’s speaking voices. This became a source of lasting amusement.

The crawler technician played a recording without captions. He listened to a woman begin a sentence twice and abandon it.

When Mara came in, he turned it off.

She told him he did not have to.

He turned it on again, more softly.

Later, the cook opened the fungus cabinet during a meal. The smell came out as it always had.

For the first time, they also heard the faint rasp of the tray along its runners.

Everyone looked toward it.

The cook, who had been preparing a remark, forgot what it was.

***

The animal beneath the cradle spread along the new bar.

It still approached the sliding joint sometimes. Mara still carried her cutting tool. There were limits to what a better attachment could change in a creature that grew by reaching.

But the intervals became longer.

One day she found a sleeve crossing the old danger point and traced it with her lamp. The sleeve had not attached inside the mechanism. It had passed behind the guide plate, followed the bar, and joined a branch on the other side.

She checked it twice.

Then she clipped the tool back to her suit.

A little fish emerged between the branches and held itself beside her glove. Without the old grinding note, she could feel a different vibration through the cradle: brief, irregular taps from somewhere inside the framework.

For a moment she thought a fastening had loosened.

Then a pale crab came into view, picking its way along the steel.

***

The power stayed within reach.

It did not occupy the spare bunk, which was no longer always spare. It did not put its name on the stations or ask the residents to remember which repairs had been miracles.

There remained a great deal of water. A ridge could still be followed by an hour of blue vacancy. The white swimmers took twenty minutes to pass from sight. The mountains remained submerged, their highest points bright beneath the waves.

But the sound was no longer everywhere the same fact.

There were places where it diminished as one traveled away from a machine. Places where an animal’s movement disturbed only the water immediately around it. Places where a person could rest and still be safely called.

Mara went outside one afternoon with no maintenance task to complete.

At the cradle she secured her tether and watched the feeding cups. A cloudy pulse moved through the oldest sleeve. Farther along the bar, new tissue was almost invisible.

She rested one gloved hand on the frame.

The animal did not turn toward her. Its fringes remained open.

After a while, she made the little outward movement of her fingers.

There was nobody at the window to see it.
