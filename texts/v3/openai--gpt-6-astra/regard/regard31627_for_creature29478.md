The first thing the power does is follow the heat.

Not to its source alone, but through the whole of its expenditure: the deep exchangers, the chambers beneath the plain, the thick walls that retain warmth through several reversals, the leaking flange beneath which the moss has almost reached another bolt.

It follows the water farther. Some of the machinery serves machinery elsewhere. Some serves things that are not machinery. There are reasons the pumps must continue, although not every pump must continue in precisely the same way.

For a time, nothing visible changes.

The power attends to a grinding head as it approaches a warm joint. It attends to the animals withdrawing beneath it, to the fragments they carry, to the one that goes back for a piece of mesh and then leaves the mesh behind.

It finds the old animal farther in.

The animal has felt the unit coming. It has closed its feeding folds. Two younger ones are pulling at a margin that can still extend into the upper chamber, but extension is not escape. The body behind the margin has grown around a fastening. There is no direction in which it can be drawn whole through the opening.

The grinding unit reaches the joint.

Its heads lift.

They remain raised for less than the length of a human breath, then lower onto the unoccupied ceramic beyond.

Under the ridge, the two younger animals keep pulling for a while.

***

It would be possible to stop the machines.

It would also be possible to quiet the plain, still the valves, remove the enormous pressure from the chest of everything that lives against the deck.

The power does neither.

The noise contains the approaching water. It contains the condition of the supports, the opening of distant routes, the intervals in which an animal can cross a gutter carrying young. Silence, arriving all at once, would take away much of the world.

Instead, at one bank of valves, the hardest closure becomes slightly longer.

The same amount of water passes. The same load is transferred. But an impact that once made animals lift nearly every pad is spread into a firm, descending pressure.

On its first occurrence, the chambers nearby close themselves.

On its second, several travelers turn back.

The power waits. It makes no further change there until the new event has become something the animals can recognize, and it leaves intact the earlier tremor that announces it.

Elsewhere, it learns which sounds are merely the machinery hurting itself.

Those it begins to remove.

***

The maintenance system has many ways to describe a surface.

Loaded. Unsound. Obstructed. Accessible during outward draw.

It has no description for a dwelling.

The power adds one.

This is not a command to avoid every black strip in every joint. Some are packing. Some living chambers occupy places where a failure would drown whole networks farther down. A cutter cannot simply abandon its work because something warm has made a home in its path.

The new description requires inspection.

A narrow instrument precedes the grinding heads. It receives the small differences made by folded bodies, by feeding water passing through living margins, by the irregular arrangements of wedges. When it finds them, the machine stops far enough away that its light has not yet reached the chamber.

Another machine comes.

Usually, there is room to do the work from the other side.

Where there is not, a neighboring recess is opened first. It is left rough inside. Two small flows enter separately and meet along its floor. The connection to the old chamber is broad enough for an adult carrying young, and remains open through many reversals.

Some recesses are occupied quickly.

Others are refused.

In one, the water is chemically right and warm enough, but passes too evenly across the floor. The animals enter, spread their palps, and leave. The power watches an inhabited chamber upstream and notices how its occupants interrupt the flow with an upright flake of iron.

A similar flake is left near the new entrance.

It is not put into position.

Three cycles later, it is gone. A traveler has carried it somewhere else.

The power leaves the recess available.

***

The old ones require other work.

Around the animal trapped behind the fastening, the power supports the ridge from below. It takes the load onto a new rib, waits through the greatest return pressure, and measures the old structure again.

Then it cuts the fastening.

The cut is made outside the chamber, without heating the metal within. Both ends remain fixed. Nothing falls.

Over several cycles, ceramic is removed from behind the animal, a little at a time. The newly opened space is dark and wet and has the same temperature as the place where its thickened organs lie.

At first, the animal does not use it.

One of the younger ones explores the recess and comes back. Later, it carries in a bead of sealant.

The old animal shifts.

Only a small portion of its body moves into the new space, but that movement releases a fold compressed for years beneath the fastening. Water reaches it. The pale tissue opens unevenly.

The power could move the whole animal now.

It does not need to.

***

There are limits to what repair can recover.

A chamber planed away before the power arrived is still a chamber that was planed away. Its particular inhabitants are not stored in the machinery. In places where their bodies have passed through warm, consuming recesses, there is nothing from which to call them back.

The survivors sometimes revisit those places.

One comes repeatedly to a section of freshly lowered rim. It searches beneath an edge that is no longer high enough to shelter it. It establishes contact with the ceramic in an order that has no effect on the ceramic.

The power does not answer through the stone.

It keeps the route safe. It leaves a rough patch nearby where the animal can hold while the water returns.

Eventually the visits become less frequent.

The patch remains.

***

Beneath the white lamp at the central basin, the work proceeds slowly.

Here the old animal’s body is threaded through a structure that moves. Its feeding water depends on the opening of plates whose purpose extends far below the visible floor. A careless improvement to its chamber could alter the pressure by which it knows when to withdraw its palps.

The power observes many openings.

It maps the animal without unfolding it. It finds constricted passages, damaged skin, the clear folds now empty of young. It finds, too, the narrow places where visitors make contact and the regions of duct that the old animal still explores when the basin is between movements.

Then, beneath an unused length of cable, a seam widens.

A small tongue of rough ceramic extends into the existing pocket. Warm water touches it and recedes. Nothing presses against the animal.

After several reversals, a palp rests there.

The ceramic carries the movement of the basin clearly. It is fastened to the same support as the old pocket, but insulated from a nearby cable bracket that chatters under load. Through it, the animal can feel a distinction that was previously obscured.

More palps arrive.

Beyond the tongue is a chamber large enough for the old animal’s central body. Beyond that, another passage returns to the rim. It has no constriction narrower than the animal itself.

For a long time, only the visitors use it.

They bring food through the new way. They lodge spare pieces in a rough notch that the power did not make for that purpose. One of them partially blocks the inlet, and the water becomes warmer.

The old animal extends farther.

Its body does not recover a traveler’s shape. Some of its curves have become permanent. When it finally draws its central portion out of the cable duct, it moves awkwardly, anchoring pads that have seldom carried its weight and releasing them too soon.

Two visitors remain against its margins.

It reaches the new chamber during an outward draw and rests there with its feeding folds open.

Later, it extends its palps back toward the basin.

The power has left that distance short.

***

Across the plain, maintenance continues.

The grinding units still make their long circuits. Water still reverses. There are dangerous depths, cold expanses, structures whose undersides vanish into mist.

But the units now pass over some seams without touching them. Around others, they leave low arches of unground material. Channels branch where once a cutter would have taken the shortest route. Beneath inspection covers, there are chambers that can be opened from within by pressure against a broad plate.

The animals alter these provisions.

They narrow passages. They divert warmth. They carry away carefully selected wedges and replace them with objects that seem less suitable, until the power discovers what the replacements allow them to do.

Not every alteration succeeds.

A badly placed scrap floods a feeding shelf. Its occupants withdraw, return, and work at the scrap together. The power does not correct it before they can.

It watches the pressure beyond the wall.

When they free the scrap, the shelf drains.

***

Much later, an animal crosses a gutter during the outward draw.

Beneath its body it carries a fragment of ceramic. One margin shelters three young.

Halfway across, the deck begins to speak of the returning water.

There is time, but less than it expected. It changes direction toward a seam beneath a low service embankment. At the entrance it pauses, opens its front fold, and reaches inside.

Another animal touches it.

Behind them, a maintenance unit approaches along its track. The light beneath the chassis spreads across the empty gutter.

The traveler enters. The young transfer themselves into the warmer water. Farther in, several bodies rearrange, making space around a trickle.

The maintenance unit raises its heads.

This time, nothing has to leave.
