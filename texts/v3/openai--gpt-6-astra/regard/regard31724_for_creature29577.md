The power does not put a hand upon the plateau.

There is nowhere to put one.

For a while it attends to the animal beside the root. It follows the moisture beneath the throat, the faint movement under the plates, the strain in the foot that has not yet let go. It learns the difference between stillness and inability to move.

This takes longer than watching the tooth advance.

The animal has no wish to be taken out of the fissure. Exposure is something its whole body is working to end. Nor would it benefit from being carried into a great chamber below. Its tissues must develop as they enter the stone. Space acquired too quickly would be as useless as food offered to an organ it has not yet grown.

The power leaves it where it is.

But leaving it where it is does not exhaust the possibilities.

—

The root has been damaged farther up the shelf.

A grazer passed over it some months ago. Most of the crushed tissue has sealed, but one narrow band no longer carries water. Moisture still reaches the animal along the outer hairs. Soon, during a clear interval, that film will break.

The power follows the root into the low forest. It finds the living tissue on either side of the injury.

It restores the narrow band.

No new spring appears. The frost does not melt. Nothing accelerates. Water moves again through a passage too small to hold a visible drop.

Beside the fissure, the animal continues drinking.

This is the first thing the power does, and it does not mistake it for the last thing that will be needed.

—

Below the shelf, a turning organ has been dragging against its pouch for eighty years.

The change has been gradual enough that the animal has grown around it. Muscular bands on one side are thick and overworked. Elsewhere, chambers receive less than they require. The small bearing grazers can no longer reach a pocket where deposits accumulate.

The adult knows the trouble as a return that comes late.

It sends pressure toward the chamber, receives the altered pressure back, and sends again. It has done this through thousands of surface frosts.

The power listens.

Then, from the troublesome chamber, it returns a pressure of its own.

The animal closes three valves.

The power waits.

After a long interval, one valve opens. A small quantity of fluid enters the chamber and stops.

The power does not yet know whether this is investigation, defense, or merely the next stage of the animal’s ordinary response. It repeats nothing until it has learned more.

They proceed in this way for several seasons.

What develops is not speech. There are no names for the plateau, or the light, or the being attending from beyond the stone. There is a pressure that announces a possible change. There is a way of holding that change short. There is, eventually, a response after which it may continue.

The animal keeps its other valves closed when it permits the power to lift the burden from the damaged bearing.

The wheel rises by less than the thickness of a leaf.

Fluid enters the worn place. The little grazers follow it.

For the first time in generations, they can feed along the whole surface.

The power holds the wheel there while the animal grows a new bearing beneath it.

—

It would be possible to turn every organ for them.

The power considers this.

But the turning is not simply labor imposed upon the animals. It maintains their bodies, distributes their nourishment, carries much of what they can perceive. To replace it all at once would be to interrupt a life in the act of trying to ease it.

So the power learns to distinguish work from damage.

It learns the ordinary strain of a full chamber, and the strain of a chamber that cannot empty. It learns which pauses are repairs. It learns how a body signals before abandoning a vessel, and how the signal changes when the creatures inside have not escaped.

Where it can, it opens a route for them.

Where pain serves to direct repair, it preserves the information and eases the excess. Where a fault is tearing living tissue faster than the tissue can withdraw, it holds the opposing faces apart—not forever, not across the continent, but long enough for the attachments to release.

Sometimes the animal refuses the first intervention offered.

The power learns other ways.

—

It also attends to the deaths.

A stopped body is not an empty body. Some of its chambers are already nurseries. Others still contain living organs cut off from one another, spending their final stores against sealed passages.

The power does not restart them merely because it can make them move.

It traces what remains connected. It looks for a body that can again sustain itself, rather than a wheel that can be made to turn.

When recovery is possible, it offers a connection.

When it is not, it eases the failing tissues. It keeps a descending passage warm until the small inhabitants have reached water. Then it lets the warmth diminish.

The young enter afterward by their own routes.

Nothing in the old chambers requires them to become the animal that died.

—

Across the plateau, the changes are difficult to see.

There are still white districts and green districts. The dark herds still travel beneath their burdens of smaller lives. Predators feed. Clouds spill over the cliffs and are taken apart by leaves.

But fewer injuries become decades of useless effort.

A blocked discharge opens before the chambers behind it rupture. Two bodies contesting a seam discover, through unfamiliar returning pressures, another fracture neither had reached. They do not have to share a body. They grow separately into the available stone.

The power remains attentive to what these changes cause.

A saved animal needs food. A repaired chamber produces waste. A safer nursery may send more young into a shelf than the shelf can hold. Each kindness enters the same crowded country as everything else.

There is no final gesture after which attention becomes unnecessary.

—

At the fissure, the last exposed foot releases.

The animal draws it beneath the plate and rests. Above its back, the frost remains undisturbed.

It does not know that anything has intervened. The moisture against its throat carries no message. It is not required to recognize a benefactor, or to become remarkable, or even to survive forever.

Inside the stone, its first exploratory tissue swells toward a darker seam.

The power follows its progress without pushing.

For now the passage is sound, the root is wet, and the body has enough.

For now, nothing more is done.
