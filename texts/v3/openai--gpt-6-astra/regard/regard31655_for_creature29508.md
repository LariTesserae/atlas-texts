For a long time, the attention changes nothing.

It rests beneath the moving animal, where one foot is still searching for roots. It follows the pressure of the gripping hairs, the small expenditure required to hold the body above the stone. It notices that the foot has searched the same place before.

The animal is not confused about where the roots end.

There was warmth here at the last crossing. The stone remembers some of it. Farther down, a chamber has collapsed, and water that once passed beneath this seam now drains into a crack too deep for any root to follow.

The animal can feel the old passage. It cannot feel why it has gone quiet.

The power could lift it. It could place it on the richest bed in the basin, close every mouth against living tissue, and keep the tissue warm.

Instead, it learns what being lifted would mean.

To have all the feet lose contact at once. To lose the ground’s countless small reports. To have no pressure beneath the body and no way to distinguish rescue from the beginning of being eaten.

It leaves the feet where they are.

***

The power’s first touch is much farther down.

There is a loose grain lodged against a moving membrane. Each contraction presses it deeper. Around it, the flexible glass has begun to whiten.

The grain moves.

That is all.

The membrane completes its contraction. A cylinder fills, then empties into a passage that has remained closed for several nights. Water advances through the rootwork. A pale cleaner turns in the returning current and begins to graze.

Near the exposed stone, the searching foot becomes still.

The animal waits through several cycles.

Then its muzzle extends again.

There is not enough warmth to make the crossing safe. The power does not mistake a little improvement for enough.

Under the seam, beyond the reach of living tissue, it draws two broken surfaces of rock closer together. They meet without crushing anything between them. The escaping water finds its former level.

Across the basin, a pool sinks slightly.

The power attends to that pool as carefully as to the traveler. Something is brooding beneath its overhanging bank. The water stops falling well before the eggs are exposed.

At the seam, moisture darkens the stone.

A foot reaches forward and holds.

***

Afterward, the power remains.

It learns that a warm animal is not always a comfortable animal. Sometimes warmth is the last of its reserves leaving through a wound.

It learns that stillness may be feeding, vigilance, exhaustion, or a struggle conducted with such force that a whole root bed bends beneath it.

It learns the difference between the repeated testing of a route and the repetition that begins when no route remains.

These distinctions take attention. Power alone does not supply them.

Nor does the plateau have a single need.

A little more thaw allows one kind of grazer to breed and drowns the eggs of another. An open passage brings water to a failing mat and admits a predator to a nursery. A plant freed from its dependence on an animal’s heat grows over the channels through which the animal breathes.

The power does not turn the plateau warm.

It does not make all the pools full.

But where a fracture is about to empty an entire basin, it holds the fracture closed. Where a storm strips the last cover from a youngster, it bends a nearby mat just enough to make shelter accessible from underneath.

The youngster enters on its own feet.

Fragments catch in its skin channels. Most fail to root. Enough survive.

***

Eventually, some of the animals become familiar with a kind of place that was uncommon before.

These places have more than one entrance.

Their warmth does not depend on a single chamber. When one passage closes, another takes the water. The roots remain loose enough for a body to withdraw without tearing its feeding mouths.

Nothing summons the animals there.

A traveler encounters a faint, steady activity through the stone, tests it, and sometimes changes direction. Another pauses at the edge and goes elsewhere. The place remains available.

Small grazers find it too. Cleaners occupy the cylinders. Needles grow over the exposed margins.

The power has to keep learning.

In one hollow, the new shelter becomes a trap. A flat predator settles beneath the only approach broad enough for an old animal. The second entrance, sufficient for a youngster, is useless to anything larger.

Before a traveler arrives, the power widens it.

The predator stays. It is hungry.

That hunger also enters the power’s attention.

For a while, there is no simple thing to do.

***

The oldest animal in the basin has not traveled for many thaws.

Its back carries three distinct needle fields. A downward-growing frond occupies the hollow behind its nursery fold, although there is no fissure there: only a deep crease in the body.

Its feeding contacts still work. Two of its hearts do not.

The power could restore them.

It attends first to the animal.

Beneath the weight of roots there are still attempts to move: an edge tightening, a buried foot pressing against a stone it can no longer release. The motion stops, begins again, stops.

The power loosens one root.

Then another.

It does not remove the covering. It supports the living mat while freeing the fold beneath it, keeping the cold away from the newly exposed skin.

For the first time in years, the animal opens that margin completely.

Water moves through the compressed channels. A cluster of mites disperses. The old foot extends into a space that was not there before.

The animal leaves it extended.

For several nights, that is the whole change.

Later it draws the foot back and presses outward again, farther this time.

The power begins making room.

***

There are deaths still.

There are also struggles that end sooner, wounds that close, and journeys completed by animals that would otherwise have stopped between feeding beds.

The work does not become finished merely because it becomes difficult to see.

Far below, lightning opens the cloud.

Above it, the plateau spends its stored charge. The leaf fields tighten. Warmth passes downward through the roots.

In one sheltered hollow, an old animal moves less than the length of a foot.

Its plants move with it.

Behind it, a narrow strip of stone is exposed to the air. Ahead, several gripping surfaces find unfamiliar roots and test them carefully.

The power does not hurry it.
