The power did not bring rain.

It had done so elsewhere, with excellent results. Here it watched a split stem seal against the air, followed the movement of dust through a grazer’s flank, and let the clouds remain below.

For a while it stayed on the western rim.

The young fliers bit at it. It made itself less available to their mouths without taking away the warmth they had discovered, and spent the long twilight learning which parts of the cold were necessary.

***

Eshet was trying to flatten the corner of the photograph when something knocked at her door.

It used the door rather than the comb. This suggested a visitor, a fault in the communications tissue, or her neighbor carrying something with both hands.

Outside stood a person she did not recognize. Their coat hung oddly, as though its weight had only recently been settled.

“Are you lost?” she asked.

“No. But I am new here.”

“That isn’t always different.”

The visitor considered this.

Behind Eshet, the warming wall gave a small contraction. Her supper had reached the point at which it would begin becoming part of the house again.

“Come in or don’t,” she said.

The visitor came in.

It stepped over the ridge in the floor.

***

Later, Eshet would have trouble explaining why she believed what it told her. There had been no demonstration. Nothing in the room had become luminous, except the exposed edge of her supper, which was ordinary.

Perhaps it was the way the visitor admitted what it did not know.

It could have separated the old crowns beneath the town without breaking a single inhabited chamber. It could have lifted the great supporting creatures out whole, carried them through the air, and laid them down in some carefully prepared place.

It did not know whether they would survive being understood that suddenly by their own surroundings.

It could make a door to almost anywhere.

It had spent several hours examining the third doorway at the station and was not yet prepared to call what lay beyond it a place.

“What do you want?” Eshet asked.

“To help, if I can.”

“With what?”

“That is partly what I came to ask.”

She pushed the photograph across the table.

“Hold that edge. Not the corner. The edge.”

The power held it.

With both hands free, she warmed the bent horn, eased the photograph flat, and fixed a narrow strip of binding to its back.

The younger Eshet smiled up between two other people. The visitor did not make them move.

***

She took it to work the next morning.

At first she regretted this. People who learned quickly were often intolerable during their second hour.

The visitor, however, remained useful. It held the insect cases out of the wind. It diverted traffic without starting arguments. When Eshet told it to put its hand beside hers, it did so without trying to finish the repair.

Under their palms the temporary path was becoming a road.

The feet that had passed yesterday had compressed a shallow channel. The plateau had begun reinforcing it. Farther down, where the channel crossed a nursery of transparent burrowers, that reinforcement was closing the fine openings through which the animals fed.

Eshet could feel resistance. The visitor could distinguish the little bodies inside it.

“How many?” she asked.

It told her.

She sat back on her stool.

“That makes it a different job.”

“Can we move them?”

“Some. The rest will break.”

The power waited.

Eshet looked along the path, then toward the junction she had repaired.

“We could keep the first section. Turn it there. Bring it back around.”

“That would make the walk longer.”

“Eleven paces.”

They marked out the curve.

It took most of the morning. The power could have changed the tissue in an instant, but Eshet needed to see how it responded, and the burrowers needed time to withdraw from the pressure. At midday a freight tender came to object to the new alignment. Eshet made him put down his measuring stick and look into a seam.

He remained annoyed. He helped them move the stool.

***

The power stayed.

There were requests almost immediately, followed by incompatible requests, followed by a meeting to decide who had been entitled to make the first requests.

It attended the meeting.

Someone asked it to restore the enormous trunks.

Someone else asked whether this would put the eastern districts in permanent shade.

A third person wanted the trunks restored, but farther away.

The power showed them what it could determine about the old supporting layers. Several had become inhabited. Others could no longer bear the weight of what had once stood above them. Growing new supports would displace towns, feeding chambers, migration passages, and creatures for which the inhabitants had names only in the language of excavation warnings.

The drawing remained on the public wall for months. People added to it.

No great trunks rose that year.

Instead, a collector beyond the northern grazing country began to fail, and the people who maintained it asked for help reaching its upper joint.

The power held a platform steady for six days.

The pale animals inside the joint had been losing their nests to a mineral change no one had noticed. The repair involved an unpleasant paste, several baskets of replacement fiber, and a worker lying on her side with one arm inserted to the shoulder.

When it was finished, the collector turned.

The town gained nothing visible on its skyline.

***

Not all the help was slow.

During a spore release, a bridge tore loose while three children were crossing it.

For an instant the bridge had a support that reached neither ground nor building. It held until the children scrambled off. Then it remained in place for the repair crew.

The power did not ask anyone’s permission first.

Afterward it helped examine the torn attachment. There were other bridges of the same age.

***

Eshet’s sister took it to the sealed doorway.

They listened to the wind. Pollen accumulated in a shallow dish.

“Can you open it safely?” her sister asked.

“Not today.”

It returned the next day, and many days after that.

Eventually it made a chamber around the frame, with another chamber around that, and instruments the tenders could read without its assistance. It explained what each instrument could fail to notice.

They sent nothing living through.

The first thing recovered was a length of dark fiber. The second was a broken shell. The third was the probe itself, much older along one side than the other.

Eshet’s sister ordered the trial stopped.

The power stopped it.

That evening, for the first time, she invited it home.

***

There were things it offered without waiting for someone to know they could be requested.

It found a way to keep a detached nursery casing at a safe pressure for several days. Parents examined it, distrusted it, asked for changes, and examined it again.

It worked with a bootmaker on soles that gave a warning before an uncertain footstep became a dangerous one. The first pair was too emphatic. An old man described walking in them as being continually corrected by a small relative.

The second pair was better.

At the transit station, it could do nothing simple about the sickness of passage. It could, however, keep a traveler’s dust supported while the body recovered. The station attendants learned the method. A porter who had endured the journey twice daily sat down after his first assisted crossing and became unexpectedly angry.

No one told him he ought to be grateful.

They had not known how much it hurt.

***

The plateau took longer.

There was no single conversation, no awakening voice beneath the country.

There were experiments stopped because tissue withdrew. There were grafts accepted in one district and refused in the next. Once, three distant basins changed their growth after a question had been put to a fourth, but the same thing happened later when no question had been asked.

The power kept both observations.

With Eshet and several hundred other workers, it cultivated places where responses could become easier to distinguish. They were sheltered, lightly traveled patches, scattered through the country. Some remained inert. Some acquired troublesome growths. One became exceptionally attractive to grazers and had to be abandoned as an experiment.

In another, after four years, the ground learned to move a warning bud before opening a warm vent.

People began planting that variety beside the old pale roads.

***

Eshet did not become young.

She was offered help with the stiffness beneath her ribs and accepted it after asking exactly what would be changed. She was offered other things and said she would think about them.

The photograph remained a photograph.

Once she asked whether the power could bring back her partner.

It did not answer quickly.

They spoke for a long time about the difference between finding someone and making someone who would remember being them. It did not pretend that her wanting was a small matter. Nor did it offer certainty where it had none.

Afterward she was tired of it for several weeks.

It continued holding the northern platform while the maintenance crew worked. When she wanted to see it again, she knew where to go.

***

The ridge still rose in her sitting room.

One evening the power arrived to find her kneeling beside it, knife in hand.

“I could help retrain this part of the house,” it said.

“I know.”

“Or hold the cut open while you work.”

She considered the ridge.

“Do that.”

It knelt.

Outside, the towers lowered into the wind. A freight load passed beneath the arcade, and somewhere far under the floor an animal continued the journey it had begun before Eshet was born.

She cut carefully. The power kept the healthy tissue out of the blade’s path.

When they were finished, she put her palm against the floor and waited for the edges to close.
