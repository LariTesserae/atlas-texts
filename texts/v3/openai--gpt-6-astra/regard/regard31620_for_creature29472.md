The power waited for the call.

Not because it was afraid to cross the floor. It had no weight unless it chose to have weight. But something was about to happen between the sleeper, the animal beneath it, and the crab holding one leg above the brick. It wished to see that happen before adding anything of its own.

The throat opened. The pier gave its wooden bellow.

Along the joint, the transparent edge loosened and drew inward. The crab lowered its foot. Beneath the basin, the bubble burst.

The power listened through many more risings of the water.

It listened at the broken flap, where each blow made the soft tissue contract. It followed that contraction through a narrow neck, past a feeding lobe, into a vessel where another part of the animal continued slowly gathering food. Injury did not occupy the whole creature at once. Nor did the power mistake this for an absence of injury.

At the collapsed jar, it found the cord pinched almost through.

These were the first places it touched.

It did not lift the floor.

During an interval when the broken flap hung clear, the power held it there. Water passed around it. The old hinge gave a small, dissatisfied click.

The wounded tissue remained contracted.

The power waited.

After a time the fine nap lifted along the margins of the injury. A little current began to move there. Loose matter slipped away from the wound.

Only then did the power alter the flap’s travel. It gave the clay a stop, placed where the blow would fall against sound pottery rather than flesh. The stop was small. Its making raised a pale cloud in the water.

The next rise came. The flap swung and struck.

The sound was different by scarcely anything.

Beneath it, the wound did not open.

At the collapsed jar, the work took longer. The broken pottery was carrying part of the floor’s weight. Simply pulling it apart would have made a larger injury out of a smaller one.

The power supported the load on either side. It settled two curved pieces beneath it, leaving the water its old passage. Then, over several cycles, it eased the biting edges apart.

At first the cord kept its constricted shape.

Water entered the space that had been made around it. The dull tissue beyond the pinch stirred very slightly. Later, a crease that had been deep enough to collect lime began to flatten.

The power did not pull it straight.

It went on looking.

There were passages whose obstructions mattered, and passages whose obstructions had become useful. One cracked vessel was now a nursery. A fallen peg divided a current that would otherwise wash young fish out from beneath the basin’s rim. Behind a sheet of loose lime, seven crabs had made their homes.

The court offered many opportunities to repair the wrong thing.

So the power learned which knocks preceded which movements. It watched the hunting creatures fold themselves into the roof. It watched eggs adhere to the pale cushions, and the cushions swell beneath them. It watched the oldest crab cross at an angle that added several steps to its journey but kept its burden from catching in a particular joint.

It left that joint alone.

For the roof, it made supports within the thickness of the clay, where no hunter rested. For the wall, it secured the bricks around the inhabited hollows without filling the hollows themselves. The thumbprints remained. Water still found the cord mark across the sloping floor.

At the drains it attended to the water outside as carefully as to the water within. The lower habits were not a promise. Far away, a blocked channel was slowly raising the pressure.

The power cleared a second way for that excess to pass, beyond the court. It did not lower the water here, or abolish its return. On the next filling, the amphibians woke in their familiar order.

There was still the matter of room.

The animal had grown into nearly every space available to it. Some of its sealed ends pressed against mortar that would never yield without bringing down what stood above. The power could have made the creature smaller. It could have lifted it out and held every lobe in a new arrangement.

Instead, beside three of those ends, it made passages.

They led into chambers of porous fired earth. These were low and dark, with slow water moving through them. Nothing tugged at the animal from the other side. No sudden current brought an unfamiliar taste to all its feeding surfaces.

For a long time, nothing entered.

One sealed end withdrew farther into its old vessel.

Another remained exactly where it was.

At the third, the tissue softened against the new opening. A fold passed through, stopped, and developed a fringe. Over many days it spread along the chamber wall.

The power kept the other passages open.

It also watched the small ones.

An occupied lump rolled toward a drain. Rather than catching it and returning it to the basin, the power followed. Beyond the drain lay a rough descent, then a sump with little air and almost no food. Thousands of pierced fragments had gathered there.

The power made shallows beyond the sump, where light reached wet clay and a film could grow. It gave the outlet places to catch and places to pass through. The next occupied lump lodged in a sheltered notch.

After two risings, its inhabitant extended a fringe.

Others stayed in the court. Some were eaten by fish. The power did not turn its attention away from those small endings; neither did it take the fish’s hunger for a defect that could be removed without care. Its work extended beyond the first relief, into questions that required more than one answer.

Within the court, the changes were quiet enough to be mistaken for another season of wear.

The flap clicked. The jar boomed. The pier answered.

A scar began to close beneath the oldest floor.

In the new chamber, a pale surface spread across clay that bore no ancient marks. As it moved, its margin gathered into shallow ridges. Silt settled between them. Water divided, followed the ridges, and joined again beyond them.

For the first time, this part of the animal was making the shape of the place.

The power remained nearby, attentive to what that shape would become.
