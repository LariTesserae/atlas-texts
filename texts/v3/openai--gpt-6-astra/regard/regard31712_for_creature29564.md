The power waited until the pump stopped.

It could have held the chamber rigid. But the silk had been laid under tension, and the female knew its yielding through the leg extended into the entrance. To make it suddenly immovable would have been another disturbance.

So the twelve seconds passed. The silk relaxed. Inside, she shifted the brood a little farther beneath her.

The power attended to the loose nut.

There was no need to turn it back along the thread. Turning it would send a vibration through the bracket, the folded aluminum, the silk. There was no need, either, to remove it. Along its upper face lay a deposit of organic dust, and in that deposit two mites were feeding.

Instead, where the last engaged thread met the bolt, a small area of metal ceased to be separate from another small area of metal.

The nut stayed where it was.

The feeding mites stayed where they were.

Nothing below received the weight.

For a while, this was all the power did.

It listened through the mounting frame, through the floor of the gorge, through the feet planted on either side of an emptied shell. It followed heat out of the resistor and into the washer, then onward through connections so poor that a gap narrower than a hair divided a neighborhood from an uninhabitable country.

It attended to the female without assuming that her stillness was peace.

It attended to the cold mite without assuming that its stillness was distress.

There were differences here that could not be understood by enlarging the animals in imagination and giving them human faces. There were differences that could not be understood by calling them machinery, either.

The power had time to learn them.

When the pump started again, it followed the air.

The atmospheric sample entered through an external port. The violent current through the inhabited enclosure was incidental: an aging seal allowed the diaphragm’s motion to draw air where no measurement required it. A repair could have stopped the current entirely.

But the current brought food.

It moved dead things out of inaccessible cracks. It carried spores and fragments through the vents. The district around the warm washer had gathered where it had partly because the washer interrupted the flow.

The power let several more cycles pass.

Then, inside the pump housing, beyond any passage an animal could enter, the hardened seal became a little more supple.

Not new. Not perfect.

At the next start, the knock was smaller. The hanging fiber rose, but did not strike the stone. Carbon dust moved along the floor rather than lifting into a cloud.

Inside her chamber, the female braced.

The silk pressed inward less far.

She remained braced after the pump stopped, for as long as she had before. Nothing in her required her to understand an improvement on its first occurrence.

Behind the cable bracket, the scavenger drew an antenna free of its old skin.

The power watched that happen, too.

***

Water required greater care.

A droplet would have been an immense object here. Surface tension could hold an animal more securely than a claw. A wet seam might support food, or rot the brood chamber, or conduct electricity between points meant to remain apart.

The power did not make a spring.

In the mounting cement there were pores too small for a mite to enter. Some already held moisture, surrendered reluctantly to the dry air. Over many hours, a few of those pores acquired a little more.

The change reached the fungus first.

Deep inside the collapsed insect egg, a thread extended along the inner curve of shell. Later, the mite that grazed the crack found something where its mouthparts had found nothing on its previous visit.

There was no abundance. There was less distance between one sufficient feeding and the next.

Near the folded aluminum, moisture remained bound in the same way, available without presenting an open surface. The female would encounter it when she emerged.

She did not emerge yet.

She worked at the silk from within. Where the bright grain had drawn two strands together, she added another attachment. The power left the grain alone. It was supported now, and removing it would undo something she had made.

***

The instrument had eleven months before its next scheduled service.

The transformer would probably last longer than that. One of its connections might not. The inspection plate would be opened by a person wearing thick gloves, whose attention would be divided between the equipment, the wind, and the difficult descent.

The power considered the glove.

It considered the replacement unit waiting in a warehouse far below.

It considered what would happen if the old housing were brought down into warmth: the sudden activity, the unfamiliar organisms, the drying air of a storeroom. Removal was not necessarily rescue.

It did not decide all of this at once.

For now, the failing connection was relieved of the tiny strain that would otherwise have broken it. The heat remained as it had been. The technician had not yet begun the journey. There was time to arrange a meeting between human care and this place, without making the animals endure a revelation.

The power stayed.

***

Inside the silk chamber, the brood changed.

The pale cluster was no longer composed only of closed shapes. Small limbs lay folded against small bodies. The female adjusted her position, and one of the young moved independently beneath her.

Outside, the green indicator continued to burn.

The hunter’s descendants would still hunt. The mites would withdraw from their approach. Not every egg would hatch, and the power had not mistaken a crowded enclosure for a problem solved by making it more crowded.

But the nut would not fall.

The next pump cycle would not tear the newly molted scavenger from its shelter.

When the female left her chamber to feed, the warmth would still extend across the narrow crossing she needed to take.

She rested with one leg against the entrance. Beyond the silk, a pale mite passed, paused, and continued along the folded metal.

For the moment, nothing needed to move.

The power did not ask to be noticed.
