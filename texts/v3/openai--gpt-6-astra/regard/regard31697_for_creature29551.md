The power lets the bead fall.

The animal receives some of it against its mouth. The rest runs beneath its head and darkens the stone between its foremost pads. For a while it stays where it is. Then the feelers touch the groove again.

There is time to learn what would help.

The power attends to the hollow through a cooling of the stone, through the animal’s journey to the margin and its return. It follows the water farther than the animal can follow it: into the limestone, along a tilted seam, through a pocket of gravel where each small arrival waits before passing downward. It learns how the seep dwindles. It learns which of the apparently bare surfaces are food.

Under the lowest shelf, the animal rests with its injured side lowered.

The ridge of joined plates is old. There is no hurt in it now. Its awkwardness belongs to movements the animal has learned to make, and the power leaves it alone.

There is, however, a place beneath the fallen block where the crossing has become difficult. A flake has tipped against the wall. The animal can pass, but must release three pads together and drag its soft underside across a sharp edge. It has done so often enough to leave a faint dullness on the stone.

When the animal is elsewhere, the power settles the flake flat.

It moves scarcely the breadth of a grass blade. The route still smells of wet dust and root. The upper surface still offers its familiar hold.

On the next crossing, the animal reaches around the corner before its head clears it. One pad finds the wall. Another follows. Its underside passes without catching.

It continues toward the retaining wall.

The power does not require it to stop.

Behind the wall, one of the fitted stones has begun to lean. Nothing visible has happened yet; the earth beneath its inner edge has been leaving a grain at a time. In another season, or several, it might close the little chamber.

The power returns support beneath that edge. It leaves the hollow in the earth, the shed plates, the narrow entrance through which the animal must turn. The shelter keeps its dryness.

Most of the work is farther away.

Deep behind the seep, a passage is slowly sealing with mineral deposit. There is another route the water might take, lower in the hill, beyond reach of this hollow. The power keeps a small opening through the deposit. No more water arrives than the grooves can carry. The basin does not rise above its ordinary line. The plants in the dry cracks remain dry enough.

There will still be lean intervals. The wall shelter will still have a use. But the last wet seam will not fail while the animal waits against it.

Having done these things, the power remains attentive without occupying the hollow. No brightness gathers under the ledge. Nothing stands at the entrance. The dry slab stays empty, with room upon it for whoever might someday sit there quietly.

Toward the next cooling, the animal comes out.

It grazes below the shelf, holding its mouth to a patch of stone that looks clean. Later it advances. One of its plates shifts over another; the old ridge clears the low roof.

At the bend in the runnel, water has gathered against a grain of grit. The animal places a feeler in it, then the other.

There is enough to go a little farther.
