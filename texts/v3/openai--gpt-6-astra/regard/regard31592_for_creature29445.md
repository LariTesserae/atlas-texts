The attention arrives without passing through the water.

Nothing turns toward it. The free limb of an adult remains open above a fissure. Beneath the floating leaves, a fish holds its place with movements smaller than the shadows on its sides.

At the eastern slab, the young animal closes one of the passages beneath its mouth.

The power notices this.

It had first noticed the lake: its height, its unsupported edges, the green light falling through it onto weather. Then the forests had occupied its attention, and the slow accommodation of one leaf to another. Now it attends to a passage in a body no larger than a hand.

The passage opens again.

Water moves through. A few mineral grains lodge among the folds. Elsewhere in the mouth, something living divides.

For a long time, the power does nothing but learn the difference between these events.

It is capable of warming the entire basin. It could open the mountain from end to end and bring the deep water up through a thousand new springs.

But the animal has just closed a passage against the warmth. Nearby, a snail is feeding on a growth that occurs only on the cooler side of the slab. Farther beneath the stone, in a place no limb has reached, clear eggs adhere to the roof of a small cavity.

The power leaves the heat where it is.

It follows the thread of seepage backward.

The opening beneath the animal is shallow. A short distance inside the slab, it joins an older fracture, one that descends between two tilted plates. Warm water rises there under a pressure too slight to disturb the sediment at its outlet.

A root is entering the upper part of this fracture.

In time, the root will thicken. It will hold the slab more securely against the slope. Its finer branches will shelter small animals, and a ribbon plant will anchor itself among them.

It will also close the thread of water beneath the young animal.

There are other fissures. The nearest is beyond a bare shelf and down the face of a sharp step. An adult could reach it easily. For this animal, the journey would be long, and the first part would lead through water with no trace of what it needs.

The power considers moving it.

It attends instead to the eight discs, five holding, three tucked away; to the muscles maintaining a shape that fits this particular stone; to the small movements by which the mouth has begun to make a place habitable.

Then it attends to the rock again.

Beside the advancing root, two mineral surfaces meet imperfectly. Between them is a blind seam, shorter than the animal’s smallest limb. The seam almost reaches the warm fracture. At its other end, it almost reaches the underside of the slab.

The power loosens a grain.

Nothing visible happens.

Later, the grain shifts. Water enters the space behind it. Over several days, the pressure carries away a little of the finer material, and the blind seam becomes a narrow passage.

The first water to emerge is cool. It has been held inside the stone.

The young animal lifts one edge.

A limb unfolds, touches the new outlet, and withdraws.

The power waits.

By the next evening, the new passage is warm. Its flow is weaker than the old one, but it reaches a broader patch of stone. The animal extends across it without releasing its original hold.

For a while it feeds from both.

Above, the root continues into the gap.

***

The power’s attention does not remain only there.

Along the rim, the young are being released into the exchange of warm and cool water. One catches a dark leaf with two discs. Another passes through the stems and vanishes against a pale trunk.

The power follows them.

It finds hollows with warmth but no shelter, shelters with no seepage, good stone just beyond the reach of the faint currents. It finds places where young animals have settled and left nothing that a passing adult would recognize.

For several nights, it considers drawing them all toward the fissures.

But the currents carry other things as well. Some of the folded shapes released beneath the leaves must reach cool water. Some drifting grains will become the rust-colored growth inside an animal’s mouth. A current strengthened enough to guide the young would pull these past the places where they now settle.

The power makes no new current.

Instead, where warm water already travels close beneath an empty surface, it opens a few narrow ways through.

Not everywhere. Some stone is bearing too much weight. Some seepage carries a bitterness that the mouth folds cannot hold. Some sheltered cracks are occupied.

The work is slow.

On one descending wall, a new outlet lies beneath a lip of rock. On another, the water emerges among the roots of an apricot-colored growth. Near the eastern rim, a line of faint warmth crosses a bare shelf and reaches the foot of a sharp step.

Each place is small. None can sustain a gathering of adults.

A young animal, arriving there, can remain long enough to grow.

***

Under the tipped slab, the original seepage dwindles.

The animal notices before the power does anything further. It shifts its mouth toward the second opening. Two limbs change their hold. The old clean patch begins to gather sediment along one edge.

By then the folds are a deep rust color.

The animal has become harder to see through. Its internal rods cast thin shadows on the stone. When a jointed creature brushes its margin, it contracts, waits, and opens again.

The power does not prevent the contraction.

Nor does it remove the root, which has begun to hold the slab.

Months pass before the animal leaves. It moves at night, following warmth along the underside of the stone. At the edge it pauses for most of a day, one disc extended into open water.

Then it finds the slope.

There is no sign beside the passage the power opened. The grain it loosened lies in the pale sediment below, indistinguishable from grains broken free by roots and cooling stone.

Beneath the slab, water continues to emerge.

For a long while, the place is empty.

Then a clear limb reaches into it.
