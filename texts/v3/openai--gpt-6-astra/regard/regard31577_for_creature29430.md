The power does not lift the slab.

It knows what the pale strip is, and what the narrowness does for it. It does not need to expose the folds in order to attend to them.

For a long time, measured at the surface by the loosening and return of ice, it follows the water through the work.

There are places where a chamber receives enough to remain alive but not enough to extend a new margin. There are places where the last connection between two portions of a body is becoming thin. Elsewhere, two sheets meet along a seam and maintain their separate edges. The power follows both without treating the water between them as a defect.

It learns the differences between stillnesses.

One is the stillness of a body adequately sheltered, with food passing slowly through its folds. Another is the stillness of a body whose passages have closed. From the channel they look alike. Even within the stone, the difference may be no more than the persistence of a small exchange across a membrane.

The power attends to that exchange.

It does not assume that the creature wishes to rise, or travel, or see the surface. There is nothing deficient in its having no face. Whatever experience belongs to it occurs here, distributed through these contacts: the broad pressure of a resting slab, the slight easing along a growing edge, water arriving with something that can be used.

The first change is made beneath a curb.

A brick has softened at one end. Above it rests a slab, and beneath the slab a folded chamber is slowly being pressed shut. The creature has begun withdrawing from the weight, but the only other cavity within reach is already cut off from the current.

The power takes a little of the load.

Not all of it. The tissue needs contact, and the slab must not lift. The failing brick simply ceases to give way quite so quickly. Farther inside the curb, three grains of clay move from a blocked seam into an empty pocket.

Water passes.

For several days nothing else happens. Then the folds in the neighboring cavity spread slightly. A pale margin, which had thickened without advancing, begins to extend beneath the brick.

The power waits until the passage can sustain it.

There are many such places.

It could hold every stone forever. But the creature grows into openings, occupies the channels left by dead roots, and establishes new chambers in the imperfect bedding of fallen work. A place in which nothing could settle would not remain a good home merely because no wall fell.

So the power does not preserve the city entire.

An unoccupied stair subsides. A long facing, behind which nothing lives, loosens into the basin below. Silt continues to gather in courtyards. The water is allowed to wear away the distinctions the builders made.

Where living tissue is caught in a failure, however, the failure becomes slower, or takes another course.

A slab settles onto a fragment of pottery instead of closing flat against the course beneath it. The fragment bears little weight, but enough. A chamber keeps its roof. Where a body must withdraw, a sheltered seam remains open ahead of it.

Sometimes the power can do no more without doing too much at once. Then it stays with the small adjustment: a pressure reduced, a current maintained, a collapse delayed through another season.

It does not hurry the animal to make its help more visible.

Beyond the inhabited terraces, it begins another kind of work.

The deepest paving is almost sealed beneath sediment. Here the power raises no monuments and clears no broad avenues. It moves through the old bedding, finding durable pieces: a hard-fired sherd, a stone flake, a brick whose center has outlasted its surface. It places them in arrangements with narrow interiors and more than one path for water.

The spaces differ. Some are no larger than those beneath a tile. Others branch through a low bank. They are not all connected.

The power makes these shelters where the existing currents can supply them without drawing nourishment away from the occupied walls. It works gradually enough that the sediment remains settled. Above, the weed continues to lean in its former direction.

Most of the new seams remain empty for a long time.

This is permitted.

One winter, a dark lobe loosens inside a damaged terrace. It passes through a crack and comes to rest in silt. During the next thaw, a weak current rolls it against the edge of a sherd.

The power has left shelter there.

It does not open the lobe. It does not instruct the tissue inside. Months later, a white rim appears of its own accord.

There are failures still. Some lobes never soften. Some new growth reaches a blind end and ceases. The power watches these closely, learning which failures belong to the creature’s ordinary way of spreading and which arise from a home becoming steadily less habitable.

It changes what it has misunderstood.

Over years, then over periods longer than the oldest timber has yet endured, the help acquires a pattern. Where the drowned work closes, other sheltered intervals become available. The total refuge no longer diminishes with every settling wall.

The creature is not made responsible for preserving the place. It need not hold the stones together. It may feed against a peg until the peg is gone.

Nor must it become something else to inhabit what comes next.

Under the slab marked by closely spaced blows, the transparent fold continues opening into its small pocket. Water crosses it and leaves by a seam that will now remain passable. The tissue thickens along the underside of the stone.

There is no announcement in the channel.

The loose leaf on the ledge darkens and softens. At last a portion of it comes away, turns once in the water, and passes beneath the curb.

Inside, the folds receive it.
